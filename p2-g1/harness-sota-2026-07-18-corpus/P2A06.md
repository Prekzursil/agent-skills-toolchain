# P2A06 — Multi-agent orchestration: when it wins, when it hurts, and how far to fan out

**Unit:** P2A06  
**Date:** 2026-07-18  
**Lens:** External research only. No local harness/repository claims were used.  
**Question:** When does multi-agent orchestration beat a single agent, when does it hurt, what are the coordination and error-compounding costs, and what evidence supports particular fan-out sizes?

## Executive conclusion

Multi-agent orchestration is not a general capability multiplier. The best-supported discriminator is **task structure**: parallel workers help when they can pursue genuinely independent evidence, hypotheses, or components and a central coordinator can verify and merge their outputs; they hurt when the work depends on a single evolving world state, ordered constraint satisfaction, frequent cross-worker communication, or overlapping writes. A strong single agent is the correct default.

The most informative controlled study currently available evaluated 260 configurations across six agentic benchmarks, five topologies, nine models, matched prompts/tools, and a mean 4,800-token reasoning budget. It measured outcomes from +80.8% for centralized coordination on decomposable financial reasoning to −70.0% for independent agents on sequential planning; every multi-agent topology slightly degraded average SWE-bench Verified performance. It also measured superlinear turn growth, large architecture-specific overhead, and much greater trace-level error amplification without a central verifier. This is one recent preprint, not a universal law, and its 20-instance SWE-bench/Terminal-Bench cells have wide bootstrap intervals; nevertheless, its task-structure result agrees with separate debate evaluations, a seven-framework failure study, a distributed-coordination benchmark, and production guidance from Anthropic and Claude Code. [Google/MIT scaling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [MAST failure study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657), [Silo-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01045), [Anthropic production report](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [Claude Code agent-team docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)

There is **no established universal optimum fan-out**. Anthropic reports using 3–5 parallel research subagents and recommends effort bands from one agent for simple fact lookup to 2–4 for comparisons and more than ten only for unusually broad research; its figures are vendor-internal and therefore **UNVERIFIED — settle by replaying a preregistered harness workload at equal token/tool budgets for fan-out 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, and 9**. The controlled scaling paper found per-agent capacity becomes thin beyond 3–4 under a fixed budget, but a separate agent-count sweep peaked at seven for a weaker model and at or before five for a stronger one. Silo-Bench found monotonic degradation from 2 to 100 agents on distributed-information computation. The defensible operational policy is therefore a small default (one coordinator plus at most three workers), an explicit exception for many truly independent shards, and measurement rather than mythology. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [scaling paper, pp. 20–22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [Silo-Bench, Table 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01045)

## Method and evidence standard

I prioritized peer-reviewed papers, current primary preprints with released methods/code, official product documentation, and first-party engineering reports. Framework READMEs establish that a mechanism exists, not that it improves outcomes. Vendor-internal numbers are explicitly marked **UNVERIFIED**. Results on static question-answering or pass@k-style voting are not generalized to stateful tool-using agents. Recommendations below preserve the existing Claude Code hook/subagent/skill/filesystem bus and reject a second orchestration runtime, vector/KG memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, and guardrail framework.

## 1. What is actually established

### 1.1 Task decomposability matters more than the label “multi-agent”

The Google Research/DeepMind/MIT study is the cleanest architecture comparison found. It held prompts, tools, and total reasoning budget constant over 260 configurations spanning BrowseComp-Plus, Finance-Agent, PlanCraft, Workbench, SWE-bench Verified, and Terminal-Bench. Centralized coordination improved decomposable Finance-Agent performance by 80.8%; decentralized coordination improved BrowseComp-Plus by 9.2% and Workbench by 5.6%; every topology degraded PlanCraft by 39–70%; and every topology degraded mean SWE-bench Verified by 2.1–14.9%. Its mechanism analysis distinguishes parallelizable sub-proofs from sequential state/constraint tracking. [Paper, experimental design and results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

This result is directionally corroborated by Anthropic’s production report: research is described as breadth-first and naturally parallel, while coding is described as having fewer truly parallel tasks and requiring more shared context. Anthropic’s claimed 90.2% internal research-eval uplift and up-to-90% latency reduction are **UNVERIFIED — settle with public prompts, per-query outputs, token/tool counts, and a single-agent equal-budget baseline on an independently scored research set**. The production architecture itself—lead agent, independent search workers, central synthesis, and a later citation pass—is documented. [Anthropic engineering report](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

Official Claude Code guidance reaches the same operational boundary: use subagents for self-contained, high-volume side work; use the main conversation for frequent back-and-forth, shared-context phases, quick changes, and latency-sensitive work. Its agent-team documentation says teams work best on independent research, reviews, competing debugging hypotheses, or separately owned modules, and poorly on sequential tasks, same-file edits, or dependency-heavy work. [Subagent docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents), [agent-team docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)

**Established boundary:** multi-agent value comes from parallel work and context isolation, not from multiplying personas. The topology must mirror the dependency graph.

### 1.2 Coordination has a measurable tax

In the controlled scaling study, average turns were 7.2 for one agent, 11.4 for independent workers, 26.1 decentralized, 27.7 centralized, and 44.3 hybrid. Relative overhead was 58%, 263%, 285%, and 515%, respectively; success per 1,000 tokens fell from 67.7 for the single agent to 42.4, 23.9, 21.5, and 13.6. Across agent counts, turns fit a power curve with exponent 1.724 (R² 0.974); the authors caution that topology-specific variance is substantial. [Scaling paper, Table 5 and §4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

Anthropic reports ordinary agents using about 4× chat tokens and its multi-agent research system about 15× chat tokens. This is **UNVERIFIED — settle by publishing the query distribution and per-run input/output/cache token ledger for chat, single-agent, and multi-agent modes**. Claude Code’s official cost guide independently states that token use scales with active teammates and estimates plan-mode agent teams at approximately 7× a standard session. [Anthropic engineering report](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [Claude Code cost guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)

Latency can fall even while total work rises, but only when work is parallel. Waiting for the slowest worker creates a straggler barrier; Anthropic explicitly describes synchronous subagent batches as blocking the lead and says asynchronous execution adds state-consistency and error-propagation problems. [Anthropic, production challenges](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

### 1.3 Error cancellation requires independence; communication can destroy it

Sampling-and-voting can improve static tasks because independently sampled errors may cancel. “More Agents Is All You Need” demonstrated this on static reasoning/coding benchmarks using sampling and majority voting; it did not establish that communicating, stateful tool agents improve monotonically. [TMLR paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bgzUSZ8aeg)

On interactive agentic tasks, errors instead travel through messages and actions. The controlled scaling paper measured trace-level error amplification of 17.2× for independent aggregation, 7.8× for decentralized, 4.4× for centralized, and 5.1× for hybrid coordination. Centralized validation reduced contradictions and context omissions relative to less controlled topologies. These are paper-specific operational metrics, not universal constants. [Scaling paper, Table 5 and error taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

The ACL 2024 discussion study directly observed two failure mechanisms: judge mistakes and wrong-answer propagation, including agents abandoning initially correct answers for a wrong consensus. Its three- and six-agent results also show that six agents were not consistently better than three. [ACL paper, Tables 1–3 and §5.3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331.pdf)

Conformity is independently documented. ACL 2025 experiments found all tested models sometimes conformed to a majority regardless of their initial correctness, with greater conformity under uncertainty; a separate multi-agent persona study found perceived peer pressure and even pro-debate instructions destabilized opinions/personas. [Conformity in Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.195/), [Persona inconstancy study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.c3nlp-1.2/)

The implication is mechanical: multiple outputs are useful only to the extent their failure modes are not strongly correlated. Showing every worker every other worker’s answer before it commits an independent result reduces that independence.

### 1.4 Multi-agent debate has not earned default status

An ACL 2024 study across three reasoning datasets found that a strongly prompted single agent was comparable to the best discussion system; discussion helped mainly when demonstrations were absent. On its ChatGPT-3.5 averages, a demonstrated single agent scored 75.63, while three-agent Debate scored 73.88 and six-agent CMD scored 74.46. [ACL 2024 study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331.pdf)

A broader 2025 evaluation compared five debate methods with Chain-of-Thought and Self-Consistency across nine benchmarks and four models. None of the debate methods beat CoT in more than 20% of the 36 model×benchmark settings, and under equal call/token comparisons they generally fared worse against self-consistency. The authors used the methods’ public implementations and repeated experiments three times. [MAD evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788)

A newer 2026 test-time-compute preprint reports the other side: at equal compute on MMLU-Pro and BBH, debate and mixture-of-agents exceeded self-consistency by 1.3 and 2.7 percentage points. This establishes that debate can occupy a Pareto frontier in some static reasoning settings, but not that it is a good default for tool-using harness work. [Compute-efficiency preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01566)

### 1.5 Real multi-agent failures cluster at design, alignment, and verification boundaries

MAST analyzed 1,642 traces from seven systems across coding, math, and general tasks. Its 14 failure modes fall into system design, inter-agent misalignment, and verification/termination; examples include step repetition, unclear termination, task derailment, information withholding, ignored peer input, premature termination, and incorrect verification. The initial human taxonomy achieved κ=0.88; the few-shot automated annotator achieved 94% accuracy and κ=0.77 on held-out human labels. [MAST paper and dataset methodology](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657)

Simple prompt and topology interventions helped but did not erase the problem. In the study’s ChatDev case, prompt refinement moved ProgramDev-v0 from 25.0% to 34.4% and a cyclic topology to 40.6%, while HumanEval moved only from 89.6% to 90.3% and 91.5%. The authors explicitly characterize these as non-substantial and non-universal. [MAST intervention results, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657)

Silo-Bench isolates distributed-information coordination. Across 30 algorithmic tasks, three protocols, three models, and agent counts 2/5/10/20/50/100, all models degraded as count rose. DeepSeek-V3.1 averaged 61.2% at two agents, 48.5% at five, 39.9% at ten, 33.6% at twenty, and 18.1% at one hundred; even two agents lost 15–49% of single-agent performance depending on task level. At 50+ agents on difficult tasks, agents retained partial information but achieved 0% exact success, showing that collection was not synthesis. [Silo-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01045)

### 1.6 Fan-out evidence: bounded, topology- and capability-dependent

The controlled scaling paper’s count sweep used 1/3/5/7/9 agents. Gemini 2.0 Flash peaked at seven before degrading; the stronger Gemini 2.5 Pro decentralized system peaked earlier, and centralized Pro showed diminishing returns beyond five. Under the matched 4,800-token budget, the authors found capacity became prohibitively thin beyond 3–4 agents. [Scaling paper, §§4.4–4.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

Anthropic says it commonly runs 3–5 research workers in parallel and encodes task-effort bands: one agent/3–10 calls for fact lookup, 2–4 workers/10–15 calls each for comparisons, and more than ten only for complex research with clearly separated responsibilities. These operational thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle by equal-budget replication on owner-representative tasks with quality, wall time, tokens, duplicate-work ratio, and failure rate**. [Anthropic engineering report](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

Claude Code’s current experimental agent-team guide recommends starting with 3–5 teammates and says three focused teammates can outperform five scattered ones. This is product guidance, not controlled evidence. It also warns that coordination overhead, conflicts, and diminishing returns grow with team size. [Claude Code agent-team docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)

**What is established:** “more agents” is not monotonic on agentic work. **What is not established:** whether 3, 4, 5, or 7 is optimal for this owner’s workload.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 Query-adaptive fan-out is **very likely (80-90%)** better than one fixed team size

Evidence: controlled results vary from large positive to large negative by decomposability and baseline capability; Anthropic independently scales effort by query class; static debate evaluations show sensitivity to prompts, models, and hyperparameters. A cheap rule-based decomposability gate therefore has a strong mechanistic basis. It remains **UNVERIFIED for this harness — settle with a stratified A/B test against always-one and always-three policies**. [Scaling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [MAD evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788)

Learned workflow search is more speculative. AFlow reports a 5.7% average gain over manually designed workflows on six static datasets and much lower dollar cost for some small-model workflows, but it uses Monte Carlo tree search over workflow code and adds a workflow-optimization runtime. [AFlow, ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z5uVAKwmjf)

### 2.2 Independent-first, compare-later review is **likely (55-80%)** more robust than open peer chat

Evidence: conformity and wrong-answer propagation appear after agents see peers; centralized validation had lower measured error amplification than independent concatenation or peer-heavy topologies; moderately sparse communication performed best in an EMNLP 2025 causal topology study. Exact gains are **UNVERIFIED for code/research tasks — settle by comparing blinded independent reports plus adjudication against free-form peer discussion at equal tokens**. [ACL conformity study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.195/), [ACL discussion errors](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331.pdf), [EMNLP topology study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.623/)

### 2.3 Heterogeneous models may add epistemic diversity, but are **roughly even (45-55%)** as a harness-wide win

Evidence for: ReConcile attributes gains to model diversity, and the 2025 MAD evaluation reports heterogeneity improving debate. Evidence against: the controlled agentic scaling paper’s 13 BrowseComp-Plus heterogeneous configurations found centralized mixtures 12.6 points worse than strong-model homogeneous teams and decentralized mixtures only +2.0 points, mostly attributable to the stronger constituent. This is **UNVERIFIED for the owner’s provider mix — settle with equal-dollar heterogeneous versus homogeneous runs and correlation analysis of error fingerprints**. [ReConcile](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.381/), [MAD evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788), [scaling paper limitations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

### 2.4 Direct-to-artifact handoffs are **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce lossy “telephone” effects

Evidence: Anthropic documents filesystem artifacts as a production pattern that avoids retransmitting large worker outputs through the lead; Claude Code provides isolated subagent contexts and worktree isolation natively. Exact quality and token savings are **UNVERIFIED — settle by measuring claim/detail retention and parent-context tokens for inline summaries versus artifact references**. [Anthropic appendix](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [Claude Code subagents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents), [worktrees](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/worktrees)

## 3. What is hype, and how to tell

### 3.1 “More agents is all you need” is an overgeneralized static-ensemble result

The TMLR paper demonstrates sampling-and-voting gains on static benchmarks. Calling each sample an “agent” does not establish benefits for communicating tool users with divergent environment state. The later controlled agentic study explicitly finds opposite scaling on sequential and coding tasks. [Agent Forest](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bgzUSZ8aeg), [agentic scaling study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

**Hype detector:** if workers do not act, maintain separate state, or communicate, the result is best-of-N/self-consistency evidence, not multi-agent orchestration evidence.

### 3.2 Role names are not demonstrated specialization

MetaGPT and ChatDev implement software-company roles, but repository existence and benchmark demonstrations do not show that persona labels create independent competence. MAST found role/specification violations, repetition, context loss, and verification failures across such frameworks; a separate persona study found debate instructions could increase instability. [MetaGPT repository](https://github.com/FoundationAgents/MetaGPT), [ChatDev repository](https://github.com/OpenBMB/ChatDev), [MAST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657), [persona study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.c3nlp-1.2/)

**Hype detector:** require an ablation against the same model, tools, prompt budget, and a single agent. A “researcher/reviewer/architect” label without different evidence access, tools, model, or objective is cosplay until the ablation says otherwise.

### 3.3 Best-score tables without equal compute are not orchestration evidence

Anthropic’s own BrowseComp analysis says token use alone explained 80% of performance variance and three factors together 95%; its numbers are **UNVERIFIED — settle by publishing the regression data and equal-budget baselines**. The systematic MAD evaluation found apparent debate advantages often disappeared against strong CoT/self-consistency baselines at comparable calls/tokens. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [MAD evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788)

**Hype detector:** reject comparisons lacking total input/output/cached tokens, tool calls, wall time, model prices, and the strongest single-agent equal-budget baseline.

### 3.4 Universal fan-out numbers are not supported

Three to five is a useful operational starting range, not a scaling law. Controlled peaks moved with model strength/topology, Silo-Bench degraded monotonically, and Anthropic’s >10 worker guidance is internal research practice rather than replicated science. [Scaling count sweep](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [Silo-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01045), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

**Hype detector:** a fan-out recommendation must name the task class, dependency structure, total budget, aggregation method, and stopping rule.

### 3.5 A new orchestration framework is not automatically a better harness

AutoGen currently describes itself as in maintenance mode and directs new users to Microsoft Agent Framework; Claude Code already supplies subagents, background execution, worktrees, and experimental teams. Adding another runtime would create duplicated state, permissions, cancellation, and observability surfaces without proving quality. [AutoGen repository](https://github.com/microsoft/autogen), [Claude Code parallel-agent overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agents)

**Hype detector:** demand an owner-workload win that cannot be implemented on the existing hook/skill/subagent/filesystem bus.

## 4. Specific things this owner’s harness should adopt

### 4.1 CONFIG — UNVERIFIED: Single-agent default with an explicit decomposability gate

Before delegation, classify the task on four binary questions: (a) can workers finish without seeing one another’s intermediate state; (b) can each own a disjoint evidence set/hypothesis/module; (c) can outputs be verified independently; (d) is the expected useful work substantially larger than startup+synthesis cost? Delegate only when at least three are yes. Force single-agent execution for sequential planning, same-file changes, small fixes, and work requiring a continuously shared state.

This policy is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve cost-adjusted reliability because it directly matches controlled task-structure results and official product guidance. Exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle with a labeled replay set and compare false-positive/false-negative delegation decisions against measured outcomes**. [Scaling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [Claude Code guidance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is prompt/configuration on the existing bus; no new runtime or rejected component.

### 4.2 CONFIG — UNVERIFIED: default to one lead plus at most three workers; require evidence to exceed five

Use fan-out 1 for ordinary work, 2 for a genuine compare/contrast or competing hypotheses, and 3 for broad but separable investigation. Permit 4–5 only when there are at least that many named, non-overlapping deliverables. Permit >5 only for embarrassingly parallel shards with deterministic merge/verification and a recorded budget exception.

This exact cap is **likely (55-80%)** to be near the owner’s best default: the strongest controlled study finds capacity thinning beyond 3–4 under a fixed budget; Anthropic and Claude Code operationally recommend 3–5; stronger models peaked earlier than weaker ones. It remains **UNVERIFIED — settle with fan-out 1/2/3/5/7/9 at equal tokens and tools on research, review, debugging, and implementation strata**. [Scaling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [Claude Code teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. A budget rule on native delegation, not a second scheduler.

### 4.3 CONFIG — UNVERIFIED: Centralized star topology; independent-first results; no default peer debate

Have the lead assign disjoint tasks, require workers to commit findings before seeing peers, and have only the lead compare, verify, and synthesize. Allow one targeted rebuttal round only when workers disagree on a decision-relevant claim. Do not create all-to-all chat or multi-round consensus by default.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** safer than free-form team chat for this harness because centralized validation showed lower error amplification, and independent studies document conformity, wrong-answer propagation, and debate’s weak equal-budget performance. The magnitude is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a controlled topology ablation on the same tasks**. [Scaling paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [ACL discussion study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331.pdf), [MAD evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. Uses existing parent/subagent messaging.

### 4.4 CONFIG — UNVERIFIED: Typed delegation and handoff contract

Every worker assignment should contain: original goal, bounded subtask, exclusions/ownership boundary, available inputs, required output artifact/schema, evidence standard, stop condition, token/tool/time budget, and what uncertainty must be preserved. Every return should contain: result, evidence pointers, confidence, unresolved gaps, files changed, verification performed, and terminal `SUCCESS:<id>` or `FAILED:<id> reason`.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce duplicate work and missing coverage because Anthropic reports vague tasks caused overlap/gaps, while MAST directly identifies specification, information-withholding, termination, and verification failures. Exact improvement is **UNVERIFIED — settle by scoring duplicate-work ratio, uncovered requirements, and handoff defects before/after**. [Anthropic delegation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [MAST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. A small protocol on the existing message bus.

### 4.5 NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it) — UNVERIFIED: Filesystem artifacts and worktree isolation

For research/review, workers write durable artifacts and return references rather than long lossy summaries. For parallel code edits, use native `isolation: worktree` and partition files/modules so no two workers own the same file. Keep read-only investigations in ordinary isolated subagent contexts.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce context loss and edit collision: Anthropic recommends artifact handoffs, and Claude Code officially supports per-subagent worktrees and warns that same-file team edits overwrite. Token savings are **UNVERIFIED — settle with parent-context and detail-retention measurements**. [Anthropic artifact pattern](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [Claude Code worktrees](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/worktrees), [agent-team conflict warning](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. Native functionality; no additional executor.

### 4.6 BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED: Delegation ledger and budget telemetry

Add a hook-side JSONL record per delegation: task class, dependency score, topology, model, worker count, token/tool budget, actual tokens/tool calls, wall time, duplicate-work estimate, worker terminal state, synthesis verdict, and end-state success. Enforce hard maximums and cancel redundant/failed workers; do not capture hidden reasoning or full conversation content.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** necessary for evidence-based tuning because cost and quality vary sharply by topology, and official Claude tooling exposes usage/cost fields while warning client-side dollars are estimates. Whether <200 lines is sufficient is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a spike that records one foreground and one background delegation without changing execution semantics**. [Scaling metrics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296), [Claude Agent SDK cost tracking](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/cost-tracking)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. Local append-only telemetry on the hook bus, not observability SaaS or a durable-execution engine.

### 4.7 BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED: Equal-budget multi-agent admission test

Maintain a small owner-representative replay set stratified into research breadth, competing-hypothesis debugging, code review, sequential implementation, tool-heavy operations, and same-state planning. For every proposed multi-agent rule, compare against the strongest single-agent prompt at equal total tokens and tool calls. Report end-state success, wall time, total tokens, duplicate work, error propagation, and p50/p90; require a material Pareto improvement before enabling by default.

This is **almost certain (90-99%)** to prevent benchmark-story overfitting because multiple studies show conclusions reverse under stronger baselines, equal compute, agentic rather than static tasks, or different topologies. Exact test-set size is **UNVERIFIED — settle with sequential power analysis after an initial 20-task pilot, rather than fixing a folklore number**. [MAD evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788), [ACL discussion reevaluation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331.pdf), [agentic scaling controls](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. A thin evaluation script/skill, no trained critic or new runtime.

### 4.8 CONFIG — UNVERIFIED: Do not enable experimental agent teams globally

Prefer subagents for bounded delegation. Enable Claude Code agent teams per task only when workers must communicate directly, have disjoint file ownership, and the extra coordination is itself necessary. The feature is currently experimental and documents limitations in resumption, task status, shutdown, nesting, and same-file isolation.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** the lower-risk posture based on the official feature status and the external evidence that peer communication adds overhead and propagation paths. The net value of agent teams on this owner’s work is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the admission test in §4.7 against ordinary star-topology subagents**. [Claude Code teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams), [parallel-agent comparison](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agents)

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It explicitly avoids a second orchestration/runtime layer.

### 4.9 CONFIG — Explicit non-adoptions

- **Do not add AutoGen, Microsoft Agent Framework, CrewAI, LangGraph, MetaGPT, or ChatDev as a second harness runtime.** The evidence reviewed here does not demonstrate an owner-workload advantage that compensates for duplicated lifecycle/state/permission surfaces, while existing native delegation covers the required bus. AutoGen itself is now maintenance-mode. [AutoGen repository](https://github.com/microsoft/autogen), [Claude Code parallel-agent overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agents)
- **Do not add learned topology search/AFlow to production.** It violates the anti-overfit gate by introducing an optimizer/runtime for benchmark-specific workflows. AFlow is promising research, but its six evaluations are static datasets and its search cost/maintenance surface are not justified here. [AFlow](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z5uVAKwmjf)
- **Do not add a trained critic, vector/KG coordination memory, durable-execution engine, guardrail framework, or observability SaaS.** Nothing in the reviewed evidence shows these are required to obtain the primary gains; typed handoffs, central verification, native artifacts/worktrees, and a small ledger address the measured failure modes on the existing bus.

## 5. What I could not determine

1. **The owner-specific optimal fan-out.** No external paper can determine it. Required experiment: §4.2’s equal-budget 1/2/3/5/7/9 sweep on a representative replay set.
2. **Whether the reported ~45% single-agent saturation threshold generalizes.** It comes principally from one recent controlled preprint; its absolute cross-domain predictive R² is modest and its SWE/Terminal cells are small. Required experiment: fit a simple threshold only after collecting local single-versus-multi paired outcomes. [Scaling paper limitations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296)
3. **Independent replication of Anthropic’s 90.2% research uplift, 15× token use, and up-to-90% latency reduction.** These remain **UNVERIFIED — settle with released eval data or an independent equal-budget reproduction**. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
4. **Generalization of Silo-Bench to repository work.** Its distributed algorithmic tasks cleanly measure coordination but do not model shared codebases, tests, or edit conflicts. Required experiment: information-sharded repository questions with known answers and controlled communication.
5. **Whether heterogeneous providers reduce correlated errors enough to justify routing complexity.** Published results conflict by task and topology. Required experiment: same-dollar, blinded, independent-first model mixtures with pairwise error-correlation measurement.
6. **Security propagation rates in this owner’s MCP/tool graph.** I found research on multi-agent prompt-injection propagation and defenses, but not enough mature, independently replicated evidence to recommend a new security framework. Required experiment: controlled poisoned-tool-output canaries through one-agent, star, and peer topologies with least-privilege tool sets. [BlindGuard preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08127), [security-principles position paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24019)
7. **A universal stopping rule.** Production reports and MAST establish premature stopping and endless continuation as dual failures, but not one task-independent optimum. Required experiment: compare fixed budget, evidence-coverage, disagreement, and marginal-information stopping on the replay set.

## Bottom line for the next design unit

Build the harness around a single strong agent. Treat parallel workers as a conditional resource for independent breadth, not as an organizational metaphor. Use a star topology, small fan-out, independent-first outputs, typed contracts, artifact references, native worktrees, hard budgets, and equal-budget admission tests. Do not introduce another orchestration runtime. The radical move supported by evidence is not “more agents”; it is **measurement-gated delegation that can confidently choose zero extra agents**.
