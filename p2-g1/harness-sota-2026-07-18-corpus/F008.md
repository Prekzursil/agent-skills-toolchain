# F008 — Frontier research: long-horizon planning and plan repair

**Unit:** F008  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** long-horizon planning and plan repair for the owner's Claude/Codex harness  
**Mode:** research and design only; the unpacked subject was read-only and no subject configuration was changed

## Executive judgment

The frontier does **not** establish that a general-purpose LLM can write a long plan once and then reliably execute it. What is established, within bounded domains, is narrower and more useful: plans become more reliable when steps are grounded in environment state, failures produce external evidence, repair is allowed, and a verifier—not the model's confidence—decides whether a step is complete. Peer-reviewed results support as-needed decomposition, dependency/change-impact planning for certain repository-wide edits, and feedback-driven backtracking on software repair; they do not establish months-long general autonomy ([ADaPT, NAACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.264/), [CodePlan, FSE 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/codeplan-repository-level-coding-using-llms-and-planning-2/), [SWE-Search, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a1e6783e4d739196cad3336f12d402bf-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

The most defensible harness design is therefore a small **plan-state protocol on the existing task/hook/ledger bus**, not a second planner runtime. It should expose assumptions, dependencies, acceptance checks, evidence, status, and the next action; trigger repair only from typed failure/change/stall signals; retain successful independent work; and cap repair attempts. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve recovery diagnosability and reduce whole-plan restarts because the local harness already has additive ledger events, typed fresh-receipt checks, repeated-no-progress detectors, and native task primitives; the uncertainty is whether it improves end-task success rather than merely trace quality, and the settling experiment is the local stratified A/B specified in Recommendation 7. Local evidence: `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:851-880`, `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1828-1949`, and `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2197-2228`.

The radical alternative—a durable workflow service with tree search, a learned value model, graph memory, and continuous planner agents—is rejected. It reintroduces several components forbidden by the anti-overfit gate, while the best directly relevant tree-search paper spent roughly 5-14 times the API cost of its adapted linear baseline and showed small or inconsistent advantages over compute-matched pass@5 baselines for several models ([SWE-Search cost and compute-matching tables](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a1e6783e4d739196cad3336f12d402bf-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

## Evidence standard and source handling

“Established” below means replicated as a design principle across at least two mechanically different signals—such as a peer-reviewed controlled evaluation plus local implementation evidence, or two independent peer-reviewed task regimes. It does **not** mean universal across domains or current frontier models. Every scope limitation is part of the finding.

The 2026 items labeled preprint are **UNVERIFIED outside their authors' reported experiments; independent reproduction on their released benchmark and a local harness A/B would settle them**. They are placed under “promising,” not “established.” ACL 2026 material is post the usual model-knowledge cutoff but is peer-reviewed primary material and is treated as current evidence, not as a historical consensus.

Audit findings were treated only as hypotheses; conclusions below cite the unpacked subject or primary sources directly.

## 1. Established, with citations

### 1.1 Long horizon is a reliability problem, not merely a context-window problem

METR's task-horizon work found a strong relationship between human task duration and agent success over a suite of software and reasoning tasks: the 2025 report says the evaluated models were near 100% on tasks under four minutes but below 10% on tasks above roughly four hours. METR also explicitly says the best agents were not then able to carry out substantive projects by themselves. The exponential time-horizon trend is a measurement and forecasting result, not proof that arbitrary multi-day work is solved ([METR methodology and results](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)).

Anthropic's later engineering experiment reached the same diagnosis through a mechanically different signal: across context windows, compaction alone left successor sessions guessing about half-completed work or prematurely declaring completion. Its mitigation used explicit feature state, incremental work, version-control history, progress notes, and end-to-end tests. Anthropic calls continued multi-context progress an open problem and says the demonstrated setup was optimized for full-stack web-app work, not general domains ([Anthropic, “Effective harnesses for long-running agents”](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)).

**Established boundary:** explicit continuity artifacts and objective tests address observed cross-window failure modes; an ever-larger context or summarizer does not by itself establish reliable autonomy. This boundary has two independent signals: a quantitative task-duration study and an operational multi-context harness experiment.

### 1.2 External feedback is the dependable repair signal; intrinsic self-critique is not

The TACL critical survey of self-correction found no demonstrated general success from feedback produced only by prompted LLMs, except on tasks unusually suited to self-correction; it found reliable external feedback useful and large-scale fine-tuning another viable regime ([Kamoi et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/)). Reflexion did report large gains across selected sequential decision, coding, and reasoning tasks, but its mechanism consumes task feedback and stores linguistic reflections; it is evidence for feedback-backed retry, not proof that free-form introspection is a verifier ([Reflexion, NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/1b44b878bb782e6954cd888628510e90-Abstract-Conference.html)).

The local harness already implements the correct authority ordering. A claimed fix requires a green rerun of the same failure fingerprint (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2167-2195`), and claim families require a fresh typed receipt after the last mutation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2197-2228`). The cross-tool contract likewise says terminal-state tool outputs are preferred and failures carry falsifiable preconditions (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:28-39`).

**Established boundary:** tests, build/type checks, explicit constraint violations, and other environment observations are suitable repair triggers. “Reflect harder” without a new observation is not an established repair method.

### 1.3 Decompose as needed, not uniformly

ADaPT recursively decomposes a subtask only when the executor cannot complete it. In ALFWorld, WebShop, and TextCraft, the peer-reviewed paper reports improvements up to 28.3, 27, and 33 percentage points over its baselines, and reports that decomposition depth adapts to model capability and task complexity ([ADaPT](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.264/)). This establishes conditional decomposition in those interactive environments; it does not establish a universal threshold or a coding-specific microstep size.

Anthropic's production-oriented guidance independently recommends the simplest composable pattern that meets the need, says agentic systems trade cost and latency for capability, and advises adding complexity only when evaluation demonstrates improvement ([Anthropic, “Building effective agents”](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

**Established boundary:** a plan router should distinguish direct execute-and-verify work from work needing explicit decomposition, and should deepen the plan after demonstrated difficulty. Universal “every multi-step task gets a comprehensive plan” and universal two-minute microsteps are not established.

### 1.4 Dependency and change-impact information can make repository-scale plans useful

CodePlan combines incremental dependency analysis, change-may-impact analysis, and adaptive planning. On two repository transformation classes—C# package migration and Python temporal edits—spanning changes to 2-97 files, it passed validity checks on 5 of 7 repositories; no-plan baselines using the same contextual information passed any repository. The paper is peer-reviewed and releases non-proprietary data and evaluation scripts ([CodePlan publication and repository](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/codeplan-repository-level-coding-using-llms-and-planning-2/), [CodePlan repository](https://github.com/microsoft/codeplan)).

This is strong evidence for explicit dependency/change-impact planning when edits are pervasive and mechanically checkable. It is not evidence that every issue repair needs a dependency graph: the evaluation has seven repositories and two structured transformation families.

**Established boundary:** on genuinely coupled, repository-wide work, plan steps need dependencies and affected scope; prose sequence alone omits the information that enables selective repair.

### 1.5 Backtracking and iterative refinement can improve software repair, but inference cost matters

SWE-Search reported a 23% relative improvement across five models on SWE-bench Lite by combining an adapted software agent, tree search, LLM value feedback, and discriminator debate ([SWE-Search](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a1e6783e4d739196cad3336f12d402bf-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The same paper's cost appendix is essential: GPT-4o cost rose from $40.86 for Moatless-Adapted to $576 for SWE-Search; other listed models were about fivefold more expensive. Under compute matching, SWE-Search pass@5 was only slightly above the adapted baseline for Qwen and Llama, below it for DeepSeekCoderV2.5, and more clearly above it for GPT-4o-mini.

The established result is therefore **bounded alternative exploration can recover from a bad trajectory**. It is not “MCTS is the best default harness.” The compute-matched results and cost table are a mechanically independent check on the headline accuracy claim.

### 1.6 Formal plan validity benefits from non-LLM verification

NeurIPS 2023 PlanBench work found the best evaluated model averaged roughly 12% success at autonomously producing executable plans across its classical planning domains ([critical planning investigation](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/efb2072a358cefb75886a315a6fcf880-Abstract-Conference.html), [PlanBench dataset paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/7a92bcdede88c7afd108072faf5485c8-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html)). The follow-on ICML 2024 position argues for LLM-Modulo systems in which model proposals interact with external model-based verifiers rather than relying on self-verification ([Kambhampati et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/kambhampati24a.html)).

That categorical paper title should not be generalized to “LLMs never plan”: it is a position supported by formal-planning experiments, and open-ended coding is a different regime. The useful established rule is narrower: whenever constraints are mechanically representable, use a checker to validate a proposed plan or step.

### 1.7 Visible, persistent task state is a native substrate, not a reason to add a planner service

Claude Code's official documentation says its task list stores pending/in-progress/completed items, persists across context compactions, and can be shared across sessions by a named task-list ID ([Claude Code task list](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/interactive-mode#task-list)). The pinned corpus proves native task operations are materially used: `TaskUpdate` has 3,710 calls at 0.38% error and `TaskCreate` has 2,181 calls at 1.51% error (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:94-117`). The same corpus contains one obsolete `TodoWrite` call and it failed (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:3630-3636`), providing a known-positive control that the detector can distinguish old and current task primitives.

**Established boundary:** use the native visible task surface for the operator and the existing event ledger for cross-tool projection. Do not duplicate them with a hidden plan database.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 Constraint-triggered selective plan repair

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that changing only the invalidated step and its downstream dependency cone will outperform restarting a whole plan on coupled coding tasks. Evidence: CodePlan's successful mechanism explicitly uses dependency and change-impact analysis; ADaPT deepens only the failed subtask; and SWE-Search benefits from backtracking rather than irrevocable linear execution ([CodePlan](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/codeplan-repository-level-coding-using-llms-and-planning-2/), [ADaPT](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.264/), [SWE-Search](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a1e6783e4d739196cad3336f12d402bf-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The exact gain in this heterogeneous harness is **UNVERIFIED; replay 20-50 real failed trajectories with whole-plan restart versus dependency-cone repair, using the same model, tools, and budget, to settle it**.

### 2.2 Explicit assumption invalidation as a repair primitive

It is **likely (55-80%)** that attaching a falsifiable precondition/assumption and evidence reference to each load-bearing step will reduce repeated work and false orchestrator constraints. Evidence: the local Claude contract already requires load-bearing assumptions and rechecking after failure (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:14-20`); ledger failure events already preserve preconditions (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:730-738`); and the observed D4 incident propagated a fabricated Write-tool constraint through about 15 prompts (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`). The end-task effect is **UNVERIFIED; inject seeded false assumptions into a sandboxed fan-out task and compare detection, repair latency, and downstream error count with and without evidence-linked preconditions to settle it**.

### 2.3 A compact task/progress projection across context boundaries

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a compact projection containing `task_id`, `status`, `next_action`, dependency state, evidence pointers, and invalidated assumptions will improve resume fidelity. Evidence signal one: Anthropic's multi-context experiment found progress files, feature state, git history, and clean incremental checkpoints addressed successor-session guessing and premature completion ([long-running harness experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)). Evidence signal two: the local compaction checkpoint demonstrably includes goal, decisions, and unresolved failures but no task/status/next-action projection (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1488-1513`); independently, the state reducer enumerates goal, decisions, failures, and mutation position but no task projection (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:701-758`). Those known-present fields validate both detectors before the missing task fields are reported.

The improvement magnitude is **UNVERIFIED; interrupt matched long tasks at compaction, resume in fresh contexts, and score correct next-action selection, redundant reads, stale-step execution, and false completion with and without the projection to settle it**.

### 2.4 Small bounded lookahead at irreversible decisions

It is **likely (55-80%)** that generating two materially distinct options before an expensive or irreversible commitment, then choosing with deterministic evidence where available, will capture part of search's benefit without its runtime cost. Tree of Thoughts established that deliberate branching can improve selected puzzle tasks, but its evaluations are narrow ([Tree of Thoughts, NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/271db9922b8d1f4dd7aaef84ed5ac703-Abstract-Conference.html)); SWE-Search shows both benefit and steep cost in coding. The harness-wide benefit is **UNVERIFIED; compare greedy action, two-candidate bounded lookahead, and five-branch search on high-reversibility-cost cases under equal token budgets to settle it**.

### 2.5 2026 diagnostic benchmarks as a source of adversarial cases

Three new benchmark lines are promising as **test generators**, not yet as design proof:

- DeepPlanning is peer-reviewed ACL 2026 and evaluates multi-day travel and multi-product shopping under information acquisition and coupled constraints. It reports that frontier agents still struggle, but those simulated planning tasks do not establish coding-harness interventions ([DeepPlanning, ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.335/)). It is **likely (55-80%)** that its constraint classes can seed useful local planning tests because coupled budgets and information dependencies resemble research/operations plans; transfer is **UNVERIFIED; translate ten cases into repository/research equivalents and measure whether rankings correlate to settle it**.
- AdaPlanBench is a June 2026 preprint with 307 household tasks and progressively revealed world/user constraints; its authors report only 67.75% for the best of ten models ([AdaPlanBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05622)). It is **likely (55-80%)** to expose stale-assumption repair defects, based on its direct constraint-revelation protocol. This is **UNVERIFIED outside the authors' benchmark; reproduce the released suite and a local constraint-injection subset to settle it**.
- PlanBench-XL is a June 2026 preprint with 327 retail tasks and 1,665 tools; its authors report GPT-5.4 falling from 51.90% without blocks to 11.36% under the strongest tool blocking ([PlanBench-XL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22388)). It is **likely (55-80%)** to be relevant to this harness because the local corpus includes a dead MCP repeatedly called and high structured-output failure, but transfer is **UNVERIFIED; replay matched unavailable-tool, silent-failure, and distracting-tool cases against the local router to settle it**.

### 2.6 Planning-specific evaluation rather than end-to-end success alone

The June 2026 Agent Planning Benchmark preprint separates holistic planning, feedback-conditioned planning, tool noise, broken tools, and unsolvable tasks across 4,209 cases and reports that benchmark-guided refinement improves both plan and downstream execution metrics ([APB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04874)). It is **likely (55-80%)** that separating plan validity, execution validity, repair selection, and completion validity will make local regressions easier to diagnose because one end score cannot localize the failing layer. This remains **UNVERIFIED for this harness; label a local gold set with those four failure layers and test inter-rater agreement plus defect-localization time to settle it**.

### 2.7 Future-aware lookahead and value propagation

FLARE argues that stepwise reasoning behaves myopically and adds lookahead, reward estimation, value propagation, and limited commitment. Its January 2026 preprint reports gains across several structured deterministic environments ([FLARE preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22311)). It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that its learned/model-scored value propagation would improve this harness under equal compute: the environments have explicit transitions and evaluation signals, while heterogeneous coding/research state is only partially observable and model value estimates can be correlated with the generator. This is **UNVERIFIED; a compute-matched local comparison against two-candidate evidence-ranked lookahead would settle it**. Do not adopt the value-model component before that test.

## 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

### 3.1 “Autonomous for hours/days” without a reliability curve

**Tell:** duration, token count, or number of steps is the headline, but there is no success-by-horizon curve, no fresh-context resume test, and no account of human interventions. METR shows why duration alone is misleading: success declines strongly with human task length, while a few long successes can coexist with poor reliability ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)).

### 3.2 “Self-healing” where the model supplies both diagnosis and proof

**Tell:** generator, critic, and completion judge share the same model/context and no new environment observation enters the loop. The TACL survey's central result is that prompted intrinsic feedback lacks demonstrated general self-correction while reliable external feedback works ([self-correction survey](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/)). A reflection paragraph is not a repair receipt.

### 3.3 A planning gain with no compute-matched baseline

**Tell:** best-of-N/tree search is compared with one greedy trajectory, with token, model-call, wall-clock, or dollar cost absent. SWE-Search's own appendix shows how a 23% relative headline coexists with roughly 5-14x cost and much smaller or inconsistent compute-matched gains ([SWE-Search](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a1e6783e4d739196cad3336f12d402bf-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### 3.4 Plan quality judged as prose quality

**Tell:** an LLM judge scores coherence or detail, but executability, constraint satisfaction, final environment state, and regression checks are missing. Formal PlanBench results show fluent models can still produce invalid plans ([PlanBench investigation](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/efb2072a358cefb75886a315a6fcf880-Abstract-Conference.html)). The harness should score verified outcomes and repair precision, not plan eloquence.

### 3.5 One static master plan presented as long-horizon control

**Tell:** the architecture has decomposition but no explicit invalidation condition, no state reconciliation, no replan trigger, and no preservation rule for already-valid independent work. A static checklist can aid visibility; it is not plan repair. CodePlan's distinctive components are incremental dependency/change-impact analysis and adaptive planning, not the presence of a long plan document ([CodePlan](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/codeplan-repository-level-coding-using-llms-and-planning-2/)).

### 3.6 Anthropomorphic orchestration diagrams without component ablations

**Tell:** roles named “manager,” “strategist,” “critic,” and “researcher” are treated as mechanisms, while no ablation shows which role improves outcomes. Anthropic's guidance explicitly favors simple composable patterns and warns that frameworks can obscure prompts and responses ([Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). A role name is not an independent signal.

### 3.7 Multi-agent planning claimed from fan-out throughput

**Tell:** parallel research throughput is reported as improved planning even though sequential dependencies, integration errors, and correlated judges are not measured. Parallel workers are useful for independent slices; they do not repair a coupled plan by existing. This harness must preserve the independence gate and centralized verification rather than infer plan quality from agent count.

### 3.8 “Persistent memory solves planning”

**Tell:** a vector database, knowledge graph, or ever-growing transcript is added without defining which state is authoritative, how facts expire, or how contradictions invalidate steps. Anthropic's long-running experiment succeeded with simple feature/progress files and version history, and still calls generalization open ([long-running harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)). Persistence can preserve a wrong plan just as effectively as a right one.

### 3.9 Frontier benchmark score presented as production establishment

**Tell:** a single author-created benchmark, one model family, no external replication, no contamination analysis, and no longitudinal deployment. AdaPlanBench, PlanBench-XL, APB, and FLARE are valuable 2026 preprints; until independent reproduction, they are hypotheses and test sources, not harness mandates ([AdaPlanBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05622), [PlanBench-XL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22388), [APB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04874), [FLARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22311)).

## 4. Specific things this harness should adopt

### Recommendation 1 — UNVERIFIED local thresholds: difficulty-gated planning policy

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Adopt a three-route policy in the shared behavior contract and planning skill:

1. **Direct:** one local, reversible, low-uncertainty outcome → execute, then verify.
2. **Light plan:** several cohesive but mostly independent outcomes → native visible tasks, each with an acceptance check.
3. **Dependency plan:** coupled scope, high uncertainty, expensive rollback, multi-context duration, or hidden/accumulating constraints → structured dependencies, assumptions, and repair triggers.

Do not use task count alone. Route on coupling, uncertainty, reversibility, expected context crossings, and verifier availability. Deepen a task only when execution demonstrates it is too broad, matching ADaPT's as-needed result ([ADaPT](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.264/)).

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce planning overhead without reducing success on simple work because conditional decomposition beats uniform decomposition in ADaPT's tested domains and Anthropic independently recommends adding complexity only when measured ([Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). Exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; the Recommendation 7 stratified A/B settles them**.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is policy on the existing plan/task bus, adds no runtime, and explicitly keeps the direct 100-line-loop-like path available.

### Recommendation 2 — Native visible task state as the operator-facing plan

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it).**

Use Claude Code's persistent task list for visible pending/in-progress/completed state, and the corresponding native Codex plan/task surface when operating in Codex. Do not render a second hidden dashboard. Claude's task list persists across compaction and supports a shared named ID ([Claude task list](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/interactive-mode#task-list)); the local corpus shows thousands of successful TaskCreate/TaskUpdate calls (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:94-117`).

Keep plan prose concise; the visible task description should be a verifiable outcome, not a chain-of-thought transcript. Use plan mode for analysis/approval when the task is consequential, while recognizing that official docs establish behavior, not comparative efficacy ([Claude permission modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes#plan-mode)).

**Confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that this avoids duplicate state because the native task surface and actual local usage are directly documented. Any end-task performance gain is **UNVERIFIED; the resume and operator-comprehension arms in Recommendation 7 settle it**.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. Pure native use; no new orchestration or persistence component.

### Recommendation 3 — UNVERIFIED size estimate: minimal plan-step contract on the existing ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a versioned additive `plan_step`/`plan_repair` event projection with only these fields:

```text
task_id, step_id, objective, status, deps[], affected_scope[],
assumptions[{claim, confidence, evidence_ref, checked_at}],
acceptance_checks[], evidence_refs[], next_action,
invalidated_by[], attempt, max_attempts, updated_at
```

Constraints:

- `status` is an enum such as `pending | ready | in_progress | blocked | invalidated | verified | abandoned`.
- A step becomes `verified` only through its acceptance receipt, never model declaration.
- `deps` is a partial order, not a generic “do these in order” list.
- `affected_scope` is advisory for conflict detection; it must not be treated as complete unless a post-edit change-impact check confirms it.
- Assumptions require a probe/evidence reference or the inline marker `UNVERIFIED` plus the settling experiment.
- The reducer stores only the current compact projection; raw tool traces stay in the existing event log.

Fit is directly evidenced: `EventLedger.append` already accepts sanitized additive fields (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:851-880`). The current reducer's known-present goal/decision/failure/mutation fields validate the inspection, while task/dependency/next-action fields are not among the reducer fields (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:701-758`).

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that this can remain BUILD-SMALL because the append path is schema-additive and task primitives already exist. This size estimate is **UNVERIFIED; a spike that adds the event dataclass, reducer projection, and tests and counts non-test changed lines settles it**.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It rides the current ledger and task bus; it is not a graph database, knowledge graph, or durable-execution engine. If implementation exceeds about 200 non-test lines or requires a scheduler, stop and redesign rather than silently upgrading it to BUILD-LARGE.

### Recommendation 4 — UNVERIFIED activation: evidence-triggered dependency-cone repair

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Implement a bounded transition rule, initially shadow-only:

```text
on fresh failed receipt | changed fact | invalidated assumption | repeated-no-progress:
  identify the directly affected step
  mark it invalidated
  mark only transitive dependents pending/invalidated
  preserve verified non-dependents
  require a new observation or materially different hypothesis before retry
  after two same-fingerprint failures, enumerate >=2 alternatives or escalate
  after max_attempts, report blocked with evidence; do not loop
```

The existing anti-degeneration module already detects identical no-progress, result-shape repetition, A-B-A-B cycling, post-error haste, and low information gain (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1828-1949`). The existing receipt gate already checks freshness after mutation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2197-2228`). The missing action is a typed repair transition rather than another generic nudge.

The heading carries **UNVERIFIED** because safe activation depends on measurable hook precision, and durable hook telemetry has not yet been located. **The settling experiment is exactly the live-defects D3 test: search the event ledger for a known-fired hook name and independently search transcripts for its system-reminder output; if both are empty, add minimal reason-code telemetry before shadow evaluation** (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-147`).

**Confidence:** once telemetry exists, it is **likely (55-80%)** that dependency-cone repair will reduce wasted retries, based on CodePlan, ADaPT, and SWE-Search; local precision and net success remain unmeasured.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes conditionally. It reuses the hook, ledger, receipt, and native task buses. It must remain a small state transition, not become a continuously running planner or durable workflow engine.

### Recommendation 5 — UNVERIFIED cross-provider adherence: evidence-linked assumption challenge and expiry

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Bring the Claude-side assumption discipline into the cross-provider contract consistently. Every load-bearing environmental claim supplied by an orchestrator or previous agent should be one of:

- `VERIFIED:<probe-id>@<time>` with a reproducible probe and bounded validity period;
- `USER-CONSTRAINT` when it is an instruction rather than an empirical fact; or
- `UNVERIFIED:<experiment>` when neither has been established.
