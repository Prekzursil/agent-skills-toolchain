# X155 — Cross-domain borrow: archival science → parallel-worker orchestration

**Unit:** X155  
**Source field:** archival science (provenance, appraisal, retention schedules, authenticity)  
**Target problem:** deciding how many parallel workers to run and when more stops helping  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Lens constraint honored:** external research and design only; no local-repository grounding was performed.

## Executive result

The strongest transferable mechanism is **function-based macroappraisal, operationalized through a disposition/retention schedule and provenance-bearing appraisal report**. It is not a formula for choosing *n* workers. In archival practice, it changes the unit of decision from individual documents to the functions and relationships that created them, identifies the best locations of essential evidence, tests that hypothesis against selected records, examines duplication across organizational boundaries, and records an implementable keep/dispose decision. Library and Archives Canada's (LAC's) method explicitly asks the appraiser to hypothesize “which and how many records would provide sufficient archival documentation,” test the hypothesis, and inspect suspected duplication across functional or structural boundaries ([LAC, *Macro-Appraisal and Functional Analysis, Part B*, pp. 2–3](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf)).

The valid borrow is a **worker-coverage appraisal schedule**: before launching a worker, assign it a distinct function/evidence mandate; after each small wave, appraise the returned evidence by provenance, uniqueness, completeness, and coverage; admit another worker only for an uncovered high-value function, a distinct evidence channel, or a deliberate independent check; stop when no eligible mandate has enough expected marginal value to justify its cost. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce redundant parallel work on research and audit tasks, because the archival mechanism was specifically designed to select sufficient evidence amid large volumes and cross-boundary duplication, and current agent-scaling evidence independently reports diminishing or negative returns where work is sequential or agents are homogeneous ([LAC Part B, pp. 2–3, 7–8](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf); [Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). The exact effect size is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** replay a stratified set of completed research/audit/coding tasks under fixed-headcount orchestration and the proposed coverage schedule, holding model and token budget constant, then compare unique accepted claims, independently supported claims, task score, latency, and total tokens.

The analogy does **not** transfer as a universal autoscaler. Archival appraisal is a reasoned selection discipline under long-term evidentiary goals; it does not estimate the live causal contribution of another generative worker. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a task-independent archival rule such as “stop at four workers” would be unjustified, because neither LAC nor NARA specifies an agent-like concurrency optimum, while controlled agent research finds that topology, task decomposability, tool use, baseline capability, and error propagation change the scaling result ([Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/); [Kim et al., *Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems*, 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). The archive gives the *decision representation* and *questions*; harness experiments must calibrate the threshold.

## 1. The archival mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 The selection problem archival science faced

Modern institutions produce more records than an archive can preserve and make usable. NARA describes appraisal as the process of determining final disposition—temporary or permanent—and says the purpose is both to preserve important records and avoid maintaining too much documentation ([NARA, “Appraisal and Approval”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/nara-review)). An approved records schedule describes content and format, the context or function, and whether and when records may be destroyed or must be transferred permanently ([NARA, “Records Scheduling and Appraisal”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/sch-appraisal)).

This is not merely deletion by volume. NARA's appraisal policy says appraisal is not rote, requires informed judgment and resource awareness, and applies multiple questions together rather than as a points checklist ([NARA, “Appraisal Policy”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)). NARA further says volume should affect appraisal only when archival value is marginal, and defines sampling as selecting items so that they are representative of the whole (same source, Appendix 1). Thus archival appraisal balances evidentiary sufficiency, uniqueness, future use, context, and cost; it does not equate “more” with “better.”

### 1.2 Macroappraisal: assess the generating function before the individual artifact

LAC's current history says macroappraisal emphasizes the context of creation over content and analyzes a creator's role, relationships to other institutions and citizens, mandate, and activities over time ([LAC, “A brief history of the LAC macroappraisal methodology”](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/government/information-disposition/records/methodology.html)). The Society of American Archivists (SAA) dictionary summarizes the same shift: macroappraisal uses top-down functional analysis of the records universe, followed by microappraisal of the records chosen for permanent retention ([SAA Dictionary, “macroappraisal”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/macroappraisal.html)).

The concrete LAC procedure is unusually close to the orchestration problem. Its approved guidelines direct archivists to:

1. research the complexity and relative importance of functions, subfunctions, programs, and activities;
2. find the structural sites where important functions occur;
3. understand where the impact of the function and public interaction are best documented;
4. form a hypothesis about the function's relative importance, location of critical records, and **which and how many records constitute sufficient documentation**;
5. test that hypothesis against selected records;
6. examine records outside the immediate program when duplication is suspected or systems cross boundaries; and
7. document the appraisal and preservation context in an appraisal report.

These steps appear in [LAC Part B, pp. 2–3 and 13](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf). The guidelines also give an example in which isolated appraisal by four departments would probably have produced four accessions rather than one, demonstrating that cross-institutional functional comparison is an explicit anti-duplication mechanism ([LAC Part B, pp. 7–8](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf)).

The important concept is **provenance as functional context**, not merely a filename or current custodian. A record's evidentiary significance depends on who created it, in the course of what function, under which relationships and process. That lets the archivist recognize that two differently housed series may document the same function, while two superficially similar items may document different roles or interactions.

### 1.3 Documentation strategy: coordinate distributed collectors around gaps

Macroappraisal is complemented by **documentation strategy**, a plan for adequate documentation of an issue, activity, function, or subject across multiple institutions. The SAA dictionary describes an ongoing mechanism involving creators, records administrators, archivists, users, experts, and beneficiaries; it is regularly refined as conditions and knowledge change. It explicitly distinguishes multi-institutional documentation strategy from collection analysis inside one institution and says cooperation is required because no single institution can document society alone ([SAA Dictionary, “documentation strategy”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/documentation-strategy.html)).

In its own field, this mechanism prevents every repository from collecting opportunistically in isolation. Participants first study the domain and existing documentation, identify priorities and gaps, then coordinate acquisition and retention. The direct orchestration-relevant feature is not “many archivists”; it is that **distributed effort is assigned against a shared documentation map**.

### 1.4 Retention/disposition schedules: make the decision executable

Appraisal becomes operational through a schedule. NARA says a schedule is the legal authority that identifies whether and when records may be destroyed or transferred permanently ([NARA, “Records Scheduling and Appraisal”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/sch-appraisal)). Its scheduling guidance distinguishes granular items from aggregate “big bucket” items based on common work processes, activities, or functions. Function-based aggregate items are less sensitive to organizational reorganizations, but all included series must genuinely share relevant treatment; individual series have less ambiguity but cost more to track ([NARA, “Scheduling Records,” aggregation guidance](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

Schedules also contain a **cutoff**, an event or date at which active use ends and retention/disposition begins. NARA calls a clear cutoff the foundation of an implementable disposition instruction and gives event-based examples such as completion, closure, supersession, or obsolescence ([NARA, “Scheduling Records,” cutoff guidance](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). This is valuable because “keep until it feels done” is replaced with an observable state transition.

### 1.5 Authenticity and provenance constrain what counts as evidence

Selection alone is not enough. NARA's policy says observational data appropriate for long-term retention should possess authenticity, reliability, integrity, and usability, and that intellectual linkage to related metadata is essential. Its recommended metadata include purpose, time period, collection site, methods and instrumentation, processing history, and quality assessment ([NARA, “Appraisal Policy,” observational data guidance](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)). ISO 15489 likewise covers records, record metadata, recurrent analysis of business context, record controls, and processes for creating, capturing, and managing records ([ISO 15489-1:2016 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/62542.html)).

In the proposed borrow, this is why “three workers agree” must not automatically count as three signals. If their claims descend from the same source, query, generated summary, or upstream worker, provenance reveals one evidentiary channel copied three times.

## 2. Structural mapping to orchestration

| Archival construct | Its role in archives | Harness translation |
|---|---|---|
| Function/subfunction | Stable context in which records are created | Task objective decomposed into decision-relevant questions, risks, and evidence classes |
| Structural site / office of primary interest | Location most likely to hold authoritative evidence | Repository area, primary source class, tool, environment, or stakeholder most likely to resolve a question |
| Record series | Related records produced by a common activity | A worker's evidence bundle or claim set under one mandate |
| Macroappraisal hypothesis | Prediction of what evidence, where, and how much is sufficient | Initial coverage plan plus expected marginal value of each worker mandate |
| Microappraisal | Inspect selected records to confirm value | Validate returned citations, tests, diffs, or observations before accepting them |
| Documentation strategy | Coordinates collecting across institutions and identifies gaps | Shared coverage ledger that allocates non-overlapping worker mandates |
| Provenance/metadata | Preserves creation context and supports authenticity assessment | Machine-readable lineage: worker, mandate, sources, tool calls, upstream dependencies, time, transforms |
| Retention/disposition schedule | Executable keep/transfer/destroy rule | Admit, continue, cancel, merge, or stop a worker/output based on event cutoffs |
| Appraisal report | Records rationale and context for a disposition decision | Short orchestration decision record explaining why another worker was or was not launched |

The mapping is about **evidentiary coverage**, not worker personas. A “security reviewer,” “researcher,” and “critic” may all search the same sources and restate the same claim; archival provenance would classify that as one functional/evidentiary channel with duplicated manifestations. Conversely, two workers with the same nominal role may be valuable when assigned different primary-source classes or truly independent verification paths.

## 3. Proposed harness change — **UNVERIFIED: coverage-appraisal controller**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

**UNVERIFIED premise:** the existing orchestration bus exposes worker-start, worker-complete, and worker-cancel/deny decision points plus enough structured output to maintain a per-run ledger. **Experiment to settle it:** implement a no-op hook that records those lifecycle events and validates that a known-present worker launch and completion are both observed; independently compare the event count with the orchestrator's own completed-worker list. If those events are not exposed, this exact BUILD-SMALL form does not transfer and must not be silently promoted to a daemon.

Build a per-run **Coverage Appraisal Schedule (CAS)** as a small JSON document maintained by the existing orchestration layer:

```json
{
  "objective": "decision the run must support",
  "coverage_cells": [
    {
      "id": "F2.E1",
      "function": "question or risk to resolve",
      "priority": "must|should|could",
      "evidence_channel": "primary-doc|runtime-test|code-path|counterexample|stakeholder",
      "structural_site": "where authoritative evidence is expected",
      "status": "unassigned|active|supported|contested|blocked|waived",
      "required_independence": 1,
      "accepted_lineages": []
    }
  ],
  "worker_authorities": [],
  "cutoff": {
    "coverage": "all must cells supported, contested, blocked, or explicitly waived",
    "marginal_value": "no admissible next mandate exceeds calibrated cost threshold",
    "budget": "hard owner/runtime ceiling"
  }
}
```

### 3.1 Admission rule

Do not launch a worker merely because a slot is free. Require a short **worker disposition authority** containing:

- the coverage cell(s) it owns;
- the distinct evidence channel or structural site it will inspect;
- expected artifact type (citations, failing test, design alternative, counterexample, etc.);
- explicit exclusions to prevent scope collision;
- declared lineage dependencies;
- expected marginal value and estimated cost; and
- the event cutoff that ends the assignment.

A proposed worker is admissible only if it meets at least one condition:

1. it covers an unassigned `must` or `should` cell;
2. it supplies a provenance-independent signal required for a high-risk cell;
3. it adjudicates a contested cell using a distinct method or source class; or
4. it is a predeclared diversity probe on a task class where replay data shows positive value.

This policy is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform role-name-based delegation, because function and evidence-channel identity directly measure complementarity while role labels do not; evidence is the archive's explicit comparison of records across functions/sites and the 2026 multi-agent diversity preprint's finding that homogeneous agents saturate earlier than heterogeneous channels ([LAC Part B, pp. 2–3, 7–8](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf); [Yang et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). It remains **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** label a replay corpus by mandate overlap and test whether overlap predicts rejected/duplicate findings better than agent-role identity.

### 3.2 Progressive-wave rule

Start with the smallest wave that covers independent top-priority cells. On completion, microappraise outputs and update the ledger before admitting the next wave. Parallelize cells that can be resolved independently; keep dependent cells sequential.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** safer than launching the maximum worker count at time zero, because a later wave can target observed gaps and avoid duplicating evidence already found, and controlled agent-system research reports gains on parallelizable work but degradation on sequential workflows ([Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). The magnitude and ideal wave width are **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** for each task stratum, replay widths 1, 2, 4, and the current maximum under equal token budgets; estimate quality/latency/cost Pareto fronts rather than selecting by quality alone.

### 3.3 Microappraisal rule for returned work

Each worker return must distinguish:

- claim or decision implication;
- source/evidence locator;
- provenance lineage (direct observation versus inherited claim);
- method/tool used;
- coverage cell addressed;
- whether the result confirms, contests, or leaves the cell unresolved;
- novelty relative to accepted lineages; and
- limitations or failed probes.

The controller should compute cheap deterministic signals: source-URL/file/test overlap, shared upstream-worker IDs, duplicate claim fingerprints, and coverage-cell collision. A lead agent makes the final semantic judgment. Provenance is evidence *about dependence*, not proof of truth.

### 3.4 Stop rule

Stop admitting workers when all three conditions hold:

1. **Coverage cutoff:** every `must` cell is supported, contested, blocked with named missing authority, or explicitly waived with rationale.
2. **Independence cutoff:** high-risk accepted claims meet their predeclared number of mechanically independent evidence channels; multiple workers descending from one source count once.
3. **Marginal-value cutoff:** among currently admissible mandates, none has predicted value above a task-class-specific cost threshold.

The third condition must be empirical. Let the replay log estimate for a candidate mandate class:

`EMV = P(unique accepted contribution | task class, uncovered cell, channel, wave) × contribution value − normalized token/latency/coordination cost`.

Launch only while `EMV > 0`, subject to the hard owner budget. “Contribution value” should be tied to externally scored task outcome or a predeclared rubric, not self-rated worker confidence.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this combined stop rule will avoid both premature stopping and runaway fan-out, because coverage prevents a count-only stop while marginal value prevents ceremonial duplication. The prediction is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** conduct an online shadow trial in which CAS recommends admit/stop without enforcing it, then compare counterfactual recommendations against the unique accepted contribution of each actually launched later worker.

No fixed numerical worker cap is proposed. A hard cap remains a safety/budget ceiling, not the definition of sufficiency.

### 3.5 Reappraisal rule

Archives reappraise as context, use, and policy change; documentation strategies are explicitly refined with changing information and expertise ([SAA Dictionary, “documentation strategy”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/documentation-strategy.html)). The harness analogue is to reopen the schedule when:

- a worker discovers a new task function or high-severity risk;
- accepted sources conflict;
- an expected structural site is unavailable;
- a test changes the interpretation of earlier evidence; or
- the owner changes the decision objective.

Reappraisal must not mean endless decomposition. A new cell needs a stated decision impact and priority. It is **likely (55-80%)** that this catches material unknown-unknowns without turning every tangent into another worker, because the gate couples discovery to explicit appraisal. This is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** on replay tasks with known late discoveries, measure recall of outcome-changing discoveries and the number of low-value cells added.

## 4. What the mechanism would catch that simpler orchestration misses

These are predicted catches, not claims about the current local harness; this unit intentionally did not inspect it.

### 4.1 Pseudo-parallel duplication

Three workers with different titles may cite the same page or inherit the same untested assertion. The lineage ledger collapses them into one evidence channel. This catch is **very likely (80-90%)** where source and upstream-worker IDs are available, because equality/overlap is mechanically observable. Semantic paraphrase detection beyond shared locators is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** hand-label duplicate claim pairs and measure precision/recall of URL/file overlap plus claim fingerprints.

### 4.2 Structure-based fragmentation

Splitting by directory, team, or document can cause multiple workers to cover one function while nobody covers a cross-cutting function. Function-first coverage makes the omitted cell visible. This catch is **likely (55-80%)**, supported by LAC's requirement to examine cross-functional boundaries and its example where isolated departmental appraisal would yield four accessions instead of one ([LAC Part B, pp. 2–3, 7–8](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf)). **UNVERIFIED experiment:** compare omission rates between structure-based and function-based decompositions on tasks with known cross-cutting defects.

### 4.3 False independence

Votes or consensus can look stronger than they are when every worker shares a source lineage, prompt assumption, model, or tool failure. Provenance marks the common ancestor; independent verification cells require a different method or source class. This catch is **very likely (80-90%)** for explicitly recorded dependencies and **unlikely (20-45%)** for hidden correlations such as shared model priors, because metadata can expose the former but not fully characterize the latter. **UNVERIFIED experiment:** inject both visible shared-source errors and model-common misconceptions, then measure which the lineage rule detects.

### 4.4 Premature stop caused by headcount or apparent consensus

A run with many completed workers can still lack a must-cover evidence class. The schedule stops on coverage state, not worker count. This catch is **very likely (80-90%)** when must-cover cells were correctly appraised at the start, because the missing state is explicit; it is only **roughly even (45-55%)** when the initial functional map is poor. **UNVERIFIED experiment:** seed evaluation tasks with concealed but spec-derivable requirements and score whether the initial/reappraised map captures them.

### 4.5 Runaway fan-out after sufficiency

Once must-cover cells have enough independent support, additional workers need a new mandate with positive calibrated marginal value. Rephrasing an occupied mandate is rejected. This catch is **very likely (80-90%)** for exact coverage collisions and **likely (55-80%)** for semantically overlapping mandates, because exact IDs are deterministic while semantic equivalence needs model judgment. **UNVERIFIED experiment:** replay known fan-out runs and measure workers denied, useful findings lost, and cost avoided.

### 4.6 Missing interaction or affected-party evidence

LAC's macroappraisal explicitly asks where public interaction with a program is documented, not only where official policy resides ([LAC history](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/government/information-disposition/records/methodology.html); [LAC Part B, pp. 2 and 5–6](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartB.pdf)). A harness can therefore require a user-impact, downstream-consumer, or adversarial-use cell when the task warrants it. This is **likely (55-80%)** to expose design failures missed by implementation-only workers on user-facing changes. **UNVERIFIED experiment:** compare downstream requirement/compatibility defect recall with and without an affected-party cell.

### 4.7 Unusable evidence bundles

A correct-looking conclusion without method, locator, or processing history cannot be independently checked. Minimum provenance fields flag it as unaccessioned/unaccepted. This catch is **almost certain (90-99%)** for missing required fields, because schema validation is deterministic; whether the supplied metadata is truthful is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** randomly audit recorded lineage against raw tool traces.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 5.1 Selection after creation is not allocation before generation

Archivists mainly appraise records already produced; an orchestrator decides whether to spend resources producing an unknown result. Expected marginal value is therefore a prediction absent from the archival mechanism. Retention schedules can structure the choice but cannot estimate the counterfactual contribution of an unlaunched worker. This is the central disanalogy.

**Consequence:** use archival appraisal as the representation and admission protocol, then learn thresholds from harness trials. Do not cite archival authority as evidence that a particular worker count is optimal.

### 5.2 Archival value is not task correctness

A record can be authentic evidence of what an institution did and still contain a false assertion. Provenance authenticates context and lineage; it does not verify semantic truth. NARA treats authenticity, reliability, integrity, usability, metadata, uniqueness, and completeness as separate appraisal considerations, confirming that these qualities are not interchangeable ([NARA, “Appraisal Policy”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)).

**Consequence:** retain executable tests, primary-source checks, and adversarial verification. Never let provenance scores replace outcome evaluation.

### 5.3 Stable functions are easier to appraise than evolving tasks

Government mandates and functions can persist across reorganizations; NARA notes that function-based aggregate schedules are less likely to need revision when organization changes but functions do not ([NARA, “Scheduling Records”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). Agent tasks can change after each observation, and exploratory research may not reveal its real subfunctions upfront.

**Consequence:** the schedule must support reappraisal and contested/blocked states. It must not freeze the initial decomposition.

### 5.4 Redundant records and redundant reasoning are not equivalent

Two record series may be informationally duplicative. Two workers inspecting the same evidence may still add value through independent interpretation, error correction, or adversarial challenge. Conversely, different sources may repeat one upstream error.

**Consequence:** duplication is a presumption requiring semantic appraisal, not automatic cancellation. Deliberate replication should be labeled as verification, with a distinct method where possible.

### 5.5 Long-horizon archival judgment tolerates latency that live orchestration cannot

Formal appraisal can involve research, consultation, negotiation, and public review. A coding or research run may have seconds or minutes to decide. Full archival procedure would cost more than it saves on small tasks.

**Consequence:** use a tiny ledger and progressive waves only above a task-complexity/risk trigger. For a one-file deterministic edit, one worker plus tests may be the entire schedule.

### 5.6 Sampling is dangerous when a single rare defect matters

Archival sampling seeks representative evidence for some voluminous series. Software/security work may be governed by rare catastrophic cases rather than representative behavior. NARA itself treats sampling as context-dependent and says clearly permanent records should be retained regardless of volume ([NARA, “Appraisal Policy,” volume and sampling](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)).

**Consequence:** never translate “representative sample” into permission to ignore high-severity tails. Risk-tagged cells need targeted counterexample search, not population sampling alone.

### 5.7 Human appraisers have accountable institutional authority; model appraisers may be correlated

NARA appraisers work with agencies and public input, and the Archivist has final authority ([NARA, “Appraisal and Approval”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/nara-review)). If the same model proposes the decomposition, judges novelty, and declares sufficiency, errors can be self-confirming.

**Consequence:** deterministic provenance checks and external outcome metrics must constrain semantic judgment. High-impact waiver or stop decisions should remain visible to the lead/owner.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

The proposal passes the specified anti-overfit gate only in its BUILD-SMALL form:

- It rides worker lifecycle hooks and structured worker returns already belonging to the orchestration bus.
- It stores one ephemeral per-run JSON ledger, not a vector database or knowledge graph.
- It adds no second orchestration runtime, daemon, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- It uses deterministic overlap/lineage features plus the existing lead model's judgment.
- Its learned threshold can be a small table by task class and wave, not a trained critic.

A BUILD-LARGE appraisal service is **unlikely (20-45%)** to produce enough additional value to justify its coordination and maintenance cost at this stage, because the archival borrow's useful information is compact and current scaling research identifies coordination overhead as a limiting factor ([Kim et al., 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). This is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it:** prototype the BUILD-SMALL shadow controller and measure whether event volume, latency, or cross-run calibration actually exceeds what an in-process ledger and offline report can handle. Until that failure is demonstrated, BUILD-LARGE is rejected.

## 7. Evaluation plan that can falsify the borrow

### Phase A — detector validation

1. Record one known-present worker start and completion through the proposed hook.
2. Independently reconcile those events against the orchestrator's worker registry.
3. Record source/tool/upstream-worker lineage and audit a sample against raw traces.

Failure means the BUILD-SMALL integration premise is false; do not infer that workers or provenance are absent.

### Phase B — retrospective appraisal

Select a stratified replay set covering parallel research, repository audit, sequential debugging, implementation, and mixed tasks. Human reviewers, blind to cost, label:

- unique accepted contribution per worker;
- duplicate or inherited contribution;
- evidence lineage;
- missed must-cover functions;
- outcome-changing dissent; and
- whether each later worker justified its marginal cost.

Test whether function/evidence-channel overlap predicts low marginal contribution better than headcount, worker title, or launch order. The archival borrow is supported if it improves prediction out of sample; otherwise reject or narrow it.

### Phase C — fixed-budget counterfactual replay

Compare:

- single worker;
- current fixed/default fan-out;
- maximum immediate fan-out;
- progressive CAS waves; and
- CAS with provenance-independent verification.

Hold model family, total token ceiling, tools, and task set constant. Report task score, pass rate, unique accepted contributions, independently supported high-risk claims, wall time, tool errors, tokens, and coordination overhead. Plot Pareto fronts; do not average away task strata.

### Phase D — shadow then enforced deployment

First let CAS recommend admit/deny/stop without changing runs. Estimate false-stop risk: useful contribution made by a worker CAS would have denied. Enforce only after a predeclared non-inferiority bound on task quality and a positive cost/latency effect are met.

The progressive controller is **very likely (80-90%)** to show different optimal policies by task class, because Google reports opposite scaling behavior for parallelizable versus sequential tasks and tool/coordination effects vary by workload ([Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). The exact strata and thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle them:** the fixed-budget replay above, followed by held-out evaluation.

### Explicit rejection criteria

Reject the borrow if any of the following holds on held-out tasks:

- mandate preparation consumes more tokens/latency than it saves;
- coverage cells are too unstable to predict contribution;
- CAS stops lose outcome-changing findings beyond the predeclared non-inferiority margin;
- provenance overlap does not predict dependence or duplication;
- the lead cannot appraise outputs reliably without a second elaborate runtime; or
- simple task decomposition plus a hard cap matches CAS on quality, cost, and latency.

This makes “archival science does not transfer” an acceptable measured result rather than something the design is insulated against.

## 8. Alternatives considered

### Fixed retention-period analogy — rejected

Mapping “retain seven years” to “run seven workers” is structurally wrong. Archival retention periods respond to legal, business, evidentiary, or historical needs and time/event cutoffs; they do not estimate parallel marginal returns. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that this analogy would produce arbitrary headcounts, given the absence of any worker-like quantity in scheduling guidance and the task-sensitive agent-scaling evidence already cited.

### Original order as a scheduling algorithm — rejected

Respect des fonds/original order is useful for maintaining creation context, but it primarily governs arrangement and provenance, not how many acquisition/appraisal workers to deploy. It informs lineage preservation, not the stop policy.

### Archival sampling as the main autoscaler — rejected

Sampling can reduce appraisal volume, but representative selection does not address rare, outcome-dominating defects and does not tell an orchestrator when a generative worker will add a novel method. It remains a possible tactic *inside* a bounded evidence channel, not the top-level controller.

### Documentation strategy alone — insufficient

Documentation strategy supplies distributed gap coordination, but without a disposition cutoff it can keep expanding the documentary universe. The transfer requires macroappraisal priorities plus explicit schedule cutoffs and provenance-aware microappraisal.

### Full records-management service — rejected by anti-overfit gate

A durable archive, knowledge graph, or orchestration daemon would reintroduce prohibited complexity without evidence that an ephemeral ledger is insufficient. The source-field metaphor does not justify copying the source field's institutional machinery.

## 9. Bottom-line verdict

**Partial transfer: yes; numeric autoscaling rule: no.**

The transferable archival insight is: **appraise the function and evidentiary context before multiplying collectors; coordinate collectors against a shared documentation map; preserve provenance so duplication and false independence are visible; turn sufficiency into explicit event-based disposition instructions; reappraise when context changes.**

Translated to an agent harness, that becomes an ephemeral coverage-appraisal ledger and progressive admission/stop rule on the existing worker bus. It would make “why this worker, why now, and what independent evidence can it add?” mechanically answerable. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the representation will expose duplicate mandates and uncovered high-priority cells when lineage and coverage IDs are recorded, because those are direct deterministic comparisons. It is only **likely (55-80%)** that enforcing the resulting stop policy improves end-to-end cost/quality, because generative worker value, semantic novelty judgment, and changing task structure are not solved by archival science. Both predictions remain **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle them:** validate event detectors, perform stratified retrospective labeling, run fixed-budget replays, and require held-out non-inferiority before enforcement.

The most important negative finding is equally firm: archival science gives no defensible universal answer to “how many workers?” Any design claiming a fixed number from this analogy is forcing the borrow beyond its structural support.
