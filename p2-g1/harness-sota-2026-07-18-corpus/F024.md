# F024 — Frontier research: cross-model ensembling and adversarial peer review

**Research date:** 2026-07-19 UTC  
**Scope:** inference-time ensembles, heterogeneous-model review, LLM-as-judge panels, multi-agent debate, and adversarial review as mechanisms for a personal Claude/Codex agent harness. This is a research/design unit, not an implementation unit.

## Executive verdict

Cross-model ensembling is a real technique, but “more models agree” is not a reliability theorem. What is established is narrower:

1. Independent sampling followed by answer aggregation can substantially improve tasks with a canonical answer, and adaptive stopping can recover most of that benefit with fewer samples ([Wang et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171); [Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.761/)).
2. Equal-compute comparisons repeatedly find that multi-round debate does not reliably beat simple voting or self-consistency; debate often adds persuasion and conformity without adding information ([Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Debate or Vote, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Zhang et al., 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788)).
3. Heterogeneous models sometimes add useful diversity, and task-specific panels have beaten a single large judge, but the general “cross-family votes are independent” premise is false. A 2026 preprint measured nine judges from seven families as only about two effective independent votes, with the best single judge matching or beating the panel across its tested conditions ([PoLL, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796); [Kohli, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)).
4. The useful role of an adversarial peer is to expose a concrete counterexample, unsupported claim, missing requirement, or divergent interpretation. Its verdict is advisory until grounded by execution, sources, or a human decision. Code-based outcome graders are cheap, objective, reproducible, and preferred for coding outcomes; model graders require human calibration ([Anthropic, agent evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

The harness should therefore adopt a **selective independent-review protocol**, not a standing parliament: one fresh cross-vendor reviewer for high-stakes, non-mechanically decidable work; blinded evidence-bearing findings; at most one author response; deterministic receipts above every model verdict; and disagreement as escalation, never majority as truth. It should use ensembles only where an objective selector exists (tests, executable checks, exact answers, or claim/source support records). This is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve net reliability per review token on this owner’s workload because it directly targets correlated-review and persuasion failure modes while riding the already configured Claude↔Codex and native multi-agent surfaces; the residual uncertainty is transfer to this heterogeneous personal workload, which only the local ablation specified in §4.6 can settle ([local Codex config](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/codex/config.toml), lines 77-80 and 101-130; [local cross-vendor rule](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/rules/common/cross-vendor-dispatch.md), lines 1-72; [Anthropic production cost evidence](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

## 1. Established: what has survived meaningful comparison

### 1.1 Repeated independent attempts help when answers can be aggregated objectively

Self-consistency samples diverse reasoning paths and chooses the most frequent final answer. On the evaluated arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks it improved GSM8K by 17.9 points, SVAMP by 11.0, AQuA by 12.2, StrategyQA by 6.4, and ARC-Challenge by 3.9 ([Wang et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)). Adaptive-Consistency then showed across 17 reasoning/code-generation datasets and three models that a stopping rule based on observed agreement reduced samples by as much as 7.9× with less than 0.1 point average accuracy loss ([Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.761/)).

This establishes a test-time-compute pattern, not a general peer-review result. It works best where outputs collapse to a canonical answer or an external checker can select a candidate. A majority of free-form plans, architectural opinions, security narratives, or factual essays has no analogous correctness operator. The distinction is explicit in the self-consistency method and in later debate-vs-vote work ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171); [Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### 1.2 Debate is not established as superior to equal-compute ensembling

The first influential multi-agent-debate work reported gains in mathematical/strategic reasoning and factuality from multiple model instances exchanging answers over rounds ([Du et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)). That result demonstrated possibility, not comparative necessity. Subsequent controlled work changed the conclusion:

- ICLR 2024 found intrinsic self-correction could degrade performance and that multi-agent debate was no better than self-consistency at an equivalent number of responses ([Huang et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
- A broad benchmark of existing debate strategies found they did not reliably outperform self-consistency or multi-path ensembling and were highly prompt/hyperparameter sensitive ([Smit et al., “Should we be going MAD?”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371); the associated benchmark reports carefully prompted single-agent approaches can be up to 13× cheaper in its tested setup ([OpenReview paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=Bfr0m4Ucl6))).
- A 2025 systematic study of five debate methods, nine benchmarks, and four foundation models found no reliable win over chain-of-thought/self-consistency despite extra compute ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788)). This is a preprint, so it is supporting rather than sole evidence.
- NeurIPS 2025 formally and empirically decomposed debate into sampling plus communication and found simple majority voting accounts for most observed gains; its homogeneous-agent analysis says debate alone does not improve beyond voting ([Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

The established operational conclusion is therefore negative but useful: do not pay for rounds of conversation unless the communication step demonstrates incremental value against an equal-call independent-sampling baseline.

### 1.3 Correlation, conformity, and common misconceptions are first-order failure modes

The independence assumption behind majority-vote intuition is fragile. NeurIPS 2024 showed theoretically that similar capabilities or responses yield static dynamics that converge to the majority, including a majority produced by a shared misconception, and demonstrated interventions on four benchmarks ([Estornell & Liu](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/32e07a110c6c6acf1afbf2bf82b614ad-Abstract-Conference.html)). ICLR 2025 directly studied conformity in LLM multi-agent systems ([Weng et al., ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=st77ShxP1K)). NeurIPS 2025 found preserving correct initial responses—not conversation by itself—was the lever that improved its debate variants ([Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

A fresh 2026 preprint makes the panel version concrete: nine frontier judges from seven families on three NLI datasets supplied about two effective independent votes; actual accuracy fell 8-22 points below an independent-vote counterfactual, and the best judge matched or beat the panel across tested conditions ([Kohli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)). Because this item is very recent and not peer-reviewed, the numeric generalization is **UNVERIFIED outside its NLI/RewardBench settings; the settling experiment is the local effective-vote/error-correlation evaluation in §4.6**. Its qualitative warning is nevertheless consistent with the older peer-reviewed theory and conformity results.

### 1.4 Diverse judge panels can help, but only as calibrated task-specific instruments

PoLL used panels from disjoint model families across three judge settings and six datasets, reporting better human agreement than a single large judge, less intramodel bias, and more than 7× lower cost ([Verga et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)). ChatEval reported better alignment to human judgments on two text-evaluation benchmarks through multi-agent referee discussion, with distinct role prompts mattering ([Chan et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/25cc3adf8c85f7c70989cb8a97a691a7-Paper-Conference.pdf)). These are real positive results.

They establish “panels can beat a chosen single judge on a chosen evaluation distribution,” not “panels certify truth.” LLM judges have documented position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning biases even when aggregate human agreement exceeds 80% in a benchmark setting ([Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). Anthropic’s production research eval found one rubric-based judge call more consistent and human-aligned than multiple component judges for its own distribution, which is a useful counterexample to “more judges is always better” ([Anthropic multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Anthropic’s current eval guidance explicitly says model graders are nondeterministic, more expensive, and require calibration against human graders ([Anthropic agent evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

### 1.5 External evidence changes the value of critique

Intrinsic “look again” review is weak because the reviewer has no new information. ICLR 2024 showed GPT-4 dropping from 95.5 to 89.0 on GSM8K and from 49.0 to 43.0 on HotpotQA after two intrinsic self-correction rounds; it also showed that improvements credited to some correction schemes depended on oracle labels or better instructions introduced only in the feedback prompt ([Huang et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

By contrast, SAFE decomposes long-form responses into atomic facts, searches for evidence, and classifies support. On about 16,000 facts it agreed with crowd annotators 72%; on a random 100-case disagreement sample it won 76% of adjudications and cost over 20× less than the crowd process ([Wei et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/937ae0e83eb08d2cb8627fe1def8c751-Abstract-Conference.html); [official code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/long-form-factuality)). For code, Anthropic recommends deterministic tests/outcome checks as the natural primary grader and model rubrics only as supplements ([Anthropic agent evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

The established design principle is: a peer adds value by bringing an independent derivation, source, execution, counterexample, or task-specific rubric—not merely another fluent opinion.

### 1.6 Cost and coordination are material, not footnotes

Anthropic reports that its agents use about 4× the tokens of chat and its multi-agent systems about 15×, while its strongest internal gain was on breadth-first research with independent directions. It explicitly says highly interdependent work and most coding tasks are poorer fits than heavily parallel research ([Anthropic multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). This is vendor-reported internal evidence, not an independent benchmark, but it is primary production evidence about a deployed system.

Locally, the cross-vendor adapter has already suffered a real completion/transport failure: the rule records three Codex-backed subagents producing zero production artifacts because of adapter failures, requires blocking dispatch, output-file existence, parse/schema validation, prompt-by-file, explicit timeout, and terminal state (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/rules/common/cross-vendor-dispatch.md:1-72`). Any ensemble multiplies that failure surface. The corpus’s StructuredOutput error rate is 22.9% (846/3,694), while the local rule makes schema validity part of a cross-vendor success AND-gate (`corpus.json:94-102`; `cross-vendor-dispatch.md:19-30`). Therefore a proposal that fans out several over-strict typed reviewers would reproduce a measured local defect, not add reliability.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 Selective debate on actual disagreement

Selective systems spend debate only on uncertainty/disagreement. SELENE, published in the EACL 2026 Industry Track, reports nearly 50% lower token use while improving accuracy/calibration on BoolQ, CosmosQA, and an internal Q&A benchmark ([Verma et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-industry.7/)). This makes selective review **likely (55-80%)** to dominate always-on review for this harness because the cost direction agrees with established adaptive sampling and the local workload includes many mechanically checkable coding operations; evidence is peer-reviewed but narrow to QA, so **UNVERIFIED for this harness; a randomized local gate-vs-always-on ablation measuring accepted-case error, latency, and tokens would settle it**.

### 2.2 Heterogeneous initial generation before any interaction

The 2025 systematic debate preprint reports heterogeneity improving its debate frameworks, while a controlled 2025 logic-puzzle preprint says intrinsic model strength and group diversity dominate structural parameters ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788); [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)). The proposition that Claude and Codex independent first passes will expose more distinct failure modes than same-model replicas is **likely (55-80%)**, supported by those studies and PoLL’s disjoint-family result, but **UNVERIFIED on this owner’s tasks; §4.6’s per-defect overlap and effective-vote measurements would settle it**. Cross-vendor labels are a weak proxy for error diversity, not proof of it ([Kohli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)).

### 2.3 Support-versus-refute teams for source-grounded research

CARP, published at ICWSM 2026, uses heterogeneous models in support and refutation teams plus claim decomposition, retrieval, and multi-hop planning, and reports gains over single-agent and homogeneous multi-agent baselines on HOVER and FEVEROUS ([Lo et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42710)). Combined with peer-reviewed SAFE evidence, a bounded support/refute pass is **likely (55-80%)** to find more research-claim defects than unstructured prose review; the evidence covers fact verification, not the owner’s mixed technical research, so **UNVERIFIED locally; blind adjudication of atomic claims from 20-50 past reports against primary sources would settle it**.

### 2.4 Anti-conformity prompts and preserving minority findings

Free-MAD, an ACL Findings 2026 paper, eliminates final-round consensus, uses one debate round, explicitly tells agents not to adopt a majority merely because it is a majority, and reports improvements across eight reasoning datasets ([Cui et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1600/)). “Preserve initial answers and strongest dissent” is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce harmful flips in this harness because it agrees with NeurIPS 2025’s preservation result and established conformity failure; **UNVERIFIED for open-ended code/research review, and a before/after false-flip measurement on the local gold set would settle it** ([Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### 2.5 Agentic code review

The March 2026 c-CRAB preprint constructs held-out tests from human PR reviews and reports that the tested review agents together solve only about 40% of tasks; agent and human reviews often cover different aspects ([c-CRAB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23448)). That makes a fresh model reviewer **likely (55-80%)** to add some defect recall to a code author, but **UNVERIFIED whether a cross-model panel improves precision or net utility over one strong reviewer; a mutation-seeded, human-adjudicated local PR benchmark would settle it**. The 40% ceiling is also evidence against treating any model reviewer as an acceptance authority.

### 2.6 Learned aggregation, confidence routers, and effective-vote weighting

Recent work proposes confidence-modulated debate, minority sentinels, robust medians, and learned debate routers ([Demystifying MAD, ACL Findings 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1694/); [Minority Sentinel, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29270); [RoPoLL, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30931)). These are **unlikely (20-45%)** to justify production integration here now: their evaluations are recent/narrow, some train task-specific classifiers, and the harness lacks a local calibrated outcome set. **UNVERIFIED premise: that their confidence or debate-log features remain calibrated under changing Claude/Codex versions; rolling time-split evaluation on §4.6’s gold set would settle it.** They also risk reintroducing the explicitly rejected trained-critic/second-runtime class, so they fail this unit’s anti-overfit gate for adoption.

## 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

### 3.1 “Society of minds” without an equal-compute baseline

**Hype tell:** the paper compares a three-agent, multi-round system to one greedy call, then attributes the gain to deliberation. The correct controls are equal total calls/tokens, best single model, independent samples plus vote, self-consistency, and a no-communication panel. ICLR 2024 and NeurIPS 2025 show why this control changes the conclusion ([Huang et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### 3.2 Consensus presented as verification

**Hype tell:** “three agents agreed” appears where a test, source, state inspection, or human rubric should appear. Shared training and correlated errors violate the independent-vote intuition; debate can converge on a common misconception ([Estornell & Liu](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/32e07a110c6c6acf1afbf2bf82b614ad-Abstract-Conference.html); [Kohli, recent preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)). Consensus is a routing signal, not a receipt.

### 3.3 Personas claimed as genuine epistemic diversity

**Hype tell:** labels such as “skeptic,” “security expert,” and “CTO” are counted as independent signals although every role uses the same model, context, and evidence. ChatEval shows role prompts can improve a particular text-evaluation task, but it does not show that personas make errors independent ([ChatEval](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/25cc3adf8c85f7c70989cb8a97a691a7-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Genuine diversity must be measured by disagreement quality, defect complementarity, and error correlation.

### 3.4 Cross-vendor branding treated as independence

**Hype tell:** “Claude + GPT” is itself the validation argument. Different providers may still share web data, post-training conventions, benchmark exposure, and judge biases; a seven-family panel recently supplied only about two effective votes on its tested tasks ([Kohli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)). Cross-vendor review is worth testing, not trusting by label.

### 3.5 Debate transcripts optimized for rhetorical realism

**Hype tell:** evaluation scores coherence, role fidelity, or convergence rather than correct end state, recovered defects, false-positive cost, or supported claims. Anthropic distinguishes transcript from outcome and recommends deterministic outcome graders for coding ([Anthropic agent evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). Fluent adversarial theater can make the wrong answer more persuasive.

### 3.6 Universal review gates with no abstention or trigger policy

**Hype tell:** every edit launches a council, every council must return a winner, and cost/latency are omitted. Production multi-agent use costs roughly 15× chat tokens in Anthropic’s reported system and fits parallel breadth better than tightly dependent coding ([Anthropic multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). SELENE’s selective result and Adaptive-Consistency both point toward conditional compute ([SELENE](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-industry.7/); [Adaptive-Consistency](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.761/)).

### 3.7 A learned “truth router” before local labels exist

**Hype tell:** a confidence score, semantic entropy, or learned judge decides whether work is safe without in-distribution calibration and accepted-case error measurement. Model graders require human calibration ([Anthropic agent evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). For this harness, a trained router/critic also violates the explicit anti-overfit exclusion unless local evidence first demonstrates a large residual gap that simpler gates cannot close.

## 4. Specific adoptions for this harness

### 4.1 NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it) — independent first passes, not conversational debate

For an allowed high-stakes task, launch at most two fresh native subagents independently before either sees the other’s answer. Give both the same objective, artifact/evidence access, and done signal; do not include the author’s rationale, confidence, model identity, or another reviewer’s verdict. Preserve both first-pass artifacts verbatim. Claude Code natively runs subagents in their own context windows and supports independent parallel sessions/agent teams ([official Claude Code subagent documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)); the local Codex configuration already enables multi-agent and fan-out with eight concurrent threads (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/codex/config.toml:77-80,101-130`). This therefore does not require a second orchestrator.

Use this only for independent decomposition/re-derivation or review, never for multiple agents editing the same dependent code path. Anthropic’s production evidence says parallel breadth is the fit and dependent coding is often not ([Anthropic multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

**Expected outcome:** **likely (55-80%)** to improve defect/alternative recall on ambiguous, high-value tasks, supported by independent-sampling and heterogeneous-model evidence; **UNVERIFIED on this harness, settled by §4.6**.  
**Anti-overfit gate:** PASS. Uses native agents and existing artifacts only; no new runtime, memory layer, trained critic, SaaS, or durable engine.

### 4.3 BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — typed evidence-bearing review receipt

### 4.4 BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — one-shot adversarial reconciliation

When the reviewer returns a concrete challenge, allow exactly one response from the author containing `accept`, `refute-with-evidence`, or `needs-human`. The reconciler applies this order:

1. Task-bound deterministic receipt or reproduced counterexample.
2. Primary source or direct state inspection.
3. Human decision for preference/risk choices.
4. Model judgment only as advisory evidence.

Do not ask reviewers to converge. Preserve unresolved minority findings and report them. Do not run a second free-form debate round. NeurIPS 2025 shows debate itself adds little beyond voting and that preserving correct initial responses matters; Free-MAD reports benefits from anti-conformity and one-round trajectory-aware selection ([Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Free-MAD](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1600/)).

**Expected outcome:** **likely (55-80%)** to reduce harmful correct-to-incorrect flips and runaway token use versus open-ended debate; **UNVERIFIED on long-horizon code changes, settled by logging improve/worsen/unchanged flips in §4.6**.  
**Anti-overfit gate:** PASS. One bounded skill/ledger transition; no durable workflow engine.

### 4.5 BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — task-specific ensemble selectors

Permit multi-candidate generation only behind an explicit selector:

- Code: run the smallest task-bound failing test, then impacted tests; select only candidates passing the same checks. Reviewers may propose counterexample tests but cannot vote a failing patch into acceptance.
- Fixed-answer reasoning: start with two independent answers; if they agree, stop; if they disagree, take one additional independent sample and use the canonical answer rule. Never apply this to open-ended plans.
- Research: decompose load-bearing factual claims, then classify each as supported/contradicted/insufficient using primary-source URLs and passages. One cross-vendor verifier is enough initially.
- Subjective architecture/product choice: collect two independent options, but escalate the trade-off to the owner; do not manufacture a truth verdict.

## 5. Anti-overfit decision: what not to build

Do **not** build a debate daemon, marketplace of agents, full-mesh council runtime, vector/KG “collective memory,” durable debate workflow engine, trained critic, semantic-entropy router, or observability SaaS. Every useful proposal above fits native agents plus the existing hook/skill/MCP/ledger buses. The evidence does not show that orchestration complexity is the causal gain; equal-compute studies instead attribute most gains to additional independent samples, task difficulty, evidence, and objective selection ([Huang et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

A BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification) ensemble layer is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to outperform the proposed thin protocol on owner utility after cost and failure surface because the harness already has native fan-out, an existing cross-vendor rail, a measured 22.9% structured-output failure rate, and a documented adapter loss incident (`config.toml:77-80,101-130`; `corpus.json:94-102`; `cross-vendor-dispatch.md:1-72`). A future BUILD-LARGE proposal should be reconsidered only if §4.6 proves a large persistent gain that cannot be expressed through the existing bus and an ablation shows the new runtime—not merely extra compute—causes it.
