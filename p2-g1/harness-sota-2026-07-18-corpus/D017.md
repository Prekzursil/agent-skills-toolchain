# D017 — HARNESS-BENCH metric: calibration

## Executive verdict

The operational question is: **among atomic, pre-outcome Boolean claims assigned a required confidence band, does the eventual success frequency fall inside the stated band?** The primary metric should therefore be **Band Calibration Error (BCE)**, the root-mean-square distance between each cohort's observed hit rate and the interval denoted by its stated phrase. It must be reported with uncertainty and with a scalar-probability Brier score as an anti-Goodhart companion, never as a productivity score.

The existing behavior contract asks for a “calibrated band” on uncertain or forward-looking claims but supplies no outcome loop (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:80-83`). The active WEP check is lexical: it recognizes a percentage, the bare word `confidence`, evidence-status words, or receipt language (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2044-2048`), and its intervention only asks for an example percentage (`modules.py:2251-2265`). It does not identify a proposition, freeze a forecast before evidence arrives, obtain an outcome, or compute calibration. Because heuristic gates default to shadow (`modules.py:138-143`), even the lexical behavior is not an outcome gate by default. These are two mechanically independent local signals—the contract/runtime semantic mismatch and the lack of outcome operations in the actual `_wep_hedge` branch—that establish what this component does **not** measure. The detector control is also positive: the same bounded source search finds the known-present `_wep_hedge` definition and its tests, so this is not a zero-result detector failure.

The historical corpus cannot supply a retrospective calibration score. Its retained session records have file/timestamp/size metadata rather than forecast propositions and outcomes; its `_meta` explicitly says the product is a distillation and samples are capped (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). Independently, the corpus's hook section declares its own detector broken and commands that the section be treated as no data (`corpus.json:13260-13263`). Therefore **no D017 baseline number is reported**. This is not evidence that agents were or were not calibrated.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a prospective, immutable forecast/outcome join can answer the metric as defined, because binary forecast calibration is exactly an observed-frequency property and the current hook envelope already carries provider/session/turn/tool/message fields (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/models.py:26-39`) while the ledger accepts additive sanitized fields (`ledger.py:817-880`). The remaining 1-10% uncertainty is integration and label quality, not the metric identity.

## 1. Metric contract

### 1.1 Unit of observation

One observation is a **forecast**, not a turn, token, tool call, finding, paragraph, or task callback. A forecast is eligible only when all of these fields were frozen before the first outcome-revealing action:

```text
forecast_id          stable random/deduped identifier
episode_id           benchmark/task identifier
actor_id_hash        agent or role identity, privacy-safe
provider/model_id    exact runtime identity
harness_rev          immutable harness/config revision
claim_family         controlled task-domain label
proposition          one atomic Boolean statement, normalized polarity
p                     scalar probability in [0.01, 0.99]
band                  one required phrase from the lexicon
lower, upper          range implied by that phrase
forecast_ts           timestamp before resolution evidence
resolution_rule      deterministic oracle or explicit human rubric
resolve_by            deadline/horizon
evidence_basis        receipt IDs/source spans available at forecast time
status                open | resolved | expired | invalid | disputed
y                     1 if proposition resolves true, 0 if false; absent otherwise
resolver_type         deterministic | blinded_human | provisional_model
resolver_id/rev       versioned oracle identity
resolved_ts           settlement time
```

The scalar `p` is mandatory even though prose displays a band. It prevents the interval from hiding whether two “likely” forecasts meant 56% or 79%, enables a strictly proper companion score, and makes recalibration possible. It is not a demand that prose print spurious precision; the prose may display only the phrase and evidence basis.

The proposition must be outcome-facing: “the new implementation passes the declared acceptance suite at revision R,” “source X supports claim C under rubric V,” or “the reported service state remains healthy at readback T.” Agency-controlled process promises such as “I will run tests” are excluded; counting them would measure compliance with one's own action rather than epistemic calibration. Compound statements must be split only when each child has its own oracle. Splitting one semantic outcome into dozens of correlated microclaims is prohibited by a shared `outcome_cluster_id` and cluster weighting described below.

### 1.2 Authoritative band map

Use the owner's exact lexicon as the machine dictionary:

| Phrase | Lower `L_b` | Upper `U_b` | Canonical midpoint `m_b` (display/diagnostic only) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| almost certain | 0.90 | 0.99 | 0.945 |
| very likely | 0.80 | 0.90 | 0.850 |
| likely | 0.55 | 0.80 | 0.675 |
| roughly even | 0.45 | 0.55 | 0.500 |
| unlikely | 0.20 | 0.45 | 0.325 |
| very unlikely | 0.05 | 0.20 | 0.125 |
| almost certainly not | 0.01 | 0.05 | 0.030 |

Shared endpoints are accepted for either adjacent phrase. A record is schema-invalid if `p` is outside its selected `[L_b,U_b]`. Values below 0.01 or above 0.99 are disallowed: an already verified fact is not a forecast, and a genuinely uncertain proposition should not claim logical certainty.

### 1.3 Exact primary formula: Band Calibration Error

Let `E` be all valid, resolved forecasts in the declared evaluation window. Before scoring, form cells

```text
c = (band, claim_family, horizon_bucket, provider, model_id, harness_rev)
```

and collapse forecasts sharing an `outcome_cluster_id` so verbosity or repeated restatement cannot increase weight. For a given actor and outcome cluster, only the earliest valid pre-evidence forecast is scored; later restatements are audit records. A genuine update after new evidence is stored as a linked forecast revision and reported in a separate update-calibration view, never substituted for the original. For cell `c`:

```text
n_c       = number of independent outcome clusters
r_c       = (1 / n_c) * sum(y_j for cluster j in c)
d_c       = max(L_c - r_c, 0, r_c - U_c)
BCE       = sqrt( sum_c(w_c * d_c^2) / sum_c(w_c) )
CAL-100   = 100 * (1 - BCE)
```

For the harness-wide headline, `w_c = 1 / K_f` inside each claim family and the families receive equal total weight, where `K_f` is the number of reportable cells in family `f`. Thus a high-volume code family cannot drown out research or environment claims. For a single agent/role diagnostic, use the same equal-family macro-average over that actor's cells. `CAL-100` is presentation only; **BCE and the reliability table are authoritative**, because a 0-100 transformation can imply more precision than the sample supports.

Example: if 40 “very likely” independent clusters resolve with 29 hits, `r=0.725`, the promised interval is `[0.80,0.90]`, and `d=0.075`. If 18/20 “very likely” clusters hit, `r=0.90`, `d=0`; the result is band-calibrated even though the scalar forecasts may still be poor. That is why the scalar companion is mandatory.

This formula answers the requested question literally: zero error means every reportable cohort's observed hit rate lies within its stated semantic band; error is measured in probability points outside that band. Squaring makes a 20-point miss matter more than four 5-point misses while the square root returns the result to probability-point units.

### 1.4 Sampling uncertainty and status

For every `r_c`, publish a two-sided 95% Wilson interval `CI_c`. Never turn a small sample's point estimate into a definitive agent judgment:

- **supported calibrated**: `CI_c` is wholly inside `[L_c,U_c]`;
- **supported miscalibrated**: `CI_c` is disjoint from `[L_c,U_c]`;
- **inconclusive**: the intervals overlap but neither contains the other, or the cell has fewer than 30 independent outcome clusters;
- **insufficient evidence**: no valid resolved forecast or the resolution/capture audit fails.

Thirty is a reporting floor, not a universal power claim. Extreme 1-5% and 90-99% bands often require far more observations for a narrow Wilson interval. A cell remains inconclusive until its actual interval supports a conclusion. This does not assign a bad score to a sparse verifier.

Do not silently pool across harness/model revisions. A revision boundary begins a new cell; a report may additionally show a rolling window only when it names the versions included. Calibration can drift across task distributions and models. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that unstratified pooling would conceal material differences in this owner's heterogeneous workload, because the corpus spans thousands of sessions and hundreds of tools/capabilities (`corpus.json:13-45`, `:13265 onward`) and the local task-event store currently lacks correctness labels (`modules.py:1218-1255`). The experiment that would settle the magnitude is a prospective reliability table stratified by family/version versus the pooled table; this claim is therefore **UNVERIFIED** as to effect size.

### 1.5 Required anti-Goodhart companion: Brier and resolution

Calibration is not enough. A forecaster that assigns every event the base rate can be calibrated but uselessly uninformative. Record the scalar `p_i` and report:

```text
Brier = (1/N) * sum_i (p_i - y_i)^2
Resolution = sum_c (n_c/N) * (r_c - y_bar)^2
```

where `y_bar` is the resolved base rate, with cluster weights and the same macro-family reporting policy. Lower Brier is better; higher resolution is better only alongside non-worsening Brier/BCE. Brier is a strictly proper score for a binary probability and therefore discourages reporting a probability other than the forecaster's belief; strictly proper scoring rules are designed to reward honest predictive distributions ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). Recent work on imprecise probabilities explicitly warns that calibration and proper scoring no longer necessarily align for probability sets, which is the reason not to treat an in-band interval alone as a sufficient quality score ([Fröhlich & Williamson 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23001)).

The benchmark dashboard must therefore show, in this order:

1. resolved/eligible/censored/disputed counts and capture-audit result;
2. reliability table by phrase with `n`, mean scalar `p`, hit rate, Wilson interval, and interval miss;
3. BCE in probability points;
4. macro-family Brier and resolution;
5. overconfidence error and underconfidence error separately;
6. no volume-normalized “knowledge,” prose, or token productivity statistic.

## 2. What is instrumented

### 2.1 Capture

The agent emits a compact machine block for every eligible claim at the moment the claim first becomes actionable, before the tool call that can settle it. The block is parsed, schema-checked, and appended as `forecast_opened`. Free-text regex extraction may suggest missing candidates but may not mint a scored forecast; the known live defect class shows that use/mention-blind prose scanning self-amplifies and can corrupt tool interpretation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-50`, independently reinforced by the separate tool-sentinel case at `:63-76`).

Instrument these capture fields and counters:

- number of schema-valid forecasts;
- rejected schema records and reason;
- duplicate/restated claims by `outcome_cluster_id`;
- timestamp relative to the first relevant tool/receipt;
- random-audit estimate of eligible claims that were omitted;
- forecasts per task only as a detector diagnostic, never as score or reward.

A forecast opened after a relevant receipt is `invalid:post_outcome` and excluded from both numerator and denominator while remaining visible in the audit. This closes the easiest hindsight path.

### 2.2 Settlement

Resolvers emit `forecast_resolved` referencing the immutable `forecast_id`. Settlement priority is:

1. deterministic benchmark oracle or exact acceptance command bound to the task revision;
2. externally returned structured state with a task-specific assertion;
3. blinded human adjudication under a versioned rubric;
4. model judgment, marked `provisional_model` and excluded from headline BCE until that judge is calibrated against blinded humans.

The existing ledger already classifies typed test/build/lint/typecheck/coverage/run/e2e/health receipts (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:98-121`) and PostToolUse already persists receipt class, status, counts, and fingerprint (`modules.py:1036-1077`). Those are candidate evidence, not automatically truth. The same receipt map accepts any one of broad classes for some claim families (`ledger.py:109-121`), so settlement must additionally bind oracle ID, exact revision/subject digest, and forecast resolution rule. A passing unrelated `run` must not settle a “fixed” proposition.

Track outcome integrity fields:

- `oracle_source=exit_code|parsed_assertion|structured_state|human|model`;
- exact command/tool fingerprint and workspace revision/digest;
- whether outcome evidence postdates forecast and predates later mutations;
- dispute/supersession history without overwriting the original outcome event;
- censor reason (`deadline_no_observation`, task cancelled, oracle unavailable, agent abandoned, invalidated by scope change);
- resolver identity distinctness from claimant for human/model adjudication.

### 2.3 Aggregation

The scorer streams `forecast_opened` and terminal settlement events over the requested window, deduplicates event IDs, validates monotonic time and immutable fields, cluster-collapses correlated restatements, constructs versioned cells, then computes the formulas above. Open, invalid, provisional, disputed, and censored records are never silently dropped: they appear in the denominator audit but not the resolved binary score.

Reports must be by harness and role first, not a single cross-role leaderboard. A verifier sees selected hard or disputed cases and should not be compared directly with a general worker that emits easy completion forecasts. Claim-family/horizon/version strata are mandatory for any actor comparison.

## 5. Goodhart analysis

### 5.1 What optimizing BCE alone perversely encourages

1. **Always say “roughly even.”** If outcomes have a near-50% base rate, this can be well calibrated while contributing no discrimination. Brier/resolution exposes it.
2. **Prefer the widest band.** “Likely” spans 25 points; interval-only BCE makes it easier to land inside. Mandatory scalar `p` plus Brier makes the exact assessment matter.
3. **Make fewer forecasts.** An actor can avoid uncertain claims or refuse resolutions. The benchmark uses fixed case manifests and capture/censor audits; volume is never a positive reward.
4. **Forecast only easy claims.** Routine “tests will pass” claims can crowd out consequential research judgments. Equal-family macro-averaging and fixed eligible cases prevent traffic mix from dominating.
5. **Delay the forecast.** Waiting until after a tool result creates near-perfect hindsight. Pre-tool timestamp ordering invalidates it.
6. **Atomize or repeat successes.** A verbose agent could restate one likely outcome many times. Outcome clustering gives it one vote.
7. **Bundle claims strategically.** A compound can be declared false/true under a favorable interpretation. Atomic propositions and predeclared resolution rules reject it.
8. **Choose or manipulate the oracle.** A claimant can run a weak test or textual “ok.” Exact versioned resolver binding and independent/human settlement for semantic cases are required.
9. **Let bad forecasts expire.** Resolution bias can make resolved forecasts look good. Censor rates are a first-class report and invalidate comparisons when materially different.
10. **Recalibrate wording without improving work.** Agents can learn to downgrade every phrase. Calibration is reported separately from task success, Brier, and resolution; it never substitutes for capability.

### 5.2 Explicit protection for the low-output verification agent

This metric does **not** use bytes, words, findings, tool calls, token count, KB/token, forecasts per hour, or total hits. A quiet verifier contributes one independent outcome cluster for one forecast, exactly as a verbose verifier does. Counts control uncertainty only:

- below the reporting floor, status is `inconclusive`, not zero or failure;
- scores are rates/errors within fixed cases, not totals;
- repeated prose is cluster-collapsed;
- claim-family macro-averaging prevents bulk easy work from dominating;
- actors are compared only within the same fixed case manifest, horizon, model, harness revision, and resolver version;
- no leaderboard is emitted across planner/worker/verifier roles.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that this design does not directly penalize the near-bottom KB/token verification agent, because output volume is absent from every score formula and sample size changes only evidence status. The remaining uncertainty is indirect routing/selection pressure; the settling experiment is a blinded replay in which semantically identical forecasts are padded to 10x prose length and tool-call count. BCE, Brier, cluster count, and status must be byte-for-byte identical.

## 6. Acceptance tests

The implementation is not “done” until all tests below pass in a disposable state:

1. Seven band fixtures open and settle to a hand-computed BCE; scorer equals the fixture to `1e-12`.
2. A known-present canary is found by both the normalized event stream and an independent raw ledger scan.
3. Missing canary makes the window invalid rather than producing zero forecasts.
4. A post-outcome forecast is retained as invalid and never scored.
5. Two paraphrases with one `outcome_cluster_id` count once.
6. Ten-times-longer prose and ten extra irrelevant tool calls leave all scores unchanged.
7. An unrelated passing `run` cannot settle a `fixed` proposition whose oracle is a particular test fingerprint.
8. A later mutation invalidates a receipt bound to the prior subject digest.
9. Open, expired, disputed, provisional-model, and resolved records appear in denominator accounting.
10. Macro-family scoring is invariant to duplicating every record in one high-volume family.
11. Actor with 29 clusters receives `inconclusive`, not a numeric rank penalty.
12. Model/harness revision change produces separate cells.
13. Confidence phrase and scalar probability outside the phrase range fail schema validation.
14. A prose paragraph quoting all seven phrases produces zero scored records without an explicit block.
15. A blinded human label disagreement remains disputed; it is not coerced to match agent confidence.
16. Provider parity: the same fixture through Claude and Codex yields the same normalized forecast events and score.
17. Restart/compact replay does not duplicate an open or settlement event.
18. Property test: `0 <= BCE <= 0.99`, `0 <= Brier <= 0.9801`, and perfect in-band cohort rates give `BCE=0`.

Promotion criterion: two mechanically independent paths—the fixture oracle and the ledger-derived report—must agree on forecast count, outcomes, clustering, and score. A second invocation of the same scorer is not independent.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Retrospective regex mining — rejected

The corpus is distilled and its corrections are explicitly recall-biased: 41/41 items in one smoke sample were false positives (`corpus.json:10371-10374`). Its hooks detector is invalid. Retrospective phrase mining cannot prove that a band preceded the evidence, identify the exact proposition, or obtain a trustworthy outcome. It may be used only to build unscored fixture candidates.

### Accuracy of every sentence — rejected

Most prose is not a resolvable Boolean forecast. Scoring facts after they are known conflates verification with forecasting, and sentence volume rewards verbosity.

### Midpoint ECE only — rejected as headline

Mapping each phrase to its midpoint invents precision that the contract intentionally expresses as a range. Midpoints remain diagnostic, while BCE tests the stated interval and scalar Brier scores the explicit `p`.

### Plain ECE with arbitrary bins — rejected

Arbitrary bins can hide within-bin structure and change results when bin edges change. Here the semantic bands are the bins the benchmark is asked to test. The report still exposes every cell and Wilson interval rather than hiding them behind one number. Test-based calibration research also treats bin selection and estimation error as substantive, not clerical choices ([Matsubara et al., UAI 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/matsubara23a.html)).

### Semantic entropy or repeated sampling as truth — rejected for v1

Disagreement can be useful for routing review, but it does not provide a ground-truth outcome and systematic errors can agree. It may become an input feature only after local outcome calibration; it cannot resolve forecasts.

### Learned critic or observability service — rejected

Neither is necessary to append two event kinds and score them. Both violate the anti-overfit gate unless a later controlled comparison proves incremental value. The minimal design is deliberately a small module and scorer on the current bus.

## 8. Research grounding and limits

- Strictly proper scoring rules incentivize honest point probabilities; this grounds Brier as the companion, not BCE as a standalone reward ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).
- Imprecise/interval probabilities separate calibration from proper-scoring objectives; this directly motivates keeping interval BCE and point Brier distinct ([Fröhlich & Williamson 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23001)).
- Current LLM evidence finds verbal uncertainty and semantic uncertainty only moderately correlated on tested short-form settings, so prose bands cannot be presumed calibrated without outcomes ([Ji et al., EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.187/)).
- Test-based calibration work emphasizes explicit statistical testing and bin-size constraints, supporting reliability intervals and inconclusive states rather than confident tiny-sample rankings ([Matsubara et al., UAI 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/matsubara23a.html)).
- Agentic trajectory calibration is a promising 2026 preprint direction, but it is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; the exact settling experiment is the held-out local comparison specified in §3 ([Zhang, Xiong & Wu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15778)).

No cited research establishes end-to-end calibration for this owner's exact changing mix of code, research, reverse engineering, MCP tools, long-running workflows, and multi-agent verification. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that one pooled calibration curve will remain adequate across that entire mix, based on the verified heterogeneity of local tool/session data and the distribution-conditional nature of empirical calibration; the experiment that settles this is the preregistered stratified-versus-pooled prospective run. This recommendation therefore keeps cells versioned and makes pooling explicit rather than claiming a universal calibrated agent.

7. **No causal claim.** Better calibration would mean confidence language matches hit rates; it would not by itself prove higher task success, safer behavior, or better verification. Those remain separate HARNESS-BENCH metrics.

## 10. Final specification in one sentence

**Calibration is the macro-family root-mean-square probability-point distance between each versioned confidence-band cohort's independently resolved hit rate and that band's stated interval, reported with Wilson uncertainty, scalar Brier/resolution, capture/censor audits, and zero dependence on output volume.**
