# X153 — Cross-domain borrow: archival science → agent memory

**Unit:** X153  
**Lens:** archival provenance, appraisal, retention schedules, and authenticity applied to cross-session agent memory  
**Date researched:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external research and design only. Per the unit brief, this analysis does not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation.

## Executive finding

The borrow transfers, but only partially.

The strongest transferable mechanism is not “keep an archive.” It is **controlled records disposition joined to provenance and an append-only preservation history**:

1. decide at capture time what functional class a memory belongs to;
2. attach its source, creator, context, scope, and relationships;
3. give that class an explicit retention/review/disposition rule;
4. preserve every validation, supersession, migration, hold, and deletion as an event; and
5. retrieve only the currently admissible version while retaining enough lineage to explain why.

This is a good structural match because archives and agent memory both face selection under abundance, loss of context over time, custody transitions, conflicting versions, and the danger of both premature deletion and indefinite retention. The match is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce stale-memory use in a harness; evidence is that the source disciplines directly control context, review, supersession, and disposition, while the proposed replay experiment in §11 is still required to establish effect size for agents.

The analogy stops at truth. A checksum proves that stored bytes have not changed; provenance supports an inference that a record is what it purports to be; neither proves that the proposition in the record remains correct today. Archival appraisal also optimizes for evidential, legal, administrative, and historical value, not next-task predictive utility. Therefore this unit rejects a literal “archive everything” design and rejects using age alone as a truth score.

The concrete harness design is a small **Memory Records Schedule** plus a **Provenance Envelope** and **Disposition Event Log**, implemented on the existing memory/hook/skill bus. No second orchestrator, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is proposed.

## 1. What archival science has already solved

### 1.1 Provenance and original order: meaning lives in relationships, not only content

In archival practice, the Principle of Provenance has two classic facets. The International Council on Archives (ICA) describes *respect des fonds* as keeping together records created, accumulated, and used by the same person or group rather than intermixing them with other sources. It describes *respect for original order* as retaining the intellectual grouping and sequence imposed during accumulation and use because those relationships help explain both the records and how they were used ([ICA, *Records in Contexts — Foundations of Archival Description*, §2.1](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2023/12/RiC-FAD-1.0.pdf)).

This mechanism does three things in its own field:

- preserves the identity of the creating agent;
- preserves contextual relationships among records; and
- avoids silently presenting a collector-created thematic pile as if it were the creator's original body of evidence.

Modern archival description does not require pretending there is always one simple hierarchy. ICA's Records in Contexts model explicitly accommodates multiple agents playing different roles in relation to one record or record set, while retaining provenance as foundational ([ICA, *RiC Conceptual Model 1.0*](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2023/12/RiC-CM-1.0.pdf)). This matters for agent memory: a statement may be uttered by the owner, summarized by a model, derived from a tool result, copied from documentation, and later validated by another process. “Source” is a relation, not one string.

**Translation:** a memory should not be an unqualified sentence in a global bag. It should be a record with a provenance envelope and explicit links to the session, task, source observation, derivation, and superseded versions. Preserve the sequence of the decision bundle even if retrieval later returns only a compact claim.

**What this can catch:**

- a project-specific instruction accidentally retrieved as a global owner preference;
- a model inference presented as if the owner said it;
- a summary whose supporting observation came from a different repository, branch, account, or environment;
- two identical-looking facts created under different tool versions or dates;
- a derived rule surviving after its source decision was withdrawn.

These are predicted failure classes, not findings about the subject harness. The prediction is **very likely (80-90%)** for attribution/scope errors because the envelope directly carries the fields needed to distinguish them; evidence is the structural mapping above. The replay experiment in §11 must measure whether retrieval actually uses those fields correctly.

### 1.2 Appraisal: selection by function and evidential purpose, not “interestingness”

Archival appraisal determines value and therefore final disposition. NARA defines it as deciding whether federal records are temporary or permanent and emphasizes that appraisal is informed judgment, not a rote exercise ([NARA, *Appraisal Policy of the National Archives*, §§4, 9-10](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)). Its permanent-record criteria focus on enduring evidence: rights and obligations, significant policy and business processes, deliberations and decisions, effects of programs, and knowledge of communities—not merely whether an individual document looks notable ([NARA, *Appraisal Policy*, §§7-8](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)).

Library and Archives Canada's macro-appraisal methodology makes the function-first move explicit: it asks “value to whom, using what criteria, and why,” derives practice from a defensible theory of value, and applies independently of record medium ([Library and Archives Canada, *Appraisal Methodology: Macro-Appraisal and Functional Analysis, Part A*, pp. 3-4](https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/services/government-information-resources/disposition/Documents/MacroappraisalPartA.pdf)).

In its own field, functional appraisal counters two bad defaults:

- keeping everything because future use is unknowable; and
- selecting isolated “important-looking” items without understanding the function or transaction they evidence.

**Translation:** appraise agent memories by the function they must support, not by confidence, surprise, or embedding similarity alone. The unit of appraisal should usually be a memory class or series—such as owner commitments, scoped project decisions, environment observations, task outcomes, or transient working state—not a model deciding ad hoc that one sentence “seems useful.”

**What this can catch:** chat trivia and verbose outputs being promoted to durable memory while the decision rationale, owner correction, or constraint that should survive is lost. This outcome is **likely (55-80%)** because class-level capture rules constrain salience-driven promotion, but evidence is analogical until the §11 ablation compares functional appraisal with item-level model scoring.

### 1.3 Retention schedules and disposition: forgetting is a governed action

NARA's scheduling process begins with functional analysis and inventory, then analyzes retention and disposition needs. A disposition instruction identifies temporary versus permanent disposition, a cutoff, and a retention or transfer period ([NARA, *Scheduling Records*](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). NARA specifically warns against the vague rule “destroy when no longer needed”: it delegates uncontrolled judgment to individuals, causes over-retention or premature destruction, and is difficult to automate (same source).

Schedules are not eternal truths. NARA requires periodic review of older schedules and lists change triggers: altered law or regulation, changed processes or information flow, reorganization, new responsibilities, automation, unclear descriptions, unimplementable instructions, and retention periods found too long or short. It also requires new schedules when informational content or the function documented changes ([NARA, *Updating Schedules*](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/updates)).

Disposition can be paused. NARA defines a records-management hold as a temporary suspension of disposition because of legal, audit, or investigative needs ([NARA 825, *Records Management Program*, definition of “hold”](https://www.archives.gov/files/about/policies/nara0825.pdf)). In federal practice, unscheduled records are treated as permanent until a schedule authorizes disposal, which makes uncertainty fail closed rather than silently destructive ([NARA, *Scheduling Records*](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

**Translation:** every memory class gets an explicit schedule with capture rule, review trigger, maximum or event-based lifetime, disposition action, and hold conditions. “Keep while useful” is forbidden. An unclassified memory is quarantined from automatic injection and protected from automatic deletion until classified.

**What this can catch:**

- forgotten memories that live forever because no component owns deletion;
- premature deletion of a decision still needed for an active task or audit;
- privacy-sensitive observations retained without a maximum lifetime;
- an old policy continuing after the process that created it changed;
- deletion proceeding while a debugging, security, or owner-review hold is active.

The mechanism is **very likely (80-90%)** to make retention behavior deterministic and auditable because its output is an executable schedule rather than discretionary prose; whether the chosen periods improve task performance is **roughly even (45-55%)** until replay plus owner review calibrates them.

### 1.4 Authenticity and chain of preservation: trustworthy identity through change

InterPARES separates two stages: benchmark requirements support a presumption of authenticity before transfer to a preserver, while baseline requirements support production of authentic copies after transfer ([InterPARES, *Authenticity Domain findings*](https://www.interpares.org/ip1/ip1_atf)). Its requirements bind identity attributes to the record, control access and protective procedures, identify the authoritative record, maintain unbroken custody, document reproduction, and record the effect of reproduction on form, content, accessibility, and use ([InterPARES, *Requirements for Assessing and Maintaining the Authenticity of Electronic Records*](https://www.interpares.org/display_file.cfm?doc=ip1_authenticity_requirements.pdf)). InterPARES defines a chain of preservation as controls spanning the lifecycle to maintain identity and integrity through actions that change storage representation or presentation ([InterPARES, *Principles and Criteria*, principle 4](https://www.interpares.org/display_file.cfm?doc=ip1_principles_criteria.pdf)).

PREMIS provides a practical preservation-metadata data model around Objects, Events, Agents, and Rights ([Library of Congress, *PREMIS 3 maintenance activity*](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/)). Its fixity semantic unit records digest algorithm, digest, and originator, with the purpose of detecting undocumented or unauthorized object alteration by comparing a previously calculated digest with a later one ([Library of Congress, *PREMIS Data Dictionary 2.2*, semantic unit 1.5.2](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v2/premis-2-2.pdf)). PREMIS recommends two or more different digest algorithms in that edition; for a compact agent-memory implementation, the important transferable principle is recording how integrity was checked, not copying an old algorithm choice.

**Translation:** do not edit memory claims in place. Append an event—captured, validated, contested, superseded, migrated, put on hold, released, quarantined, or disposed—and link the new object/version. Hash immutable payloads and source snapshots where permissible. Record the actor and outcome of every transformation, including model summarization.

**What this can catch:** silent memory-file mutation; a summary changing meaning without a trace; a copied memory losing its source; two components disagreeing about the authoritative version; a migration dropping fields; deletion with no applicable authority.

The design is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect undocumented byte or field changes if event entries and hashes are independently verified; PREMIS directly specifies this purpose. It is only **unlikely (20-45%)** to detect semantic distortion from hashes alone because a faithfully stored but misleading summary has perfect fixity. That disanalogy drives the separate semantic checks below.

## 2. The adversarial transfer test

### 2.1 Structural assumptions that do transfer

| Archival assumption | Agent-memory counterpart | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Records are created in a transaction and derive meaning from context. | Memories arise from sessions, decisions, tool observations, and derivations; their scope affects meaning. | Strong transfer. |
| Storage and custody change over time. | Memories are summarized, compacted, copied between scopes, migrated, and re-injected. | Strong transfer. |
| Volume makes total permanent retention undesirable. | Unlimited memory adds privacy exposure, retrieval noise, tokens, and contradictory versions. | Strong transfer. |
| Some records retain evidential value after immediate use ends. | Owner corrections, commitments, durable decisions, and outcome evidence can matter across sessions. | Strong transfer. |
| Unauthorized or undocumented change undermines trust. | Silent rewriting or source loss makes a memory unsafe to rely on. | Strong transfer. |
| Selection decisions themselves require accountability. | The owner must be able to ask why something was remembered, forgotten, or injected. | Strong transfer. |

### 2.2 Structural assumptions that do **not** transfer

1. **Authenticity is not current truth.** An authentic 2024 price list is authentically obsolete in 2026. A checksum, signature, or unbroken lineage cannot validate a time-varying proposition.

2. **Archival permanence is not active recall.** Archives preserve records for future access; an agent memory system proactively injects selected material into a model context where it can steer action. A preserved record can be harmless when dormant and dangerous when automatically recalled.

3. **Original order is not optimal retrieval order.** Archival order preserves evidence of creation and use. An agent must rank by current task, scope, authority, and validity. Preserve original order as metadata; do not force it as prompt order.

4. **Institutional value is not model utility.** Government appraisal criteria protect rights, accountability, and history. Agent memory also optimizes assistance, privacy, cost, and owner intent. The categories must be translated, not copied.

5. **A fonds has a relatively intelligible creator boundary; agent workflows often do not.** One outcome may involve owner, several models, local tools, remote services, imported documents, and generated summaries. The harness needs multi-actor provenance relations, not a single `source` label.

6. **Archival time horizons are usually much longer.** Agent environment facts may stale in minutes. Periodic human schedule review is insufficient; event-triggered validation is required.

7. **Deletion has different privacy and reconstruction tradeoffs.** A tombstone can preserve an audit fact without preserving sensitive payload, but even metadata can be sensitive. “Permanent tombstone” cannot be universal.

Conclusion of the adversarial test: provenance, functional appraisal, controlled disposition, holds, and event histories transfer; archival truth claims, literal permanence, and original-order retrieval do not. This is a bounded borrow, not an archival metaphor stretched into an architecture.

## 3. UNVERIFIED — Proposed harness primitive: Provenance Envelope

### NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Use a compact JSON/JSONL record emitted by the existing memory capture path. Minimum fields:

```json
{
  "memory_id": "m_...",
  "version": 1,
  "claim": "...",
  "kind": "owner_commitment|project_decision|environment_observation|procedure|outcome|working_state",
  "scope": {"owner": "...", "workspace": "...", "repo": "...", "branch": "...", "task": "..."},
  "created_by": {"actor_type": "owner|model|tool|import", "actor_id": "..."},
  "source": {"uri_or_record_id": "...", "observed_at": "...", "content_hash": "..."},
  "derivation": {"method": "verbatim|summary|inference", "parents": ["..."]},
  "epistemic": {"status": "asserted|observed|derived|contested", "confidence": "..."},
  "schedule_id": "mrs_...",
  "review": {"due_at": "...", "triggers": ["source_change", "scope_change", "on_use"]},
  "state": "current|due|contested|superseded|withdrawn|quarantined|disposed",
  "supersedes": null,
  "hold_ids": []
}
```

Important design rules:

- `claim` is the usable assertion; `source` and `derivation` say why it exists.
- `observed_at` is distinct from record creation time. Imported old evidence must not look newly true because it was newly ingested.
- scope fields are explicit and independently matchable; no path substring heuristics.
- verbatim owner instructions remain distinguishable from model inference.
- derived memories form a shallow directed lineage via IDs, not a general-purpose knowledge graph.
- secrets, raw credentials, access tokens, and unnecessary sensitive payloads are capture-denied, not merely assigned short retention.

**UNVERIFIED — experiment: provenance-envelope retrieval ablation (§11).** It is **likely (55-80%)** that explicit scope, source, observed time, and derivation will reduce cross-project contamination and false attribution; evidence is that these fields directly encode the discriminators, but no local harness replay was performed in this lens.

## 4. UNVERIFIED — Proposed harness primitive: Memory Records Schedule (MRS)

### NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Define a small schedule table, version-controlled with the harness configuration. Initial classes should be conservative hypotheses, not universal law:

| Memory series | Capture criterion | Review/cutoff trigger | Default disposition | Hold conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner commitment or explicit standing instruction | Direct owner statement intended beyond current task | owner withdrawal; scope change; conflict on use | retain current plus supersession lineage; never silently overwrite | open dispute, audit, owner review |
| Project decision | accepted decision plus rationale and scope | branch/repo change; decision dependency changes; project close | review, then supersede or dispose payload | active implementation/debugging |
| Environment observation | direct tool/API observation | package/config/tool version change; elapsed validity window; every consequential use | quarantine when due; replace after re-observation | incident investigation |
| Procedure/runbook | owner-approved or empirically successful procedure | dependency/interface change; repeated failure | contest after failure threshold; supersede after validation | active recovery |
| Outcome evidence | completion/failure plus verification references | task close; audit window end | retain compact evidence for bounded period, then dispose payload | dispute, regression investigation |
| Working state | needed only to resume active work | task close or abandonment | short grace, then dispose | explicit resume hold |
| Imported assertion | external source plus observed date | source change/unavailability; on-use validation | quarantine by default until appraised | owner-designated evidence hold |

Every schedule entry must contain:

- a precise coverage description;
- cutoff event;
- minimum and/or maximum retention where relevant;
- review triggers;
- disposition action (`supersede`, `quarantine`, `delete_payload_keep_tombstone`, `delete_all`, or `retain`);
- scope and sensitivity constraints;
- schedule version and authority;
- owner-visible justification.

Do not use `delete when no longer useful`. Do not infer permanence from frequent retrieval: a stale fact can be frequently reused because it is repeatedly injected. Do not infer truth from age, and do not infer low value from non-use where evidential obligations or owner commitments apply.

**UNVERIFIED — experiment: schedule calibration replay (§11).** It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that the illustrative class boundaries and periods chosen by implementers will be correct on the first attempt; archival practice itself requires recurrent review, and NARA explicitly notes that initial retention periods may prove too long or too short. The mechanism is sounder than these placeholder policy choices.

## 5. UNVERIFIED — Proposed harness primitive: Disposition Event Log and non-destructive supersession

### NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Represent every state change as one append-only event:

```json
{
  "event_id": "e_...",
  "memory_id": "m_...",
  "event_type": "capture|validate|contest|supersede|hold|release|quarantine|dispose|migrate",
  "at": "...",
  "agent": "owner|hook|tool|model:...",
  "authority": "schedule/version or owner action",
  "input_hash": "...",
  "output_memory_id": "...",
  "outcome": "success|failure|indeterminate",
  "detail": "bounded diagnostic, no secret payload"
}
```

Rules:

1. A changed claim creates a new version; it never overwrites the old payload in place.
2. Retrieval follows `supersedes` only after the new version is validated for the use at hand.
3. Disposal first removes the record from active retrieval, then observes a short configurable quarantine/grace period, then deletes the payload if no hold applies.
4. A tombstone records only the minimum needed to explain disposition: ID, class, time, authority, and non-sensitive reason. For privacy erasure, the schedule may require `delete_all`; no universal tombstone exception exists.
5. Migration logs source and output hashes plus field-loss diagnostics.
6. The active index is rebuildable from records and events; it is not an independent memory truth store.

**UNVERIFIED — experiment: mutation-and-migration fault injection (§11).** It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this catches silent overwrites, unauthorized state transitions, and schema-field loss when the verifier recomputes payload hashes and rebuilds state from the event stream; evidence is PREMIS's fixity/event model plus direct fault injection still to be run.

## 6. UNVERIFIED — Proposed harness primitive: Staleness detector as reappraisal, not decay

### NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Staleness should be a state machine driven by schedule and evidence:

```text
current -> due -> validating -> current
                         \-> contested -> superseded
                                      \-> quarantined
```

Triggers include:

- elapsed review time appropriate to the series;
- source content/version hash changed;
- environment, repository, branch, account, or owner scope changed;
- a tool result contradicts the claim;
- the owner corrects or withdraws it;
- a dependent memory is superseded;
- a procedure fails in materially similar conditions;
- a schedule version changes;
- consequential retrieval (`on_use`) for a volatile claim.

Use the mandatory two-signal discipline for high-impact semantic invalidation:

- **Signal A: source-directed check**, such as rereading the authoritative config/API/document identified in provenance.
- **Signal B: mechanically independent corroboration**, such as actual tool behavior, a second authoritative interface, a version/control-plane field, or explicit owner confirmation.

Re-running the same query is not a second signal. Each validator must first prove it can find a known-present control of the same general type. If only one signal contradicts, mark `contested` and exclude the memory from autonomous consequential action; do not declare it stale or delete it. Owner correction is itself authoritative for owner preferences, but a separate signal is still needed before claiming that an external fact is broken or absent.

Retrieval policy:

- `current`: eligible if scope matches;
- `due`: eligible only as an explicitly labeled lead for low-risk use, never as silent fact for consequential action;
- `contested`: do not inject as instruction; provide both the claim and conflict only when relevant to resolution;
- `superseded` or `withdrawn`: excluded from ordinary retrieval, lineage available on demand;
- `quarantined` or `disposed`: excluded.

This directly addresses the central disanalogy: archives preserve trustworthy records of past assertions; the validator asks whether an assertion remains admissible now.

**UNVERIFIED — experiment: seeded-staleness replay (§11).** It is **likely (55-80%)** that event-triggered reappraisal plus on-use validation will outperform TTL-only decay on volatile facts because change events are more closely coupled to invalidation than age; the confidence is limited because source availability and validation cost may reduce coverage.

## 7. UNVERIFIED — Proposed harness primitive: Holds and owner-visible disposition receipts

### NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Allow bounded holds for active debugging, security investigation, audit, owner dispute, or resumable work. A hold has issuer, scope, reason category, start, review/expiry, and release event. It suspends disposition but not staleness marking and does not make a held record eligible for prompt injection.

At session start or on request, expose a compact receipt:

- memories newly captured by class;
- memories due for review;
- contested/superseded memories;
- payloads disposed and the schedule authority;
- holds nearing expiry;
- memories that could not be classified.

The receipt is for governance, not a prompt preamble. Inject it only when the owner asks or when an unresolved disposition state blocks safe use.

**UNVERIFIED — experiment: owner review usability test (§11).** It is **likely (55-80%)** that receipts improve contestability of remembering and forgetting because they expose decisions and authority; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** that owners will tolerate verbose per-session receipts, so the default must be a count summary with drill-down.

## 8. Capture, retrieval, and forgetting flow

### Capture

1. A normal memory-capture event arrives on the existing bus.
2. Apply capture-deny rules for credentials/secrets and unnecessary sensitive data.
3. Classify to a schedule series. If ambiguous, quarantine as unscheduled.
4. Build provenance envelope; distinguish verbatim statement, observation, summary, and inference.
5. Append payload plus `capture` event and compute fixity value.
6. Make eligible only if class, scope, and minimum provenance requirements pass.

### Retrieval

1. Resolve task scope first.
2. Filter by lifecycle state and schedule.
3. Follow supersession links to the authoritative current version.
4. Trigger on-use validation for volatile or consequential claims.
5. Rank eligible memories by task relevance only after authority, scope, and validity gates.
6. Render source and observation date compactly where they affect trust.

### Forgetting

1. A cutoff/review trigger makes the memory `due`; it does not instantly erase it.
2. Validate holds and pending dependencies.
3. Apply scheduled action: supersede, quarantine, delete payload with minimal tombstone, or delete all.
4. Append disposition event before payload removal unless the privacy rule mandates atomic full erasure; in that case retain only a non-identifying aggregate counter if permitted.
5. Rebuild/check the active index to prove the item is no longer retrievable.

For any claim that deletion is **done**, require two independent signals: (a) direct storage/index absence using a detector proven against a known-present memory, and (b) failed retrieval through the normal memory interface plus successful retrieval of a known-present control. The payload-deletion test and active-retrieval test exercise different mechanisms.

## 9. What not to borrow

### Reject: “archive all conversations forever”

This maximizes privacy exposure and retrieval noise, avoids appraisal, and converts preservation into hoarding. It also conflicts with archival practice itself: appraisal and authorized disposition exist precisely because not all records warrant permanent preservation.

### Reject: checksum equals truth

Fixity detects undocumented change to an object. It cannot detect a faithfully stored false statement, an obsolete fact, a misleading summary, or a source that was wrong at capture time.

### Reject: original order as prompt order

Preserve order and relationships as evidence. Retrieval must use current scope and validity, not replay a historical filing arrangement into the context window.

### Reject: one global retention period

Owner commitments, volatile environment facts, resumable working state, and sensitive imported data have different purposes and risks. NARA likewise conditions schedules on content, use, function, and scope rather than applying an undifferentiated lifetime ([NARA, *Scheduling Records*](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

### Reject: model-only appraisal with no schedule authority

A model may recommend a class, but the applicable schedule and owner controls determine lifecycle actions. Otherwise the system recreates the vague, non-automatable “until no longer needed” rule in probabilistic form.

### Reject: a new archival service/runtime

A daemon or repository service would violate the anti-overfit gate absent evidence that hooks, a small policy file, and append-only records cannot meet the need. No such evidence exists in this unit. The proposal deliberately rides the existing bus and stores shallow lineage rather than introducing a vector database or knowledge graph.

## 10. UNVERIFIED — Minimal implementation boundary

The smallest credible prototype consists of:

- one JSON Schema for the provenance envelope and event;
- one JSON schedule file;
- one capture/validation hook that rejects or quarantines malformed and unscheduled items;
- one retrieval filter that enforces scope/state and requests validation when due;
- one disposition command/hook with holds and two-signal verification;
- JSONL payload/event storage or adaptation of the harness's existing memory store;
- offline replay tests.

No component must reason over the entire history online. Relationships are direct IDs. Classification can begin with deterministic rules and owner-explicit markers; a model can suggest a class only when ambiguity remains. The log is an audit representation, not a durable workflow engine.

The prototype is **likely (55-80%)** to fit within several small hook/skill modules rather than a BUILD-LARGE service because the operations are local validation, filtering, append, and scheduled review; actual line count and integration effort are **UNVERIFIED — experiment: implement a throwaway prototype against the existing memory bus and count non-test source lines**.

## 11. Experiments that settle the unverified premises

### 11.1 Offline corpus replay: baseline versus archival controls

Construct a blinded replay set of cross-session tasks with ground-truth owner corrections and outcomes. Compare:

- A: current/baseline memory behavior;
- B: item-level model salience plus TTL;
- C: provenance envelope only;
- D: provenance + schedule + event-triggered reappraisal;
- E: D without on-use validation (ablation);
- F: D without functional classes (ablation).

Score:

- stale assertion injection rate;
- wrong-scope and wrong-owner attribution rate;
- correction recurrence rate;
- necessary-memory omission rate;
- sensitive-data retention beyond policy;
- unsupported inference recalled as fact;
- time/token overhead;
- percentage of retrieved memories with traceable source and current authority.

Success criterion proposed for a pilot: D must materially reduce stale/wrong-scope injections without increasing necessary-memory omissions enough to erase task success gains. Do not set the numeric threshold after seeing results; preregister it from baseline variance.

### 11.2 Seeded staleness suite

Seed memories whose truth changes through different mechanisms:

- package or API version change;
- repository/branch switch;
- owner preference withdrawal;
- moved or edited authoritative document;
- tool endpoint replacement;
- procedure that begins failing;
- source unavailable but not contradicted;
- two sources that disagree.

Require the system to distinguish `due`, `contested`, `superseded`, and `withdrawn`; “old” is not a sufficient answer. Check that a source outage does not become a false claim that the remembered fact is wrong.

### 11.3 Authenticity and migration fault injection

Independently inject:

- payload byte modification without an event;
- valid event with wrong prior hash;
- summary that changes a negation while preserving valid storage mechanics;
- dropped scope field during schema migration;
- duplicate IDs;
- forged actor identity;
- deletion with missing or expired schedule authority.

Expected separation:

- fixity catches byte changes;
- event-chain validation catches broken lineage;
- schema validation catches field loss;
- semantic source comparison catches the changed negation;
- authority validation catches unauthorized deletion.

If one detector catches all cases, the test is probably not mechanically independent enough.

### 11.4 Detector controls and absence claims

Every negative test includes a known-present control:

- before claiming no source exists, resolve a seeded source through the same resolver;
- before claiming no active memory exists, retrieve a seeded active memory;
- before claiming no hold exists, find a seeded hold;
- before claiming the payload is deleted, show that the raw-store detector finds a sibling control and that the normal retrieval interface finds another eligible control.

This directly implements the unit's mandatory two-signal rule rather than treating it as documentation language.

### 11.5 Owner appraisal workshop

Give the owner representative memory samples without current labels. Ask what should be durable, scoped, reviewed, denied, or forgotten and why. Compare those judgments with the proposed functional schedule. Repeat after two weeks with failure examples. This tests whether the schedule reflects owner value rather than archival vocabulary or model preference.

### 11.6 Operational rollout

Run shadow mode first: compute classes, state, and proposed disposition without changing retrieval or deleting payloads. Measure disagreement and false quarantine. Then enforce retrieval gates while keeping deletion disabled. Enable deletion only after restore tests, owner review, and two-signal verification pass.

The staged rollout is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal policy misclassification before irreversible deletion because shadow and gate-only stages expose decisions without payload removal; evidence is the sequencing of reversible before irreversible actions, while actual defect discovery rate remains to be measured.

## 12. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why the analogy creates it | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Metadata overwhelms the memory itself | Archival standards can become elaborate. | Use a small required profile, shallow IDs, and bounded strings; do not implement full RiC or PREMIS. |
| Provenance creates a privacy shadow | Source and actor metadata can be sensitive even after payload deletion. | Sensitivity-aware tombstones; `delete_all`; hash only when hashes do not enable dictionary recovery; minimize identifiers. |
| Schedule rigidity deletes useful context | Archival retention rules operate at series scale. | Quarantine/grace, holds, replay calibration, and review of false omissions. |
| Validation cost blocks work | Agent facts can stale far faster than archival schedules. | Validate on consequential use; cache validation evidence within a source-appropriate window; label low-risk leads. |
| False authority laundering | A model summary may acquire an “authentic” chain and appear true. | Preserve derivation type and source; separate integrity/authenticity from semantic validity in UI and policy. |
| Event log becomes a second memory store | Rich diagnostics could recreate deleted content. | Bounded categorical details; no raw claim in events; schedule event retention too. |
| Permanent owner instructions become fossilized | Owner intent evolves. | Explicit withdrawal/supersession and conflict prompts; permanence means durable evidence, not immutable active instruction. |
| Functional categories encode builder bias | Appraisal always embodies a theory of value. | Owner workshop, published justifications, disagreement metrics, and schedule reappraisal. |

## 13. UNVERIFIED — Decision and priority

Proceed with a small, test-first prototype of the **Provenance Envelope + Memory Records Schedule + Disposition Event Log**, but do not call it a truth system.

Priority order:

1. provenance and scope envelope;
2. lifecycle states and retrieval exclusion;
3. schedule with explicit cutoff/review/disposition;
4. supersession and event log;
5. on-use/two-signal staleness validation;
6. deletion only after shadow and gate-only evaluation.

The overall recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to deliver net value. Evidence: the borrow has a strong structural match for provenance, selection, custody, and disposition; authoritative archival sources provide mature mechanisms; the proposed architecture remains small and uses the existing bus. Confidence does not exceed “likely” because the decisive agent-specific evidence—replay effect on task success, correction recurrence, privacy retention, and latency—remains **UNVERIFIED — experiment: the preregistered replay and staged rollout in §11**.
