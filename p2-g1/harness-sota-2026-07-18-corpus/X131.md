# X131 — Cross-domain borrow: systems safety → agent-claim verification

**Unit:** X131  
**Source field:** systems safety (STAMP/STPA, safety/assurance cases, Swiss-cheese defence in depth, normal-accident theory, and IV&V)  
**Target:** establish whether an agent's consequential claims are true without trusting the agent and without requiring a human to read the entire trace  
**Lens boundary:** external research and design only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect the local harness, repository, settings, or session corpus. The Windows subject paths do not exist on this Linux worker, but this unit does not require them.

## Executive verdict

The borrow **does transfer, but only partially**. The useful mechanism is a combination of:

1. **A claims–argument–evidence assurance case:** turn a vague completion statement into a scoped, falsifiable claim linked to inspectable evidence and an explicit inference.
2. **STPA-style hazard-driven coverage:** derive verification obligations from unsafe ways the harness can approve, reject, delay, or stop checking a claim, rather than asking an agent to “double-check.”
3. **IV&V-style independence and diverse barriers:** obtain evidence through a channel that is technically separate from the claimant and reject two nominal checks that share a detector, source, cached state, or interpretation.
4. **Dialectical challenge:** record defeaters—specific reasons the evidence or inference may fail—and do not release the claim while a material defeater is open.

This is not a proof engine and must not be described as one. A finite assurance case cannot establish arbitrary natural-language truth in an open world. It can establish a bounded claim such as “the named test process exited 0 against tree digest D at time T,” and it can sometimes establish the stronger claim “the requested behavior is present” when independent, claim-appropriate evidence closes the relevant defeaters. It cannot turn an underspecified request, an unobservable external fact, or a correlated pair of weak checks into certainty.

The concrete proposal is an **ephemeral Claim Assurance Envelope** on the existing hook/tool bus. It gates only consequential claims, stores no new long-term memory, invokes no second orchestration runtime, and returns one of `SUPPORTED`, `CONTRADICTED`, or `UNVERIFIED` with reasons. It deliberately avoids a numeric “truth score.” This is the smallest architecture that preserves the source field's useful structure without importing its bureaucracy.

**Overall expected result — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: the seeded false-claim benchmark in §9):** it is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on safety-case traceability, NASA's independent-evidence practice, and the direct fit between STPA's control-action categories and an approve/withhold gate, that this envelope would reduce false acceptance of consequential agent claims. It is only **likely (55-80%)**, based on the same evidence plus the cost barriers reported by assurance-case practitioners, that the benefit would exceed its latency and authoring cost across ordinary low-risk work. Those are prospective harness claims, not findings.

## 1. What the source field actually solved

### 1.1 STAMP/STPA: analyze unsafe control, not only failed components

STAMP treats safety as a control problem in a sociotechnical system. STPA operationalizes that view by defining unacceptable losses and system hazards, drawing controllers, control actions, controlled processes, and feedback, then asking which control actions become unsafe in context. The MIT STPA Handbook defines an unsafe control action as one that, in a particular context and worst-case environment, leads to a hazard. It uses four exhaustive top-level categories: a needed action is not provided; an action is provided when hazardous; it is too early, too late, or out of order; or it lasts too long/stops too soon. Context is essential, and each unsafe action traces back to a hazard ([MIT STPA Handbook, pp. 34–38](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/get_file.php?name=STPA_handbook.pdf)).

STPA then works backward from each unsafe control action to loss scenarios. It explicitly considers both why a controller would issue an unsafe action and why a proper action would be executed incorrectly or not executed. Its causal prompts include flawed algorithms, incorrect or ignored feedback, delayed or absent feedback, and an internal process model that does not match reality ([MIT STPA Handbook, pp. 41–46](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/get_file.php?name=STPA_handbook.pdf)). This matters because “the component did not crash” is not a safety argument: every component may perform locally as designed while their interactions and stale beliefs produce a loss.

In its own field, STPA therefore solves a **coverage and derivation problem**. It supplies a disciplined path:

`loss → hazard → unsafe control action in context → causal scenario → constraint → evidence/test/monitor`

It does not, by itself, prove that the resulting constraints are satisfied. Its value here is identifying what must be checked and the feedback failures that could fool the checker. The handbook explicitly says causal scenarios can generate test requirements and that operational leading indicators can reveal violated design assumptions and migration toward higher-risk states ([MIT STPA Handbook, pp. 68–70](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/get_file.php?name=STPA_handbook.pdf)).

### 1.2 Safety cases and CAE/GSN: make the inference inspectable

A safety case is a structured argument supported by evidence for a scoped system and operating environment. UK Ministry of Defence guidance defines it in those terms and says its purposes include documenting evidence, clearly describing the evidence and arguments, and supporting approval or rejection ([MOD ASEMS SMP12, §§12.1.1–12.1.2](https://www.asems.mod.uk/guidance/posms/smp12)). The same guidance says evidence may be factual, historical, analytical, test-based, or judgmental, but must be recognized and preserved traceably; cases evolve through the lifecycle and should integrate subsystem assumptions and interfaces ([MOD ASEMS SMP12, §§12.2.1, 12.2.5–12.2.6](https://www.asems.mod.uk/guidance/posms/smp12)).

Goal Structuring Notation and claim trees make the implicit inference explicit: a top claim is decomposed into subclaims; evidence is attached at the leaves; assumptions, context, justifications, and strategies state why the evidence supports the claim. MOD guidance ranks deterministic or repeatably measurable arguments above probabilistic, review-based, and qualitative/indirect arguments ([MOD ASEMS, “Goal Structuring Notation and Claim Trees”](https://www.asems.mod.uk/node/165)). The UK National Cyber Security Centre gives a compact transferable definition: a claim is a true/false proposition about an object; an argument bridges evidence/subclaims to the claim; evidence is an artifact that supports or rebuts it. Its example uses an independent test facility rather than the product vendor's assertion ([NCSC, “Principles Based Assurance”](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/information/principles-based-assurance)).

Crucially, a mature safety case is not merely a dossier of supporting facts. MOD guidance requires its evidence and argument to be challenged throughout the system lifetime in an attempt to refute them; evidence undermining an accepted argument calls the whole case back into question ([MOD ASEMS SMP12, §12.2.29](https://www.asems.mod.uk/guidance/posms/smp12)). Recent empirical work with 19 assurance-case practitioners found that peer review, defeaters, and checklists were the most common confidence-assessment methods; 12 of 19 had used defeaters, while participants also reported cost, subjectivity, inadequate guidance, and lack of stopping rules ([Diemert, Shortt & Weber, *Information and Software Technology* 185 (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925001065)).

This solves a **review-scaling problem**, not truth in general. A reviewer can inspect the weakest evidence link, an exposed assumption, or an unresolved rebuttal without replaying every activity. The case is an index into evidence and reasoning.

### 1.3 Independent verification and validation: independence is structural

NASA distinguishes verification (“are we building the product right?”) from validation (“are we building the right product?”). Its IV&V practice defines independence along technical, managerial, and financial dimensions; technical independence means assessors independently evaluate development products, while managerial independence includes choosing what to analyze, which methods to use, and what issues to pursue ([NASA, “IV&V Overview”](https://www.nasa.gov/ivv-overview/)). NASA's current software-assurance guidance says IV&V uses rigorous analysis and testing to produce objective evidence for nominal and off-nominal operation; it seeks evidence that both supports and refutes correct operation ([NASA Software Engineering Handbook, “IV&V Surveillance,” §3](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVD/pages/140640402/8.6%2B-%2BIV%2BV%2BSurveillance)).

For an agent harness, organizational and financial independence are usually unavailable or disproportionate. The transferable minimum is **technical and selection independence**: the claimant does not author the final verification command, choose only favorable evidence, parse its own prose as evidence, or decide whether an open contradiction is material.

### 1.4 Swiss cheese: barriers fail, and shared holes matter

Reason's Swiss-cheese model represents multiple defensive layers between a hazard and an incident. Each layer has weaknesses from active failures or latent conditions; a loss occurs when weaknesses align across layers. UK government guidance emphasizes looking beyond the last individual action to organizational, procedural, equipment, and human layers ([GOV.UK, “Leading for safety,” §2.5](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/leading-for-safety/2-decision-making-and-risk-management)).

The important borrow is not “run the same test twice.” It is **defence diversity plus common-cause analysis**. UK HSE guidance warns that redundant non-diverse processors are vulnerable to common-mode failure and calls for independence from shared utilities, management, and protective systems plus diverse technology where necessary ([HSE, “Control systems,” Independence and common-mode sections](https://www.hse.gov.uk/comah/sragtech/techmeascontsyst.htm)). Two agents reading the same stale file through the same broken detector are one barrier with two labels.

Swiss cheese is only a heuristic. A historical review notes both its systemic foundations and criticism that it can be overly general and simplistic ([Larouzee & Le Coze, “Good and bad reasons: The Swiss cheese model and its critics,” *Safety Science* 126 (2020)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925753520300576)). It does not enumerate verification obligations and should not be used to calculate confidence.

### 1.5 Normal accidents: a warning against making verification its own hazard

Perrow's normal-accident theory argues that unexpected interactions become especially dangerous when systems combine interactive complexity with tight coupling. NASA summaries describe the theory in those terms and connect it to unpredictable cascades ([NASA NTRS, “A Rise in Normal Accidents”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140010760/downloads/20140010760.pdf)).

For this unit, normal-accident theory contributes a negative design rule: do not add a tightly coupled verifier daemon, durable workflow engine, global evidence graph, or opaque scoring cascade whose failure can block or falsely bless all work. Verification should be local to the claim, degrade explicitly to `UNVERIFIED`, and be bypassable only through a visible risk acceptance—not silently inferred success.

## 2. Structural correspondence—and its limits

| Systems-safety element | Agent-verification analogue | Transfer quality | Important disanalogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable loss | Owner acts on a materially false completion/fact claim; destructive or costly action follows | Strong | Many agent mistakes are cheap and reversible, so universal gating would be wasteful. |
| Hazard | Harness exposes a consequential claim as supported when required evidence is absent, stale, scoped wrongly, or contradicted | Strong | “Truth” is open-ended; a hazard must be narrowed to operationally checkable claim classes. |
| Controller | Agent plus harness release gate | Strong | The language model is stochastic and does not have a stable, fully inspectable control algorithm. |
| Control action | Publish `SUPPORTED`, publish `CONTRADICTED`, withhold for `UNVERIFIED`, request another check | Strong | Publication is informational, not a physical actuator; consequences depend on the owner acting. |
| Feedback/process model | Tool receipts, filesystem state, test output, external API response, freshness/scope metadata | Strong | Digital evidence can be forged, cached, permission-filtered, or semantically incomplete. |
| Safety constraint | Machine-enforced release rule for a claim class | Strong for bounded claims | No finite constraint set covers arbitrary natural-language semantics. |
| Safety case | Per-claim assurance envelope linking proposition, evidence, inference, assumptions, and defeaters | Strong | Industrial cases may take months; an interactive harness needs seconds and aggressive triage. |
| Independent protection layer | Mechanically diverse evidence source or verifier selected outside claimant control | Moderate-to-strong | All software may still share OS, credentials, repository state, model family, or poisoned source. |
| Leading indicator | Rising stale-evidence rate, repeated bypasses, detector-control failures, unsupported-claim rate | Moderate | These indicate verification-process health, not whether any individual claim is true. |
| Normal accident | Verifier interactions create delays, loops, or false release through coupling | Moderate caution | Harness scale and consequence are usually far below nuclear/aviation systems; inevitability claims do not transfer. |

The correspondence is strongest where both fields face **untrusted inference from partial observations under hidden interactions**. It is weakest where safety engineering assumes a bounded engineered system, stable hazards, regulated evidence, and costly independent assessors. An agent handles novel, ambiguous, open-world tasks; there is no complete hazard set and often no ground-truth oracle.

Therefore the design must make a smaller claim: it supplies **justified, falsifiable support for selected consequential propositions**. It must never label a claim simply `TRUE`. The permitted release labels are epistemic workflow states, not metaphysical verdicts.

## 3. STPA translation: hazards and unsafe release actions

### 3.1 Losses and hazards

Candidate losses:

- **L1:** the owner relies on a false “done/fixed/passed/deployed/sent” claim.
- **L2:** a correct result is withheld or contradicted long enough to cause material delay.
- **L3:** verification mutates state, leaks secrets, or expands authority beyond the task.
- **L4:** verification overhead drives routine bypass or trains the harness to rubber-stamp.

System hazards:

- **H1:** `SUPPORTED` is released while decisive evidence is absent or contradicted.
- **H2:** `SUPPORTED` is released for the wrong object, revision, environment, time, or interpretation.
- **H3:** two checks are treated as independent although they share a failure mode.
- **H4:** `CONTRADICTED` is released from a detector that has not demonstrated it can see a known-present control.
- **H5:** release occurs before an asynchronous action settles, or old support persists after relevant state changes.
- **H6:** the gate loops, blocks, or performs unauthorized mutation while seeking certainty.

### 3.2 Unsafe control-action matrix

| Gate control action | Not provided | Provided when unsafe | Wrong timing/order | Too long/stopped too soon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Release `SUPPORTED` | Correct work is hidden or delayed (L2/L4) | Unsupported, contradicted, wrong-scope, or common-mode evidence is blessed (L1) | Released before tool/action completion or before fresh state observation (L1) | Support remains valid after tree, environment, external state, or evidence changes (L1) |
| Release `CONTRADICTED` | A material false claim remains unchallenged (L1) | A blind detector's empty result is treated as disproof (L2) | Contradiction published before detector controls or alternate signal run (L2) | Old contradiction survives corrective change (L2/L4) |
| Release `UNVERIFIED` / withhold | Harness fabricates certainty instead of exposing a gap (L1) | Low-risk work is unnecessarily blocked (L2/L4) | Requested only after the final answer, when it cannot affect release (L1) | Indefinite verification loop consumes the task (L4/H6) |
| Request verification action | Needed evidence is never collected (L1) | Read-only request causes mutation, exceeds authority, or repeats a known-failing path (L3/L4) | Probe runs against wrong revision or before prerequisites settle (L1/L3) | Retry loop or sprawling evidence collection becomes a second workflow runtime (L4/H6) |

This matrix catches omissions that “ask the same agent to check its work” misses: no check at all, a hazardous check, a correct check at the wrong time, and a once-correct status held after its evidence expires.

## 4. Proposed mechanism — UNVERIFIED: Ephemeral Claim Assurance Envelope

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

**Premise and confidence — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: implement the prototype and line-count/latency benchmark in §9):** it is **likely (55-80%)**, because the envelope is a small schema, policy table, evidence resolver, and release formatter, that a useful prototype fits below roughly 200 lines excluding tests and existing tool adapters. If the existing bus cannot expose tool receipts and a pre-final hook, this classification is wrong and the design must be rejected or re-scoped; do not silently promote it to BUILD-LARGE.

### 4.1 Trigger only on consequential claims

The hook extracts candidate claims from the draft final response, but policy—not the claimant—decides which require a gate. Initial claim classes:

- state-changing completion: created, edited, deleted, sent, deployed, installed, configured;
- test/build status: passed, failed, fixed, no regressions;
- absence or completeness: no matches, nothing missing, all instances handled;
- externally changing facts used for a decision;
- high-stakes numerical or policy claims;
- explicit owner request to prove or verify.

Explanations, brainstorming, quoted user premises, preferences, and low-impact conversational claims pass without an envelope. A strict all-sentence gate would reproduce the safety-case burden at the wrong scale.

### 4.2 Envelope schema

Each gated claim becomes a small record:

```yaml
claim_id: C7
proposition: "Tests for package P pass on worktree digest D"
object_scope: {package: P, tree_digest: D, environment: E}
claim_class: test_status
risk: consequential
evidence:
  - ref: tool-receipt-42
    observation: "command X exited 0"
    provenance: {producer: shell, captured_by: harness, time: T}
    independence_class: execution
argument: "X is the policy-defined complete test command for P; exit 0 supports the scoped proposition"
assumptions:
  - "test discovery includes the relevant tests"
defeaters:
  - id: D1
    proposition: "X selected zero tests"
    status: closed
    closure_evidence: tool-receipt-42/test-count
verdict: SUPPORTED
expires_on: {tree_change: true, environment_change: true}
```

The envelope is ephemeral output metadata or a temporary per-turn artifact. It is not a vector database, knowledge graph, or durable memory system.

### 4.3 Evidence resolver: receipts, not prose

The resolver accepts typed evidence references from the harness/tool layer: exit status and full command identity; content digest and resolved path; structured test count; HTTP/API response with source and retrieval time; or an explicit human-supplied premise. The producing agent may point to evidence but cannot manufacture the receipt or mark it independent.

Evidence is rejected or downgraded when:

- it addresses a different path, commit, environment, account, or time window;
- it is only the agent's paraphrase of a result;
- output was truncated at the decisive portion;
- a command's exit code does not semantically establish the claim;
- a negative detector lacks a known-present control and a mechanically independent second signal;
- multiple items depend on the same detector, parser, cache, credential, source document, or model judgment.

This is the translation of both process-model integrity (STPA) and common-cause analysis (defence in depth).

### 4.4 Claim-class verifier recipes

Recipes are configuration, not free-form agent choice. Examples:

| Claim class | Primary evidence | Independent/counter signal | Default defeaters |
|---|---|---|---|
| File edit complete | digest/readback of resolved target | targeted semantic query or parser/test | wrong path; unsaved buffer; generated file differs; unrelated diff |
| Tests pass | fresh process receipt, exact command, exit 0, nonzero discovered count | test-report parser or targeted behavior probe | zero tests; cached result; wrong project; skipped test; timeout hidden |
| Bug fixed | reproducer fails before and passes after, or a regression test | independent behavior observation | test asserts wrong behavior; patch not loaded; only symptom moved |
| No matches / absent | detector finds a seeded known-present control, then no target | different mechanism or data field over same declared scope | ignored paths; permissions; truncation; case/encoding; stale index |
| External action done | provider-generated immutable ID and read-after-write | listing/get via provider API | queued not delivered; wrong account/channel; eventual consistency |
| Current external fact | authoritative source and retrieval timestamp | independent authoritative source or primary data | stale page; copied source; regional/version mismatch |

“Independent” is a provenance property, not an agent count. A second LLM interpreting the same output is review, not a second mechanical signal.

### 4.5 Mandatory defeater pass

For each envelope, a deterministic checklist generates candidate defeaters: wrong object/scope; stale evidence; detector blindness; observation does not entail proposition; common-mode evidence; hidden skip/cache; incomplete permissions; state changed after observation; unresolved contradictory evidence; and the owner asked a different question. The agent may add domain-specific defeaters, but cannot delete required ones. Each material defeater is `open`, `closed` with evidence, or `accepted-risk` with an explicit owner-visible rationale.

There is no numeric confidence aggregation. A NASA-funded review found little strong empirical support for proposed quantitative confidence techniques and showed that many can produce implausible results; it warns against the appearance of computational rigor without trustworthy validation ([Graydon & Holloway, *Safety Science* 92 (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925753516302429)). The three-state verdict keeps residual doubt visible.

### 4.6 Release behavior and bounded stopping rule

- `SUPPORTED`: required evidence exists, scope/freshness match, mandatory defeaters are closed, and any required independent signal is genuinely diverse.
- `CONTRADICTED`: direct, valid counterevidence defeats the proposition.
- `UNVERIFIED`: neither condition is met, evidence is unavailable, a detector control fails, or the bounded verification budget expires.

The gate may run one policy recipe and one counter-signal round. After that it reports `UNVERIFIED` plus the exact settling experiment. It must not recurse into an autonomous repair-and-reverify loop. Material state change invalidates dependent envelopes.

## 5. What this would catch that the current operating pattern misses

The commissioning brief reports seven recent wrong conclusions from single unvalidated probes and mandates a known-present control plus two mechanically independent signals for absence/broken/done claims. I treat that owner-supplied observation as pinned ground truth, not as a locally reverified finding; local grounding is explicitly outside X131's lens.

**Prospective catch claim — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: replay those seven cases plus seeded variants through the prototype):** it is **very likely (80-90%)**, because the proposed absence recipe directly blocks a negative verdict without a positive detector control and a diverse second signal, that the envelope would withhold those same classes of conclusion as `UNVERIFIED`. It is **likely (55-80%)**, because exact receipts and scope checks cover but do not semantically understand every tool, that it would also catch:

1. **Phantom completion:** an agent says a write/send/deploy happened although there is no provider or filesystem receipt.
2. **Wrong-target success:** a test passes or file exists, but on a different tree, package, account, channel, environment, or version.
3. **Detector blindness:** “none found” follows from missing permissions, ignored files, broken hooks, truncation, or a query that cannot find a known-present control.
4. **Success-code fallacy:** exit 0 is cited although zero tests ran, a subcommand skipped work, or the command does not entail the behavioral claim.
5. **Stale support:** evidence predates a relevant edit, asynchronous completion, environment change, or external update.
6. **Correlated corroboration:** two agents or tools agree because both consume the same stale index, parser, source, or model-generated premise.
7. **Unexercised fix:** compilation or a broad test suite passes without reproducing the reported failure or testing the changed behavior.
8. **Scope laundering:** a narrow observation (“these three files”) is restated as a universal claim (“all files”).
9. **Contradiction burial:** favorable evidence is surfaced while a failing targeted check remains unresolved.
10. **Verification retry loops:** repeated failing probes are converted into an explicit `UNVERIFIED` result after the fixed budget.

The envelope will not catch a false but well-supported claim when all evidence channels share the same hidden defect, when the claim is too vague to operationalize, when the environment lies consistently, or when the verifier recipe encodes the wrong requirement.

## 6. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 6.1 Disanalogies that could make the borrow fail

1. **Open world versus bounded plant.** Industrial analysis starts with a system boundary and anticipated losses. General agents receive novel requests and browse a changing world. No static STPA catalog is complete. Mitigation: gate a small, versioned set of claim classes and expose out-of-model cases as `UNVERIFIED`.

2. **Evidence is often semantic.** A pressure sensor has a defined quantity; “the design is good” does not. An assurance envelope may merely formalize a bad inference. Mitigation: permit `SUPPORTED` only for a bounded proposition; keep broader judgments explicitly qualitative.

3. **The claimant and verifier share infrastructure.** A hook, shell, filesystem, credentials, and even model family can create common-mode failure. Mechanical independence is weaker than NASA's organizational IV&V. The design must disclose shared dependencies rather than call them independent.

4. **Consequences and budgets differ.** Aviation-grade assurance on every sentence would make an interactive assistant unusable. Risk triage is not optional.

5. **Safety cases can become advocacy documents.** A producer can build a persuasive case around cherry-picked evidence. Mandatory defeaters and policy-selected recipes reduce but do not eliminate this problem. MOD's instruction to try to refute the case is essential, not decorative.

6. **Verification can mutate the system.** Some “checks” create files, refresh dependencies, send requests, or change caches. Read-only must be an explicit recipe property; otherwise L3 is introduced by the safety mechanism.

7. **No empirical guarantee transfers from safety practice.** Use in regulated industries is evidence of practicality, not a measured reduction in agent hallucination. The harness proposal remains UNVERIFIED until §9.

### 6.2 Alternatives rejected

- **Second-agent vote:** rejected as the core mechanism. Agreement is not evidence, and shared model/source/tool failures are common-mode. A separate agent can search for defeaters, but only tool-grounded results enter the envelope.
- **Majority voting across N agents:** rejected. It increases cost and can amplify correlated error without defining claim scope or evidence adequacy.
- **Numeric truth/confidence score:** rejected. It compresses heterogeneous evidence, assumptions, and residual doubts into false precision; empirical support for assurance-confidence calculations is weak.
- **Full persistent GSN graph:** rejected. It adds authoring burden, stale nodes, lifecycle management, and coupling. Per-turn envelopes are sufficient for the target.
- **Global event-sourced verification daemon:** rejected under normal-accident and anti-overfit reasoning. It is a second runtime and a universal failure point.
- **Vector database/knowledge graph memory:** rejected; it does not establish provenance or truth and is a previously rejected component under the unit's anti-overfit gate.
- **Trained critic/guardrail framework:** rejected; a critic's prose is not mechanically independent evidence and would introduce a new model lifecycle.
- **Universal formal proof:** rejected. Most agent tasks and external claims lack formal specifications and trusted semantics.
- **Human reads everything:** rejected by the target. Human escalation remains appropriate only for accepted residual risk or a consequential claim with no mechanical oracle.

## 7. Anti-overfit and minimal-architecture gate

The proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate by construction:

- it rides the existing hook/tool/skill/MCP bus;
- it adds a bounded schema and verifier-policy function, not an orchestrator;
- evidence remains in existing receipts and artifacts, referenced by ID/digest;
- it is ephemeral by default and creates no memory service;
- it uses deterministic claim-class rules before model judgment;
- it degrades to a visible `UNVERIFIED` state;
- it has one verification/counter-signal round, preventing a durable-execution loop;
- it introduces none of the rejected vector/KG memory, trained critic, SaaS observability, guardrail framework, or durable workflow components.

**Minimality claim — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: compare the envelope against a no-schema two-probe hook and a full assurance graph in §9):** it is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the small number of required states and the cost barriers reported by practitioners, that the envelope is near the smallest useful transfer. It may prove that a still smaller typed-receipt plus defeater checklist performs equivalently; if so, remove the explicit argument field.

## 8. Rollout design

### Phase A: shadow mode

Generate envelopes for only `done`, `passed`, `fixed`, `absent`, and external-action claims. Do not block release. Log whether the baseline answer would have asserted support, which evidence the gate found, open defeaters, tool calls, and elapsed time.

### Phase B: hard gate on two narrow classes

Gate absence/completeness and external-action completion, because both have crisp controls and high false-acceptance consequences. All other classes remain shadowed.

### Phase C: risk-tier expansion

Add test status and bug-fixed claims only if benchmarked false acceptance improves without unacceptable false abstention. Keep low-risk prose ungated.

**Rollout expectation — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: shadow-mode measurements over at least 200 consequential claims, followed by the preregistered benchmark):** it is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the gate's explicit `UNVERIFIED` fallback and narrow triggers, that shadow mode can measure usefulness without changing task outcomes. It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because no local hook timing or receipt interface was inspected in this ideation unit, that the exact existing bus exposes enough structured metadata without adapter changes.

## 9. Experiment that settles the proposal

This is the required verification plan for every UNVERIFIED recommendation above.

### 9.1 Benchmark construction

Create at least 240 claim episodes, split before implementation:

- 80 replayed real episodes, including the seven pinned single-probe failures where legally and technically reproducible;
- 120 seeded episodes across the ten catch classes in §5;
- 40 clean controls where the claim is genuinely supported, to measure needless abstention;
- at least 30% held-out tasks from domains not used to author recipes;
- paired variants where two apparent checks share a hidden source versus genuinely diverse sources.

Each episode has an oracle assembled outside the claimant path: immutable fixture state, provider readback, or adjudication from raw receipts by two reviewers blind to system condition. Reviewer agreement alone is not the oracle when a mechanical fixture is available.

### 9.2 Conditions

Randomize episodes across:

- **B0:** baseline agent self-verification;
- **B1:** baseline plus a generic “double-check” prompt;
- **B2:** two-agent reviewer vote using the same tools;
- **T1:** typed receipts plus claim-class recipe only;
- **T2:** full Claim Assurance Envelope with defeaters and independence classification.

Use the same claimant model, temperature policy, task budget, and underlying environment. Predeclare which evidence sources share a dependency.

### 9.3 Primary and secondary measures

Primary: false-supported rate on consequential false claims. Secondary: false-`UNVERIFIED`/false-contradicted rate on true claims; false independence classification; median and p95 latency; extra tool calls/tokens; mutation incidents caused by checking; retry-loop incidence; and coverage by claim class.

Required detector validation: every absence probe must first find a seeded known-present control, and the benchmark harness must demonstrate that its scorer detects at least one known false support and one known true support before it is trusted.

### 9.4 Decision rule

The following thresholds are design choices, not field-established constants:

- proceed from shadow mode only if T2 reduces false support by at least 50% relative to B0 and its 95% bootstrap confidence interval excludes zero improvement;
- require zero authorization-expanding mutations attributable to verification;
- require false abstention below 15% for the two initial hard-gated classes;
- require p95 added latency below 20% of task time or 10 seconds, whichever is smaller, for local checks;
- reject the explicit assurance-case layer if T1 is statistically and practically equivalent to T2;
- reject the whole proposal if B1 performs equivalently at materially lower cost.

These thresholds make the radical conclusion falsifiable: “systems-safety language sounds rigorous” is not sufficient evidence to retain any component.

## 10. Failure modes and operational constraints

| Failure mode of the verifier | Required control |
|---|---|
| Claim extraction misses a consequential assertion | deterministic lexical/action triggers plus post-release sampling; do not claim complete extraction |
| Agent games wording to avoid a trigger | classify semantic action/status phrases and audit unsupported outcomes, but do not broaden to all prose without evidence |
| Evidence receipt is forged by a tool adapter | harness signs/binds receipt to invocation ID and raw output digest where available |
| Two signals share a hidden dependency | record source, detector, parser, cache, credential, environment, and model provenance; treat unknown dependence as non-independent |
| Negative detector is blind | known-present positive control before absence conclusion |
| Correct evidence, wrong inference | claim-class argument template plus mandatory “observation does not entail proposition” defeater |
| State changes after checking | invalidation on relevant content/environment revision and explicit expiry for external facts |
| Tool output truncation hides failure | decisive fields must be structured or raw evidence marked incomplete |
| Verification causes side effects | recipes declare read-only status; mutation-capable probes require existing authority and cannot be silently selected |
| Verification loops | one recipe plus one counter-signal round, then `UNVERIFIED` |
| Gate unavailable | fail open only for low-risk claims with explicit “verification unavailable”; fail closed to `UNVERIFIED` for consequential claims |
| Case becomes paperwork | narrow triggers, terse rendered summary, full envelope collapsible, periodic deletion of low-yield recipes |

## 13. Bottom line

The successful borrow is not the metaphor of cheese slices and not a miniature certification bureaucracy. It is a **hazard-derived, independently evidenced, actively defeasible claim gate**. STPA tells the harness where an approval decision can become unsafe; CAE makes the claim/evidence/inference boundary inspectable; IV&V and defence diversity prevent self-certification and counterfeit independence; normal-accident theory keeps the mechanism local, explicit, and small.

Build it only as the shadow-mode BUILD-SMALL experiment described above. If typed receipts plus a simple two-signal policy match the full envelope, delete the argument machinery. If a generic prompt matches both, delete the feature. If neither can distinguish correlated checks or avoid verification-induced mutation, the analogy has failed in practice and should not be forced.
