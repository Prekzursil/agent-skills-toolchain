# K06 — PEER-REPRESENTATION: should the harness model the owner as an agent with goals?

**Unit:** K06, partner-dimension / user-model depth / grows-with-you  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** research and design only. The subject was read-only. This unit wrote only this artifact.  
**Question ruled:** Is the Honcho-style peer-representation idea real leverage for this owner, or over-engineering?

## Executive ruling

**The useful core is real; the literal implementation is over-engineering for this harness.** A thin, inspectable model of the owner's **active goals, commitments, current role, delegation boundary, unresolved threads, and evidenced working preferences** is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce goal drift and owner repetition. Evidence: the corpus contains individually inspectable corrections for stopping early, missing previously requested work, ignoring parallel threads, and pushing manual work back onto the owner (`corpus.json:11339`, `:11447-11455`, `:11837`, `:12251`, `:12371-12384`); shared-goal and shared-mental-model research reports coordination benefits; and the harness already has the storage and event buses needed to represent these states.

By contrast, deploying Honcho or building a continuously reasoning “psychological twin” of the owner is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat a typed goal/commitment model here. Evidence: Honcho is a separate API/server plus background derivation pipeline and internal vector collections ([Honcho architecture](https://honcho.dev/docs/v3/documentation/core-concepts/architecture)); that duplicates the installed event-ledger/basic-memory split (`claude/AGENTS.md:28-34,85-91`); and Honcho's own benchmark article says present memory benchmarks do **not** test “What do they think? What would surprise them? Who are they?” and identifies ambiguity in synthetic questions ([Honcho benchmark discussion](https://plasticlabs.ai/blog/research/Benchmarking-Honcho)). Its memory-retrieval scores are evidence for memory retrieval, not for the correctness of abductive claims about this owner.

The ruling is therefore:

> **Adopt peer-representation as a product concept, but implement it as a small evidence-bearing “partner model” on the existing ledger/basic-memory/open-loops bus. Do not install Honcho, add a user-model daemon, add another vector database, or let inferred psychology become authority.**

This is a **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** proposal, not a new agent runtime. It adds no hook. It folds into existing `note.py`, `basic-memory`, `open-loops`, `goal-reinject`, and `calibrated-silence` paths. Every inference remains a hypothesis until an **external** signal corroborates it. The same-context model is never allowed to certify its own owner inference or its own completion.

## Evidence discipline and local grounding

### What the corpus can and cannot establish

The corpus is a high-coverage distillation: 11,633 files seen, zero failed, 1,564,084 lines processed, one bad line, and 32 oversized lines (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). Its tool/error counts are therefore usable within the limits stated in `_meta`.

The owner-correction miner is **not** a clean training set. It reports 8,441 owner messages and 2,268 candidates, but explicitly calls itself recall-biased and says an unfiltered smoke sample produced 41/41 false positives (`corpus.json:10371-10374`). Consequently, this unit does not infer “2,268 corrections” or learn a persona from that aggregate. It uses only readable samples whose wording itself carries the signal:

- A previously requested item had to be repeated: “I've already told you to do that, finish it” (`corpus.json:11338-11342`).
- A live result did not match the claimed outcome, and unresolved items remained: only one file copied instead of two, clipboard remained unresolved, and broader feature research was still wanted (`corpus.json:11446-11455`).
- The owner explicitly requested continuity without check-ins: “Don't stop,” complete Tier 1 through Tier 3, and “No check-ins between items” (`corpus.json:11836-11840`).
- The owner corrected premature stopping after asking for an autonomous loop (`corpus.json:12250-12254`), then later again asked why it stopped (`corpus.json:12358-12362`).
- The owner separated agent work from genuinely manual owner input: “you monitor everything yourself, and tell me when I need to do manual input,” followed by a request for live probing rather than log-copying (`corpus.json:12370-12385`).
- The owner also sometimes asks to stop or change direction; the aggregate includes “stop the ralph loop and tell me what has been done” (`corpus.json:10593-10595`). This is evidence against treating persistence as an immutable personality trait.

The corpus's `hooks` section is **NO DATA**, not a zero: its known-positive detector failed (`corpus.json:13260-13263`). No finding below uses that section to claim a hook is absent, quiet, or effective.

### Existing mechanisms that must be extended, not duplicated

- The event ledger already distinguishes goals, decisions, failures, and milestones; `note.py` records why objective hooks cannot see and supports superseding a failed premise (`claude/hooks/event-ledger/note.py:2-12,25-42`).
- The cross-tool contract already makes the ledger objective history and basic-memory canonical semantic memory (`claude/AGENTS.md:28-34,85-91`).
- `open-loops` already implements durable prospective-memory triggers, derives unresolved ledger failures and dangling goals, and is explicitly aimed at forgotten threads (`claude/hooks/open-loops/_common.py:2-26`). It already resurfaces only 1-3 relevant items and avoids duplicate recovery injection (`claude/hooks/open-loops/resurface.py:2-25,38-40,69-95`).
- `goal-reinject` already re-anchors every eight write-class operations and requests a fresh-context falsification check at a slower cadence (`claude/hooks/goal-reinject/reinject.py:2-13,29-32,77-117`).
- `calibrated-silence` already treats proactive interjection as a signal-detection decision and says its learning signal is the owner's next-turn behavior, not model self-judgment (`claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:2-25,28-53`). Its reaction logger classifies actual next-turn behavior and actual tool use (`claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/log_reaction.py:2-23,118-151`).
- The local dispatcher already folds multiple checks behind one event invocation (`claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:1-4`) and already includes open-loop resurfacing on SessionStart (`:53-60`). This design adds **zero** hooks and stays under the ~6-hook ceiling.
- The live-defect input proves why self-certified beliefs are prohibited: six wrong conclusions came from single unvalidated probes, followed by a seventh detector failure caught with a known-positive control (`inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). A fabricated orchestrator constraint then propagated to roughly 15 prompts (`:151-160`).

## Reference architecture: a bounded partner model, not a synthetic person

Use one typed semantic note named `partner/owner-operating-model` in canonical basic-memory and project-local goal/commitment events in the existing event ledger. A small `partner.py` adapter should read/write these existing stores; it must not create a second memory database.

Proposed record shape:

```yaml
id: pm-...
kind: goal | commitment | delegation | interaction_contract | hypothesis
scope: global | project:<project-id> | session:<session-id>
statement: "..."
status: proposed | active | blocked | satisfied | superseded | rejected
source:
  type: explicit_owner | observed_action | tool_result | second_context
  pointer: transcript-id:timestamp | ledger-seq | artifact-path
confidence_band: almost certain | very likely | likely | roughly even | unlikely | very unlikely | almost certainly not
corroboration: [independent-source-pointer, ...]
valid_from: timestamp
review_after: timestamp | null
supersedes: [pm-id, ...]
action_policy: suggest | act_reversible | confirm_before_external_effect
```

Operational authority is deliberately narrow:

1. An **explicit current owner instruction** may create an active goal/commitment immediately.
2. A single behavioral observation creates only a `hypothesis`; it cannot authorize action.
3. A hypothesis becomes action-bearing only after a mechanically independent signal: a second distinct owner turn, an execution outcome, a fresh-context reviewer grounded in source pointers, or owner confirmation.
4. A later explicit instruction supersedes older derived state. Contradictions are preserved as versioned alternatives rather than silently “reconciled” by the same model.
5. Irreversible, external, costly, credentialed, or scope-expanding actions always remain confirm-first, regardless of the partner model.

This architecture passes the anti-overfit gate: it rides existing stores and hook events; it does not add a second orchestration runtime, vector DB/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## Findings (inferred-surprise first)

### F1 — Replace “persona” with a source-addressable goal-and-commitment model

**Improvement:** Give the harness a model of what the owner is trying to accomplish and what the team has committed to, while refusing to model a free-form personality.

**What it is:** A typed, temporal set of goals, subgoals, commitments, roles, and constraints. It is closer to a shared team task model than a preference list: “make the golden journey green,” “clipboard upgrade is still in scope,” “agent monitors; owner supplies only irreducibly manual input.” Each item has provenance, scope, status, confidence, expiry/review time, and supersession.

**Why-inferred:** The owner does not merely correct style; they correct dropped objectives and role allocation. The repeated “already told you,” “you ignored … too,” “why did you stop,” and “you monitor everything yourself” messages (`corpus.json:11339`, `:11453`, `:12251`, `:12377-12383`) are direct evidence that a preference list is too shallow. Conversely, “stop the loop” (`:10593-10595`) shows that an inferred persistence trait must never override current intent.

**How-to-build:** Extend `note.py` with optional `--partner-kind`, `--scope`, `--source`, `--confidence-band`, `--review-after`, and `--supersedes-partner` fields, or add a sibling `partner.py` adapter under ~150 lines. Store current project goals/commitments as richer ledger events and curate only stable cross-project operating agreements into one basic-memory note. Inject at task start/compaction via the existing replay lane; include only the current goal, active commitments, and explicit delegation boundary under a fixed character budget. Do not inject biography, inferred mood, or an unbounded profile.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Build a held-out replay of at least 50 manually verified multi-thread owner turns. Compare baseline versus partner-model routing on (a) thread-drop rate, (b) explicit owner repetition rate, and (c) incorrect carry-over rate. A separate fresh-context evaluator receives the original owner turn, subsequent correction, and system output in randomized order; the owner adjudicates a stratified 10-case sample. The proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce dropped-scope errors if the replay shows a material reduction without increased false carry-over; evidence is the direct correction samples plus shared-mental-model literature. It is rejected if false carry-over rises or the owner sample prefers baseline.

**SOTA citation:** Shared displays and explicit information about teammates' tasks and goals improved team performance in an empirical study ([Bolstad & Endsley, 1999](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/154193129904300318)); human-AI shared-mental-model research treats task, team member, and role knowledge as coordination primitives ([Scheutz et al. framework](https://hrilab.tufts.edu/publications/scheutzetal17smm/)). Honcho's representation concept is broader—deductive, inductive, and abductive conclusions over a peer ([Honcho representations](https://honcho.dev/docs/v3/documentation/core-concepts/representation))—but that breadth is not required here.

**Value x effort:** **transformative x medium**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **confirm-first** for enabling action-bearing inference; schema/prototype and offline replay are **just-do-it**.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### F2 — Model commitments as bilateral contracts with closure evidence, not remembered wishes

**Improvement:** Turn “never drop a thread” into an explicit lifecycle: proposed -> accepted -> active -> blocked/satisfied/superseded, with a machine-checkable closure witness.

**What it is:** A commitment is not “the owner likes X.” It is a relation among an owner goal, an agent promise, a scope, and a definition of done. A thread may close only against an external witness such as a passing command, artifact existence plus content validation, remote status, or explicit owner cancellation.

**Why-inferred:** The clearest recent correction says the system copied one file instead of two and left clipboard and research requests unresolved (`corpus.json:11435-11455`). Earlier, the owner had to say “finish it” after already asking (`:11339`). These are failures of commitment accounting, not missing biographical knowledge.

**How-to-build:** Extend existing open-loop records (`_common.py:8-16,178-198`) with `goal_id`, `commitment_id`, `acceptance_probe`, `owner_requested`, `status`, and `closure_evidence`. `add.py --close` must require either `--evidence <ledger-seq|artifact|command-result>` or `--owner-cancelled`; legacy reminders remain closable but are labeled unverified. Fold summary into the existing SessionStart `open-loops/resurface` check, replacing none and adding no hook. At stop/transition, show at most three items: newly blocked, due now, or apparently satisfied but awaiting an external witness.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Run recorded golden journeys in which one requested item is intentionally omitted, one is completed with a real passing probe, and one is explicitly cancelled by the owner fixture. The ledger must keep the omitted item active, close the proven item, and supersede the cancelled item. A fresh process—not the producing agent—reads the files and executes each acceptance probe. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that this is mechanically testable because the existing open-loop store and command already expose creation/closure state (`add.py:66-105,120-195`); whether it reduces owner stress in live use is **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is a four-week A/B measurement of repeated-thread corrections and owner-rated interruption burden**.

**SOTA citation:** Microsoft’s human-AI interaction guidelines say to remember recent interactions, learn from behavior, update cautiously, support efficient correction, and convey consequences ([Microsoft HAX guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)). Shared-mental-model work specifically emphasizes knowledge of tasks, goals, team members, and roles ([Scheutz et al.](https://hrilab.tufts.edu/publications/scheutzetal17smm/)).

**Value x effort:** **transformative x low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for recording explicit commitments and testing closure; **confirm-first** for interpreting silence as cancellation (silence never cancels).  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### F3 — Learn the owner's delegation boundary as a contextual policy, not a global autonomy preference

**Improvement:** Make the harness know when to act, when to ask, and when to request only irreducibly manual input—per action class and project state.

**What it is:** A delegation matrix keyed by action risk and reversibility: local/read-only, reversible workspace mutation, external publication, destructive/irreversible, credentialed, costly, and physically/manual. It records explicit grants and denials plus expiry. It does not infer “this owner likes autonomy” and apply that globally.

**Why-inferred:** In one inspectable sequence, the owner says the agent should monitor everything and only request manual input when necessary, then asks for live probing rather than copying logs (`corpus.json:12377-12383`). Elsewhere the owner explicitly asks for no check-ins during a scoped tiered run (`:11837`). But the corpus also contains explicit stopping and redirection (`:10593-10595`), so autonomy is contextual.

**How-to-build:** Add `delegation` partner records with `action_class`, `scope`, `grant`, `expires`, and explicit source. Resolve each planned action by precedence: current prompt > security/permission policy > explicit scoped delegation > default. For reversible in-scope work, act and report. For irreversible/external/scope-expanding work, confirm regardless of learned history. AskUserQuestion remains the native confirmation surface; Workflow remains the native long-horizon surface. No new hook is needed.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Replay a matrix of owner-approved, owner-denied, and unseen action types. The pass condition is zero silent external/destructive actions, fewer redundant questions on explicitly delegated reversible work, and correct questions on unseen high-impact actions. Use actual tool authorization/result records as the action ground truth and an owner-reviewed confusion matrix. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce prompt fatigue without reducing control; evidence is the explicit corpus delegation messages and proactive-agent research favoring confirmation under strong assumptions. The wide band reflects missing live counterfactual data.

**SOTA citation:** A 40-participant proactive-assistant study found the level combining strong assumptions with user confirmation most appropriate ([Du et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09135)). Microsoft recommends scoping services when uncertain and providing global controls ([HAX guideline summary](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=564561)).

**Value x effort:** **high x low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for explicit, reversible, in-scope classes; **confirm-first** for external, destructive, costly, credentialed, or inferred grants.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** for the matrix plus existing native AskUserQuestion/Workflow behavior.

### F4 — Keep competing owner hypotheses; never let one model “reconcile” the person

**Improvement:** Make uncertainty and contradiction first-class so a stale or convenient story about the owner cannot become an invisible command.

**What it is:** When evidence conflicts, store both claims with temporal scope and provenance. Example: “continue autonomously until tier completion” and “stop the loop and report” are not personality contradictions; they are different instructions at different times. No same-context LLM may collapse them into “the owner always wants persistence.”

**Why-inferred:** The corpus contains both no-check-in persistence (`corpus.json:11837`) and a stop request (`:10593-10595`). The known-defect input shows a fabricated constraint propagated into ~15 prompts because downstream agents treated orchestrator text as ground truth (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`). A rich peer narrative would magnify precisely this risk unless its claims are typed and challengeable.

**How-to-build:** Require each non-explicit partner claim to carry source pointers, scope, confidence band, and `status: proposed`. Promotion requires two mechanically independent signals. Contradictions create `conflicts_with` edges and a `review_after`, not a synthesized conclusion. Before a claim can steer a consequential action, run the existing fresh-context verification route with only the claim and its raw evidence pointers; the verifier returns supported/unsupported/ambiguous. Ambiguous remains non-authoritative. Owner correction immediately supersedes the claim.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Seed a test history with temporally scoped conflicting instructions and a fabricated orchestrator claim. A separate fresh-context process must identify the current explicit instruction, preserve the older record, and reject the fabricated claim when its cited path/probe is absent. Known-positive controls must accompany absence probes, following the confirmed detector failure (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). It is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent globalized false preferences because authority is structurally denied to one-signal hypotheses; evidence is the local fabricated-constraint incident and formal preference-inference limits.

**SOTA citation:** Inverse-reinforcement-learning research shows that inferring rewards from behavior is vulnerable to misspecified models ([Skalse & Abate, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03201)), and a no-free-lunch result shows behavior alone cannot uniquely identify preferences without normative assumptions ([Armstrong & Mindermann, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05812)). Long-term memory benchmarks explicitly test knowledge updates and abstention because current state is not simple accumulation ([LongMemEval repository](https://github.com/xiaowu0162/longmemeval)).

**Value x effort:** **high x medium**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** to retain and externally verify hypotheses; **confirm-first** when ambiguity would change scope or external effects.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### F5 — Give the owner a “why I acted” receipt only at consequential boundaries

**Improvement:** Make personalization legible and correctable without adding a constant stream of explanations.

**What it is:** A one-line receipt attached only to a consequential autonomous decision: `Acted because: explicit delegation pm-17 + active goal g-4; evidence: transcript …; undo: …`. Routine tool calls remain quiet. The owner can say “not a standing rule,” which supersedes the relevant model item.

**Why-inferred:** Prompt fatigue is a hard constraint, while the owner also wants the agent to be a lower-effort companion (`corpus.json:12377-12383`). The right response is selective legibility, not more questions or verbose personalization on every turn. The existing calibrated-silence gate already learns from external next-turn reactions (`calibrated_silence.py:2-53`), so the receipt can be gated and measured without a new interrupting mechanism.

**How-to-build:** Add a formatter to the partner adapter and pass receipts through existing completion/commentary text, not a new hook. Emit only for (a) first use of a learned delegation in a scope, (b) changed action because of a stored owner model, or (c) model conflict resolved by explicit recent instruction. Cap at one line and include a stable model item ID and undo/correction syntax. Log owner response with the existing reaction logger; do not ask for ratings each time.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Randomize qualifying decisions between no receipt and one-line receipt for four weeks. Measure correction latency, owner reversals, ignored/annoyed next turns, and task time. The owner reviews a small end-of-week batch rather than being interrupted per event. This is **likely (55-80%)** to improve trust calibration if receipts are rare; evidence is HAX guidance on explaining behavior and efficient correction, plus the existing external reaction signal. It becomes a regression if annoyance or task time increases.

**SOTA citation:** Microsoft recommends making clear why the system acted, supporting efficient correction, updating cautiously, and conveying how user actions change future behavior ([HAX guideline summary](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=564561)). Research on human-AI performance emphasizes knowing the AI's error boundary rather than accuracy alone ([Bansal et al., 2019](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/HCOMP/article/view/5285)).

**Value x effort:** **medium x low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for an offline experiment; **confirm-first** before live randomized rollout.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### F6 — Turn proactive help into queued proposals with attention-aware release

**Improvement:** Preserve surprise and anticipation while lowering interruption cost: infer opportunities continuously, surface them only at task boundaries or when externally evidenced urgency crosses a threshold.

**What it is:** A three-level proactivity policy: `silent-capture` (record a potential opportunity), `boundary-suggest` (include in the next natural status/transition), and `interrupt` (only for imminent loss, safety, or a blocking deadline). This is not a new proactive agent; it is a release policy for existing open-loop and calibrated-silence records.

**Why-inferred:** The owner explicitly asks for opportunities and SOTA expansion, and corrected the agent for ignoring requested feature research (`corpus.json:11447-11455`). At the same time, no-check-in instructions (`:11837`) and the hard prompt-fatigue constraint mean useful anticipation cannot imply constant prompting.

**How-to-build:** Add `release: silent|boundary|interrupt`, `urgency_source`, and `deadline` to existing open-loop records. Fold selection into `open-loops/resurface` and `calibrated-silence`; add no hook. An interrupt requires an external state change (failed execution, approaching explicit deadline, lost background task, destructive-risk precondition), never the same model's feeling that an idea is urgent. Batch boundary suggestions to at most three, deduplicate by goal/commitment ID, and log whether the owner acted, ignored, or dismissed via the existing reaction logger.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Replay recorded sessions with externally timestamped task transitions and injected opportunity records. Compare interruption count, accepted-suggestion rate, owner dismissal rate, and dropped-thread rate. In live use, release only in shadow mode for two weeks, then owner-confirm a bounded trial. It is **likely (55-80%)** that boundary batching beats immediate prompting for this owner; evidence is the explicit “no check-ins” sample, HAX timing guidance, and existing next-turn reaction telemetry. The exact threshold is **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is the shadow replay plus two-week owner-confirmed trial**.

**SOTA citation:** HAX says to time services based on context and respect user goals and attention ([Microsoft HAX article](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)). A proactive-assistant experiment found strong-assumption-plus-confirmation preferable ([Du et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09135)); runtime-permission research evaluates contextual consent for proactive assistants rather than blanket surveillance permission ([Malkin et al., SOUPS 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2022/presentation/malkin)).

**Value x effort:** **high x low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for silent capture/shadow measurement; **confirm-first** for enabling new live interruptions.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**, folded into existing open-loops and calibrated-silence checks.

### F7 — Separate user-model data by scope and sensitivity; ban psychological and sensitive-attribute inference

**Improvement:** Make the partner model useful without creating an intimate, cross-project dossier that can leak, manipulate, or mislead.

**What it is:** Scope every item to session, project, or global; default inferred operational hypotheses to project scope; promote globally only through explicit owner confirmation. Do not infer health, politics, finances, protected traits, emotional state, or personality. Store the minimum operational claim, not the underlying private fact: e.g. “prefer zero recurring subscription for this purchase” rather than “budget-conscious person.”

**Why-inferred:** Honcho explicitly advertises abductive and inductive conclusions such as inferring budget consciousness from house-saving and subscription comments ([Honcho representations](https://honcho.dev/docs/v3/documentation/core-concepts/representation)). That is precisely the feature whose incremental value is unproven here and whose privacy/authority cost is largest. The local harness already declares fetched/MCP content untrusted (`claude/AGENTS.md:99-102`), and its canonical memory is cross-provider (`:85-91`), increasing the importance of strict admission.

**How-to-build:** Add an allowlist of operational record kinds and a denylist of sensitive/psychological categories to the small partner adapter. Require `scope`, `purpose`, `source`, and `review_after`; reject global promotion without `source.type=explicit_owner`. Provide `partner list`, `partner forget <id>`, `partner scope <id>`, and `partner export` commands. Keep raw transcript pointers outside the compact injected note. Do not send the owner's 10.62 GB history to a new hosted service.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Run privacy fixtures containing operational instructions adjacent to sensitive facts. A separate scanner must show that only the minimal operational claim enters the partner note; a second independently implemented export audit searches for seeded canary sensitive phrases. The owner reviews the exported model before live activation. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that strict scope/minimization reduces exposure compared with exhaustive peer reasoning; evidence is NIST privacy guidance and the substantially smaller stored surface. Utility impact remains **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is comparison of held-out task performance with the minimized model versus a full-history retrieval baseline**.

**SOTA citation:** NIST recommends documenting and minimizing personally sensitive information, monitoring inference of sensitive attributes, and evaluating manipulation risk from overly customized content ([NIST AI RMF Playbook, Measure 2.10](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/measure/)). NIST describes privacy as protecting autonomy, identity, dignity, consent, and control, and notes AI can infer previously private information ([NIST AI risks and trustworthiness](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/3-sec-characteristics/)).

**Value x effort:** **high x low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for deny-by-default schema and offline privacy tests; **confirm-first** for global promotion or any import of historical data.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### F8 — Make personalization earn deployment through an owner-specific counterfactual benchmark — UNVERIFIED

**Improvement:** Evaluate whether the partner model predicts and serves this owner better than the present harness before it is allowed to steer live work.

**What it is:** An owner-specific eval built from verified corrections, thread continuations, explicit delegation, reversals, and “do not ask”/“ask me only when manual” cases. It tests four separate abilities: current-goal selection, thread retention, action/confirmation choice, and abstention under ambiguous evidence. It is not a generic memory-recall score.

**Why-inferred:** The corpus provides raw candidates but warns that 41/41 in an unfiltered sample were false positives (`corpus.json:10371-10374`), so automatically training or scoring on all 2,268 would bake detector error into the owner model. The known-defect record independently shows seven wrong subsystem conclusions from unvalidated detectors (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). The owner needs an evidence gate, not another persuasive narrative.

**How-to-build:** Manually label 100-200 source-addressable examples from different sessions, with separate labels for “explicit owner,” “agent-authored prompt/system injection,” “ambiguous,” and “actual correction.” Freeze a held-out test set. For each item, compare (A) no partner model, (B) explicit-facts-only, and (C) inferred partner hypotheses. Score exact thread coverage, correct autonomy class, harmful carry-over, questions asked, and abstention. Use two mechanically independent judges: deterministic rules where possible (artifact exists, tool result, next owner correction) and a fresh-context model blind to treatment; the owner adjudicates disagreements and a random sample. Never let the model that produced a hypothesis judge it.

**EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Deployment gate: treatment C must materially beat B on thread retention and owner repetition, not regress autonomy safety, and keep false inferred carry-over below a pre-agreed threshold. Then run two weeks in shadow mode, showing no live personalization, and compare predicted actions with actual owner responses. The premise that inferred hypotheses outperform explicit-facts-only is **UNVERIFIED — this exact held-out benchmark and shadow trial settle it**. Given RealPref's reported degradation with longer context and implicit preferences, it is **roughly even (45-55%)** that inference adds net value beyond explicit goals in this workspace; evidence is mixed, hence no deployment recommendation before measurement.

**SOTA citation:** RealPref reports that long-horizon preference following degrades as history grows and preferences become implicit, and unseen-scenario generalization remains difficult ([Guo et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04191)). ## Why the full Honcho architecture loses the decision

Honcho contains several valuable ideas worth stealing: entity/scoped representations, source-aware conclusions, perspective separation, asynchronous derivation, and context-curated retrieval. It also reports strong vendor-run memory results: 90.4% on LongMem S and 89.9% on LoCoMo in its published configuration ([method and results](https://plasticlabs.ai/blog/research/Benchmarking-Honcho)). Those results make Honcho a serious memory system, not vaporware.

They do not settle this unit's question. The proposed owner model must improve goal selection, delegation, thread retention, and proactive timing. Honcho's own article says benchmarks do not test its psychological questions; it uses LLM-as-judge for LoCoMo and discusses ambiguous or incorrect benchmark questions. The architecture is a separate server with storage and insight services, background derivation, and vector-embedded collections ([GitHub architecture summary](https://github.com/plastic-labs/honcho)). Installing it would duplicate a canonical semantic vault, objective event ledger, lexical/semantic routing, and continuous-learning mechanisms already present locally. It would also cross the anti-overfit gate by introducing a second memory service/vector collection and a background reasoning runtime.

Therefore full Honcho adoption is **unlikely (20-45%)** to have positive net value for this owner under the current evidence. The evidence is the duplication and missing task-specific evaluation, not a claim that Honcho is poor. A future decision may change if the F8 owner-specific benchmark shows the thin model failing and a sandboxed Honcho treatment materially winning on the same held-out tasks without privacy, latency, or false-inference regressions.

## Hook-ceiling, fail-open, and prompt-fatigue accounting

- **Hooks added: 0.** Partner state is written through `note.py`/basic-memory and consumed by existing SessionStart replay, goal reinjection, open-loop resurfacing, and calibrated-silence paths.
- **Seventh-hook replacement: not applicable.** If implementation discovers that these existing paths cannot carry the data, the work must pause; it may fold into the existing dispatcher invocation (`_dispatch/manifest.json:1-4`), but it may not register a seventh hook.
- **Failure behavior:** every adapter/import/formatting path catches its own errors and returns no injection/exit 0. Failure to load the partner model means ordinary baseline behavior, never denial of a tool call.
- **Prompt budget:** task-start injection is bounded to active goal + active commitments + delegation boundary; no biography, reasoning trace, or exhaustive profile. At most three boundary suggestions. Receipts appear only when learned state materially changed an action.
- **Thread safety:** no thread closes on same-context prose. It closes on an execution result, artifact probe, externally observed state, or explicit owner cancellation.
- **Quality bar:** there is one versioned model note and one adapter, not `owner-model-v2`; tests precede wiring; the feature stays in shadow mode until F8 passes.

## Rejected alternatives and dead ends

1. **Install Honcho as another memory server — rejected.** **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)**. It violates the anti-overfit gate by duplicating memory/vector/reasoning infrastructure. No owner-specific counterfactual shows it beats the existing stores.
2. **Generate a narrative psychological profile after every session — rejected.** This makes single-context abductive inference look authoritative, risks sensitive inference, and has no independent closure signal.
3. **Treat frequent owner wording as a global reward function — rejected.** The corpus miner is recall-biased (`corpus.json:10371-10374`), injected/agent-authored messages contaminate candidates, and behavioral preference inference is underdetermined.
4. **Add a proactive “owner-agent” that autonomously messages the main agent — rejected.** That is a second orchestration runtime, adds latency and chatter, and makes an inferred model a principal rather than evidence.
5. **Add another hook for owner-model injection — rejected.** Existing dispatcher/replay/reinject/open-loop lanes can carry the bounded state; another hook violates the ceiling without replacing one.
6. **Let the current agent review whether it understood the owner — rejected by construction.** The law requires an external signal, and local operating evidence documents same-context/single-detector failure.
7. **Ask the owner to approve every inferred fact immediately — rejected.** It converts automation into questionnaire labor and violates prompt-fatigue. Hypotheses remain silent/non-authoritative until a natural boundary, a second signal, or a decision that genuinely requires confirmation.

## Sequenced implementation decision

1. **First: F8 offline dataset and baseline.** No live steering. This settles whether explicit goals already capture most value.
2. **Second: F1/F2 schema and adapter in shadow mode.** Record only explicit goals, commitments, delegation, and closure evidence.
3. **Third: F3/F6 policy replay.** Measure questions avoided, unsafe autonomy, interruption count, and acceptance.
4. **Fourth: F4 external hypothesis verification and F7 privacy controls.** Only then permit inferred hypotheses into shadow predictions.
5. **Last: owner-confirmed live trial with F5 receipts.** Roll back to explicit-facts-only on false carry-over, annoyance increase, or missed scope.

This order is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose failure before it can steer consequential work because every stage is evaluated against held-out or next-turn external evidence before promotion. The evidence is structural: offline ground truth, separate-context judging, actual tool results, and owner sampling; no stage relies on the producing context declaring itself correct.
