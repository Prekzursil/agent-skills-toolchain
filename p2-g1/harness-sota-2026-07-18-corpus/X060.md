## Executive result — UNVERIFIED recommendation

The analogy transfers, but only in a constrained form. The useful transfer is **observer and feedback-loop discipline**, not the claim that an agent is a linear plant or that “trust” can literally be controlled. Control theory contributes five concrete ideas:

1. represent reliability as a **latent, time-varying state** rather than a permanent reputation number;
2. refuse to estimate components that are not **observable/identifiable** from independent outcomes;
3. monitor **innovations** (prediction residuals), not merely the running success average;
4. introduce limited **persistent excitation** through cheap, controlled canaries when operational traffic supplies no evidence;
5. stabilize decisions with **dead bands/hysteresis** and prevent reputation lock-in with an **anti-windup** analogue.

The central recommendation is a thin trust-observer layer on the existing hook/skill/MCP bus. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Its contract is to maintain a small state record for each `(agent version, task family, verifier class)` cell, accept only adjudicated outcome events, emit a probability interval plus an observability status, and expose innovation alarms. A separate configuration applies routing hysteresis. It is not another orchestration runtime and does not choose or execute tasks.

This transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to improve trust discrimination and earlier drift detection in a shadow-mode replay, because Kalman-style innovations are a mature way to compare predictions with later measurements, agent trajectories contain process-level failure signals in recent empirical work, and operational outcomes provide delayed observations; the uncertainty is that agent/task outcomes are non-Gaussian, policy-selected, sparsely labeled, and often ambiguously adjudicated ([Kalman 1960](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3662552); [Zhang, Xiong, and Wu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15778)). A temporal replay with the experiments in §9 settles this premise.

Literal Kalman filtering is **unlikely (20-45%)** to outperform a simpler hierarchical Beta/EWMA baseline across the whole harness, because binary and ordinal correctness labels violate the linear-Gaussian model and because task mixtures change. This is an inference from the model assumptions in Kalman's formulation and the target's data structure, not a measured harness result ([Kalman 1960 PDF](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/fluidsengineering/article-pdf/82/1/35/5518977/35_1.pdf)). The proposed experiment therefore compares the structural borrow against simple probabilistic baselines rather than presupposing a Kalman implementation.

## 1. What control theory solved in its own field

### 1.1 Observability: do the outputs contain enough information to reconstruct state?

For a dynamical system, the internal state is not automatically knowable from its outputs. In the linear time-invariant case,

\[
x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k,\qquad y_k=Cx_k+Du_k,
\]

observability asks whether a finite history of known inputs and measured outputs uniquely determines the initial state. The familiar rank test stacks `C, CA, …, CA^(n-1)`; full rank means every state direction leaves a distinguishable trace in the output history. Kalman's 1960 system-theoretic work introduced observability and controllability as structural properties, and his filtering paper formulated recursive state estimation together with the covariance of estimation error ([Kalman, *Contributions to the Theory of Optimal Control*, bibliographic copy and author commentary](https://garfield.library.upenn.edu/classics1979/A1979HE37100001.pdf); [Kalman, *A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems*](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3662552)). Åström and Murray give the modern state-space treatment and emphasize that observability is a property of the dynamics-and-measurement pairing, not of a sensor in isolation ([*Feedback Systems*, official Caltech public PDF](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/fbs-public_24Jul2020.pdf)).

The important mechanism is a **precondition check**. An observer is not licensed merely because an estimator can output a number. If different latent states generate indistinguishable observations, no tuning can recover the missing distinction. The honest result is “unobservable under these measurements,” followed by a sensor/design change.

### 1.2 Controllability and persistent excitation: can inputs reveal or move the relevant directions?

Controllability asks whether admissible inputs can move the state throughout the relevant state space. Its exact LTI rank test is dual to observability. For identification rather than regulation, the closely related practical idea is **persistent excitation**: data must keep carrying fresh information about the parameters being estimated. Green and Moore characterize how richness in inputs propagates to outputs, states, and regression vectors, and explain its role in convergence of online identification, adaptive state estimation, prediction, and control ([Green and Moore 1986](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-6911%2886%2990052-6)). Bittanti and Campi state the core identification problem directly: reliable estimates of time-varying parameters require data that continually contain fresh information ([Bittanti and Campi 2005](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0363012998343483)).

The mechanism is not “collect more of the same.” It is to choose or retain sufficiently diverse input conditions so that competing parameter hypotheses produce distinguishable outputs. Without excitation, confidence can increase numerically while identifiability does not.

### 1.3 The state observer and innovation: predict, compare, correct

Kalman's filter alternates two operations:

1. propagate a prior state estimate and its uncertainty through a dynamics model;
2. compare the predicted measurement with the actual measurement and correct the state in proportion to the relative uncertainty.

For measurement `y_k`, predicted measurement `ŷ_k`, and innovation covariance `S_k`, the **innovation** is

\[
\nu_k=y_k-\hat y_k,
\]

and the normalized innovation squared is

\[
\epsilon_k=\nu_k^T S_k^{-1}\nu_k.
\]

Under the correctly specified linear-Gaussian model, innovations have expected distributional properties; abnormal bias, scale, or serial structure is evidence that the model, noise assumptions, sensor, or plant has changed. A NASA fault-detection dissertation shows the engineering use concretely: standardized innovations have approximately zero mean and unit variance under normal behavior and support detection of biased/noisy instruments and changes in parameters or structure ([Carbone 2020, NASA NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205008505/downloads/CarboneDissertation.pdf)). This is more diagnostic than watching the raw output alone because it asks, “How surprising was this outcome relative to what the estimator claimed?”

Sequential residual monitoring can use Page's cumulative-sum idea: accumulate evidence for a persistent shift while discounting variations too small to matter. Page's original continuous-inspection scheme also defined average run length, making the false-alarm/detection-delay tradeoff explicit ([Page 1954](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100)).

### 1.4 Dead zone and hysteresis: suppress chatter around a decision boundary

An on/off controller with one switching threshold can oscillate rapidly when noise moves the measured quantity back and forth around that boundary. A dead zone ignores sufficiently small errors; hysteresis uses different thresholds for switching in opposite directions, so a small reversal does not immediately undo the prior action. Åström and Murray discuss both as standard modifications to on/off feedback and connect them to avoiding oscillatory switching ([*Feedback Systems*, §1 examples and figures](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/am08-hyperref_19Jul11.pdf)).

This mechanism does not make the state estimate more accurate. It stabilizes the **decision driven by the estimate**.

### 1.5 Anti-windup: stop an integrator accumulating error it cannot act on

Integral control removes persistent offset by accumulating error. If an actuator saturates, however, the demanded control can keep growing internally even though the actuator cannot deliver it. When the system eventually leaves saturation, the stored integral produces overshoot and slow recovery. Åström and Rundqwist analyze this “reset windup” and anti-windup methods; common implementations condition integration on whether it can help or feed the difference between requested and realizable actuation back into the integrator (“back calculation”) ([Åström and Rundqwist 1989](https://doi.org/10.23919/ACC.1989.4790464); [Lund University publication record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/8517647)). Åström and Murray show a PID implementation with an explicit anti-windup feedback path ([official Caltech PID chapter](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM05/pdf/am08-pid_19Jul11.pdf)).

The general lesson is subtle: an error accumulator must know when the downstream action is saturated or disconnected. Otherwise internal state reflects commands that were never physically realized.

## 2. Structural translation to agent trust

### 2.1 Define the latent state narrowly

The target state is not “how good is agent A?” A useful cell is:

`(agent implementation/version, task family, verifier class, environment epoch)`

with a latent probability that an output will satisfy a declared acceptance criterion. Illustrative task families are code modification, factual research, repository diagnosis, UI work, and destructive operations; illustrative verifier classes are executable tests, schema/contract checks, source-grounded review, downstream acceptance, and owner correction. These examples are not a claim that the eventual taxonomy is correct.

The required estimator output is:

- posterior mean or forecasted success probability;
- an uncertainty interval;
- effective adjudicated sample size and age of newest evidence;
- `observable`, `weakly-observable`, or `unknown` status;
- recent signed innovation and sequential-change statistic;
- provenance counts by verifier class.

This state design is **likely (55-80%)** to discriminate failures better than a global per-agent success rate, because heterogeneous tasks are a direct mixture-confounding problem and recent agent-calibration work reports gains from trajectory-level features across diverse benchmarks; the exact cell taxonomy is UNVERIFIED until a temporal clustering and replay experiment compares held-out calibration by taxonomy ([Zhang, Xiong, and Wu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15778)). The experiment is §9.2.

Log self-reported confidence as an input feature, never as the adjudicating observation. Tian et al. show that elicitation can improve confidence calibration for language models, which makes it potentially informative, but that is not evidence that a model's stated confidence is ground truth ([Tian et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/)). Human trust research likewise finds that showing confidence can improve trust calibration without necessarily improving joint decisions ([Zhang, Liao, and Bellamy 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114)).

### 2.2 Map the signals explicitly

| Control-theory object | Harness translation | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Plant state `x` | latent probability/odds of acceptable output for one agent-task-verifier cell | reliability is socially defined and can jump after model, prompt, tool, or environment changes |
| Control input `u` | assigned task, prompt constraints, available tools, verification policy | these inputs influence outcomes but do not deterministically “drive competence” |
| Measurement `y` | independently adjudicated outcome, usually delayed | many outcomes are missing, subjective, censored, or correlated |
| Measurement model `C` | relationship between verifier result and actual task success | verifier false positives/negatives must be modeled or at least stratified |
| Observer estimate `x̂` | trust forecast plus interval | must be `unknown` when evidence cannot identify the cell |
| Innovation `ν` | adjudicated outcome minus predicted probability | binary outcomes require Bernoulli-aware variance, not blind Gaussian math |
| Process noise `Q` | modeled drift between versions/epochs and over time | no stationary value is guaranteed |
| Measurement noise `R` | verifier ambiguity/error and label disagreement | “independent verifier” is often only nominally independent |
| Excitation | controlled canary tasks and small exploration allocation | canaries sample only the skills they embody |
| Saturation | trust/routing score at a floor or ceiling; no more/less work can be routed | accumulated evidence can make recovery or degradation detection arbitrarily slow |
| Dead band/hysteresis | separate promote and demote thresholds | creates deliberate detection lag |

## 3. Concrete harness design — UNVERIFIED recommendations

### 3.1 UNVERIFIED — Trust event contract — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Define one append-only event shape on the existing bus:

```json
{
  "agent_id": "...",
  "agent_version": "...",
  "task_family": "...",
  "environment_epoch": "...",
  "prediction": 0.73,
  "prediction_time": "...",
  "outcome": 0,
  "outcome_time": "...",
  "verifier_class": "executable-test",
  "verifier_id": "...",
  "label_confidence": 0.95,
  "selection_policy": "normal|canary|exploration|manual",
  "correlation_group": "...",
  "evidence_ref": "..."
}
```

Use binary `outcome` initially. Add partial/ordinal outcomes only after their semantics are fixed. `correlation_group` groups labels derived from the same test suite, judge model, owner correction, or downstream incident so ten manifestations of one failure do not masquerade as ten independent measurements.

This schema is **very likely (80-90%)** to be implementable without a new runtime because it is an event contract carried by an existing bus; this is a design judgment, not verified against local hook schemas by instruction. A schema-validation dry run against 100 representative events settles compatibility. No claim is made that the current harness lacks an equivalent event.

### 3.2 UNVERIFIED — Trust observer — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Implement a single small consumer with two deliberately simple candidate estimators:

1. **Baseline:** recency-weighted Beta-Bernoulli state per cell, with hierarchical shrinkage to task-family and agent-version parents when evidence is sparse.
2. **Optional challenger:** a Bernoulli state-space filter on log odds, with bounded random-walk drift.

The output must distinguish `high estimated success with strong evidence` from `high prior with little evidence`. It must never collapse interval, freshness, and observability into a single traffic-light label.

For each adjudicated event, compute a standardized Bernoulli residual such as

\[
z_k = \frac{y_k-p_k}{\sqrt{p_k(1-p_k)+\sigma^2_{label}+\epsilon}},
\]

then maintain signed EWMA/CUSUM statistics for unexpected failure and unexpected recovery. The exact statistic and thresholds must be estimated from temporal holdout data, not copied from Gaussian NIS tables.

The observer is **likely (55-80%)** to detect a sustained reliability shift earlier than waiting for a large change in aggregate pass rate, because sequential residual schemes accumulate small same-direction surprises and Page's method explicitly targets continuous shift detection ([Page 1954](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100)). This is UNVERIFIED for agent outcomes until §9.3 measures false alarms and detection delay on injected and naturally occurring changes.

### 3.3 UNVERIFIED — Observability ledger — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

For every cell, publish why it is or is not estimable:

- adjudicated events and effective independent count;
- number and identity of verifier classes;
- task-feature coverage;
- time coverage and newest evidence age;
- missing-outcome rate;
- proportion selected by normal routing versus canary/exploration;
- dominant correlation group;
- version/epoch changes since the evidence was collected.

Do not compute a literal observability matrix unless a defensible dynamics and measurement model exists. The ledger is a **structural-identifiability checklist**, not rank-theater. A cell becomes `unknown` after a version/epoch boundary until bridging evidence arrives. If it borrows a prior from related cells, the UI/API must label that distinction.

An observability ledger is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent false precision in sparse cells, because it exposes evidence coverage without depending on estimator correctness; the remaining uncertainty is whether task-family labels themselves are stable. Temporal label-stability analysis and inter-rater agreement on 200 tasks settle that premise.

### 3.4 UNVERIFIED — Canary excitation skill — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Create a low-cost skill that schedules fixed, versioned diagnostic tasks only when a cell's evidence is stale, sparse, or confounded. Each canary must have:

- an executable or otherwise external oracle;
- known task-family coverage;
- controlled tool/environment prerequisites;
- positive and negative controls for the outcome detector;
- a version hash and retirement date;
- a strict cost/rate budget;
- exclusion from ordinary product artifacts.

Use a small exploration quota for real tasks only where owner policy permits and rollback is cheap. Log the selection propensity/policy so evaluation can separate operational traffic from deliberately sampled evidence.

Canaries are **likely (55-80%)** to restore evidence for common, oracle-rich task families such as schema production or testable code edits, based on the persistent-excitation requirement that identification data contain fresh distinguishing information ([Green and Moore 1986](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-6911%2886%2990052-6)). They are **unlikely (20-45%)** to establish trust for open-ended research, taste, novel architecture, or high-stakes destructive work, because fixed probes cannot span those outcome spaces. A coverage study mapping canary feature vectors to operational task clusters settles both claims.

### 3.5 UNVERIFIED — Routing hysteresis and dead band — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Separate estimation from action. Candidate configuration: demote only when the upper confidence bound falls below a demotion threshold and promote only when the lower confidence bound rises above a higher promotion threshold. Require a minimum dwell time unless two independent alarms fire. In the neutral band, preserve the current route while collecting evidence.

Exact thresholds are UNVERIFIED and must be optimized on shadow replay for owner-weighted costs. This proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce routing chatter if score noise currently crosses a single boundary, because hysteresis specifically creates path-dependent separation between opposite switches ([Åström and Murray](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/am08-hyperref_19Jul11.pdf)). Whether current routing chatters is UNVERIFIED; a count of route reversals per cell near decision thresholds, compared with a known-present synthetic oscillation control, settles it.

### 3.6 UNVERIFIED — Evidence anti-windup — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Keep immutable raw evidence, but bound its influence on current routing state:

- cap effective historical sample mass;
- decay or epoch old evidence after agent/model/tool changes;
- freeze integration when adjudication is unavailable rather than treating missing outcomes as success or failure;
- when a route is saturated at “never assign,” allow canary/recovery evidence to update the state;
- when a route is saturated at “always assign,” retain degradation monitoring;
- use asymmetric recovery only when the owner's false-trust and false-distrust costs justify it.

This is the anti-windup translation: raw audit history remains intact, while the control-facing accumulator cannot grow so large at a 0/1 routing limit that changed behavior takes impractically long to matter. It is **likely (55-80%)** to shorten recovery from obsolete reputations and detection from stale perfect records, because capped/decayed effective evidence prevents unbounded pseudo-count inertia; the tradeoff is noisier estimates. A version-change replay comparing recovery delay, false promotion, and false demotion settles the claim.

## 4. What this would catch that an outcome average misses

These are detection capabilities of the proposed mechanism, **not claims that the current harness lacks every one of them**. Establishing current absence would require two mechanically independent local probes, which this ideation-only unit intentionally did not perform.

1. **Task-conditional weakness hidden by a strong global average.** Separate cells reveal an agent that is reliable for code edits but unreliable for research synthesis. A global average cannot distinguish mixture shift from capability shift.

2. **Silent post-change drift.** Epoching plus unexpectedly negative innovations flags outcomes inconsistent with a pre-change forecast even before a long-run rate visibly moves.

3. **Overconfident failure.** A failure predicted at `0.98` is a much larger innovation than a failure predicted at `0.55`; raw error counts treat them alike.

4. **Verifier degradation or correlated false reassurance.** Residual bias by verifier class, correlation-group dominance, and disagreement across genuinely different verifier classes can implicate the measurement channel rather than the agent.

5. **Evidence-free reputation.** The observability ledger emits `unknown/weak` instead of converting a parent prior or stale record into apparent certainty.

6. **Non-recovery after a fix.** Anti-windup plus canary evidence permits a previously demoted agent version to demonstrate recovery without waiting for a huge historical pseudo-count to wash out.

7. **Routing oscillation.** Hysteresis prevents repeated promote/demote actions caused by noise near a threshold.

8. **Endogenous blindness.** A low-trust agent receives no work and therefore no new outcomes; bounded canaries provide limited fresh evidence rather than allowing “no data” to confirm the demotion indefinitely.

9. **Slow, same-direction deterioration.** A CUSUM/EWMA over standardized surprises can alarm on a run of moderately unexpected failures that individually remain below an outlier threshold.

10. **Detector failure disguised as perfect performance.** Every canary family includes known-present success and failure controls. The system cannot report “no failures observed” unless the same measurement path detects its known failure control and an independent aggregate/outcome field agrees.

Capabilities 1-10 are **likely (55-80%)** in oracle-rich cells if the event labels are timely and the controls actually traverse the same measurement path; this joint premise is UNVERIFIED until the shadow replay and live canary A/B in §9. The claim is weaker for subjective or delayed-outcome cells.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 5.1 An agent is not a known dynamical plant

The classical observer assumes a specified state transition and measurement relation. Agent behavior changes discontinuously with model updates, context, tools, prompts, workload, and task novelty. “Reliability” is a constructed statistic, not a physical state with conservation laws. Therefore a Kalman covariance can be numerically precise while conceptually wrong.

**Consequence:** borrow predict/compare/correct and uncertainty accounting, not Kalman's optimality claim. Start with Beta/EWMA baselines and treat a state-space filter as a challenger.

### 5.2 Success is not a clean sensor reading

Tests can be incomplete, schemas can validate the wrong semantics, human judgments can disagree, and downstream failures can be delayed. Treat verifiers that share a model, tests, or assumptions as correlated. Counting nominal verifiers as independent would understate uncertainty.

**Consequence:** stratify by verifier class, record correlation groups, estimate disagreement, and require different mechanisms—not repeated runs of one judge—before declaring drift or recovery.

### 5.3 Observability does not map to a literal rank test

There is no justified `A` and `C` matrix for most agent-task behavior. A fabricated observability Gramian would give mathematical decoration without identification validity.

**Consequence:** implement an evidence-coverage/identifiability ledger. Say `unknown` when task strata lack independently adjudicated outcomes.

### 5.4 Controllability mostly does not transfer

The harness cannot drive an agent's latent competence to an arbitrary state through prompts or routing. It can control exposure, constraints, tools, and diagnostic tasks, but those change both the behavior and the measurement context.

**Consequence:** do not name routing “trust control.” The legitimate borrow is persistent excitation for identification. Canaries probe a defined operating envelope; they do not prove global competence.

### 5.5 Feedback changes the data-generating process

Routing based on trust causes selection bias: trusted agents see more and perhaps harder work, while distrusted agents stop generating evidence. Verification intensity may also rise after alarms, making post-alarm outcomes incomparable. This is a closed-loop identification problem, not passive scoring.

**Consequence:** log selection policy/propensity, preserve a bounded canary or exploration channel, compare like task strata, and evaluate on temporally held-out traffic. Do not interpret an improving routed average as improving agent capability without a stable evaluation stream.

### 5.6 Dead bands and anti-windup create their own failures

Hysteresis trades chatter for detection lag. Forgetting/capping evidence enables recovery but can also forgive recurring failures too quickly. Canary traffic can be memorized or cease to represent real work.

**Consequence:** publish dwell time, alarm delay, effective history, and canary age; rotate canaries; retain raw evidence; set thresholds from explicit owner costs.

### 5.7 Trust calibration is not outcome optimization

A calibrated `70%` agent can still be the wrong choice if failures are catastrophic, and a less calibrated agent can be useful with a strong verifier. Zhang, Liao, and Bellamy empirically show that better trust calibration alone need not improve human-AI joint decisions ([2020 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114)).

**Consequence:** trust probability is one routing input alongside impact, reversibility, verification strength, cost, and latency. Never expose it as an authorization decision by itself.

## 6. UNVERIFIED — Stability design for the complete loop

The relevant loop is:

`forecast trust → route/verify → obtain selected outcomes → update trust → change route/verification`.

Three destabilizing delays exist: adjudication delay, task-completion delay, and policy dwell time. Model/version changes add abrupt disturbances. The safe design uses:

- bounded update gain/effective sample mass;
- explicit event-time processing, not arrival order alone;
- epoch boundaries on material agent/model/tool changes;
- separate promote/demote thresholds;
- minimum dwell time for ordinary alarms;
- emergency action only on two mechanically independent signals;
- a stable canary stream outside normal route selection;
- shadow evaluation before automated routing;
- rollback to the prior routing policy without deleting evidence.

Candidate emergency two-signal rule: require `(negative innovation CUSUM)` **and** either `(held-out canary failure-rate shift)` or `(independent verifier-class failure)`. Re-running the same test or the same judge does not count. Before any “no drift” conclusion, the detector must identify a known injected shift; before any “recovered” conclusion, both a positive recovery canary and an operational/verifier signal must agree.

This loop design is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce oscillatory routing and selection-induced blind spots compared with a single-threshold score, based on the established roles of hysteresis, excitation, and residual monitoring. It is UNVERIFIED as an integrated agent-routing policy until shadow replay shows lower owner-weighted loss without unacceptable detection lag.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 8.1 One Elo score per agent

Rejected as the primary design. Elo-like ratings are attractive for pairwise competitions but collapse task type, verifier quality, evidence age, and absolute acceptance probability. They can be a display ranking only after a calibrated per-task model exists.

### 8.2 Trust the agent's declared confidence

Rejected as adjudication. Confidence elicitation is useful evidence and has empirical support for language-model calibration ([Tian et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/)), but self-report and output correctness share a generator and are not mechanically independent.

### 8.3 Literal multidimensional Kalman filter

Rejected as the default. It would demand invented linear dynamics, Gaussian errors, and stable covariance matrices. The transfer is the observer architecture and innovation discipline, not the exact filter.

### 8.4 Learned trajectory critic

Rejected at this stage. Recent agentic calibration work indicates that trajectory features can predict correctness ([Zhang, Xiong, and Wu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15778)), but a trained critic is explicitly disfavored by the anti-overfit gate, introduces distribution shift and a new verifier whose own calibration must be monitored, and is unnecessary to test the simpler hypothesis.

### 8.5 All-real-task exploration

Rejected. Deliberately routing consequential owner work to uncertain agents solely for identification externalizes experiment cost onto the owner. Exploration is limited to cheap, reversible tasks under an explicit policy; fixed canaries handle the rest.

### 8.6 Permanent blacklist after repeated failures

Rejected as a trust estimator. It confuses safety policy with belief state and creates integrator-like lock-in. A separate safety policy can prohibit an operation regardless of trust; require the estimator to remain capable of representing new evidence after a version change.

## 9. Verification and falsification program

### 9.1 Detector controls first

Before measuring benefit:

1. inject a known-present successful outcome, known-present failed outcome, delayed outcome, duplicated/correlated outcome, and version boundary;
2. verify each through the event-consumer output;
3. independently reconcile event IDs/counts against the source outcome field;
4. confirm a known-absent synthetic cell remains `unknown`;
5. fail the experiment if any control is missed or duplicates inflate effective independent evidence.

This satisfies the two-signal discipline: consumer state and independent source reconciliation are mechanically distinct. A repeated consumer query is not a second signal.

### 9.2 Retrospective temporal replay

Partition by time, never random-row split. Infer task families using a frozen taxonomy, then manually audit a stratified sample. Compare:

- global per-agent rate;
- per-task sliding-window Beta baseline;
- hierarchical recency-weighted Beta observer;
- optional Bernoulli state-space challenger;
- self-reported confidence alone, where available.

Report Brier score, log loss, reliability diagrams/ECE, interval coverage, risk at fixed automation coverage, and calibration separately by task family, verifier class, agent version, and evidence sparsity. Recent agentic work makes trajectory-feature calibration a relevant frontier comparator, but this unit does not recommend importing its learned model ([Zhang, Xiong, and Wu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15778)).

**Pass gate:** the small observer must improve temporally held-out Brier score or owner-weighted selective risk over the best simple baseline in multiple material task families, without hiding poor strata in a global mean. Exact numeric margin is UNVERIFIED; the owner must set it before results are viewed to prevent threshold shopping.

### 9.3 Drift and stability replay

Test both natural version/epoch changes and controlled perturbations:

- abrupt 5/10/20 percentage-point degradation;
- gradual degradation;
- verifier false-positive shift;
- burst of correlated failures;
- long missing-label interval;
- genuine recovery after version change;
- task-mixture shift without within-task degradation.

Measure false alarms per 1,000 adjudicated events, median detection delay, missed-shift rate, promote/demote reversals, recovery time, and routed owner-weighted loss. Compare no hysteresis, symmetric hysteresis, and confidence-bound hysteresis. Compare unbounded counts with capped/decayed effective evidence while retaining the same raw audit log.

**Falsification:** abandon innovation alarms if they do not beat a plain windowed-rate detector on the false-alarm/delay frontier. Abandon hysteresis if the reduction in reversals costs more in delayed degradation response than the owner assigns to chatter. Abandon canaries in a task family if their performance does not predict operational outcomes after controlling for agent version.

### 9.4 Shadow deployment

For at least one full operational cycle, emit estimates and counterfactual routing decisions without changing routes. Review every alarm with two independent signals. Publish the unknown/weakly-observable share; do not score coverage gaps as successes.

Automated routing after a successful shadow is **roughly even (45-55%)** to deliver net owner value, because calibrated estimates can improve selective automation but routing itself induces selection bias and the cost asymmetry is owner-specific ([Zhang, Liao, and Bellamy 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114)). A randomized or stepped-wedge rollout on cheap, reversible task families settles this claim.

## Bottom line

Control theory does not give the harness a magic trust score. It gives a better question: **what latent claim are we estimating, through which measurements, under what excitation, with what residuals, and what happens when the estimate drives the data we later observe?** That question transfers strongly.

The best concrete borrow is a small, falsifiable trust observer with an observability ledger, normalized outcome surprises, bounded memory, controlled canaries, and hysteretic routing. The literal plant/controller analogy stops at that boundary. If the temporal replay cannot beat a windowed per-task Beta baseline on calibration and drift detection, retain only the evidence contract and observability ledger and stop; forcing a Kalman-shaped solution would be cross-domain theater.
