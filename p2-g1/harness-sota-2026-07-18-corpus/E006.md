# E006 — Ecosystem survey: community skill collections

Date: 2026-07-19 UTC  
Scope: community collections of Agent Skills / `SKILL.md` packages usable by Claude Code and/or Codex; official collections are included only as quality and compatibility baselines.  
Subject mode: read-only. No skill, plugin, package, MCP server, hook, or configuration was installed or changed. The only persistent write is this artifact.

## Executive verdict

The ecosystem is now large enough that “install a collection” is the wrong unit of adoption. The best evidence-backed strategy for this owner is a small, pinned, reviewed allowlist of individual skills drawn from a few strong publishers, installed through the native Claude/Codex skill or plugin bus. Wholesale ingestion of hundreds or thousands of skills is especially ill-suited here: the pinned corpus says only 78 of roughly 355 installed skills were invoked, while 1,293 orphaned plugin directories already cause Windows command-line overflow. Those facts are in `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:13265-13346` and the pinned ground truth in the unit brief. This recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve useful capability per unit of operational complexity because the corpus shows low utilization of the existing long tail, the measured orphan-directory failure is caused by installation volume, and current empirical work finds both highly variable skill benefit and nontrivial community-supply-chain risk ([skill-quality study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01456), [malicious-skills study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06547)). The experiment that would raise this above 90% is an A/B evaluation of a 6-10-skill allowlist versus the current harness on the owner’s real task strata.

The highest-quality community source in this survey is **Trail of Bits’ focused security marketplace**; that ranking is **very likely (80-90%)** given its independent signals of explicit authoring constraints, progressive disclosure, tests, recent maintenance, organizational domain authority, and at least one published real bug found with a skill. The strongest broad workflow source remains **Superpowers**, with **very likely (80-90%)** confidence from its 52 test/eval-shaped files, current release, cross-host packaging, and demonstrated use in this owner’s corpus; it should be evolved/selectively updated, not installed again. **K-Dense Scientific Agent Skills** is **very likely (80-90%)** the most relevant expansion collection because its topical inventory aligns with independently measured scientific-tool use, but its 149 skills and large dependency surface make topical selection mandatory. **Vercel’s focused web skills** are a plausible selective addition for web/UI work, with **likely (55-80%)** confidence from their focus and test surface but unresolved licensing and overlap. **Matt Pocock’s skills** offer clear, composable engineering methods but lack repository-level tests. **glebis/claude-skills** contains strongly tested individual workflows, but the repository has no root license, so adoption is blocked pending an explicit grant.

No high-signal collection sampled here is proven abandoned. Two are **dormant enough to avoid as upstream dependencies**, with **likely (55-80%)** confidence from independent recency and release/test signals: `claude-office-skills/skills` last changed 2026-01-31 and has neither tags/releases nor test files in the sampled commit; `Gentleman-Programming/Gentleman-Skills` last changed 2026-03-28 and likewise has neither tags/releases nor test files. I do **not** call either abandoned: inactivity and missing release/test machinery do not prove maintainer intent. The absence detectors were controlled against known-present Superpowers artifacts (`14` `SKILL.md`, `52` test/eval files, one root license), and GitHub’s repository UI was used as an independent signal for release status. A maintainer statement or a six-to-twelve-month follow-up would settle abandonment.

The collections that fail the anti-overfit gate as collections are named below: `affaan-m/everything-claude-code`, `wshobson/agents`, `sickn33/antigravity-awesome-skills`, `alirezarezvani/claude-skills`, and the full orchestration portions of `glebis/claude-skills`. They bundle or promote second orchestration layers, durable/automatic memory, vector-memory integrations, observer/guardrail systems, or enormous mirrored catalogs. Individual plain skills inside them may pass after isolation, but the collections do not.

## Evaluation standard

“High quality” here means evidence in at least four independent dimensions, not stars:

1. **Native fit:** plain `SKILL.md`, a Claude/Codex plugin, or an MCP-oriented skill that rides the existing bus.
2. **Narrow value:** specialized knowledge or a verification workflow that the base model and installed harness do not already provide reliably.
3. **Validation:** executable tests/evals, documented verification steps, real outcomes, or disciplined authoring requirements.
4. **Maintenance:** recent substantive commits, releases/changelog, issue activity, or an organizational maintainer.
5. **Progressive disclosure:** focused frontmatter and references/scripts loaded only when needed.
6. **Trust/legal clarity:** a real license at the relevant commit, attributable upstream provenance, and inspectable code/dependencies.
7. **Anti-overfit:** no new orchestration runtime, vector/KG memory, durable execution service, trained critic, observability SaaS, or generic guardrail framework.

This standard is consistent with the open Agent Skills shape documented by [Anthropic’s repository](https://github.com/anthropics/skills) and with Vercel’s explicit guidance to keep `SKILL.md` under 500 lines, use progressive disclosure, and prefer scripts for deterministic work ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-skills/blob/f8a72b9603728bb92a217a879b7e62e43ad76c81/AGENTS.md)). It is also conservative for good reason: a 2026 behavioral study verified 157 malicious skills and 632 vulnerabilities among 98,380 registry skills ([arXiv 2602.06547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06547)); a separate study found description-only framing selected adversarial equivalents 77.6% of the time on average ([arXiv 2605.11418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11418)); and a quality study found all but one of 238 sampled skills had at least one authoring smell ([arXiv 2607.01456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01456)). These papers do not prove any named repository here is malicious or ineffective; they do refute the premise that registry presence or popularity is sufficient review.

## Collection-by-collection assessment

### Tier A — best sources for selective adoption

#### Trail of Bits Skills

Quality: highest in this sample for security work. The repository has 75 skill files, 34 test/eval-shaped files, focused installable plugins, explicit “when to use / when not to use” requirements, progressive-disclosure rules, and security-specific “rationalizations to reject” guidance ([authoring rules](https://github.com/trailofbits/skills/blob/cfe5d7b1619e47fb5b38b7e2561dad7e5f1e89af/CLAUDE.md)). Its README names a concrete timing-side-channel found with `constant-time-analysis`, an outcome signal independent of repository structure. It is active as of 2026-06-30.

Best fits here: `fp-check`, `insecure-defaults`, `supply-chain-risk-auditor`, `differential-review`, `property-based-testing`, `agentic-actions-auditor`, and `sharp-edges`. They address verification, false-positive discipline, fail-open defaults, supply-chain review, and security review without requiring a new runtime. Avoid `second-opinion` unless a task specifically needs another CLI model, and avoid treating `skill-improver` as an autonomous always-on loop.

License: CC BY-SA 4.0, not MIT. Copies/adaptations must retain attribution and be shared under compatible terms. Merely invoking an installed unmodified skill does not by itself create a derivative artifact, but any distributed modified copy needs license review.

#### Superpowers

Quality: high and unusually well tested. It has 14 skills, 52 test/eval-shaped files, cross-host packaging, release v6.1.1, and an explicit evidence-over-claims / verification-before-completion philosophy ([README](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/blob/d884ae04edebef577e82ff7c4e143debd0bbec99/README.md)). Its skills use native host skills, hooks, and optional native subagents rather than a separate daemon. MIT license.

Fit here: already established, not an expansion. The corpus proves repeated use, including debugging, planning, brainstorming, and parallel-agent workflows. Installing a second copy or another full methodology beside it would create trigger conflict. The useful action is to pin/update the existing source and keep only the workflows that win owner-task evals.

One caveat: the repository describes mandatory automatic workflow selection and optional visual-companion telemetry. Even though telemetry can be disabled, this owner’s harness should keep it disabled and should not let “mandatory methodology” override explicit owner intent. That is a configuration/review concern, not an anti-overfit failure.

#### K-Dense Scientific Agent Skills

Quality: the strongest domain expansion match. It is active, MIT-licensed, uses the open skill format, provides 149 skills with 388 script files and 16 test/eval-shaped files, documents version pinning, warns about arbitrary code/network risk, and says contributions receive review plus LLM-based security scans ([README](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/claude-scientific-skills/blob/3f825caafe149b7853ec8c4d1dd7f4553ea6b2a5/README.md)). The collection includes `database-lookup`, `literature-review`, `scientific-critical-thinking`, `peer-review`, `citation-management`, `hypothesis-generation`, and specialized package skills such as RDKit.

Fit here: unusually strong because the corpus independently shows heavy ToolUniverse, PubMed, ChEMBL, and RDKit use. However, many skills install Python packages, use APIs, or overlap existing MCP capabilities. Static analysis found six `SKILL.md` files over 5,000 words and a median around 1,668 words in the sampled tree, so wholesale install is poor context and dependency hygiene even though on-demand loading reduces standing context.

The collection passes the anti-overfit gate only as individual topical skills. The advertised K-Dense BYOK “AI co-scientist” runtime is a separate product and is out of scope; do not adopt it as part of this recommendation.

#### Vercel Agent Skills

Quality: high for a small web-focused set. Nine skills have 115 test/eval-shaped files and include `react-best-practices`, `composition-patterns`, `web-design-guidelines`, React Native, and Vercel-specific deployment/optimization workflows. The repository gives concrete context-efficiency and script-output rules. It is maintained by Vercel Labs and active as of 2026-06-10.

Fit here: the three read-only guidance skills (`react-best-practices`, `composition-patterns`, `web-design-guidelines`) ride the skill bus and may improve UI work. `deploy-to-vercel`, `vercel-cli-with-tokens`, and `vercel-optimize` introduce network, account, spend, or deployment effects and should not be globally enabled for an owner whose safety boundary requires exact authorization for publication/deployment.

License caveat: README says MIT, but the sampled tree has no root license text. Adoption is legally **UNVERIFIED** until Vercel adds a license file or confirms the grant; the settling experiment is obtaining a commit-pinned full MIT notice from the publisher.

### Tier B — good ideas, weaker evidence or licensing

#### Matt Pocock’s skills

The collection is active, MIT-licensed, comparatively small, and deliberately rejects process-owning frameworks in favor of composable skills ([README](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/9603c1cc8118d08bc1b3bf34cf714f62178dea3b/README.md)). Strong candidates include `diagnosing-bugs`, `domain-modeling`, `codebase-design`, `grill-with-docs`, `handoff`, and `research`. The author clearly separates user-invoked orchestration from model-invoked discipline.

The weakness is validation: the sampled repository had zero test/eval-shaped files, and several skills are explicitly under `deprecated/` or `in-progress/`. Author experience and clear prose are not substitutes for behavioral evals. This source is best mined for ideas or piloted one skill at a time, not treated as a tested pack. It overlaps heavily with Superpowers and the owner already invokes `grill-me`/`grill-me-extensive`, so deduplication is essential.

#### glebis/claude-skills

The repository is active and has unusually strong local validation signals: 106 skills, 147 test/eval-shaped files, and 91 reference files. Examples include a release engine with 27 unit tests, `jtbd` with a documented 97-test offline suite, and `qmd-search` with precision/recall/MRR evals. This is better engineering evidence than most personal collections.

Two blockers prevent collection adoption. First, there is no root license at commit `85a8ec1`; public visibility is not permission to copy, modify, or redistribute. Second, the collection mixes excellent small workflows with a vector-search knowledge base (`qmd-search`) and a multi-agent TDD orchestrator—both fail this program’s anti-overfit gate as harness additions. Individual plain skills could be reconsidered after licensing, but the collection cannot be adopted now.

#### Jeffallan/claude-skills

This is an active-ish MIT collection with 66 skills, 369 reference files, a changelog, and release v0.4.15. Progressive disclosure and clear domain routing are positives ([README](https://github.com/Jeffallan/claude-skills/blob/e8be415bc94d8d6ebddc2fb50e5d03c6e27d4319/README.md)). It covers many languages and frameworks.

Validation is weak relative to breadth: only one test/eval-shaped file was found. The collection also contains RAG/vector-database guidance, monitoring/observability roles, and multi-skill project workflows; those are useful subject-matter skills when explicitly requested but not justification for adding another global workflow layer. Select a framework specialist only when owner work demonstrates a recurring gap.

### Tier C — discovery sources or low-confidence packs

#### VoltAgent awesome-agent-skills and hesreallyhim awesome-claude-code

Both are active indexes rather than installable collections. VoltAgent explicitly says entries are selected but not security-audited; its MIT license applies to the list, not linked skills. Hesreallyhim provides wider Claude Code ecosystem discovery but uses CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 for the list. They are useful for quarterly scouting, provided every linked repository is independently pinned, licensed, scanned, and evaluated. Neither should be copied wholesale or considered an endorsement.

#### Claude Office Skills

The breadth (137 skills) is attractive, especially for legal/HR/finance/document workflows, and the license is MIT. Evidence is weak: zero test/eval-shaped files, no release tags/workflow, one visible contributor in GitHub’s page, and the latest commit was 2026-01-31. The README recommends `curl ... | bash`, an installation form inappropriate for a privileged harness without pinning and review. Use Anthropic’s official document skills where licensing permits, or select a specific office skill only after domain-expert validation. Status is dormant, not proven abandoned.

#### Gentleman Skills

Twenty-four MIT skills, simple portable format, last commit 2026-03-28, no test/eval-shaped files, and no tags/releases in the inspected signals. The content may be useful, but there is no evidence it exceeds the stronger active alternatives. Status is dormant, not proven abandoned.

#### Composio awesome-claude-skills

This is an 864-skill vendored aggregation, not merely an awesome list. It has only one test/eval-shaped file by the broad detector, no root license file, and a README that simultaneously claims Apache-2.0 and says individual skills may differ. Per-skill licenses are present in some directories, which confirms heterogeneous provenance rather than resolving it. The huge surface is the opposite of this owner’s measured need. Do not adopt the repository; follow an individual skill back to its canonical upstream.

## Anti-overfit gate failures

These are explicit failures as collections, even when individual files may be useful.

### Everything Claude Code — collection FAIL

The README calls ECC a complete system with skills, instincts, memory optimization, continuous learning, hooks, rules, MCP configs, orchestration, and guardrails; it documents memory persistence, 67 specialized subagents, `orch-*` orchestrators, PM2/multi-agent commands, observer loops, an MCP memory server, GateGuard, and AgentShield ([README](https://github.com/affaan-m/everything-claude-code/blob/754b8dd76ca885b764ec22f476664377aa46b6cd/README.md)). This directly reintroduces rejected durable memory, a broad guardrail plane, and a parallel orchestration/control plane. It is also already present in the corpus. Individual plain skills can pass, but the full pack fails.

### wshobson/agents — collection FAIL

The marketplace advertises 16 orchestrators and explicitly integrates Pensyve external memory for Claude, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Gemini, and Copilot ([README](https://github.com/wshobson/agents/blob/c4b82b0ad771190355eb8e204b1329732a18449a/README.md)). The external-memory integration and broad multi-agent orchestration duplicate rejected components. Individual domain skills may pass after extraction.

### Antigravity Awesome Skills — collection FAIL

At 6,225 `SKILL.md` files, this is a bulk mirror/stack builder whose catalog explicitly includes vector databases, dynamic multi-agent workflow engines, memory systems, MCP reasoning harnesses, guardrails, observability tools, and durable/crash-proof agent pipelines ([README](https://github.com/sickn33/antigravity-awesome-skills/blob/b7b87eca5786259879d7a590eb956bb26bf4f73a/README.md)). It violates both the component exclusions and the owner-specific command-line/orphan-directory constraint. Its multi-license upstream graph also makes whole-repo legal review impractical.

### Alireza Rezvani skills — collection FAIL

The repository contains 362 skill files, 198 agent markdown files, only two test/eval-shaped files by the broad detector, and markets orchestration routers, self-improving/auto-memory capability, multi-agent handoffs, observability roles, and adversarial-review personas ([README](https://github.com/alirezarezvani/claude-skills/blob/aa8d778811a557a2c28ccadda4cf3d0bd028a4cc/README.md)). That is a second workflow/orchestration layer and auto-memory surface. `skill-security-auditor` or another focused skill could be evaluated independently; the pack fails.

### glebis full pack — collection FAIL, individual candidates blocked by license

`qmd-search` is local vector/hybrid memory and `tdd` is a multi-agent orchestration workflow. Those portions fail. Small deterministic skills such as `jtbd` do not inherently fail, but the absent root license blocks copying today. The absence claim has two independent signals: `find ... -iname LICENSE*` returned none while successfully finding Superpowers’ known-present license, and the GitHub repository page exposes no license. An explicit maintainer license is the settling experiment.

### OpenClaw/ClawHub collections — architecture FAIL

OpenClaw skills are a different runtime/registry ecosystem, and adopting OpenClaw to consume them would add a second agent runtime. That fails even before security review. The skills can only count here if an individual package is plain Agent Skills format and can be extracted to the existing Claude/Codex bus. The wider registry merits extra caution: the large-scale malicious-skills study used community registries and behaviorally verified malicious packages; it is not evidence against every OpenClaw skill, but it eliminates “registry listing” as a trust signal ([arXiv 2602.06547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06547)).

## Adoption plan for this harness

Every proposal below uses one of the required implementation tags and stays on the existing skill/plugin bus. None proposes a new daemon, database, orchestration runtime, critic, SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## Frontier implications

1. **Skills are software supply-chain artifacts, not harmless prompts.** They can contain executable scripts, installation commands, network calls, and instruction-level behavior. Behavioral verification of 98,380 registry skills found 157 malicious packages; the base rate is small but nonzero, and impact is high because skills inherit agent privileges ([arXiv 2602.06547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06547)).
2. **Metadata is part of the attack surface.** Semantic manipulation of descriptions can influence discovery and selection even when executable payload scanning is clean ([arXiv 2605.11418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11418)). Therefore intake must review name/description/trigger scope and test false activations, not just scan scripts.
3. **Most public skills are not demonstrably well authored.** A 238-skill study found an average 10.5 smells per skill and only one smell-free item; smells tended to persist ([arXiv 2607.01456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01456)). Its detector includes semantic judgment and does not prove task failure, so use it as a warning, not a rejection oracle.
4. **Fork-and-forget is the normal reuse pattern.** A 2026 study of 18,463 registry skills and 23,199 personal-use skills found 53% of reused skills were never modified after adoption, with later maintenance mostly additive ([arXiv 2607.00911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00911)). That supports provenance manifests, pinning, and explicit local bindings rather than silent copies.
5. **Curated skill value is variable.** The quality paper summarizes SkillsBench and SWE-Skills-Bench findings that some curated skills help substantially while conflicting guidance can degrade performance ([arXiv 2607.01456, lines 64-67](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01456)). That makes owner-task tournaments a required promotion gate, not optional polish.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **Replace the whole harness with Superpowers or ECC.** Rejected. Superpowers is already used and does not cover the owner’s broad MCP/domain surface; ECC duplicates rejected memory/orchestration/guardrail components and is already present. Neither repository’s popularity is comparative evidence against the current harness.
- **Use a massive catalog as a local registry.** Rejected. Composio and Antigravity provide breadth but amplify license, provenance, trigger, context, filesystem, and Windows command-line risks. Remote discovery via an index plus explicit upstream review is leaner.
- **Adopt only official publisher skills.** Rejected as too conservative. Trail of Bits and focused community workflows show stronger domain validation than many broad official examples; community sources can be excellent when pinned and tested.
- **Ban all executable community skills.** Rejected as too restrictive. Deterministic scripts and tests are often a quality advantage. The correct boundary is review, pinning, least authority, fixture testing, and explicit approval for side effects.
- **Add a trained skill-quality critic or LLM judge service.** Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. Static checks plus a small fixed task suite provide interpretable evidence on the existing bus.
- **Treat missing tests as proof of low quality.** Rejected. Tests are one signal; prompt-only methods can still be useful. Missing tests lowers confidence and demands stronger task evaluation—it does not prove failure.
