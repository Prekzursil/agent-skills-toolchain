## Executive verdict

The analogy **partly transfers, but only as a control protocol, not as a worker-count formula**.

Nuclear operations has not solved the mathematical problem “the optimal crew size for an arbitrary job.” What it has solved is narrower and useful: how to add a second human check only where consequence warrants it; how to preserve that checker's independence; how to put several non-identical barriers between an initiating error and harm; how to stop when actual conditions depart from the expected operating envelope; and how to force a deliberate self-check around a consequential state transition. The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) handbook also explicitly says to avoid an excessive number of reviewers “to avoid team errors” (Volume 2, p. 49), a rare direct warning from the source field that adding people can make a process worse ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The concrete borrow is a **graded concurrency-control envelope**: admit workers in small waves only when they have demonstrably independent scopes; reserve a genuinely independent worker for verification when consequences are high; review two mechanically independent marginal-value signals after each wave; stop expanding when both signals show a plateau or when uncertainty invalidates the plan. This is a thin admission-and-review policy on the existing orchestration bus, not a second runtime.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this policy would reduce runaway fan-out, duplicate work, correlated “independent” answers, integration conflicts, and reviewer dilution in a general agent harness. Evidence: DOE distinguishes peer, concurrent, and independent verification, warns about complacency and excessive review, and requires independent observation; Anthropic reports early systems spawning 50 agents for simple questions, duplicate work from vague delegation, and about 15× chat token use; MAST finds multi-agent failures in system design, inter-agent alignment, and verification/termination ([DOE Volume 2, pp. 43-55](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf), [Anthropic multi-agent engineering report](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), [Cemri et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)). The magnitude is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the shadow-mode and randomized concurrency experiment specified in “Validation experiment,” using owner-representative tasks and matched compute**.

It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** valid to copy a universal “two-person rule” into “always run two agents.” Evidence: DOE grades verification by task and consequence, says organizations should select appropriate tools rather than use every tool, identifies dependence and complacency failure modes, and warns against excessive reviewers; Anthropic finds multi-agent systems a poor fit where work has many dependencies or requires shared context ([DOE Volume 2, pp. i, 43-55](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

## 1. What nuclear operations actually solved

### 1.1 Not one “two-person rule,” but a graded family of checking modes

The popular phrase “two-person rule” collapses mechanisms that nuclear human-performance guidance keeps separate. DOE Volume 2 documents at least four distinct work-team controls:

1. **Peer-checking:** a peer checks a performer's intended action around execution. DOE warns that the performer may act before the peer is ready, that each may assume the other will self-check, that the performer may become less attentive, and that over-use can make both complacent (p. 43). This is an error-prevention aid, not independent assurance ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
2. **Concurrent verification:** two people separately confirm a risk-important condition before, during, and after an action at the same time and place. DOE says true independence cannot be achieved because they work side by side; both should nevertheless preserve as much objectivity as possible. It is usually reserved for components where error could produce irreversible harm (pp. 44-45) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
3. **Independent verification (IV):** a second individual, separated from the performer in time and distance, determines the as-found equipment or document condition without relying on the performer's observation or oral confirmation. DOE says IV tends to catch errors more often than peer-checking or concurrent verification because the verifier did not make the change or product and their knowledge is not affected by the performer (pp. 46-47) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
4. **Peer review:** a qualified fresh set of eyes checks a risk-important work product or decision. DOE requires a defined purpose, scope, method, time allowance, and acceptance/rejection criteria. It says the reviewer should be equally or more qualified than the preparer, should not be involved in preparation, should not review while performing concurrent tasks, and that an excessive number of reviewers should be avoided to prevent team errors (pp. 49-50) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

This taxonomy answers two orchestration questions better than a slogan does:

- **What is the extra worker for?** Production capacity, an in-process check, an independent end-state check, or product review are different roles.
- **When is an extra worker no longer a safety layer?** When it shares the producer's cues, reasoning, inputs, or action; lacks qualification; assumes someone else will catch the problem; or joins a reviewer crowd with diffuse accountability.

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) independently recognizes these as operational human-performance categories: its coding definitions identify non-conservative failure to stop work, inadequate team coordination, inadequate independent verification/peer checking, and inadequate STAR self-checking as separate conditions ([NRC Human Factors Definition List, codes 190-197](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). This second source confirms the categories are not merely labels invented for this analogy.

### 1.2 Independence is a property of failure paths, not a head count

DOE defines IV independence as “freedom of thought” promoted by separation in time and distance; the verifier is not to see or hear the performer and is to inspect the as-found condition against the governing document (pp. 46-47). DOE also warns that a verifier who relies on a process indicator alone can miss alternate flow paths, and recommends a local check when possible in addition to remote indication (p. 47) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) generalizes the same structural idea. Defence in depth uses consecutive and independent protection levels so that one technical, human, or organizational failure cannot directly cause harm. It calls the independent effectiveness of levels necessary, while also using diversity and redundancy ([IAEA Safety Fundamentals SF-1, paras. 3.31-3.32](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/publications/PDF/Pub1273_web.pdf)). More detailed IAEA design guidance says failure at one level should not propagate to the next, calls for physical separation, electrical isolation, functional independence, and communication independence, and explicitly requires analysis of common-cause failures. It also states that perfect independence is impossible and dependencies must be identified and removed as far as reasonably practicable ([IAEA draft DS508, paras. 3.53-3.58](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/21/03/draft_ds508.pdf)).

The orchestration implication is not “two agents agree, therefore confidence doubles.” Two identical models with the same prompt, retrieved documents, tool failure, stale task state, and synthesis rubric can be two replicas of one failure path. The transferable unit is a **diverse evidence path**, not an agent process.

### 1.3 Conservative decision-making preserves the operating envelope

DOE defines a conservative decision as placing facility safety above near-term production. Its method is to clarify the goal, enumerate and analyze options, plan implementation, measure effectiveness, use contradictory data rather than discount it, minimize uncertainty, consider cumulative risk, and develop contingencies. It suggests assigning a devil's advocate to challenge team assumptions and oversights (pp. 63-65) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

“Pause when unsure” makes that principle operational: if uncertainty persists, stop, put the work site into a safe condition, notify supervision, and get knowledgeable help. DOE explicitly lists unexpected results, conditions outside the plan or procedure, missing knowledge, and another person's expressed concern as cues. It identifies “not having clear abort criteria” as an at-risk practice (pp. 16-17) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

This is not generic caution. It is a transition rule: when observations leave the assumptions under which the work was authorized, production pressure does not authorize improvisation.

### 1.4 STAR wraps each critical state transition in a local feedback loop

DOE's STAR self-check is **Stop, Think, Act, Review**. Before an important action, the performer pauses and removes distraction; predicts the correct component, action, and expected result against the governing document; acts; then verifies that the observed result matches the prediction and executes a contingency or escalates if it does not (pp. 18-19) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The important feature for orchestration is not the mnemonic. It is the explicit prediction before the irreversible or resource-expanding action, followed by observation after it. Without a stated expected contribution, an orchestrator cannot tell whether another worker helped.

## 2. Why this maps to parallel-agent orchestration

Parallel-worker admission is a state-changing action. It consumes budget, creates coordination edges, produces potentially conflicting state, and may widen tool/write authority. More workers can increase breadth, but the benefit depends on decomposition and independence.

Current external evidence shows both sides:

- Anthropic's production research system reports that a lead Opus 4 agent with Sonnet 4 subagents outperformed single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on its internal research evaluation. The benefit was strongest for breadth-first questions with independent directions. It normally launches 3-5 agents in parallel and reports research-time reductions of up to 90% for complex queries ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). These are vendor internal measurements, not independent general results.
- The same report says multi-agent systems consume about 15× the tokens of chats, are a poor fit for domains with shared context or many dependencies, and that early versions spawned 50 subagents for simple tasks, duplicated searches, or continued after sufficient evidence existed. Anthropic ultimately encoded effort bands: one agent for simple lookup, 2-4 for direct comparisons, and more than ten only for complex research with clearly divided responsibilities ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).
- A 2026 ACL study across MMLU-Pro and BBH found up to +7.1 accuracy points at 20× chain-of-thought compute and found debate and mixture-of-agents 1.3 and 2.7 points better than self-consistency at equal compute. It constructs a Pareto frontier rather than claiming monotonic benefit, and concludes that mixture-of-agents is most efficient when parallel generations outnumber sequential aggregation steps ([Wunderlich et al., ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.1/)). These are reasoning benchmarks, not long-horizon coding or state-mutating work.
- MAST analyzes 150+ traces across five multi-agent systems and identifies 14 modes in system design/specification, inter-agent misalignment, and task verification/termination. Examples include unproductive communication cycles that do not change the artifact and verifier agents that only compile code rather than test required behavior ([Cemri et al., 2025, sections 4.1-4.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)).

The shared structure is real: both nuclear work and multi-agent work involve fallible actors, state transitions, common-cause paths, production pressure, verification, and coordination overhead. The **likely (55-80%)** transferable principle is that worker count must be governed by task consequence, separability, and independence rather than a fixed maximum. Evidence is the mechanism match above plus DOE's explicit excessive-review warning and Anthropic's empirical fit/non-fit distinction. Its effectiveness across this owner's workload is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the matched-compute experiment below**.

## 3. Proposal [UNVERIFIED — settle with section 7]: the Concurrency Control Envelope

### 3.1 Spawn contract [UNVERIFIED — compare declarations with traces] — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Every fan-out request carries a small, inspectable contract:

```text
goal:                  the parent outcome
worker_role:           PRODUCER | PEER_CHECK | INDEPENDENT_VERIFY | REVIEWER
unique_scope:          what this worker alone owns
expected_delta:        evidence/artifact/test expected that existing workers will not produce
read_set:              known shared inputs
write_set:             files/resources/state it may mutate
acceptance_check:      observable end-state test
stop_or_abort_cues:    contradiction, tool failure, overlap, budget, stale state
independence_class:    same-path | context-isolated | evidence-diverse | mechanism-diverse
budget:                tool calls/tokens/time
```

Admission fails closed for **additional workers**, not for the entire task, when `unique_scope`, `expected_delta`, or `acceptance_check` is empty. The parent can continue with one worker or refine the plan. A mutating worker is not admitted if its prospective write set overlaps an active worker's write set unless the underlying operation is explicitly commutative or partitioned.

This is the orchestration analogue of deciding whether a task calls for a performer, concurrent checker, independent verifier, or reviewer. It also preserves accountability: ten producer labels cannot masquerade as ten independent verifications.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that requiring explicit unique scopes would expose obviously duplicative fan-out before execution. Evidence: the check is syntactic/semantic at the plan boundary, and Anthropic reports that vague task descriptions made subagents repeat the same search while detailed boundaries reduced duplication ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Whether an LLM can populate accurate prospective read/write sets is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by comparing declared sets with actual tool traces over the validation corpus**.

### 3.2 STAR admission loop [UNVERIFIED — shadow-mode experiment] — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Implement one pre-spawn/post-wave hook, not a daemon:

1. **Stop:** snapshot active workers, remaining budget, current accepted artifacts/evidence, unresolved requirements, and live write sets.
2. **Think:** for each proposed worker, state its unique scope, expected delta, acceptance check, and abort cues. Reject scope or write collisions. Classify the worker as producer or verification capacity.
3. **Act:** spawn one bounded wave through the existing worker mechanism.
4. **Review:** compare results with predictions and decide whether another wave has positive marginal value.

The hook records only a small wave ledger beside the normal run record. It does not schedule work itself; it returns `ADMIT`, `ADMIT_REDUCED(n)`, `HOLD_FOR_VERIFY`, or `STOP_EXPANSION(reason)` to the existing orchestrator.

The worker number is therefore adaptive:

- Start at **one producer** when the task cannot be partitioned into independent acceptance units.
- Admit **one worker per independent acceptance unit**, bounded by the harness's existing concurrency and budget limits.
- Admit a **second producer** for the same unit only when deliberate diversity is the purpose and the contract specifies a different evidence path or approach.
- For high-consequence mutation, spend the next slot on **independent verification**, not another producer.
- Expand in waves; never infer from “slots available” that work exists for them.

No universal numeric cap is proposed. Anthropic's 1, 2-4, 3-5, and >10 examples are task-specific production heuristics, not a transferable law ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). A universal cap would overfit one model, pricing regime, tool set, and workload.

The runtime policy is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent the most blatant spawn storms because it denies fan-out without distinct acceptance units and requires re-authorization after a wave. Evidence: Anthropic's reported 50-subagent failure was an effort-allocation error, and DOE's STAR/Pause methods put a deliberate check at the transition. The prediction is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by running the hook in shadow mode and counting proposed versus admitted workers, then auditing false denials**.

### 3.3 Dual-signal plateau detector [UNVERIFIED — labeled replay experiment] — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

After each wave, stop further expansion only when **two mechanically independent signals** agree that marginal value has ended:

**Signal A — semantic novelty from delivered work**

- Fraction of accepted claims, requirements, failing tests, files, or solution alternatives in the newest wave that were not present in the pre-wave ledger.
- Calculate against normalized artifacts/evidence, not worker self-reports.
- A result rejected by the acceptance check contributes zero accepted novelty.

**Signal B — operational productivity from execution records**

- Increment in passed acceptance checks or closed independent task units per unit of token/tool/time budget.
- Penalize coordination events that do not change accepted state: duplicate searches, conflicting writes, merge rework, retries caused by shared resource contention, and synthesis/hand-off overhead.
- Derive it from scheduler/tool/test records, not semantic similarity.

`STOP_EXPANSION` fires when both A and B fall below calibrated lower bounds for the newest wave. If only one fires, `HOLD_FOR_VERIFY`: do not claim a plateau; inspect a sample or run the independent acceptance test. This directly implements the mandatory two-signal discipline and mirrors DOE's preference for both a local hands-on check and independent indications rather than relying on a single process parameter (p. 47) ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The exact novelty and productivity thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with receiver-operating curves on labeled historical/replayed runs, optimizing separately for research, coding, and state-mutating task classes**. Until that experiment, shadow mode must report candidate stops but must not cancel workers automatically.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that two signals will be more robust than worker self-reported “done” or semantic duplication alone. Evidence: the signals observe different objects (artifact delta versus execution/acceptance delta), and MAST documents both unproductive interaction and inadequate task verification as distinct failure classes ([Cemri et al., 2025](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)). This confidence does not imply calibrated thresholds.

### 3.4 Independence-preserving verification [UNVERIFIED — seeded-defect experiment] — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

For risk-important outputs, the verifier receives:

- the original user requirement and governing constraints;
- the proposed end state or artifact;
- the acceptance criteria and permitted verification tools;
- no producer chain-of-thought, recommendation, confidence, or conversation before forming its own findings.

The verifier first reconstructs the expected end state independently, then observes the actual end state with a different mechanism where available: test rather than prose inspection, API read-back rather than mutation response, source retrieval rather than producer citation summary, filesystem state rather than success text. Only after recording its own observation may it compare notes with the producer. Verifier and producer outputs remain separately attributable.

For especially consequential actions, use diversity intentionally: a different model family, tool, query formulation, or deterministic checker. Merely starting a fresh context with the same faulty governing source is only context isolation, not mechanism diversity. The ledger must display the independence class rather than calling every additional call “independent.”

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this catches correlated confirmation and superficial review better than a reviewer exposed to the producer's reasoning. Evidence: DOE says time/distance separation promotes freedom of thought, prohibits reliance on performer cues, and warns against involving a reviewer in product preparation ([DOE Volume 2, pp. 46-50](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The transfer to LLMs is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with a seeded-defect trial comparing blind reconstruction, producer-rationale-visible review, and deterministic checks at equal compute**.

### 3.5 Three layers of defence [UNVERIFIED — extension-point inspection and trial] — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Use three layers that observe different failure surfaces:

1. **Prevent:** the spawn contract rejects missing unique scope, overlapping non-commutative writes, and unbudgeted waves.
2. **Detect:** the dual-signal review detects redundant or nonproductive expansion from artifact and execution evidence.
3. **Mitigate:** stop admitting workers, cancel only workers that have not crossed a declared critical mutation boundary, preserve artifacts, and route the current end state to independent verification or the owner.

This is defence in depth only if the layers are not all implemented as one LLM rereading its own summary. The admission check uses declared topology/write sets, detection uses actual artifacts plus tool/test records, and mitigation uses the existing scheduler/cancellation and artifact bus. IAEA explicitly warns that shared systems, shared communications, and human factors can defeat nominally separate levels ([IAEA DS508, paras. 3.53-3.58](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/21/03/draft_ds508.pdf)).

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this architecture can remain thin because all three actions are validation, observation, or cancellation at existing lifecycle boundaries rather than a second execution engine. Evidence: the design contains no independent scheduler, queue, durable runtime, model training, or external storage service. Integration with any particular harness bus is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by locating the actual pre-spawn, post-result, and cancellation extension points before implementation**.

## 4. What this would catch that ordinary “max workers” settings miss

1. **Runaway but legal fan-out.** A maximum of 20 still permits 20 useless workers. The contract asks whether there are 20 independent acceptance units; STAR rechecks after the first wave.
2. **Duplicate scopes with different wording.** Semantic novelty detects multiple workers returning the same evidence or touching the same requirement even when task labels differ.
3. **Common-cause consensus.** The independence class exposes five agents that all inherited the same context, source, and tool path as redundant replicas, not five confirming signals.
4. **Coordination work that produces no state change.** Operational productivity detects discussion, hand-offs, or retries that close no task unit and pass no new acceptance check, matching MAST's example of seven interaction cycles without a code update ([Cemri et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)).
5. **Concurrent write collisions.** Prospective and actual write sets catch workers whose tasks sound separable but mutate the same file, branch, configuration, ticket, or external resource.
6. **Reviewer dilution and social loafing.** Explicit roles and one accountable verifier avoid the DOE-described failure where each person assumes another will self-check, and the peer-review warning about excessive reviewer count ([DOE Volume 2, pp. 43-50](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
7. **Superficial verification.** Independent reconstruction plus end-state observation catches a “verifier” that merely repeats the producer or checks compilation while ignoring required behavior, the failure MAST describes ([Cemri et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)).
8. **False plateau from a broken detector.** The controller cannot claim diminishing returns from semantic novelty alone; actual acceptance/execution productivity must agree. A detector disagreement creates a hold and investigation, not a conclusion.
9. **Continuing outside the plan.** Unexpected tool errors, changed task state, invalidated assumptions, or contradictory evidence activate Pause When Unsure. The conservative action is to stop expansion and stabilize state, not blindly spawn more agents to “try harder.”
10. **Using all capacity because it exists.** Available concurrency is an upper resource bound, not evidence of marginal benefit. Admission follows independent work units and measured deltas.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 5.1 Consequence asymmetry

A reactor manipulation can produce immediate, irreversible physical harm. Most read-only agent research produces reversible text at much lower consequence. Blanket nuclear ceremony would dominate the work and reduce throughput. The transfer is therefore **graded**: one producer and ordinary self-check for low-consequence reversible work; independent verification for external mutation, destructive actions, security changes, expensive execution, or claims where falsehood is costly.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that blanket two-agent approval would be net harmful on low-consequence tasks. Evidence: DOE says its handbook is a menu and no organization is expected to use every tool, warns that over-used peer checks cause complacency, and says to avoid excessive reviewers; Anthropic measures roughly 15× chat token use for its multi-agent system ([DOE Volume 2, foreword and pp. 43, 49](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Exact owner-workload cost is **UNVERIFIED — settle it in the matched-compute experiment**.

### 5.2 Nuclear procedures assume a comparatively stable plant and validated controls

Nuclear operators work against engineered equipment, controlled procedures, known safe envelopes, qualification programs, and decades of operating experience. Agent tasks are open-ended, specifications shift, models are stochastic, and “correct end state” may be subjective. A spawn contract cannot manufacture a reliable acceptance test.

Therefore STAR can ensure that a prediction was made and checked, but cannot ensure the prediction or check was correct. Defence in depth can reduce single-path dependence but cannot eliminate correlated model or source error. DOE itself says procedures may contain hidden flaws and must not be followed blindly (pp. 20-21), while IAEA states complete independence cannot be achieved ([DOE Volume 2](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf), [IAEA DS508](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/21/03/draft_ds508.pdf)).

### 5.3 Humans and model instances fail differently

Physical separation reduces interpersonal cueing between humans. Two model instances can remain strongly correlated despite separate contexts because they share weights, training data, system prompts, tool descriptions, retrieval indexes, and upstream outages. “Time and distance” must be translated to **information and mechanism separation**, not process IDs.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that context isolation alone provides less independence than the human IV analogy suggests. Evidence: shared model/tool/source paths remain common causes by construction, and IAEA treats shared support systems and communication as dependencies. The degree of residual LLM correlation is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with seeded defects stratified by shared model, shared source, shared tool, and shared prompt**.

### 5.4 Conservative decision-making does not mean “always do less”

In nuclear operations, stopping preserves a designed safe state. In an agent harness, stopping can itself cause harm: an incomplete migration, half-applied edit, unhandled incident, or missed deadline. The conservative response to a plateau or uncertainty is not necessarily cancel-all. It is: stop **new admission**, prevent new mutations, let currently critical operations reach a declared safe checkpoint when interruption would be worse, snapshot state, then verify or escalate.

This nuance is essential. A crude kill switch copied from the source field would be a forced analogy and is rejected.

### 5.5 The source field does not yield an optimal N

DOE's guidance tells us when and how to check, not the marginal quality curve for language-model workers. Anthropic's worker ranges are empirical heuristics for research, and the ACL Pareto results concern MMLU-Pro and BBH. Neither supplies a universal concurrency function for coding, operations, or mixed tool use.

Any proposed constants before workload measurement are **UNVERIFIED — settle them by randomized worker-count arms for each task class**. This unit deliberately proposes a measurement-and-admission mechanism rather than inventing `N=4`.

## 6. Rejected borrows and dead ends

### Rejected: always require two agents

This confuses a head count with independent verification, doubles low-value work, and can amplify common-cause error. DOE's own taxonomy and cautions contradict it.

### Rejected: majority vote as independent verification

A vote counts outputs but does not establish diverse evidence paths or a correct end state. MAST notes that straightforward majority voting or resampling interventions can be inconsistent and argues verification must be domain-specific ([Cemri et al., section 5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)). A deterministic acceptance check or blind reconstruction is more analogous to the verifier examining as-found equipment.

### Rejected: one LLM “safety officer” deciding all admissions and completion

That creates a single common-cause layer and violates defence-in-depth independence. The proposed dual signals use distinct data surfaces, and the safety policy is deterministic where possible.

### Rejected: fixed fleet sizes copied from Anthropic

Anthropic's 3-5 concurrent subagents and effort bands are evidence that explicit scaling guidance helps its research product, not a general optimum. Its own article says coding is less parallelizable and synchronous subagents create information bottlenecks ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

### Rejected: new orchestration runtime or durable workflow engine

The control can ride pre-spawn/post-wave hooks, existing task records, and existing cancellation. A second runtime would increase common-cause and integration surface while violating the anti-overfit constraint. No evidence found in this lens justifies it.

### Rejected: observability SaaS, vector/KG memory, trained critic, or guardrail framework

None is required to record a small wave ledger, compute artifact/test deltas, or gate a spawn. These components are explicitly outside the anti-overfit gate and do not solve the central independence problem.

## 7. Validation experiment [UNVERIFIED thresholds] — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

### 7.1 Detector controls before any claim

Before evaluating orchestration outcomes, prove each detector can see a known-present control:

- Seed two workers with an identical artifact paragraph and confirm semantic novelty flags overlap.
- Seed two workers with disjoint accepted artifacts and confirm it does not flag overlap.
- Run a worker that spends tool calls but changes no accepted artifact/test and confirm operational productivity reports zero accepted delta.
- Run a worker that closes a known failing test and confirm productivity reports a positive delta.
- Seed overlapping write sets and confirm admission blocks them; seed disjoint sets and confirm admission permits them.
- Expose one verifier to producer rationale and blind another; confirm the ledger records different independence classes.

No “absent,” “broken,” “plateau,” or “done” conclusion is allowed until both a positive and negative control pass for the relevant detector.

### 7.2 Shadow mode

Replay or observe owner-representative tasks without changing scheduling. Record proposed contract, admission decision, predicted delta, actual unique accepted delta, execution productivity, conflicts, token/tool/time cost, and final quality. Human review labels whether each denied worker would have added material value.

Shadow thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle them from these labels, separately by task class and consequence tier**. Do not pool read-only research with shared-state code edits.

### 7.3 Randomized matched-compute trial

Stratify tasks by breadth, dependency density, mutation consequence, and availability of deterministic acceptance tests. Randomize within strata:

- single strong agent;
- fixed two-worker fan-out;
- fixed four-worker fan-out;
- current/native orchestration;
- concurrency-control envelope;
- envelope plus blind independent verifier for high-consequence tasks.

Match total model/tool compute in one analysis and allow natural cost in a second. Measure:

- owner-judged end-state quality and requirement coverage;
- deterministic test/acceptance pass rate where available;
- unique accepted contribution per additional worker;
- duplicate-work fraction;
- write conflicts and integration rework;
- tokens, tool calls, wall time, and synthesis time;

## 10. Evidence quality and gaps

- DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2 is an official public DOE good-practice handbook grounded in DOE and commercial nuclear experience, but it explicitly says it is illustrative, not definitive, and not a requirements document ([DOE landing page](https://www.energy.gov/ehss/articles/doe-hdbk-1028-2009), [Volume 2 foreword](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). I therefore use it for mechanisms, not quantitative effect sizes.
- IAEA SF-1 is authoritative safety-principle guidance. DS508 is explicitly a draft guide, so it supports the explanation of independence/common-cause reasoning but is not represented as a final binding standard ([IAEA SF-1](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/publications/PDF/Pub1273_web.pdf), [IAEA draft DS508](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/21/03/draft_ds508.pdf)).
- NRC's definition list independently confirms the operational categories but is a coding taxonomy, not evidence that any particular control reduces event rates ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)).
