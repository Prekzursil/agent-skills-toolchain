# X180 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → harness observability without a baseline

**Unit:** X180  
**Lens:** experimental design (pre-registration, power analysis, blinding, negative controls)  
**Target:** deciding whether a harness change helped when no trustworthy baseline exists  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external methodological research and concrete design only. Per the unit instruction, this report does not inspect or make claims about the local harness repository.

## Executive verdict

The borrow transfers, but the transferable mechanism is narrower than “apply scientific rigor” and more demanding than “add observability.” The useful mechanism is a **prospective, concurrently controlled, randomized harness trial with a frozen decision protocol, concealed allocation, blinded judgment where judgment exists, and built-in negative and positive controls for the measurement system**.

The key move is to stop looking for a missing historical baseline. Experimental design creates the relevant baseline prospectively: eligible work arriving during the same time window is randomly assigned to the current harness or the candidate harness. That concurrent control estimates the counterfactual under the same task mix, model release, upstream service conditions, owner behavior, and calendar time. CONSORT's methodological explanation says properly implemented randomization eliminates selection bias in treatment assignment, balances known and unknown prognostic factors in expectation, and permits probability-based assessment of chance differences ([CONSORT explanation and elaboration](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2844943/)). FDA's platform-trial guidance gives the directly relevant reason to prefer concurrent controls: nonconcurrent controls can be biased by temporal shifts in participant characteristics, trial conduct, or standard of care ([FDA master-protocol guidance recap](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/guidances-drugs/guidance-recap-podcast-master-protocols-drug-and-biological-product-development)). In the harness setting, model versions, MCP availability, task mix, and operator expectations are the analogues of those time-varying conditions.

**Bottom line:** it is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the causal protection of concurrent randomization described by CONSORT and FDA plus the established use of randomized online experiments in software ([Kohavi et al., controlled experiments on the web](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1)), that this design gives a more trustworthy answer than an unrandomized before/after dashboard when both harness variants can be run concurrently. This confidence is about the *design's comparative validity*, not about whether any particular harness change helps.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that the first candidate change tested under this design will improve end-to-end owner success; the evidence is deliberately uninformative because no candidate, task population, effect estimate, or baseline variance is supplied in this ideation unit. The experiment exists to replace that ignorance with evidence.

The design does **not** make all harness evaluation problems experimental. It cannot by itself establish that metrics represent owner value, that the enrolled tasks represent future tasks, that treatment does not leak across sessions, or that a rare catastrophic failure is acceptably bounded. Those disanalogies are material and are addressed below.

## 1. What experimental design has already solved

### 1.1 Concurrent randomized control: manufacture the missing counterfactual

#### In its own field

A causal comparison needs the outcome that would have occurred under the alternative treatment, but that outcome is unobservable for the same experimental unit. Random allocation creates groups that are exchangeable in expectation, so the contemporaneous control group's outcomes can estimate that missing counterfactual. Proper allocation concealment prevents the person enrolling units from steering particular units into a preferred arm. CONSORT distinguishes sequence generation, allocation concealment, and implementation, and requires reporting the mechanism used to conceal assignments until intervention allocation ([CONSORT 2010 statement, items 8–10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2844940/)). The elaboration notes empirical evidence that inadequately or unclearly concealed allocation is associated with larger estimated effects ([CONSORT explanation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2844943/)).

A concurrent control is not merely a convention. FDA warns that nonconcurrent control data can introduce systematic differences and bias through temporal changes ([FDA master-protocol guidance recap](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/guidances-drugs/guidance-recap-podcast-master-protocols-drug-and-biological-product-development)). This is exactly why “last month's sessions versus this month's sessions” is not a baseline substitute when the environment drifts.

#### Translation to a harness

Define the experimental unit before assignment. The default should be an entire owner task/session, not an individual tool call. Hash a stable task/session identifier with an experiment-specific secret salt and map it to `control` or `candidate`. Record eligibility before revealing the assignment. Keep that task on the assigned arm until completion. Stratify or block only on a small set of pre-treatment variables fixed in the protocol, such as task family and risk tier; never stratify on post-assignment behavior.

The estimand should be explicit:

> Among eligible owner tasks during the experiment window, what is the intention-to-treat difference between assignment to candidate and assignment to current harness in the predeclared primary outcome, with predeclared guardrails for severe failure, cost, and latency?

“Intention-to-treat” means analyze by the assigned arm even if routing failed or a fallback occurred. A separate per-protocol diagnostic may explain failures but cannot replace the primary analysis, because silently dropping failed treatment applications would reward broken routing.

This catches what a raw observability dashboard misses:

* apparent improvement caused by an easier post-change task mix;
* apparent regression caused by a model or upstream service outage coinciding with rollout;
* selective routing of difficult tasks away from the candidate;
* survivorship bias from analyzing only completed sessions;
* fallback behavior that makes a candidate look good only because failures are excluded;
* calendar-time learning by the owner or agents;
* regression-to-the-mean after a change was motivated by an unusually bad week.

### 1.2 Pre-registration: freeze the decision before outcomes can move it

#### In its own field

Preregistration timestamps the hypotheses, sampling plan, measures, exclusions, stopping rule, and analysis before the relevant outcome data are inspected. Its core value is not ceremonial prediction; it makes confirmatory analysis distinguishable from exploration. A detailed preregistration supports valid interpretation by exposing whether hypotheses and analyses were chosen after seeing results ([van den Akker et al., *Ensuring the quality and specificity of preregistrations*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7725296/)). Registered Reports go further: study questions and methods receive review before collection, and a methodologically sound protocol can receive in-principle acceptance independent of whether the eventual result is positive ([Scientific Reports Registered Reports policy](https://www.nature.com/srep/journal-policies/registered-reports)).

Preregistration is not proof of a good design. It can freeze a bad metric or vague analysis. Published critiques explicitly argue it is neither sufficient nor necessary for good science ([Pham & Oh, 2021](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jcpy.1209)). Therefore the harness analogue must pair a frozen record with reviewable definitions, raw-enough event data, and declared deviations.

#### Translation to a harness

For each candidate, create an immutable, content-addressed experiment manifest *before assignment starts*. It must contain:

1. candidate and control configuration digests;
2. eligibility and exclusion rules, written only in terms of pre-assignment facts;
3. randomization unit, allocation ratio, salt commitment, stratification, and start time;
4. primary outcome with exact numerator, denominator, aggregation unit, missing-data treatment, and direction of benefit;
5. a small set of safety/cost/latency guardrails;
6. the minimum practically important effect, not merely “any significant effect”;
7. sample-size method, maximum sample, fixed analysis time, and any emergency harm stop;
8. planned estimator, uncertainty interval, multiplicity treatment, and subgroup analyses;
9. all negative controls, the detector-sensitivity sentinel, and failure thresholds;
10. ship / reject / continue-learning rule;
11. a deviation ledger rule: changes are allowed, but are timestamped and the affected analysis is relabeled exploratory.

The manifest prevents metric shopping (“errors did not improve, but token count did”), optional stopping after a favorable fluctuation, post-hoc task exclusions, and retrospective redefinition of success. It does not prevent a team from preregistering twenty convenient metrics; a protocol review gate must reject that design before launch.

### 1.3 Power analysis and an internal pilot: make “no detectable difference” interpretable

#### In its own field

Power analysis connects sample size, outcome variance or event rate, significance threshold, and the minimum effect the design is intended to detect. It can solve for power, required sample size, or minimum detectable effect ([Bausell & Li, *Power Analysis for Experimental Research*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511541933.004)). Without this step, a null result can mean either “the effect is too small to matter” or “the study was too noisy to see a valuable effect.”

When nuisance parameters such as outcome variance are not known, experimental design offers blinded sample-size re-estimation using pooled interim outcomes without revealing treatment effects. FDA describes sample-size adaptation based entirely on pooled outcome data as blinded sample-size re-estimation ([FDA adaptive-design guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)). The purpose is to update a nuisance parameter without letting an attractive interim treatment difference dictate the stopping rule.

#### Translation to a harness with no baseline

Start with a minimum practically important difference derived from the rollout decision. Examples are “at least three fewer owner corrections per 100 eligible tasks,” “at least a 20% relative reduction in terminal tool-failure tasks,” or a noninferiority margin on task success paired with a meaningful cost decrease. Do not derive this threshold from whatever effect the first data happen to show.

If the outcome variance/event rate is unknown, use one of two preregistered paths:

* **Conservative fixed design:** for a binary primary outcome, use a conservative variance assumption and accept the resulting larger sample.
* **Blinded internal pilot:** after a fixed first tranche, compute only the pooled event rate or pooled variance with arm labels hidden; update the required sample within a preregistered minimum and maximum; do not calculate or reveal the arm difference. Include the pilot observations in the final analysis under the prespecified method.

Power must be calculated at the randomization/analysis unit. Thousands of tool calls inside tens of sessions are not thousands of independent observations. If the candidate changes an error that clusters by task or repository, simulations must preserve that cluster structure.

This catches “no regression observed” claims based on tiny samples and stops high-volume low-level events from creating fake precision. It also turns an infeasible evaluation into an honest statement: if the available sample cannot detect the minimum useful effect, do not claim equivalence.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that ordinary task-volume experiments can tightly bound very rare catastrophic harms; the evidence is the arithmetic of rare events—when the expected count is near zero, the confidence bound remains wide even if common outcomes are well powered. Such harms need deterministic safety checks, adversarial suites, or much longer surveillance rather than a casual “zero observed” claim.

### 1.4 Blinding and allocation concealment: protect judgment, not bytes

#### In its own field

Blinding can apply to participants, intervention providers, outcome assessors, and analysts; allocation concealment protects assignment before treatment, while blinding protects behavior and assessment afterward. CONSORT explicitly asks who was blinded and how ([CONSORT 2010 statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2844940/)). NIH's rigor guidance likewise asks for allocation concealment, blinded outcome assessment, randomization/stratification, exclusions, missing data, and reporting of all results ([NINDS rigorous study design](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/funding/preparing-your-application/preparing-research-plan/rigorous-study-design-and-transparent-reporting)).

The empirical literature is not uniform. A 2020 meta-epidemiological study found no average evidence of effect-estimate differences by blinding status across its included meta-analyses ([Moustgaard et al., BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.l6802)), while a 2025 within-trial analysis reported substantial exaggeration for subjective outcomes assessed without blinding ([Hróbjartsson et al., Journal of Clinical Epidemiology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0895435625001209)). The correct borrow is therefore targeted: blind subjective adjudication when feasible; do not pretend blinding is universally effective or possible.

#### Translation to a harness

The agent cannot be blinded to a changed instruction or tool; the intervention works by changing what the agent sees or can do. The owner may also infer the variant from behavior. Full double blinding does not transfer.

Three narrower protections do transfer:

* conceal the allocation salt and upcoming assignment from whoever decides task eligibility;
* present human adjudicators with normalized task input/output transcripts labeled `A`/`B`, removing explicit configuration names and change markers, then reveal mapping only after scores are locked;
* have the analysis job compute and sign the declared tables before the arm-name mapping is revealed to the decision maker.

Blinding adds little to immutable objective events such as process exit status or billed tokens; deterministic metric code and auditability matter more there. Transcript redaction can itself remove evidence needed to judge quality, so the protocol must state exactly what was removed and run a small adjudication-feasibility pilot.

### 1.5 Negative controls: test the evaluator's assumptions, not only the candidate

#### In its own field

A negative-control exposure or outcome is chosen because it should have no causal relation of the target kind but is subject to relevant sources of confounding or measurement bias. A detected association then warns that the study or analysis can generate an effect where none should exist. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen distinguish negative-control exposures and outcomes and describe the conditions under which they reveal confounding and bias ([Lipsitch et al., *Epidemiology*, 2010](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-21f6-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)). Negative controls are falsification devices: a failure can invalidate an analysis, while a pass cannot prove that all bias is absent.

#### Translation to a harness

Use three complementary controls:

1. **A/A routing control.** A small, separately declared lane assigns two labels whose effective harness configuration digests are byte-identical. A nonzero effect beyond the declared false-positive tolerance, arm-dependent missingness, or sample-ratio mismatch points to routing, logging, selection, or analysis defects—not a harness benefit.
2. **Placebo assignment.** Generate a second independent deterministic label for every eligible task, never feed it to routing, and run the frozen analysis against it. Association with outcomes indicates a join, time-bucketing, repeated-unit, or analysis problem. It is not guaranteed to share every confounder and therefore is diagnostic, not absolution.
3. **Pre-treatment invariant outcomes.** Test whether assigned arm predicts facts fixed before assignment, such as input byte length, declared task family, or eligibility timestamp bucket. Material differences beyond randomization expectations can expose allocation corruption, post-assignment eligibility edits, or lossy joins.

Add a **positive detector-sensitivity sentinel**, which is not a negative control: inject a synthetic, non-owner canary with a known, unmistakable event into the evaluation stream and require the detector to find it. This meets the stronger operational standard that a “clean” detector must first demonstrate it can detect a known-present condition. For example, the canary can emit a unique terminal status and a fixed transcript token; check those through two mechanically independent paths.

This control set catches failures that treatment/control outcome comparison alone misses: broken randomization, asymmetric telemetry loss, incorrect arm joins, post-treatment filtering, metric code that always returns zero, and a dashboard that silently drops failed sessions. Microsoft's experimentation work identifies sample-ratio mismatch as a high-value symptom of diverse data-quality failures and warns that ignoring it can reverse ship decisions ([Fabijan et al., Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/diagnosing-sample-ratio-mismatch-in-online-controlled-experiments-a-taxonomy-and-rules-of-thumb-for-practitioners/)); separate Microsoft work explains how telemetry loss can bias experimental estimates ([Gupchup et al., *Trustworthy Experimentation Under Telemetry Loss*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12470)).

## 2. Concrete harness design

### Proposal A — experiment manifest and decision contract

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Add a versioned JSON/YAML manifest schema and require a content hash before enrollment. This is deliberately configuration rather than a service. The existing harness launcher or hook reads the frozen fields; analysis refuses data whose experiment ID, manifest hash, or configuration digest is missing.

Minimum schema:

```text
experiment_id
hypothesis
unit_of_randomization
eligibility_pre_assignment
control_digest / candidate_digest
allocation_ratio / salt_commitment / strata
primary_estimand
primary_metric {event_source, numerator, denominator, aggregation, missingness}
guardrails[]
minimum_practical_effect
power {method, assumptions, pilot_n, min_n, max_n}
stopping {fixed_horizon, emergency_harm_rule}
negative_controls[]
positive_sentinel
analysis_version / multiplicity_rule
decision_rule
deviation_ledger
```

The manifest alone does not create causal evidence. Its job is to make undisclosed researcher degrees of freedom mechanically visible.

### Proposal B — concurrent assignment and exposure ledger — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Implement a deterministic assignment hook and append-only exposure records on the existing event bus. The premise that the bus has an early-enough event to record eligibility before routing and can keep one session sticky is **UNVERIFIED; the “pre-routing hook and sticky-session spike” experiment settles it by routing 100 synthetic session IDs twice, confirming identical assignments, confirming the eligibility record precedes the configuration-load record, and comparing the loaded configuration digest with the assigned digest**. Because this premise affects the proposal, the heading carries `UNVERIFIED`.

Each record should include experiment ID, manifest hash, stable unit ID hash, eligibility timestamp, arm pseudonym, expected configuration digest, observed configuration digest, stratum, fallback status, and terminal/missing outcome status. Do not log secrets or raw owner content merely for experimentation.

An implementation under roughly 200 lines is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the narrow responsibilities—hash assignment, schema validation, sticky lookup, and event append—but this estimate remains subordinate to the named routing spike because hook semantics and current event formats were intentionally not inspected in this unit.

### Proposal C — frozen analysis and trust checks

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Create a single-purpose analysis command that consumes manifest plus event records and emits:

* enrollment flow: eligible, assigned, exposed, terminal, missing, analyzed;
* expected versus observed allocation, including per-stratum sample-ratio checks;
* assignment-versus-observed-digest mismatch;
* missing outcome rate by arm;
* primary intention-to-treat effect with an uncertainty interval;
* guardrails, clearly separated from exploratory metrics;
* A/A, placebo-assignment, and pre-treatment invariant results;
* positive-sentinel recovery from two independent event paths;
* manifest deviations and analysis code digest;
* a decision of `ship`, `reject`, or `inconclusive` computed from the declared rule.

The command should fail closed to `inconclusive` on an unexplained sample-ratio mismatch, missing positive sentinel, mismatched configuration digest, or arm-differential telemetry loss. “Fail closed” here affects only the experiment conclusion, not live harness availability.

### Proposal D — blinded outcome packet for subjective success — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Generate paired, arm-pseudonymous adjudication packets for tasks where objective completion is unavailable. Use a predeclared rubric: goal attainment, correctness, unauthorized side effects, need for owner correction, and severity. Lock ratings before revealing mappings.

Whether redaction can conceal treatment without destroying information needed for judgment is **UNVERIFIED; the “blinding integrity and rubric feasibility pilot” settles it by having assessors guess the arm and rate judgeability on a held-out packet set, while a separate reviewer compares scores using full versus redacted evidence**. Because this feasibility premise affects the recommendation, the heading carries `UNVERIFIED`.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce expectancy-driven scoring when output quality is genuinely subjective, based on CONSORT's rationale and the 2025 within-trial evidence for observer bias in subjective assessments; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** to remain blinded for conspicuous instruction or tool changes, because the output can reveal the intervention. Both conclusions must be reported, not averaged into a fiction of full blinding.

### Proposal E — repeated confirmation before “done”

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Encode the decision rule so “candidate helped” requires at least two mechanically independent signals:

1. the predeclared primary effect from randomized assignment; and
2. an independently sourced confirmation appropriate to the claim—e.g., a blinded human task-success rating when the primary is event-based, or an objective terminal outcome when the primary is human-rated.

In addition, require a clean routing/telemetry validity bundle: allocation ratio plus observed configuration digests, and end-event reconciliation plus the known-present positive sentinel. These pairs use distinct fields and failure mechanisms; rerunning the same query is not confirmation.

For high-impact replacement decisions, run a fresh confirmatory block with a new salt and later calendar window after the first positive block. The second block tests temporal portability and avoids turning exploratory findings into a one-shot shipping claim. Replication is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce the chance that a favorable random fluctuation or one transient upstream condition is called a durable improvement; the evidence is its independent randomization and later time block, though the exact reduction depends on prespecified error rates and is not estimated here.

## 3. End-to-end operating protocol

1. **State the decision.** Identify candidate, control, eligible task population, risk exclusions, and what operational decision follows each result.
2. **Choose the unit.** Prefer session/task. Use owner/repository clusters if a session can influence later work in the same repository. Never claim tool calls are independent merely because they are numerous.
3. **Define one primary estimand.** Include direction, aggregation, missingness, fallback treatment, and uncertainty interval.
4. **Set minimum practical effect and guardrails.** Separate “worth shipping” from “statistically nonzero.” Treat safety noninferiority separately from efficacy superiority where necessary.
5. **Design controls.** Concurrent randomized control, A/A lane, placebo assignment, pre-treatment invariants, and known-present sentinel.
6. **Plan sample size.** Use conservative assumptions or the blinded pooled internal pilot; cap exposure. If infeasible, label the experiment underpowered before it runs.
7. **Freeze and hash.** Commit the manifest, metric implementation version, randomization commitment, and analysis command digest before first eligibility record.
8. **Enroll and conceal.** Record eligibility before assignment revelation; keep assignment sticky; log expected and observed digests.
9. **Monitor only safety and experiment integrity.** Do not repeatedly inspect efficacy unless a sequential design with valid thresholds was preregistered. Emergency stops are for predeclared harm, not early celebration.
10. **Lock subjective judgments.** Blind adjudication where feasible; log unblinding guesses and judgeability.
11. **Run validity checks first.** If A/A, SRM, digest, missingness, or sentinel checks fail, the result is `inconclusive`, even if the candidate looks excellent.
12. **Run frozen confirmatory analysis.** Report absolute effects, relative effects where meaningful, uncertainty, counts, missingness, all guardrails, and all deviations.
13. **Separate exploration.** Unexpected subgroup or metric findings become hypotheses for a new experiment, not evidence for the current ship decision.
14. **Replicate material wins.** Use a new salt/time block, particularly for full-replacement decisions.
15. **Continue post-ship surveillance.** Randomized evidence answers the enrolled-window estimand; it does not guarantee performance under future distribution or model changes.

## 4. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 4.1 Why the analogy does transfer

The relevant causal structure is shared:

* there is an intervention (harness candidate);
* there are repeatable experimental units (tasks/sessions);
* each unit exposes only one realized outcome, leaving a missing counterfactual;
* assignment can often occur before the outcome;
* outcomes and nuisance conditions vary;
* observers and analysts have incentives or expectations that can bias judgment;
* telemetry and selection defects can manufacture effects.

Software experimentation already demonstrates that randomized controlled designs can operate on live digital systems, including concerns about power, sample size, variance reduction, metric interpretation, and data-quality failures ([Kohavi et al. practical guide](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1); [Fabijan et al. SRM taxonomy](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/diagnosing-sample-ratio-mismatch-in-online-controlled-experiments-a-taxonomy-and-rules-of-thumb-for-practitioners/)). Thus the mapping is not metaphor alone.

### 4.2 Where the analogy breaks or weakens

#### The unit may not be independent

Harnesses can write files, update memory, alter repositories, or teach the owner. One arm's work may change later tasks assigned to the other arm. This violates the no-interference assumption more readily than many parallel clinical trials. Mitigation is cluster assignment by repository/owner/time block or isolated replay, but cluster randomization reduces effective sample size. If contamination cannot be bounded, a simple session-level A/B estimate is not causal.

#### Treatment is not stable

A “harness” includes model, prompts, skills, MCPs, external services, repository state, and fallbacks. If candidate digest does not capture the effective treatment or external components change mid-run, the treatment has multiple versions. Concurrent control helps with common drift, but only observed configuration digests and exposure logs reveal asymmetric treatment drift.

#### Outcomes are multidimensional and partially subjective

Clinical endpoints can also be complex, but harness “success” is especially susceptible to proxy gaming. Lower tool errors can coexist with worse goal attainment; lower latency can result from premature stopping. No amount of randomization validates a bad metric. Independent owner-centered adjudication and explicit guardrails are required.

#### The agent cannot be blinded

An instruction/tool intervention is cognitively available to the agent by construction. Performance bias cannot be removed the way a matched placebo might remove it. Only allocation concealment, assessor blinding, analyst blinding, and sham/A/A instrumentation checks transfer.

#### Carryover and learning are first-order effects

The owner changes prompts after seeing behavior; repositories retain artifacts; model providers update systems. A crossover design that sends the “same” live task through both arms can be impossible or unethical after the first arm changes state. Paired replay is useful for deterministic, isolated benchmarks but may not represent live work.

#### Heavy-tailed and rare harms defeat naïve power

One destructive event can dominate thousands of routine successes. Mean-based power and a single composite score can hide this. Severe harms need event-specific guardrails, deterministic prevention, adversarial testing, and conservative exposure caps.

#### Enrollment changes the system

Extra logging, transcript normalization, or human scoring can add latency and alter behavior. The experiment bus must be thin, and A/A measurement of overhead must precede treatment conclusions.

### 4.3 Transfer verdict by mechanism

| Mechanism | Transfer verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Concurrent randomized control | Strong, when sticky assignment and interference control are possible | It directly constructs a contemporaneous counterfactual under drift. |
| Preregistration | Strong as an audit/decision constraint, weak as quality assurance alone | It exposes outcome-dependent choices but can freeze a bad plan. |
| Power / minimum detectable effect | Strong for common, well-defined outcomes; weak for rare catastrophe | It makes nulls interpretable but cannot manufacture rare events or independence. |
| Allocation concealment | Strong | It prevents eligibility/routing choices from anticipating arm. |
| Agent/owner blinding | Usually weak or impossible | The treatment is visible through instructions, tools, or behavior. |
| Outcome-assessor/analyst blinding | Moderate, outcome-dependent | Useful for subjective judgment; irrelevant to immutable machine events and vulnerable to treatment clues. |
| Negative controls | Strong as falsification, weak as validation | A failure exposes evaluator defects; a pass never proves their absence. |
| Internal pilot | Moderate to strong if blinded and preregistered | It estimates nuisance variance without selecting a favorable treatment result. |

## 5. What not to build

The proposal deliberately does not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It stores a small manifest and appends experiment events on the existing bus. Analysis is a bounded command, not a resident service.

A learned “judge” is not proposed as the primary outcome because it would introduce a changing, potentially treatment-sensitive measurement model that itself needs calibration and versioning. A model judge may be exploratory, but it cannot replace objective events or blinded human adjudication until separately validated.

A historical synthetic control is not the default. It adds modeling assumptions exactly where the problem statement says there is no baseline. If concurrent assignment is impossible, a prospective interrupted time series or matched replay may be useful for hypothesis generation, but the causal claim must be labeled observational. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that statistical adjustment alone will recover all relevant drift in a rapidly changing harness environment; the evidence is FDA's warning about temporal bias in nonconcurrent controls and the number of plausibly changing factors in the target system.

An always-on autonomous experimentation daemon is not proposed. The marginal value is unproven, and it would violate the anti-overfit gate by creating a second runtime. A manifest, hook, and offline command are sufficient to test the design.

## 6. Failure modes and explicit stop conditions

Do not conclude that the candidate helped if any of the following remains unexplained:

* expected allocation differs materially from observed enrollment (sample-ratio mismatch);
* expected and observed configuration digests disagree;
* eligibility was recorded after assignment revelation;
* terminal/missing outcomes differ by arm and the primary missing-data rule cannot bound the result;
* the A/A lane shows an arm effect or asymmetric missingness beyond its declared tolerance;
* the unused placebo label predicts outcomes;
* assigned arm predicts immutable pre-treatment facts beyond randomization expectations;
* either positive-sentinel path cannot detect its known-present marker;
* human adjudicators cannot judge redacted packets or identify the arm far above chance through intervention clues;
* the sample is below the preregistered requirement;
* unplanned exclusions, metric definitions, or stopping decisions affect the result;
* severe-harm guardrails cross their stopping boundary;
* one arm contaminates the other through shared state.

An isolated negative-control failure does not identify a unique root cause. It says the experiment is not yet trustworthy. Diagnosis can then use arm exposure records, raw event reconciliation, timing, and joins; the confirmatory clock restarts only after correction under a new experiment ID.

## 7. Expected value and limits, stated honestly

* The combined design is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch more false “improvement” claims than a before/after dashboard, because concurrent randomization targets time/task confounding, preregistration targets outcome-dependent analysis, and the controls target routing/telemetry defects through different mechanisms. This is a comparative methodological claim supported by CONSORT, FDA concurrent-control guidance, Lipsitch et al., and Microsoft's SRM/telemetry-loss research.
* It is **likely (55-80%)** to fit as a thin extension on an existing event bus, because its persistent state is a manifest plus exposure/outcome records and its active logic is deterministic routing and validation. Actual fit is constrained by the explicitly UNVERIFIED pre-routing hook premise and must be settled by the named spike.
* It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that negative controls alone can certify the evaluator as unbiased; Lipsitch et al.'s mechanism is falsification under specified causal assumptions, not a proof that every bias is absent.
* It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that blinding the owner or agent is sustainable for material harness changes, because the intervention is often visible in instructions, available tools, latency, or behavior. Targeted assessor and analyst blinding remains useful.
* It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that any single omnibus “task success” score will remain stable across task families without family-specific rubric evidence; tasks are heterogeneous and no validation study is provided. The “rubric transport validation” experiment would settle this by testing agreement and predictive relevance separately across preregistered task families on a held-out set. Until then, any recommendation relying on that omnibus score would need an inline `UNVERIFIED` label.
* It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a concurrent control consumes some short-term opportunity to expose all tasks to the candidate; the evidence is the allocation itself. That cost is the price of identifying the counterfactual and can be reduced with a bounded experiment, not wished away.

## 8. Minimal first experiment

The first use should validate the evaluator, not a glamorous harness replacement.

1. Choose a harmless candidate expected to have no meaningful behavioral effect.
2. Run an A/A lane with byte-identical effective configurations.
3. Enable placebo assignment and two pre-treatment invariant checks.
4. Inject the known-present sentinel and verify it through two independent event paths.
5. Measure experiment-bus overhead on latency and missingness.
6. Run the frozen analysis and demand `inconclusive` for deliberately corrupted copies: drop 10% of one arm's terminal events, alter several observed digests, and post-filter failures.
7. Only after the evaluator catches each seeded defect should it evaluate a real candidate.

The evaluator is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect the deliberately large corruptions in this calibration if the checks are implemented as specified; the evidence is that each corruption directly violates an explicit invariant. Detection of subtler real-world failures remains unproven and should be measured by progressively smaller seeded faults rather than assumed.

## 10. Final recommendation

Adopt the experimental-design borrow as a **counterfactual-and-validity layer**, not as another monitoring stack:

* prospectively create the absent baseline with a concurrent randomized holdout;
* pre-register the outcome, exclusions, stopping, power, and decision rule;
* conceal allocation and blind only those human/analytic steps where knowledge can change judgment;
* run A/A, placebo-assignment, pre-treatment invariant, sample-ratio, digest, and telemetry-completeness checks;
* prove detector sensitivity with a known-present sentinel through two mechanically independent paths;
* require an independent outcome signal and later replication before calling a material replacement “done”;
* treat failed controls, insufficient power, and unresolved interference as `inconclusive`, not as a loss or win.

This recommendation passes the anti-overfit gate: every component is configuration or a small extension riding the existing hook/event/skill/MCP bus, and none recreates a rejected runtime, memory substrate, critic, SaaS, or guardrail framework. The radical part is epistemic rather than infrastructural: a harness change no longer earns rollout because the graph moved after deployment; it earns rollout only after surviving a prospective attempt to falsify the claim that it helped.
