## Executive conclusion

The borrow **does transfer, but only at the process-structure level**. The useful clinical mechanism is not a vague exhortation to “reason like a doctor.” It is a compact diagnostic-safety bundle:

1. keep more than one live explanation for an observation (a differential);
2. verify the leading explanation against expected and contradictory evidence;
3. treat the measuring instrument as fallible, validate it with known-positive/known-negative controls, and obtain a genuinely method-independent corroborating signal before a high-consequence claim;
4. explicitly preserve uncertainty and specify what future observation would reopen the conclusion (safety netting);
5. maintain a contextual registry of known ways the diagnostic intervention itself can mislead or cause harm (iatrogenics/contraindications);
6. evaluate the added verification step using absolute benefit and harm, analogous to NNT/NNH, rather than assuming more tests are always better.

The most important adversarial qualification comes directly from diagnostic-test science: two agreeing results are not automatically truth. FDA guidance says that agreement with a non-reference standard cannot establish correctness, that two agreeing methods may both be wrong, and that repeating the same test adds no useful information about performance [S3]. Thus the clinical analogy supports the owner’s “mechanically independent signals” rule, but it also makes that rule stricter: independence must be argued in terms of **failure modes and evidence lineage**, not tool names or call count.

My primary proposal is an interruptive **Diagnostic Claim Gate** for consequential claims of absence, breakage, completion, identity, and causation. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** It requires a structured claim record containing a differential, a validated detector control, two non-circular signals with declared failure domains, disconfirming evidence, and a calibrated conclusion. It does not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It therefore passes the anti-overfit gate.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this gate would catch the stated class of single-unvalidated-probe errors before user-visible reporting, because the gate’s minimum fields directly negate both necessary features of that failure class—“single” and “unvalidated”—and FDA’s analysis explains why independent reference evidence matters [S3]. This confidence does **not** establish net benefit, alert compliance, or the right trigger scope. Those remain **UNVERIFIED; settle them with Experiment E1 (shadow replay plus randomized prospective activation), specified below**.

## 1. What clinical medicine has already solved

Medicine has not solved diagnostic error in the sense of eliminating it. It has, however, developed distinct mechanisms for preventing premature closure, exposing bad instruments, limiting treatment-caused harm, and expressing the absolute value of an intervention. Those mechanisms map separately; collapsing them into one metaphor would lose their safety properties.

### 1.1 Differential diagnosis plus diagnostic verification

In its own field, a differential diagnosis is a set of plausible explanations for a patient’s findings, refined as information arrives. The National Academies’ *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care* describes the working diagnosis as one or multiple hypotheses, says uncertainty should be communicated, and defines diagnostic verification as checking whether the leading diagnosis adequately explains the findings, coheres with patient context, and is appropriately singular [S1]. It also describes a repeated counterfactual operation: for each candidate, ask what findings would be expected if it were true, then compare those expectations with history, examination, tests, or consultation [S1].

This matters because the mechanism is not “list alternatives” alone. It has four functional stages:

- **Hypothesis generation:** retain multiple causal explanations.
- **Discriminating predictions:** state what each explanation predicts.
- **Information acquisition:** seek evidence that separates the candidates.
- **Verification/revision:** test the leader’s explanatory coverage and reopen the differential when evidence conflicts.

A randomized experiment with 90 advanced medical students found that a supplied differential-diagnosis checklist improved accuracy only when the correct diagnosis was on the checklist (mean accuracy 0.75 versus 0.49 in control, P<0.001); when the correct diagnosis was omitted, performance was 0.43 and not significantly better than control [S2]. That is unusually important negative evidence for this unit: a static menu can create a bounded search space and may deepen omission rather than cure it. The transferable mechanism is therefore an **open differential plus disconfirmation**, not a fixed taxonomy presented as complete.

Translation to agent reliability:

> Observation: `probe P returned no matches`  
> Candidate explanations: (A) target truly absent; (B) wrong scope/path; (C) unsupported syntax or quoting; (D) permission/transport failure; (E) truncated/paginated result; (F) stale index/cache; (G) detector cannot represent the target; (H) target exists in another revision or environment.  
> Discriminating predictions: a known-present control should be found if P is functioning; a different evidence plane should agree if A is true; stderr/exit status or metadata may support C–F; direct enumeration should contradict a stale search index.

The harness does not need to enumerate every possible failure universally. It needs to prevent one observation from being silently promoted into one explanation.

### 1.2 Reference standards, controls, and orthogonal confirmation

Clinical tests are measurements, not reality. Their results are interpreted against a reference standard when possible, with sensitivity and specificity describing performance relative to condition status [S3]. FDA’s diagnostic-test guidance makes three points that transfer with exceptional precision:

- if a comparator is not a reference standard, agreement is not the same as accuracy [S3, lines 217–258];
- even when two tests agree, both may be wrong [S3, lines 427–441];
- repeating the same candidate test or the same non-reference comparator does not reveal performance, and a reference standard should be constructed independently of the test being evaluated [S3, lines 253–255, 439–445].

Controls answer a narrower but essential question: “Can this instrument detect something known to be present in the current conditions?” A positive control does **not** prove the target conclusion, but failure on the control invalidates a negative result. In agent work, the analogue is exact:

- Before saying “there are no skills,” ask the same detector to find a known-present skill.
- Before saying “this API has no field,” make the parser find a known-present neighboring field in the same response format.
- Before saying “the file does not exist,” show that the same path mechanism can resolve a known-present sibling, then corroborate through a different substrate (for example, direct filesystem metadata versus a search index).

“Orthogonal” must mean distinct causal failure paths. `grep` and `rg` over the same truncated input are cosmetically different but evidentially coupled. Two LLM summaries of the same omitted context are also coupled. Direct filesystem enumeration and a repository-tree object can be independent with respect to a stale working-tree index, but not necessarily with respect to looking at the wrong revision. The declared hypothesis determines what counts as independent.

### 1.3 Safety netting: uncertainty with a re-evaluation trigger

Medicine often cannot obtain certainty in one encounter. Safety netting makes a provisional conclusion safer by assigning follow-up responsibility, timing, and red flags that cause re-evaluation. NICE guidance for suspected cancer explicitly advises discussing alternative diagnoses, warning about false negatives, reviewing and acting on test results, planning review within an agreed time, and returning when symptoms recur, persist, or worsen [S5].

The agent analogue is not merely appending “I may be wrong.” It is an operational contract:

- state the current conclusion and confidence;
- state what was not observed or tested;
- identify the invalidation condition;
- name the exact next probe or future event that should reopen the issue;
- assign whether the agent can execute that follow-up now, or whether it must be handed to the owner.

Example: “No matching workflow was found in the checked working tree and origin/main tree. This does not cover generated files or un-fetched remote refs. If the deployment reports that workflow name, reopen by querying the deployed manifest.” This is useful only after the current feasible checks; safety netting is not permission to stop early.

### 1.4 Iatrogenics and contraindication registries

Iatrogenics is the recognition that an intervention intended to help can itself cause harm. Prescription labeling operationalizes one part of this through contraindications: the unsafe situation is stated precisely, its consequences described, and serious interaction-based contraindications are cross-referenced to detailed interaction or pharmacology evidence [S4]. FDA’s example decision-support work combines a drug-risk list with patient-specific risk factors and actionable alternatives rather than issuing generic warnings [S6].

The reliability analogue is a **probe contraindication registry**: a small, contextual set of known invalid uses and adverse effects of evidence-producing tools. Examples of record shape (illustrative, not claims about this harness) are:

```yaml
probe_family: text-search
context: absence claim over generated or ignored files
contraindication: search scope excludes ignored/generated locations
consequence: false absence
required_alternative: direct scoped enumeration plus declared exclusions
evidence: incident-or-test-id
severity: high
expires_or_revalidate: tool-version-or-date
```

```yaml
probe_family: api-list
context: paginated collection
contraindication: next-page token present but not consumed
consequence: false absence or false completion
required_alternative: exhaust pagination; compare reported total where available
evidence: incident-or-test-id
severity: high
```

This is not a universal ban list. Like a medical contraindication, it is a relation among intervention, context, and consequence. A search tool can be appropriate for discovery but contraindicated as sole evidence of global absence.

Alert fatigue is a direct disanalogy and design warning. FDA reports that some clinical decision-support systems have 60–90% of alerts ignored and argues for evidence-based, relevant, actionable alternatives at the point of care [S6]. A hook that interrupts every sentence will be bypassed or templated around. The registry should therefore trigger only when claim semantics and consequence cross a threshold, and it should present the required alternative probe, not merely complain.

### 1.5 Number needed to treat and number needed to harm

In evidence-based medicine, NNT is the number of patients who must receive a treatment to prevent one additional bad outcome. It is the inverse of absolute risk reduction: `NNT = 1 / (control event rate − experimental event rate)` [S7]. The corresponding harm framing is NNH, based on absolute risk increase.

The faithful transfer is an **evaluation metric**, not an online truth algorithm:

- `NNV` (number needed to verify) = eligible high-risk claims gated / additional wrong conclusions prevented.
- `NNH_verify` = eligible high-risk claims gated / additional task failures caused by the gate (timeouts, inability to complete, destructive extra probes, or owner-abandoned runs).
- Also report median added tool calls, latency, and token cost per prevented wrong conclusion.

NNT depends on baseline event rate. Therefore NNV must be stratified by claim family and context; one aggregate score will hide that absence claims, causal diagnoses, and low-risk descriptive statements have different baseline error and verification yield. It must also use an actual control group or credible counterfactual. Counting corrections after rollout alone confounds changes in workload and model capability.

## 2. Concrete harness design

### 2.1 Proposal A — Diagnostic Claim Gate — **UNVERIFIED; settle with Experiment E1**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Trigger the gate at the boundary immediately before a consequential conclusion becomes user-visible. Start with a narrow semantic set:

- universal negative: “none,” “no X exists,” “never,” “not configured”;
- system diagnosis: “broken,” “dead,” “unsupported,” “unavailable”;
- completion: “done,” “fixed,” “all tests pass,” “fully migrated”;
- sole-cause attribution: “the root cause is X”;
- security or destructive-action premise: “unused,” “safe to delete,” “no live consumer.”

The gate should require a compact claim envelope:

```yaml
claim: "No X exists in scope S"
scope:
  included: [...]
  excluded: [...]
claim_class: absence
consequence: medium|high|critical
differential:
  - hypothesis: target truly absent
  - hypothesis: detector/scope/transport/index failed
detector_control:
  known_present_item: "Y"
  result: found
signals:
  - observation: "..."
    evidence_ref: "tool-call-or-artifact locator"
    failure_domain: "search/parser/index"
  - observation: "..."
    evidence_ref: "tool-call-or-artifact locator"
    failure_domain: "direct metadata/alternate authority"
independence_argument: "Why one failure cannot trivially cause both results"
disconfirming_evidence_sought: "..."
resolution: confirmed|provisional|unresolved
confidence_band: "exact permitted phrase"
safety_net:
  invalidated_by: "..."
  next_probe: "..."
```

The implementation can be deliberately thin:

1. A pre-response hook or response-lint step matches candidate consequential claims using deterministic phrases plus an explicit agent marker such as `<diagnostic-claim>`. Deterministic matching favors auditability; the explicit marker handles paraphrases.
2. If triggered, the hook checks envelope completeness and distinct `failure_domain` values.
3. It does **not** decide truth. It returns a short actionable message naming the missing element (for example, “negative detector lacks a known-present control” or “both signals share the same cached index”).
4. The agent either gathers evidence, narrows the statement, labels it provisional/UNVERIFIED with a settling experiment, or removes the claim.
5. Every fired gate and resolution is logged locally for later NNV/NNH evaluation.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that deterministic phrases plus an explicit marker will cover most high-damage conclusion types without a second model call, because the initial set consists of linguistically salient universal/terminal predicates and the marker lets the agent self-declare less obvious cases. The wide interval reflects unknown language diversity in real responses. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1’s shadow-replay recall analysis settles it**.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a hard gate on every matched claim would improve end-to-end productivity, despite improving evidential discipline, because extra probes can add delay and false triggers can induce alert fatigue; clinical CDS has documented 60–90% ignore rates for poorly targeted alerts [S6]. Therefore the initial deployment should hard-stop only high/critical consequence claims and merely annotate medium consequence claims. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1’s randomized arm settles it**.

What it catches that a generic “double-check your work” reminder misses:

- a valid command that searched the wrong scope;
- a detector that returns empty because it cannot see the target class;
- two superficially different probes sharing the same bad input, index, parser, revision, or authority;
- a conclusion whose alternative explanation was never articulated;
- a provisional result phrased as final;
- a “done” assertion based on running a test command without checking exit status, expected test discovery, or changed artifact;
- a root-cause assertion supported only by temporal adjacency;
- a “safe to delete” assertion that proved absence of a conventional consumer but never tested a live runtime reference.

### 2.2 Proposal B — Contextual Probe Contraindication Registry — **UNVERIFIED; settle with Experiment E2**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Store a versioned, human-readable registry of high-severity probe/context combinations learned from confirmed incidents and controlled tests. Each entry must contain:

- probe family and relevant version/environment;
- precise contraindicated context;
- failure consequence (false absence, false completion, wrong identity, false cause);
- evidence reference;
- required alternative or control;
- severity;
- owner and revalidation/expiry condition.

The Claim Gate reads this registry and emits the prescribed alternative when context matches. Entries must not be added from suspicion alone; require either a reproduced incident or a test fixture with known truth. This mirrors FDA labeling’s requirement for precise situation and consequence, and its preference for demonstrated or well-supported risk rather than generic boilerplate [S4].

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a small registry covering recurrent high-frequency failure modes will prevent more harm per alert than a broad tool-warning catalog, because contextual CDS provides actionable alternatives while generic alerting is associated with high ignore rates [S6]. The analogy does not quantify agent behavior, so this is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E2 (six-week shadow match followed by stepped activation) settles it**.

Experiment E2 should measure: match precision against adjudicated incidents; percentage of entries never matched; alerts acted on; overrides with reasons; false blocks; and incidents recurring after a matching warning. Delete or demote low-precision entries. This proposal is configuration only because the enforcement machinery belongs in Proposal A; do not build a new registry service.

### 2.3 Proposal C — Safety-netted conclusion template — **UNVERIFIED; settle within E1**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

For conclusions that remain provisional after feasible checks, require four fields in normal prose:

1. current bounded conclusion;
2. confidence band with evidence;
3. untested scope or remaining alternative;
4. exact observation/experiment that would change the conclusion.

Example:

> Within the checked working tree and named remote tree, X was not found. It is **very likely (80-90%)** absent from those two scopes because direct enumeration and repository-object lookup independently returned no X while each found known-present Y. Generated deployment artifacts were not inspected, so global absence is **UNVERIFIED; inspect deployed manifest M to settle it**.

This creates an honest stopping rule without converting uncertainty into paralysis. It also makes later evidence routable: a future contradictory observation names the claim it invalidates.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the template will reduce overstatement among cases that cannot be fully resolved, because it forces bounded scope and an explicit falsifier; NICE safety netting similarly links uncertainty to re-evaluation conditions [S5]. Transfer to autonomous agents is not directly demonstrated, so this is **UNVERIFIED; E1 should score blinded outputs for scope overstatement and actionable falsifiers**.

### 2.4 Proposal D — NNV/NNH verification scorecard — **UNVERIFIED; requires E1 data**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Compute, by claim class and consequence:

```text
absolute error reduction = error_rate(control) - error_rate(gated)
NNV = 1 / absolute_error_reduction
absolute induced-failure increase = induced_failure_rate(gated) - induced_failure_rate(control)
NNH_verify = 1 / absolute_induced_failure_increase
```

Always publish event counts and confidence intervals alongside these ratios. If the absolute difference crosses zero, a single NNV/NNH point estimate is misleading; report the interval and direction uncertainty. Also report cost per 1,000 eligible claims and owner-correction severity prevented.

This scorecard prevents two symmetric errors: deploying a ritual that catches almost nothing, and removing a useful check because it adds visible latency while its prevented failures remain invisible. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that randomized or matched-control measurement is necessary for a credible NNV, because NNT is mathematically defined from the difference between control and experimental event rates [S7]. Whether the proposed gate yields a favorable NNV/NNH is **UNVERIFIED; E1 settles it**.

## 3. Experiment E1: the settling test

This design is intentionally staged so an attractive analogy cannot smuggle itself into production.

### Phase 0 — Define outcomes before looking

Create a blinded adjudication rubric for an eligible conclusion:

- correct and adequately scoped;
- correct but overstated;
- wrong due to detector failure;
- wrong due to scope/revision/authority mismatch;
- wrong due to circular or correlated evidence;
- wrong due to premature completion/causation;
- unresolved and honestly reported.

Define a “mechanically independent” pair before evaluation: no shared decisive input transform, cache/index, parser, revision assumption, or authority for the failure under test. Independence is hypothesis-relative, so adjudicators must evaluate the submitted independence argument rather than merely count tool names.

### Phase 1 — Shadow replay

Replay a stratified sample containing known corrected conclusions plus ordinary successful runs. The gate observes candidate response text and tool provenance but does not interrupt. Measure:

- trigger recall on adjudicated high-consequence erroneous claims;
- precision among all triggers;
- detector-control deficiency recall;
- correlated-evidence deficiency recall;
- percentage of errors not expressible by the envelope;
- estimated added calls if enforced.

Include negative controls: correct absence claims with valid independent evidence, and statements containing “no” that are not diagnostic claims. Include adversarial paraphrases. Keep the adjudicators blind to whether the gate fired.

Precommit a deployment threshold. A reasonable starting hypothesis—not a conclusion—is ≥80% recall for known single-probe high-consequence errors and ≥70% precision for hard-stop triggers. These thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; E1 Phase 1 settles whether they are achievable and a cost review must approve whether they are acceptable**.

### Phase 2 — Prospective randomized activation

Randomize eligible high/critical claims to:

- control: current response path;
- intervention: Claim Gate plus contraindication suggestions and safety-net template.

Evaluate final conclusions, not merely whether the envelope was completed. Primary outcome: adjudicated wrong or materially overstated conclusion per eligible claim. Secondary outcomes: correction requests, task completion, latency, tool calls, token use, false blocks, override rate, and destructive actions averted. Stratify by absence/broken/done/root-cause/safe-to-delete claim class.

Use held-out tasks and newer model versions. A fixed checklist may overfit known incidents, as the medical checklist experiment showed when omitted diagnoses erased benefit [S2]. At least one test stratum should contain detector failures absent from the registry.

### Phase 3 — Decision rule

Ship narrowly only if the intervention lowers adjudicated error without an unacceptable increase in induced task failure and if hard-stop precision remains high enough to avoid alert fatigue. Expand trigger classes only with class-specific favorable NNV/NNH. If benefits concentrate solely in explicit absence claims, keep it there; the cross-domain theory does not justify universalization.

## 4. Why the analogy transfers

The structural correspondence is strong in five ways:

| Clinical structure | Agent reliability structure | Relevant invariant |
|---|---|---|
| Latent patient condition is not directly observed | Repository/system state is not always directly observed | A probe result is evidence, not ground truth |
| Tests have false positives/negatives and context limitations | Tools have scope, parser, transport, cache, truncation, and permission failures | Negative result requires instrument validation |
| Differential retains competing disease explanations | Differential retains state-versus-detector explanations | Prevent premature closure |
| Reference/orthogonal evidence can expose test error | Different authority/failure domain can expose probe error | Agreement helps only when errors are not coupled |
| Follow-up reopens a provisional diagnosis | Safety net reopens a bounded conclusion | Uncertainty needs an operational falsifier |

The transfer is strongest for claims about externally checkable states: presence/absence, command success, schema shape, configuration reachability, artifact completion, and reproducible cause. These have observable discriminators and often admit known-positive controls.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the analogy is structurally valid for those claim families because both domains infer a latent state through fallible measurement and because FDA explicitly distinguishes test agreement from truth [S3]. The remaining uncertainty concerns implementation efficacy in agents, which is **UNVERIFIED; E1 settles it**.

## 5. Where the analogy breaks—and required guardrails against forcing it

### 5.1 There may be no gold standard

Medicine sometimes has a reference standard; software/system questions often do not. “Best architecture,” “owner intent,” and “code quality” are evaluative, not latent binary conditions. Two probes cannot manufacture objectivity. For these, the gate should require explicit criteria and competing interpretations, not pretend to verify truth.

FDA’s warning is decisive: comparing one non-reference test with another yields agreement, not correctness, and even concordant tests can both be wrong [S3]. Therefore the harness must use `resolution: provisional` when no reference-quality evidence exists.

### 5.2 Repeated evidence can be correlated in hidden ways

Two shells may call the same executable. Two tools may read the same stale index. Two agents may share the same truncated prompt. A direct file check and a Git check may both inspect the wrong branch. Medicine also confronts conditional dependence among tests; the transferable response is to model failure paths, not multiply apparent confirmations as though independent.

Thus “two tools” is an inadequate policy. The envelope’s `failure_domain` and `independence_argument` are essential, and adjudication must reject merely cosmetic diversity.

### 5.3 A differential can anchor and omit

The randomized checklist study found benefit only when the correct diagnosis appeared on the list [S2]. A centrally curated catalog of agent failure explanations can become a new source of blindness. Registry suggestions must include “other/unmodeled failure,” allow free-form alternatives, and be treated as prompts rather than exhaustive ontology.

This is why I reject a vector database or knowledge graph of all failure modes. Besides being explicitly excluded by the anti-overfit gate, it would lend false completeness to an open-world problem.

### 5.4 Additional tests have cost and can cause harm

Clinical investigations can expose patients to radiation, complications, incidental findings, and delay. Agent verification can consume tokens/time, hit rate limits, mutate state, leak data, or trigger destructive behavior. “Always run two more commands” is not safe.

The gate must prefer read-only corroboration, obey the existing authorization boundary, and accept a bounded provisional conclusion when safe independent verification is unavailable. No analogy can authorize an external write, deployment, message, purchase, deletion, or privilege escalation.

### 5.5 Clinical stakes and base rates differ

Patient harm and software-task harm are not morally interchangeable. Clinical prevalence, test characteristics, and treatment effects do not provide numerical priors for agent errors. NNT values cannot be imported; only the absolute-risk-difference method transfers. The scorecard must learn baseline rates from agent tasks.

### 5.6 Clinicians receive longitudinal feedback; agents may not

Medical diagnoses may be confirmed by disease course, pathology, or response to treatment. Many agent sessions end before outcome feedback arrives. Safety netting without a durable follow-up owner becomes decorative. The design therefore requires an explicit handoff target or future trigger; it does not propose a durable-execution engine, which the anti-overfit gate rejects.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Always ask a second agent” — rejected

Two agents sharing context and model priors are not mechanically independent, and an extra agent adds orchestration without validating the detector. This risks correlated confidence rather than corroboration. It would also drift toward a second orchestration runtime, prohibited by the anti-overfit gate.

### “Require exactly two tool calls” — rejected

Call count has no epistemic content. Re-running the same failed probe, or using two wrappers around one index, can reproduce the same error. FDA explicitly notes that repeating the candidate or non-reference test does not reveal performance [S3].

### “Use a universal diagnostic checklist” — rejected

Empirical evidence shows a checklist helps when it contains the right answer and can fail when it omits it [S2]. A small contextual contraindication registry plus open differential is safer than a supposedly complete catalog.

### “Put a warning before every response” — rejected

Clinical decision-support alerts can be ignored at very high rates when not relevant or actionable [S6]. Interrupt only consequential claim classes, supply the missing action, and measure overrides.

### “Train a critic to judge all conclusions” — rejected

This violates the anti-overfit gate’s trained-critic exclusion, creates correlated model failure, and adds opacity. Deterministic envelope validation plus real evidence provenance is smaller and more auditable.

### “Build a reliability daemon and knowledge graph” — rejected

This is BUILD-LARGE, reintroduces explicitly rejected runtime and memory components, and is unnecessary for a response-boundary gate. A hook plus a small versioned registry rides existing buses and can be removed cleanly if E1 fails.

### “Apply NNT directly as a runtime threshold” — rejected

NNT is a population effect measure, not a patient-level diagnostic rule [S7]. Its correct use here is retrospective/control-based evaluation of verification policy, stratified by baseline risk.

## 7. Minimal adoption sequence

1. **CONFIG:** define the eligible high-consequence claim vocabulary and the claim-envelope schema.
2. **BUILD-SMALL:** run the gate in shadow mode; no user-visible blocking.
3. Independently adjudicate a stratified replay; validate that the detector finds known-present bad claims and known-present good controls before making any claim about gate coverage.
4. **CONFIG:** add only reproduced, high-severity contraindications with actionable alternatives.
5. Randomize prospective hard-stop activation for high/critical claims.
6. Calculate class-specific NNV/NNH, latency, and false-block rates.
7. Retain only trigger classes with demonstrated net benefit; keep medium-risk checks advisory or remove them.

This sequence is **likely (55-80%)** to be implementable without replacement of the harness runtime because every element is a schema, hook-bound check, or local analysis of its output; no new service is logically required. ## 8. Evidence ledger and confidence audit

### Supported conclusions

- Medicine’s diagnostic process explicitly uses multiple working hypotheses, verification, uncertainty communication, and iterative information gathering [S1].
- A differential checklist is not universally beneficial; its experimental benefit depended on inclusion of the correct diagnosis [S2].
- Agreement between non-reference tests is not accuracy; concordant tests may both be wrong; repeat testing by the same method does not establish performance [S3].
- Contraindication labeling is contextual and calls for precise scenarios, consequences, and evidence-linked detail [S4].
- Safety netting ties provisional conclusions to follow-up responsibility, timing, and re-evaluation conditions [S5].
- Non-actionable clinical alerts can have high ignore rates, motivating narrow and actionable triggers [S6].
- NNT is based on absolute risk reduction and therefore requires control and intervention event rates [S7].

### Forward claims, bands, and settling evidence

- Claim Gate catches the stated single-unvalidated-probe class: **very likely (80-90%)**, because its required detector control and independent second signal directly negate that class and align with reference-test principles [S3]. **UNVERIFIED; E1.**
- Deterministic trigger plus explicit marker covers most consequential claims: **likely (55-80%)**, based on the narrow initial predicate classes but tempered by unknown paraphrase diversity. **UNVERIFIED; E1 shadow replay.**
- Universal hard stops improve productivity: **roughly even (45-55%)**, because error prevention competes with added work and documented alert fatigue [S6]. **UNVERIFIED; E1 randomized phase.**
- A small contextual contraindication registry yields better alert value than a broad warning list: **likely (55-80%)**, supported by the contextual/actionable CDS principle [S6] but not agent-specific evidence. **UNVERIFIED; E2.**
- Safety-net templates reduce overstatement: **likely (55-80%)**, because they force scope and falsifiers, analogous to NICE’s re-evaluation contract [S5]. **UNVERIFIED; E1 blinded output scoring.**
- Net benefit and favorable NNV/NNH: no directional claim is made. **UNVERIFIED; E1 is required.**

## 9. Gaps

1. This review supplies no agent-harness randomized trial establishing that a medical-style diagnostic envelope reduces wrong conclusions; it did not conduct a systematic literature review and therefore makes no global absence claim. All implementation-effect claims are inline-tagged **UNVERIFIED** with E1 or E2.
2. This ideation unit intentionally did not inspect the local corpus, defects, audit findings, repository, configuration, hooks, or Windows-only paths. It cannot claim current integration points, line counts, or compatibility. The pinned failure count is used only as supplied ground truth.
3. “Mechanical independence” has no universal automated classifier. The proposed failure-domain declaration is auditable but can be wrong or gamed. E1 needs human adjudication of independence arguments before automation is trusted.
4. This unit was not provided with, and did not derive, absolute baseline rates for each claim family, so it cannot calculate NNV or NNH.
5. Safety netting across sessions may require an existing follow-up mechanism. Building a durable executor is deliberately out of scope and rejected by the anti-overfit gate.

**[S2]** Sibbald et al., “Differential diagnosis checklists reduce diagnostic error differentially: A randomised experiment,” *Medical Education* (2022). Checklist benefit when the correct diagnosis was included and lack of benefit when omitted. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9290564/

**[S3]** U.S. Food and Drug Administration, *Statistical Guidance on Reporting Results from Studies Evaluating Diagnostic Tests*. Reference standards, agreement versus accuracy, independent construction, concordant errors, and invalid repeat/discrepant resolution. https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda

**[S4]** U.S. Food and Drug Administration, *Warnings and Precautions, Contraindications, and Boxed Warning Sections of Labeling for Human Prescription Drug and Biological Products — Content and Format*. Precise contraindicated situation, consequences, evidence, and cross-reference. https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Warnings-and-Precautions--Contraindications--and-Boxed-Warning-Sections-of-Labeling-for-Human-Prescription-Drug-and-Biological-Products-%E2%80%94-Content-and-Format.pdf

**[S5]** National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, NG12, “Recommendations on patient support, safety netting and the diagnostic process” (updated 2026). Alternative diagnoses, false-negative awareness, result ownership, timed review, persistence/worsening triggers. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG12/chapter/recommendations-on-patient-support-safety-netting-and-the-diagnostic-process

**[S6]** U.S. Food and Drug Administration, “Integrated Clinical Decision Support System to Advance the Safe Use of Antibiotics.” Context-specific risk integration, actionable alternatives, and reported 60–90% ignored-alert range. https://www.fda.gov/drugs/safe-use-initiative/integrated-clinical-decision-support-system-advance-safe-use-antibiotics

**[S7]** Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine, University of Oxford, “Number Needed to Treat (NNT).” Definition and `NNT = 1/ARR`. https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/resources/ebm-tools/number-needed-to-treat-nnt
