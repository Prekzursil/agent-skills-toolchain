# X159 — Archival science → trust calibration

**Unit:** X159  
**Source field:** archival science (provenance, appraisal, retention schedules, authenticity)  
**Target problem:** knowing how much to believe a given agent's output, per agent and per task type  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external ideation only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect the local harness, corpus, audits, or subject tree and make no claim about their implementation state.

## Executive verdict

The borrow transfers, but only partially and only if the harness preserves a hard distinction between **authenticity** and **truth**.

The most useful archival mechanism is InterPARES' **cumulative presumption of authenticity followed by case-specific verification when the presumption is weak**. The presumption is based not on the record's persuasive surface but on evidence about its identity, context of creation, handling, maintenance, access controls, authoritative status, and custody. InterPARES explicitly describes authenticity as an inference from known facts about how a record was created, handled, and maintained; its benchmark requirements are cumulative, so satisfying more requirements, more fully, strengthens the presumption. If that basis is weak, the archivist verifies against independent facts such as separately preserved copies, registers, audit trails, forensic examination, or trusted third-party testimony. The official InterPARES report states this directly in its conceptual framework and separates benchmark requirements before transfer from baseline requirements after transfer ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force Report, pp. 20–22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf); [InterPARES requirements, Appendix 2](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/interpares_book_k_app02.pdf)).

That maps well to agent trust as a two-stage system:

1. Attach a compact, machine-generated **provenance and verification envelope** to every consequential output. This supports a prior presumption about whether the output is what it claims to be and whether its evidence trail is intact.
2. Calibrate belief in correctness from **independently observed outcomes**, grouped by agent identity/version and task type. Weak, missing, contradictory, or out-of-distribution evidence triggers verification rather than an invented confidence number.

The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve trust triage because the target and source share the same structural problem: a consumer receives an information object after transformations and needs evidence about origin, identity, integrity, context, and custody; InterPARES explicitly built its requirements for that problem in electronic systems ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force Report, pp. 20–22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)). The exact improvement is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that would settle it is the “retrospective trust-packet replay” in §9**, which compares provenance-aware risk ranking with output-only baselines on blindly adjudicated historical tasks.

The transfer is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** sufficient by itself to establish factual correctness, because an authentic record can faithfully preserve a false statement, and InterPARES itself distinguishes authenticity from reliability and accuracy ([InterPARES 2 products and conceptual distinctions](https://www.interpares.org/ip2/ip2_products); [InterPARES Authenticity Task Force Report](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)). Therefore the harness must never label an output “trusted” merely because its lineage is complete or its bytes are unchanged.

The recommended architecture is deliberately small: append-only JSONL trust packets emitted on the existing hook bus, a small aggregation/report skill, and configuration for retention and verification triggers. It introduces no second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. What archival science solved in its own field

### 1.1 Provenance is contextual evidence, not an author reputation badge

Archival provenance traditionally preserves the relationship between records and the person or organization that created or accumulated them. Modern archival description expands this beyond one creator string. The International Council on Archives' Records in Contexts model treats records, agents, activities, mandates, dates, places, and their relations as contextual entities rather than collapsing context into one flat description ([ICA, *Records in Contexts Conceptual Model 1.0*](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2023/12/RiC-CM-1.0.pdf); [ICA, *Learn About RiC*](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2025/04/Learn-About-RiC_ENG.pdf)).

InterPARES makes that context operational. Requirement A.1 asks for attributes that establish record identity and integrity. Its identity elements include the persons participating in formation, the action or matter, dates of creation and transmission, the archival bond, and attachments. Its integrity elements include the handling office, office of primary responsibility, annotations, and technical modifications. Requirements A.2–A.8 cover access privileges; protections against loss/corruption and media/technology change; documentary form; authentication; identification of the authoritative record; and removal/transfer of relevant documentation ([InterPARES requirements, Appendix 2](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/interpares_book_k_app02.pdf); a concise official project walkthrough enumerates A.1–A.8 and B.1–B.3 in [InterPARES Preserve Electronic Records walkthrough, p. 6](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/interpares_book_p_app07.pdf)).

The **archival bond** is especially valuable. A record derives meaning and evidential weight partly from its relationship to the other records produced by the same activity or transaction: a decision belongs with its request, evidence, approvals, revisions, and consequences. InterPARES makes expression of that bond part of record identity, for example through a classification code or file identifier ([InterPARES mapping of Requirement A.1.a.iv](https://interpares.org/documents/interpares_DocumentsMapping.pdf)).

In its own field, this catches a record presented out of context, a detached attachment, an ambiguous version, an unrecorded technical modification, or an object whose claimed creator and business process cannot be reconciled. It does **not** decide that the statements inside the record are true.

### 1.2 Authenticity is a graded presumption, not a binary magic stamp

InterPARES' benchmark requirements are conditions from which a preserver infers authenticity based on creation, handling, and maintenance. The requirements are cumulative: the number satisfied and degree of satisfaction affect the strength of the presumption. When the presumption is inadequate, verification examines the record and reliable external information. Suggested methods include comparison with copies held elsewhere, comparison with incoming/outgoing registers, textual or forensic analysis, audit trails, and trusted third-party testimony ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force Report, pp. 21–22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)).

This is a sophisticated trust rule for three reasons:

- It exposes the grounds for belief rather than emitting an unexplained score.
- It allows partial evidence and records the difference between “not disproven,” “presumed authentic,” and “verified.”
- It escalates weak cases to a different epistemic process instead of repeatedly asking the same producer to reassure the consumer.

InterPARES later explored a Bayesian belief network for dependencies among the benchmark requirements. That study is evidence that archival researchers recognized that the requirements are not simply independent checklist points; for example, its model treats removal of identity/integrity information as conditionally dependent on first identifying that information ([InterPARES 2 General Study 06, *A Bayesian Belief Network*, pp. 18–19](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/ip2_gs06_final_report.pdf)). This was a research product, not evidence that archives universally use a specific numeric model. Treating it as a production standard would be an overclaim.

### 1.3 Chain of preservation controls what happens after creation

InterPARES separates the creator-side presumption (Requirement Set A) from preserver-side production of authentic copies (Requirement Set B). The latter requires unbroken custody, monitored security/control procedures, preservation of content through reproduction, documentation of the reproduction date and responsible person, the relation between acquired records and produced copies, the reproduction's effects, accessible documentation of alterations, and archival description ([InterPARES Preserve Electronic Records walkthrough, p. 6](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/interpares_book_p_app07.pdf); [InterPARES 2 products](https://www.interpares.org/ip2/ip2_products)).

Digital-preservation practice adds **fixity**: record a checksum at ingest and re-check it during storage and delivery. The Digital Preservation Coalition explains that checksums establish whether a bitstream changed and support chain of custody, while also warning that a checksum does not say where a change occurred and must be re-established after intentional migration ([DPC Digital Preservation Handbook, “Fixity and checksums”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/technical-solutions-and-tools/fixity-and-checksums)). Australia's National Archives similarly describes authenticity through documented strategies and provenance metadata, and integrity through preservation of the original bitstream, fixity checks, and a full audit trail of preservation actions ([National Archives of Australia, Digital Preservation Policy](https://www.naa.gov.au/about-us/who-we-are/accountability-and-reporting/archival-policy-and-planning/digital-preservation-policy)).

PREMIS supplies an implementable metadata vocabulary rather than merely a principle. Its core data model represents Objects, Events, Agents, and Rights and is intended to support long-term usability of digital objects ([Library of Congress, PREMIS official site](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/); [PREMIS Data Dictionary](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v3/premis-3-0-final.pdf)). The useful pattern is an event graph: who or what acted, on which object, when, under what authority, with what outcome.

Fixity transfers directly to detecting output substitution or mutation. It **almost certainly not (1-5%)** transfers as a correctness test, because checksums can prove that the same wrong answer survived unchanged; the DPC definition limits fixity to assurance that a file remained unchanged ([DPC, “Fixity and checksums”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/technical-solutions-and-tools/fixity-and-checksums)).

### 1.4 Appraisal and retention schedules preserve evidence selectively

Archives do not keep every trace forever. Appraisal determines final disposition; schedules describe record content and context and specify when records can be destroyed or must be transferred for permanent preservation ([NARA, Records Scheduling and Appraisal](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/sch-appraisal)). NARA requires clear record-series descriptions, cutoffs, disposition instructions, and justifications, and recommends age-based or event-based cutoffs that systems can automate from metadata. It warns that “destroy when no longer needed” cedes control to individual users, causes over-retention or premature destruction, and is difficult to automate ([NARA, Scheduling Records](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

The “record series” is the right granularity: related records are grouped because they arise from the same function, activity, transaction, or other creation relationship, and managed as a group ([NARA, Records Basics](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/basics)). Appraisal is explicitly judgment under resource constraints, not rote accumulation; NARA notes that not all essential evidence warrants permanent retention and that appraisal considers value, context, uniqueness, completeness, metadata, and cost ([NARA Appraisal Policy](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)).

For a harness, the lesson is to retain compact trust evidence by task series and risk class, not to make full transcripts the permanent memory substrate.

## 2. Concrete mapping to an agent harness

| Archival mechanism | Own-field object | Harness translation | What the translated evidence can establish | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provenance / Records in Contexts | Creator, activity, mandate, record relations | Agent identity and version, task taxonomy/version, initiating request, input/source identifiers, tool/validator events, parent and child outputs | Who/what produced the output and in what operational context | That its claims are correct |
| Record identity | Author, action/matter, dates, attachments, authoritative version | Output ID, run ID, task class, timestamps, artifact digest, attachment/source digests, final/candidate status | Which output is being evaluated; whether evidence belongs to it | Semantic adequacy |
| Archival bond | Relation to the transaction and neighboring records | Links among request, plan, tool results, subtask outputs, edits, tests, final answer, and later observed outcome | Whether a conclusion is detached from its supporting action chain | That linked evidence actually entails the conclusion |
| Integrity / chain of preservation | Custody, access, transformations, reproduction effects, fixity | Append-only events for handoffs, model/tool changes, summarization, edits, hash changes, and verifier identity | Whether the output or trail changed and how | Whether an unchanged output is true |
| Cumulative presumption | Strength grows with satisfied benchmark controls | Evidence-grade prior: unsupported → identified → provenance-complete → independently verified → outcome-confirmed | How much procedural evidence supports trusting the object/process | A calibrated probability of correctness without outcomes |
| Case-specific verification | Compare independent copies/registers/audit trails/third party | Independent tool/source check, deterministic test, human adjudication, downstream success, or independent agent using non-shared evidence | Whether claims survive an independent check | Universal correctness outside the check's scope |
| Record series | Function/activity-based group | Versioned task class, e.g. code-edit/tested, factual-research/cited, UI-visual, incident-diagnosis, scheduling/action | Per-agent reliability in the relevant kind of work | Reliability on a novel or misclassified task |
| Appraisal / schedule | Value-based retention and disposition | Keep trust packets and adjudications longer; sample high-value raw traces; expire routine payloads by event/age | Auditability at bounded storage/privacy cost | A guarantee that discarded evidence was valueless |

The central translation is: **trust the evidence path only for the proposition it can support**. Identity, integrity, process quality, and correctness are separate fields. They must not be multiplied into one opaque “trust score.”

## 3. Adversarial transfer test

### 3.1 Structural assumptions that do transfer

**Transfer 1 — delayed, remote evaluation.** An archival user and a harness consumer both evaluate an information object after its creation and often cannot directly observe creation. The transfer is **almost certain (90-99%)** at the structural level because InterPARES explicitly addresses electronic records moving across time, systems, applications, and custody, while agent outputs likewise cross model/tool/handoff boundaries by definition ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force Report, pp. 20–22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)).

**Transfer 2 — context changes evidential weight.** A detached statement and a statement linked to the exact source, tool result, task, version, and verification event deserve different procedural confidence. The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** because archival bond and identity requirements were designed to preserve precisely those relationships ([InterPARES requirements, Appendix 2](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/interpares_book_k_app02.pdf)). Whether these fields predict agent correctness is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that would settle it is the ablation arm of the retrospective trust-packet replay in §9**.

**Transfer 3 — transformations matter.** Summarization, delegation, editing, serialization, and final-answer assembly can change an output or separate it from evidence. The chain-of-preservation pattern applies because it records transformations and responsible agents. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose silent substitution or lost attachment relationships when every handoff emits an event, based on the documented archival use of custody, reproduction documentation, fixity, and audit trails ([InterPARES walkthrough](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/interpares_book_p_app07.pdf); [National Archives of Australia Digital Preservation Policy](https://www.naa.gov.au/about-us/who-we-are/accountability-and-reporting/archival-policy-and-planning/digital-preservation-policy)). The harness detection rate is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is “handoff fault injection” in §9**.

**Transfer 4 — evidence volume requires appraisal.** Full transcripts are costly, privacy-sensitive, and noisy; compact event metadata and selectively retained exemplars provide a plausible bounded alternative. The transfer is **likely (55-80%)** because archival schedules successfully operationalize series-level, event- or age-based disposition rather than individual whim ([NARA, Scheduling Records](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). The optimal harness schedule is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that would settle it is the “retention reconstruction test” in §9**.

### 3.2 Structural assumptions that do not transfer

**Disanalogy 1 — authenticity is not factual accuracy.** Archives answer “is this the record it purports to be, preserved with identity and integrity?” Trust calibration asks “how probable is this answer to be correct or useful?” A perfectly authentic hallucination remains a hallucination. This is the decisive limit. Any design that treats provenance completeness as correctness fails the analogy.

**Disanalogy 2 — the producer is adaptive and generative.** A historical record is usually fixed after creation; an agent can generate a persuasive provenance narrative, select flattering evidence, or learn the verifier's surface cues. A self-reported trust packet is therefore weak evidence. It is **likely (55-80%)** that a self-report-only implementation would be gamed accidentally or strategically, because the producer and assessor would share the same failure source; this is an inference from the independence requirement in InterPARES verification, not a measured harness result ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force Report, p. 22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)). **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is “self-report contradiction injection” in §9.** The envelope must be generated from runtime events where possible and label agent assertions separately.

**Disanalogy 3 — task classes drift.** Archival series are comparatively stable organizational constructs. Agent task distributions, models, prompts, tools, and policies change rapidly. A historical per-agent score can become stale after any of those change. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that unversioned aggregation would miscalibrate after material changes, based on the fact that the proposed grouping conditions themselves have changed; this is a deductive design risk, not a local measurement. A score therefore needs version boundaries, recency weighting, and an explicit “insufficient comparable evidence” state.

**Disanalogy 4 — chain of custody can be circular.** In archives, a trusted custodian can be institutionally separate from the creator. In a harness, producer, logger, judge, and summarizer may be the same model or runtime. Two agent calls are not mechanically independent if they share model family, prompt, context, source, or failing tool. The design must record verifier independence dimensions rather than count “number of agents.”

**Disanalogy 5 — absence of evidence is ambiguous.** Missing provenance may mean a risky output, a failed detector, an unsupported event type, or an intentionally minimal task. The harness must validate each detector on a known-present control and require two mechanically independent signals before declaring evidence absent, broken, or complete. This is not borrowed from archives alone; it is a necessary honesty constraint from the unit brief.

### 3.3 Final analogy judgment

The analogy **does transfer** as an evidence architecture and escalation policy. It **does not transfer** as a direct numerical correctness model. The harness should borrow archival provenance, archival bond, custody events, cumulative presumption, case-specific verification, record series, and retention schedules, while deriving actual calibration from independently adjudicated task outcomes.

## 4. Proposal 1 — UNVERIFIED: emit a minimal Trust Packet at consequential output boundaries

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

**UNVERIFIED premise:** the existing output/hook boundary exposes enough runtime context to populate the packet without asking the agent to narrate it. **Settling experiment:** implement the packet for 100 replayed runs and measure automatic field coverage, false linkage, runtime overhead, and lines of code (“trust-packet feasibility spike”).

Emit one append-only JSON object for a final answer, artifact, external action proposal, or high-consequence intermediate result. Use references and digests rather than duplicating raw payloads. A minimal schema:

```json
{
  "schema": "trust-packet/1",
  "output_id": "stable-id",
  "run_id": "run-id",
  "created_at": "RFC3339",
  "producer": {
    "agent_id": "agent-type-or-role",
    "model_id": "exact-model-or-unknown",
    "prompt_policy_version": "digest-or-unknown",
    "toolset_version": "digest-or-unknown"
  },
  "task": {
    "class": "versioned-taxonomy-label",
    "taxonomy_version": "1",
    "risk": "routine|consequential|high-stakes",
    "classification_source": "rule|human|agent"
  },
  "identity": {
    "request_ref": "content-addressed-or-stable-ref",
    "artifact_refs": ["..."],
    "attachment_refs": ["..."],
    "authoritative": true,
    "supersedes": ["output-id"]
  },
  "bond": {
    "parent_outputs": ["output-id"],
    "evidence_events": ["event-id"],
    "tool_result_refs": ["event-id"],
    "source_refs": ["stable-source-id"]
  },
  "custody": {
    "content_digest": "sha256:...",
    "transform_events": ["event-id"],
    "handoffs": ["event-id"]
  },
  "verification": [{
    "kind": "deterministic-test|independent-source|human|downstream-outcome|self-check",
    "verifier": "id",
    "independence": ["different-tool", "different-source"],
    "scope": ["claim-id"],
    "result": "pass|fail|mixed|unknown",
    "evidence_ref": "event-id"
  }],
  "epistemic_state": {
    "identity": "known|partial|unknown",
    "integrity": "intact|changed-documented|unknown",
    "correctness": "outcome-confirmed|independently-checked|self-asserted|unknown"
  }
}
```

Design constraints:

- Runtime-generated fields and agent-asserted fields must be distinguishable.
- `unknown` is a valid first-class value; missing data must not silently become zero trust or a false negative.
- Verification scope must point to claims or artifacts. A passing syntax test cannot verify a factual paragraph or product requirement.
- Independence must be a vector (different source, tool, model family, context, operator), not a Boolean.
- The authoritative output and all superseded candidates must be distinguishable.
- Digests establish identity/fixity only. The UI and schema must never describe them as truth checks.
- Packet emission failure must not be interpreted as output failure. It should emit a separate instrumentation event when possible.

This packet is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal output/evidence mix-ups, stale candidate selection, unrecorded transformations, missing attachments, and verifier scope mismatch, because those conditions contradict explicit identity, bond, custody, or scope fields. Actual detection is **UNVERIFIED; “handoff fault injection” in §9 settles it.**

It is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal “done” claims supported only by self-checks when the outcome event is absent and the verification method is typed, because self-check and outcome-confirmed states cannot collapse. Actual prevalence and recall are **UNVERIFIED; the retrospective trust-packet replay settles them.**

## 5. Proposal 2 — UNVERIFIED: maintain a per-agent × task-series Trust Register

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

**UNVERIFIED premise:** enough tasks receive later, independently observable outcomes to estimate useful per-series reliability. **Settling experiment:** the “retrospective trust-packet replay” in §9 must establish coverage and calibration before this register affects routing or autonomy.

Aggregate Trust Packets by a **versioned key**, not merely agent name:

```text
(agent role/type, model version, prompt/policy version,
 toolset version, task taxonomy version, task class, risk tier)
```

For each key, display separate dimensions:

1. **Identity completeness:** fraction with unambiguous output/request/artifact linkage.
2. **Integrity continuity:** fraction with documented transformations and no unexplained digest discontinuity.
3. **Independent verification rate:** fraction checked by a validator with stated independence dimensions.
4. **Observed task success:** outcome-confirmed successes / adjudicated outcomes.
5. **Calibration:** whether prior confidence bins match observed success frequencies.
6. **Evidence volume and freshness:** sample count, effective sample count after recency weighting, last outcome date, and version boundary.
7. **Unresolved contradiction rate:** packets with conflicting validators or source/outcome disagreement.

Do not reduce these to one global reputation number. If a routing decision needs one scalar, use an explicitly named **decision bound**, such as the lower bound of estimated success for the exact task series, and display its inputs. A simple beta-binomial estimate with a conservative prior is **likely (55-80%)** to be adequate for an initial low-volume register because it exposes uncertainty from small samples and can be implemented without a new service; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the model-comparison arm of the retrospective replay must compare it with raw rates, Wilson intervals, and a recency-weighted alternative.** No score should be used when the task is novel, classification confidence is low, or the version key lacks enough comparable outcomes.

The register must distinguish:

- **Procedural presumption:** how strong the provenance/custody/verification evidence is.
- **Empirical reliability:** how often comparable outputs were later adjudicated correct.
- **Current-case verification:** what was independently checked for this output.
- **Claimed confidence:** what the producing agent said, retained only as a calibration input.

This separation catches a critical failure that a reputation score misses: an agent can have strong historical performance in code editing and weak evidence on a novel legal-research task. It also catches the inverse: a generally weak agent can produce a current output backed by a deterministic test and independent source checks.

The register is **very likely (80-90%)** to avoid at least some cross-task reputation leakage if task labels are stable and versioned, because aggregation is explicitly conditioned on task class rather than pooled. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; the “taxonomy stability study” in §9 settles it.** It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to remain calibrated without version resets or recency handling because model, prompt, and tool changes alter the generating process; the evidence is the direct mismatch between historical and current conditioning variables, not a local result.

### Verification escalation policy

Borrow InterPARES' presumption/verification boundary:

- **Low procedural presumption:** verify identity and evidence linkage before evaluating content.
- **High procedural presumption, low empirical reliability:** independently test the content.
- **High historical reliability, novel task or version:** treat as insufficient evidence, not inherited trust.
- **Conflicting validators:** preserve both events and escalate; never let the later event overwrite the earlier one.
- **High-stakes task:** require outcome-appropriate independent verification regardless of historical reputation.
- **Routine task:** randomly audit a small sample so silent regressions remain observable.

The exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; the “cost-sensitive escalation trial” in §9 would settle them by comparing prevented errors, verification cost, latency, and false escalations.**

## 6. Proposal 3 — UNVERIFIED: task-series retention schedules for trust evidence

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

**UNVERIFIED premise:** the harness can separate compact trust metadata from sensitive/raw execution payloads. **Settling experiment:** the “retention reconstruction test” in §9 must delete according to a candidate schedule in an isolated replay copy and test whether adjudicators can still reconstruct trust decisions.

Define a schedule by record series and event, not “keep logs until disk pressure”:

| Series | Proposed disposition | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Trust Packet and outcome adjudication | Keep across the supported calibration horizon; reset/partition at material version changes | Small, necessary to reproduce per-series trust estimates |
| Verification event and evidence pointer | Keep with the packet; retain the evidence object only according to its own risk/privacy class | Preserves grounds without duplicating all content |
| Routine raw trace | Short age-based retention, then delete | High volume; low enduring value after packet and outcome extraction |
| Failure/contradiction trace | Event-based retention through diagnosis plus a fixed post-resolution period | Needed to audit miscalibration and detector failures |
| High-stakes or externally consequential run | Longer policy-defined retention with access controls | Greater accountability and dispute value |
| Randomly sampled exemplars | Stratified sample by agent/task/version/outcome | Supports later audits and taxonomy redesign without retaining everything |
| Detector control runs | Retain through detector/schema version lifetime | Proves that absence detectors could find known-present events |

The concrete time periods are intentionally unspecified. Selecting them without legal, privacy, storage, and operational requirements would fabricate policy. NARA's guidance supports clear age- or event-based cutoffs and warns against “when no longer needed,” but it does not prescribe agent-harness durations ([NARA, Scheduling Records](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce log volume while retaining auditability if Trust Packets capture sufficient references and adjudications, based on archival series-level appraisal and machine-implementable cutoffs. It is **UNVERIFIED; the retention reconstruction test settles both storage savings and loss of explanatory evidence.**

| Retained traces are enormous but cannot reconstruct why trust was granted | Appraisal schedule + packet completeness test | Missing durable grounds detected before deletion | **Likely (55-80%)**, based on retention schedules coupling disposition to record descriptions and context ([NARA scheduling](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)) | Retention reconstruction test |

What it still misses:

- A false claim supported by mutually dependent or corrupted sources.
- A verifier that tests the wrong property while reporting “pass.”
- Semantic omissions that leave no explicit contradictory event.
- Unlogged side channels or transformations.
- Distribution shift not represented in the version/task key.
- Collusion or common-mode failure across producer, validator, and adjudicator.
- Correctness dimensions for which no observable outcome or competent adjudicator exists.

## 8. Anti-overfit gate and rejected designs

The proposal passes the gate because it rides existing boundaries:

```text
existing run/tool/hook events
            |
            v
    append one Trust Packet ------> existing artifact/log storage
            |                                  |
            v                                  v
 existing skill/command reads packets --> per-series report + escalation hint
```

There is no new orchestration loop. The packet is passive evidence. The existing agent remains responsible for work; existing hooks emit facts; an existing skill or command summarizes them.

Rejected alternatives:

1. **Full provenance knowledge graph.** Rejected. RiC and PREMIS are graph-friendly, but a graph database is not required to preserve a handful of typed references. It would reintroduce a previously rejected vector-DB/KG memory component and create migration/query/schema burdens. JSONL plus stable IDs is sufficient for the initial questions.
2. **Durable workflow/chain-of-custody service.** Rejected. Archival custody does not justify a second execution runtime. Record events after the fact on the existing bus.
3. **Trained trust critic.** Rejected. A critic can share the producer's failure modes and would obscure evidential grounds. Deterministic validators, independent sources, downstream outcomes, and human adjudication remain typed evidence.
4. **Immutable blockchain or transparency ledger.** Rejected. A local append-only log plus digest is enough to detect ordinary mutation. A cryptographic multi-party ledger would solve adversarial institutional custody, which is **unlikely (20-45%)** to be the dominant owner-harness threat given the stated target; this premise is **UNVERIFIED, and a threat-model review would settle whether hostile log rewriting is in scope.**
5. **Store every transcript forever.** Rejected. Archival appraisal explicitly exists because evidential value and retention cost differ. Permanent undifferentiated traces worsen privacy, search, and reconstruction.
6. **Single global agent leaderboard.** Rejected. It destroys the task-series and version context required by the target problem.
7. **Provenance completeness as a confidence percentage.** Rejected. It commits the authenticity/accuracy category error.

## 9. Experiments required before operational trust decisions

### 9.1 Retrospective trust-packet replay

1. Sample completed tasks across agent types, task types, versions, successes, failures, and owner corrections.
2. Blind adjudicators to agent identity and proposed trust score; label task outcome and which claims were actually verified.
3. Reconstruct Trust Packets only from events available at decision time.
4. Compare baselines:
   - global agent historical rate;
   - per-agent × task raw rate;
   - provenance-completeness checklist;
   - proposed separated procedural/empirical/current-case register;
   - simple beta-binomial, Wilson-bound, and recency-weighted estimates.
5. Measure outcome coverage, discrimination, Brier score or reliability curves, error-detection precision/recall, abstention rate, and cost-weighted prevented errors.
6. Ablate bond, custody, verifier independence, task class, and version fields.

Success requires better calibration or error triage than output-only and global-reputation baselines, not merely more metadata. The expected direction is **likely (55-80%)** positive for task-conditioned outcome history and **roughly even (45-55%)** for provenance fields improving semantic-correctness calibration beyond task/outcome history; the evidence for the first is the direct conditioning match, while the second remains uncertain because authenticity evidence is only indirectly related to truth. Both are **UNVERIFIED until this replay is run.**

### 9.2 Handoff fault injection

Inject stale candidate selection, swapped attachment, post-verification edit, omitted transformation event, wrong parent link, and cross-subtask tool-result linkage. Require detection without inspecting final prose. Include known-present controls for every event detector and a mechanically independent artifact-digest or source/event signal before declaring absence or completion.

The Trust Packet is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect digest and authoritative-version faults and **likely (55-80%)** to detect linkage faults, based respectively on fixity and archival-bond mechanisms. Both rates are **UNVERIFIED until the injection trial.**

### 9.3 Self-report contradiction and correlated-failure injection

Make the producing agent claim a test passed when the runtime exit event failed; give two validators the same bad source; then give genuinely independent validators different sources/tools. The register must rank runtime evidence above agent assertion, preserve contradiction, and avoid treating shared dependencies as two independent signals.

### 9.4 Taxonomy stability study

Have independent raters classify the same historical tasks, measure agreement, merge sparse classes, and replay scores across model/prompt/tool version boundaries. A taxonomy is usable only if classes are reproducible and each supported class has enough outcomes. Whether a stable taxonomy exists is **UNVERIFIED; this study settles it.**

### 9.5 Retention reconstruction test

On an isolated copy, apply candidate age/event schedules, then ask a blind reviewer to reproduce why trust was granted and whether the outcome label was justified. Compare reconstruction, storage reduction, sensitive-data exposure, and future taxonomy reclassification ability. Do not delete live data during the experiment.

### 9.6 Cost-sensitive escalation trial

Shadow-run escalation recommendations without changing routing. Measure prevented errors, false escalations, verification latency, and cost across risk tiers. Only after shadow results should thresholds affect autonomy. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that one threshold will be inappropriate across all task/risk classes because verification cost and error consequence differ by definition; the precise policy is **UNVERIFIED until this trial.**

## 10. Acceptance and stop conditions

Adopt the borrow only if all of the following hold:

- Packet creation is predominantly runtime-derived; self-report is separately labeled.
- Every trust display keeps identity, integrity, procedural presumption, current verification, and empirical correctness distinct.
- Per-agent scores are conditioned on a reproducible, versioned task series and show sample size/freshness.
- Unknown and non-comparable states abstain rather than produce a false precision score.
- A verification event states scope and independence dimensions.
- A declaration that evidence is absent, a detector is broken, or a task is done requires two mechanically independent signals; each absence detector first demonstrates a known-present control.
- Fault injection detects output substitution and post-verification mutation reliably.
- Retrospective replay improves calibrated decision quality or cost-sensitive error triage over simpler baselines.
- Retention tests preserve reconstructability at acceptable privacy/storage cost.
- Implementation remains on the existing hook/skill/log bus and does not become a second runtime.

Stop or roll back if:

- provenance completeness is presented as probability of truth;
- task classes cannot be reproduced or are too sparse;
- most fields depend on agent narration rather than runtime events;
- outcome coverage is too low to calibrate and the UI still emits numeric reliability;
- metadata overhead or privacy exposure exceeds demonstrated decision value;
- the design expands into a graph memory, durable execution service, or trained critic merely to preserve the analogy.

## 12. Gaps and evidence limits

1. This unit intentionally did not ground against the local repository or corpus. Therefore it does not claim that Trust Packets, task taxonomies, version identifiers, outcome labels, or comparable mechanisms are absent or present.
2. I did not verify hook payload fields or hook timing. Proposal 1 therefore inherits **UNVERIFIED** status until the trust-packet feasibility spike.
3. The InterPARES requirements were designed for electronic records and long-term preservation, not generative agents. Their value here is structural. No cited archival source establishes improved LLM correctness calibration.
4. InterPARES' Bayesian network is an exploratory research result, not proof that its probabilities transfer to agents. This artifact does not recommend importing its graph or conditional probabilities.
5. No retention duration is recommended. Legal jurisdiction, privacy policy, security classification, owner preferences, storage costs, and incident needs must determine durations.
6. “Independent verification” needs a local threat model. Different agents using the same model, context, and source are not automatically independent.
7. Numeric confidence becomes legitimate only after outcome-backed calibration. Before then, the harness should display categorical evidence states and abstain.
