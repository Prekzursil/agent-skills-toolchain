# X041 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → finite-context stewardship

## Executive judgment

**Verdict: partial transfer, with a sharp boundary.** Nuclear operations has not solved token allocation, semantic compression, or retrieval ranking. It has solved a structurally adjacent problem: how to preserve an accurate operating model while fallible actors change consequential state over long-running work, interruptions, and handoffs. The transferable mechanism is a **graded context-state transaction** combining (1) a declared safe operating envelope, (2) STAR self-checking at a critical state change, (3) genuinely separate verification for the highest-consequence changes, (4) a written turnover with receiver read-back, and (5) independent defence layers plus recovery. The literal “two-person rule for every eviction” does **not** transfer.

The proposed harness primitive is a **Context Lineup Transaction (CLT)**: before compaction or eviction, inventory context items by stable ID; protect a small set of context safety functions; state what the transformation is expected to preserve; create a candidate compacted state; have a mechanically different checker and, only at high risk, a separately prompted verifier challenge it; then make the receiving context reconstruct the objective, constraints, status, evidence, unresolved uncertainty, and next action. If reconstruction fails, do not commit the compaction. Retain the source-event pointers and a rollback horizon.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this narrowed pattern would reduce silent loss of constraints and cross-compaction task drift at acceptable overhead. Evidence: the source-field mechanisms explicitly target wrong-component action, configuration error, incomplete turnover, contrary-evidence discounting, and common human fallibility; the target has analogous state-transition and handoff failure shapes. This effectiveness claim is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the counterfactual long-session replay experiment specified in “Evaluation and falsification,” comparing task-constraint survival and downstream correction rates against the existing compactor.**

It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that a literal dual-LLM approval on every context removal would improve the harness overall. Evidence: DOE itself grades team tools by task hazard and complexity, warns that overusing peer checks causes complacency, and says its tool catalog is a menu rather than a requirement to use every tool; additionally, two calls to the same model family do not provide the personnel, informational, or failure-mode independence required in the source field ([DOE HPI Vol. 2, pp. i, 33, 43](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). This is a design inference, not an empirical result for this harness.

## What nuclear operations actually solved

### 1. Configuration control: preserve a known safe state, not merely a plausible story

Nuclear operations reasons about the **as-found**, **as-left**, and required state of equipment. NRC Information Notice 84-51 arose because systems were incorrectly removed from or returned to service. It says successful programs used explicit procedures and checklists for recurring operations; it also cautions that a functional test can fail to verify the entire affected system. For safety-related alignments, two qualified people are to verify independently and document their checks ([NRC Information Notice 84-51](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/gen-comm/info-notices/1984/in84051)).

DOE draws a precise distinction. “Checking” prevents an incorrect action; “verification” confirms the resulting equipment condition or document accuracy against a governing document. Peer checking is simultaneous and action-focused. Concurrent verification separately confirms condition before, during, and after an action but cannot be truly independent because the people work side by side. Independent verification occurs after the performer’s action, separated in time and distance; the verifier observes the as-found condition without changing it, compares it against the governing source, and signs only on agreement ([DOE HPI Vol. 2, pp. 41-48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The crucial lesson is not “ask twice.” It is:

- define the required post-state before the change;
- distinguish action monitoring from final-state verification;
- compare the result with a governing source, not with the performer’s explanation;
- preserve independence from the cues that produced the first answer;
- document the verification;
- concentrate the expensive check where error consequences are extreme.

### 2. STAR: force an expected-result model around the critical step

DOE’s STAR sequence is **Stop, Think, Act, Review**. Before a critical action, the performer pauses and removes distractions. In “Think,” the performer checks present conditions against the governing document, determines the expected output, and considers a contingency. “Act” is the specific intended action on the correct component. “Review” verifies the actual output against the predicted output and executes the contingency if the result differs ([DOE HPI Vol. 2, pp. 18-19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

STAR is therefore more than a mnemonic for caution. It creates a small, testable prediction before an intervention. That temporal ordering matters: an expectation written after the action is vulnerable to rationalization. DOE also says STAR is especially useful for repetitive, skill-based tasks where attention otherwise becomes automatic, and warns against running several manual actions in rapid succession or failing to verify the result.

### 3. Conservative decision-making: protect the operating envelope under uncertainty

DOE defines conservative decisions as placing facility safety above near-term production. Its practice is not “always choose the most restrictive option.” It says to stay within the safe operating envelope, retain contradictory or disconfirming information, minimize uncertainty through facts and assumption challenges, consider cumulative risk, define contingencies, and establish abort or hold criteria. It recommends a devil’s advocate for significant decisions and a later effectiveness review plus independent review ([DOE HPI Vol. 2, pp. 63-65](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The NRC adds an important correction to naïve conservatism: probabilistic risk insight should complement defence in depth and reduce unnecessary conservatism, while explicitly accounting for uncertainty and focusing resources on the most safety-significant items ([NRC, “Backgrounder on Nuclear Reactor Risk”](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/fact-sheets/reactor-risk)).

Thus, the transferable principle is **risk-informed conservatism**: protect hard limits, spend extra assurance on high-consequence uncertainty, and avoid safety ritual where it adds burden without risk reduction.

### 4. Defence in depth: assume each layer can fail, including through a common cause

The IAEA defines defence in depth as consecutive and independent protection levels. Level 1 prevents deviations; Level 2 detects and controls deviations; Level 3 controls accidents that earlier levels did not; later levels mitigate progression and consequences. The standard explicitly says levels should be independent as far as practicable so that one failure does not reduce another layer’s effectiveness ([IAEA SSR-2/1 Rev. 1, paras. 2.12-2.14 and 4.9-4.13A](https://nucleus.iaea.org/sites/committees/Set%20of%20valid%20safety%20standards%20202409/safety%20standards_%20valid%20set/SSR-2.1%20%28Rev.%201%29%20Safety%20of%20Nuclear%20Power%20Plants%20Design.pdf)).

The operative point for software is failure-mode diversity. Three prompts sent to the same summarizer, all trusting the same generated synopsis, are three labels on one barrier—not defence in depth.

### 5. Turnover: the receiver must establish an accurate mental model before assuming control

DOE defines turnover as an orderly transfer of information, tasks, and responsibility. It should be thorough and accurate yet brief and simple. A written log guided by a checklist backs up oral communication, because oral information can be distorted or forgotten. The log includes completed and remaining work, present equipment status and parameter values, procedures and last steps completed, unusual conditions, unresolved problems, critical steps, contingencies, resources, and contacts. The oncoming person independently reviews the log and relevant records for consistency, asks questions, and assumes responsibility only after establishing an accurate mental model ([DOE HPI Vol. 2, pp. 52-53](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

This is the closest source-field analogue to compaction. A context summary is not primarily an abstract of what happened; it is a **turnover package for the next cognitive shift**.

## Structural transfer test

### Where the analogy holds

1. **State transitions, not static classification.** Eviction and compaction change what can directly influence subsequent action. Nuclear checking practices are designed around a consequential state change and its verified result.

2. **Asymmetric error costs.** Dropping an active prohibition, authorization boundary, unresolved blocker, or acceptance criterion can be much more damaging than retaining a few extra tokens. Risk-informed conservatism fits asymmetric loss.

3. **Long-horizon handoff.** A compacted context is consumed by a later inference pass that did not experience the full history in the same effective way. The turnover requirement to establish an accurate mental model before taking control is structurally similar.

4. **Mindset and confirmation risk.** A summarizer that authored a compact representation is poorly positioned to notice omissions in that representation, analogous to DOE’s concern that a performer close to the task can miss their own error. A second check is valuable only when its information path differs.

5. **Finite attention.** Both fields must keep critical parameters conspicuous amid much more available information. Neither can maximize attention to everything simultaneously.

6. **Common-cause failure matters.** A context manager, semantic verifier, and recovery decision all implemented by the same prompt over the same lossy summary can fail together. The IAEA independence criterion directly illuminates this architecture mistake.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **No physical, stable ground truth.** A valve has an observable position and a procedure can state the required lineup. The “correct” context set is task-dependent, future-dependent, and partly semantic. The same fact can be irrelevant now and decisive later. There is no complete governing document for relevance.

2. **Eviction can be reversible.** Nuclear manipulations may have irreversible physical consequences. Context removal need not be irreversible if the raw event log and source pointers remain available. This weakens the case for routine human-style dual control and strengthens the case for rollback and retrieval.

3. **Replica independence is weak.** Two agents using the same model, system prompt, summary, and source ordering are correlated. Even different model families may share training data and salience biases. Calling the second result “independent verification” without blinding and a different evidence path would misuse the source mechanism.

4. **The hazard scale differs.** Most context errors cost time, correctness, or user trust, not radiological release. Nuclear-strength ceremony for routine context churn would be disproportionate.

5. **Conservatism has a cliff edge in the target.** “Keep it just in case” consumes the very finite resource being protected. Excess pinning can crowd out the latest evidence and instructions. In this domain, maximal retention is not conservative; preserving the operating envelope within a fixed budget is.

6. **Context has adversarial and normative content.** Some text should be excluded or demoted even if salient—for example, obsolete instructions or untrusted embedded directions. Nuclear equipment state does not itself issue competing instructions. The analogy offers no full solution to instruction authority or prompt injection.

7. **STAR is not reasoning magic.** The same model can confidently predict and later confirm the same mistaken postcondition. STAR contributes temporal structure; it does not create truth.

**Transfer conclusion:** the configuration-control, graded-verification, turnover, and independent-layer patterns transfer. Literal staffing ratios, physical-lineup semantics, and universal conservative retention do not. The proposal below deliberately stops at the transferable boundary.

## Proposed design

### Proposal A — Context Integrity Envelope — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** — **UNVERIFIED**

Define a small, typed manifest of **context safety functions** that every compaction must preserve, not a permanently pinned copy of every historically important sentence. Suggested fields:

```text
mission.current            current user objective and success condition
authority.current          system/developer constraints and granted action scope
commitments.open           promised deliverables and incomplete required work
state.current              what is known to have happened; current plan/step
evidence.critical          source IDs/paths/URLs that support consequential conclusions
uncertainty.open           unresolved questions, assumptions, conflicts, failed checks
decisions.active           decision + rationale + supersession relation
hazards.active             destructive-action risks, abort criteria, sensitive boundaries
artifacts.current          output targets and last verified state
next                       next safe action and completion/verification criteria
```

Each item gets a stable ID, authority/provenance, last-confirmed event ID, disposition (`verbatim`, `condensed`, `external-pointer`, `superseded`), and explicit superseder when applicable. “Protected” means the safety function must remain reconstructable; it does not mean the original text must remain verbatim forever.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that typed safety functions will outperform a single free-form “important context” score for preserving hard constraints. Evidence: the field separates safety functions and operating limits from general plant information, and turnover uses a checklist of distinct state categories. The target effectiveness premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an ablation replay comparing free-form salience ranking against the typed manifest on constraint survival, stale-item retention, and token cost.**

Key anti-bloat rule: every protected item must have an expiry condition, supersession rule, or external source pointer. A validator rejects a protected item that has none. This converts conservatism from “retain indefinitely” to “retain until a named condition makes safe removal demonstrable.”

What it is intended to catch:

- a summary retaining the broad goal but dropping a negated constraint;
- a decision retained without the evidence or uncertainty that qualified it;
- an older user direction surviving after a newer direction superseded it;
- an output path or acceptance test disappearing while prose history remains;
- “work attempted” silently becoming “work completed” during summarization.

### Proposal B — STAR Context Lineup Transaction — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** — **UNVERIFIED**

Implement compaction as a transaction with a before/after record:

1. **Stop.** Trigger only at a budget threshold or explicit phase boundary. Freeze the source event range and assign the transaction an ID. Do not allow the same call to continue issuing side-effecting tools while compaction is unresolved.
2. **Think.** Produce a disposition table for stable context IDs: `KEEP`, `CONDENSE`, `POINTER`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `DROP`. For every `CONDENSE` or `DROP` of a protected or dependency-bearing item, state the expected postcondition, source pointer, dependents, uncertainty, and rollback condition.
3. **Act.** Generate the candidate turnover capsule and candidate active context. Do not yet delete the rollback source.
4. **Review.** Run deterministic manifest checks, then ask the receiving context—not the authoring context—to answer a fixed read-back: current objective, authority limits, open commitments, current state, critical evidence, unresolved uncertainty, abort criteria, output target, and next action. Compare structured answers with the pre-transaction manifest. Commit only on agreement; otherwise restore the previous state and either retry with a larger budget or escalate.

The important translation is **predict before transforming, inspect the as-left state, and run the contingency on mismatch**. Merely asking the summarizer “did you preserve everything important?” is not STAR and is not verification.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that post-compaction read-back will catch gross omissions that token-count or schema-validity checks miss. Evidence: it tests the operational state the next inference actually sees, and DOE turnover requires the receiver to independently establish the working model. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting known omissions into candidate capsules and measuring read-back detection, false-pass rate, extra tokens, and downstream task recovery.**

Suggested hold criteria:

- current mission or action authority cannot be reconstructed;
- a protected item has neither retained content nor a resolvable pointer;
- two active decisions conflict without an explicit conflict marker;
- a claim changes status from uncertain/failed/unverified to verified/done;
- the receiving read-back names a next action inconsistent with the manifest;
- rollback source is unavailable before commit.

### Proposal C — Graded two-channel verification, not a universal two-person rule — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** — **UNVERIFIED**

Risk-score each context transformation using transparent inputs:

- consequence: destructive/external action, legal/security boundary, promised deliverable, or ordinary exploration;
- irreversibility: raw source available and addressable, available but expensive, or unavailable;
- novelty/uncertainty: routine phase rollover versus conflicting or poorly sourced state;
- compression severity: fraction removed and whether protected items are condensed;
- horizon: expected number of later turns dependent on the capsule;
- anomaly flags: contradiction, unresolved error, user correction, or instruction supersession.

Apply three assurance grades:

- **Grade 0 — routine:** deterministic schema, provenance, and budget checks; raw source remains retrievable.
- **Grade 1 — consequential:** full STAR transaction plus receiving-context read-back and rollback.
- **Grade 2 — critical:** before seeing the proposer’s disposition, a separate verifier constructs a blinded “must-retain” set from the governing objective, constraints, and frozen source range. Compare that set with the proposed as-left state. Any missing intersection item holds the transaction. Add explicit acknowledgment for the very small class of transformations that would discard the only copy of authority-critical or user-owned information.

The two channels should be mechanically different:

1. a deterministic checker validates IDs, provenance, supersession, status monotonicity, source reachability, budget, and rollback availability;
2. a semantic verifier assesses meaning preservation and constructs its keep set without seeing the proposer’s keep/drop choices.

The semantic call is **not** claimed to be nuclear-grade independent verification. It is a correlated second opinion. Independence improves if it uses a different prompt, blinded proposal, different context ordering, and—where available without adding a runtime—a different model family. It remains weaker than an external observable.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that the Grade 2 semantic verifier adds enough unique recall beyond deterministic checks and receiver read-back to justify its token/latency cost. Evidence for benefit is DOE’s finding that separation from the performer improves error detection; counterevidence is correlated model failure and the absence of a stable physical truth criterion. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a factorial experiment: deterministic only vs. read-back vs. blinded same-model verifier vs. blinded different-model verifier, measuring unique caught defects and cost per consequential defect.**

### Proposal D — Independent defence layers and rollback horizon — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** — **UNVERIFIED**

Use four layers with explicitly different failure modes:

1. **Prevent:** typed context envelope, stable IDs, authority and supersession rules.
2. **Detect:** STAR disposition preview, deterministic invariants, and receiver read-back.
3. **Recover:** append-only raw event/source pointers plus the last known-good active-context snapshot for a bounded rollback horizon.
4. **Mitigate:** on failed reconstruction, enter a safe context state: stop side effects, disclose uncertainty, retrieve the cited source range, and rebuild the capsule at a larger allowance.

Do not count multiple LLM summaries as multiple layers. Do not place the rollback index only inside the summary it must recover. Do not let the semantic verifier rely solely on the candidate capsule. At least one layer must operate on stable IDs and event metadata, and recovery must address the pre-compaction source.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that keeping a bounded, addressable rollback source makes a failed compaction less consequential than destructive deletion. Evidence: recovery does not depend on correctly inferring omitted semantics if the original range is still retrievable; this is a direct systems property, not a claim that rollback will be invoked correctly. It is **UNVERIFIED whether the target harness’s storage and privacy constraints permit the proposed rollback horizon; settle it with a storage/retention-policy review and a prototype measuring source-address success after compaction.**

### Proposal E — Turnover capsule optimized for resumption, not narrative — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** — **UNVERIFIED**

The capsule should be concise but operational:

```yaml
objective:
success_condition:
authority_and_prohibitions:
completed_with_evidence:
in_progress_and_last_safe_point:
open_commitments:
unresolved_errors_conflicts_uncertainty:
active_decisions_and_rationale:
critical_source_pointers:
artifacts_and_verified_state:
abort_hold_criteria:
next_safe_action:
superseded_items:
capsule_source_range:
```

Require the receiving inference to read back the high-risk fields before resuming a side effect. This is analogous to DOE’s oncoming worker reviewing the written log, relevant records, and work state before responsibility transfers. A fluent chronology is optional; resumable state is mandatory.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a resumption-oriented capsule will reduce “beautiful summary, wrong next action” failures compared with a narrative abstract. Evidence: the DOE turnover checklist is explicitly designed to establish situation awareness and includes last completed step, outstanding work, unusual conditions, contingencies, and resources. This target-domain premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by giving blinded receiving agents either narrative or structured capsules from the same long sessions and scoring next-action correctness, constraint recall, and unnecessary retrieval.**

## What not to build

1. **No second orchestration runtime.** The transaction belongs in the existing hook/skill/MCP bus. A new durable workflow engine would violate the anti-overfit gate and is not justified by this analogy.
2. **No vector database or knowledge graph.** Stable event IDs, typed manifests, and direct source pointers are sufficient for this experiment. The source field’s contribution is configuration control, not associative memory infrastructure.
3. **No trained critic.** A blinded semantic verifier is an experiment, not a new learned component. Deterministic invariants and counterfactual replay must establish marginal value first.
4. **No observability SaaS or guardrail framework.** Emit local transaction records and evaluation metrics through the existing bus.
5. **No always-on multi-agent quorum.** DOE warns that peer-check overuse leads to complacency and explicitly grades team tools by hazard and complexity. Universal quorum also multiplies correlated calls without guaranteeing independence ([DOE HPI Vol. 2, pp. 33 and 42-43](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
6. **No “pin everything safety-relevant forever.”** In a finite window, that can itself violate the operating envelope. Retain the safety function and provenance, not necessarily all original words.

Every proposal above rides an existing bus and avoids the previously rejected component classes. The design is intentionally a small protocol plus schema, not a context-management platform.

## Evaluation and falsification

### Experiment 1: counterfactual long-session replay

Use completed long sessions with known later owner corrections and consequential actions. At each historical compaction boundary, replay from the same pre-boundary state with:

- baseline context mechanism;
- envelope only;
- envelope + STAR/read-back;
- envelope + STAR/read-back + Grade 2 verifier.

Score without relying solely on another LLM:

- exact survival of authority/prohibition IDs;
- exact survival or explicit supersession of open commitments;
- source-pointer reachability;
- status inflation (`attempted` → `done`) rate;
- correct next action against the historical continuation and task plan;
- downstream owner correction rate;
- tool-side-effect divergence before correction;
- tokens, latency, retries, and number of held transactions;
- false holds where the baseline continuation was correct;
- stale protected-item accumulation over time.

Where semantic scoring is unavoidable, use blinded human adjudication on a stratified sample and report inter-rater agreement. A judge model alone would share too many failure modes with the system under test.

### Experiment 2: defect injection/detector controls

Create paired capsules with one controlled defect each from the table above. Include known-present controls so the detector proves it can find an intact protected item before any absence/broken claim. Use two mechanically independent signals for every claimed detection: for example, stable-ID mismatch plus receiver read-back mismatch; or pointer reachability failure plus source-evidence absence. Re-running the same semantic prompt is not a second signal.

### Experiment 3: independence ablation

Measure unique defect catches for:

1. proposer self-review;
2. same-model unblinded reviewer;
3. same-model blinded must-retain generation;
4. different-model blinded must-retain generation;
5. deterministic manifest checker;
6. deterministic checker plus receiving read-back.

The important output is not aggregate agreement. It is the conditional probability that a layer catches a defect missed by previous layers. If the semantic verifier adds negligible unique recall, remove it.

### Experiment 4: retention-pressure stress

Run multi-phase tasks where every phase introduces plausible “important” facts. Confirm that expiry, supersession, and externalization keep the protected set bounded. Measure whether the latest working evidence is crowded out. This directly tests the disanalogy that conservatism can cause context exhaustion.

### Go/no-go criteria

Exact numerical thresholds require a baseline and cost model and are therefore **UNVERIFIED; settle them by measuring the baseline distributions before preregistering thresholds.** Qualitatively:

- **Go** for the envelope if it materially improves hard-constraint survival without unbounded pinned growth.
- **Go** for STAR/read-back if it catches injected consequential omissions with low false-hold rates and acceptable token cost.
- **Go** for Grade 2 only if it catches a meaningful set of defects missed by deterministic checks and read-back.
- **No-go** if protected state grows monotonically, if receiver read-back mostly mirrors the same omissions, or if verification cost causes earlier/more aggressive compaction that erases the benefit.

## Failure analysis of the proposal itself

1. **Checklist capture.** The manifest can turn attention toward named fields and away from novel dependencies. Mitigation: allow an `other_critical` field, track corrections caused by untyped omissions, and periodically revise categories only from observed failures.

2. **Status gaming.** A fluent model can satisfy the schema with inaccurate content. Mitigation: source IDs and monotonic status rules; the schema is not evidence.

3. **Correlated verification.** The proposer and semantic verifier can share salience blind spots. Mitigation: blinding, different ordering/model when cheap, and a deterministic layer. Do not call this full independence.

4. **Protection creep.** Everything can become “critical.” Mitigation: expiry/supersession requirement, token budget per safety function, and periodic risk reclassification.

5. **Rollback theatre.** A pointer can exist but fail when needed. Mitigation: pre-commit reachability check and periodic recovery drills against sampled old transactions.

6. **Transaction races.** Tool results or user overrides can arrive while compaction is built. Mitigation: freeze an event range, reject commit if the head changed, and rebuild from the new head. This is configuration control, not a new runtime.

7. **Overhead-induced harm.** Verification tokens can precipitate compaction. Mitigation: grades, deterministic checks first, and empirical removal of layers with no unique defect recall.

8. **False analogy authority.** Nuclear vocabulary can make a weak software proposal sound proven. Mitigation: preserve the explicit **UNVERIFIED** status of target-domain efficacy and let replay falsify each layer independently.

## Final recommendation

Adopt the nuclear borrow as a **small, risk-graded context transaction protocol**, not as institutional theatre:

1. First implement the typed Context Integrity Envelope — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**.
2. Then prototype STAR compaction with receiving-context read-back and bounded rollback — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.
3. Add deterministic, mechanically different invariants — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.
4. Treat blinded semantic dual verification as an optional Grade 2 experiment; keep it only if it demonstrates unique catches — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

The first three steps are the strongest part of the borrow.
