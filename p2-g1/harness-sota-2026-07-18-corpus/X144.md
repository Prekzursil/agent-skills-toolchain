# X144 — Cross-domain borrow: cartography → agent memory

## Executive verdict

**The analogy transfers, but only partially.** Cartography's useful contribution is not the vague maxim “compress old information.” It is the combination of (1) purpose- and scale-specific representations, (2) named generalisation operators that make omission deliberate, (3) persistent links between detailed source objects and every coarser representation, and (4) a generalisation log that permits backward authentication and forward invalidation/update propagation.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this is a structurally sound borrow for *working-memory views and stale-summary detection*: the evidence is that cartographic multiple-representation databases already link one reality across abstraction levels, including 1→0 omission and n→1 aggregation, and use derivation histories for update propagation ([Zhang et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2018.05.022); [Zhou et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2009.06.004)). Agent memory has the same relevant shape when a detailed evidence record gives rise to a session brief, project summary, or owner preference summary.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that cartography does *not* supply a sufficient rule for irreversible forgetting: the evidence is that generalisation normally derives a less detailed product while retaining or presupposing a more detailed source database; ArcGIS explicitly describes maintaining data at the largest scale and generalising it for smaller-scale display ([ArcGIS, “Understanding conflict resolution and generalization”](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/cartography/understanding-conflict-resolution-and-generalization.htm)), while the generalisation-log work is designed around source-to-product derivation and versioning, not destruction of source truth ([Zhou et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2009.06.004)). Therefore this unit recommends reversible omission from active context and invalidation of derived memories, but it does **not** claim that cartography tells the harness when privacy, retention, cost, or user-requested deletion warrants destroying canonical records.

The concrete borrow is a small **memory atlas**: canonical evidence-backed claims remain the “large-scale” source; task-appropriate memory views are derived “map products”; every derivation records inputs, operator, parameters, and rule version; a source change dirties all dependent views; and retrieval chooses a view according to an explicit scope/purpose contract. This proposal remains an ideation result rather than a validated harness design. Its effectiveness is **UNVERIFIED; the `Atlas Replay A/B` experiment defined below would settle it.**

## Scope and method

This is deliberately a research-wide, cross-domain lens. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, repository, corpus, audit findings, or known defects. “What we currently miss” below means failure classes that ordinary unlinked summaries cannot detect; it is not a claim that this owner’s harness in fact lacks a particular implementation.

I used primary or operationally authoritative sources where possible: Ordnance Survey product documentation for actual production operators, USGS and ArcGIS documentation for generalisation principles and workflows, an ISPRS paper for explicit scale-dependent rules, and peer-reviewed papers for multiple-representation databases and derivation logs. No Windows-only input was required.

## What cartography actually solved

### 1. A map is a purpose- and scale-bounded representation, not a shrunken copy

Cartographic generalisation begins with an output constraint. Less display space at a smaller scale forces fewer objects and less geometry, but the aim is to preserve the important elements and characteristic arrangement—not to uniformly compress every feature. Ordnance Survey defines generalisation in its production guide as reducing scale and complexity while maintaining the important elements and characteristics of the location. Its actual operator list includes selection/omission, simplification, exaggeration, aggregation, symbolisation, and displacement ([OS VectorMap District product guide, pp. 6–7](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/product-support/user-guide/os-vectormap-district-product-guide.pdf)). USGS likewise describes choosing the proper amount of content for a map and how it is displayed ([USGS CEGIS, “Generalization”](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/science-topics/generalization)).

Purpose matters alongside nominal scale. ArcGIS's documented workflow begins by establishing target scale, display specification, and map purpose, then reduces feature count and detail, and finally detects conflicts under the final symbology ([ArcGIS, “Automating conflict resolution and generalization workflows”](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/cartography/conflict-resolution-and-generalization-with-geoprocessing.htm)). Ordnance Survey makes the same point in plainer terms: map features are selected according to the map's purpose and intended audience ([Ordnance Survey, “What is Cartography?”](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/what-is-cartography)).

This solves a problem that “summarize to N tokens” does not solve. The cartographic output is conditional on intended use. A road atlas, geological map, and tourist map of the same area retain different things even at the same physical scale. Capacity is a constraint; purpose determines salience.

### 2. Generalisation uses typed transformations, including deliberate omission

The operator vocabulary is specific enough to audit:

| Cartographic operator | How it works in cartography | Memory translation |
|---|---|---|
| Selection / omission | A feature is retained or left out at the target scale. An omitted feature may still exist in the detailed source. | Include a claim in the active view, or record a deliberate 1→0 omission without deleting its source. |
| Simplification | Remove detail while preserving essential form; for example, fewer vertices represent a winding stream. | Shorten a claim or episode while retaining qualifiers that define its decision-relevant meaning. |
| Aggregation / amalgamation | Combine nearby or similar objects into one coarser object. | Combine repeated episodes into a pattern such as “owner consistently prefers X,” preserving links to every episode. |
| Collapse / symbolisation | Change representation dimension; for example, a city polygon becomes a point or a detailed station becomes a symbol. | Replace a detailed procedure with a compact pointer/label that can be expanded on demand. |
| Exaggeration | Keep a small but important feature visible by enlarging it beyond literal scale. | Promote a rare but high-cost constraint—security boundary, explicit prohibition, irreversible-action rule—even if frequency-based summarisation would discard it. |
| Displacement | Move a depiction to prevent overlap while retaining the underlying relationship. | Separate conflicting claims in presentation instead of blending them into a false consensus; this is only a presentational analogy, not permission to change claim semantics. |

The table's cartographic meanings are directly supported by Ordnance Survey's production guide ([OS VectorMap District product guide, pp. 6–7](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/product-support/user-guide/os-vectormap-district-product-guide.pdf)). ArcGIS supplies concrete implementations: aggregate nearby polygons, collapse dual roads to centerlines, simplify geometry while preserving essential shape, and preserve topological relationships on shared edges ([ArcGIS Generalization toolset](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/cartography/an-overview-of-the-generalization-toolset.htm)).

The key memory lesson is that omission is an operator with provenance, not evidence that the omitted thing is false, unimportant at every scale, or safe to erase. This distinction makes “forget from the prompt” reversible.

### 3. Multiple-representation databases link every scale to the same object

A multiple-representation database (MRDB) stores datasets at different abstraction levels and connects representations of the same real-world object across scales. The links are not merely one-to-one. Generalisation yields n→1 aggregation, 1→0 deletion/omission, and n→m typification; these relations can be recorded explicitly ([Zhang et al., 2018, especially the MRDB discussion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2018.05.022)).

This is more than a folder of summaries. Persistent cross-scale identity enables two different traversals:

1. From a coarse map feature backward to its detailed sources for authentication and quality analysis.
2. From a changed source forward to every derived depiction that may now need recomputation.

Zhou et al. explicitly distinguish these directions and require a unique persistent identifier for every feature or representation ([Zhou et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2009.06.004)). That bidirectional traceability is the central mechanism worth borrowing.

### 4. A generalisation log records how the view was made

Zhou et al.'s generalisation log represents derivation as a directed acyclic diagram of prioritised process instances and atomic operations. It stores parameters, feature roles, history, and versions. The full history can support authentication, quality analysis, tuning, and subsequent update propagation ([Zhou et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2009.06.004)).

This addresses the otherwise invisible difference between:

- “The source never contained this detail.”
- “The source contained it and an explicit rule omitted it.”
- “Several sources were aggregated into this statement.”
- “This output was produced under an older rule.”
- “This source changed after the output was produced.”

Without derivation lineage, those cases look identical in a short summary.

### 5. Updates propagate through dependencies, and local changes can have chain reactions

Incremental generalisation updates a detailed or timely source and propagates the change across linked representations rather than rebuilding or manually reconciling every product. Zhang et al. report a framework that uses cross-scale links and inferred generalisation history, then handles cartographic conflicts and “chain reactions,” where a local update affects neighboring features and linkage structure ([Zhang et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2018.05.022)). Earlier work describes an explicit sequence: examine whether a target object is created/modified/deleted, propagate and validate, generalise for the target scale, then resolve spatial conflicts ([Harrie, 1998](https://www.gis.lu.se/lars-harrie/publication/6e810476-eea3-4099-8d9d-84166356e389)).

The important point is not that cartography has perfectly automated updates. It has framed the consistency problem correctly: a derived view becomes suspect when its sources or transformation context change, and the affected neighborhood can be wider than the directly edited object.

### 6. Scale rules can be data, not hard-coded control flow

The scale-dependent framework of Meijers et al. represents class importance, compatibility, attractiveness regions, and generalisation operations as functions of scale and class. Its motivation is to replace “one fits all” and hard-coded operator choice with explicit, adjustable knowledge ([Meijers et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-XLII-4-425-2018)). OGC's Maps API standard similarly makes scale denominator an explicit request parameter rather than an implicit guess ([OGC API – Maps – Part 1: Core](https://docs.ogc.org/is/20-058/20-058.html)).

For memory, this suggests that retrieval detail and salience rules belong in inspectable policy, parameterized by task scope and purpose, rather than being hidden inside one summarisation prompt.

## Concrete translation: the memory atlas

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: linked, scale-dependent memory views

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Build a pilot as a small library plus existing lifecycle-hook calls; do not create a daemon, scheduler, second agent runtime, or new database service.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED because no evidence in this unit shows that this harness's existing memory bus exposes the required read/write and lifecycle points; the `Bus Fit Probe` experiment below would settle it.** Conditional on those points existing, I am **likely (55-80%)** that a useful pilot fits under roughly 200 lines: the evidence is that the minimum behavior is append-only JSONL records, adjacency lookup, dirty marking, and policy-selected rendering, not a novel persistence service. The wide confidence band reflects unknown host APIs and Windows concurrency behavior.

Use five record types:

```text
source_claim
  id, subject, predicate, object/content
  evidence_refs[], observed_at, valid_from?, valid_to?
  source_kind, confidence, sensitivity, retention_class

view
  id, purpose, scope, detail_band, token_budget
  generated_at, policy_version, status={fresh,dirty,contested}

derivation_edge
  source_ids[], output_id
  operator={select,simplify,aggregate,collapse,exaggerate,omit}
  parameters, generator_id, generator_version, created_at

conflict
  claim_ids[], relation={contradicts,supersedes,qualifies}
  resolution_status, resolution_evidence?

invalidation
  changed_id, affected_id, cause, detected_at, repaired_at?
```

The “large-scale source” is not an unbounded transcript. It is a set of atomic, evidence-addressable claims and episodes. The “smaller-scale maps” are views such as:

- **Task viewport:** detailed claims for the current objective, nearby constraints, and open decisions.
- **Project map:** durable decisions, interfaces, hazards, and unresolved contradictions; most execution minutiae omitted.
- **Owner atlas:** stable preferences and prohibitions aggregated across projects, with rare high-impact constraints exaggerated.
- **Orientation map:** a compact index of projects/topics that collapses details to expandable pointers.

“Scale” must be an explicit tuple rather than one scalar: `{scope breadth, decision stakes, time horizon, token budget, audience}`. A focused debugging task requests a large-scale detailed viewport; portfolio orientation requests a small-scale overview; a security-sensitive action raises decision stakes and forces otherwise tiny constraints into view.

Each rendered statement carries a compact lineage token. The user or agent can “zoom in” by resolving it to source claims, evidence, and the transformation log. A view may omit a claim, but the derivation log records 1→0 omission with its policy and purpose. That makes absence from a view explainable.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: stale-view detection by forward invalidation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** On creation, correction, contradiction, expiry, or deletion of a source claim, traverse outgoing derivation edges and mark dependent views `dirty`. Do not ask an LLM to infer freshness from prose. At the next retrieval boundary, either regenerate the dirty fragment or disclose it as stale and pull the underlying evidence.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED because the useful invalidation radius and false-positive rate are unknown; the `Stale Atlas Mutation Test` below would settle it.** I am **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit lineage catches direct stale summaries more reliably than timestamp-only expiry: the evidence is the mechanically defined source→derivative dependency and the cartographic use of forward traceability for update propagation ([Zhou et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2009.06.004)). I am only **roughly even (45-55%)** that automatic *repair* will preserve semantic quality without review: the evidence against confidence is cartography's own need for contextual conflict handling and chain-reaction management ([Zhang et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2018.05.022)). The safe pilot invalidates automatically but regenerates conservatively.

Neighbor invalidation uses explicit semantic relations, not embedding proximity. For example, changing “deployment target is Linux” invalidates the project summary statement derived from it, plus procedures explicitly linked as depending on that target. It does not dirty every vaguely similar memory.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: deliberate omission and reversible forgetting

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Define per-view importance curves and hard preservation constraints:

```text
preserve regardless of token pressure:
  explicit owner prohibitions
  security/privacy boundaries
  irreversible-action constraints
  unresolved contradictions relevant to the task
  active acceptance criteria

prefer at detailed task scale:
  exact commands, file locations, observed errors, failed attempts

aggregate at project scale:
  repeated episodes, implementation minutiae, closed substeps

omit from active view, retain in source:
  irrelevant episodes with a recorded omission edge
```

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED because cartographic salience does not establish the optimal importance function for agent tasks; the `Scale Policy Ablation` below would settle it.** I am **likely (55-80%)** that typed omission reduces silent loss relative to free-form summarisation: the evidence is operational cartography's explicit selection/omission and exaggeration operators ([OS VectorMap District product guide](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/product-support/user-guide/os-vectormap-district-product-guide.pdf)), while the uncertainty comes from semantic evaluation being harder than spatial legibility.

Irreversible deletion remains a separate retention policy:

- Delete when the owner explicitly requests deletion.
- Delete when a governing retention or privacy rule requires it.
- Delete transient sensitive data at its declared retention boundary.
- Otherwise evict from active views before deleting canonical evidence.

These are normative harness rules, not results borrowed from cartography. Their adequacy is **UNVERIFIED; a privacy/retention threat-model review would settle the required classes and deletion semantics.** Cartography supports the last bullet's separation of source from depiction, but it does not justify the first three.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: conflict preservation instead of summary blending

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** When sources disagree, preserve both claims and a typed conflict edge. At coarse scale, render “deployment target contested” rather than choosing or averaging. At detailed scale, show both evidence chains. Resolution creates a new evidence-backed `supersedes` edge; it does not rewrite history.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED because the cartographic idea of resolving graphical overlap does not itself validate semantic contradiction handling; the `Contradiction Replay` experiment below would settle it.** I am **likely (55-80%)** that explicit conflict state prevents a derived summary from presenting false consensus: the evidence is logical—two retained incompatible claims remain observable—rather than an empirical result from cartography. The borrowed part is narrower: cartography warns that local simplification can create contextual conflicts and therefore checks features collectively ([ArcGIS, “Understanding conflict resolution and generalization”](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/cartography/understanding-conflict-resolution-and-generalization.htm)).

## What this catches that unlinked memory views miss

The following are capability claims about the proposed mechanism, not claims about the current local harness:

1. **Stale summary after correction.** A source preference changes, but an old project brief repeats the former preference. Forward lineage marks the exact brief fragment dirty.
2. **False absence.** A constraint does not appear in the current context. A 1→0 omission edge distinguishes deliberate scale-specific omission from never-recorded information or derivation failure.
3. **Aggregation overreach.** “Usually prefers concise output” was aggregated from three episodes, then treated as universal. Backward lineage exposes the sample and permits a new counterexample to dirty the aggregate.
4. **Lost rare hazard.** A once-observed destructive-action prohibition disappears under frequency-based compression. Exaggeration policy keeps rare, high-cost constraints visible.
5. **Rule drift.** Views generated under an obsolete generalisation policy remain in storage. `policy_version` permits bulk invalidation without guessing from text.
6. **Conflicting source memories blended into confidence.** Typed conflict links prevent compression from producing a single unsupported statement.
7. **Changed context with dependent procedures.** A source fact changes and linked procedures become suspect even though their own text did not change—the memory equivalent of a cartographic chain reaction.
8. **Premature destructive forgetting.** Token pressure removes a claim from the active view but not the canonical layer; zoom/drill-down can recover it.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that items 1, 2, and 5 are detectable if all derivations are logged, because detection follows directly from graph reachability and stored rule versions. I am **likely (55-80%)** that items 3, 4, 6, and 7 improve real task outcomes, based on the explicit evidence paths and policies, but that outcome effect is **UNVERIFIED; `Atlas Replay A/B`, `Contradiction Replay`, and `Scale Policy Ablation` would settle it.** Item 8 is **almost certain (90-99%)** only in the narrow technical sense of recoverability when the canonical layer is retained; the evidence is the proposed storage invariant, not a measured user benefit.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Structural correspondences that hold

| Cartography | Memory | Why the structure matches |
|---|---|---|
| Detailed source feature | Evidence-backed claim/episode | Both can have persistent identity and multiple derived representations. |
| Map product at a scale and purpose | Context view for a task and audience | Both face bounded display/context capacity and purpose-dependent salience. |
| Generalisation operator | Memory transformation | Both select, simplify, aggregate, or replace detail with a pointer. |
| Cross-scale relation | Derivation edge | Both need many-to-one, one-to-zero, and many-to-many provenance. |
| Updated terrain/source database | Corrected/new evidence | Both can invalidate downstream representations without editing their text directly. |
| Generalisation log | Memory lineage log | Both support backward authentication and forward invalidation. |

The strongest isomorphism is therefore **source → typed transformation → purpose-specific representation**, not “geographic distance equals semantic similarity.”

### Disanalogies that limit or defeat parts of the borrow

1. **Maps represent a shared spatial substrate; memories often do not.** Geographic objects have coordinates, topology, and neighborhood. Claims have heterogeneous relations whose extraction may be uncertain. There is no safe general equivalent of “within 20 metres, aggregate.” Consequently, semantic neighbor invalidation must use explicit dependency edges; embedding similarity is not a cartographic transfer.

2. **Cartographic scale is substantially ordered; memory detail is multidimensional.** A 1:100,000 map is coarser than 1:10,000 in a well-defined physical sense. A project overview may be terse about implementation but extremely detailed about compliance. Treating memory scale as a single token count would force a false ordering. The tuple `{scope, stakes, horizon, budget, audience}` is a repair, but it weakens the elegance of the analogy.

3. **A master map database can be treated as authoritative for derivation; memory sources can be wrong.** Conversation statements conflict, preferences evolve, and model-generated claims may be hallucinated. The canonical layer must therefore mean “retained evidence and claims,” not “ground truth.” Confidence and conflict cannot be flattened away.

4. **Omission is usually tolerable in a map product because users can change scale or consult the source.** An agent can act irreversibly before anyone notices an omitted constraint. This makes high-stakes “exaggeration” and drill-down gates necessary, but it also means cartographic legibility metrics are insufficient safety metrics.

5. **Cartographic generalisation may distort geometry to preserve legibility.** Displacement and exaggeration are acceptable depiction techniques when map readers understand the convention. Semantic memory cannot move a date, alter a quantity, or strengthen confidence for readability. Only presentation priority—not factual content—may be exaggerated or displaced.

6. **Incremental map-update research often assumes synchronous, logically consistent representations.** Zhang et al. state this premise explicitly before propagating updates ([Zhang et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2018.05.022)). Agent memories are frequently asynchronous and contradictory. Therefore automatic update propagation cannot safely mean silent rewriting; the transfer is automatic *invalidation*, followed by evidence-aware regeneration or review.

7. **Generalisation is not forgetting.** The map product omits detail while the source persists. Actual deletion involves privacy, consent, legal retention, security, and cost—dimensions the source field does not solve. Any claim that cartography determines memory TTLs would be a category error.

8. **Maps are often generated with deterministic geometric operators; LLM summaries are stochastic and semantically lossy.** Logging a prompt and model version does not guarantee reproducibility. A regenerated view needs semantic checks against source claims, and this proposal does not establish a sufficient checker.

Because of disanalogies 3, 4, 6, and 8, I am **very unlikely (5-20%)** to endorse fully automatic source mutation or irreversible deletion based solely on this borrow. The evidence is the mismatch between cartography's consistency premise and contested semantic memory, plus the absence of a cartographic analogue for privacy/retention authority.

## Anti-overfit gate and rejected extensions

All proposals above ride an existing hook/skill/MCP lifecycle and use ordinary records plus deterministic graph traversal. They do not require a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph service, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The derivation edges form a small DAG, but this is not a proposal for a “knowledge graph memory” component. It is an append-only provenance index over IDs, representable as JSONL and rebuilt in memory. Calling it a graph does not justify deploying graph infrastructure.

Rejected extensions:

- **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime — needs its own justification): rejected.** A continuously running “memory cartographer” would duplicate lifecycle orchestration and has no evidence of benefit over event hooks.
- **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime — needs its own justification): rejected.** A vector database for semantic neighborhoods would import uncertain similarity into invalidation and reintroduce a previously rejected component.
- **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime — needs its own justification): rejected.** A trained critic to score generalisation quality has no task-specific labels and would add a second learned failure surface.
- **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus): rejected for the pilot.** Automatic rewriting of all dirty views is small enough mechanically, but semantically unsafe until regeneration quality is measured; dirty marking plus on-demand regeneration is the narrower test.

The minimal architecture is deliberate: provenance and invalidation add one missing invariant, while the model remains responsible for task execution. I am **likely (55-80%)** that this is less prone to scaffold overfit than a separate memory runtime, because it constrains only storage lineage and retrieval while leaving reasoning in the base model; this comparative outcome remains **UNVERIFIED, and `Atlas Replay A/B` against both plain summaries and any proposed larger runtime would settle it.**

## Experiments that settle the unverified premises

### 1. `Bus Fit Probe`

Goal: establish whether a BUILD-SMALL pilot can ride existing lifecycle surfaces.

Procedure:

1. Identify a known-present session-start read point, correction/write point, and pre-context-compaction point.
2. Validate each detector with a synthetic marker before interpreting absence; use a second mechanically independent signal such as produced log record versus visible injected context.
3. Implement no memory semantics. Measure whether an append-only claim, edge, and dirty marker can be written/read atomically under concurrent sessions on Windows.
4. Pass criterion: all three lifecycle events are observed through two independent signals, and 1,000 concurrent synthetic appends produce no truncation or malformed record.

This experiment specifically prevents a broken detector from being mistaken for absent hook support.

### 2. `Atlas Replay A/B`

Goal: test whether lineage-backed multiscale views improve cross-session work.

Procedure:

1. Sample at least 200 historical task continuations containing a later correction, changed decision, or task resumption; reserve a blinded evaluation subset.
2. A: existing/plain summary retrieval. B: equal-token memory-atlas view. Keep model, prompt, and tool access fixed.
3. Score correction adherence, unsupported-memory rate, recovery of decisive constraints, task success, context tokens, and latency.
4. Have evaluators trace every claimed remembered fact to source evidence. Measure both beneficial retrieval and harmful stale retrieval.
5. Pass criterion proposed for pre-registration: ≥25% relative reduction in stale/unsupported memory errors, no statistically credible task-success regression, and ≤10% retrieval-latency increase.

The numeric thresholds are design choices, not cartographic facts. Whether they are appropriate is **UNVERIFIED; pre-registration review by the harness owner would settle acceptance criteria before results are seen.**

### 3. `Stale Atlas Mutation Test`

Goal: measure invalidation completeness and blast radius.

Procedure:

1. Generate synthetic sources and views with known 1→1, n→1, 1→0, n→m, conflict, and supersession relations.
2. Mutate, expire, contradict, and delete one source at a time.
3. Compare marked-dirty views with the oracle dependency closure; independently inspect rendered views for semantically affected statements not represented by edges.
4. Pass criterion: 100% direct dependency recall, ≥95% oracle transitive recall, and <10% unrelated-view invalidation.

The second inspection is necessary because perfect traversal can coexist with missing edges.

### 4. `Scale Policy Ablation`

Goal: determine whether typed operators and purpose-conditioned salience beat token-only compression.

Procedure:

1. Compare token-only summaries, typed operators without purpose, and typed operators with `{scope, stakes, horizon, budget, audience}`.
2. Use tasks that deliberately trade off minutiae, recurring preferences, one-off prohibitions, and unresolved contradictions.
3. Score decisive-detail recall, irrelevant-detail load, forbidden-action rate, and ability to explain every omission.
4. Ablate exaggeration, 1→0 omission logs, and scale tuple dimensions separately.

### 5. `Contradiction Replay`

Goal: test conflict preservation.

Procedure:

1. Seed paired claims that are genuinely contradictory, merely time-separated, or compatible under different scopes.
2. Compare summary blending with typed `contradicts`, `supersedes`, and `qualifies` edges.
3. Score false-consensus rate, unnecessary clarification rate, and correct use of the latest applicable claim.
4. Require evidence-chain inspection as a mechanically independent check on answer-level scoring.

### 6. `Retention Threat-Model Review`

Goal: define true forgetting independently of cartographic generalisation.

Procedure:

1. Enumerate sensitive data classes, owner deletion requests, legal/policy constraints, backups, logs, derived views, and caches.
2. For every deletion event, verify both canonical-record removal and inability to retrieve it through an independent derived index.
3. Specify tombstone behavior without retaining the deleted sensitive content.
4. Do not ship irreversible forgetting until this review passes.

## Recommended decision

Proceed only with the small lineage-and-invalidation pilot, not a memory platform replacement.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** The first increment consists of persistent IDs, derivation records, dirty propagation, and one purpose/scale policy. It explicitly excludes automatic canonical deletion, vector retrieval, a daemon, and automatic rewriting of contested memories.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED because its value and bus fit have not been tested; `Bus Fit Probe` followed by `Atlas Replay A/B` would settle it.** Conditional on successful bus fit, I am **likely (55-80%)** that the pilot will reveal stale-summary and unexplained-omission failures that plain summaries cannot, based on the source-to-derivative lineage mechanism and cartography's demonstrated use of that mechanism for update propagation. I am **unlikely (20-45%)** that the full cartographic operator vocabulary is needed in the first implementation; the evidence is that `select/omit`, `aggregate`, `collapse`, and `exaggerate` cover the principal memory cases, while `displacement` is mostly a warning about presentation and `simplify` is already inherent in summarisation.

If the replay shows no material reduction in stale or unsupported memory errors at equal token budget, stop. The analogy would then remain intellectually neat but operationally non-transferable. If it succeeds, add operators only when an ablation demonstrates incremental value.

## Gaps

- I did not inspect the local harness by design, so this unit does not establish whether equivalent lineage, view versioning, or invalidation already exists.
- The strongest generalisation-log paper is accessible here through its peer-reviewed abstract and indexed section excerpts rather than open full text; the cited claims are limited to those visible passages.
- No source found in cartography supplies a validated semantic salience function for agent memory, a privacy-aware deletion schedule, or a reliable LLM-summary equivalence test. Those are target-domain research questions.
- The proposed ~200-line pilot size depends on available host hooks and storage primitives and is therefore explicitly UNVERIFIED by `Bus Fit Probe`.
- No empirical result in this unit compares memory-atlas retrieval to modern long-context models. `Atlas Replay A/B` is required before an implementation claim.
