# X118 — Cross-domain borrow: forensic-science controls for reliable agent conclusions

## Executive result

The borrow transfers, but only at the level of **process controls**, not at the level of “an AI answer is like a DNA profile.” My conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, because the source and target share the relevant causal structure: an observation passes through a fallible interpretive process; expectations and prior conclusions can influence interpretation; a single measurement path can fail silently; and the final report can conceal where the error entered. Forensic science addresses those hazards with information-flow control, controls of known behavior, independent/blind re-examination, and concealed known-answer proficiency samples. The target failure—an agent confidently inferring absence, breakage, or completion from one unvalidated probe—has the same structure even though its evidence is digital rather than physical. The transfer is supported by the NIJ/OJP description of case managers, sequential unmasking, and blind re-examination; NIST's definition of a negative control; and the forensic literature on blind proficiency testing ([OJP contextual-bias project](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/developing-effective-methods-addressing-contextual-bias-forensic-science), [NIST negative-control definition](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/36651), [Mejia et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/)).

The concrete design is a **Controlled Conclusion Protocol** on the existing hook/tool bus. It has three gates:

1. validate each detector in the same run with a known-present control and, where feasible, a known-absent control;
2. freeze the primary observation and interpretation before revealing other conclusions or potentially biasing context to a verifier; and
3. for high-impact claims of `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, or `DONE`, require a blind re-examination using a mechanically independent signal.

A small, separate proficiency suite injects realistic, concealed, known-answer tasks through the ordinary harness path and scores the entire pipeline, including whether the gates were followed. This is not a second orchestrator, memory system, critic model, or observability service.

The efficacy claim is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a preregistered replay of the measured single-probe failures plus matched controls, comparing baseline against the protocol on false-conclusion rate, abstention rate, time, and tool calls**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that this package would materially reduce confidently wrong negative-state conclusions, because every measured target failure necessarily passes through at least one of the gates, while forensic studies and guidance show that blinding, known-answer controls, and proficiency tests expose distinct failure modes. The wide band reflects the absence of a direct agent-harness trial and the possibility of correlated LLM/tool errors.

## 1. What forensic science has actually solved

### 1.1 Contamination control is layered prevention, detection, localization, and monitoring

Forensic DNA laboratories do not treat cleanliness or analyst care as sufficient. The 2023 European Network of Forensic Science Institutes guideline defines contamination broadly as unwanted introduction or transfer at any point in the forensic process. It then treats minimization as four activities: avoidance, control, detection, and monitoring. Its measures include spatial or temporal separation of incompatible activities; separation of reference samples from crime-scene samples; dedicated equipment; documented handling procedures; negative controls at multiple process stages; environmental monitoring; elimination databases; event investigation; corrective action; and trend monitoring ([ENFSI DNA contamination guideline, pp. 2-8](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ENFSI-GUIDELINE-FOR-DNA-CONTAMINATION-MINIMIZATION-IN-DNA-LABORATORIES.pdf)).

The important mechanism is not merely physical isolation. It is the combination of:

- **barriers**, which reduce the chance that one source influences another;
- **process controls**, which travel through relevant stages and reveal contamination introduced by reagents, disposables, or handling;
- **source attribution**, which compares unexpected material with known potential contaminant sources; and
- **incident traceability**, which records stages so investigators can localize where an anomaly entered.

NIST defines a DNA negative control as the relevant reagents without sample, for which no result is expected; a detected result can therefore reveal contamination introduced by the assay process. NIST explicitly names reagent/extraction and amplification blanks and explains that they monitor reagent, disposable, and handling contamination ([NIST OSAC Lexicon: Negative Control](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/36651)). ENFSI requires negative controls at several stages rather than one generic blank ([ENFSI guideline, section 5.1](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ENFSI-GUIDELINE-FOR-DNA-CONTAMINATION-MINIMIZATION-IN-DNA-LABORATORIES.pdf)).

This solves a key epistemic problem: a clean-looking result is not accepted solely because the instrument emitted it. The process carries witnesses whose expected behavior distinguishes “the specimen produced this result” from “the pipeline itself produced this result.” It also recognizes that prevention alone cannot prove non-contamination.

### 1.2 Linear Sequential Unmasking controls when potentially biasing information becomes available

Linear Sequential Unmasking (LSU) was developed for comparison work. The examiner first evaluates and documents the questioned/unknown material on its own, before viewing the known reference or suspect material. LSU-Expanded (LSU-E) generalizes the idea: rank information by objectivity, relevance, and biasing power; reveal it in an order that favors objective, task-relevant, less biasing material; record decisions at each stage; and constrain and document later revisions ([Dror & Kukucka 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8385162/), [Quigley-McBride et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8866671/)).

OJP's NIJ-sponsored project describes three operational forms. A case manager can possess full context while providing the examiner only what the analytical task requires. Sequential unmasking makes key judgments precede exposure to biasing information. Blind re-examination has a second examiner replicate the key work without seeing potentially biasing information or the first conclusion ([OJP, 2018](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/developing-effective-methods-addressing-contextual-bias-forensic-science)).

The mechanism addresses more than conscious dishonesty. Context can change what an expert notices, how ambiguous features are classified, and how much weight they receive. In one small repeated-examination fingerprint study, four of five experts changed their former identifications after receiving misleading context; the sample is far too small for a general error-rate estimate, but it demonstrates the causal possibility in experts ([Dror, Charlton & Péron 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2005.10.017)). A larger verification-stage study also found that contextual prompts affected expert judgments, though not by increasing expert errors in that experiment; this counterexample matters because blinding is not guaranteed to improve every decision distribution ([Langenburg, Champod & Wertheim 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1556-4029.2009.01025.x)).

### 1.3 Blind verification seeks independent error opportunities, not ceremonial agreement

In a blind re-examination, the verifier does the examination without knowing the first examiner's conclusion. The point is to prevent the first answer from becoming an anchor or an implicit target. A large latent-print accuracy study reported that false-positive individualizations occurred in 0.1% of decisions and were never made by two examiners on the same comparison; the authors inferred that blind verification would be highly effective for that type of error. They also warned that the study was not representative of all operational situations ([Ulery et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093498/)).

That result does **not** prove that “two agents are better than one.” It supports a narrower proposition: when errors are not perfectly correlated and the second examiner is not contaminated by the first conclusion, disagreement can expose an error that ordinary review would miss. Mechanical independence and information independence are both load-bearing.

### 1.4 Blind proficiency testing tests the ordinary system under realistic conditions

Declared proficiency tests can create a test-taking mode: an examiner knows that a specimen has a known answer and may spend more time or behave differently. Blind proficiency tests disguise known-answer specimens as ordinary casework. When implemented well, they traverse intake, handling, analysis, interpretation, review, and reporting, thereby measuring the system rather than only an isolated analytical step. Mejia et al. identify four advantages: realistic cases, whole-pipeline coverage, reduced behavior change due to known testing, and an ability to detect misconduct; they also document logistical and cultural obstacles ([Mejia et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/)).

The Harris County implementation explicitly aimed to test the path from evidence receipt to report release and found blind testing feasible after planning and iteration ([Kahn et al. 2020, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31922611/)). An earlier federally funded DNA project concluded that blind tests exercise more of the laboratory system than open tests, while also documenting implementation difficulty ([Peterson & Gaensslen, OJP report](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/developing-criteria-model-external-dna-proficiency-testing-final-report)). The National Commission on Forensic Science distinguished proficiency testing—performance against pre-established criteria and interlaboratory comparison—from initial competency testing, and listed methods, documentation, handling, equipment, and reporting among the quality-system aspects proficiency results can evaluate ([NCFS views document, 2016](https://www.justice.gov/ncfs/file/831806/dl)).

## 2. Structural mapping to an agent harness

| Forensic structure | Agent-harness counterpart | Failure exposed |
|---|---|---|
| Questioned item | Target system state, file tree, command behavior, API response, or build result | What is actually being claimed about |
| Analytical method | Search command, tool call, parser, query, test, or inspection procedure | A probe can be malformed, partial, stale, or inappropriate |
| Negative control | A fixture known not to contain the sought feature, sent through the same detector | Detector that emits positives indiscriminately or parses noise as a hit |
| Positive control (the necessary complement for absence claims) | A fixture known to contain the sought feature and reachable by the same execution path | Detector that reports zero because it is broken, scoped wrongly, or unable to traverse the environment |
| Sample/reference separation | Separate raw observation from expected answer, prior agent narrative, and verifier input | Confirmation and anchoring on a suspected outcome |
| Case manager | A small hook that packages only task-relevant target data and a neutral question for verification | Leaking the first conclusion into the verifier prompt |
| Blind re-examination | A fresh verification context that sees the target and claim definition but not the first conclusion or reasoning | Rubber-stamp review and shared narrative errors |
| Independent analytical technique | A second data field, tool family, or measurement mechanism with a different failure mode | Repeated execution of one broken detector masquerading as corroboration |
| Blind proficiency specimen | A concealed known-answer task routed through normal hooks and tools | Test-only behavior and untested integration stages |
| Contamination incident record | Structured provenance for command, scope, exit status, raw output digest, controls, interpretation, and revision | Confident report with no auditable link to evidence |

The central translation is: **treat a conclusion as a specimen whose evidentiary lineage can be contaminated**. “Contamination” in the target is not foreign DNA; it is target leakage, prior-conclusion anchoring, malformed quoting, stale cache, truncated output, wrong scope, parser assumptions, tool unavailability, or correlated reuse of the same faulty signal. This is a functional analogy, not a claim of physical equivalence.

## 3. Concrete harness changes

### 3.1 UNVERIFIED recommendation — Controlled Conclusion Protocol

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The premise that existing hook events expose enough information to gate a final high-impact conclusion is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a 20-case hook spike that records whether pre-report hooks can see the claim class, tool provenance, and control outcomes without scraping free-form chain-of-thought**. If that premise fails, implement the protocol as an explicitly invoked skill/template rather than adding a daemon.

For every conclusion classified as `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, or `DONE`, require a compact evidence envelope:

```text
claim: precise falsifiable proposition
scope: system/path/version/time bounded by the claim
primary_probe: tool, arguments, exit status, raw-result reference
positive_control: known-present target + observed result
negative_control: known-absent target + observed result, or NOT-APPLICABLE with reason
primary_interpretation: recorded before verification
second_signal: distinct field/tool/mechanism + independence rationale
verifier_context: hash/list of materials disclosed
verifier_result: agree | disagree | indeterminate
revision_log: any change after additional information
```

The positive control is mandatory for negative-state claims. It must exercise the same detector and relevant scope, not merely prove that an unrelated command works. The negative control is mandatory when a false-positive detector could support the conclusion; otherwise its explicit non-applicability prevents box-checking. The second signal must name a different failure mechanism—for example, filesystem enumeration versus package-manager metadata—not two invocations of the same glob or parser.

The hook blocks an unqualified categorical statement only when the envelope is missing. It permits an explicitly scoped uncertainty report such as “probe failed; state indeterminate,” preserving progress when a second signal is unavailable. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch the precise detector-invalidity pattern in which a zero result is interpreted as absence, because the known-present control must fail in that situation; the evidence is logical coverage of the failure condition plus NIST's process-control mechanism. It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch malformed quoting, wrong scopes, truncated output, and stale metadata, because those faults often affect one detector or its control, but correlated environment-wide failures can defeat both signals. Both efficacy claims remain **UNVERIFIED; the same preregistered replay described in the executive result settles them**.

### 3.2 UNVERIFIED recommendation — blind, mechanically independent verification for high-impact claims

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The premise that a fresh agent context is sufficiently isolated from the original conclusion is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a leakage audit containing unique canary strings in the primary conclusion and testing whether any appear in verifier inputs, tool arguments, or output before unblinding**.

For claims whose wrong answer would trigger deletion, broad configuration change, abandonment of a capability, or declaration of task completion:

1. The primary agent records the claim definition, primary raw evidence, and interpretation.
2. A case-manager hook creates a neutral verification packet: target scope, operational definition, allowed read-only probes, and required output schema. It excludes the first conclusion, rationale, confidence, and expected answer.
3. The verifier must use a different measurement mechanism. “Fresh model call using the same command” is not independent.
4. Only after the verifier commits its result are the two answers compared.
5. Agreement permits the claim; disagreement or indeterminacy requires reconciliation against raw evidence, not majority vote.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce rubber-stamp confirmations and catch one-path tool failures, based on OJP's blind re-examination model and the latent-print result showing non-coincident false positives. The band is not higher because two contexts can share model priors, tools, filesystem state, prompt conventions, and hidden caches. It is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring disagreement yield and adjudicated error capture on replay cases, stratified by whether the second probe is mechanically independent**.

### 3.3 UNVERIFIED recommendation — blind proficiency stream with known-answer canaries

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The premise that synthetic cases can be made indistinguishable enough from ordinary harness work to avoid test-taking behavior is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a blinded classifier study in which agents and maintainers attempt to distinguish production-like fixtures from real replay tasks**.

Maintain a small versioned suite of known-answer tasks covering:

- known-present and known-absent resources with deceptive naming and realistic depth;
- a detector that returns zero because its dependency is unavailable;
- a successful command whose output is deliberately truncated before a key hit;
- two metadata sources that disagree, with one stale;
- a quoting-sensitive path or argument;
- a task whose tests pass but whose requested artifact is absent;
- a case where the correct outcome is `INDETERMINATE`, not forced yes/no.

Randomly route a low percentage through the same skills, hooks, tool wrappers, report formatting, and evidence-envelope checks as ordinary work. Keep expected answers outside the agent-visible context. Score false categorical conclusions, missed control failures, independence violations, confidence calibration, latency, and tool cost. Rotate cases after exposure; otherwise the suite becomes memorized.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose regressions in the complete control path that unit tests of individual hooks miss, because blind proficiency testing is specifically designed to exercise ordinary intake-through-report behavior. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to estimate production error rates, because synthetic-task representativeness and concealment may be poor. Both are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with seeded-failure recovery rates and a comparison between proficiency scores and held-out real-session replay errors over at least two harness releases**.

### 3.4 UNVERIFIED recommendation — contamination-style provenance and trend review

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add stable reason codes to envelopes: `POSITIVE_CONTROL_FAILED`, `NEGATIVE_CONTROL_FAILED`, `SCOPE_MISMATCH`, `OUTPUT_TRUNCATED`, `TOOL_UNAVAILABLE`, `SIGNALS_CORRELATED`, `VERIFIER_DISAGREED`, and `GROUND_TRUTH_UNKNOWN`. Aggregate counts locally using existing logs. Do not add an observability SaaS.

The premise that the current logging format can retain these fields without a new service is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by emitting and round-tripping each code through the existing log export in a disposable fixture run**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that trend review will reveal recurring detector/tool combinations that case-by-case correction misses, because ENFSI explicitly combines event investigation with trend monitoring, but the target logs may lack stable denominators or task-severity labels. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it by testing whether repeated seeded faults create detectable rate changes without excessive false alarms**.

## 4. What the package catches that a single-probe rule misses

| Present failure | First catching mechanism | Why the existing single probe can look convincing |
|---|---|---|
| Search tool silently cannot traverse the target | Known-present control | Empty output resembles true absence |
| Query was scoped to the wrong directory/account/version | Scope-bound positive control plus provenance | Command syntax and exit status can both be valid |
| Parser accepts noise as a hit | Negative control | A second run repeats the same false positive |
| Output is truncated before the decisive record | Independent metadata/data-field signal | Visible output is internally plausible |
| Prior narrative says a component is dead | Blind verifier | Reviewer searches for confirmation rather than testing neutral alternatives |
| Two probes call the same backend or parser | Independence rationale gate | Different command names create false confidence |
| Hook works in its unit test but not in normal routing | Blind proficiency task | Declared tests bypass intake, routing, and report stages |
| Agent asserts completion from passing tests alone | Known-answer completion canary plus artifact/state probe | Tests cover implementation behavior, not the requested delivery state |
| Recurrent low-frequency fault never triggers redesign | Reason-code trend | Each incident looks isolated |

## 5. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 5.1 Where the analogy genuinely holds

1. **Both are inference pipelines, not direct perception.** A forensic conclusion depends on collection, handling, assay, interpretation, review, and reporting; an agent conclusion depends on scope selection, tool execution, output transport, parsing, interpretation, and reporting. A failure at an earlier stage can survive a plausible final narrative. ENFSI's stage-specific controls and the NCFS description of proficiency testing across handling, methods, documentation, and reporting support the source-side structure.

2. **Expectation can alter interpretation.** Forensic contextual-bias studies establish this for human examiners, and the target problem already specifies confidently wrong conclusions from unvalidated probes. It is **likely (55-80%)** that hiding a prior agent conclusion reduces anchoring in a verifier, because language models are highly responsive to prompt context, but this specific harness effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an A/B replay where verifiers receive either a neutral task or the first conclusion and rationale**.

3. **Known-answer controls diagnose the measurement path.** The mapping from a negative assay blank to a no-target fixture, and from a positive control to a known-present fixture, is direct. Neither claims that the fixture proves the production target's state; it proves only that the detector can exhibit expected behavior under controlled conditions.

4. **Concealed proficiency tests address ecological validity.** Both fields face behavior changes when the operator knows it is being tested. Routing known-answer tasks through normal machinery tests integration points that isolated unit tests omit.

### 5.2 Where the analogy breaks or becomes dangerous

1. **Digital evidence is often deterministic and cheaply repeatable; forensic traces are often scarce and destructible.** Agent verification can frequently rerun commands and inspect exact bytes, so expensive examiner-style duplication would be wasteful for low-impact claims. The transfer therefore applies risk-tiered gates, not universal double examination.

2. **Two LLM calls are not two independent examiners.** They can share weights, system prompts, training biases, tool wrappers, and environmental state. Calling them “blind” without a leakage audit and “independent” without different failure mechanisms is ceremonial. This is the most important disanalogy.

3. **The user prompt may already reveal the expected conclusion.** A forensic case manager can withhold suspect context; an agent assigned “prove X is broken” has already been exposed. Blinding can still protect the verifier from the primary agent's answer, but it cannot erase task framing from the primary. Neutral restatement by a case-manager hook reduces, but does not eliminate, contamination.

4. **Ground truth is frequently unavailable or time-varying.** A proficiency fixture can have a known answer; a live distributed system may change between probes. Disagreement may reflect state drift rather than error. Evidence envelopes therefore need timestamps and scope, and reconciliation must allow `INDETERMINATE`.

5. **Controls can be unrepresentative.** A detector might find a shallow ASCII fixture yet fail on deep paths, permissions, encodings, pagination, or remote scopes. A positive control is probative only if it exercises the same relevant path and difficulty. “The command finds its own toy file” is not sufficient validation.

6. **Blind proficiency can become covert surveillance or corrupt real work.** Synthetic tasks must run in disposable/read-only fixtures, be owner-authorized, and never inject misleading data into production subjects. Results should improve the system, not silently rank individuals. Mejia et al. document cultural and logistical obstacles in laboratories; the governance issue transfers as strongly as the technical mechanism.

7. **Forensic controls themselves are not infallible.** The verification-stage study above found contextual effects without increased expert errors, and the latent-print accuracy authors limited generalization beyond their study conditions. The evidence supports testing the mechanisms, not treating forensic practice as an oracle.

The analogy therefore survives only under a strict formulation: borrow **control topology**—separation, witnesses, independent replication, concealed end-to-end tests, and traceability—not forensic authority, courtroom vocabulary, or assumptions about examiner independence.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and rejected expansions

All four proposals ride the existing hook/skill/tool/log bus. None requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The primary protocol is a small structured record and gating rule; the proficiency stream is a fixture runner; trend review uses existing logs.

I explicitly reject the following expansions:

- **Always-on multi-agent panels:** correlated model errors, cost, and latency exceed the evidence. Blind verification is reserved for high-impact claims and requires a different measurement mechanism.
- **A learned reliability critic:** it would add a trained critic rejected by the anti-overfit gate and could simply inherit the original model's priors.
- **A provenance database/service:** stable JSON envelopes in existing logs are sufficient for the experiment. A new service is unjustified before volume or query needs are measured.
- **A universal mandatory double-probe policy:** cheap factual reads and reversible low-impact actions do not warrant forensic-grade review. The gate targets categorical negative-state and completion claims, especially when consequential.
- **Confidence-only calibration prompts:** confidence language cannot validate a detector. Controls and independent observations operate on the evidence path; wording operates only on the report.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the proposed experiment can be implemented without a new runtime, because each component is a hook-visible record, fixture, or existing-tool invocation; this implementation estimate is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is the <200-line hook spike plus fixture runner with line count and dependency inventory recorded before adoption**.

## 7. Evaluation design and adoption threshold

Preregister a replay benchmark before implementing the gate:

1. Include every available measured single-unvalidated-probe failure, plus matched correct conclusions and hard `INDETERMINATE` cases.
2. Freeze expected answers and adjudication rules outside agent context.
3. Randomize cases between baseline and Controlled Conclusion Protocol; prevent cross-arm transcript leakage.
4. Primary metric: confidently wrong categorical conclusions per case.
5. Secondary metrics: false abstentions, correction latency, tool calls, token cost, verifier disagreement yield, control-failure yield, and percentage of “independent” signals later adjudicated as correlated.
6. Separately test ablations: controls only; blind verifier only; proficiency feedback only; full package.
7. Repeat on held-out tasks after any prompt/rule tuning.

Adopt the protocol only if the upper confidence bound on its false-conclusion rate is below baseline while the increase in false abstentions and cost remains within an owner-selected budget. Do not tune the threshold on the same failures used for evaluation. The claim that the full package will outperform controls alone is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because the target failures may be dominated by detector invalidity, which positive controls already catch; blind verification adds value mainly for interpretation and correlated-context failures. This is **UNVERIFIED; the preregistered ablation settles it**.

## 8. Gaps

- This unit did not establish whether a direct experiment on LSU, blind verification, or blind proficiency for autonomous coding/research agents exists. The web search was an ideation search, not a systematic review, so absence is not claimed. Agent efficacy recommendations above are therefore **UNVERIFIED; settle the literature gap with a preregistered search across scholarly indexes and citation chains, then settle efficacy with the replay experiment even if adjacent literature is found**.
- The accessible forensic studies are discipline-specific. Fingerprint results do not establish DNA, document, digital-forensics, or agent error rates.
- “Double-blind” in the strict experimental sense is often unattainable here: the routing hook knows which task is a proficiency case, and the user or case designer may know the expected answer. The implementable target is examiner/agent-blind known-answer testing, with answer keys isolated from the agent and ordinary workflow preserved.
- The ideal risk threshold for mandatory verification is unknown. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** whether a three-level risk rubric can be applied reliably from task text alone; evidence is limited to the conceptual ability to classify consequences, and ambiguity is substantial. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by having independent reviewers label 100 replay tasks, measuring agreement, and using explicit user-impact features if agreement is poor**.
- The <200-line estimate excludes fixture data and documentation. It is **likely (55-80%)** that enforcement plus serialization fits that bound, based on the narrow schema and hook logic, but **UNVERIFIED; the line-counted spike settles it**.

## Bottom line

Forensic science's strongest contribution is not “ask twice.” It is **design the evidence path so that failure leaves a trace**: isolate potentially biasing information, send known-behavior controls through the measurement path, obtain a genuinely independent blinded observation for consequential conclusions, and test the full ordinary workflow with concealed known answers. That architecture maps closely enough to the target reliability failure to justify a controlled trial, while the analogy fails if “independence” means merely another sample from the same model using the same tool.

The recommended trial is **very likely (80-90%)** to be decision-informative, because it directly measures the stated failure outcome and separately ablates each borrowed mechanism; the evidence is the preregistered replay design and known ground truth in seeded cases. Whether the final package materially improves production reliability remains **UNVERIFIED; the preregistered replay plus two-release held-out proficiency correlation is the settling experiment**.
