# X011 — Cross-domain borrow: clinical medicine → compute and quota cost-control

## Scope and research boundary

This unit is an ideation lens. It does not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. It asks whether four clinical-medicine mechanisms—differential diagnosis, iatrogenic-harm control, contraindication registries, and number-needed-to-treat—have structural equivalents that can govern agent compute. External evidence is linked inline. The concrete examples from the commissioning brief (including repeated calls to a disconnected provider and expensive schema failures) are treated only as supplied use cases, not independently reverified findings.

## Executive finding

The borrow transfers, but only in a narrow form. The useful core is **clinical threshold decision-making**: do not seek certainty uniformly; choose among stop, obtain more information, and act according to whether information can plausibly cross a decision boundary after benefit, harm, and cost are considered. Pauker and Kassirer's original model defines testing and test-treatment thresholds; the National Academies' exposition states the operational rule directly: perform a test only if it can move probability across the treatment threshold ([PubMed abstract of the 1980 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/); [National Academies exposition](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)). The National Academies' later diagnostic-process review also stresses that the goal is not certainty but enough uncertainty reduction to make the best treatment decision, accounting for the harms and benefits of both treatment and further information gathering ([Improving Diagnosis in Health Care](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)).

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a lightweight, measured version of this mechanism can reduce agent compute without degrading accepted-answer quality, because the source mechanism explicitly prices information by its ability to change action, and the target has the same three-way choice—stop, probe, or escalate. This remains a forward-looking estimate, not an efficacy result; the settling experiment is a paired replay in which the same stratified tasks run under the current policy and the proposed gate, with blinded outcome grading and complete token/tool-cost accounting.

Three qualifications are decisive:

1. Medicine has partially observable but relatively stable disease states, validated tests, and clinically interpretable outcomes. Agent tasks are open-ended, distributions drift, model calls are correlated, and “answer changed” is not the same as “answer improved.” Therefore clinical equations cannot be copied literally.
2. NNT is a reporting discipline, not an online routing oracle. It becomes useful only after counterfactual uplift has been measured by task stratum and time horizon.
3. Broad differential generation and generic checklists do **not** justify uniform multi-agent fan-out. AHRQ reports mixed checklist evidence: generic process checklists often show no significant accuracy effect, and in one comparison checklists reduced accuracy on easy cases while a content-specific checklist helped difficult cases ([AHRQ diagnostic-checklist review](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)). That is direct evidence against indiscriminate “more reasoning” as a safety policy.

The minimal architecture is therefore one small policy hook plus an offline evaluator—not a second orchestration runtime, memory database, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It rides the existing hook/skill/tool bus and passes the anti-overfit gate.

## Mechanism 1: test–treatment thresholds and value of information

### How it works in clinical medicine

The threshold model starts with a pre-test probability of disease and three possible regions:

- Below the testing threshold: do not test and do not treat, because even a positive result is not expected to justify treatment.
- Between testing and test-treatment thresholds: test, because its possible results can change management.
- Above the test-treatment threshold: treat without further testing, because testing is not worth its delay, cost, or harm.

Thresholds depend not merely on diagnostic accuracy but on treatment benefit and harm, test sensitivity/specificity, and the cost or burden of the test. The source account explicitly describes this as expected-value decision-making and notes that costs and benefits must be put on a common scale ([National Academies, “The Use of Diagnostic Tests”](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)). The 1980 paper's abstract likewise says the estimated disease probability determines whether to withhold treatment, test, or treat without exposing the patient to further-test risks ([Pauker & Kassirer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7366635/)).

Health technology assessment adds opportunity cost: funding one intervention can displace health-producing activity elsewhere. NICE requires decision-makers to consider healthcare programs displaced by adopting a technology, including programs outside the appraisal itself ([NICE committee recommendations, §6.2.25](https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg36/chapter/committee-recommendations-2)). NICE also identifies value-of-information analysis as a way to decide whether reducing a particular uncertainty is worth additional research ([NICE research-recommendations process, §2.2.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg45/chapter/nice-research-recommendations-process)).

The transferable principle is thus stronger than “use confidence thresholds”: **purchase information only when its expected action-changing value exceeds its direct cost, downstream harm, delay, and opportunity cost.**

### Proposal 1 — Decision-changing compute gate [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

UNVERIFIED premise: cheap observable features can predict useful escalation well enough to beat a simple fixed policy; settle it with a paired, stratified replay and reliability/calibration plots for predicted versus observed escalation uplift.

Add a pre-escalation hook that chooses one of three actions:

1. **Stop/continue cheaply** when no plausible result from the expensive action would change the next decision.
2. **Buy a cheap discriminator** when uncertainty is material and a cheap probe can cross the action boundary—for example, inspect one authoritative file, perform a provider health check, run a narrow test, or ask one targeted verification call.
3. **Escalate directly** when baseline failure risk and consequence are high and the expensive intervention has demonstrated benefit in that task stratum; do not spend first on ritual diagnostics.

For a candidate escalation `e`, the offline evaluator estimates:

`net_e = P(e changes the chosen action) × value(correct change) − incremental compute − latency cost − P(e induces harm) × harm − opportunity cost`

The online hook need not calculate a falsely precise universal utility. A small scorecard can use calibrated bins:

- task stratum (routine transform, coding/debugging, research/current facts, high-stakes decision, destructive action);
- current uncertainty (explicit contradiction, failed test, missing required evidence, ambiguous intent, no observed anomaly);
- consequence of a wrong answer;
- whether the proposed call has distinct information, rather than another sample from the same evidence;
- provider/tool health and remaining quota;
- empirical uplift and harm interval for this escalation in this stratum.

The hook should log the decision and features locally, then execute through the existing bus. It should not own task orchestration. Default thresholds should be monotone: as consequence rises, less evidence is required to justify a discriminating check; as cost or correlation with prior attempts rises, more predicted benefit is required.

This gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch “expensive action whose possible outputs cannot alter the plan,” because that is its explicit rule and the clinical threshold model is designed around the identical decision structure ([National Academies threshold model](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)). It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch redundant retries, uniform high-model use, reflexive web/tool calls, and fan-out whose branches all lead to the same next action; the evidence is the structural mapping, while the wide band reflects unmeasured task-feature calibration. The paired replay named above settles the prediction.

It also addresses the supplied “known-disconnected provider” example without a special global ban: a provider-health discriminator can move the action from “call” to “fallback,” while repeated failure makes expected information value approach zero. That behavior follows by construction; whether the hook correctly recognizes every provider state is **likely (55-80%)**, conditional on a fresh health signal, and should be tested by fault injection covering disconnected, slow, unauthorized, rate-limited, and recovered states.

### Why the analogy can fail

- **No stable latent diagnosis.** A software or research task may have several simultaneously valid answers. The gate must predict decision-changing utility, not the probability of one “true disease.”
- **Calls are not independent tests.** Multiple agents with the same model and context have correlated errors. Counting branches as independent evidence would exaggerate information value.
- **Utility is multidimensional.** Tokens, wall time, quota scarcity, user interruption, correctness, and catastrophic risk lack a natural common unit. A single scalar can conceal owner preferences.
- **Delay sometimes dominates.** As in emergency treatment, a high-consequence task may warrant immediate escalation; demanding another score or probe can itself cause harm.
- **Selective labels.** We readily observe tool errors, but often do not observe whether an uncalled expensive model would have improved the answer. Naïve logs therefore bias toward the current policy.

Mitigations are to retain a small randomized exploration slice, publish estimates by task stratum, show separate cost/quality/harm columns before any scalar score, and permit explicit owner overrides. The claim that these mitigations prevent harmful under-escalation is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with randomized shadow routing plus a non-inferiority bound on severe-error rate, not merely average acceptance.

## Mechanism 2: NNT/NNH as absolute-effect accounting

### How it works in clinical medicine

Number needed to treat is the expected number of people who must receive an intervention rather than a comparator for one additional beneficial outcome over a stated time frame. It is the inverse of absolute risk reduction; the harm-direction analogue is NNT for an additional harmful outcome (often NNH/NNTH) ([Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/resources/ebm-tools/number-needed-to-treat-nnt); [Cochrane Handbook §15.4.2](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

The discipline matters because a large relative improvement can correspond to a tiny absolute change when baseline risk is low. Cochrane notes that absolute effects are particularly useful for benefit–harm tradeoffs and that a relative risk of 0.75 could mean an 80%→60% reduction or a 4%→3% reduction ([Cochrane Handbook, effect measures](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-06)). NNT is not portable without qualification: Cochrane requires an explicit comparator risk and warns that risk differences, hence NNTs, vary across baseline-risk groups and follow-up periods ([Cochrane Handbook §15.4.3](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

### Proposal 2 — “Number needed to escalate” ledger [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

UNVERIFIED premise: accepted-answer improvement and escalation-induced harm can be operationalized with adequate inter-rater reliability; settle it with a preregistered rubric, duplicate blinded grading, agreement statistics, and adjudication on a stratified sample.

For each optional compute intervention and task stratum, report:

- **NNE-B (number needed to escalate for one additional beneficial outcome)** = `1 / (benefit rate with escalation − benefit rate with comparator)`.
- **NNE-H (number needed to escalate for one additional harmful outcome)** = `1 / (harm rate with escalation − harm rate with comparator)` when harm increases.
- incremental tokens, tool calls, latency, and quota per escalation;
- compute per additional benefit = `mean incremental compute × NNE-B`;
- outcome definition, comparator, model/version, task stratum, and evaluation window;
- confidence interval, including “effect unresolved” when it crosses no difference.

“Benefit” must mean a user-relevant binary event defined before evaluation—for example, a previously failing task becomes accepted without severe defect—not “the answer changed,” verbosity rose, or a judge preferred style. “Harm” should include a formerly correct answer becoming wrong, destructive action, unsupported assertion, privacy boundary violation, or failure to finish within the budget.

Example, explicitly hypothetical: if escalation raises acceptance from 70/100 to 74/100, NNE-B is 25. If it costs 20,000 extra tokens per case, the point estimate is 500,000 extra tokens per additional accepted task. If severe harm rises from 1/100 to 2/100, NNE-H is 100. These numbers are only interpretable for the stated task mix, comparator, and evaluation period.

The ledger is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose interventions that look impressive in relative terms but rarely change outcomes, because NNT's mathematical purpose is to express absolute rather than relative effect and Cochrane explicitly ties interpretation to baseline risk ([Cochrane §15.4](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)). It is **likely (55-80%)** to change spending toward high-baseline-risk strata once surfaced in routing reports; that behavioral effect is not guaranteed, and a six-week randomized policy comparison would settle it.

### Critical disanalogy

Clinical NNT usually has a defined comparator, population, event, and horizon. Agent outcomes are often graded, multi-objective, and revisable; users also select tasks non-randomly. Therefore there must be no global “NNE for the large model” or “NNE for multi-agent.” Pooling would invite Simpson's paradox and hide the very baseline-risk dependence that makes NNT valuable. NNE is a retrospective measurement vocabulary only. Using it as an individual-task causal prediction without stratified experimental data is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** justified, because Cochrane explicitly cautions against transporting absolute effects across differing baseline risks ([Cochrane §15.4.3](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

## Mechanism 3: iatrogenics and contraindication registries

### How it works in clinical medicine

Iatrogenic reasoning treats the intervention itself as a source of harm. More testing and treatment are not monotonically beneficial: the diagnostic-process review identifies risks from overly aggressive testing and says further information gathering must be weighed for harm and delay ([National Academies diagnostic process](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)). Follow-up cascades from incidental findings are common and not always clinically meaningful; a national physician survey reported patient harms as well as clinician frustration and anxiety ([AHRQ PSNet summary of Ganguli et al.](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/cascades-care-after-incidental-findings-us-national-survey-physicians)).

Contraindications turn accumulated harm knowledge into a pre-action exclusion. FDA defines a contraindication as a clinical situation in which risk clearly outweighs any possible therapeutic benefit and the medicine must not be used; warnings and precautions cover serious risks that require identification, prevention, management, or stopping criteria ([FDA labeling FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fdas-labeling-resources-human-prescription-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-about-labeling-prescription-medicines); [FDA guide to prescribing information](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/oncology-center-excellence/how-do-i-use-prescription-drug-labeling)). The important design detail is tiering: not every risk is a hard contraindication.

### Proposal 3 — Versioned compute-contraindication registry [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

UNVERIFIED premise: a small registry can remain current enough that avoided known harms exceed false blocks; settle it with a challenge suite plus a 30-day shadow run measuring true blocks, stale-rule blocks, overrides, and recovered-provider behavior.

Define a compact, human-reviewable registry consumed by the existing pre-tool/pre-agent hook. Each entry contains:

- intervention identifier and version/range;
- context predicate;
- tier: `contraindicated`, `warning`, or `dose-limit`;
- concrete harm and evidence reference;
- safer alternative;
- owner, creation date, review/expiry date;
- recovery condition and override procedure.

Candidate rule forms include:

- hard-block a provider only while an independently observed health state says it is unavailable; route to an existing fallback;
- hard-block payloads across a privacy boundary when the selected provider is not eligible;
- warn or cap repeated same-evidence retries after measured marginal yield collapses;
- cap fan-out when branches share model, prompt, and evidence and therefore add little independent information;
- disallow a strict output schema for a task/output class known not to satisfy it, while allowing a looser native response path;
- reserve enough quota for verification or completion instead of allowing speculative fan-out to exhaust the session.

The registry should be deny-by-evidence, not deny-by-anecdote. A disconnected provider is a contraindication only while the health fact is fresh; past failure alone becomes a warning. Rules expire closed-to-review but open-to-normal behavior unless the underlying safety boundary is timeless. Every hard block must state a fallback, or it converts cost control into task failure.

This mechanism is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent repeated spend on a known-ineligible intervention when the predicate is accurate, because exclusion happens before the call. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that a manually curated registry yields net savings over time without an accountable review owner; drug labeling has institutional maintenance and agent tools change faster. The proposed shadow run and stale-rule injection test settle that operational premise.

### Why not a learned guardrail or global denylist

A learned contraindication predictor would be a new opaque failure surface, require its own training/evaluation loop, and violate the anti-overfit gate's rejection of a trained critic/guardrail framework. A global denylist loses context and recovery semantics. The transferable medical property is a small, explicit, tiered, versioned negative-knowledge registry—not the bureaucracy surrounding it.

## Mechanism 4: differential diagnosis as selective hypothesis triage

### How it works in clinical medicine

A differential diagnosis is a set of plausible explanations updated as evidence arrives. Its value is avoiding premature closure and ensuring “can't miss” conditions receive attention even when they are not most likely. AHRQ summarizes that broad differentials can limit missed diagnostic opportunities and diagnostic timeouts are intended to reduce bias ([AHRQ PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/diagnostic-time-outs-improve-diagnosis)). The National Academies describes Bayesian updating of disease probability and selection of further information based on likelihood, treatment benefits/harms, and patient preferences ([Improving Diagnosis in Health Care](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)).

But evidence for checklist-like implementations is mixed. AHRQ reports no effect for a general SLOW intervention, mostly non-significant findings for process-focused checklists, reduced accuracy from checklists on easy cases in one study, and some benefit from a content-specific checklist on difficult cases ([AHRQ checklist review](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)). Thus the genuine mechanism is **selective hypothesis triage under consequential uncertainty**, not mandatory enumeration.

### Proposal 4 — Triggered “likely / dangerous / discriminating probe” card [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

UNVERIFIED premise: observable trigger conditions identify difficult cases better than they burden easy ones; settle it with a preregistered A/B evaluation split by trigger status, reporting both quality and added compute.

When, and only when, a trigger fires—contradictory evidence, repeated failed fixes, high-consequence irreversible action, low confidence plus large consequence, or a result inconsistent with a test—require three short fields before more compute:

1. **Most likely failure mode** and the cheapest observation that separates it.
2. **Dangerous-to-miss failure mode** and the smallest check that rules it below the action threshold.
3. **Iatrogenic failure mode**: how the proposed escalation itself could worsen correctness, cost, privacy, or completion.

The card must produce at most one next discriminating probe by default. It does not spawn one agent per hypothesis. If no candidate observation can change the next action, the threshold gate stops the investigation. Content-specific cards may exist for a few empirically high-loss classes (destructive repository operations, current/high-stakes research, flaky provider diagnosis), but there should be no universal long checklist.

This selective card is **likely (55-80%)** to catch premature closure and the neglected possibility that the intervention causes the failure, based on the clinical purpose of differential diagnosis and iatrogenic review. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a generic always-on version improves overall accuracy, because AHRQ's review finds mixed or negative effects for generic/easy-case checklist use ([AHRQ checklist review](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)). Consequently, always-on differential fan-out is rejected.

## Integrated minimal design [UNVERIFIED]

The four useful pieces form one existing-bus policy, not four services:

1. A trigger detects consequential uncertainty or a proposed optional expensive action.
2. The contraindication table first excludes contextually ineligible actions.
3. The three-field differential card runs only for triggered difficult/high-consequence cases.
4. The threshold gate chooses stop, one cheap discriminator, or direct escalation.
5. The evaluator records randomized counterfactual samples and publishes NNE-B/NNE-H plus cost by task stratum.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

UNVERIFIED integration premise: the hook can access adequate task-stratum, cost, provider-health, and outcome features without becoming a second runtime; settle it by building a read-only prototype against recorded events and measuring feature coverage before enabling routing.

The online portion should remain deliberately coarse. The evaluator may be a script run periodically over local records; it is not a daemon. If the prototype cannot fit approximately within the stated small-build boundary or requires cross-session state beyond a compact results file, the recommendation fails the anti-overfit gate and should be reduced rather than promoted to BUILD-LARGE.

### Evaluation protocol

The following experiment is required before any quality-preserving savings claim:

- Pre-register task strata, benefit/harm outcomes, severe-error non-inferiority margin, compute measure, and stopping rule.
- Freeze model/version, temperature, available tools, and task inputs within each paired comparison.
- Run baseline and gated policies on the same tasks; randomize ordering and blind graders to policy and cost.
- Preserve a small randomized exploration arm so counterfactual benefit is observable even when the gate says stop.
- Report absolute rates and confidence intervals, NNE-B/NNE-H, tokens, tool calls, latency, completion rate, and severe harms by stratum.
- Run fault injections for provider recovery, stale contraindications, correlated agent branches, false-low confidence, false-high confidence, quota exhaustion, and delayed high-consequence action.
- Reject the policy if severe harm breaches the margin even when average cost falls.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this protocol can distinguish grossly wasteful escalation from useful escalation because it directly randomizes the optional intervention and measures absolute outcome differences. It is **likely (55-80%)** that sparse severe harms will remain statistically unresolved at ordinary harness sample sizes; the evidence is their low expected base rate and the resulting need for large denominators, so the correct result may be “insufficient evidence,” not presumed safety. Sequential accumulation across releases, with model/version stratification, is the settling experiment.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Uniform cheap-first routing

Rejected as the primary mechanism. It ignores the clinical model's upper region, where acting/escalating directly is preferable because an extra test adds cost or delay without changing action. It can also under-spend on high-consequence tasks.

### Uniform maximum-compute routing

Rejected. It assumes information has non-negative marginal value, contrary to evidence on over-testing cascades and checklist burden ([AHRQ cascade summary](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/cascades-care-after-incidental-findings-us-national-survey-physicians); [AHRQ checklist review](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)).

### One global confidence threshold

Rejected. Clinical treatment thresholds vary with treatment benefit/harm and test properties; likewise agent escalation thresholds must vary with consequence, incremental cost, and intervention-specific uplift ([National Academies threshold model](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)). Self-reported confidence alone is not a value-of-information estimate.

### One global NNE ranking

Rejected. Absolute effects depend on baseline risk, comparator, outcome, population, and time horizon. Cochrane explicitly warns about this dependence ([Cochrane Handbook §15.4.3](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

### Always-on multi-agent differential

Rejected. It converts a selective safety technique into uniform spending and mistakes correlated samples for independent tests. The available clinical checklist evidence does not support universal use and includes degraded easy-case performance ([AHRQ checklist review](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)).

### New orchestration or budget daemon

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. None of the transferred mechanisms requires durable execution, a vector database/knowledge graph, a trained critic, a second orchestration runtime, observability SaaS, or a guardrail framework. Adding one would contribute more iatrogenic surface than the small policy justifies.

## What this catches that uniform cost control misses

Subject to the unverified effectiveness experiments above, the design targets five blind spots:

- **Non-decision-changing spend:** a call can be accurate yet worthless if every possible result leads to the same next action.
- **Iatrogenic compute:** extra branches can introduce false claims, distract from a correct answer, trigger cascades, consume the verification reserve, or prevent completion.
- **Contextual ineligibility:** an intervention may be useful generally but contraindicated for this provider state, privacy boundary, schema/task pairing, quota state, or correlated evidence set.
- **Baseline-risk blindness:** the same escalation may have low NNE-B on difficult debugging tasks and extremely high NNE-B on routine transforms; an average hides both.
- **Asymmetric stakes:** low-probability dangerous-to-miss failures justify a small discriminator, while low-consequence uncertainty often does not.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that these categories are conceptually distinct from a uniform token ceiling or uniform model tier, because each conditions on action change, harm, eligibility, baseline risk, or consequence rather than raw spend. It is **UNVERIFIED** whether they account for a material share of this owner's actual cost; the settling experiment is to label a representative month of optional calls with the five categories, validate labels using two independent reviewers, and compute avoidable cost with counterfactual quality checks.

## Gaps and stopping conditions

- No external source validates this exact agent-harness translation. Every performance prediction is therefore banded and tied to an experiment.
- No local harness or corpus was inspected, by unit instruction. Actual integration points, event-field availability, and the current proportion of non-decision-changing calls remain UNVERIFIED; the read-only recorded-event prototype described above would settle them.
- Clinical threshold models depend on quantified benefits and harms that are difficult to put on one scale. If separate outcome columns produce conflicting choices and the owner has not supplied preferences, the gate must ask or preserve the current policy; it must not hide the tradeoff in an arbitrary scalar.
- If randomized exploration is unacceptable, causal NNE estimates for suppressed escalations will remain biased. In that case, use the registry only for independently established contraindications and stop short of adaptive routing.
- If task distribution or model versions change faster than estimates accumulate, the NNE ledger will not transfer. Expire estimates by model/version and return to conservative routing rather than treating old evidence as current.

## Bottom line

Clinical medicine contributes a defensible cost-control rule: **spend to cross an action threshold, not to maximize certainty**. Pair that rule with absolute-effect reporting (NNE-B/NNE-H), tiered contraindications, and a triggered—not universal—differential card. The transfer is structurally sound at the decision level and structurally unsound if interpreted as literal disease probabilities, independent-agent “tests,” a global confidence cutoff, or an always-on checklist. The recommended implementation is a small existing-bus hook/config plus offline experimental evaluation; anything larger fails the anti-overfit gate without new evidence.
