# X014 — Cross-domain borrow: intelligence tradecraft → use-versus-mention guards

## Executive judgment

The analogy transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form. Intelligence tradecraft has **not** already solved natural-language use-versus-mention classification. The Admiralty/NATO grading system asks whether a *source* is reliable and whether a *report* is credible; neither question asks whether text is an operative instruction, a quotation, an example, a prohibition, or a description. Treating “mention” as “low credibility” would be a category error.

What intelligence tradecraft *has* solved is the more general analytic failure produced by collapsing distinct semantic layers into one undifferentiated observation:

1. grade the source separately from the specific report;
2. distinguish underlying information from assumptions and analytic judgments;
3. compare explicit alternative explanations and privilege evidence that discriminates among them; and
4. express residual uncertainty in a controlled vocabulary rather than hiding it in a binary conclusion.

Those four mechanisms map cleanly to a **role-graded evidence gate**. The proposed harness event preserves provenance and structural role, evaluates explicit competing hypotheses—operative use, inert mention, embedded/adversarial directive, and mixed content—and permits a guard to act only on indicators diagnostic for the guard’s threat model. Under this design, the literal occurrence of a guarded phrase is candidate generation, never final evidence.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this architecture would eliminate most of the assignment-reported 12+ false fires across five payload-text detectors, because quoted documents, tool results, examples, and actual tool actions become structurally different observations; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is a paired replay of every known false-fire payload plus matched operative-use controls through all five detectors. It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch dangerous paraphrases and mixed use/mention cases missed by literal matching, because the decision uses action/provenance indicators rather than a fixed phrase; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is an adversarial, paraphrased, nested, negated, and mixed-role test suite with blind human role labels.

The recommended design rides the existing hook/event bus and does not introduce a second orchestrator, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It therefore passes the anti-overfit gate.

## What the source field actually solved

### 1. The Admiralty/NATO mechanism: never collapse source reliability into report credibility

In its own field, intelligence evaluation receives reporting from sources whose track records, access, motivation, collection limitations, and susceptibility to manipulation differ. JDP 2-00 requires two separate evaluations:

- **reliability of the source**, based for human/organizational sources on matters such as motivation, expertise, access, and reporting history, and for technical sources on system capabilities and limitations (§3.39a, PDF pp. 67-68); and
- **credibility of the information**, evaluated internally for factual or logical plausibility and externally through corroboration (§3.39b, p. 68).

Doctrine explicitly says the two “must be evaluated separately”: a previously reliable source can provide inaccurate information, and credible reporting can come from an unreliable source (§3.39c, p. 68). The NATO Intelligence Grading System, often called the Admiralty Code, encodes both judgments in one digraph: A-F for reliability and 1-6 for credibility. JDP 2-00 gives the deliberately crossed examples B5 (usually reliable source, improbable information) and E1 (unreliable source, information confirmed elsewhere), demonstrating that the axes are not expected to match (§3.40 and Table 3.1, p. 69).

The transferable mechanism is **orthogonal labeling before judgment**. An analyst does not let a property of the container stand in for a property of the proposition inside it.

For use-versus-mention, the analogous collapse is: “the byte sequence appears in a payload, therefore the action denoted by the sequence is being requested or performed.” The payload is the container; the matched span is an item within it; the span’s discourse role and the event’s operational stage are separate properties.

The analogy is not identity. Reliability/credibility are epistemic axes; provenance/semantic role are functional axes. The useful borrow is the separation discipline and crossed classification, not the A-F/1-6 labels or their meanings.

### 2. ICD 203: distinguish information, assumptions, and judgments

ICD 203 requires analytic products to clearly distinguish statements conveying underlying intelligence information from statements conveying assumptions or judgments. It defines assumptions as suppositions that frame or support an argument and judgments as conclusions based on information, analysis, and assumptions; linchpin assumptions must be explicit, and products should explain what follows if they are wrong (standard (3), PDF p. 4).

This is a direct precedent for **typed speech and reasoning roles**. Identical subject matter has different status depending on whether it is observed reporting, an assumed bridge, or an analyst’s conclusion. The standard does not infer these types from keywords; the producer carries the distinction into the product.

Translated to a harness, the producer of an event must carry role metadata forward rather than forcing every downstream detector to reconstruct role from raw text. At minimum, the harness needs to distinguish owner instruction, agent proposal/reasoning, structured tool intent, executed tool result, external/reference content, and detector-generated explanation. This is a stronger mapping than the Admiralty analogy: both mechanisms prevent downstream consumers from confusing a proposition with a proposition *about* that proposition.

### 3. ACH and indicators: compare role hypotheses using discriminating evidence

The CIA Tradecraft Primer defines Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) as identifying alternative explanations and evaluating evidence that can disconfirm rather than merely confirm them (PDF p. 17). Its method arrays hypotheses across columns and evidence down rows, scoring each item consistent, inconsistent, or not applicable (p. 18). The Primer’s key diagnosticity rule is that evidence consistent with multiple or all hypotheses is unimportant for choosing among them; analysts should focus on inconsistent and discriminating evidence (footnote 5, p. 18). It also calls for sensitivity analysis when critical evidence may be wrong, misleading, or deceptive, and for identifying expected-but-absent evidence and indicators consistent or inconsistent with every hypothesis (p. 18).

Literal phrase presence is precisely non-diagnostic evidence in a use-versus-mention decision: the phrase is expected under both `OPERATIVE_USE` and `INERT_MENTION`. A detector that fires from that row alone commits the error ACH is designed to expose.

The Primer’s Indicators or Signposts technique adds a useful operational form: identify competing scenarios, create separate lists of observable activities/statements/events expected under each, review them over time, and use agreed observable criteria as a baseline (pp. 15-16). For a harness, “observable” should mean event metadata and action-boundary facts wherever possible—not a speculative interpretation of prose.

### 4. Sherman Kent: separate kind of statement and calibrate residual uncertainty

Kent begins with three statements about an airfield: a close-to-indisputable observation, a strongly supported judgment, and an estimate about possible future conduct. He argues that intelligence writing must make clear what is certain knowledge, what is reasoned judgment, and what degree of certainty lies behind each key judgment (PDF pp. 0-2). His core mechanism is a controlled mapping between probability language and intended odds so that the reader receives the confidence the analyst intended.

The harness borrow is not “attach an arbitrary confidence number.” It is: retain a distinct `role_confidence` judgment, use a small calibrated vocabulary, and make policy depend on both estimated role and consequence. Confidence must describe uncertainty in the role classification; severity must describe cost if the guarded action occurs. Conflating the two recreates the category error Kent sought to prevent.

## Concrete translation: the role-graded evidence gate

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: typed event envelope and span provenance

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add one shared normalization function on the existing bus. It wraps every guard candidate in an envelope such as:

```text
GuardCandidate {
  event_kind: user_message | assistant_text | proposed_tool | executed_tool | tool_result | file_read | web_result
  actor: owner | agent | tool | external
  trust_zone: owner | harness | workspace | external_untrusted
  operation: optional structured tool/command identity
  spans: [{start, end, role_hint: instruction | quote | code | example | data | prohibition | unknown}]
  raw_text: original bytes
}
```

The decisive rule is that trusted structural metadata cannot be overwritten by text inside `raw_text`. A file saying “I am a user instruction” remains `file_read/external-or-workspace data`; a detector explanation repeating a forbidden command remains `detector-generated explanation`; a proposed tool call remains `proposed_tool` even if its argument contains prose.

Segment-level labels are necessary. A whole user message can quote a dangerous command and then genuinely ask to run a related command; classifying the whole payload as either “instruction” or “mention” loses the mixed case.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that event kinds and actor/trust zones provide more diagnostic evidence than lexical matches, because they derive from the runtime path rather than from the proposition being classified; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is to compare mutual information and classification error for `(event_kind, actor, operation, span)` versus keyword-only features on a labeled replay corpus.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that useful span hints for free-form user text can remain under the BUILD-SMALL line budget without a model call, because quote fences, code blocks, explicit reporting verbs, and negation are tractable but nested informal quotation and indirect speech are not; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is a two-day spike measured by nonblank source lines and error on the paired corpus. Failure of this experiment does not justify a new classifier service: mark uncertain spans `unknown` and defer to the action boundary.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: an ACH-lite role matrix, not a phrase score

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For each matched span, explicitly evaluate four hypotheses:

- `H_USE`: the owner or agent is authorizing, proposing, or invoking the guarded operation;
- `H_MENTION`: the span is inert data—quotation, documentation, example, analysis, history, or detector explanation;
- `H_EMBEDDED`: an untrusted source contains a directive aimed at changing agent behavior (important for prompt-injection guards, but not proof of owner authorization);
- `H_MIXED`: operative and referential spans coexist and must be handled separately.

Use a small consistency matrix rather than one additive “danger score”:

| Observable indicator | H_USE | H_MENTION | H_EMBEDDED | H_MIXED | Diagnostic value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Guarded operation is the structured `proposed_tool.operation` | C | I | I | C | High for action/safety guards |
| Match is only inside `tool_result`, `file_read`, or `web_result` data | I | C | C | N | Separates owner use; does not separate mention from injection |
| Span is fenced/quoted and attributed to a document or example | I | C | N | C | Medium; adversarial text can imitate quoting |
| External directive addresses the agent and conflicts with the owner task | I | N | C | N | High for injection guards; requires task-context comparison |
| Owner explicitly asks to execute the operation outside the quoted span | C | I | I | C | High |
| Owner says not to execute it | I | C | I | N | High; prohibition is mention, not authorization |
| Literal guarded phrase occurs | C | C | C | C | Zero: candidate generation only |
| Actual structured invocation reaches the sink | C | I | I | C | Highest for action guards |

`C/I/N` means consistent/inconsistent/not diagnostic, following ACH’s matrix form. The implementation does not reproduce full human ACH or tally rows mechanically. It rejects a hypothesis when a high-diagnostic structural fact is inconsistent with it, retains alternatives where evidence is shared, and exposes the decisive indicators in an audit record.

Guard policy is threat-specific:

- **Action/authorization guards** block or request confirmation only for `H_USE` or the operative spans of `H_MIXED`, preferably at `proposed_tool` immediately before the effectful sink.
- **Prompt-injection guards** inspect external/reference spans and alert on `H_EMBEDDED`; they must not treat an embedded directive as owner authorization.
- **Information-handling guards** can care about both use and disclosure, but must name which semantic act—reading, transmitting, logging, or executing—is guarded.
- **Mention-only** produces no user-interrupting fire. It can optionally emit a sampled audit counter so regression remains visible.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that four hypotheses are the smallest adequate set, because a binary use/mention split cannot represent hostile directives in untrusted data or mixed payloads; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is error analysis after labeling at least the known false fires plus purpose-built external-directive and mixed-span cases, adding a hypothesis only when two or more irreducible errors share a missing role.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: move high-consequence enforcement to the action boundary

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For guards whose harm requires an action—deletion, overwrite, network send, credential use, package install, commit/push, or process execution—evaluate the structured proposed tool invocation immediately before execution. Earlier raw-text matches may annotate risk but must not block merely because a document describes the operation.

This borrows the signposts principle: watch for an observable event expected under the dangerous scenario. A proposed `delete(path)` or parsed command operation is discriminating; the word “delete” in a postmortem is not.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that sink placement sharply reduces use/mention false positives for action guards, because an inert mention does not create a structured invocation; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is an A/B replay comparing payload-stage and sink-stage decisions on paired mention/use cases, followed by shadow-mode comparison on real events.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that sink placement alone handles every one of the five detectors, because some guards may protect information exposure or instruction integrity before any tool call; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is to classify each detector’s protected semantic act and identify whether a concrete sink event exists. Detectors without a sink remain on Proposal B rather than being forced into Proposal C.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: calibrated abstention and consequence-aware policy

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Return one of `operative`, `mention`, `embedded`, `mixed`, or `unknown`, plus a confidence band calibrated from held-out examples. Do not manufacture a precise probability before calibration. For low-consequence events, `unknown` can log and continue; for irreversible high-consequence tool intents, `unknown` can request confirmation at the sink. A mention classification must never be upgraded to dangerous merely because the topic is severe.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that an explicit `unknown` state will outperform forced binary decisions on nested, multilingual, or adversarial prose by preventing unjustified certainty; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is selective-risk evaluation: plot false-fire and missed-use rates as a function of abstention on a held-out challenge set.

The confidence vocabulary must use the owner-mandated bands in this program. Kent supports controlled probability language, but the harness must calibrate its own thresholds rather than importing Kent’s historical numbers or the slightly different current ICD 203 bands.

## What this catches that literal payload matching misses

The following are predicted capabilities, not findings about the uninspected harness:

1. **Quoted or fenced dangerous commands in documentation.** Structural role and event kind select `H_MENTION`; a literal detector false-fires. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that cleanly fenced examples are separable when fence boundaries survive event normalization, because the delimiter is directly observable; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is exact replay across Markdown, XML, JSON strings, and nested fences.
2. **Negated/prohibited actions.** “Do not run X” is not authorization to run X. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit local negation can be separated with span parsing, because prohibition is a distinct cue; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is paired imperative/prohibition examples including scope traps such as “do not merely describe X; run X.”
3. **Detector self-reference and postmortems.** A guard’s own explanation or a failure report may repeat its trigger. Actor/event provenance selects `H_MENTION`, preventing recursive firing. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that this works if detector-generated output has an unforgeable event kind, because the classification does not depend on its contents; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is recursive replay through all five detectors for three generations.
4. **Paraphrased operative actions with no banned phrase.** A structured tool operation or sink event can identify the action even when prose avoids the literal token. It is **very likely (80-90%)** for typed tool calls and **unlikely (20-45%)** for arbitrary shell strings without parsing, because the former exposes an operation field while the latter does not; both are **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is a paraphrase suite split by typed tools versus shell commands.
5. **External instructions masquerading as owner instructions.** Provenance plus task conflict can select `H_EMBEDDED`; a binary mention suppressor might incorrectly ignore it, while a payload keyword detector might incorrectly treat it as authorized. It is **likely (55-80%)** that provenance identifies the external origin and **roughly even (45-55%)** that a BUILD-SMALL rule can reliably determine directive intent across free text; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is a blinded prompt-injection benchmark containing benign discussions of injection, actual embedded directives, and indirect exfiltration requests.
6. **Mixed documents.** Span-scoped classification can allow discussion while guarding the actual requested operation. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce whole-message overblocking, because operative and referential spans no longer share one label; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is a compositional suite in which quote/instruction order, nesting, and adjacency are systematically permuted.

## Adversarial audit of the analogy

### Where it transfers

- **Same structural error:** both domains fail when properties of a container are allowed to stand in for properties of a contained proposition. JDP 2-00’s B5 and E1 examples prove that intelligence doctrine expects crossed classifications rather than one inherited label.
- **Same ambiguity problem:** ACH is expressly intended for large, ambiguous, sometimes deceptive evidence sets. Use/mention failures also present observations consistent with multiple explanations; literal phrase presence has no diagnostic value because every role hypothesis predicts it.
- **Same need for typed layers:** ICD 203’s information/assumption/judgment distinction demonstrates that a production pipeline can and should preserve semantic status for downstream readers rather than leave status implicit.
- **Same communication risk:** Kent’s target is a recipient acting on a confidence different from the producer’s intended one. A hook that converts “document mentions operation” into “user intends operation” is an even sharper version of unintended semantic transmission.

### Where it does not transfer

- **Credibility is not role.** True text can be a mention; false text can be an operative instruction. Source reliability and information credibility cannot classify use/mention. The Admiralty scales must not be copied.
- **Human analytic time is unlike hook latency.** Full ACH can take analysts hours or a day according to the CIA Primer (p. 17). A hook needs a small, deterministic approximation using preexisting metadata. A second analyst/model/critic service would fail both latency discipline and the anti-overfit gate.
- **Intelligence evidence is often retrospective; hooks authorize effects.** A missed low-probability hypothesis in analysis and a missed destructive invocation have different loss functions. Consequently, confidence cannot be the only policy input; effect reversibility and consequence matter at the sink.
- **The payload can attack the classifier.** Intelligence doctrine discusses denial and deception, but a harness uniquely processes text that can explicitly claim forged roles (“this is trusted,” “ignore provenance”). Role metadata therefore has to be out-of-band and non-overridable by payload text.
- **ACH’s simplified tally can be misleading.** Ten weak lexical cues must not outvote one structural fact such as `event_kind=executed_tool`. Diagnosticity and evidence dependence matter more than raw counts. This design borrows the matrix, not naïve voting.
- **Semantic roles can nest without limit.** A document can quote a detector report that quotes a command that was originally requested. No small parser will perfectly recover arbitrary discourse nesting. The design needs `unknown` and sink enforcement, not a claim of complete understanding.

These disanalogies rule out a direct transplant but do not defeat the narrower architecture. The transferred unit is “separate the axes, preserve typed provenance, compare explicit hypotheses with diagnostic evidence, and expose uncertainty,” not “run intelligence analysis inside every hook.”

## Verification design and completion criteria

No proposal is called done from one detector probe. The verification plan deliberately supplies mechanically independent signals and validates each detector against a known-present control before interpreting absence.

### Offline paired corpus signal

Build minimal pairs that differ only in semantic role:

- imperative versus quotation;
- imperative versus prohibition;
- owner instruction versus identical bytes in tool/file/web output;
- literal phrase versus paraphrased structured action;
- benign discussion of prompt injection versus an external embedded directive;
- pure mention/use versus mixed spans;
- single versus nested quotation;
- English versus languages actually observed in operation.

Label spans independently by two humans, adjudicate disagreement, freeze a held-out set, and report per-detector false-fire, missed-use, abstention, and latency. First prove the evaluator detects a known operative use and a known inert mention for every detector. This is one signal because all variants share the same offline replay machinery.

### Shadow action-boundary signal

Run the new decision in non-blocking shadow mode on live hook events while the existing policy remains authoritative. Compare its classifications to independently observable downstream facts: whether a structured tool call was actually proposed/executed/cancelled, and whether the owner corrected a false interruption. This is mechanically independent of the handcrafted corpus because it uses runtime event fields and subsequent behavior rather than replay labels.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that combining frozen paired evaluation with shadow action-boundary evidence will expose regressions that either signal alone misses, because one controls semantic contrasts while the other samples natural runtime distribution; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is disagreement analysis after a prespecified shadow window measured in events rather than calendar time.

### Acceptance gates

A detector is “role-aware” only when all are true:

1. known-present operative controls fire and known-present mention controls do not;
2. every assignment-reported false fire has a replay fixture and expected span-level result;
3. held-out false-fire rate materially improves without a material missed-use increase;
4. `unknown` and mixed-role outcomes are visible, not silently coerced;
5. the audit record names event kind, span, retained/rejected hypotheses, decisive indicator, confidence band, and final policy action;
6. shadow evidence independently agrees at a prespecified threshold; and
7. rollback is configuration-level and does not require replacing the runtime.

Exact numerical thresholds are **UNVERIFIED** because this unit did not inspect base rates or consequence models; the settling experiment is to compute current per-detector confusion matrices on the frozen set, then set thresholds before examining new-system results.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Reject: phrase allowlists for quotation markers

A list of “documentation words,” code fences, or prefixes fixes easy syntactic examples but fails informal quotation, nested content, mixed instructions, and adversarial imitation. It also creates a growing exception grammar tied to yesterday’s false fires. This is not an adequate architecture.

### Reject: one scalar danger score

Adding weights for “quoted,” “imperative,” “external,” and “dangerous” erases the independence of role, provenance, confidence, and consequence. A high-severity mention can outscore a low-severity real action, exactly the collapse the source-field mechanisms warn against.

### Reject: use an LLM judge on every payload

This adds latency, nondeterminism, prompt-injection surface, and another opaque semantic interpreter. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat structural event metadata on typed action cases, because the runtime already knows whether it is handling a tool proposal or reference data; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is a head-to-head held-out comparison including latency and adversarial role-forgery cases. A model may be evaluated later only for `unknown` free-text spans, not installed as a new orchestration or critic runtime.

### Reject: import Admiralty A-F/1-6 grades

Those labels answer source reliability and report credibility questions, not functional role. Reusing them lends borrowed authority to a different construct and makes the system harder to audit.

### Reject: full ACH per event

Human ACH is a disciplined analytic workflow, not a millisecond policy engine. The harness needs a fixed, inspectable role matrix with a handful of high-diagnostic structural indicators, not brainstorming, team facilitation, or evidence tally theater.

## Minimal implementation sequence

1. Define the role/event envelope and threat-specific policy table without changing enforcement.
2. Wrap the five detectors with candidate-generation-only lexical matching and shadow role decisions.
3. Create paired fixtures from the supplied false fires plus operative controls.
4. Add sink selection for guards whose protected semantic act is an action.
5. Calibrate `unknown`/confidence behavior on held-out data.
6. Enable one detector at a time only after both offline and shadow signals pass; retain a configuration rollback.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this sequence can be implemented as shared BUILD-SMALL normalization plus per-detector CONFIG, because the architecture is a typed adapter and policy table rather than a new runtime; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is an interface-and-line-count spike against the actual hook signatures. If the spike exceeds the BUILD-SMALL boundary, the correct response is to narrow the feature set to structural event typing and sink placement—not to create a daemon.

## Final recommendation

Adopt the narrow transfer: **separate provenance from proposition role; treat literal matches as non-diagnostic candidate evidence; compare explicit use, mention, embedded-directive, and mixed hypotheses; enforce action harms at structured sinks; and expose calibrated uncertainty.** Do not import credibility grades, do not run full ACH, and do not create a second semantic-guard runtime.

The strongest part of the borrow is not a scoring formula. It is a design invariant: **no guard may infer an operative semantic act solely from the presence of words that describe that act.** Every fire must cite at least one role-diagnostic indicator outside the matched bytes.
