**Date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive result

The borrow transfers, but only after correcting the unit of analysis.

The useful mechanism is a **pre-registered, blinded, negative-controlled, group-sequential dose-response experiment** in which:

* the *task instance* is the experimental unit;
* worker count (for example 1, 2, 4, or 8) is the treatment dose;
* quality, cost, and elapsed time are outcomes;
* task families are pre-specified strata;
* interim looks occur after batches of tasks, not after each newly spawned worker;
* arm-dropping and stopping rules are fixed before comparative results are visible;
* graders do not see worker-count labels; and
* negative controls test whether the scoring and orchestration pipeline can manufacture a parallelism “gain” when the effective output is unchanged.

This mechanism can determine a calibrated default worker-count policy for recurring task classes. It does **not** establish the optimal count for one unprecedented live task. Workers are treatment components, not independent research participants; their outputs share prompts, models, context, tools, and often errors. Treating `n workers` as statistical sample size would be pseudoreplication. A runtime gate derived from the experiment may make a budgeted decision, but it must report “stopped under the calibrated policy,” not “additional workers cannot help.”

My overall judgment is **very likely (80-90%)** that the offline calibration borrow is structurally valid. Evidence: adaptive trials already pre-specify sample-size adaptations and interim rules while preserving inferential integrity ([FDA guidance, pp. 13-17](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)); the agent target has repeated task instances on which worker-count arms can be compared; and current agent-scaling experiments show task structure and diversity materially change scaling behavior ([Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/); [Yang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)).

My judgment is **unlikely (20-45%)** that classical power or group-sequential boundaries can be validly applied *inside one novel task* without a separate empirical model. Evidence: the available observations within a task are dependent worker outputs, there is no population of exchangeable task instances inside that run, and Yang et al.'s own information-theoretic treatment requires independent-evidence and coverage assumptions that it explicitly says may fail for real agents ([paper, limitations and assumptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). The settling experiment is a prospective replay study comparing predicted stop decisions with the blinded marginal utility of actually running the withheld next batch across at least the pre-registered task strata.

## 1. What experimental design has already solved

### 1.1 The central problem: looking, adapting, and stopping without fooling oneself

A fixed design chooses sample size and analysis before outcomes are observed. A group-sequential or adaptive design permits planned interim analyses and planned changes—such as early stopping for efficacy or futility, dropping arms, or modifying sample size—while accounting for the fact that repeated looks create additional chances to see an apparently favorable result.

The key is not “adapt whenever the curve looks flat.” The key is **prospective specification of the adaptations, decision rules, estimand, maximum information, and analysis**. The FDA states that estimates and confidence intervals accounting for a group-sequential design should be prospectively planned; that sample-size modification rules should be pre-specified; and that indiscriminate sample-size modification based on interim comparative results can more than double Type I error in an example described in its guidance ([FDA, pp. 13-17](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)). The Adaptive designs CONSORT Extension defines a pre-planned adaptation as one specified before seeing accumulating arm-level data and recorded in an audit trail, and calls for reporting the interim decision rules and uncertainty ([ACE statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7298968/)).

In its own field, a typical group-sequential mechanism works as follows:

1. Define the treatment effect and primary outcome.
2. Choose the smallest effect worth detecting, tolerated false-positive rate, desired power, variance assumptions, maximum sample size, and interim-look schedule.
3. Derive efficacy and futility boundaries—analytically or by simulation—that give acceptable operating characteristics under relevant scenarios.
4. Randomize experimental units to treatment arms, keep allocation/outcomes blinded where feasible, and collect data.
5. At each pre-planned information fraction, an authorized interim process compares results to the fixed boundaries. It may stop, continue, or make only the adaptations permitted by the protocol.
6. Report every look, adaptation, deviation, and final uncertainty using an analysis that accounts for the sequential design.

This solves a governance-and-inference problem: it allows learning during an experiment without silently changing the success criterion after seeing favorable or disappointing data. It does not guarantee that the assumptions, endpoint, or scientific model are correct.

### 1.2 Pre-registration: freeze the decision contract before results can steer it

Pre-registration records hypotheses, outcomes, exclusions, analysis, and decision rules before the relevant data are observed. The Center for Open Science distinguishes registration before data collection, before human observation, before researcher access, and before analysis, and requires disclosure of prior access because observation can compromise confirmatory status ([COS preregistration](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg)). Registered Reports go further: study question, procedure, analysis pipeline, and sampling/power plan are reviewed before data collection, with publication not conditioned on the significance or direction of the result ([Scientific Reports policy](https://www.nature.com/srep/journal-policies/registered-reports)).

The mechanism separates two legitimate activities:

* **Confirmatory:** run the frozen plan and interpret its specified endpoint.
* **Exploratory:** inspect surprising patterns and generate a revised hypothesis, clearly labeled as post hoc and tested on new held-out data.

For orchestration, this directly maps to preventing a harness designer from trying worker counts, graders, task slices, cost weights, and stopping thresholds until one combination makes a preferred topology look best.

### 1.3 Power analysis: decide what “no useful gain” could mean before spending the budget

Power analysis does not prove the null. It connects a minimum effect of interest, noise, significance criterion, design, and sample size to the probability of detecting an effect if it exists. The FDA lists outcome variability, Type I error, hypothesized effect size, and desired power among the factors in sample-size determination ([FDA, p. 16](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)). A negative result from an underpowered comparison is therefore not evidence of equivalence.

For worker parallelism, the most important translation is conceptual: define a **minimum worthwhile marginal utility** before measuring the scaling curve. “No statistically detectable improvement” and “no improvement large enough to pay for” are different claims. The harness needs the latter. Equivalence or non-inferiority framing is more appropriate than failure to reject a zero-effect null when deciding that a cheaper worker count is good enough.

### 1.4 Blinding: break the feedback loop between preferred result and scoring choices

Blinding prevents knowledge of arm assignment or the anticipated result from influencing treatment, measurement, analysis, or judgment. Blind analysis is also used outside medicine: particle-physics methods hide signal regions or transform values so analysts can finalize selections without knowing whether the favored result is appearing; Roodman reviews the motivation as protection against experimenter bias ([Roodman, 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0312102)). NIH guidance asks researchers to plan allocation concealment and blinded outcome assessment or explain why blinding is infeasible ([NINDS rigorous study design](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/funding/preparing-your-application/preparing-research-plan/rigorous-study-design-and-transparent-reporting)).

For orchestration, blind assessment means the evaluator receives canonicalized artifacts with worker-count, topology, token use, runtime, model, and run-order metadata removed. Cost and latency are joined only after quality judgments are sealed. Otherwise a grader who expects “eight agents must be better” can reward verbosity, polish, or apparent effort.

### 1.5 Negative controls: test whether the measurement system invents an effect

A negative control is selected so that the causal effect of interest is absent while relevant bias pathways remain. If an association appears in that control, confounding, selection, measurement, or analysis bias may be producing the primary result. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen describe negative controls as tools for detecting uncontrolled confounding and other bias that creates spurious causal inference; they also warn that a control with an actually plausible causal relationship can falsely indict the main study ([Lipsitch et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). Later work emphasizes that negative controls can lack sensitivity and specificity and cannot prove a null control association ([Penning de Vries and Groenwold, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10515451/)).

For orchestration, the cleanest negative-control exposure is **label-only parallelism**: pass the exact same candidate artifact through the grader twice, attach randomly permuted hidden arm labels upstream, and require a null difference after canonicalization. Any systematic score shift cannot be caused by worker count because the graded content is byte-identical. It reveals metadata leakage, order effects, caching contamination, or a grader path that is not actually blind.

## 2. Exact structural mapping

| Experimental-design object | Harness object | Translation constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Experimental unit | A task instance drawn from a declared task population | Not an individual worker. Repeated workers on one task are dependent components of one treatment. |
| Treatment / dose | Orchestration policy and worker count, e.g. `k ∈ {1,2,4,8}` | Keep model, total per-worker budget, tools, and synthesis policy fixed unless they are explicitly factorial treatment dimensions. |
| Blocking / stratification | Task class: parallel-search, independent verification, patch decomposition, sequential debugging, etc. | Strata must be declared before outcomes; post-hoc relabeling is exploratory. |
| Primary estimand | Task-level marginal net utility of `k2` versus `k1` | Quality, tokens/cost, wall time, failure rate, and coordination overhead must not be collapsed using weights chosen after results. |
| Sample size | Number of independent task instances | `k=8` is a treatment level, not sample size eight. |
| Interim look | Analysis after a batch of completed task instances | Never treat every worker completion as a fresh independent look. |
| Efficacy boundary | Evidence that a larger worker count yields at least the pre-specified worthwhile gain | Must account for repeated looks and multiple arms. |
| Futility / equivalence boundary | Evidence that the larger count is unlikely to achieve the pre-specified worthwhile gain | “Futile for this declared use and threshold,” not universally useless. |
| Blinded outcome assessor | Canonicalized objective oracle and/or blinded adjudicator | Withhold count, topology, model, cost, latency, and run order until quality is sealed. |
| Negative control | Byte-identical artifact with shuffled pseudo-arm label; sealed replay with count metadata removed | Detects scoring-path leakage; does not establish correctness of the primary metric. |
| Protocol deviation | Spawn retry, missing arm, tool outage, budget overrun, grader unblinding, manual intervention | Log; do not silently exclude. |

The most important mapping is the experimental unit. If a benchmark has 100 independent tasks and evaluates four worker counts on every task, it has 100 paired experimental units, not 1,500 units because `1+2+4+8=15` worker trajectories were generated per task. Treating trajectories as independent would narrow uncertainty spuriously because shared task difficulty, prompt, model, retrieval results, and synthesizer induce correlation.

### 3.2 Paired, randomized execution

Run every sampled task under each eligible worker-count arm. Randomize arm order within task to distribute transient load, provider drift, cache warmth, and time-of-day effects. Use the same starting state and pin model/tool versions where possible. If state cannot be reset, use separate clean worktrees/sandboxes and pre-register the contamination policy.

Pairing makes each task its own control. Analysis must aggregate at task level and preserve the within-task correlation rather than pretending worker trajectories are independent. For destructive or stateful real-world tasks that cannot be replayed safely, use archived tasks in a sandbox or exclude them by the sealed population rule; do not quietly substitute them after seeing outcomes.

### 3.3 Two-stage outcome measurement and blinding

Stage A seals quality without cost metadata:

1. Canonicalize the final artifact.
2. Strip count, topology, model, run ID, timestamps, token usage, and comments that disclose the arm.
3. Apply an executable oracle where the task has one: tests, schema validation, exact factual keys, or a deterministic acceptance harness.
4. Independently apply a blinded rubric adjudicator to properties the oracle does not cover.
5. Seal both results and reveal the arm only afterward.

Stage B joins cost, latency, retry, and failure metadata and computes the pre-specified task-level utility or lexicographic decision.

The gate must not claim “no quality gain” from a single detector. It may make that claim only when two mechanically independent signals agree—for example, a validated executable oracle and a separately blinded adjudication—and each detector first succeeds on a known-present difference. On tasks lacking two defensible quality detectors, the result must be “inconclusive for plateau,” even if the cost increase is obvious. This directly operationalizes the required two-signal absence rule.

The blinded-judge component is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce expectation-driven scoring bias. Evidence: blind analysis is designed to break feedback between observed results and analyst choices ([Roodman, 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0312102)); confidence is not higher because an agent artifact can leak its production process through style or verbosity even after metadata stripping. The settling experiment is a label-leakage test plus an arm-identification test: graders should be unable to predict worker count above the pre-specified tolerance from canonicalized artifacts, and identical artifacts with shuffled pseudo-labels should score equivalently.

### 3.4 Negative and positive controls

Use both controls because “the detector saw no effect” is uninterpretable until the detector demonstrates sensitivity.

**Negative control: label permutation.** Duplicate the exact artifact bytes, route copies through pseudo-arms with shuffled hidden count labels, and grade them in randomized order. The expected content effect is exactly zero. A systematic pseudo-arm difference identifies leakage, order bias, nondeterministic grading beyond tolerance, or a faulty join.

**Positive control: known score difference.** Inject a pre-declared, mechanically verifiable defect into one duplicate—such as removing a required test result, corrupting a known answer, or violating a schema—and require both the oracle and adjudicator to detect it at their pre-specified rates. This proves the plateau detectors can find a known-present quality difference before they are trusted to report absence.

Do **not** use “sequential tasks should not benefit from multiple workers” as a negative control. That causal null is not guaranteed: a second worker could independently catch an error even when execution order is sequential. Calling it a control would force the analogy and could falsely condemn the measurement system.

The label-permutation control is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch direct arm-label leakage or arm-dependent join bugs. Evidence: byte identity removes content as a causal path, leaving pipeline/measurement paths as the explanation for systematic arm differences; negative-control methodology is specifically intended to surface such bias paths ([Lipsitch et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to catch subtler content-mediated unblinding from verbosity or writing style because those features are absent from the byte-identical control. The settling experiment for that gap is the blinded arm-identification test described above.

### 3.5 Sequential decision and reporting

At each pre-registered task-batch boundary, compare larger counts with the next smaller count inside each stratum. Drop a larger-count arm for futility/equivalence only when:

1. both validated quality signals place the gain below the pre-specified minimum worthwhile effect using the sequentially valid rule;
2. cost or latency is worse under the pre-specified resource endpoint; and
3. the arm has met its minimum information requirement.

Promote a larger count only when its lower uncertainty bound clears the worthwhile-gain threshold and its resource trade-off satisfies the frozen owner preference. Otherwise continue to the next look or finish at the maximum task count. Publish all arms, looks, missing runs, retries, deviations, confidence intervals, and the protocol hash.

Do not invent numerical stopping boundaries from this ideation unit. Selecting them requires pilot variance, correlation, missingness, number of strata/arms, and an owner-set minimum worthwhile effect. The design’s operating characteristics should be simulated under null, small-benefit, large-benefit, heterogeneous-stratum, correlated-grader, and outage scenarios before use; FDA guidance likewise emphasizes prospective planning and evaluating adaptive-design operating characteristics ([FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)).

The confirmed table becomes a simple policy such as `task_stratum → default worker count and maximum count`. Initially run it in shadow mode: predict the stop count, but sometimes execute the withheld next arm to measure regret. Only after prospective performance meets the sealed acceptance rule should the harness enforce it.

A live staged version may spawn a small initial batch, measure unique contributions and disagreement, and conditionally request another batch. This runtime adaptation is **UNVERIFIED** and **roughly even (45-55%)** to outperform the simpler fixed-by-stratum table. Evidence for possible benefit: Yang et al. report that useful scaling tracks complementary effective channels rather than raw agent count ([Yang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). Evidence against confidence: their theory assumes forms of independent evidence and coverage that may not hold, and label-free output diversity can reward diverse wrong answers. If the live rule stops, its audit record must say:

> stopped under policy `parallelism-v1` because the pre-registered marginal-value gate did not clear; the counterfactual benefit of unrun workers is unknown

It must not say:

> more workers would not help

That wording preserves the difference between a resource decision and a causal conclusion.

## 4. What this catches that ad hoc orchestration misses

The following detection claims are prospective and therefore explicitly confidence-banded:

1. **False plateaus caused by low power.** The design is **very likely (80-90%)** to distinguish “evidence of practical equivalence under the declared margin” from “not enough task instances to detect the margin,” because minimum effect, task-level sample size, and sequential uncertainty are specified together. Evidence: power/sample-size planning links effect size, variability, error rates, and detection probability ([FDA, p. 16](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)).

2. **Winner’s curse from repeated peeking.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to flag runs where the chosen count was selected after unplanned looks or threshold changes, because protocol hashes and look records make the deviation observable. Evidence: unadjusted comparative sample-size adaptation can inflate false positives, while pre-planned sequential methods account for repeated looks ([FDA, pp. 16-17](https://www.fda.gov/media/78495/download)).

3. **Task-mix confounding.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal that a worker count wins only because it received easier or more parallelizable tasks, because arms are paired within task and results are stratified. Evidence: external controlled evaluation reports opposite multi-agent effects on parallelizable versus sequential tasks ([Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). Confidence is below very likely because a poor pre-specified taxonomy can retain heterogeneity.

4. **Judge expectation and metadata leakage.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch direct leakage through shuffled-label negative controls and known-difference controls; evidence and settling experiments are in §3.4. It may not catch every style-mediated cue.

5. **Pseudoreplication.** It is **almost certain (90-99%)** to prevent the specific error of counting worker trajectories as independent sample size if the task-level aggregation rule is enforced. Evidence: the protocol explicitly defines task as the unit and uses paired comparisons; remaining uncertainty is implementation compliance, settled by checking that every inferential row key is a unique task ID rather than a trajectory ID.

6. **Homogeneous worker redundancy.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to expose cases where 8 homogeneous workers add cost without worthwhile quality over 2 diverse workers. Evidence: Yang et al. report that two fully heterogeneous agents surpassed sixteen homogeneous agents in their tested vote/debate configurations and warn that useful gain depends on correct, complementary channels ([Yang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). Generalization to this harness is UNVERIFIED; the paired trial is the settling experiment.

7. **Outage and retry survivorship bias.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to show whether larger arms look better only after failed or expensive runs are silently excluded, because missing-arm and deviation policies are frozen and all attempts are reported. Evidence is the design mechanism itself; settle the implementation claim with a fault-injection trial that forces a known fraction of worker failures and verifies inclusion in the task-level endpoint.

8. **Metric-weight shopping.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal post-hoc changes in quality/cost/latency weights if the sealed protocol hash and raw endpoint table are retained. Evidence: pre-registration records analytical decisions before results can influence them ([COS](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg)). It cannot prove the original weights reflect the owner’s true preferences.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 5.1 Workers are treatment ingredients, not exchangeable subjects

Clinical-trial sample-size logic gains information from additional experimental units. Spawning another worker changes the treatment itself. Workers share a base model, prompt, retrieved context, tools, and upstream assumptions; a synthesizer can couple them further. Therefore a within-task worker count cannot be inserted into a standard power formula as `n`.

This disanalogy does not kill the offline borrow because repeated task instances can serve as experimental units. It does kill the strongest version of the live claim: a single run cannot provide conventional population-level power merely because it contains many trajectories.

### 5.2 Interference is the point, not a nuisance

Standard causal designs often rely on one unit’s assignment not changing another unit’s outcome. In an agent team, debate, shared files, messages, and synthesis intentionally create interference. The treatment must therefore be the **whole orchestration policy**, and the observed outcome must be task-level. Attempting to estimate a clean per-worker causal contribution inside an interacting team is generally ill-posed without a much richer design.

### 5.3 The outcome is multidimensional and partly subjective

A treatment trial may have a clinically meaningful primary endpoint. Agent work has correctness, completeness, novelty, maintainability, safety, cost, latency, and owner satisfaction. A scalar utility can conceal trade-offs. The protocol should preserve the endpoint vector and use either a pre-specified lexicographic rule or owner-set weights; post-hoc scalarization is not confirmatory.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a single stable scalar utility will generalize across all task families. Evidence: agent-scaling benefit already varies by task structure ([Google Research, 2026](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)), and cost/latency preferences differ by use context. The settling experiment is an owner preference elicitation over blinded quality-cost trade-off pairs, followed by a held-out consistency check.

### 5.4 Model and tool drift violate stationarity

Clinical trials occur over time too, but agent models, provider behavior, tools, prompts, and repositories can change abruptly. A calibrated policy can decay. The protocol must record versions and define expiration/recalibration triggers. It is **likely (55-80%)** that unversioned pooling across materially different model/tool releases would bias a scaling curve, because the intervention and error distribution have changed; settle the magnitude with an interaction analysis comparing worker-count effects before and after each version boundary.

### 5.5 Blinding is incomplete

Even after metadata removal, eight-worker synthesis may be longer, more structured, or stylistically different. A grader may infer the arm. Blinding therefore needs an empirical manipulation check, not a declaration. If count can be predicted from canonicalized artifacts above tolerance, report partial unblinding and use objective outcomes or matched presentation where possible.

### 5.6 Negative controls diagnose paths; they do not certify the system

Byte-identical label controls detect label and pipeline effects, but not a biased primary rubric, correlated hallucination, benchmark leakage, or style-mediated inference. A passing control cannot be translated to “the experiment is unbiased.” This limitation is intrinsic to negative-control methodology ([Penning de Vries and Groenwold, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10515451/)).

### 5.7 Sequential stopping optimizes a declared population, not every future task

An arm dropped for futility in a task stratum can still be best for a rare task. The output is a population policy with uncertainty and scope, not a universal law. If the optional runtime extension in §3.6 is tested, its already-proposed shadow replay should retain randomized withheld-next-batch audits so that policy drift and rare counterexamples remain measurable; this is not a separate runtime mechanism.

That audit rate is **UNVERIFIED**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that some continued exploration is needed to detect drift, based on the nonstationarity above; the exact rate cannot be recommended without costs and change frequency. The settling experiment is a simulation and prospective shadow deployment measuring detection delay and regret at candidate audit rates.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate

The proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate:

* It rides the existing skill/hook/agent-spawn bus.
* It is a protocol, logger, canonicalizer, and analysis gate—not a second orchestration runtime.
* It adds no vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
* It retains raw JSONL records and small protocol files rather than building a service.
* Its runtime result is a lookup table or small conditional rule, not a learned controller.
* If the implementation cannot stay below roughly 200 lines by reusing existing spawn and scoring mechanisms, split off only the offline statistical analysis as an external one-shot notebook/script; do not grow the hook into a daemon.

The mechanism earns its complexity only if the prospective trial shows lower cost/latency or higher quality than the existing worker-count policy on held-out tasks. Otherwise the correct outcome is to remove the gate and retain only the experimental record.

## 7. Rejected alternatives and dead ends

### 7.1 Rejected: spawn until worker agreement is high

Agreement is not correctness. Homogeneous agents can share the same wrong premise, and dense communication can reduce independent evidence. Yang et al. explicitly distinguish beneficial diversity among correct reasoning paths from arbitrary diversity and note that their independence assumptions can fail ([Yang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). Agreement may be a secondary runtime feature, never the sole stopping endpoint.

### 7.2 Rejected: treat each worker as a powered replicate

This is the central pseudoreplication error. It manufactures precision from correlated trajectories and confuses treatment dose with experimental sample size.

### 7.3 Rejected: “stop after two consecutive workers add nothing”

Without a frozen definition of “nothing,” a minimum worthwhile effect, detector validation, and correction for repeated looks, this is optional stopping with a subjective endpoint. It can stop immediately after an unlucky redundant pair even when the next specialized worker would add decisive evidence.

### 7.4 Rejected: online bandit/controller as the first implementation

That would introduce policy-learning complexity before the reward and task strata are validated. It risks becoming a second orchestration runtime and violates the minimal-architecture intent. An offline paired trial plus a static table is the smallest falsifiable intervention.

### 7.5 Rejected: trained critic to judge marginal contribution

This is a previously rejected component under the anti-overfit gate. It also moves the unresolved measurement problem into an opaque learned score. Use objective oracles, blinded adjudication, and explicit controls instead.

### 7.6 Rejected: call known sequential tasks a negative control

The null is not guaranteed; parallel verification can still help a sequential workflow. A false negative-control premise would make valid gains look like detector bias.

### 7.7 Rejected: optimize worker count and topology simultaneously without factorial accounting

If worker count, model diversity, prompt roles, communication topology, and token budget all change together, “the effect of count” is unidentified. Either hold them fixed, define the whole bundle as the treatment, or pre-register a factorial/multi-arm design with enough independent tasks to estimate interactions. The simplest first trial varies count only and runs a separate diversity comparison.

## 8. Minimal acceptance and falsification plan

The proposal should be rejected, not rationalized, if any of the following occurs in the confirmatory replay:

1. Canonicalized graders can predict worker-count arms above the pre-specified tolerance.
2. The byte-identical shuffled-label negative control shows a systematic arm effect.
3. The known-difference positive control is not detected by both intended plateau detectors.
4. The task-level sample is too small to distinguish the minimum worthwhile gain from noise at the maximum information limit.
5. Worker-count rankings reverse across nearby reasonable, pre-declared owner utility weights without a stable Pareto improvement.
6. The policy fails prospectively on held-out tasks or incurs unacceptable false-stop regret in shadow mode.
7. Maintaining the gate requires a daemon, learned controller, or new orchestration runtime.

Outcome interpretation must be disciplined:

* **Pass:** a scoped, versioned worker-count policy is supported for the declared task population.
* **Inconclusive:** retain data, revise the design prospectively, and do not claim a plateau.
* **Fail:** remove or do not enable the gate; the cross-domain mechanism did not produce reliable operational value in this harness.

## 10. Gaps and scope limits

* No local repository, harness configuration, corpus, audit finding, or subject path was read, by instruction. Therefore this unit makes no claim that a particular hook, setting, skill, agent type, or scoring interface currently exists beyond the owner-provided statement that proposals should ride the existing bus.
* The recent agent-scaling sources are research outputs, not universal laws. Google reports controlled evaluation over 180 configurations, and Yang et al. report strong diversity results, but neither establishes performance on this owner’s task distribution ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/); [Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)).
* No numeric power calculation is possible without a minimum worthwhile effect, task-level variance/correlation, arm missingness, number of strata, and endpoint definition. Supplying a worker limit such as “always use four” would be fabricated precision.
* Objective oracles will not exist for every task. Where two mechanically independent and validated quality signals cannot be constructed, the harness may enforce a budget policy but must not claim that additional quality benefit is absent.
* Cost and latency can be measured, but owner preference over quality-cost-latency trade-offs is not supplied. It must be pre-specified or reported as a Pareto frontier.

## Bottom line

Experimental design’s transferable achievement is not a magic formula for the right number of agents. It is a disciplined way to **learn a scoped worker-count policy without optional-stopping bias, grader expectation, pseudoreplication, or false “no benefit” conclusions**. The correct implementation is a small pre-registered replay and audit gate on the existing bus. The task is the experimental unit; worker count is the dose; interim looks happen across tasks; controls validate the measurement path; and runtime stopping remains a policy decision whose counterfactual is unknown unless audited.
