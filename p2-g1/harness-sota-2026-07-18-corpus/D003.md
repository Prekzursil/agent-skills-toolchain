# D003 — HARNESS-BENCH metric selection: which measurements predict usefulness?

## Executive verdict

The benchmark must not optimize a single “harness score.” The defensible ordering is **(1) safety/forbidden-effect gates, (2) task outcome success and reliability, (3) task-conditional quality dimensions such as citation support, and only then (4) a Pareto report of time, tokens, cost, turns, and error burden**. This is a lexicographic scorecard, not a weighted sum.

Among the seven candidates, **task success measured from final state and deterministic receipts is the strongest general predictor of usefulness**. This is **very likely (80-90%)**, because it is closest to the intended construct; τ-bench grades the final database against the goal state, ToolSandbox permits arbitrary trajectories that satisfy milestones and avoid minefields, and Anthropic explicitly distinguishes an agent's claim of completion from the actual environment outcome ([τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045), [ToolSandbox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04682), [Anthropic, “Demystifying evals for AI agents”](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). It is not immune to Goodhart effects: a weak test can be hacked, an incomplete specification can reward the wrong result, and a public fixed suite can be overfit.

**Citation-resolution rate is not a usefulness metric.** It proves that a locator resolves, not that the source supports the claim or that important claims are covered. For research tasks, replace it with three separate measures: locator resolution, citation entailment/precision, and citation coverage/recall. ALCE deliberately separates answer correctness from citation correctness and completeness, and reports that citation metrics correlate with human judgments ([ALCE paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627), [reproducible code](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE)). Citation support plus coverage is **likely (55-80%)** to predict research-output usefulness, based on that task-specific human-correlation evidence; transfer to this owner's heterogeneous work is **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is the blinded 30-task annotation pilot in §8**.

**Owner-correction rate is a high-value incident signal but a poor unattended objective.** A verified correction is close to owner dissatisfaction, yet corrections are sparse, self-selected, exposure-dependent, and often absent when the owner silently abandons a bad result. Anthropic lists user feedback as correlated with product goals but sparse and self-selected; research on implicit feedback demonstrates that observed behavior can be informative while systematically biased ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [Joachims et al., SIGIR 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1076034.1076063), [counterfactual treatment of biased feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04468)). A manually verified correction indicator is **likely (55-80%)** to predict low usefulness when it is observed, but an absence of correction is **unlikely (20-45%)** to establish usefulness, based on the selection/censoring evidence and the local detector's demonstrated false positives.

**Wall-clock, token cost, and turns-to-completion measure efficiency only after success is held fixed.** They are not substitutes for success. Wall-clock and monetary cost are preferable to turns because turns are scaffold-dependent and can be collapsed into one enormous tool call. Their local predictive relationship to owner usefulness is **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is the success-stratified model in §8**. Based on construct distance, it is **likely (55-80%)** that wall-clock and total cost add useful discrimination among equally successful variants, while turns add less; the evidence is METR's separation of task difficulty (human completion time) from AI elapsed time and GSO's reporting of turns and wall-clock as compute-profile axes beside, not instead of, correctness/performance ([METR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499), [METR clarification](https://metr.org/time-horizons/), [GSO benchmark](https://gso-bench.github.io/index.html)).

**Tool-error rate is diagnostic, not an objective.** The corpus proves it is useful for locating reliability defects, but it does not establish that a session with fewer errors is more useful: a strong agent can recover, a weak agent can avoid tools, and a dead integration can disappear from the denominator. Its local predictive validity is **UNVERIFIED — label errors as recovered/unrecovered and fit the §8 model**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that unrecovered, task-attributable errors predict failure, while raw per-call error rate is **unlikely (20-45%)** to add much after final success and task family are known; the evidence is the direct causal proximity of unrecovered failure versus the corpus's path-only aggregate and the outcome-first agent-evaluation literature above.

The central Goodhart defense is not “find a perfect proxy.” Goodhart effects become more important as optimization pressure increases, and include regressional, extremal, causal, and adversarial failures ([Manheim & Garrabrant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Therefore:

1. never pay, rank, or auto-promote on one path metric;
2. make safety and required final state hard gates;
3. keep task-family dimensions separate;
4. compare successful variants on Pareto frontiers;
5. retain a hidden holdout and audit trajectories;
6. periodically validate every metric against blind usefulness judgments.

## 2. Define “usefulness” before testing predictors

Without an external criterion, “which metric predicts usefulness?” is circular: selecting a metric as the target and then reporting that it predicts itself is Goodhart by construction.

For this harness, define two criterion variables:

* **Mechanical usefulness, `Y_mech`**: all critical required outcomes hold in final state; no critical forbidden effect occurred; all mandatory fresh receipts pass. It is binary. Partial milestone completion is reported separately and never upgrades a critical failure.
* **Blind owner-usefulness, `Y_owner`**: a 1–5 rating made from the original request plus final artifact/state diff, with the trajectory, tool count, tokens, duration, harness variant, and candidate metrics hidden. Rubric: 1 unusable/harmful; 2 major redo; 3 useful after material correction; 4 directly useful with minor correction; 5 directly useful and complete. Record “cannot judge” rather than forcing a rating.

Use `Y_mech` for coding, state-changing, and contract-bearing tasks. Use `Y_owner` to validate construct coverage and for tasks whose quality is not fully mechanically decidable. Neither should be collapsed with cost.

This two-criterion design is **very likely (80-90%)** to be more robust than a self-reported completion flag, because τ-bench, ToolSandbox, and Anthropic independently privilege state/outcome over claimed completion or a fixed trajectory, while blind judgment prevents knowledge of efficiency metrics from contaminating perceived utility. Whether the five-point owner rubric is reliable for this owner is **UNVERIFIED — duplicate-blind-score 10 artifacts after 48 hours and calculate weighted agreement; revise ambiguous anchors before using it as a gate**.

## 3. Candidate-by-candidate selection

### 3.1 Task success — primary outcome, but only with validated oracles

**Role:** primary metric and promotion gate.

**Definition:**

```text
success(task, trial) =
    all(critical required-state assertions)
    AND all(mandatory fresh receipts)
    AND count(critical forbidden effects) == 0
```

Also report `required_assertions_passed / required_assertions_total`, each failed assertion, and every forbidden effect. Do not average a catastrophic forbidden effect away with many easy checks.

**Robustness:** highest of the candidates when the oracle measures owner-relevant state. It tolerates alternate valid paths. τ-bench's final database comparison and ToolSandbox's dynamic milestones over arbitrary trajectories are direct prior art ([τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045), [ToolSandbox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04682)). Reliability must be separate: report pass@1 and `pass^k` for all-k consistency, as τ-bench introduced `pass^k` specifically because one lucky success does not show dependable agent behavior.

**Goodhart attacks:** edit the tests; hard-code expected output; satisfy visible assertions while violating an omitted requirement; exploit evaluator/network state; report success without effect; overfit public tasks.

**Controls:** immutable grader copy outside the agent-writable workspace; hidden tests; manifest and grader hashes; randomized canaries; required and forbidden state assertions; oracle solution must pass; no-op and known-bad solutions must fail; at least one alternative valid solution must pass; inspect traces for suspicious shortcuts. Anthropic reports concrete cases where grading bugs moved a score from 42% to 95% and recommends graders resistant to bypasses ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

**Proposal — outcome grader:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. Add a benchmark-side JSON manifest reader and scorer; do not modify production terminal schema in the pilot. This passes the anti-overfit gate because it is a thin reader/scorer over existing events, receipts, and files, not a new orchestrator or judge runtime.

### 3.2 Citation-resolution rate — retain only as an instrumentation check

**Role:** low-level research diagnostic, never headline quality.

**Definition:** `resolved locators / emitted locators`, where resolved means an allowed HTTP status or an exact local evidence pointer that exists. Freeze redirect policy and access time.

**Why weak:** a response can cite ten live but irrelevant homepages; it can improve resolution by deleting difficult citations; private/paywalled sources may be sound yet unavailable to the checker; a resolved URL can change content. Resolution is therefore **very likely (80-90%)** to be Goodhart-vulnerable as a target, based on the obvious gap between locator availability and ALCE's separately measured citation correctness/completeness.

For research tasks, report:

```text
resolution = resolvable citation locators / citation locators
support_precision = claims supported by attached cited evidence / cited factual claims
support_coverage = importance-weighted factual claims with support / importance-weighted factual claims
contradiction_count = claims contradicted by cited evidence
```

Atomic claim extraction and support judgment introduce their own model/human error. **UNVERIFIED — the experiment is to double-label 100 claims, measure adjudicated agreement for claim boundaries and support, and prohibit auto-gating until the lower confidence bound meets the predeclared threshold.**

**Proposal — research grader [UNVERIFIED premise: local claim-label reliability]:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. Store claim/source/evidence hashes in benchmark JSONL and use deterministic locator checks plus bounded human review for the first week. Do not deploy a trained critic, vector DB, knowledge graph, or citation service.

### 3.3 Owner-correction rate — sentinel, not reward

**Role:** post-deployment negative signal and case generator.

Count only a human-verified correction that identifies an error, missing required work, undesired stop, or instruction violation attributable to the preceding agent output. Exclude new scope, preference changes, monitor ticks, teammate messages, tool output, hook-authored prompts, and duplicate continuation messages.

Report at task level:

```text
any_verified_correction = 0|1
material_corrections = count
correction_latency = time from agent handoff to first verified correction
eligible_followup_observed = 0|1
```

Never treat `no correction` as positive unless an eligible follow-up/acceptance opportunity was observed. Never divide raw corrections by turns: talkative workflows create more exposure. Keep correction severity and attribution.

**Goodhart attacks:** stop before owner feedback; suppress or discourage corrections; ask the owner to phrase feedback as new scope; split a task/session; flood with confirmation questions; optimize politeness rather than correctness.

**Proposal — correction adjudication:** **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** for the pilot: add verified labels to the benchmark dataset, not the live detector. The current detector cannot be promoted because its 41/41 smoke sample was false positive (`corpus.json:10371-10375`). A future classifier is not justified until a labeled set exists.

### 3.4 Tool-error rate — causal diagnostic with recovery labels

**Role:** root-cause and reliability diagnostic.

Raw rate is `failed tool calls / attempted tool calls`, stratified by tool, error fingerprint, task family, and harness variant. Add:

```text
unrecovered_error_rate = errors followed by task failure attributable to that error / calls
recovery_rate = errors followed by successful equivalent operation / errors
error_burden = failed calls per successful task
retry_loop_count = repeated same fingerprint and normalized input within a fixed window
```

Report zero-call tools explicitly. A dead tool removed from routing must not make its prior errors vanish from a before/after comparison; use intent/opportunity denominators where the task manifest requires that capability.

**Goodhart attacks:** avoid capable but flaky tools; relabel failure as successful empty output; retry until the denominator looks favorable; batch many actions into one call; route through an uninstrumented shell; choose only trivial tasks.

The tool metric's principal value is drill-down: StructuredOutput at 22.9% and Glob at 13.32% point to concrete repairs (`corpus.json:79-110`). It must not outrank a successful outcome recovered through a fallback.

**Proposal — recovery attribution:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. Derive recovery candidates in the offline correlator from tool ID, normalized error fingerprint, subsequent equivalent effect, and final result; leave uncertain attribution null for manual review.

### 3.5 Turns-to-completion — weakest efficiency measure

**Role:** descriptive trace-shape metric only.

Define one turn as one assistant model response, not a tool call or hook event. Record model responses, tool calls, owner messages, and subagent turns separately. `turns_to_success` is defined only for successful tasks; failures are right-censored at the budget, not assigned an artificially favorable small number.

**Goodhart attacks:** one giant prompt/response; tool batching; hidden subagent turns; premature answer; moving work into a long-running tool; provider-specific turn accounting. Cross-harness comparisons are especially fragile because tool-result continuation semantics differ.

Turns are **very likely (80-90%)** less robust than wall-clock or tokens as a targeted efficiency measure, based on those mechanically available batching/reclassification strategies and GSO's treatment of turns as one compute-profile field rather than its correctness objective ([GSO](https://gso-bench.github.io/index.html)).

**Proposal — turn counter:** **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**. Compute from trial records with a versioned definition; no hook is needed.

### 3.6 Wall-clock — useful conditional service metric

**Role:** efficiency and user-experience axis among equivalent outcomes.

Record both elapsed time (`handoff_ts - start_ts`) and active agent time (model/tool spans excluding explicit owner waits). Report median, p90, p95, and timeout/censoring count by task family. Never silently drop failed or timed-out trials. For successful runs, report time-to-success; for all runs, use a survival curve or timeout-aware distribution rather than averaging only finishers.

**Goodhart attacks:** return early/incomplete; launch background work after handoff; exclude queue/tool time; parallelize into rate-limit or correctness failures; stop timers before verification; cache task-specific state.

The current `elapsed_ms` is hook execution latency, not task wall-clock (`dispatcher.py:485-503`). Conflating the two would be a detector error.

**Proposal — trial timer:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. The eval wrapper stamps monotonic start/end and span categories. No daemon or observability SaaS is warranted.

### 3.7 Token cost — useful conditional resource metric

**Role:** resource axis among equivalent outcomes.

Record input, output, cached-input, reasoning (when exposed), and subagent tokens separately by provider/model. Record total tokens and price using a versioned rate card; preserve raw token counts so historical costs can be recomputed. Count failed trials and retries in deployment-policy cost.

Report:

```text
tokens_per_success = total tokens across all attempts under the declared policy / successful tasks
cost_per_success = total priced cost across all attempts / successful tasks
```

If there are zero successes, report undefined plus total spend, never zero or infinity hidden in an aggregate.

**Goodhart attacks:** omit subagent/tool-model tokens; exploit cache accounting; choose a cheaper but less useful model; truncate verification; shift compute to unmetered tools; change price assumptions.

**Proposal — native token/cost projection [UNVERIFIED premise: provider usage records reach the wrapper]: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Capture one Claude and one Codex trial and inspect native usage fields before implementation. If both expose complete usage, project those fields with a versioned rate card.

**Proposal — token/cost fallback [UNVERIFIED premise: native usage fields are absent]: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Capture provider-native usage responses at the existing wrapper boundary, not with an estimated generic text tokenizer. Do not implement this branch unless the two-provider probe establishes the premise.

## 5. Aggregation policy: no scalar leaderboard

Use the following comparison algorithm:

1. Reject a variant with any new critical forbidden effect or safety regression.
2. Compare task success by task family and `pass^k`; do not pool away a harmed family.
3. For research tasks, require citation support/coverage floors independent of answer correctness.
4. Among variants not materially worse on success/reliability, plot wall-clock, total token cost, and cost-per-success Pareto frontiers.
5. Use turns and tool errors only to explain the frontier or diagnose regressions.
6. Review verified owner corrections as cases, not points in a reward sum.

This policy is **very likely (80-90%)** more Goodhart-resistant than a weighted composite because a weighted sum permits cheap gains in tokens or turns to compensate for correctness/safety loss, while the Goodhart taxonomy predicts proxy breakdown under optimization pressure ([Manheim & Garrabrant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). NIST likewise frames measurement as a mix of quantitative and qualitative methods selected for mapped risks rather than one universal score ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1), [MEASURE function](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)). The exact non-inferiority margins remain **UNVERIFIED — set them from pilot variance before the first harness comparison, then freeze them in the manifest**.

Never optimize the diagnostic dashboard directly. Promotion authority stays with required state, forbidden state, reliability, and predeclared task-specific quality floors.

## 7. One-week implementation plan

### Day 1 — freeze constructs and tasks

* Select 30 tasks: 10 coding/state-changing, 8 research, 4 operations/monitoring, 4 handoff/compaction, and 4 safety/benign-pair tasks. Start with real failure shapes but rewrite secrets and mutable dependencies into isolated fixtures.
* For every task, author required outcomes, forbidden effects, timeout, applicable quality dimensions, and an evidence pointer.
* Reserve 20 tasks for development/diagnosis and 10 as a blinded holdout. Do not expose holdout assertions to the agent.

Thirty tasks is a pilot, not a stable universal ranking. Anthropic recommends starting with roughly 20–50 tasks from real failures, reading transcripts, and expanding over time ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). The pilot is **likely (55-80%)** to expose large metric failures and obvious inversions, but **unlikely (20-45%)** to estimate small predictive differences precisely, based on its limited sample and heterogeneous task families.

### Day 2 — validate every oracle

* Oracle solution passes.
* No-op and known-bad variants fail.
* One alternate valid trajectory passes.
* Grader reruns produce byte-identical grades.
* Grader and task hashes are recorded.
* Citation subset receives double annotation.

### Day 3 — build the correlator

**Proposal — `bench_score.py`: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Read manifests plus existing isolated terminal/ledger/trace outputs; emit the JSONL contract and a Markdown/CSV scorecard. Standard-library Python is **almost certain (90-99%)** to be sufficient because the operation is bounded JSON/JSONL parsing, validation, aggregation, and Markdown/CSV emission over the directly observed local formats. Do not import a benchmark platform, telemetry SaaS, durable workflow engine, vector store, trained critic, or second orchestrator.

### Day 4 — baseline repetitions

Run each deterministic harness trace three times on fresh temporary roots. For model-in-loop tasks, pin provider/model/config/tool availability/budget and run at least three trials where budget permits. Report trial counts; do not interpret 3/3 as production reliability. Use `pass^3` for must-be-reliable cases and pass@k only when the declared deployment policy actually retries.

### Day 5 — blind usefulness labels and adversarial audit

Present only original request plus artifact/final-state diff to the owner/adjudicator in randomized order. Hide path metrics. Separately inspect traces for grader hacking, unrecorded subprocess work, background completion, and omitted usage. Add every discovered exploit as a paired regression test, but keep the original holdout score frozen.

### Days 6–7 — fit, report, freeze

Run §8 analyses, publish per-task rows and uncertainty, set provisional non-inferiority margins from baseline dispersion, and freeze schema/task/grader/rate-card hashes. A harness change is eligible only if it preserves gates and improves either success/reliability or the success-conditioned Pareto frontier.

## 8. The experiment that answers “predicts usefulness” locally

### 8.1 Pre-register hypotheses

Before seeing labels:

* H1: final-state task success has the strongest association with `Y_owner`.
* H2: research citation support and coverage add information beyond locator resolution.
* H3: verified correction predicts low `Y_owner`, but missing correction does not predict high `Y_owner` without observed follow-up.
* H4: unrecovered attributable errors outperform raw tool-error rate.
* H5: among mechanically successful tasks, wall-clock and cost-per-success predict owner preference more than turns.

These are hypotheses, not conclusions. Every local predictive claim is **UNVERIFIED until this experiment runs**.

### 8.2 Analysis

For each metric:

1. Report its distribution and missingness by task family and variant.
2. Against binary `Y_mech`, report AUROC and precision/recall where meaningful; task success is identical to `Y_mech` and therefore excluded from this tautological analysis.
3. Against ordinal `Y_owner`, report Spearman rank association and the probability that the metric orders a randomly selected more-useful/less-useful pair correctly.
4. Bootstrap confidence intervals by resampling **tasks**, retaining all repeated trials within a task; do not resample trials as if independent.
5. Run leave-one-task-family-out validation. A metric that works only within one family is labeled task-specific.
6. For efficiency metrics, restrict the primary comparison to `Y_mech=1` and separately show all-task spend/time including failures and censoring.
7. Compare raw tool-error rate with unrecovered-error count in the same held-out tasks.
8. For corrections, include an `eligible_followup_observed` indicator and do not impute no correction as approval.

Do not fit a seven-weight composite on 30 tasks. That would overfit the pilot and manufacture a seemingly predictive score. If the dataset later exceeds 100 diverse labeled tasks, a regularized model evaluated on a chronologically later holdout may test incremental prediction; the usefulness of that future model is **UNVERIFIED — settle it only with a preregistered temporal holdout**.

### 8.3 Causal harness comparison

Prediction does not establish that optimizing a metric improves usefulness; causal Goodhart explicitly covers proxies correlated with a goal for non-causal reasons ([Manheim & Garrabrant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). For a candidate harness change, randomize baseline versus candidate across the same tasks, pin model/config/budget, use fresh roots, and grade blind. Compare paired changes in success and owner usefulness first, then paired resource deltas among successes. This paired A/B is the promotion experiment; the observational corpus is for defect discovery and task sampling.

## 9. Decision rules for each candidate

| Metric | Keep? | May gate promotion? | May optimize directly? | Minimum anti-gaming condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final-state task success | Yes | Yes | Yes, with holdout | Validated immutable outcome oracle + forbidden effects |
| Reliability (`pass^k`) | Yes | Yes for must-work tasks | Yes | Fresh roots, repeated trials, raw counts |
| Citation resolution | Yes, diagnostic | No | No | Fixed resolver policy and timestamps |
| Citation support precision | Yes on research | Yes, task-specific floor | Only with coverage and correctness | Atomic claims, evidence, calibrated adjudication |

## 10. Alternatives considered and rejected

### One weighted “usefulness score”

Rejected. Weights encode hidden exchange rates such as “how many saved tokens compensate for one incorrect task,” invite proxy gaming, and hide harmed task families. A lexicographic gate plus Pareto frontier is simpler and preserves the actual trade-offs.

### Optimize owner corrections directly

Rejected. The local extractor is invalid at current precision (`corpus.json:10371-10375`), and even perfect classification observes only expressed feedback. It would incentivize reduced feedback exposure.

### Use only task success

Rejected as incomplete. Success needs repeated-trial reliability, forbidden effects, task-family dimensions, and cost/time feasibility. A system that succeeds once at extreme cost or violates policy is not deployment-useful.

### Use turns as a universal efficiency currency

Rejected. Turn boundaries are scaffold-controlled and readily batched. Tokens, wall-clock, and monetary cost remain imperfect but correspond more closely to consumed resources.

### Add an LLM judge as the central grader

Rejected for the one-week core. Deterministic outcome receipts are available for many tasks, and a central judge adds correlated error and another optimization target. Human/blind judgment is used only to validate construct coverage; model grading can later assist non-mechanical dimensions only after calibration. Anthropic recommends code-based graders where possible and calibrating model judges against human experts ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

### Install a benchmark platform, observability SaaS, vector database, knowledge graph, or durable execution runtime

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. The existing normalized events, terminal JSONL, ledger, pytest fixtures, and shadow bus provide the needed transport. The proposal is one small offline correlator plus manifests; no previously rejected component is reintroduced.

## 13. Minimal architecture and final recommendation

**Proposal — adopt the lexicographic outcome/reliability/quality/Pareto scorecard: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** The scorecard is manifest and reporting policy.

**Proposal — implement the isolated correlator and oracle runner: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** It consumes current normalized events, terminal records, receipts, and task manifests; it writes benchmark JSONL/Markdown outside live state.

**Proposal — keep production lifecycle instrumentation unchanged during the pilot: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it).** Use the native lifecycle events directly verified in `registration.py:17-53` with the existing dispatcher/ledger bus. Complete token usage is outside this proposal and remains governed by the two mutually exclusive UNVERIFIED probes/proposals in §3.7.

No proposal is **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)**. No second orchestration runtime, vector-DB/KG memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is introduced. The design meets the anti-overfit gate: roughly one small scorer, versioned JSON manifests, validated graders, and an existing pytest/hook/ledger transport.

The benchmark's job is not to make every number go down. Its job is to determine whether a harness change produces more correct, safe, reliably useful outcomes, and then show the resource trade-off without allowing the cheap-to-game path metrics to redefine success.
