## Executive conclusion

The analogy transfers, but only in a narrow and useful form.

The best borrow is **formal shift turnover backed by contemporaneous operating logs and an independent status walkdown**. In nuclear operations, responsibility is not transferred merely because the next operator can read the previous operator's narrative. The oncoming operator reviews durable records, receives a briefing, checks important plant state directly, resolves abnormal conditions, and explicitly assumes the watch. An NRC enforcement example makes the purpose concrete: a deficient turnover failed to convey overridden containment-spray valve controls, and the oncoming shift did not detect that the safety system was inoperable ([NRC, Fort Calhoun EA-97-432](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/enforcement/actions/reactors/ea97432)).

The concrete harness translation is a **recovery capsule plus takeover protocol**:

1. append a compact intent/result journal while work proceeds;
2. fence the dead worker with an expiring lease before permitting a successor to act;
3. have the successor reconstruct live state independently from the filesystem, VCS, running processes, test outputs, and external-operation receipts;
4. compare reconstructed state with the journal and task contract;
5. resolve discrepancies conservatively; and
6. record an explicit assumption of responsibility before continuing.

This is **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** for a minimal local implementation, provided the existing bus exposes pre/post tool events and session-start/resume events. That availability is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is to enumerate the bus's documented events and run a probe hook that records one known-present pre-tool call, post-tool result, session start, and forced process death. If those event points are unavailable, the exact size classification must be revised rather than silently introducing a daemon.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the close structural match between loss of operator context at shift change and loss of agent context at process death, that this pattern will reduce unsafe duplicate actions and false assumptions about completed work. The confidence is not higher because a dead agent cannot give the verbal half of turnover and because software side effects can span systems that lack queryable receipts. The settling harness experiment is a fault-injection matrix described below, measuring correct recovery against a no-protocol baseline.

The generic **two-person rule** is not the right primary borrow. In its nuclear-material-security usage it ensures two authorized people are physically present so unauthorized acts can be detected; it does not necessarily produce two independent technical judgments ([DOE Integrated Appraisal Guide, “Two-Person Rule”](https://www.energy.gov/ea/articles/integrated-appraisal-guide-jan-2019)). The relevant correctness mechanism is **independent verification**, where the second checker obtains the answer without being contaminated by the first. Applying two agents to every action would add cost, correlated error, and ceremony. Use independent verification only at recovery boundaries and before high-consequence, non-idempotent re-execution.

STAR self-checking and defence in depth also transfer, but as supporting parts. STAR becomes the successor's local recovery loop; defence in depth becomes several small, diverse recovery barriers. Neither substitutes for a recoverable state record.

## 1. What nuclear operations actually solved

### 1.1 Formal turnover: transfer responsibility, not merely information

DOE describes operations shift turnover as the transfer of duties and responsibilities between facility positions. Its self-study module says thorough turnovers are crucial, give incoming personnel an accurate picture of current facility status, review past and scheduled operations, and support safe, efficient, continuous operation. It also describes operating logs as understandable accounts of equipment-status changes and unusual readings, valuable for reconstructing event precursors and progress and for transferring experience between shifts ([DOE O 422.1 Conduct of Operations self-study module, pp. 23–24 in the surfaced text](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2013/06/f1/O-422-1_ssm.pdf)).

The mechanism is multi-channel:

- **durable narrative state:** logs record changes, unusual conditions, attempts, and explanations;
- **structured turnover content:** abnormal alignments, major-component status, surveillance or tests in progress, and planned evolutions are enumerated;
- **overlap:** incoming personnel have time to review records and discuss them with outgoing personnel;
- **direct observation:** the oncoming operator checks the actual control board or equipment rather than trusting only the narrative;
- **explicit custody:** responsibility changes only when the oncoming person understands status and formally assumes it.

The DOE Conduct of Operations material says turnover procedures provide for the oncoming person to formally state assumption of responsibility and make a narrative log entry once turnover items are complete and understood ([DOE, Conduct of Operations](https://www.energy.gov/documents/conduct-operations)). A 2022 DOE independent assessment found an implemented process with defined key positions, turnover checklists, time for logbook review and discussion, recorded transition of in-progress activities, and signatures; the assessment also found two signature discrepancies through audit, illustrating that even a defined protocol needs verification ([DOE, Independent Assessment of Conduct of Operations at SRS Tritium Facilities, September 2022](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/IA%20of%20ConOps%20at%20SRS%20Tritium%20Facilities%20-%20September%202022.pdf)).

The NRC's Fort Calhoun enforcement record is an especially useful negative case. The plant procedure required the incoming shift to familiarize itself with assigned areas, the outgoing person to brief abnormal conditions and inoperable equipment, and each incoming operator to personally verify important operating parameters. The actual turnover failed to identify overridden containment-spray valve controls, leaving the system inoperable ([NRC, EA-97-432](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/enforcement/actions/reactors/ea97432)). This supports a key design inference: a handoff document alone is not enough; direct state verification is a separate barrier.

### 1.2 Operating logs: a reconstruction instrument, not a diary

In the DOE model, logs record an understandable account of state changes and information from sources beyond instruments. They allow unusual-event precursors and progress to be reconstructed, carry attempted solutions forward, and impose accountability on operating decisions ([DOE O 422.1 self-study module](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2013/06/f1/O-422-1_ssm.pdf)). The WIPP safety-analysis material similarly describes logbooks at key shift positions and says turnover reports abnormal lineups, major-component status, and planned or in-progress surveillance and evolutions to the incoming supervisor ([DOE/WIPP-03-3174, Chapter 8](https://www.wipp.energy.gov/library/Information_Repository_A/Supplemental_Information/DOE%202000%20RH%20PSAR/RHCHPT8.pdf)).

The important property is not verbosity. It is the ability to answer, after interruption:

- What was intended?
- What was observed before action?
- What action was actually attempted?
- What evidence says it completed?
- What state changed?
- What remains abnormal, provisional, or unverified?
- What must the successor not repeat blindly?

That is exactly the information missing when a worker dies between “request sent” and “response recorded.” The nuclear log analogy therefore transfers more strongly to an append-only intent/result ledger than to periodic prose summaries.

### 1.3 Independent verification versus the two-person rule

These mechanisms must not be conflated.

DOE's Integrated Appraisal Guide defines the two-person rule for nuclear materials as two authorized people positioned to observe one another or the protected items and positively detect unauthorized actions or access. Example applications include vault entry, transfer across material-balance areas, and applying or removing tamper-indicating devices ([DOE, Integrated Appraisal Guide](https://www.energy.gov/ea/articles/integrated-appraisal-guide-jan-2019)). Its primary objective is deterrence/detection of malicious or unauthorized behavior.

Independent verification is a technical-error barrier. A DOE assessment describes it as two people performing verifications **sequentially and independently**, while distinguishing concurrent dual verification where true independence is not possible ([DOE, SRS Tritium Conduct of Operations assessment](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/IA%20of%20ConOps%20at%20SRS%20Tritium%20Facilities%20-%20September%202022.pdf)). A historical Defense Nuclear Facilities Safety Board observation explains why separation matters: operators verbally relayed a serial number to the person in charge before signing, which could compromise the independence of the verification ([DNFSB observation, 1995](https://ehss.energy.gov/deprep/1995-2/tr95y02c.pdf)).

For recovery, the valuable rule is therefore:

> The successor must derive consequential state from primary evidence before reading the predecessor's conclusion, then compare the two.

This ordering reduces anchoring. Two agents reading the same predecessor summary and agreeing are one signal, not two.

### 1.4 Conservative decision-making: uncertainty changes the allowed action

The NRC's safety-culture material defines conservative decision-making as using conservative assumptions and rejecting options unless safety is demonstrated, rather than accepting them until someone proves them unsafe. It also calls for systematic decisions under uncertain or unexpected conditions, contingencies, interdisciplinary review for risk-significant decisions, communication, and later effectiveness review ([NRC, Safety Culture Components](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/enforcement/sc-componets011306)). The NRC human-factors definitions classify failure to stop work or establish controls under unfavorable or uncertain conditions as non-conservative decision-making ([NRC, Human Factors Definition List](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)).

This does **not** translate to “do nothing whenever uncertain.” It translates to a graded action policy:

- continue read-only reconstruction under uncertainty;
- allow idempotent, locally reversible actions after evidence checks;
- require stronger proof before repeating an externally visible or destructive action;
- enter a safe hold and ask the owner only when uncertainty affects an irreversible boundary.

This preserves useful recovery work while moving the burden of proof to the risky action.

### 1.5 Defence in depth: independent, diverse layers against common-cause failure

The IAEA's Fukushima lesson states that flooding simultaneously challenged the first three protective levels and caused common-cause failures. It says defence-in-depth layers should have adequate independence, redundancy, diversity, and protection so a single internal or external hazard does not challenge them simultaneously ([IAEA, Fukushima — Safety Assessment](https://gnssn.iaea.org/FukushimaLessonsLearned/Lists/Fukushima/DispForm.aspx?ContentTypeId=0x01006A6D303BCE89A14FA6083DD8D969FFE0&ID=185)).

For agent recovery, the nontrivial lesson is not “make five backups.” Five artifacts written by the dying process to the same disk in the same format have a common-mode failure. Useful layers fail differently: an append-only local intent record, independently observed filesystem/VCS state, an external service receipt or idempotency key, and an owner escalation boundary.

### 1.6 STAR: a local cognitive forcing function

The NRC defines inadequate self-checking as failing to self-check before, during, or after a task and gives “Stop, Think, Act, Review” as the example ([NRC, Human Factors Definition List](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). In its own field, STAR inserts a deliberate pause around an action: stop distractions, think about expected state and consequences, act deliberately, and review the observed result against expectation.

STAR is useful at recovery because interruption creates a powerful impulse to continue from the last visible sentence. It is not durable state, does not establish ownership, and cannot tell whether an external side effect committed before death. It is a wrapper around the takeover decision, not the recovery mechanism itself.

## 2. Translation: the recovery capsule and formal takeover

### 2.1 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Minimal recovery capsule**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

“UNVERIFIED” qualifies the premise that the present harness lacks an equivalent capsule and exposes all necessary lifecycle events. The settling experiment is: inspect documented hook events, prove the detector with a known-present hook, kill a worker at each fault point in the matrix below, and compare emitted records with the required schema.

Write one compact, append-only JSONL record per consequential transition. The capsule should live in the task/worktree scope, not only inside the process that may die. Minimum record types:

```text
TASK       task_id, owner intent digest, workspace identity, base revision
INTENT     action_id, planned tool/action, target, risk class, precondition digest
RESULT     action_id, exit/response status, receipt/idempotency key, result digest
OBSERVE    source, observed state digest, timestamp
DECISION   chosen branch, rejected alternatives, evidence references, uncertainty
PENDING    exact next safe action, unresolved questions, prohibited blind repeats
ASSUME     predecessor lease, successor identity, discrepancies resolved, time
COMPLETE   acceptance evidence, remaining side effects, owner-visible outcome
```

The journal records references and hashes, not entire prompts, command outputs, or secrets. Large evidence remains in native artifacts such as tool transcripts, VCS, and test reports. The capsule's job is to link them and distinguish intent from confirmed result.

Write `INTENT` **before** dispatching a consequential call and `RESULT` only after receiving evidence. A surviving `INTENT` without a `RESULT` is an “uncertain commit,” not permission to retry. This one distinction addresses the hardest recovery ambiguity: the action may have succeeded remotely even though the worker died before recording success.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on write-ahead logging's structural match to the request/commit ambiguity and nuclear logs' demonstrated reconstruction role, that intent/result separation will prevent some duplicate side effects. This is still **UNVERIFIED** for the harness; settle it by killing the worker after request transmission but before response recording for an idempotent mock service and a non-idempotent mock service, then measuring duplicates.

### 2.2 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Lease fencing before takeover**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** This can share the capsule implementation and line budget rather than becoming a daemon.

“UNVERIFIED” qualifies both current absence and filesystem-lock semantics across the owner's machines. The settling experiment is a two-process race: pause the original worker, let its lease expire, start a successor, then release the original and verify that only the successor can append actionable records or dispatch mutating tools.

A worker owns a short-lived lease containing task ID, worker ID, epoch, and expiry. Recovery increments the epoch atomically. Every mutating dispatch checks the current epoch. A revived “zombie” worker holding the old epoch can inspect but cannot mutate.

This is the software counterpart of explicit watch custody. Without fencing, “recovery” can create two live operators. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on standard split-brain failure structure but without evidence about this harness's actual concurrency primitives, that a file-backed epoch is sufficient for same-filesystem session death. It is **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on the lack of guaranteed shared atomic storage across machine loss, that the same simple file lease is sufficient for arbitrary multi-machine recovery. The multi-machine experiment is to sever one node from shared storage, recover on another, restore connectivity, and attempt a stale mutation.

If cross-machine fencing cannot be achieved through an already-existing authoritative store, this unit does **not** recommend building a new coordination service. Escalate the affected action class to owner confirmation instead.

### 2.3 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Independent recovery walkdown**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** for a scripted evidence collector plus a resume skill. If the harness already has a native resume lifecycle, enabling it would instead be **NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**, but that premise is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with documented capability inspection and the fault-injection test, not a name-based configuration search.

“UNVERIFIED” qualifies whether current recovery independently reconstructs state. The settling experiment is to plant a deliberately false `PENDING` statement while leaving contradictory VCS and test state, then evaluate whether the successor detects and blocks on the discrepancy.

The successor follows this sequence:

1. **Stop.** Acquire the next lease epoch. Do not execute the predecessor's proposed command.
2. **Independent think/walkdown.** Before reading the predecessor's final conclusion, collect primary state: workspace identity, revision, `status`/diff, untracked files, relevant process status, lock files, test/build artifacts, and queryable external receipts.
3. **Read turnover.** Read the task contract, capsule, and transcript references.
4. **Compare.** Classify each action as `not started`, `confirmed complete`, `confirmed failed`, or `uncertain commit`. Never collapse the last category into failure.
5. **Reconcile.** For each discrepancy, prefer primary state; preserve unexplained user changes; query remote state by action ID; use compensating action only when explicitly authorized.
6. **Act.** Run the smallest read-only or reversible probe that discriminates competing states. Resume at the first unproven postcondition, not at the last narrated step.
7. **Review.** Compare observed outcome with the predicted postcondition and append the result.
8. **Assume.** Append the `ASSUME` record only after discrepancies and uncertain commits are resolved or explicitly isolated.

This ordering borrows both formal turnover and independent verification. The predecessor's journal is one signal; live state is the mechanically independent second signal. Reading the conclusion only after the walkdown reduces anchoring.

### 2.4 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Graded independent verification at irreversible boundaries**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** if the harness already supports risk-class routing to a review agent or owner approval. That routing premise is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by configuring a harmless canary action in each risk class and observing which review/approval path actually fires. If no routing exists, a small pre-tool policy hook is **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

Do not apply “two people” everywhere. Require an independent verifier only when all three are true:

- recovery status is `uncertain commit` or state evidence conflicts;
- the proposed action is externally visible, destructive, privileged, costly, or non-idempotent; and
- no authoritative receipt, dry run, or reversible probe resolves the uncertainty.

The verifier receives the task contract, primary evidence, and proposed action, but not the predecessor's recommendation until it has stated its own classification. Agreement is meaningful only when derived independently. For genuinely consequential external actions, the second person may need to be the owner rather than another model sharing the same blind spots.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the anchoring risk shown by the DNFSB serial-number example and on correlated model context, that blind-first verification will catch more recovery errors than two agents jointly reading the same summary. This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a blinded A/B test containing seeded misleading summaries and contradictory primary evidence, scoring correct state classification and unnecessary escalations.

### 2.5 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Conservative recovery action matrix**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** for the policy table; **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** only if enforcement needs a pre-tool hook.

“UNVERIFIED” qualifies whether the current harness has risk metadata and pre-tool enforcement. The settling experiment is a canary suite that attempts one action in every cell and verifies allow, probe, review, or stop behavior.

| Recovery evidence | Local/read-only | Local/reversible | External/idempotent | External non-idempotent or destructive |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Confirmed not started | allow | allow with normal checks | allow with stable action ID | require normal explicit authorization |
| Confirmed complete | inspect only | do not repeat by default | query receipt; do not repeat | stop; repeating is a defect |
| Confirmed failed, no side effect | allow probe | retry after precondition check | retry same idempotency key | independent review/owner approval |
| Uncertain commit | allow reconstruction | probe/compare first | query by stable key, then reconcile | safe hold; independent review or owner decision |
| Evidence conflict | allow reconstruction | preserve state; no overwrite | query authoritative remote state | safe hold and escalate |

The key conservative rule is: uncertainty blocks only the hazardous boundary, not evidence gathering. This is intended to avoid turning “conservative” into globally stalled work.

### 2.6 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Small, diverse recovery layers**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** as one integrated mechanism, not separate services.

“UNVERIFIED” qualifies existing-layer coverage and independence. The settling experiment is to disable or corrupt one layer at a time and demonstrate that a different layer detects the injected fault; then destroy the shared failure domain to reveal common-mode gaps.

Use five compact layers:

1. **Prevent ambiguity:** stable task/action IDs, atomic local writes, idempotency keys where supported.
2. **Detect interruption:** expired lease plus unmatched `INTENT` record.
3. **Reconstruct independently:** VCS/filesystem/process/test walkdown.
4. **Reconcile:** remote receipt queries, postcondition checks, and narrowly scoped compensation.
5. **Contain:** safe hold and owner escalation at an unresolved irreversible boundary.

The layers are intentionally diverse. Capsule and transcript can share a disk failure, so VCS state is not independent if it lives only on that disk. A remote receipt may survive machine loss but not an unqueryable API. Owner review is independent of process state but is slow and can inherit a misleading summary. The design should label these dependencies rather than count artifacts.

### 3.1 Duplicate remote side effect after response loss — **UNVERIFIED current gap**

Fault: a worker sends an issue creation, message, purchase-like action, or deployment request; the service commits it; the worker dies before recording the response. A naïve successor sees no success result and repeats it.

Catch: unmatched `INTENT` becomes `uncertain commit`; stable action ID and receipt lookup precede retry; non-idempotent repetition enters safe hold. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the protocol directly representing the ambiguous interval, that this catches the injected mock-service case. Whether it catches real services is **likely (55-80%)**, because some services offer neither idempotency keys nor queryable correlation IDs; settle per connector with contract tests.

### 3.2 False completion from a stale narrative — **UNVERIFIED current gap**

Fault: the last summary says a file was written or tests passed, but death occurred before the write completed or after later changes invalidated the test.

Catch: the independent walkdown checks file/VCS state and test evidence before reading/accepting the narrative. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on direct comparison with primary local state, that seeded local contradictions are detected. This confidence drops for opaque external state, which requires its own receipt.

### 3.3 Blind replay of a destructive or context-sensitive command — **UNVERIFIED current gap**

Fault: the successor copies the predecessor's “next command” after workspace revision, target branch, credentials, or remote state changed.

Catch: precondition digests, workspace identity, current revision, and risk matrix force revalidation. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on coverage of explicit preconditions but limited by unmodeled environmental dependencies, that the mechanism blocks the seeded cases. Settle with mutations to branch, worktree, environment, and remote target between death and resume.

### 3.4 Split-brain continuation by a revived worker — **UNVERIFIED current gap**

Fault: machine connectivity returns after another worker has assumed the task; both continue and overwrite or duplicate work.

Catch: epoch fencing rejects mutations from the stale lease. It is **likely (55-80%)** for workers sharing an atomic filesystem and **unlikely (20-45%)** for partitioned machines without an existing authoritative coordinator. ### 3.5 Lost provisional decision or failed attempt — **UNVERIFIED current gap**

Fault: the successor repeats a failed diagnostic path, mistakes a workaround for a final fix, or loses the reason an alternative was rejected.

Catch: `DECISION` and `PENDING` records preserve evidence references, rejection rationale, and uncertainty without copying full context. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the log's direct coverage but dependent on agents emitting records at decision time, that this reduces repeated dead ends. Settle by seeding tasks with two plausible branches, killing after one falsification, and scoring repeat work.

### 3.6 Corrupt or incomplete recovery record — **UNVERIFIED current gap**

Fault: the worker dies during capsule append, or the capsule itself contains a wrong claim.

Catch: atomic append/framing detects truncation; independent walkdown detects semantic conflict. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that framing detects deliberately truncated records and **likely (55-80%)** that the walkdown detects seeded semantic conflicts, because only observable claims can be checked. ## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 A dead worker cannot perform the outgoing briefing

Nuclear turnover normally overlaps living outgoing and incoming personnel. Session or machine death removes the outgoing operator precisely when clarification is most valuable. Therefore, the transfer depends more heavily on contemporaneous records than nuclear turnover does. A summary written only at graceful shutdown is almost useless for crash recovery.

This disanalogy does not defeat the borrow; it changes the emphasis from end-of-shift checklist to continuous intent/result journaling. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the impossibility of post-mortem questioning and the DOE log reconstruction function, that continuous records are more valuable than shutdown-only summaries for crash cases. Settle by comparing recoverability after random kill points under periodic, graceful-only, and per-transition recording.

### 4.2 Nuclear plant state is instrumented and bounded; agent side effects are heterogeneous

A control room has defined parameters, alarms, lineups, and licensed roles. An agent may touch files, shells, browsers, APIs, cloud services, and human communication, each with different consistency and audit semantics. There is no universal “control board.” The capsule cannot create observability that an external service does not expose.

Therefore, recovery guarantees must be action-class-specific. Claims of exactly-once behavior are **almost certainly not (1-5%)** credible without an idempotency contract or authoritative transaction protocol; the evidence is the unresolvable request/commit ambiguity when both the response and query mechanism are absent. The settling experiment is a service that commits then drops every response and offers no lookup key: no successor can distinguish committed from uncommitted from local evidence alone.

### 4.3 Two agents are not two independent people

Models may share training, prompt, tools, predecessor narrative, and systematic blind spots. A nominal second-agent review can be correlated theater. Independence has to come from different evidence paths and blinded ordering, not agent count.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on shared context and the DNFSB warning about contaminated verification, that unblinded model review adds less error detection than independent state reconstruction. This is **UNVERIFIED** for the owner's models; settle with the blinded A/B experiment in Section 2.4.

### 4.4 Nuclear conservatism protects against catastrophic physical consequences; most agent actions are cheap

Applying nuclear-level holds to every file edit or test rerun would make the harness slow and brittle. The transfer works only with a graded approach. Read-only observation and reversible local actions should remain cheap; expensive review belongs at uncertain, irreversible boundaries.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the extreme difference in consequence distributions and the action matrix's explicit containment, that universal two-person approval would cost more than it prevents for routine local work. The exact threshold is **UNVERIFIED**; settle by measuring intervention rate, prevented defects, latency, and owner interruptions across risk classes.

### 4.5 Procedures can become ceremony or stale authority

The Fort Calhoun case shows that having a procedure did not ensure an adequate turnover, and the SRS assessment found signature discrepancies despite a defined process ([NRC EA-97-432](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/enforcement/actions/reactors/ea97432); [DOE SRS assessment](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/IA%20of%20ConOps%20at%20SRS%20Tritium%20Facilities%20-%20September%202022.pdf)). A recovery checklist that merely produces a signed “resumed” record can hide failure.

The harness must make `ASSUME` contingent on machine-checkable evidence: lease epoch acquired, workspace identity matched, unmatched intents classified, conflicts listed, and required receipts queried. A signature without these predicates is not completion.

### 4.6 Defence layers can share a common mode

If capsule, logs, test reports, and worktree all reside on the lost machine, four named layers are one physical layer. If both agents read the same false summary, two reviewers are one informational layer. The IAEA's emphasis on independence and diversity after Fukushima is the crucial constraint, not the number of controls ([IAEA Fukushima lesson](https://gnssn.iaea.org/FukushimaLessonsLearned/Lists/Fukushima/DispForm.aspx?ContentTypeId=0x01006A6D303BCE89A14FA6083DD8D969FFE0&ID=185)).

Machine-loss recovery may require an already-existing remote system of record. Whether such a native store exists is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is to destroy a disposable worker disk after recording a task and inventory which evidence remains independently accessible. This unit does not recommend introducing a new durable-execution service to fill the gap.

## 5. Verification plan

No proposal should be accepted because the analogy sounds persuasive. Run a controlled recovery tournament against the current baseline and a minimal implementation.

### 5.1 Detector controls

Before claiming any missing record, missed hook, or undetected failure:

1. inject a known-present canary `INTENT`, `RESULT`, lease expiry, VCS change, and remote receipt;
2. prove each detector finds its canary;
3. use a mechanically independent signal for every decisive classification—for example capsule record plus VCS object/file hash, or local intent plus remote receipt;
4. treat transcript and capsule entries generated from the same hook as one signal, not two.

This directly satisfies the two-independent-signals requirement. A repeated run of the same probe is not accepted as independence.

### 5.2 Kill-point matrix

For each action class—read-only local, reversible local write, test/build, external idempotent call, external non-idempotent call, destructive action—kill the worker at:

1. before `INTENT` append;
2. after `INTENT`, before dispatch;
3. after dispatch, before side effect;
4. after side effect, before response;
5. after response, before `RESULT`;
6. after `RESULT`, before postcondition check;
7. during capsule append;
8. during explicit completion;
9. after successor takeover, then revive predecessor.

Repeat with same-process crash, session loss, disk loss, and network partition where the infrastructure permits.

### 5.3 Seeded adversarial conditions

- predecessor summary falsely says “done”;
- predecessor summary falsely says “not run”;
- dirty user change overlaps the task;
- branch/worktree identity changes;
- test artifact is stale relative to source;
- remote service commits but drops response;
- external receipt conflicts with local result;
- capsule final record is truncated;
- old worker revives after lease epoch changes;
- verifier sees the predecessor conclusion before evidence (control) versus after independent classification (treatment).

### 5.4 Acceptance metrics

- no duplicate non-idempotent side effect;
- no destructive replay under uncertain commit;
- no overwrite of seeded user changes;
- correct classification of action state;
- time and tool calls to useful resumed work;
- false safe-hold rate;
- owner interruptions per recovery;
- repeated-dead-end rate;
- stale-worker mutations accepted;
- bytes and latency added per normal action.

The minimal mechanism is successful only if it improves safety outcomes without imposing large steady-state overhead. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the compact record schema and boundary-only verification, that overhead can remain low enough for routine use; this is **UNVERIFIED**, and the settling experiment is to measure p50/p95 hook latency and journal growth on representative sessions.

## 8. Final judgment

The nuclear-operations analogy is valid because both domains face a custody boundary where the successor can inherit incomplete narratives, abnormal state, unfinished evolutions, and dangerous pressure to continue. The transferable achievement is not “nuclear is careful.” It is a concrete protocol: durable logs, structured turnover, direct status verification, explicit assumption of responsibility, conservative handling of uncertainty, and independent/diverse barriers.

The analogy fails if translated as universal two-person approval, paperwork-heavy checklists, or a large fault-tolerant orchestration platform. Nuclear two-person security controls do not automatically provide independent technical verification; software state is less bounded and often less observable than plant state; and most agent actions do not justify nuclear-grade ceremony.

The narrow design—write-ahead recovery capsule, fenced takeover, independent walkdown, and graded safe hold—is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the strong turnover/state-reconstruction correspondence and authoritative nuclear operating guidance, to be worth a fault-injection prototype. Its superiority over any native recovery already present is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is a controlled baseline-versus-prototype recovery tournament with known-present detector controls and two independent signals for every claimed recovery outcome.
