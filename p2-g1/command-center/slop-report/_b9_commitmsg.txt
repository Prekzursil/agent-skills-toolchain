WHY BYTE-UNCHANGED PLUS A ruff EXCLUSION INSTEAD OF REFORMATTING
Committing these 45 with no ruff exclusion turns gate-lint-format RED. Measured in a fresh
LF clone of HEAD with both states run: with the exclusion, 0 files would be reformatted and
`ruff check` passes; without it, 29 files would be reformatted and `ruff check` finds 9
errors. The gate is repo-wide, so that would have blocked every sibling PR -- the exclusion is
load-bearing, not decoration, and the pair of runs is what proves it rather than one green.

Reformatting was considered and REJECTED on an honest negative. The check that would have
licensed it -- "does any committed artifact declare a digest OF one of these files" -- FAILED
its own positive control: the known provenance-locked `casualty/probes/adv0022_probe2.py` did
not match any of the 5 digests published near its name (those turn out to be digests of probe
OUTPUT, computed at _build_adv0022.py:57-58). A matcher that cannot find a lock it is pointed
straight at cannot certify the absence of one. So "no receipt depends on these bytes" is
UNVERIFIED, not established, and the byte-preserving route is the one that cannot destroy
evidence. Settling experiment: find the casualty sha256 manifest and re-run the matcher
against it; if it then finds the lock and still clears these 45, they can be formatted and the
exclusion dropped.

Same trade-off ruff.toml already settled two entries above for
`command-center/casualty/probes`: there is no per-file escape from `ruff format` that does not
itself change the bytes, so the choice is an exclusion or destroying provenance to satisfy a
style gate.
