# X166 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → synthesis

## Executive judgment

The military mechanism that genuinely transfers is **not “use an OODA loop”** and not “ask a red-team agent to critique the answer.” It is the larger command-and-control pattern by which a headquarters turns more information than one commander can personally read into a bounded decision:

1. the commander publishes a compact **intent** (purpose, key tasks, desired end state);
2. the staff maintains **running estimates** rather than repeatedly rereading the full record;
3. **commander’s critical information requirements (CCIRs)** and **decision points** determine what must be elevated and when;
4. a decision brief exposes courses of action, assumptions, risk, and dissent rather than merely delivering a consensus narrative;
5. confirmation briefs/backbriefs test whether delegated actors understood the intent; and
6. an **after-action review (AAR)** compares intent, events, causes, and changes after execution.

That stack is a strong structural match for “collapse thousands of findings into a decision without an agent reading 12 MB.” A military commander and an owner both face a bounded-attention decision over evidence gathered by distributed specialists. In both settings, raw completeness is impossible, evidence may conflict, relevance depends on intent, and the decision-maker needs to know not only the recommendation but what would invalidate it. I assess the core transfer as **very likely (80-90%)**, based on that shared many-observers/one-decision/bounded-attention topology and on doctrine explicitly assigning intent, information requirements, staff estimates, and assessment distinct roles [ADP 6-0, especially paras. 1-23–1-26 and 3-3–3-7](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34403-ADP_6-0-000-WEB-3.pdf), [JP 5-0, chapters III and VI](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf).

The transfer has a hard boundary: military hierarchy, trained staffs, common doctrine, live observation, and command authority do not exist merely because agents are arranged in a tree. Model outputs are correlated, their “confidence” is not calibrated by rank, and summarization can silently destroy evidence. Therefore this unit recommends a **small protocol on the existing skill/hook bus**, not a military-themed second orchestration runtime. The protocol must preserve source locators, contradictions, minority reports, and change triggers mechanically. This passes the anti-overfit gate: it adds bounded records and validation to the existing bus; it does not add a second runtime, vector/KG memory, durable execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The proposed mechanism is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; the settling experiment is a blinded replay of representative large finding sets in which the current synthesis and the proposed decision packet are scored against adjudicated must-include facts, contradiction retention, decision reversals after injected decisive evidence, owner reading time, and unsupported-claim rate. The implementation recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to improve decision recall without requiring a final model to read all 12 MB, because each proposed field corresponds to a doctrinal information-loss control; uncertainty remains because military staff processes depend on humans with domain training and because model-generated intermediate summaries may share failure modes.

## 1. Commander’s intent: compression by invariants, not compression by summary

### What it is in its own field

Army mission command is designed for operations in which subordinates cannot continually ask the commander what to do. Doctrine therefore separates a concise statement of what must remain true from detailed instructions that will become stale. ADP 6-0 defines commander’s intent as a clear and concise expression of the operation’s purpose and desired military end state that supports initiative, and explains that it includes purpose, key tasks, and conditions defining the end state. It also treats shared understanding and disciplined initiative as principles of mission command [ADP 6-0, paras. 1-23–1-26 and 1-74–1-79](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34403-ADP_6-0-000-WEB-3.pdf). The earlier ADRP 6-0 gives the same purpose/key-tasks/end-state decomposition and emphasizes that intent links mission and concept of operations [ADRP 6-0, paras. 2-14–2-19](https://home.army.mil/wood/application/files/7715/5751/8336/ADRP_6_0_Mission_Command.pdf).

This is a subtle compression mechanism. The commander does not compress all intelligence into a shorter intelligence report. The commander compresses the **decision’s invariants**: why the operation matters, what cannot be dropped, and what successful conditions look like. Subordinates can discard enormous amounts of detail while still improvising coherently because they have a loss function for judging local choices.

The decisive insight for synthesis is: **a summary tries to preserve the source; intent specifies which losses are acceptable.** A generic “summarize these findings” prompt has no principled way to choose between two facts competing for the last token. An intent contract does.

### Structural transfer

The analogy transfers because synthesis is an allocation problem under a hard attention budget. The owner’s decision—not the document collection—is the organizing object. A synthesis agent needs a compact statement of:

- **purpose:** what decision this synthesis will enable and why now;
- **key tasks:** which questions must be answered before the decision is defensible;
- **end state:** what the delivered decision packet must allow the owner to know or do;
- **constraints:** forbidden architectures, mandatory evidence rules, cost/time limits;
- **acceptable risk:** which uncertainty can remain and which uncertainty requires escalation.

It does **not** transfer if “intent” becomes a decorative mission statement written after analysis. In military doctrine, intent governs decentralized action; in the harness it must govern routing, field validation, and final acceptance before any reducer runs. It also does not authorize agents to invent priorities: military intent comes from a legitimate commander, while harness intent must come from the owner’s brief or an explicit owner decision.

### Recommendation 1 — UNVERIFIED: compile the owner brief into an Intent Contract — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Create a machine-readable record before fan-out:

```yaml
decision_id: stable-id
decision: "Choose evolve, replace, or hybrid harness architecture"
purpose: "Improve owner outcomes over the next operating horizon"
key_questions:
  - "Which measured failure classes dominate avoidable damage?"
  - "Which candidate mechanisms address them with evidence?"
  - "What is the smallest reversible architecture that wins?"
must_preserve:
  - source_locator
  - contradictory_evidence
  - owner_correction
  - confidence_basis
constraints:
  - "existing bus only unless BUILD-LARGE is separately justified"
decision_thresholds:
  - id: replacement
    test: "replacement dominates evolution on adjudicated outcomes and migration risk"
acceptable_unknowns: []
stop_or_escalate_if:
  - "a must-preserve field cannot be traced to source"
```

The compiler should reject a vague contract, but should not force a brittle giant schema. A small hook can require only stable identifiers, nonempty purpose, key questions, must-preserve fields, constraints, and escalation conditions; domain-specific details remain open text. The final packet must explicitly map each recommendation to at least one key question and each constraint.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by replaying at least 20 previously adjudicated synthesis tasks with and without the contract, holding model and source set constant, then measuring must-include recall, irrelevant-token proportion, and owner preference under blinded labels. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce intent drift and salience-by-recency because the contract gives reducers an explicit inclusion criterion, supported by the doctrinal role of intent; it is only “likely,” not “very likely,” because a model may obey the form superficially without changing selection behavior.

What it catches that unconstrained synthesis can miss:

- a polished recommendation that answers an interesting adjacent question rather than the owner’s decision;
- a mandatory constraint silently lost three reduction levels earlier;
- evidence included because it is frequent or recent but does not change the decision;
- a “complete” synthesis that never states the conditions under which the decision is safe.

## 2. CCIRs and decision points: filter by decision consequence

### What they are in their own field

Military staffs do not elevate everything they learn. A commander identifies critical information requirements tied to decisions; the plan identifies decision points at which the commander must choose a course or branch. JP 5-0 integrates commander’s critical information requirements, decision points, branches/sequels, assumptions, risk, and assessment into planning rather than treating intelligence as an undifferentiated stream [JP 5-0, chapters III, V, and VI](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf). ADP 6-0 likewise describes commanders driving the operations process through understanding, visualization, description, direction, leadership, and assessment, supported by staff work and information management [ADP 6-0, chapter 3](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34403-ADP_6-0-000-WEB-3.pdf).

The mechanism is not “show important facts.” It is **precommitment about what information would cause which decision**. That sharply reduces upward traffic and prevents novelty alone from defining importance.

### Structural transfer

The match is strong: thousands of findings should not become thousands of prose paragraphs competing for an aggregator’s attention. Each finding can instead declare its decision consequence:

```text
claim → supports/opposes which candidate → which criterion → magnitude → source
```

The disanalogy is that battlefield CCIRs often concern observable events (“has force X crossed line Y?”), while harness evidence is frequently interpretive (“mechanism X is maintainable”). Interpretive judgments cannot be made crisp merely by assigning them a field. The protocol therefore must distinguish observed facts, derived metrics, interpretations, and forecasts.

### Recommendation 2 — UNVERIFIED: add a Critical Decision Information and trigger ledger — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Every leaf finding should use a compact envelope, stored as line-delimited JSON or similarly streamable records rather than a monolithic strict fan-out schema:

```json
{
  "finding_id": "...",
  "kind": "observation|metric|interpretation|forecast",
  "claim": "one atomic claim",
  "source": {"locator": "URL-or-path:line", "excerpt_hash": "..."},
  "decision_relevance": {
    "candidate": "evolve|replace|hybrid|none",
    "criterion": "reliability|capability|cost|migration-risk|other",
    "direction": "supports|opposes|neutral",
    "magnitude": "decisive|material|context"
  },
  "pivotal_assumptions": ["..."],
  "reversal_trigger": "new evidence that would change this claim",
  "contradicts": ["finding-id"],
  "confidence_band": "likely (55-80%)",
  "confidence_evidence": "..."
}
```

A reducer may omit “context” findings from the decision card, but it may not omit:

- decisive findings;
- findings attached to an unresolved critical-information requirement;
- a contradiction pair;
- evidence that crosses a predeclared decision threshold;
- a finding that invalidates a pivotal assumption;
- a constraint violation.

These are mechanical promotion rules, not another judge model. The final decision packet carries a short **watch list** of reversal triggers. When new findings arrive, only triggers and affected running estimates are reevaluated.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with an injection test: place low-frequency but decision-reversing facts at random depths and positions in at least 30 large finding sets, compare promotion and correct-decision rates against free-form hierarchical summarization, and separately score false-positive promotion volume. The ledger is **very likely (80-90%)** to make hidden contradictions and decision-reversing evidence more detectable, because explicit IDs and deterministic promotion rules can be unit-tested; it is **likely (55-80%)** to improve the ultimate decision, because the interpretation and magnitude assignments can themselves be wrong.

What it catches:

- a decisive one-off finding drowned out by 50 duplicated moderate findings;
- contradictory findings summarized independently into apparently compatible prose;
- a recommendation whose pivotal assumption was invalidated after the summary was written;
- a new result that merits no full resynthesis because no reversal trigger fired;
- “high confidence” copied upward without the evidence that justified it.

## 3. Running estimates: maintain state by question, not by transcript

### What they are in their own field

Military staffs continuously maintain estimates in their functional areas so that the commander has current assessments and recommendations as the situation changes. Planning and execution are not separate one-shot activities; assessment updates understanding and may trigger reframing or replanning. This appears across Army operations-process doctrine and joint planning doctrine [ADP 6-0, chapter 3](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34403-ADP_6-0-000-WEB-3.pdf), [JP 5-0, chapters III and VI](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf).

The useful property is **bounded, responsibility-indexed state**. The commander does not read every historical report whenever a decision recurs. Each staff area maintains what is currently believed, the evidence and assumptions behind it, what changed, and what it recommends.

### Structural transfer

A harness can maintain one running estimate per decision criterion or key question. This is safer than a single global summary because independent updates cannot silently rewrite unrelated conclusions. It is also far smaller than durable semantic memory: the source artifacts remain the authority; the estimate is a disposable, regenerable index.

The analogy fails if the estimate becomes a long-lived “truth database.” Human staffs have accountable officers and recurring validation; agents do not. Any estimate must therefore carry source locators, a generation/version identifier, and an expiry or refresh condition. It must be reconstructable from the immutable finding stream.

### Recommendation 3 — UNVERIFIED: use regenerable Running Estimate Cards — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

For each key question, keep a bounded card:

```text
QUESTION / CRITERION
current judgment + confidence band + evidence
top supporting finding IDs (max 5)
top opposing finding IDs (max 5)
unresolved contradictions
pivotal assumptions
what changed since prior version
reversal triggers
recommended next evidence acquisition
source-set hash and generated-at version
```

Do not append indefinitely. Regenerate the card from finding envelopes when its source-set hash changes, and retain only the diff needed for the decision packet. Source text is never replaced by the estimate.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by applying random additions, deletions, and contradiction injections to a fixed evidence set, then comparing full-from-scratch and incremental cards for semantic equivalence and source coverage. Require deterministic validation of IDs and hashes, plus human adjudication of conclusions. It is **likely (55-80%)** to lower rereading cost while retaining decision-relevant state, based on the bounded functional-estimate pattern; benefit is not more certain because repeated model regeneration may introduce drift.

What it catches:

- an old conclusion that survives after its only source is removed;
- a new finding that changes one criterion but is allowed to rewrite the whole narrative;
- untraceable “institutional knowledge” with no current evidence;
- churn disguised as progress: a diff can show that wording changed but decision state did not.

## 4. Decision briefs and confirmation briefs: test understanding, not eloquence

### What they are in their own field

Military planning culminates in a commander’s decision among courses of action, with explicit comparison, risk, assumptions, and decision points. Subordinates then use confirmation briefs or backbriefs to demonstrate their understanding of the mission and intent; this reveals divergent interpretations before execution. Mission command depends on shared understanding, but does not equate shared understanding with identical wording [ADRP 6-0, paras. 2-09–2-19](https://home.army.mil/wood/application/files/7715/5751/8336/ADRP_6_0_Mission_Command.pdf), [JP 5-0, chapter V](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf).

The synthesis lesson is that the final product should be an **instrument for a decision**, not a smaller literature review. A backbrief is also a better test than asking “did you understand?”: the receiver must reconstruct the operational meaning.

### Recommendation 4 — UNVERIFIED: emit a one-page Decision Card plus an independent backbrief — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

The top-level artifact should begin with a bounded card:

```text
DECISION REQUIRED
INTENT: purpose / key tasks / end state / constraints
RECOMMENDED COURSE OF ACTION
WHY IT WINS: criterion-by-criterion, linked to finding IDs
ALTERNATIVES: strongest case for each, not strawmen
RISKS AND PIVOTAL ASSUMPTIONS
MINORITY REPORT / UNRESOLVED CONTRADICTIONS
REVERSAL TRIGGERS AND NEXT DECISION POINT
CONFIDENCE: band plus concrete evidence
OWNER ACTION: approve / choose alternative / request named evidence
```

Detailed annexes remain accessible by finding ID, but the owner need not read them unless a field is challenged. After the card is built, a separate context-limited pass receives only the Intent Contract and the Decision Card and must state:

1. what decision it believes is being made;
2. the recommendation and why it wins;
3. the strongest contrary case;
4. the pivotal assumption;
5. the evidence that would reverse the choice.

Compare those five answers mechanically/semantically with the card. A discrepancy is an ambiguity alarm, not an invitation for the backbrief agent to rewrite the decision.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by a blinded comprehension experiment in which evaluators answer the five questions from either the current synthesis or the decision-card package, while measuring correctness, time, and unjustified certainty. It is **likely (55-80%)** that the backbrief will detect ambiguous or internally inconsistent packets because reconstruction exposes missing links; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** to provide independent factual verification when both passes use the same model family, because their errors can be correlated.

What it catches:

- a recommendation whose rationale does not distinguish it from the runner-up;
- a nominal alternative that was never represented in its strongest form;
- a packet whose reader cannot tell which single assumption matters most;
- a vague “monitor results” instruction with no specified trigger or next decision;
- shared wording mistaken for shared understanding.

## 5. Red teaming: preserve a minority report, do not manufacture ritual opposition

### What it is in its own field

Military red teaming applies structured critical thinking and alternative perspectives to plans, assumptions, and decisions. The UK Ministry of Defence handbook describes red teaming as a set of tools and approaches for independent critical thought rather than merely playing an enemy; its publication explicitly aims to improve decision making through challenge [UK MOD, *Red Teaming Handbook*, third edition](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/a-guide-to-red-teaming). Joint planning also uses red teams and alternative analysis to expose assumptions, biases, and vulnerabilities [JP 5-0, planning discussion in chapters III–V](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf).

The valuable mechanism is organizational, not theatrical: dissent receives a protected route to the decision-maker and is evaluated against assumptions and failure modes. A red team that must persuade the plan owner through the same consensus chain is not meaningfully independent.

### Structural transfer and failure modes

The transfer is partial. Agents can be given different evidence slices, roles, or prompts, but they do not acquire genuinely independent incentives, experience, or epistemology. Two calls to the same model are correlated samples, not two mechanically independent signals. A generic “critic” also tends to produce plausible objections regardless of materiality, consuming attention and encouraging performative balance.

Therefore, the harness should red-team only the **pivotal assumptions and leading course of action**, and preserve material dissent as a minority report. It should not red-team every leaf, vote across many agents, or treat majority agreement as truth.

### Recommendation 5 — UNVERIFIED: targeted assumption challenge with protected minority report — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

After the initial course comparison, run one bounded challenge that receives:

- the intent and constraints;
- the recommended course;
- the pivotal assumptions;
- the strongest supporting finding IDs;
- explicit instructions to produce only falsifiable failure paths and disconfirming evidence requests.

Its output fields are: challenged assumption, plausible failure mechanism, supporting source/finding ID, severity if true, discriminating test, and whether the challenge crosses a decision threshold. Challenges without evidence or a discriminating test stay in an annex. Threshold-crossing challenges appear verbatim in the Decision Card’s minority-report field even if the main synthesizer disagrees.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with seeded-assumption tests, including plausible but false objections, and score true-assumption-failure recall, false-alarm rate, decision improvement, and added tokens. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a same-family red-team pass will improve the final decision, because doctrine supports protected challenge but provides no evidence that correlated language-model passes yield comparable independence. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a protected field will at least prevent a generated dissent item from being silently summarized away, because field presence and routing are mechanically testable.

What it catches:

- consensus formed by several findings that all depend on the same hidden premise;
- an attractive replacement plan with an untested migration assumption;
- a downside that the main narrative acknowledges and then rhetorically minimizes;
- dissent deleted because it was numerically outvoted rather than answered.

## 6. OODA: use the trigger logic; reject the slogan

### What it is in its own field

John Boyd’s observe–orient–decide–act construct is often drawn as a four-box circle, but Boyd’s own presentations depict interacting feedback loops in which orientation is shaped by prior experience, cultural traditions, genetic heritage, new information, analysis/synthesis, and feedback. The point is adaptation in interaction, not simply moving faster through four labels [Boyd, *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*, “The Essence of Winning and Losing”](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0151_Boyd_Discourse_Winning_Losing.pdf). Marine Corps command-and-control doctrine uses the observation–orientation–decision–action cycle to explain competitive decision tempo and recognizes orientation as the complex filtering component [MCDP 6, chapter 2](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCDP%206.pdf).

### Adversarial assessment of the analogy

As the primary answer to large-scale synthesis, OODA **does not transfer well**. It says little about provenance, hierarchical information loss, contradictory reports, course comparison, or how a commander knows a staff summary omitted a decisive fact. “Loop faster” can make those defects worse. Agent work also often lacks a reactive adversary; the chief constraint may be evidence quality rather than tempo.

One narrow part transfers: decisions should expose what observation would change orientation and reopen the decision. That is already captured by reversal triggers and next decision points. OODA therefore adds no new service or agent topology.

### Recommendation 6 — UNVERIFIED: encode reversal triggers; build no OODA component — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Add `reversal_trigger`, `observation_source`, `threshold`, and `affected_decision` to running estimates. A hook marks an estimate stale when a threshold is crossed. Do not create an “OODA agent,” a perpetual autonomous loop, or a second controller.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by replaying evidence updates around decision thresholds and measuring whether the right decisions reopen with acceptable false-trigger volume. It is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent stale decisions from appearing final because explicit triggers are testable, while it is **unlikely (20-45%)** to improve initial synthesis accuracy because OODA does not address information-loss mechanics.

## 7. After-action review: close the learning loop without building a memory empire

### What it is in its own field

The Army AAR is a guided analysis of performance. TC 25-20 organizes it around what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, why it happened, and what should be sustained or improved; it emphasizes participation, observable events, and improving future performance rather than assigning blame [TC 25-20, chapters 1–2](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA463410.pdf). This is causal comparison against an explicit plan, not a generic retrospective summary.

### Structural transfer

This transfers only after the owner’s decision has produced an observable outcome. Running an AAR immediately after generating prose measures self-consistency, not effectiveness. The harness must compare the Intent Contract and Decision Card to later evidence: did predicted risks occur, did reversal triggers fire, what did the owner correct, which finding envelope was wrong or missing, and what protocol field would have caught it?

The military assumption of candid human discussion does not transfer automatically. Agents cannot reliably infer why they failed from their own output. Outcome evidence and owner correction must outrank generated causal stories.

### Recommendation 7 — UNVERIFIED: event-triggered, evidence-first micro-AAR — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Trigger an AAR only on a decision outcome, owner correction, rollback, threshold crossing, or verified failure. Store a small record:

```text
intent and predicted outcome
observed outcome (source locator)
delta
causal claim + confidence band + concrete evidence
which assumption/finding/protocol field failed
sustain/change/experiment
owner correction, if any, quoted with locator
```

Promote only validated protocol changes or reusable error fingerprints. Do not retain a vector database of all retrospectives; the source records remain files on the existing bus, discoverable by deterministic IDs and ordinary search.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by prospective tracking across a fixed number of consequential decisions, then test whether AAR-derived changes reduce recurrence on held-out tasks versus a no-change baseline. It is **likely (55-80%)** to make corrections more actionable because it forces plan/outcome comparison, but **roughly even (45-55%)** to reduce failure rates until enough genuinely comparable outcomes exist.

What it catches:

- a synthesis celebrated as successful because it was well written even though the decision failed;
- a predicted risk that occurred without its trigger firing;
- repeated owner correction that never becomes a specific protocol test;
- a learned “lesson” based only on the model’s retrospective narrative.

## 8. Integrated design: the Intent-to-Decision Packet

### Recommendation 8 — UNVERIFIED: one bounded protocol, not seven subsystems — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

The components above should be implemented as one small protocol:

```text
Owner brief
   ↓ compile + validate
Intent Contract
   ↓ fan-out research on existing bus
Atomic Finding Envelopes (append/stream; preserve sources)
   ↓ deterministic promotion + grouping by key question
Running Estimate Cards
   ↓ course comparison + targeted assumption challenge
Decision Card + Minority Report + Evidence Index
   ↓ independent comprehension backbrief
Owner decision
   ↓ only after observable outcome/correction
Micro-AAR → validated protocol change or reusable fingerprint
```

The final agent reads the Intent Contract, bounded Running Estimate Cards, promoted contradiction pairs, the targeted minority report, and the evidence index—not 12 MB of raw findings. It can pull a raw source on demand by locator. This is controlled loss, not no loss.

Minimal viable implementation:

1. JSON Schema or equivalent for the small envelopes, with permissive extension fields and no deeply nested universal output schema.
2. A validator/promoter script under roughly 200 lines that checks IDs, source locators, confidence evidence, contradiction symmetry, must-preserve coverage, and promotion rules.
3. Prompt/config templates for Intent Contract, Running Estimate Card, Decision Card, targeted red-team challenge, and backbrief.
4. Existing file/search mechanisms for evidence retrieval; no new database.
5. Existing hooks for pre-fan-out intent validation, pre-final packet validation, stale-trigger notification, and event-triggered AAR initiation.

The integrated protocol is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with the phased evaluation below. It is **likely (55-80%)** to fit below roughly 200 lines of validation/orchestration code because the work is record validation and routing on an existing bus, but that line estimate is itself **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a spike implementing schemas and the five deterministic checks without model calls. If it exceeds the bound because of platform-specific hook plumbing, retain the architectural constraint (no second runtime) rather than gaming the count.

## 9. Why the borrow transfers—and where it breaks

| Military structural assumption | Synthesis analogue | Transfer judgment | Required adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| One commander owns the decision | One owner has final authority | Strong | Intent must originate in owner brief; agents cannot self-authorize goals. |
| Staff specialties see different parts of reality | Research units see subsets of a large corpus | Strong | Preserve atomic sources and responsibility by key question. |
| Commander cannot read every report | Final agent/owner cannot read 12 MB | Strong | Promote by decision consequence; retain drill-down locators. |
| Purpose and end state remain useful as local conditions change | Decision criteria can guide lossy reduction | Strong but bounded | Compile intent before fan-out; test must-preserve coverage. |
| Reports may conflict or be late | Findings conflict and arrive asynchronously | Strong | Contradiction IDs, running-estimate versions, reversal triggers. |
| Staff officers are trained, accountable, and organizationally distinct | Agent instances may be same-model correlated samples | Weak | Never treat agent count or vote as independence; use deterministic checks and protected dissent. |
| Commander can question staff and inspect reality | Owner/final agent may have only artifacts | Partial | Source locators, evidence pull-through, backbrief, explicit gaps. |
| Adversary reacts strategically | Many harness tasks have no live adversary | Weak for OODA | Use only update triggers; no perpetual OODA runtime. |
| Outcomes can be observed after action | Harness decisions sometimes have delayed/ambiguous outcomes | Partial for AAR | Trigger only on sourced outcomes/corrections; mark causal inference uncertainty. |
| Rank resolves action after debate | Epistemic truth is not determined by authority | Does not transfer | Owner chooses action, but evidence fields and minority report remain visible. |

Overall, it is **very likely (80-90%)** that the topology transfers at the protocol level, based on the first five strong/partial correspondences. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that copying military staff roles or command hierarchy literally would create epistemic independence, because same-model agents lack the institutional, experiential, and incentive differences on which human staffs rely.

## 10. Adversarial failure analysis

### Failure A: intent becomes a compression weapon

A too-narrow intent can filter out paradigm-changing evidence. Military mission command assumes that subordinates can exercise judgment and report changes that invalidate the mission. The harness equivalent is a mandatory `invalidates_intent_or_frame` route that bypasses normal relevance filtering and lands in the minority report.

The bypass is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by seeding findings that show the original decision frame is wrong and scoring whether they reach the final card. It is **likely (55-80%)** to improve frame-break recall if mechanically promoted, but false alarms may be high.

### Failure B: schemas create false precision

Categorizing a claim as “decisive” does not make it decisive. The field must retain evidence and a reversal trigger, and the final comparison must be allowed to downgrade it. Avoid enums for nuanced causal content; use enums only for routing.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that routing-only enums are less brittle than a universal semantic schema, based on the limited number of required invariants and the open-ended nature of research claims; the performance claim remains **UNVERIFIED**, settled by schema-mismatch and lost-information rates in replay.

### Failure C: hierarchy amplifies correlated error

If every reduction level uses the same model and prompt family, an early misclassification can become unanimous. No amount of command language fixes that. Deterministic must-preserve rules, raw-source pull-through, contradiction preservation, and owner-visible minority reports are the countermeasures. Model diversity may be evaluated later, but is not assumed here.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that deterministic checks can detect missing required fields and broken IDs, because these are syntactic invariants; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** that they detect a fluent but substantively wrong claim, because that requires domain evidence.

### Failure D: the decision card hides tail risk

One page can create unjustified confidence. The card must state coverage (number of source findings, promoted findings, unresolved contradictions, and unassessed key questions) and link to annexes. “No dissent” is not proof of agreement; it means only no threshold-crossing dissent was recorded.

The coverage counter is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose unprocessed or unlinked records if IDs are validated; whether the owner notices and acts on it is **UNVERIFIED**, settled by the comprehension experiment.

### Failure E: red teaming becomes objection spam

Restrict challenge to pivotal assumptions; demand a failure mechanism, evidence locator, and discriminating test; cap output; measure false alarms. If it does not improve seeded-failure recall net of attention cost, delete it. The doctrine-inspired design must earn its place.

### Failure F: AAR becomes self-justification

The AAR must start from sourced outcome deltas and owner corrections, not “reflect on your performance.” Causal claims require the mandatory confidence band and evidence. If the outcome is not observable, record a gap and stop.

## 11. Evaluation plan and kill criteria

### Phase 1 — representation tests

Use synthetic and real large finding sets with known controls:

- known-present source locator to prove the source detector;
- known-present contradiction pair to prove contradiction detection;
- decisive singleton buried among duplicates;
- invalidated pivotal assumption;
- frame-breaking finding;
- missing key-question coverage;
- stale running estimate after source deletion.

Before claiming any condition absent, validate the detector on the known-present control and use a second mechanically independent signal—for example, ID-graph traversal plus raw-text/source-locator search. This directly honors the program’s two-signal absence rule.

### Phase 2 — blinded replay

Sample tasks across domains and evidence sizes. Hold source set and model fixed. Compare:

- current synthesis;
- hierarchical prose summarization with the same token budget;
- Intent-to-Decision Packet.

Primary metrics:

1. adjudicated must-include finding recall;
2. decisive-singleton recall;
3. contradiction retention;
4. unsupported-claim rate;
5. correct course selection where an adjudicated answer exists;
6. evaluator comprehension of decision, rationale, contrary case, pivotal assumption, and reversal trigger;
7. owner/evaluator reading time;
8. total model tokens and wall time;
9. false promotion and red-team false-alarm volume;
10. source drill-down success.

### Phase 3 — update and learning tests

Inject evidence over time. Measure whether the correct estimate becomes stale, whether unrelated cards remain stable, and whether reversal triggers reopen the correct decision. Track prospective outcomes long enough to test micro-AAR changes on held-out work.

### Kill criteria

Delete or reduce the mechanism if any of the following holds after a preregistered replay:

- decision recall does not improve over equal-token hierarchical summarization;
- unsupported claims increase;
- owner reading time does not fall materially;
- false promotion makes the decision card routinely exceed its bound;
- same-model red teaming adds objections but no seeded-failure recall;
- running-estimate updates diverge materially from scratch regeneration;
- implementation requires a second orchestration runtime or any previously rejected platform component.

Threshold values must be chosen before replay based on owner utility; choosing them here without baseline distributions would be fabricated precision. The evaluation is **very likely (80-90%)** to distinguish syntactic/provenance benefits because those controls are deterministic, and **likely (55-80%)** to distinguish decision-quality benefits if the sample includes enough adjudicable decisions; the latter confidence is lower because many frontier architecture choices have no uncontested gold answer.

## 12. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just use a bigger context window”

Rejected as the sole mechanism. Capacity does not establish relevance, contradiction handling, provenance, decision thresholds, or update discipline. A larger context may be useful for drill-down, but it does not replace an intent-governed packet. It is **likely (55-80%)** that larger context alone would still miss low-salience decisive evidence in some tasks, based on the absence of mechanical promotion; this is **UNVERIFIED**, settled by the decisive-singleton replay.

### Literal OODA orchestrator

Rejected. It would add a controller while failing to specify information-loss controls. It also risks perpetual work without a meaningful adversary or outcome signal. This would violate the anti-overfit gate if implemented as a new runtime.

### Many-agent vote

Rejected. Votes aggregate outputs, not evidence, and same-family model calls are not mechanically independent signals. Numerical consensus can bury a correct minority finding. Course comparison plus a protected minority report is the smaller mechanism.

### Full military staff role taxonomy

Rejected. Roles such as intelligence, operations, logistics, plans, and civil affairs arise from durable human expertise and organizational responsibility. Naming agent personas after them does not create those properties. Partition by the owner’s key questions and evidence types instead.

### Vector database or knowledge graph for all findings

Rejected. The proposed finding stream, deterministic IDs, ordinary source search, bounded cards, and regenerable indices ride the existing bus and address this decision without a memory platform. Adding a vector/KG store would reintroduce a specifically disallowed component and has not been justified by this lens.

### Trained critic or guardrail framework

Rejected. The useful controls here are explicit fields, source links, routing rules, seeded tests, and owner-visible dissent. A trained critic would be a new correlated authority whose benefit would require separate evidence.

### Full durable-execution/observability service

Rejected. Versioned files, hashes, existing hooks, and the finding ledger are enough for the proposed experiment. A new daemon or observability SaaS fails the anti-overfit gate.

## 13. Concrete decision

Proceed only as a falsifiable **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** experiment: implement the Intent Contract, atomic Finding Envelope, deterministic promotion/coverage validator, Running Estimate Card, Decision Card, protected minority-report field, backbrief template, reversal triggers, and evidence-first micro-AAR as one protocol. Do not create an OODA agent, military staff persona hierarchy, voting system, new memory store, or new orchestration service.

The final recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to provide a better synthesis harness than unconstrained hierarchical prose summarization, based on the strong structural mapping and the presence of mechanical information-loss controls. It remains **UNVERIFIED**; the decisive experiment is the blinded replay with seeded decisive facts and contradictions, followed by prospective update/AAR testing. If it fails the kill criteria, retain only the source-envelope and coverage validator—the parts whose provenance guarantees can be demonstrated mechanically—and discard the doctrine-inspired ceremony.
