# X157 — Archival science → failure recovery

## Unit boundary and verdict

This is an ideation-lens unit. In accordance with the task, I did **not** inspect the local harness, corpus, audit findings, or subject tree. I researched archival-science and digital-preservation mechanisms and translated only the parts whose structure survives contact with agent failure recovery. Therefore, statements below about what a proposed mechanism can detect describe the mechanism’s capability, not a verified absence in the owner’s current harness.

**Verdict:** the borrow transfers, but narrowly. The useful mechanism is not “archive every log.” It is the combination of (1) a self-describing archival information package, (2) an accession/ingest transaction that validates identity, completeness, fixity, provenance, and usability, and (3) appraisal plus a retention/disposition schedule. A small preservation-event ledger supplies chain of custody. This is a good structural match for transferring useful task state across a worker/session/machine boundary. It is not a way to preserve a model’s hidden cognitive state or to prove that an old plan remains correct.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a compact implementation of this compound mechanism would improve recoverability across process and machine deaths. Evidence: OAIS explicitly couples content to the information needed to understand and preserve it; PREMIS models preservation actions as linked Objects, Events, Agents, and Rights; and operational digital-preservation guidance validates transfers against manifests and checksums before ingest. The band is below “almost certain” because the owner’s present recovery path was intentionally not inspected and because successful agent recovery depends on semantic task-state quality, which archival fixity cannot test.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a literal, full OAIS implementation would return enough additional recovery value to justify its complexity in an agent harness. Evidence: OAIS is a broad reference model covering ingest, archival storage, data management, access, administration, and preservation planning, whereas the target need is a bounded handoff protocol. The experiment that would narrow this estimate is the failure-injection evaluation specified below, comparing the compact package against both current behavior and a fuller OAIS-shaped prototype.

## What archival science has already solved

### 1. The archival information package: preserve the object *and what makes it intelligible*

The OAIS Reference Model’s central abstraction is an **Information Package**: Content Information plus Preservation Description Information (PDI), bound by Packaging Information and discoverable through Package Descriptions. An Archival Information Package (AIP) is the form held for preservation; Submission and Dissemination Information Packages describe transfer into and out of custody. OAIS further requires Representation Information: the structure and semantic information required to turn bits into meaningful information. PDI covers provenance, context, reference, fixity, and access-rights information. See the current primary standard, [CCSDS 650.0-M-3, Reference Model for an Open Archival Information System (OAIS), December 2024](https://public.ccsds.org/Pubs/650x0m3.pdf), especially the information-model material in sections 2 and 4.

This solves a subtle archival problem: intact bytes are not necessarily usable records. A future consumer needs to know what the object is, how it relates to other objects and the creating activity, what happened to it, how it is identified, whether its bits changed, and how to interpret it. The Digital Preservation Coalition’s overview likewise says OAIS preservation includes the digital material, management/descriptive metadata, and supporting Representation Information, and requires preserved information to be independently understandable to its designated user community ([DPC, “Standards and best practice”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/institutional-strategies/standards-and-best-practice)).

The important idea for recovery is therefore **context closure**, not exhaustive capture: put enough content, relationships, provenance, and decoding instructions into a bounded package that the intended successor can use it without asking the dead producer.

### 2. Accession and ingest: custody transfer is a tested transaction, not a file copy

Digital archives do not treat “some files arrived” as successful accession. DPC guidance says to check received records against a manifest; compare pre-transfer and post-transfer checksums; establish a verifiable manifest when none exists; document provenance and administrative history, content, structure, processing since creation, and management/use conditions; and only then ingest. It explicitly warns that without a verifiable manifest, comprehensive integrity verification may be impossible ([DPC, “Acquisition and appraisal”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/organisational-activities/acquisition-and-appraisal)).

Fixity is deliberately narrower than authenticity or usability. The NDSA guidance defines fixity as bit-level stability and says fixity information is commonly generated with checksums or cryptographic hashes. Crucially, it says generating and logging fixity is necessary but insufficient: scheduled audits and tested repair processes are also needed, and unchanged bits do not ensure that an object remains understandable ([NDSA, *Checking Your Digital Content*](https://www.digitalpreservation.gov/ndsa/working_groups/documents/NDSA-Fixity-Guidance-Report-final100214.pdf), pp. 1–2). DPC similarly describes checksums at source, storage, and delivery as support for a chain of custody, while noting that repair generally needs multiple copies ([DPC, “Fixity and checksums”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/technical-solutions-and-tools/fixity-and-checksums)).

The transferable mechanism is an explicit producer/consumer handshake:

1. Producer seals a manifest and package.
2. Custody transfer occurs.
3. Consumer independently validates package structure, object inventory, hashes, references, and prerequisites.
4. Consumer either issues an ingest receipt or quarantines the package with machine-readable reasons.

This is materially stronger than “read the last transcript” because successful receipt is an asserted and checked state transition.

### 3. Provenance and authenticity: record transformations as linked events

PREMIS is an implementable preservation-metadata standard maintained by the Library of Congress. Its data model centers on Objects, Events, Agents, and Rights; an Event is an action involving an object, while an Agent can be a person, organization, or software associated with an event. This makes migrations, validations, fixity checks, and other transformations attributable rather than mysterious ([Library of Congress, PREMIS overview](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/); [PREMIS Data Dictionary 3.0](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v3/premis-3-0-final.pdf)). The concise official introduction explains that an agent’s role in an event should be recorded and that rights metadata identifies acts a repository may perform and the basis for that permission ([Library of Congress, *Understanding PREMIS*](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/understandingPREMIS_english_2021.pdf), especially §§3.2–3.4).

In its own field, this is not a claim that a record is true. It is evidence of identity, custody, and documented change. DPC describes digital-forensic principles similarly: acquire evidence without altering it, demonstrate that fact, and make analysis accountable and repeatable; chain of custody, process control, audit, signatures, and write protection protect against uncertain provenance and alteration ([DPC, “Digital forensics”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/technical-solutions-and-tools/digital-forensics)).

That distinction matters for agents. A recovery package can prove “this is the package produced by worker W after event E, and these listed artifacts have not changed” without pretending to prove “worker W’s conclusion was correct.”

### 4. Appraisal and retention schedules: preserve selectively, dispose deliberately

Archival appraisal rejects indiscriminate retention. NARA defines appraisal as evaluating records for final disposition and distinguishes temporary records from permanent ones. A schedule describes content and format, record-creating context/function, and whether and when to destroy or transfer records ([NARA, “Records Scheduling and Appraisal”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/sch-appraisal)). NARA says appraisal both preserves important records and avoids unnecessary maintenance of excessive documentation ([NARA, “NARA Appraisal and Approval”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/nara-review)).

Schedules must have clear descriptions plus applicable cutoff and disposition instructions. NARA specifically cautions that “destroy when no longer needed” leaves decisions to individuals, is hard to automate, and can cause over-retention or premature destruction; fixed periods or bounded retention bands are more controllable ([NARA, “Scheduling Records”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

For agent recovery, appraisal answers “what is necessary evidence for resumption?” and a schedule answers “when is this evidence no longer worth its security, privacy, and storage cost?” That is a better frame than retaining every tool byte forever.

## Concrete translation: the Recovery Information Package (RcIP)

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED current-harness premise: semantic recovery package

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The premise that the current harness lacks an equivalent sealed, self-describing package is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by killing a worker after a side effect and inspecting every artifact available to a fresh worker for an inventory, hashes, provenance, prerequisites, and explicit next action**. The proposal remains useful as a design specification even if an existing feature partially implements it.

At semantically meaningful boundaries—accepted plan, verified milestone, before/after an externally visible side effect, explicit pause, context compaction, normal session close—the worker writes one small, versioned RcIP. Use an atomic write-to-new-file followed by rename. Do not checkpoint every tool call.

Minimum package contents:

- `package_version`, package ID, task/goal ID, parent package ID, creator agent/session ID, creation time, and checkpoint cause.
- Owner intent, acceptance criteria, current scope boundaries, authorization constraints, and safety constraints.
- State enum: `planned | executing | verifying | waiting-external | complete-claimed | abandoned`; the last *verified* milestone; unresolved questions; and one concrete next executable action.
- Artifact manifest: canonical locator, role, byte size, hash, creation/last-validation event, and whether it is source, derived output, evidence, or disposable scratch.
- Workspace identity: repository identity, revision/base identity, working-tree/diff digest, relevant untracked-artifact entries, and path mapping. Store references and hashes, not a blind duplicate of the repository.
- Dependency/Representation Information: required tools and versions, schema versions, commands or skills needed to interpret artifacts, relevant external object/version IDs, and portability assumptions.
- A concise decision ledger: decision, reason, evidence references, alternatives rejected, and confidence. Do **not** store hidden chain-of-thought; store only operationally necessary conclusions and evidence.
- Side-effect ledger: intended operation, target, idempotency key where supported, issued/acknowledged/verified state, receipt or remote object ID, and compensating action if known.
- Provenance events since the parent: event type, time, agent/tool, input objects, output objects, status/outcome, and evidence pointer.
- Access classification and retention class. Never copy credentials; include only the name of the credential/capability that the successor must reacquire.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that semantic-boundary capture gives a better recovery-value/overhead ratio than per-tool-call snapshots. Evidence: archival appraisal favors selective evidence, and the package’s purpose is to preserve verified state transitions and interpretive context; uncertainty remains because failure timing and task types vary. Settle this with randomized kill tests at both tool and semantic boundaries, comparing successful autonomous resumption, package size, and added latency.

What this catches that an unvalidated transcript or bare checkpoint can miss:

- A package whose bytes are intact but whose referenced files are missing.
- A package from the wrong task lineage, repository, revision, or workspace.
- Partial/torn checkpoint writes and artifacts modified after checkpointing.
- “Done” text with no linked verification event or evidence object.
- A next step that cannot be interpreted because its tool, schema, path mapping, or environment prerequisite is absent.
- An already-issued external mutation that a successor might repeat because acknowledgement was lost.
- State created by an unidentified worker/tool or transformed without a recorded event.
- A package that contains secrets or has outlived its recovery value.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that hashes plus a manifest detect missing, extra, truncated, or altered *listed* artifacts. Evidence: this is the explicit, established use of manifests and fixity checks in digital accession. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that hashes alone detect a semantically wrong plan, a malicious package and matching malicious hash, or an omitted artifact. Evidence: NDSA explicitly limits fixity to bit-level identity and says it is insufficient for understandability. Those cases require independent provenance, policy checks, and semantic validation.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED current-harness premise: accession gate for resumption

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The premise that current recovery accepts state without an accession gate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting packages with a wrong revision, missing artifact, altered hash, unsupported schema, and ambiguous side-effect receipt, then observe whether recovery blocks each one with a specific reason**.

A fresh worker must not treat discovery of an RcIP as successful recovery. It performs an ingest transaction:

1. Resolve newest eligible package by task ID and ancestry, not timestamp alone.
2. Validate schema/version and required fields.
3. Recompute manifest hashes and compare inventory.
4. Validate repository/revision/path mapping and referenced evidence reachability.
5. Check required capabilities without revealing credentials.
6. Reconcile every in-flight side effect with the remote system using its receipt/object ID when possible.
7. Re-evaluate freshness-sensitive facts and mark stale assumptions; do not silently inherit them.
8. Emit `accepted`, `accepted-with-gaps`, or `quarantined`, with reasons and a new provenance event.
9. Before mutation, state the recovered goal, last verified milestone, unresolved risks, and next action in a compact recovery receipt.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that quarantining invalid packages prevents more damage than permissive best-effort continuation in side-effecting tasks. Evidence: archival accession separates receipt from validated ingest, and side-effect ambiguity can produce duplicates; uncertainty comes from the cost of unnecessary stops. Settle it with a test matrix separating read-only tasks from mutation tasks and measure both prevented duplicate/wrong mutations and false quarantines.

The gate should be risk-tiered: integrity or task-identity failure always quarantines; missing optional evidence permits `accepted-with-gaps`; freshness failure forces revalidation; ambiguous irreversible side effect requires reconciliation or human authority. It is **likely (55-80%)** that this tiering avoids unnecessary stops better than a single fail-closed rule while still containing high-consequence faults. Evidence: the failure classes have different consequences and archival appraisal is explicitly selective rather than indiscriminate; the malformed-package test matrix above would settle the false-accept/false-quarantine tradeoff.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED current-harness overlap: PREMIS-lite preservation-event ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Whether existing telemetry already supplies equivalent object-event-agent-outcome linkage is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the blind failure-history reconstruction experiment below**.

Append compact JSONL events using the PREMIS relationship, not the full PREMIS schema: `event_id`, `event_type`, `time`, `agent_id/role`, `input_object_ids`, `output_object_ids`, `outcome`, `evidence`, and `parent_event`. Suggested controlled events are `checkpoint`, `validate`, `transfer`, `ingest`, `resume`, `migrate`, `repair`, `side_effect_issue`, `side_effect_ack`, `verify`, and `dispose`.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this ledger makes recovery disputes diagnosable—especially whether a result was produced, merely claimed, transferred, altered, or validated. Evidence: PREMIS’s object-event-agent linkage was designed to make preservation transformations attributable. The uncertainty is whether existing tool/session events already provide equivalent linkage. Settle it by asking a blind evaluator to reconstruct five injected failure histories from (a) current telemetry and (b) the proposed event ledger, scoring correct chronology and custody attribution.

Do not build a second event-sourcing runtime. The ledger is evidence riding existing hooks; it is not the orchestration source of truth.

### Proposal D — appraisal and retention schedule for recovery material

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Define classes before capture:

- **Active recovery package:** retain while task is active plus a short post-completion rollback window; superseded packages may be thinned after successor acceptance.
- **Outcome evidence:** retain the final package, verification receipts, and externally visible side-effect receipts according to the project’s audit/rollback need.
- **Transient execution detail:** short fixed retention; delete automatically after cutoff unless attached to an unresolved incident.
- **Sensitive recovery material:** avoid capture where possible; otherwise shortest fixed retention and explicit access class.
- **Incident hold:** suspend normal disposition for packages involved in a failure investigation, with an accountable release event.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit fixed cutoffs are more automatable and less ambiguous than “delete when no longer needed.” Evidence: NARA directly identifies individual discretion, over-retention, premature destruction, and automation difficulty as defects of the latter instruction. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that the initially chosen retention durations will be appropriate across task classes. Evidence: archival appraisal depends on function and use, while no workload-specific measurements were taken here. Settle durations by measuring recovery lookback age, incident-investigation lookback age, storage volume, and sensitive-data exposure for 30–90 days, then revise the schedule.

### Proposal E — failure-injection accession drill

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Create a test fixture that kills a worker at randomized boundaries and corrupts one recovery property at a time: torn package; altered artifact; missing artifact; wrong ancestry; moved workspace; tool-version mismatch; stale external fact; issued-but-unacknowledged side effect; acknowledged-but-unrecorded side effect; expired package; and malicious package with internally consistent hashes but untrusted provenance. A fresh worker attempts recovery with no access to the dead session. Score:

- correct task and workspace selected;
- time to first useful verified action;
- percentage of tasks completed without owner restatement;
- duplicate or incorrect external mutations;
- invalid packages rejected for the right reason;
- valid packages falsely rejected;
- evidence sufficient to explain the recovery decision;
- bytes and latency added per checkpoint.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this drill distinguishes bit-integrity success from actual resumability better than checking package creation alone. Evidence: archival guidance separates fixity, understandability, audit, and repair rather than equating them. The remaining uncertainty concerns representativeness of the chosen task suite; use read-only research, code editing, long-running computation, and external mutation cases.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Where the structure matches

1. **A producer may become unavailable.** An archive cannot assume the creator will explain a record later; a recovery worker cannot ask a dead session. Both need bounded, independently interpretable transfer material.
2. **Bytes and meaning fail differently.** Both domains must detect corruption while also preserving context and representation information.
3. **Custody changes.** A new process, session, or machine is a new consumer. Producer success does not prove consumer receipt or usability.
4. **Transformations matter.** Compaction, migration, repair, rebasing, or regeneration can preserve function while changing bytes; provenance must record the transformation and outcome.
5. **Retention has costs and risks.** Recovery data can contain sensitive content and can overwhelm discovery if preserved indiscriminately.

These matches make transfer **very likely (80-90%)** at the information-handoff layer. Evidence is the direct isomorphism between OAIS producer/package/consumer transfer and failed-worker/package/successor transfer, plus the operational manifest-and-ingest procedures cited above. This is an inference from the standards, not a claim that OAIS was designed for agents.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **Archives privilege stable evidence; agents need live control state.** A record is normally preserved as evidence of past activity. An agent recovery package is also an instruction surface that can cause future mutations. Treating it as passive content creates prompt-injection and stale-plan risk.
2. **Authenticity is not correctness.** Provenance and fixity may establish that the package is exactly what a failed worker produced. The worker may still have been wrong.
3. **There is no complete export of model state.** Latent attention state, unarticulated hypotheses, and transient service state cannot be faithfully serialized. Recovery must target useful continuation, not identical continuation.
4. **The environment changes faster.** APIs, branches, locks, credentials, prices, schedules, and owner intent may change between capture and resume. Authentic old state can be dangerously stale.
5. **Archival custody is commonly controlled; recovery packages may cross trust boundaries.** A signed or hashed malicious instruction remains malicious. The consumer must treat package prose as untrusted evidence and reauthorize side effects.
6. **Appraisal can discard the only clue.** Selective retention reduces noise and risk but may eliminate an unexpected diagnostic dependency. Incident holds and measured revision of schedules are necessary.
7. **Same-failure-domain storage is not disaster recovery.** A pristine package on the dead machine does not enable machine-loss recovery. At least one independently reachable copy is required for that threat model, but the destination and authorization are deployment-specific.
8. **Eventual readability is not enough.** Archives can tolerate slower retrieval. Agent orchestration needs low-latency capture and validation, so full descriptive cataloging would be counterproductive.

Because of these disanalogies, it is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** the case that an RcIP can guarantee an identical continuation of the dead worker. Evidence: model latent state is not part of the package, external state can change, and authenticity does not imply correctness. It is **likely (55-80%)** that RcIP can guarantee the narrower property “a successor either receives a verifiably intact, attributable, interpretable resumption basis or gets an explicit reason not to trust it,” provided the package store survives and the accession gate is enforced. Evidence: manifests, hashes, provenance events, representation metadata, and explicit ingest outcomes cover those properties; the band reflects schema omissions, malicious producers, and unavailable dependencies. The failure-injection drill settles this claim operationally.

## Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

All proposals use ordinary files plus existing lifecycle hooks/skills/MCP calls. They do not require a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Rejected alternatives:

- **Full OAIS repository/service:** too broad for task handoff; would be **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and fails the minimal-architecture gate absent evidence that the compact protocol cannot meet recovery objectives.
- **Checkpoint every token or tool event:** high volume, weak appraisal, and still cannot serialize latent model state. A compact event tail may remain diagnostic evidence, but it is not the recovery package.
- **Transcript as source of truth:** chronological text lacks a verified inventory, typed state transition, custody receipt, explicit dispositions, and side-effect reconciliation.
- **Checksum-only recovery:** detects bit changes, not omission, staleness, semantic error, or hostile-but-consistent content.
- **Vector/KG “memory”:** retrieval similarity does not establish authenticity, completeness, ancestry, or custody; it also reintroduces an explicitly rejected component.
- **Durable workflow engine:** may solve a different class of deterministic workflow recovery but introduces a second runtime and cannot itself make agent-produced state semantically adequate.
- **Trained critic:** authenticity and accession should be mechanically checkable where possible. A critic adds probabilistic judgment without replacing hashes, manifests, provenance, or reconciliation.

## Recommended decision

Adopt the archival borrow as a **small handoff protocol**, not an archival platform: RcIP + accession gate + PREMIS-lite event linkage + explicit retention schedule + kill/corruption drills. The core acceptance criterion is not “a checkpoint file exists.” It is: **a fresh worker, without the dead session, can establish package identity and ancestry, verify its material artifacts, understand the last verified state, reconcile pending side effects, and either take one safe useful action or refuse with a precise reason.**

The recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform transcript-only or bare-file recovery on that criterion. Evidence: each asserted property maps to a distinct archival mechanism supported by the cited standards and guidance. The recommendation does not receive a higher band until the experiment demonstrates gains on the owner’s real task distribution.
