# F022 — Frontier research: failure prediction and early-abort heuristics

**Unit:** F022  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** predicting that an already-running tool-using agent episode will fail, detecting non-progress early, and stopping or diverting it without sacrificing successful episodes. This is research and design guidance; the subject harness was read-only and no configuration was changed.

## Executive position

The frontier has two very different maturity levels.

1. **Mechanically grounded stopping is established engineering.** Finite step/token/time/cost limits, propagated deadlines, typed retryability, bounded retries with jitter, circuit breakers/retry quotas, exact action-observation loop detection, explicit terminal conditions, and external success checks all exist in mature systems and real agent frameworks. They do not “predict” semantic failure; they bound blast radius or recognize a state that is already objectively futile. LangGraph has a recursion limit, AutoGen exposes composable message/token/timeout/function-call termination, SWE-agent has per-instance call/cost limits and bounded API retries, and OpenHands ships a default stuck detector for repeated actions/errors and alternating cycles ([LangGraph recursion-limit documentation](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/errors/GRAPH_RECURSION_LIMIT), [AutoGen termination documentation](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/tutorial/termination.html), [SWE-agent model implementation](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/SWE-agent/blob/main/sweagent/agent/models.py), [OpenHands stuck-detector documentation](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/guides/agent-stuck-detector)).

2. **True early prediction remains promising research, not established deployment practice.** The strongest 2026 results use supervised prefix monitors, weakly supervised trajectory models, or hidden-activation probes. They are preprints, usually benchmark-specific, and expose uncomfortable deployment facts: AUPRC can look good while low-false-alarm operation is unusable; failure evidence is often absent until late; repeated gates accumulate false-abort risk; and hidden-state methods do not ride ordinary closed-model tool/hook APIs ([PrefixGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06455), [When Evidence is Sparse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05414), [Doomed from the Start](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)). These are **post-cutoff 2026 preprints and therefore lower-confidence evidence than peer-reviewed, replicated results**.

For this owner, the right architecture is therefore an **asymmetric stopping ladder**:

- deterministic typed evidence may abort;
- strong but non-terminal structural evidence may pause/divert after persistence;
- learned or semantic risk may only advise and collect labels until replay evaluation bounds false aborts;
- every abort must emit a typed reason, the triggering evidence, the remaining budget, and a resumable handoff;
- a detector is not trusted until a known-positive proves its input path and a known-negative measures false firing.

This conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, because it is supported independently by mature distributed-systems controls, multiple agent-framework implementations, the harness’s measured repeated failures, and the harness’s live false-positive incidents. It is not “almost certain” because no published study evaluates this exact hybrid policy on this owner’s workload.

## 1. What is established

### 1.1 Finite budgets and explicit termination conditions are baseline containment

A hard ceiling is not a predictor; it is a proof that the authorized resource budget is exhausted. Its value is that it converts an unbounded failure into a bounded, typed outcome. Current agent frameworks independently expose this primitive:

- LangGraph raises `GRAPH_RECURSION_LIMIT` when a graph reaches its maximum steps before a stop condition and explicitly identifies cycles as a common cause ([official documentation](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/errors/GRAPH_RECURSION_LIMIT)).
- AutoGen implements composable maximum-message, token-usage, timeout, external, stop-message, function-call, and custom termination conditions, evaluated incrementally after responses ([official documentation](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/tutorial/termination.html)).
- SWE-agent tracks per-instance cost, total cost, per-instance call count, and bounded retry attempts; its repository history also contains fixes ensuring cost-limit exceptions are not swallowed by retry logic ([implementation](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/SWE-agent/blob/main/sweagent/agent/models.py), [release history](https://github.com/SWE-agent/SWE-agent/releases)).
- mini-swe-agent’s core agent exposes both step and cost limits in a deliberately small implementation ([implementation](https://github.com/SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent/blob/main/src/minisweagent/agents/default.py)).

What is established is the **containment property**, not an optimal universal number. A 20-step ceiling will stop by step 20; it does not establish that step 20 is the best quality/cost point. Framework documentation itself warns that legitimate complex graphs can hit a default limit ([LangGraph](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/errors/GRAPH_RECURSION_LIMIT)). Threshold selection must therefore be workload-calibrated and must preserve a resumable state.

### 1.2 Deadlines must cover the whole call tree, not merely the waiting client

gRPC’s official guidance is unusually precise: without an explicit deadline a client can wait effectively forever; deadlines improve resource use; a server must stop spawned work after cancellation; and downstream calls should inherit the remaining deadline rather than receive a fresh full timeout ([gRPC deadlines](https://grpc.io/docs/guides/deadlines/)). Applied to agents, a parent episode’s remaining budget must be propagated to subagents, MCP calls, companion reviewers, and long-running commands. Merely timing out the parent while the child continues is not early abort; it is orphan creation.

This is established distributed-systems engineering. The exact translation from a wall-clock deadline to this harness’s mixed interactive/background tasks is **UNVERIFIED; a fixture with one parent, one nested tool, and one intentionally non-cooperative child would settle whether cancellation reaches each layer and whether any process remains**.

### 1.3 Retry only errors that can change without changing the request

Mature retry systems first classify the error, then bound attempts. AWS’s current SDK design distinguishes transient, throttling, and non-retryable failures, uses exponential backoff with full jitter, caps attempts, and maintains a retry token quota that fails fast during sustained disruption ([AWS retry behavior](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sdkref/latest/guide/feature-retry-behavior.html)). The retry quota is important: it treats retries as a limited shared resource, not an entitlement. The AWS page was updated for 2026 behavior, so its **specific default values are post-cutoff and lower confidence for portability**, while the underlying bounded/backoff/jitter/error-classification pattern is longstanding and also documented in the older AWS Builders’ Library ([timeouts, retries, backoff, and jitter](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter/)).

For an agent harness, “retryable” should mean the same normalized request could plausibly succeed because external state changed: a throttle window elapsed, a temporary transport reset cleared, or the service explicitly supplied retry timing. Schema validation failures, authentication/authorization failures, command parse failures, missing required preconditions, and a server explicitly reported not connected are terminal **for an identical retry**. Some have a deterministic remediation transition—read the file, repair the JSON, reauthenticate, select a live endpoint—but that is a new action, not a retry.

An ambiguous lost response after a mutating call requires idempotency or a read-after-write reconciliation before retry. This follows the general retry-safety problem: repeating an operation whose first result is unknown may duplicate side effects ([AWS Builders’ Library](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter/)).

### 1.4 Circuit breakers are useful, but only when scoped and recoverable

A circuit breaker stops repeatedly calling a dependency judged unhealthy, then probes recovery after a cooldown. Microsoft’s architecture guidance describes the closed/open/half-open states and warns that breaker thresholds and recovery timing must match the operation ([Azure Circuit Breaker pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/circuit-breaker)). AWS retry quotas implement a related fail-fast control under broad failure ([AWS retry behavior](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sdkref/latest/guide/feature-retry-behavior.html)).

The established lesson is not “one global breaker.” Scope matters. A breaker keyed only by tool family can suppress healthy resources because one endpoint failed; AWS explicitly warns that adaptive limiting shared across multiple resources slows unaffected resources ([AWS retry behavior](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sdkref/latest/guide/feature-retry-behavior.html)). For this harness, keys should include provider/server and normalized failure class, with operation/resource where throttling is resource-specific.

### 1.5 Structural stuck detection is real, cheap, and deployed

OpenHands enables a stuck detector by default and documents five structural patterns: repeated action-observation, repeated action-error, monologue, alternating action-observation cycles, and repeated context-window errors. It compares semantic event fields while ignoring IDs/metrics and can halt execution ([official OpenHands documentation](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/guides/agent-stuck-detector)). Its documented thresholds—four identical action-observation pairs, three action-error cycles, and six alternating cycles—are implementation choices, not universal scientific constants.

The owner’s harness independently implements exact tool+argument+result repetition, normalized same-shape results with varying arguments, ABAB alternation, low-information windows, and repeat-read memoization (`modules.py:1848-1997`). This convergence between a widely used coding-agent implementation and local design makes structural signals more credible than an unconstrained LLM “are we stuck?” prompt. However, no source found reports a cross-workload false-abort study for OpenHands’s detector. Therefore, efficacy beyond catching constructed/repeated cycles is **UNVERIFIED; replay a labeled sample of this owner’s successful and failed long trajectories through both algorithms and report time-to-alert and false-abort rate to settle it**.

### 1.6 External verification is stronger than intrinsic self-critique

Intrinsic self-correction—asking a model to find and fix its own mistake without new evidence—has mixed or negative results. The ICLR 2024 study *Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet* found intrinsic self-correction often degraded reasoning performance ([paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Abstract-Conference.html)). A critical TACL survey concluded that successful prompted self-correction generally depends on reliable external feedback or task settings unusually suited to verification ([survey](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00713/125177/When-Can-LLMs-Actually-Correct-Their-Own-Mistakes)). LLM judges also exhibit position and verbosity biases ([MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena judge study](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf), [position-bias study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)).

For early abort, the implication is direct: test exit status, schema validity, a changed file hash, an objective task receipt, a health endpoint, or explicit user denial is stronger evidence than the same model’s prose confidence. An LLM critic can suggest a different tactic; it should not be the sole irreversible abort authority unless separately calibrated on the deployment distribution.

### 1.7 Failure alerting is a selective-decision problem, not a raw-accuracy contest

Selective classification formalizes the trade between coverage and risk: rejecting more cases can reduce error among accepted cases ([JMLR foundations](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/v11/el-yaniv10a.html), [NeurIPS selective classification](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html)). Conformal risk control can calibrate thresholds to bound monotone risks such as false-negative rate under its assumptions ([ICLR 2024 Conformal Risk Control](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f3549ef9b5ff520a7e41ff3cc306ab2b-Abstract-Conference.html)).

For an abort gate, the critical deployment metric is **successful-episode survival** (one minus false-abort rate), jointly across all rounds, paired with compute/time saved and alert earliness. Per-step accuracy or AUROC can conceal an unacceptable number of good trajectories killed. Repeated gates compound false-abort risk; the 2026 hidden-state cascade explicitly optimizes a global episode-level recall target rather than independent round thresholds ([Doomed from the Start](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)). That particular agent application is new, but the underlying risk/coverage framing is established.

## 2. Promising but unproven

All items in this section are **post-cutoff 2026 preprints unless stated otherwise**. They are evidence of a direction, not evidence that this harness should deploy the method.

### 2.1 Typed prefix monitors learned from terminal outcomes

PrefixGuard converts heterogeneous traces to typed steps and trains GRU/Transformer/FSM prefix-risk monitors. It reports AUPRC 0.900 on WebArena but only 0.533 on SkillsBench and 0.557 on TerminalBench; crucially, its own first-alert analysis says strong WebArena ranking did not yield usable low-false-alarm alerts ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06455)).

**Confidence: likely (55-80%)** that a typed prefix monitor can rank some of this harness’s failures earlier than terminal outcome, because it improved over raw-text controls across four benchmarks and typed event normalization matches this harness’s event bus. **UNVERIFIED whether it can meet an acceptable successful-run survival rate here; chronological replay with owner outcomes, per-task-family splits, and a required false-abort upper bound would settle it.** The low TerminalBench AUPRC and WebArena low-false-alarm failure prevent a stronger band.

### 2.2 Hidden-activation probe cascades

*Doomed from the Start* reports that per-round linear probes over internal activations on Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3.2-3B in TextCraft could preserve stated 90-97% successful-episode recall targets and save substantial compute; behavioral-only monitors saved about half as much. The abstract says code “will be released soon” ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)).

**Confidence: roughly even (45-55%)** that the savings generalize to coding/research agents, because the result is only two relatively small open models in one environment, the code was not available at research time, and internal representations can change across models and post-training. **UNVERIFIED replication and portability; release the code, reproduce the tables, then train/test across model-version and task-family holdouts to settle it.** It also requires inference-time hidden states, which ordinary hosted agent APIs do not expose in their documented response objects; this unit did not perform a two-signal API-capability audit, so host availability is **UNVERIFIED; inspect the exact Claude/Codex response schemas and run one metadata-capture call to settle it**.

### 2.3 Sparse-evidence weak supervision

*When Evidence is Sparse* learns turn relevance from trajectory-level labels and reports that high-relevance failure evidence occupies only 4.7-11.3% of turns and first appears after 59.0-83.6% of trajectories on average across five benchmarks; its attention predictor and preference-conditioned stopping policy improve Pareto-frontier hypervolume over baselines ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05414)).

**Confidence: likely (55-80%)** that “label every prefix with the final failure label” is a bad training assumption for long tool trajectories, because failures can be introduced late and the study observes sparse/delayed evidence across varied tasks. **UNVERIFIED on this owner’s traces; annotate the earliest causally sufficient failure evidence on a stratified sample and compare naive-prefix versus sparse-evidence models to settle it.** The paper is evidence against aggressive first-turn aborting from behavior alone.

### 2.4 Conformal temporal concepts and failure directions

The conformal-interpretability work reports linearly separable success/failure temporal concepts in hidden states on ScienceWorld and ALFWorld and preliminary steering results ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19775)).

**Confidence: unlikely (20-45%)** that these exact latent directions transfer into a practical closed-model harness abort gate, because the evaluations are simulated environments, steering is preliminary, and model internals are required. **UNVERIFIED transfer; a preregistered cross-model, cross-task holdout with frozen probes and episode-level false-abort bounds would settle it.**

### 2.5 Budget self-awareness and verbalized “impossible” predictions

BAGEN studies whether agents estimate feasibility and remaining spend, reporting optimistic bias and late failure recognition; secondary summaries report token savings from an “impossible” early-stop policy, while the primary OpenReview paper is the appropriate source ([primary paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=27CuUBRFXR)).

**Confidence: unlikely (20-45%)** that an agent’s uncalibrated verbal “impossible” declaration is safe as a hard abort, because current agents recognize infeasibility late and verbal confidence is generally miscalibrated. **UNVERIFIED for the owner; log the declaration in shadow, bin outcomes by declared probability and remaining budget, then compute reliability diagrams and successful-run survival to settle it.** As an advisory handoff signal it is more plausible than as authority.

### 2.6 Static detection of unbounded agentic loops

*When Agents Do Not Stop* proposes IAL-Scan, statically extracts loop dependence graphs, and reports 68 manually confirmed findings among 74 reports across 6,549 repositories ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01641)).

**Confidence: likely (55-80%)** that static checks can find explicit unbounded orchestration paths before runtime, because bounds and feedback edges are program properties and the reported manual precision is high. **UNVERIFIED reproduction and coverage; run the released analyzer, if available, against versioned known-positive and known-negative fixtures and manually audit all findings to settle it.** This is complementary to runtime early abort, not a replacement.

### 2.7 Process reward models and LLM trajectory judges

Process reward models can rank intermediate reasoning steps, and newer generative verifiers report gains on mathematical reasoning benchmarks ([ThinkPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16828)). But verification costs tokens, step labels are expensive, judge biases persist, and mathematical chains are cleaner than heterogeneous tool trajectories.

**Confidence: unlikely (20-45%)** that adding a deployment-time LLM/PRM critic to every harness step yields net value over typed heuristics, because it adds latency/cost and correlated model error without deployment-distribution evidence. **UNVERIFIED; a shadow A/B replay comparing deterministic signals, a small trace classifier, and an LLM judge at equal cost and fixed successful-run survival would settle it.** This direction also collides with the owner’s rejected “trained critic” component unless evidence clears the anti-overfit gate.

## 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

### 3.1 “High AUC/AUPRC means it is ready to abort”

**Tell:** the report gives a ranking metric but omits the operating point: successful-episode survival, false-abort confidence interval, fraction of failures caught before a useful deadline, and net compute saved. PrefixGuard itself demonstrates the trap: high WebArena AUPRC did not imply actionable low-false-alarm alerts ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06455)). A hard-abort product showing only AUC is not deployment evidence.

### 3.2 “We saved 50% of tokens” without counting killed successes

**Tell:** savings are reported only on failed episodes, or at a chosen threshold without the global survival rate across repeated gates. An aborter can save nearly all compute by aborting everything. The hidden-state cascade correctly emphasizes episode-level recall because gate-wise errors accumulate ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)); any system omitting that denominator is incomplete.

### 3.3 “The agent knows when it is stuck” because it can explain a failure

**Tell:** the same model generates, judges, and certifies its own trajectory with no new observation, deterministic verifier, calibration set, or model-family holdout. Intrinsic self-correction often fails or degrades results ([ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Abstract-Conference.html)), and LLM judges have documented biases ([NeurIPS judge study](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)). Fluent diagnosis after the fact is not calibrated online prediction.

### 3.4 “Semantic progress” as one universal scalar

**Tell:** progress is defined by an LLM similarity/judge score over prose rather than task-owned state transitions, and there are no adversarial negatives such as productive repeated tests, polling with changed remote state, or legitimate iterative refinement. This harness has already shown that substring/prose detectors confuse mention with use and can mark successful results failed (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-82`). A universal semantic score is attractive precisely because its false positives are hard to falsify.

### 3.5 “Conformal” used as a magic safety word

**Tell:** the claim mentions coverage guarantees but omits exchangeability/distribution assumptions, calibration/test separation, repeated online decisions, and the precise loss being controlled. Conformal risk control is real mathematics ([ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f3549ef9b5ff520a7e41ff3cc306ab2b-Abstract-Conference.html)); that does not automatically validate a shifted, adaptively sampled agent stream.

### 3.6 Hidden-state probes marketed for hosted agents

**Tell:** no explanation of how per-layer activations are obtained, how model-version changes are detected, or how the probe is recalibrated. The most striking 2026 result is on open models and announced code was not yet released ([Doomed from the Start](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)). An API-agnostic claim that depends on hidden activations is internally inconsistent.

### 3.7 “99% success / 99% savings” from deterministic replay

**Tell:** repetitive tasks are recorded once and replayed, then the result is generalized to open-ended agents. A 2026 preprint makes exactly a combined 99%/99% claim for periodic tasks ([LOOP Skill Engine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14237)). Deterministic replay can be excellent automation for stable periodic workflows, but it is not general early-failure prediction. Look for task diversity, environment drift, held-out procedures, and intervention costs.

### 3.8 More orchestration sold as reliability

**Tell:** the proposed fix adds a second runtime, durable workflow engine, vector/KG memory, judge swarm, or observability SaaS before showing that a typed hook with a small state window cannot solve the measured failure. None of those components is required for deadlines, retry classification, loop fingerprints, or replay scoring. Complexity that cannot identify which measured false abort it reduces is scaffolding, not evidence.

## 4. Specific things this harness should adopt

Priority order is intentional. Each proposal rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus and is checked against the anti-overfit gate.

### P0 — Finite composite episode budgets and propagated remaining budget

**Tag: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Require every autonomous loop/subagent/long-running wrapper to declare finite limits for at least wall time and steps/tool calls, plus tokens or cost where available. The child receives `min(child_requested_budget, parent_remaining_budget)`. Expiry produces a typed `budget_exhausted` handoff with last progress receipt and resumable state; it must cancel descendants, not merely stop waiting. Allow task profiles (interactive, research, reverse engineering), but prohibit an implicit unlimited default.

Expected containment is **almost certain (90-99%)**, because a correctly enforced finite counter/deadline mechanically bounds the tracked resource; evidence is the established framework patterns and gRPC deadline propagation ([AutoGen](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/tutorial/termination.html), [gRPC](https://grpc.io/docs/guides/deadlines/)). **UNVERIFIED threshold quality and descendant cancellation in this harness; replay successful long runs against candidate profiles and run the nested non-cooperative-child fixture to settle both.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. A thin budget/deadline envelope rides existing lifecycle and runner calls and introduces none of the rejected components; task-profile values remain configuration. The <200-line size is **UNVERIFIED; a source-level design spike that maps all child-launch entry points and counts the shared wrapper plus adapters would settle the sizing before implementation**.

### P1 — Typed retry decision table plus per-endpoint retry budget

**Tag: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Normalize tool failures into a small enum:

- `terminal_same_request`: schema/parse/precondition/auth/not-connected/not-found-as-requested; do not repeat unchanged.
- `retryable_transient`: timeout/connection reset/selected 5xx; bounded retry with jitter and remaining-deadline check.
- `throttled`: honor server retry timing or longer jitter; endpoint/resource-scoped budget.
- `unknown_commit`: mutating call lost response; reconcile or use idempotency key before any retry.
- `user_denied`: stop the attempted action and hand control back; never route around denial.

For terminal classes, inject the deterministic remediation (“read first,” “repair schema,” “reauthenticate,” “choose a connected endpoint”), and fingerprint the remediation so repeating the unchanged request is blocked. The corpus’s recurring not-connected, schema, write-before-read, and quoting fingerprints provide the initial regression fixtures (`corpus.json:4281-4404`).

This will **very likely (80-90%)** reduce futile identical retries, because classification/bounded retries are established and the local classes recur. **UNVERIFIED exact savings; replay the affected trajectories and measure prevented calls, successful recovery, and false terminal classifications to settle it.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. One deterministic module on the existing failure hook; no new runtime, learned critic, or SaaS.

### P2 — Close the failure-event hole and make repetition persistent after threshold

**Tag: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Feed `PostToolUseFailure` and permission-denial events into the same per-session anti-degeneration state as successful results. Use typed fields (`tool`, normalized arguments, status/error class, result/state digest), not substring scans of payload prose. Change exact-repeat escalation from equality to persistence (`>= threshold`) with a per-signature latched state, and use a lock or compare-and-swap around the read-modify-write window. Treat repeated read-only calls as advisory first; treat three identical terminal failures with unchanged preconditions as abort/handoff.

The wiring defect is **almost certain (90-99%)** statically: the failure ledger append and anti-degeneration event filter are mechanically independent source paths (`modules.py:1170-1194,1811-1826`), and equality-only thresholding is explicit (`modules.py:1848-1865`). Runtime benefit is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on corpus retry loops and convergent OpenHands patterns ([OpenHands](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/guides/agent-stuck-detector)); **UNVERIFIED false-abort rate; replay all detected loops plus a stratified successful-long-run control to settle it**.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This repairs and extends the existing detector rather than adding a guardrail framework.

### P3 — Typed progress receipts, not a universal semantic-progress judge

**Tag: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Define a small `ProgressReceipt` emitted by existing tools/hooks when objective state changes: file/diff digest changed, failing-test set shrank, new unique source/evidence identifier appeared, task/subtask state advanced, endpoint health changed, or explicit verifier moved closer to acceptance. Store only bounded recent digests. Reset a no-progress counter on a valid receipt. Do not infer progress from reasoning length, prose novelty, or generic embedding distance.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that typed receipts improve discrimination between legitimate repetition and churn, because they bind the detector to external state and directly address the live use-versus-mention failures. **UNVERIFIED coverage across this owner’s diverse tasks; instrument receipts in shadow and manually label a stratified sample of “no receipt for N calls” windows to settle it.** Missing receipt must initially mean “unknown,” not “no progress.”

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. Bounded typed records on the ledger/hook bus; no vector database, KG, critic, or new service.

### P4 — Two-stage advisory/divert/abort policy with explicit evidence strength

**Tag: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Configure detector actions by evidence tier:

1. `advise`: first structural suspicion; show reason and request a materially different tactic.
2. `divert`: persistence after advice; require re-probe, alternate tool, or resumable handoff.
3. `abort`: budget exhausted, user denial, stop-hook re-entry, typed terminal repeat, or identical action+observation+unchanged state after the calibrated threshold.

Learned risk, LLM confidence, low-information heuristics, and normalized “same shape” signals remain advisory until replay evaluation clears an explicit successful-episode survival target.

## Bottom line

The best near-term “failure predictor” for this harness is not a model. It is a small typed state machine that knows: the remaining budget; whether an error is retryable; whether the same action observed the same state; whether objective state changed; whether Stop is re-entering; whether the owner denied the action; and whether acceptance receipts already prove success. That design is **very likely (80-90%)** to capture more verified local value per unit complexity than a learned critic, because every input maps to a measured defect or established control and it rides the existing hook/ledger bus.

The frontier should still be watched. Prefix monitors and hidden-state cascades are credible research directions, especially their focus on episode-level survival rather than raw classifier accuracy. But the adoption gate must be severe: unique lift after deterministic controls, chronological/model-version holdouts, a predeclared upper confidence bound on false aborts, measurable net savings, resumability, and no new production runtime unless that lift is large. Until those conditions are met, “predictive abort” should mean shadow advice; deterministic evidence alone should hold the kill switch.
