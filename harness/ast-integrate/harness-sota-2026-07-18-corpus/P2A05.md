# P2A05 — Automated verification of agent claims

**Unit:** P2A05 — research: verification  
**Date:** 2026-07-18  
**Scope:** External research only. This unit did not inspect or make claims about the local subject harness. It asks what primary literature, official implementations, and official product documentation establish about execution-grounded checking, adversarial verification, refutation-first design, and calibration, then translates that evidence into harness mechanisms.

## Executive conclusion

The literature does not support a universal “LLM verifier” that can be trusted as an oracle. It supports a layered verification design in which a claim is first made small and falsifiable, then checked against a source of information that was not merely copied from the generation process. For software and system-state claims, the best available evidence is direct execution or state inspection. For factual claims, it is retrieved primary evidence assessed at the atomic-claim level. For conclusions that cannot be decided mechanically, independent verification questions, explicit counter-evidence search, and adversarial criticism can improve error discovery, but their verdicts remain fallible. Confidence estimation is useful mainly as a routing signal—verify, defer, or abstain—not as proof that a claim is true.

Two independent research signals support the central external-feedback conclusion. First, CRITIC reports improvements when models use search, code interpreters, and other tools to critique and revise their outputs ([Gou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738)). Second, a separate controlled study finds that intrinsic self-correction without external feedback often fails and can reduce reasoning accuracy ([Huang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). The practical implication is not “add another agent.” It is “add a different information channel.” A second model sampling from the same context is not mechanically independent evidence.

The minimal architecture suggested by the evidence is a **claim contract plus a verification router**, not a second orchestration runtime. Every material completion claim should carry: an atomic claim, its verification class, the evidence-producing action, the observed result, a falsifier/counter-check, and a terminal verdict (`SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, or `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE`). Deterministic checks run first; model judgment is a last-mile interpreter of evidence and never the sole evidence for high-consequence claims.

## 1. What is actually established

### 1.1 Decompose long answers into atomic claims before checking them

Long-form outputs mix correct and incorrect assertions, so a single holistic “looks good” judgment hides partial failure. FActScore operationalizes factual precision as the percentage of atomic facts supported by a knowledge source and validates the approach with human annotations of generated biographies ([Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)). SAFE independently uses an LLM agent to split long-form answers into individual claims, search for evidence, and judge each claim; on roughly 16,000 facts it agreed with crowd annotations 72% of the time, while its stronger “wins 76%” claim came from a random sample of only 100 disagreement cases ([Wei et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802), [official implementation](https://github.com/google-deepmind/long-form-factuality)). ALCE likewise evaluates answer correctness separately from citation completeness and citation correctness, rather than collapsing them into one score ([Gao et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)).

What is established is the value of claim-level localization and separate evidence dimensions. FActScore’s reported automated-estimator error below 2% was measured in its particular biography/knowledge-source setting; it is not evidence of a domain-general verifier with 98% accuracy ([Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)).

### 1.2 External feedback is more reliable than ungrounded self-review

CRITIC’s generate–tool-critique–revise loop improved free-form QA, mathematical program synthesis, and toxicity reduction by exposing the model to search or executable feedback ([Gou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738)). ReAct similarly improved fact verification and multi-hop QA by interleaving reasoning with retrieval actions, so later reasoning could depend on observations rather than parametric memory alone ([Yao et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)).

The negative control matters. Huang et al. found that models asked to correct reasoning from internal knowledge alone often did not improve and sometimes changed correct answers into wrong ones ([Huang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). Chain-of-Verification obtains better results by planning verification questions and answering those questions independently before revising the draft; its experiments cover Wikidata lists, MultiSpanQA, and long-form generation ([Dhuliawala et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495)). Together these results support independence of the information path, not ritualized reflection.

### 1.3 Execution is strong evidence, but only for the property actually exercised

Software benchmarks illustrate both sides. SWE-bench evaluates patches with hidden `FAIL_TO_PASS` and regression `PASS_TO_PASS` tests in reproducible containers ([SWE-bench repository and evaluation harness](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench)). This is stronger evidence than a prose claim that a patch works because the outcome is produced by execution.

However, OpenAI’s human audit of the original SWE-bench samples found underspecified tasks and tests that could reject valid solutions or be gamed; 68.3% of reviewed samples were filtered from the Verified subset for underspecification, unfair tests, or other issues ([OpenAI, “Introducing SWE-bench Verified”](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/)). OpenAI’s 2026 follow-up reports that SWE-bench Verified itself later ceased to provide meaningful signal because of design and contamination problems, again requiring prompt/test/patch/trace auditing ([OpenAI, “Separating signal from noise in coding evaluations”](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/)). These are mechanically independent signals: one concerns test execution as an oracle; the other is human inspection of the oracle’s specification and contamination.

Therefore `tests passed` supports the narrow claim “these tests passed in this environment at this revision.” It does not by itself establish “the owner’s requirement is fully satisfied,” “no regression exists,” or “the implementation is secure.” The gap is specification coverage, not an argument against tests.

### 1.4 Verification needs three verdicts, not forced yes/no

FEVER formalized claim verification as `SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, or `NOT ENOUGH INFO`, with evidence recorded for supported and refuted claims ([Thorne et al., 2018](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/)). FEVEROUS carries the same structure across prose, tables, and lists, and its score combines verdict accuracy with evidence retrieval rather than rewarding a naked label ([Aly et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.fever-1.1/)). This prevents failure-to-find from being silently converted into proof of absence.

The need for `NOT ENOUGH INFO` is not merely taxonomic. Glockner et al. show that many fact-checking datasets lack realistic, non-leaked counter-evidence and that systems can appear effective by exploiting evidence copied from existing fact-checking articles ([Glockner et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13865)). A verifier must distinguish “I searched correctly and found refuting evidence” from “my retrieval path did not surface anything.”

### 1.5 Citations improve inspectability but do not themselves establish truth

ALCE separates citation correctness (does the cited passage entail the claim?) from citation completeness (are all claims supported?) and answer quality; even its best evaluated models lacked complete citation support about half the time on ELI5 ([Gao et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)). The separation is essential: a real URL can be irrelevant, a passage can support only part of a sentence, and a fully cited answer can still omit decisive counter-evidence.

Human factors add another limit. In an 80-participant study, LLM explanations made fact checking faster at similar accuracy to search, but users over-relied on explanations when they were convincingly wrong; showing contrastive true/false explanations reduced over-reliance but did not significantly outperform search ([Si et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12558)). Evidence excerpts and direct source access must remain visible to the final reviewer.

### 1.6 Adversarial criticism can expose errors, but a critic is not an oracle

CriticGPT was trained on human-inserted and naturally occurring code errors. Human+CriticGPT critiques were preferred over unassisted human critiques more than 60% of the time, and CriticGPT critiques were preferred to ordinary ChatGPT critiques in 63% of naturally occurring bug cases ([OpenAI, 2024](https://openai.com/index/finding-gpt4s-mistakes-with-gpt-4/), [paper](https://cdn.openai.com/llm-critics-help-catch-llm-bugs-paper.pdf)). The useful outcome is increased flaw discovery by a human/model team, not autonomous certification.

Prover–verifier games offer a stronger adversarial design: a “sneaky” prover tries to make an incorrect solution pass, and those exploits become training data for the verifier. OpenAI found this improved robustness and made grade-school-math solutions easier for humans to evaluate, but the experiment used one dataset, ground-truth labels, and specially trained GPT-4-family models ([Kirchner et al., 2024](https://openai.com/index/prover-verifier-games-improve-legibility/), [paper](https://cdn.openai.com/prover-verifier-games-improve-legibility-of-llm-outputs/legibility.pdf)). The established result is narrow: adversarially generated counterexamples can strengthen a verifier on a labeled task.

### 1.7 Generic LLM judging has systematic biases

A study of 12 judge models, roughly 40 generator models, 22 tasks, and more than 100,000 pairwise evaluations found non-random position bias whose magnitude varied by judge and task ([Shi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)). A separate study reports self-preference and a tendency to reward lower-perplexity/familiar text more than human evaluators do ([Wataoka et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819)). These are mechanically independent measurements of different judge biases.

Consequently, an LLM verdict without external evidence is another claim requiring verification. Pairwise order randomization, identity blinding, and repeated judgments can measure some instability, but agreement among correlated judges is not ground truth.

### 1.8 Confidence can route work, but it cannot certify correctness

Kadavath et al. found useful self-evaluation and calibration on multiple-choice/true-false tasks in favorable formats, while `P(IK)` calibration degraded out of distribution and sometimes failed badly on new tasks ([Kadavath et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)). Mahaut et al. compared factual-confidence estimators and found that trained hidden-state probes were strongest in their evaluation, but also that confidence was often unstable under meaning-preserving prompt variations ([Mahaut et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13415), [code](https://github.com/amazon-science/factual-confidence-of-llms)).

Semantic entropy detects confabulations by sampling semantically distinct answers and measuring uncertainty at the meaning level. It improved error detection and selective answering across the paper’s QA settings, but the authors explicitly state that it does not detect systematic errors, misleading behavior, or confidently wrong answers ([Farquhar et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)). Conformal abstention can bound error under its statistical assumptions on evaluated QA distributions, but it trades errors for abstentions and depends on a calibrated equivalence/correctness function ([Yadkori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01563)).

The established use is **selective verification**: uncertainty raises the verification budget or triggers abstention. It is not a license to mark a high-confidence output `SUPPORTED` without evidence.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 Refutation-first experiment generation

Popper asks agents to translate a hypothesis into measurable implications, design falsification experiments, execute them, and use sequential testing to control Type-I error across six scientific domains ([Huang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09858), [official repository](https://github.com/snap-stanford/POPPER)). **Likely (55-80%)** that the core pattern—write the disconfirming observation before running the check—transfers to software and operational claims, because it converts vague review into a falsifiable test and matches the independent evidence from FEVER’s explicit refutation class and CRITIC’s tool feedback. Transfer to this owner’s general-purpose harness is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is a randomized replay of historical completion claims comparing ordinary review with predeclared falsifiers, scored by later owner corrections.

The full Popper statistical machinery is not justified for a coding harness. Its sequential hypothesis-testing guarantees apply to data-generating settings with explicit statistical assumptions, not arbitrary natural-language completion claims.

### 2.2 Adversarial test generation after ordinary tests pass

CodeHacker reports that targeted stress tests and adversarial corner cases expose incorrect competitive-programming solutions that existing tests accept ([Shi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20213)). **Likely (55-80%)** that a bounded “try to break the claimed property” pass will find defects missed by happy-path tests, because SWE-bench’s audits independently show that test suites can be underspecified or gameable. The 2026 result is a recent preprint and domain-specific, so broad transfer is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is to sample 50 completed harness changes, generate one claim-targeted negative test per change, and measure unique valid defects per additional minute.

### 2.3 Debate for ambiguous, evidence-rich decisions

Debate over controversial claims improved human judgment accuracy and confidence calibration relative to single-advisor consultancy by 10% overall in one 2025 study ([Rahman et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02175)). DebateCV reports gains for multi-agent claim verification but requires a specially trained moderator because zero-shot moderators tend toward neutral judgments ([He et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19090)). **Roughly even (45-55%)** that generic two-agent debate is cost-effective for this harness: opposing searches can surface counter-evidence, but the gains may come from additional retrieval and compute rather than debate itself, while judge bias remains. This is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is an equal-token A/B/C test of single verifier, independent counter-evidence search, and debate on a labeled set of previously corrected claims.

### 2.4 Adversarial red-teaming of the verifier itself

DECEIVE-AFC reports that input-only adversarial claims reduced search-enabled fact-checker accuracy from 78.7% to 53.7% across its evaluated systems ([Ou et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02569)). Anthropic’s SLEIGHT-Bench constructs subtle agent transcripts that exploit monitor blind spots and reports that frontier monitors miss many of them ([Najt et al., 2026](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/sleight-bench/)). **Likely (55-80%)** that verifier-specific mutation tests will reveal brittle retrieval and judging behavior, based on these independent attack surfaces. Both results are recent and use constructed benchmarks, so the magnitude for ordinary owner work is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is to mutate claim wording, evidence order, source authority cues, and irrelevant context while holding ground truth fixed, then measure verdict invariance.

### 2.5 Empirically calibrated abstention thresholds

**Very likely (80-90%)** that an empirical “do not conclude” threshold can reduce false confident conclusions on a stable, labeled task distribution, because conformal abstention demonstrated error control on multiple QA datasets and semantic entropy improved selective-answering curves ([Yadkori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01563); [Farquhar et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)). Generalization across model upgrades, prompt changes, and task types is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is rolling calibration with time-ordered holdouts, reporting risk–coverage curves and recalibrating after any model or policy change.

## 3. What is hype, and how to tell

### 3.1 “The model reviewed its answer, so it is verified”

This confuses another generation with another signal. Intrinsic self-correction can degrade reasoning ([Huang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)), whereas tool-grounded feedback improves it in CRITIC ([Gou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738)). The tell is that no new observation enters between draft and verdict.

### 3.2 “Several agents agreed, so the claim is true”

Agreement can reflect shared model priors, shared retrieval results, shared prompt framing, or the same blind spot. Position bias and self-preference are independently measured in LLM judges ([Shi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791); [Wataoka et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819)). The tell is that “independence” means separate calls but not separate evidence mechanisms.

### 3.3 “It has citations, so it is grounded”

ALCE shows citation completeness and entailment are separate failure modes, with substantial incomplete support even in strong systems ([Gao et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)). The tell is evaluation that counts URLs or citation markers without checking whether the exact passage entails the exact atomic claim and without seeking counter-evidence.

### 3.4 “The automatic factuality evaluator is better than humans”

SAFE’s headline is based on 72% agreement across approximately 16,000 facts and a 76% win rate on only 100 sampled disagreement cases ([Wei et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). FActScore’s below-2% automated-estimation error is tied to its evaluated biography/knowledge-source setup ([Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)). The tell is a domain-general claim extrapolated from one benchmark, one source corpus, sampled disagreements, or model-assisted labels.

### 3.5 “Debate forces truth to win”

Prover–verifier games improved legibility on grade-school math with ground truth and trained participants, and the authors explicitly identify the single-dataset scope ([Kirchner et al., 2024](https://openai.com/index/prover-verifier-games-improve-legibility/)). Theoretical debate results depend on assumptions about provers, verifiers, protocols, and judges ([Kovařík and Carey, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04266)). The tell is an empirical claim made from a game-theoretic possibility result, or a debate comparison without equalizing retrieval and token budgets.

### 3.6 “The confidence number is the probability the answer is correct”

Calibration depends on format and distribution; it degrades out of distribution, is unstable under equivalent phrasings, and semantic entropy misses systematic errors ([Kadavath et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221); [Mahaut et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13415); [Farquhar et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)). The tell is a confidence percentage with no labeled calibration set, reliability curve, task/version boundary, or risk–coverage report.

### 3.7 “All tests passed, therefore the task is done”

Test execution is evidence for exercised behavior, not an oracle for unstated intent. SWE-bench required large human filtering because issue descriptions and tests were often inadequate ([OpenAI, 2024](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/)), and later contamination/design analysis again invalidated apparent benchmark signal ([OpenAI, 2026](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/)). The tell is a completion statement that does not name the tested property, environment, revision, negative case, and untested remainder.

## 4. Specific things this owner’s harness should adopt

Every proposal below applies the anti-overfit gate. None introduces a second orchestration runtime, vector/KG memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

### 4.1 Atomic claim contract with three terminal verdicts — CONFIG (settings/JSON only) — UNVERIFIED

Require every material “done,” “absent,” “broken,” “fixed,” or factual conclusion to be represented internally as:

```json
{
  "claim": "one falsifiable proposition",
  "class": "state|execution|source|reasoning|absence",
  "support_action": "exact command/query/check",
  "support_observation": "result identifier or quoted evidence",
  "falsifier": "observation that would disprove it",
  "countercheck_action": "mechanically independent check",
  "verdict": "SUPPORTED|REFUTED|NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE",
  "scope": "environment/revision/time/domain"
}
```

Do not require this verbose object in normal user prose; retain it in the task record or verification output and summarize only the decisive fields. Atomic decomposition is supported by FActScore and SAFE, while the three-way verdict is supported by FEVER/FEVEROUS ([Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251); [Wei et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802); [Thorne et al., 2018](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/)).

**Expected effect:** **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce unsupported global conclusions because it makes evidence gaps and `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE` explicit; evidence is the convergence of three independently developed evaluation families. Owner-specific effect is **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: label 100 historical corrected conclusions and replay half with the contract, scoring unsupported-claim rate and added latency.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is instruction/schema configuration on the existing bus, not a runtime.

### 4.2 Deterministic-first verification router — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED

Implement a stateless script that maps claim class to the strongest cheap verifier:

| Claim class | First verifier | Required independent countercheck |
|---|---|---|
| File/state exists | exact-path metadata/read | content or alternate API/query field |
| Text/config present | exact parser or fixed-string search | parse/load the owning format |
| Command succeeded | exit code plus captured output | inspect produced state/artifact |
| Code works | targeted reproduction/test | negative/adversarial case or broader regression test |
| External factual claim | primary-source passage | independently retrieved source or counter-evidence query |
| Absence | detector with known-present positive control | second mechanism or data field |
| Completion | enumerate acceptance claims | verify each and reject on `REFUTED`/`NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE` |

The router should emit only terminal records such as `SUPPORTED:<claim-id>`, `REFUTED:<claim-id> <reason>`, or `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE:<claim-id> <missing-observation>`. It should never ask an LLM to replace an available parser, exit code, test, hash, or state query. CRITIC and ReAct support tool-grounded feedback; SWE-bench demonstrates executable evaluation; the SWE-bench audits demonstrate the need to pair execution with specification checks ([Gou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738); [Yao et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629); [OpenAI, 2024](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/)).

**Expected effect:** **very likely (80-90%)** to improve evidential quality for claims with deterministic verifiers, based on execution-grounded results and the negative evidence on intrinsic self-correction. Coverage across all owner tasks is **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: shadow-run the router for two weeks and report routed fraction, valid catches, false blocks, and cost.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes if stateless and under roughly 200 lines. Reject persistence, queues, or a verifier daemon.

### 4.3 Native Stop verification against actual state — NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it) — UNVERIFIED

Use a Claude Code `Stop` hook only for tasks that generated material claims. Claude Code officially supports command, prompt, and experimental agent hooks; an agent-based `Stop` hook can inspect files and run commands, and returning `ok:false` feeds the reason back so the agent continues ([Claude Code hooks guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide), [hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)). Configure it to consume the claim contract and invoke the deterministic router. Prefer a command hook for deterministic checks; use the experimental agent hook only when evidence collection genuinely requires tool-directed inspection, consistent with Anthropic’s recommendation to prefer command hooks in production ([Claude Code hooks guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide)).

The Stop verifier must not ask “does this look complete?” It must ask “for each acceptance claim, what observation establishes it, what countercheck ran, and what remains `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE`?” It must be bounded to one verification pass plus one repair pass to avoid review loops.

**Expected effect:** **likely (55-80%)** to catch unsupported completion claims at the lifecycle point where they matter, because the native hook can inspect actual state. Reliability on this Windows installation and interaction with existing hooks is **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: canary on one project with 20 seeded false completion claims, confirming hook firing through both the hook debug log and expected blocked-stop behavior.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This enables a native lifecycle bus and calls the small router; it adds no orchestration layer.

### 4.4 Refutation-first countercheck policy — CONFIG (settings/JSON only) — UNVERIFIED

Before accepting a material claim, require one sentence defining what would disprove it and one bounded attempt to obtain that observation. Concrete policies:

- For “fixed”: reproduce the original failure first, then show it no longer occurs, then try the nearest boundary/negative case.
- For “absent”: first demonstrate the detector finds a known-present control, then use a different mechanism for the target.
- For “source says X”: search the source for direct support, then issue a separate contradiction/counter-evidence query.
- For “done”: enumerate acceptance conditions and actively seek one unmet condition before concluding.
- For “safe”: search for a violating trace or input; never infer safety from lack of an observed failure.

This adapts Popper’s falsification orientation without importing its scientific multi-agent/statistical runtime ([Huang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09858)). It is also consistent with evidence that contrastive explanations reduce human over-reliance ([Si et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12558)) and that realistic fact checking depends on obtaining sufficient counter-evidence ([Glockner et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13865)).

**Expected effect:** **likely (55-80%)** to reduce confirmation-biased conclusions. Transfer is **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: compare ordinary versus refutation-first verification on a balanced labeled set containing true claims, false claims, and insufficient-evidence cases, measuring false-support rate rather than overall accuracy alone.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is a rule on the existing reasoning/tool bus.

### 4.5 Bounded adversarial test mutation after a green check — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED

For high-consequence code or configuration changes, generate at most three claim-targeted mutations after ordinary tests pass: boundary input, invariant violation, and environment/state variation. Run them in the same sandbox and retain only tests that are valid under the stated requirement. The model proposes adversarial cases; execution decides them. Never let the model rewrite the production verifier or test oracle during this phase.

CodeHacker provides recent evidence that targeted adversarial cases can expose accepted-but-incorrect programs ([Shi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20213)); SWE-bench’s audits independently show test-oracle weaknesses ([OpenAI, 2024](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/)).

**Expected effect:** **likely (55-80%)** to find additional boundary defects in code-heavy tasks, with magnitude **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: measure unique valid failures per 100 post-green mutations and reject the mechanism if precision is below a preset threshold or median latency exceeds its defect value.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes only as a bounded post-test script. Do not create a fuzzing service, search tree, or durable execution engine.

### 4.6 Evidence-visible source verification — CONFIG (settings/JSON only) — UNVERIFIED

For external claims, require citations to the exact supporting page and preserve a short evidence excerpt or line reference in the verification record. Check separately:

1. source identity and authority;
2. passage entailment of the atomic claim;
3. coverage of every material clause;
4. freshness/time scope;
5. counter-evidence from an independently formulated retrieval query.

A URL count is never a score. ALCE’s separate citation metrics and the human over-reliance study support keeping evidence inspectable rather than presenting only fluent explanations ([Gao et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/); [Si et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12558)).

**Expected effect:** **very likely (80-90%)** to make unsupported or partially supported factual claims easier to detect; evidence comes from both automatic citation evaluation and human fact-checking behavior. The owner-specific burden/benefit ratio is **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: audit 50 research answers for atomic support coverage and reviewer time before and after the rule.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is output/evidence policy over existing web and MCP tools.

### 4.7 Calibrated routing, not decorative confidence — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED

Keep the mandated qualitative confidence bands for communication, but route verification using observed error rates on labeled historical claims. Maintain a small versioned table by claim class and verifier version: count, false-support rate, false-refute rate, and `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE` rate. Use it to raise verification depth or abstain; do not display pseudo-precise probabilities unless calibration has been measured on the current distribution.

Minimum policy:

- unknown/new class: mandatory deterministic or two-source verification;
- historically high false-support class: deeper countercheck or human escalation;
- unstable verdict under paraphrase/order changes: `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE`;
- model/tool/version change: invalidate old calibration until a rolling holdout is collected.

This follows the demonstrated OOD and paraphrase instability of confidence estimators while using abstention as the safe action ([Kadavath et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221); [Mahaut et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13415); [Yadkori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01563)).

**Expected effect:** **likely (55-80%)** to improve allocation of verification effort once enough labeled owner data exists. That data sufficiency is **UNVERIFIED**; experiment: build risk–coverage curves on time-ordered claims and require prospective error control before using thresholds operationally.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes if the table is a flat file and calculations are stateless. Do not add a learned critic, vector database, or calibration service.

### 4.8 Do not adopt default multi-agent debate or a trained verifier — BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification) — REJECT

This proposal is intentionally classified and rejected. A standing debate council, trained critic, or verifier service would reintroduce explicitly disallowed components and add correlated-model cost before the harness has measured whether simple counter-evidence search wins. Debate’s empirical evidence is task-specific, moderator-dependent, and confounded by extra computation ([Rahman et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02175); [He et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19090)); CriticGPT required specialized training data ([OpenAI, 2024](https://openai.com/index/finding-gpt4s-mistakes-with-gpt-4/)).

**Decision confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that rejecting this as the default is correct under the anti-overfit gate, because smaller external-evidence mechanisms have direct support and lower architectural cost. A narrowly scoped equal-budget experiment may still test debate later.

## 5. Recommended verification sequence

This section introduces no additional proposal; it composes the already tagged proposals in §4 into one bounded flow, and each step inherits the corresponding §4 classification and UNVERIFIED settling experiment:

1. **Extract** only material completion, absence, breakage, fix, safety, and factual claims.
2. **Atomize** compound claims and assign a verification class.
3. **Predeclare the falsifier** and scope.
4. **Run the strongest deterministic check** available.
5. **Run one mechanically independent countercheck** for absence/breakage/done claims; validate detectors with a known-present control.
6. **For external facts, inspect primary evidence** and separately seek counter-evidence.
7. **For green high-consequence code checks, try bounded adversarial cases.**
8. **Emit `SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, or `NOT-ENOUGH-EVIDENCE`.** Never coerce missing evidence into support.
9. **Route uncertainty to more checking or abstention**, never directly to acceptance.
10. **At Stop, block only on a refuted required acceptance claim or dangerous unsupported claim;** otherwise report the evidence gap plainly.

This sequence is deliberately not a universal “critic loop.” It terminates on observable states, limits model judgment to evidence interpretation, and gives later audits a falsifiable record.

## 6. What could not be determined

1. **No externally published evaluation was found that measures this exact owner’s distribution of agent completion claims.** This is not a claim that none exists; it records the boundary of this unit’s searches. The settling experiment is the historical replay proposed in §4.1, using owner corrections as labels.

2. **The literature does not provide a stable cross-domain threshold for when a second verification pass is cost-effective.** Published results use different tasks, models, error definitions, and token budgets. The settling experiment is a risk-stratified shadow deployment reporting defects found per unit of latency/cost.

3. **The degree of error correlation between same-model generator and verifier on this owner’s tasks could not be determined.** Position and self-preference biases establish mechanisms, not the local correlation coefficient. The settling experiment is to compare same-model, different-model, and deterministic verifiers on identical labeled claims with evidence held fixed.

4. **Whether Claude Code’s experimental agent-based Stop hooks are reliable enough on this Windows environment could not be determined from official documentation.** Anthropic documents the capability and explicitly labels agent hooks experimental ([hooks guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide)). The settling experiment requires both a known firing control in debug logs and a behavioral blocked-stop control; repeating one probe is insufficient.

5. **Recent 2026 adversarial-verification results are not yet mature enough to treat effect sizes as stable.** DECEIVE-AFC, CodeHacker, and SLEIGHT-Bench are valuable frontier signals, but each uses constructed/domain-specific evaluations ([Ou et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02569); [Shi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20213); [Najt et al., 2026](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/sleight-bench/)). Independent replication on ordinary agent work would settle transfer.

6. **Formal conformal guarantees cannot be carried into this harness without testing their assumptions.** Exchangeability, a stable error label, and a calibrated equivalence/correctness function are substantive requirements, especially under model and task drift ([Yadkori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01563)). The settling experiment is prospective time-split calibration with explicit assumption checks and risk–coverage reporting.

## 7. Source-quality and method notes

- Primary sources were prioritized: peer-reviewed ACL/EMNLP/ICLR/Nature papers, arXiv manuscripts when newer peer-reviewed versions were not available, official research pages, official repositories, and official Claude Code documentation.
- Claims from lab announcements were narrowed to the reported experiment and paired with papers or independent literature where possible. Product pages were used for product capabilities and first-party benchmark audits, not treated as independent validation of model superiority.
- The strongest conclusions above require convergence across mechanically different signals: execution versus human specification audit; external-tool correction versus intrinsic self-correction; citation metrics versus human over-reliance; and two distinct measurements of LLM-judge bias.
- Recent preprints are placed in “promising but unproven,” assigned mandatory confidence bands, and paired with settling experiments.
- Search-augmented evaluators are not treated as primary sources for the factual claims they verify; their papers are evidence about evaluator behavior only.
