# X143 — Cross-domain borrow: cartography → verification

## Unit boundary and result

This is an ideation-lens result. Per the task, it does not inspect or make claims about the local harness, corpus, audit, or subject tree. Its evidence is cartographic literature and standards, cited by URL. The question is narrowly whether a mechanism developed for cartographic generalisation, scale-dependent detail, and deliberate omission can help verify an agent's claims without trusting the agent and without requiring a human to read the entire evidence base.

**Result:** the analogy transfers, but only halfway. The useful mechanism is a **multiple-representation database (MRDB) with explicit cross-scale object links, declared generalisation operators, and invariant/quality constraints**. Its harness translation is an evidence-backed **claim pyramid**: atomic observations at the most detailed level, deterministic derived views at coarser levels, explicit records of aggregation and omission, and automated tests of cross-level consistency. This is a strong mechanism for detecting whether a report faithfully represents the evidence it cites. It does **not** establish that the bottom-level evidence is true of the outside world. A map can consistently generalise a wrong survey; likewise, a claim pyramid can consistently generalise a bad test, stale file, spoofed tool result, or inadequate source.

The implementation recommendation is therefore conditional: use this borrow as a **representation-integrity verifier and attention router**, never as a universal truth oracle.

## The cartographic mechanism, in its own field

### 1. Generalisation is controlled loss, not miniature reproduction

Maps have a finite display budget. The USGS defines generalisation as reducing information and detail so complex features remain legible in limited map space, with operations selecting both what content appears and how it is displayed ([USGS, “Generalization”](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/science-topics/generalization)). Ordnance Survey's product guide describes selection/omission, simplification, and exaggeration: some features disappear at smaller scales, complex lines lose vertices while retaining characteristic shape, and small but important features may be enlarged ([OS Open Map Local product guide](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/os-open-map-local-product-guide.pdf)).

This matters because a smaller-scale map is not intended to preserve every datum. It is intended to preserve the information appropriate to its purpose while making every transformation disciplined. A peer-reviewed typology distinguishes model generalisation (changes to stored digital representations) from cartographic generalisation (changes to graphic representation) and catalogues operators including selection, reclassification, collapse, combination, simplification, amalgamation, displacement, elimination, and typification ([Roth, Brewer, and Stryker 2011, “A typology of operators for maintaining legible map designs at multiple scales”](https://cartographicperspectives.org/index.php/journal/article/download/cp68-roth-et-al/html)). The USGS overview of generalisation operators emphasizes context-sensitive processing, preservation of important feature characteristics, reasoning about spatial conflicts, and network-specific cases such as road thinning and river pruning ([Stanislawski et al. 2014](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70199220)).

The key lesson is not “omit detail.” It is: **name the permissible kinds of loss and test the properties that must survive them**.

### 2. Multiple representations remain linked to one detailed model

An MRDB holds different representations of corresponding real-world objects at different scales and makes those correspondences explicit. Zhang et al. describe an MRDB in which linkages between corresponding objects across datasets are explicit; the system recovers generalisation history and uses it to propagate updates through 1:10k, 1:50k, and 1:100k representations ([Zhang et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0303243418302101)). An International Cartographic Conference implementation similarly constructs representations from 1:25k through 1:500k by model generalisation and object matching, with the goal of propagating updates from the detailed representation to the lower-resolution ones ([Çobankaya 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/530.html)).

That explicit lineage is crucial. A traditional standalone small-scale map tells a reader what survived, but not necessarily which detailed objects were combined or omitted. An MRDB can preserve the correspondence and transformation history. The coarser representation is therefore inspectable as a derivation rather than accepted as a fresh assertion.

A related USGS warning makes the information-loss issue concrete: if only a heavily generalised interpretive map is stored, the original data cannot be recovered and the map may support only its original purpose; storing primitive collected attributes allows the derived map to be recreated under the same or different criteria ([USGS Circular 977, p. 7](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/1986/0977/report.pdf)). Thus, the detailed representation must remain authoritative for derivation; the overview supplements rather than replaces it.

### 3. Constraints preserve structure while detail changes

Cartographic generalisation is not safe merely because objects are linked. It also needs constraints. Brenner and Sester model primitives and containers with explicit relationships, combine their constraint equations, enforce strict constraints, and optimize the remaining competing criteria; their examples include logical and metric relationships, and they note that parts and constraints may themselves change with scale ([Brenner and Sester 2005](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2005/htm/pdf/oral/TEMA9/Session%206/CLAUS%20BRENNER.pdf)). A schematic-map implementation makes the preservation target especially clear: it permits strong geometric abstraction while requiring the network's topology to survive ([Maheshwari et al. 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/518.html)).

Quality can be quantified rather than judged only by eye. A USGS project lists metrics including feature-density difference, polyline legibility, change in topological adjacency, change in sinuosity, Hausdorff distance, and average displacement ([Kronenfeld et al. 2023](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/generalization-quality-metrics-support-multiscale-mapping-hausdorff-and-average)). These metrics do not ask whether every small bend survived. They ask whether changes stayed within the declared semantic and geometric budget.

### 4. A scale ladder supports overview, localisation, and drill-down

Modern map delivery partitions information by both location and scale. OGC API — Tiles uses tile matrix sets: regular grids at multiple scales that let a client retrieve bounded pieces of geospatial information ([OGC API — Tiles](https://ogcapi.ogc.org/tiles/)). This is separable from MRDB generalisation—the tile standard does not itself guarantee faithful generalisation—but together the ideas permit a reader to begin with an overview, identify an anomalous region, and retrieve the corresponding fine-grained evidence without loading the entire world.

This is the human-attention solution cartography contributes: do not force exhaustive reading; make omission deliberate, preserve links, validate structural invariants, and allow local zoom.

## Concrete translation: an evidence claim pyramid

The entities are not geographic features but **claims and evidence observations**. The scale dimension is not metres per pixel but **semantic resolution**.

### Detailed level: evidence atoms

Each independently checkable observation receives a stable identifier and a machine-readable record:

```text
atom_id
claim_type                 # file-content, test-result, API-state, count, absence, etc.
subject                    # canonical object being claimed about
predicate
value
scope                      # path set, revision, host, time window, query universe
observation_method         # command/API/parser and version
raw_evidence_ref           # immutable or content-addressed stdout/result/artifact
observed_at
source_authority
independence_group         # same parser/source/credential/failure domain
status                     # support, contradict, unknown
```

An atom is a map's survey observation analogue, not prose written by the agent. For mechanically checkable claims, a verifier independently captures or replays the observation. For externally sourced claims, it records the source and retrieval context. The agent may request an observation, but it does not get to manufacture the verifier's result.

### Intermediate level: feature claims

Deterministic reducers combine atoms into bounded claims such as “all tests in package P passed at revision R” or “both detectors agree that object O is absent within scope S.” Every reducer emits:

* input atom IDs;
* operator name and version;
* inclusion predicate;
* excluded atom IDs grouped by reason;
* contradictions and unknowns;
* a coverage denominator, not merely a success numerator;
* hard invariants and soft quality scores.

These are the analogue of model-generalisation operators. The harness vocabulary should be deliberately small: `SELECT`, `FILTER`, `GROUP`, `COUNT`, `CLASSIFY`, `COLLAPSE`, and `TYPIFY/SAMPLE`. A prose “summary” is not an operator because its transformation cannot be recomputed exactly.

### Overview level: report claims

The final answer is rendered from intermediate claim records. Each sentence-level factual claim links to one or more claim IDs. The overview exposes coverage, contradiction, freshness, and independence badges, plus an omission manifest. A reader can zoom from report sentence → reducer → atoms → raw evidence. The report may omit routine successes from prose, but it may not erase their counts, the denominator, failures, unknowns, or the rule by which they were omitted.

### Cartographic constraints translated into verification invariants

| Cartographic property | Verification invariant | Failure caught |
|---|---|---|
| Feature correspondence across scales | Every factual overview claim resolves to intermediate claims and ultimately evidence atoms | Unsupported assertion or provenance break |
| Topological adjacency/connectivity | Dependencies named in the claim remain connected: subject → observation → result → conclusion | A test result attached to the wrong revision, file, host, or package |
| Feature-density change | The overview's displayed count plus deliberately hidden count equals the eligible detailed population | Cherry-picking and silent denominator shrinkage |
| Selection/omission rule | Every omitted eligible atom has a declared, recomputable exclusion reason | Suppressed failures, inconvenient counterexamples, and out-of-scope laundering |
| Classification consistency | Aggregate labels are functions of child states under a versioned truth table | “Mostly passed” rendered as “passed”; unknown rendered as absent |
| Scale-transition consistency | Recomputing a parent from its current children yields the stored parent | Stale summaries after evidence changes |
| Minimum legibility / salience | Contradictions, unknowns, stale observations, and low-independence support cannot be hidden at overview scale | Important exceptions buried in detail |
| Displacement budget | Scope, time, revision, and subject identity cannot change during aggregation | A true result moved onto a broader or newer claim |
| Independent layers | High-impact claims require support from distinct independence groups | Two checks that share one broken parser being mistaken for corroboration |

The last row is not a standard cartographic rule; it is an intentional target-domain addition. Cartographic layers can share a survey and are not automatically independent. Verification must model correlated failure explicitly.

## Recommended harness change — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: shadow-run the claim pyramid on a stratified set of completed tasks and compare blinded defect discovery against ordinary reports)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a narrow, append-only JSONL claim ledger plus a deterministic reducer/checker, invoked through the existing tool/hook bus. Do not build a new orchestration runtime, database service, vector store, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. A Markdown renderer is enough for drill-down: links can target claim blocks and raw artifacts.

The smallest viable design is:

1. A hook records tool-result atoms, content hashes, scope, time, and independence group.
2. The agent emits structured claim declarations referencing atom IDs; declarations without resolvable support remain visibly unsupported.
3. A deterministic script evaluates a small operator set and invariants, then emits a three-level Markdown report and omission manifest.
4. The final-response gate blocks only a narrow class of mechanically decidable violations: dangling evidence references, child/parent count mismatch, hidden contradiction, stale aggregate, or missing scope on an absence claim. Other warnings remain visible but non-blocking.
5. A seeded sampler chooses some green (“apparently supported”) claims for independent replay. Sampling must be generated outside the agent after the report is frozen, so the report author cannot steer which claims are checked.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this would find representation-integrity defects missed by prose-only review—especially denominator loss, stale summaries, scope widening, buried contradictions, and unsupported conclusions—because MRDB research demonstrates explicit cross-scale links and update propagation, while cartographic constraint work demonstrates machine-testable preservation across abstraction. This efficacy claim is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the shadow run described in the heading, with defect yield adjudicated blind to treatment.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a useful prototype can remain under roughly 200 lines if it supports only JSONL atoms, six or seven reducers, five hard invariants, and Markdown output. Evidence: the data model and operations above require no service or interactive UI, but implementation size has not been measured. This size claim is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by a time-boxed prototype with physical and logical line counts, excluding tests but reporting them separately.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that automatically capturing tool outputs is safer than asking the agent to restate them, because content hashes and direct result references eliminate a transcription step and make derivation replayable. This target-system effect is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with mutation tests that alter agent-authored paraphrases, tool artifacts, claim references, and revision metadata and measure which corruptions the checker catches.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a final-response blocking gate will improve end-to-end task outcomes rather than merely cause evasive phrasing or excessive “unknown” labels. Evidence cuts both ways: hard cartographic constraints detect invalid transformations, but semantic claims are less formal than geometric relations and blocking can create Goodhart pressure. This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with an A/B study comparing warn-only and block-on-hard-invariant modes on held-out tasks, measuring false blocks, escaped defects, retries, and task completion time. Begin in warn-only shadow mode.

## What it would catch that ordinary answer-level checking misses

### Silent denominator changes

Suppose 97 checks pass, two fail, and one is not run. A prose summary says “the checks passed.” A claim pyramid knows the eligible population is 100 and requires `displayed + omitted = eligible`; its truth table cannot reduce `{97 pass, 2 fail, 1 unknown}` to `pass`. Cartography's density and selection constraints become coverage accounting.

### Scope laundering

Suppose a search finds no matches in one directory, but the conclusion says the capability is absent from the system. The subject/scope edge changes between scales. This resembles displacing a feature while pretending its position is unchanged; a scope-preservation invariant rejects the aggregate.

### Correlated “independent” evidence

Two reports may both be derived from the same parser or API. The ledger's independence group prevents them from satisfying a two-signal rule. This is an extension beyond cartography, required because two visual layers can share one flawed survey.

### Buried contradictions

A detailed child contradicts its siblings, but the overview displays only the majority. A salience rule forces contradictions and unknowns to survive at every coarser scale, analogous to exaggerating a small but important landmark instead of omitting it.

### Stale conclusions

One evidence atom changes after a rerun, while the narrative summary remains unchanged. Recomputing the scale transition yields a different parent hash, marking the report stale. MRDB update propagation is directly relevant here.

### Evidence attachment errors

A passing test belongs to revision A but is used to support a claim about revision B. The topology/provenance edge breaks even though the text sounds plausible. Ordinary semantic review may miss the identity mismatch; a typed relationship check need not understand the prose.

### Aggregation laundering

Several weak observations are collapsed into a confident global statement. Classification tables preserve `unknown`, source authority, and independence counts through aggregation; evidence quantity cannot silently become evidence quality.

The system would **very likely (80-90%)** catch synthetically seeded instances of these seven defect classes when all relevant atoms are captured, because each maps to a deterministic invariant above. This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a mutation suite containing at least ten independently generated cases per class and report precision and recall separately.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 1. Representation fidelity is not correspondence truth

Cartography starts with observations or a landscape model and asks how to represent them at a useful scale. Verification asks whether the claim corresponds to reality. Those are different epistemic problems. Explicit lineage proves “this overview came from these observations under this rule,” not “these observations are correct.” A perfectly consistent map can encode a surveying error. A perfectly consistent claim pyramid can encode a flaky test or compromised tool.

Therefore the mechanism must report two axes separately:

* **derivation integrity:** are the links, operators, scope, coverage, and invariants sound?
* **evidence adequacy:** are sources authoritative, fresh, independent, and actually capable of deciding the claim?

Only the first axis is substantially borrowed from cartography. The second requires target-domain experiment design, source criticism, and independent observation.

### 2. Geographic scale is ordered; semantic abstraction often is not

Map scales have a meaningful spatial ordering and tile pyramids have regular parent/child structure. Claims can overlap, depend cyclically, use incompatible ontologies, or be incomparable. “Security,” “correctness,” and “user intent” are not adjacent zoom levels.

The transfer remains valid only for **bounded claim families with an explicit aggregation relation**. It fails if the harness tries to force all reasoning into one universal pyramid. Multiple small pyramids are preferable to a global claim graph.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that attempting a universal ontology would violate the anti-overfit gate and become a knowledge-graph project, because arbitrary semantic claims require open-ended entity and relation modeling. This forward design judgment is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by attempting to encode a heterogeneous held-out task sample using only the proposed fixed atom schema and reducers, and count required schema exceptions. The recommendation therefore explicitly rejects that expansion.

### 3. Salience is purpose-dependent and gameable

Cartographers choose what matters for a map's purpose. An agent under evaluation can choose a purpose or selection predicate that hides counterevidence. Declaring the operator does not make the criterion honest.

Mitigation must come from outside the reporting agent: fixed mandatory salience rules for contradictions/failures/unknowns; externally chosen post-freeze samples; and coverage universes obtained from independent enumeration where feasible. This does not eliminate gaming when the universe itself is unknown.

### 4. Many claims do not have machine-observable atoms

Value judgments, intent alignment, novelty, causal explanation, and claims about unobservable future behavior often lack a decisive tool replay. Rendering them with green provenance badges would create false assurance. Such claims must remain explicitly `judgment`, `prediction`, or `underdetermined`; a human may still need to inspect them.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that this mechanism can materially verify open-ended evaluative claims without domain-specific rubrics or human review, because the cartographic borrow constrains transformations but supplies no ground-truth observation process. This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by preregistering adjudication criteria on a held-out set of evaluative claims and comparing the pyramid's verdicts with independent expert panels.

### 5. Generalisation can destroy the anomaly one needs to see

Aggregation tends to smooth rare events. In verification, the one failure may matter more than a thousand successes. Standard cartographic operators therefore transfer only with a reversed priority rule: contradictions, safety-relevant exceptions, unknowns, and unsupported high-impact claims must be exaggerated at coarse scale rather than averaged away.

### 6. “Zoomable” can become “buried”

Providing a drill-down link does not excuse a misleading overview. If critical evidence is technically accessible but absent from the first view, the interface has reproduced the defect. Hard salience invariants must govern overview rendering; progressive disclosure is an attention aid, not disclosure absolution.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Plain hierarchical summarisation

Rejected. It borrows only the appearance of map scales. Without explicit child links, versioned operators, denominators, omission records, and constraints, each summary layer can hallucinate or launder the preceding one.

### Randomly sample the final report

Rejected as the sole mechanism. Sampling estimates a rate only relative to a defined population and design; it can miss a single catastrophic claim and cannot expose systematic scope changes. Sampling is useful only as an adversarial supplement to deterministic cross-scale checks.

### Have a second agent verify the first

Rejected as a cartographic translation. Two agents can share model priors, context, tools, and failure modes. A second agent is not mechanically independent merely because it is a separate invocation. The valuable independence comes from replayed observations, disjoint sources/parsers, fixed reducers, and post-freeze sample selection.

### Full knowledge graph or vector database

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate and not required by the mechanism. The bounded JSONL ledger and per-task claim pyramids ride the existing bus. A global graph would introduce precisely the second semantic runtime and durable memory substrate that this brief warns against.

### Cryptographic proof as universal answer

Rejected. Hashes can prove artifact identity and tamper evidence, not semantic adequacy or external truth. Content addressing is useful plumbing inside the detailed level, not a substitute for observation.

### Preserve every detail at every level

Rejected. This defeats the attention objective and ignores cartography's central accomplishment: a useful overview requires controlled loss. The correct demand is not losslessness in every view; it is reversible drill-down, declared loss, conserved coverage, and non-omissible anomalies.

## Evaluation plan

The borrow should earn deployment through falsifiable tests rather than analogy.

### Phase A: mutation testing

Create frozen task evidence and seed dangling references, denominator shrinkage, a hidden contradiction, scope widening, stale timestamps/revisions, shared-failure-domain double counting, and a parent not reproducible from its children. Measure per-class precision and recall. A viable hard gate requires near-zero false positives on syntactic and arithmetic invariants; semantic adequacy warnings should be scored separately.

### Phase B: retrospective shadow run

Select tasks stratified by domain, tool count, outcome, and report length. A builder who cannot see the original assessment encodes their tool evidence. Run the checker, then have adjudicators blind to treatment identify real defects. Compare ordinary final-report review with overview-plus-pyramid review on defect yield, adjudication time, and severity-weighted misses.

### Phase C: prospective warn-only run

Freeze the evidence ledger before externally sampling green claims. Replay the sample through a mechanically independent method where possible. Measure unsupported-claim rate, correlated-evidence rate, stale aggregate rate, added latency, extra tool calls, and whether agents evade claims by replacing facts with vague language.

### Phase D: narrowly gated trial

Only deterministic violations with demonstrated high precision become blocking: broken references, impossible counts, hidden explicit contradictions, revision mismatch, and unreproducible aggregates. Keep source adequacy and evaluative-claim warnings non-blocking until validated per domain.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this staged evaluation can distinguish useful integrity checking from decorative provenance, because the proposed defect classes are seedable and the reducers are replayable. This is **UNVERIFIED**; the experiment is Phases A–D, with preregistered metrics and held-out tasks.

## Decision

Adopt the concept only in its bounded form: **a per-task, append-only evidence ledger; deterministic claim generalisation; explicit omission and coverage accounting; cross-scale invariants; and externally selected drill-down samples**.

The analogy transfers because both domains must compress a large, structured reality into a human-usable overview while preserving selected relationships and making local inspection possible. It fails as a complete theory of verification because cartography assumes a source representation and controls its transformation, whereas verification must also establish whether the source observation deserves belief.

The most important operational rule is therefore: **a green overview means “faithfully derived from these captured observations under these declared rules,” never “true in the world.”** That narrower guarantee is still valuable, mechanically testable, and compatible with a minimal harness.

## Gaps

* No harness-specific integration points or line counts were inspected, by explicit unit instruction; the under-200-line classification remains **UNVERIFIED** and is tied above to a prototype measurement.
* This unit did not verify a published study directly evaluating an MRDB-style claim pyramid for autonomous-agent verification; this is a search gap, not an absence claim. Transfer efficacy is therefore **UNVERIFIED** and must be settled by the proposed shadow-run and mutation experiments.
* The cartographic sources establish generalisation operators, multiple representations, explicit correspondences, constraints, and quality measures. They do not establish mechanically independent epistemic evidence for software-agent claims; independence grouping is a target-domain addition.
* OGC tiling establishes multi-scale partition and retrieval, not semantic faithfulness. It supports drill-down architecture only and should not be cited as evidence that the generalisation is correct.
