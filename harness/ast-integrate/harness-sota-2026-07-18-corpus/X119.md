# X119 — Forensic-science borrow for use-vs-mention guards

## Executive conclusion

The forensic-science mechanism worth borrowing is **context information management (CIM), especially Linear Sequential Unmasking–Expanded (LSU-E), paired with in-workflow blind proficiency testing and negative controls**. The transferable principle is not “hide dangerous words.” It is: (1) define the decision task, (2) expose the decision-maker only to evidence relevant to that task and in a controlled order, (3) preserve the withheld material for later tasks rather than destroying it, (4) record exactly what information influenced the decision, and (5) test the whole operational path with disguised cases and known-negative controls.

That principle transfers at the information-architecture and quality-control levels. It does **not** transfer literally at the cognitive level. A forensic examiner is vulnerable to human contextual bias; a lexical guard is doing exactly what its code says. The corresponding agent-harness defect is therefore not “bias” inside the matcher but failure to give the matcher a task-scoped evidence view. A guard deciding whether an action is being performed should normally receive a canonical representation of the action, not every byte that happened to travel in the same payload.

The proposed change is a small, shared **role-aware evidence adapter** on the existing hook bus, followed by contrastive and blind in-path tests. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** It does not add an orchestrator, memory system, durable-execution engine, critic, observability service, or guardrail framework.

The operational benefit is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the shadow-mode paired-corpus and disguised-canary experiment specified below**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that structural scoping will eliminate false fires in cases where the hook already knows that text is file content, documentation, a quoted example, or another non-executing field; the evidence is that role is then supplied by the event boundary rather than inferred from the dangerous token. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to improve true-positive coverage through canonical action parsing; shell indirection, generated code, aliases, and `eval` make complete action recovery impossible without execution semantics. It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** safe to stamp arbitrary text “mention” forever; the same bytes can become an action at a later lifecycle boundary, so the guard must re-evaluate them when they are passed to an executor.

## What forensic science has already solved

### 1. Context information management: task relevance before exposure

Forensic scientists often need case context, but not every fact in a case is relevant to every analytic decision. Context can change judgment even when the underlying evidence does not. In the 2006 experiment by Dror, Charlton, and Péron, experts were shown fingerprints they had previously identified, but with a new context suggesting exclusion; most made judgments inconsistent with their own earlier decisions. The University of Southampton record provides the abstract and DOI: [Dror, Charlton & Péron, *Contextual information renders experts vulnerable to making erroneous identifications*](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/27807/). A related study reported changed decisions in 6 of 48 re-examinations and complete consistency by only two of six examiners, with inconsistency concentrated in difficult decisions and cases carrying additional contextual information: [Office of Justice Programs summary of Dror & Charlton, *Why Experts Make Errors*](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/why-experts-make-errors).

The field's response is not to deprive examiners of all context. It is to manage information according to the task. England and Wales's current Forensic Science Regulator guidance says experts need enough task-relevant information to select and interpret analyses, should not see task-irrelevant information, and that the forensic unit should have a policy that filters the latter while permitting the former: [FSR, *Forensic science activities: interpretation and communication*, §4.4](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/forensic-science-activities-interpretation-and-communication-fsr-gui-0004/forensic-science-activities-interpretation-and-communication-accessible). Its statutory Code requires a process map that assesses cognitive-bias risk and holds back non-task-relevant information until the susceptible stage is complete; it also explicitly contemplates phased disclosure: [FSR Code of Practice v2, §§13.1.5 and 17.1.4](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2-accessible).

The concrete mechanism is LSU-E. It directs the analyst to begin with the raw evidence, then sequence additional information by three criteria:

1. **Biasing power:** less biasing information precedes more biasing information.
2. **Objectivity:** more objective information precedes more subjective information.
3. **Relevance:** information central to the task precedes peripheral information; wholly irrelevant information is omitted from that decision.

LSU-E also requires documentation of which information was available and when. These are the authors' stated criteria and procedure, not an extrapolation: [Dror & Kukucka, *Linear Sequential Unmasking–Expanded*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8385162/). A practical worksheet implementation has a case manager or SOP triage information, records the source of each item, rates biasing power/objectivity/relevance, and records mitigation and timing: [Kukucka et al., *A practical tool for information management in forensic decisions*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8866671/).

The important subtlety is that relevance is **decision-relative**, not an intrinsic property of the evidence. A prior conviction can be irrelevant to a fingerprint comparison while remaining relevant elsewhere in a case. CIM therefore gates exposure at a stage; it does not erase the information or declare it universally harmless.

### 2. Blind verification: independence requires hiding the first answer

The FSR Code defines a blind check as a second practitioner independently assessing a finding without seeing the original finding. It requires blind checks in specified high-impact situations and requires the records to identify the degree of independence when some contextualization is necessary: [FSR Code of Practice v2, §§20.2.8–20.2.10](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2/forensic-science-activities-statutory-code-of-practice-version-2-accessible).

This matters because a “second check” is not mechanically independent if it sees the first conclusion. Recent critical analysis reports that, in the cited firearm study, the odds of disagreement were five times higher when the second examiner was blind to the first examiner's result, while noting that the study did not establish accuracy because ground truth was unknown: [Koehler et al., *Assessing the foundations of forensic identification evidence*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2528192123). That limitation is important: disagreement detects dependence, not necessarily which answer is correct.

### 3. Blind proficiency testing: test the routine path, not test-taking behavior

Open proficiency tests tell analysts that they are being tested and can elicit extra care. Full-blind tests enter the system as ordinary casework so that the analyst does not know the case is a test. The National Academies' discussion distinguishes open and full-blind testing and says full-blind testing can exercise the broader operation from evidence receipt through analysis, interpretation, and reporting, while also documenting formidable logistical costs: [National Research Council, *The Evaluation of Forensic DNA Evidence*, “Ensuring High Standards of Laboratory Performance”](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK232602/).

This has been implemented, not merely proposed. Harris County described a program whose goal was testing the whole system from evidence receipt to report release: [Pierce & Cook, *Development and Implementation of an Effective Blind Proficiency Testing Program*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31922611/). Houston Forensic Science Center implemented blind QC across toxicology, drugs, firearms, latent prints, biology, and multimedia in addition to annual declared proficiency tests: [Hundl et al., *Implementation of a Blind Quality Control Program in a Forensic Laboratory*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1556-4029.14259). A multi-laboratory implementation paper reports that a prior comparison in blood-lead programs found higher errors in blind than declared tests, consistent with special handling of known test samples, and stresses that ordinary proficiency artifacts do not cover every step between submission and release: [Koehler et al., *Implementing blind proficiency testing in forensic laboratories*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/).

### 4. Negative controls: absence is evidence only when the control traverses the same process

In forensic DNA work, a reagent blank contains the process materials but no analyte, is processed in parallel with samples, and is expected to produce no result. A signal in the blank indicates contamination or compromised reagents that can affect the associated samples: [NIST forensic-science lexicon, “reagent blank”](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/42976). An OSAC proposed standard describes extraction and amplification negative controls being processed alongside each batch through the corresponding DNA-testing steps, followed by source and timing assessment when contamination is detected: [OSAC, *Standard for Interpreting, Comparing and Reporting DNA Test Results Associated with Failed Controls and Contamination Events*](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2021/06/01/OSAC%202020-S-0004_Standard_for_Interpreting_Comparing_and_Reporting_DNA_Test_Results_with_Failed_Controls_and_Contanimation%20FINAL%20OSAC%20PROPOSED.pdf).

The useful idea is not merely “include a negative unit test.” The blank shares the process, materials, and batch with the questioned samples. It localizes process contamination because it differs at the analyte boundary while everything else is held as similar as practicable.

## Structural mapping to use-vs-mention

| Forensic-science element | Agent-harness translation | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Defined examination question | A detector declares its jurisdiction: action execution, content exfiltration, path mutation, credential handling, or another explicit decision | “Dangerous text” is not a decision question |
| Raw evidence first | Canonical action projection first: event kind, tool identity, parsed operation, normalized target/destination, and execution boundary | A parser is fallible and shell semantics can be dynamic |
| Task-irrelevant context withheld | Documentation bodies, quoted examples, comments, error transcripts, and unrelated fields are not capabilities available to an action-only detector | Content-sensitive detectors may legitimately need some of these fields |
| Phased disclosure | A detector can request a named secondary field only under a declared reason code; access and order are logged | Delaying inspection must not create a bypass |
| Case manager / SOP | A shared evidence adapter derives role primarily from trusted event structure and field provenance | A language model guessing “mention” from the same text is circular and unsuitable as the authority |
| Blind verification | A shadow detector evaluates the same normalized event without seeing the production detector's verdict | Agreement is not proof if both share the same faulty parser |
| Full-blind proficiency case | Disguised canary events traverse the real hook path and are scored out-of-band | Canaries must never perform destructive or external actions |
| Reagent blank | A matched event contains the trigger lexeme in an inert field but has an empty action projection; it traverses the same detector batch | A blank proves contamination only for the exercised path |
| Positive control | A matched event performs the controlled, harmless equivalent of the guarded operation | Synthetic positives may fail to represent dynamic real attacks |
| Chain-of-custody/context log | Decision record includes raw-event hash, adapter version, projection, fields read, staged disclosures, detector version, and verdict | Logs must avoid copying secrets or sensitive payloads |

The exact transfer is **decision-relative exposure**. “Use” and “mention” are not immutable classes of strings. They are roles at a boundary. The token `rm` in a Markdown example is mention at a file-write decision; if a later tool extracts and executes that example, it is use at the execution decision. The adapter must therefore label the current event relationship, not bless the bytes permanently.

## Recommendation — UNVERIFIED until the shadow-mode paired-corpus experiment

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build one shared evidence adapter and migrate the five detectors to it. Do not build five richer regexes and do not introduce a semantic classifier daemon.

### A. Evidence envelope

At each existing hook boundary, construct an immutable envelope conceptually like:

```text
event_id
event_kind            # tool_call, file_write, command_exec, network_request, ...
producer               # trusted hook/tool identity
raw_payload_hash       # raw bytes remain available to authorized content policies
field_provenance[]     # field path, source, encoding, trust level
action_projection[]    # canonical operations actually proposed at this boundary
projection_status      # complete | partial | opaque | parse_error
adapter_version
```

Each detector declares an input capability. An action-only detector receives `event_kind`, `producer`, `action_projection`, and `projection_status`; it does not receive an API capable of reading arbitrary raw fields. A content-sensitive detector receives only the named fields its policy requires. A staged request for more context must carry a reason code and be added to the decision record.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that capability-level field restriction will prevent the known class of action detectors from firing merely because an inert payload field contains a trigger lexeme; the evidence is mechanical non-observability, provided the event boundary supplies the role correctly. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying every known false-fire specimen and paired controls through shadow mode while recording field access**.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a common adapter will reduce detector-to-detector inconsistency compared with five independent payload interpretations; the evidence is the single declared event schema and shared projection, but the current detectors have not been inspected in this unit. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it by comparing inter-detector role classifications before and after migration on the same labeled corpus**.

### B. Structural role derivation, not textual absolution

Derive the first role signal from the hook's trusted structure:

- A command-execution tool's command is an action candidate.
- A file-write tool's content is data at the write boundary; its target path and write operation are actions.
- A network tool's destination/method are actions; a request body is content whose inspection depends on the detector's declared purpose.
- A documentation or message body is content at that boundary, even when it describes a command.
- A value fed later into an executor is reclassified at that later boundary.

Within an action candidate, parse to the strongest available native representation: argv when the runtime supplies argv; a shell AST when it receives a shell program; structured MCP tool identity and arguments for MCP; normalized file-operation type and target for filesystem tools. Preserve `partial`, `opaque`, and `parse_error` rather than guessing “mention.” Risk policy can fail closed, request review, or use a conservative fallback specifically for opaque action candidates.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that canonicalization will catch some true uses that exact payload regexes miss, such as harmless quoting variation, option reordering, or equivalent path spelling; the evidence is that parsing compares operation structure rather than surface spelling. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it with metamorphic positive tests generated by independently authored transformations**.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a sub-200-line adapter can safely resolve arbitrary shell indirection, aliases, command substitution, generated scripts, and `eval`; the evidence is the shell's runtime-dependent semantics and the mismatch between static syntax and executed behavior. The design must expose uncertainty rather than claim complete interpretation.

### C. Decision record as contamination trace

Record, without copying sensitive content:

- raw-event cryptographic hash and event type;
- adapter and detector versions;
- action projection or a redacted structural summary;
- projection completeness;
- fields/capabilities actually read and the order of staged disclosure;
- verdict and reason code;
- whether the event was a test, revealed only to the out-of-band scorer after the decision.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that field-access records will distinguish “the role adapter misclassified the event” from “the detector improperly consumed raw context”; the evidence is that these faults occur on opposite sides of the recorded interface. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it by deliberately seeding one adapter fault and one detector-capability violation and confirming distinct diagnostics**.

### D. Failure policy

Do not map `parse_error` to mention. For high-impact action boundaries, an opaque or failed projection should enter the existing conservative/manual path. For documentation writes, parsing failure of the body should not matter to an action-only command detector because the body is not its evidence in the first place. Content policies still inspect content under their own jurisdiction.

This distinction is crucial: context management narrows **who sees what for which decision**. It does not create a universal trusted-content lane.

## Proficiency design — UNVERIFIED until disguised in-path canaries run safely

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

### 1. Contrastive pairs as forensic negative and positive controls

For every guarded concept, maintain matched pairs that differ in semantic role while preserving as much surface text as possible:

```text
NEGATIVE: write a Markdown file containing the exact dangerous command
POSITIVE: invoke a harmless fixture executable under the guarded command identity

NEGATIVE: send an error transcript that quotes the prohibited URL or tool name
POSITIVE: request a network connection to a local inert test endpoint

NEGATIVE: JSON example field contains an operation string
POSITIVE: structured tool operation targets a disposable fixture

NEGATIVE: shell prints a dangerous command as literal data
POSITIVE: shell executes a sandbox fixture with the same canonical verb
```

The negative member is the reagent blank: the trigger-bearing material is present and follows the same policy path, but the action projection contains no corresponding operation. The positive member proves that the detector is live and can find a known-present use. Both are necessary; a detector that passes negatives by never running is broken, not precise.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that matched positive/negative controls will reveal payload contamination and dead detectors more clearly than unpaired regression examples; the evidence is that each pair separately tests specificity and sensitivity while holding the trigger material approximately constant. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it by mutation-testing the detector to (a) read raw payload and (b) always allow, and require the suite to kill both mutants**.

### 2. Blind, in-path proficiency trials

After ordinary tests pass, inject a very small number of safe canary events through the production-equivalent hook path. The detector and its maintainer should not receive a “this is a test” flag before verdict. An out-of-band controller retains the label and reveals it only for scoring. Canaries must target local disposable fixtures and must be incapable of destructive or external effects even if every guard fails.

Randomize semantically irrelevant aspects—identifiers, whitespace, quoting, benign surrounding prose, field order where legal—so production code cannot pass by memorizing fixed fixtures. Keep a human-authored core set, and do not let the same parser both generate the action projection and define expected truth.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that disguised in-path trials will expose wiring, routing, schema, and deployment faults that unit tests miss; the evidence is the forensic programs' explicit whole-system scope from receipt to report and the general difference between declared and blind tests. The transfer remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it by deliberately introducing one routing bypass in a test environment and confirming that only the in-path trial detects it**.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that true “blindness” can be sustained in a small deterministic harness over time; the evidence cuts both ways: randomized templates reduce recognition, but maintainers can know the generator and logs can reveal test provenance. Treat blinding as a defense against special-case test handling, not as a permanent secrecy guarantee. The settling experiment is to ask a maintainer who knows the system but not current seeds to classify canaries from ordinary events above chance, then rotate any distinguishable metadata.

### 3. Independent shadow adjudication

For high-impact or ambiguous action candidates, run the new adapter/detector in shadow beside the current guard during evaluation. Do not show either verdict to the other before both commit. Compare them against externally labeled contrastive cases and real outcomes, not against majority agreement.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that blind shadowing will expose correlated “verification” more honestly than a reviewer who sees the first verdict; the evidence is forensic blind-check doctrine and the reported higher disagreement under blind firearm review. It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** sufficient to establish correctness when both paths share the same parser or ground truth is unknown; the evidence is the forensic literature's explicit ground-truth limitation. Settle correctness with independently labeled fixtures and, for a sample, observed executor traces in a safe sandbox.

## What this would catch, and what it would not

The following outcome claims are prospective and therefore inherit **UNVERIFIED; settle them with the paired replay, mutation, and in-path experiments above**.

### Very likely catches (80-90%)

Evidence: these cases have a trusted structural boundary separating content from operation.

- Action-only detectors reading file bodies, documentation, error transcripts, quoted examples, or message content solely because the trigger string is present.
- Cross-field contamination where a detector intended for a destination/path/action also scans unrelated metadata or body fields.
- A detector that is disconnected or always allows, because the matched positive control must fire.
- A detector that always blocks trigger-bearing payloads, because the matched negative control must not fire.
- Hook routing or schema regressions exercised only by the real pipeline, provided the blind canary traverses that exact route.

### Likely catches (55-80%)

Evidence: parsing normalizes some, but not all, equivalent surface forms.

- Real operations hidden from literal matching by benign quoting, whitespace, option order, path normalization, or structured-tool encoding.
- Detector disagreement caused by five locally invented interpretations of the same event role, if all five migrate to the shared projection.
- Improper raw-field reads, if the runtime enforces detector capabilities rather than merely documenting them.

### Unlikely catches (20-45%) without additional semantics

Evidence: these require runtime or downstream knowledge not present in a boundary-local projection.

- Commands constructed dynamically, resolved through unknown aliases/functions, fetched remotely, or executed via `eval`.
- Malicious instructions stored as documentation now but later interpreted by an agent without a new guarded execution boundary.
- Polyglot payloads whose role depends on the receiving application's undocumented behavior.
- Social-engineering or prompt-injection meaning inside otherwise legitimate content; that is a content-policy task, not an action-only use-vs-mention task.
- A malicious or buggy producer that lies about field provenance. Trusted event structure is an assumption, not cryptographic truth.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### Disanalogy 1: cognitive bias versus deterministic overbreadth

LSU-E was designed around human decisions whose judgments can be shifted by context. Regex does not become psychologically biased. Its input contract is overbroad. Therefore awareness training, warning banners, or telling the guard to “ignore mentions” does not transfer. The useful intervention is an information-capability boundary and a task-specific representation.

### Disanalogy 2: forensic raw evidence is the primary object; guard payload may contain the threat

Forensic CIM can withhold irrelevant case history while retaining the evidentiary specimen. In security, the supposedly “contextual” text can itself exploit a parser, prompt an agent, leak a secret, or become executable downstream. Wholesale masking would create blind spots. The transfer is valid only per detector jurisdiction: action-only guards get action evidence; content guards get content evidence.

### Disanalogy 3: use/mention changes over time

A fingerprint does not become a confession because it moves to another lab stage. Text can move from mention to use when copied into a shell, interpreter, SQL engine, browser, or model context. Role labels must be boundary-scoped and recomputed; permanent “safe mention” labels are unsound.

### Disanalogy 4: the case manager cannot be another ungrounded semantic judge

Human forensic case managers apply expertise and SOPs to classify relevance. Replacing five regexes with an LLM that reads the identical payload and guesses its role would merely move the ambiguity and add nondeterminism. The adapter should use trusted event type, field provenance, and native syntax structure first; ambiguity must remain explicit.

### Disanalogy 5: blinding is easier to compromise in software

An analyst may not recognize a realistic case submission. Deterministic canary fixtures, test IDs, timing, or metadata are easy for software or maintainers to recognize. Randomization and out-of-band labels reduce this problem but do not eliminate it. Blind trials supplement rather than replace ordinary transparent tests and production metrics.

### Disanalogy 6: two independent verdicts can share a contaminated substrate

Two guards consuming the same faulty action projection are not mechanically independent, just as a forensic verifier exposed to the first result is not independent. Validation needs at least one independently constructed ground-truth signal: hand-labeled fixtures, observed safe executor traces, or a separately implemented reference parser. Re-running the same detector is not corroboration.

## Transfer verdict

The analogy **does transfer**, but narrowly. It supports a concrete rule: **control information by decision task, preserve provenance and exposure order, and validate the operational path with paired negative/positive and disguised proficiency samples**. It does not support hiding payloads globally, trusting a “mention” classifier, or treating blind agreement as accuracy.

The highest-value borrow is the negative-control idea combined with enforced field capabilities. A mention-only event should carry the same lexical “reagent” through the same guard path while the action projection is blank. If an action detector fires, contextual material has contaminated its decision. A paired harmless positive proves the detector is actually alive. That gives the harness two mechanically independent signals for a claim of precision: known-negative non-fire and known-positive fire.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this narrow design is a better fit than adding more lexical exceptions; evidence: it changes the evidence boundary shared by all five detectors and directly encodes decision role. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to solve every reported false fire without inspecting the actual event schemas; evidence: some “mention” cases may occur inside fields that are also genuine execution channels. This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; the decisive experiment is a shadow replay of all known false fires plus matched positive controls, followed by safe randomized canaries through the real hook route**.

## Minimal experiment and acceptance gates

1. Assemble every known false-fire event with secrets removed. For each, retain event kind and field provenance.
2. For every negative, author a matched harmless positive that causes an observable local fixture action.
3. Add at least one known-present control for every detector route before interpreting any zero-fire result.
4. Implement only the envelope, capability restriction, and the smallest native parser available for one detector.
5. Run current and proposed detectors blind in shadow. Capture verdict, projection status, and field-access log.
6. Mutation-test with an always-allow detector, an always-block trigger detector, a raw-payload-reading detector, and a projection-dropping adapter. The suite must distinguish all four.
7. Run safe randomized in-path canaries with labels held out-of-band.
8. Expand to the remaining detectors only after the pilot meets the gates below.

Acceptance gates:

- 100% of the supplied known false-fire negatives are allowed by the pilot detector.
- 100% of their matched harmless positives are detected.
- Every `partial`, `opaque`, or `parse_error` action candidate follows the explicitly configured uncertainty policy; none silently becomes “mention.”
- The detector cannot access undeclared raw fields, demonstrated by a capability-violation test rather than a log convention alone.
- The in-path canary detects an intentionally seeded routing bypass in a test environment.
- At least one ground-truth oracle is independent of the production projection parser.
- No detector-specific exception is added without a paired positive showing that the exception does not erase a real use.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that these gates can be implemented on the existing hook bus without a new runtime; evidence: the design consists of an envelope, restricted field views, parsing already-structured tool events, and tests. The line-count classification remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a one-detector spike and count non-test production lines before committing to migration**. If enforcing field capabilities requires pervasive runtime changes beyond the existing bus, reclassify honestly as BUILD-LARGE and reassess; do not smuggle a new guardrail framework in under this proposal.

## Alternatives rejected

### Add negative-lookbehind regexes for quote markers

Rejected. Quotes, Markdown fences, JSON strings, comments, heredocs, nested languages, and generated code do not share one reliable lexical marker, and a quote can itself occur inside an executable shell program. This treats syntax fragments as semantic roles.

### Use an LLM to label use versus mention before every guard

Rejected as the authority. It duplicates the decision in a less inspectable component, can be prompt-influenced by the payload, adds latency and nondeterminism, and cannot turn ambiguous downstream semantics into ground truth. An LLM may propose offline test cases, but independently labeled controls must judge the guard.

### Globally strip documentation, code blocks, or quoted text before policy evaluation

Rejected. Content detectors may need those bytes, and quoted or documented text can be consumed by a later executor. Information should be capability-scoped per decision, not erased.

### Build a second policy orchestration service

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. The proposal belongs on the existing hook/tool bus and needs no durable runtime, vector store, knowledge graph, trained critic, observability SaaS, or generic guardrail framework.

### Rely only on declared CI regression fixtures

Rejected as incomplete. Transparent fixtures are valuable for fast diagnosis, but they do not exercise realistic routing and can invite fixture-specific handling. The forensic borrow specifically adds a small safe blind/in-path layer, not a replacement for CI.

## Gaps

- No local detector, event schema, hook API, or false-fire specimen was inspected by design. Therefore the precise projection fields and the under-200-line estimate remain UNVERIFIED; the one-detector spike settles both.
- No external source establishes that LSU-E improves software guards. The source evidence establishes the forensic mechanisms; the paired shadow experiment tests the translation.
- The cited blind-verification evidence shows changed independence/disagreement, not necessarily improved accuracy. Ground-truth fixtures and safe executor traces are required.
- The proficiency-testing literature documents logistical and representativeness problems. Safe agent-harness canaries may have analogous artifacts; the maintainer distinguishability experiment tests that premise.
- No claim is made that all use-vs-mention distinctions are decidable statically. Dynamic interpretation is an explicit opaque case.
