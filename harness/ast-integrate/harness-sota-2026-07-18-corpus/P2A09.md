# P2A09 — How to evaluate a personal agent harness

**Unit:** P2A09 — research: evals  
**Date:** 2026-07-18  
**Lens:** external research only; no local harness inspection  
**Question:** What metrics predict usefulness, and how can one know whether a harness change helped?

## Executive conclusion

The strongest available evidence does **not** support choosing a personal agent harness by a single public benchmark, a subjective impression of speed, or an aggregate “quality” score. The best-supported target is **verified useful work delivered per unit of the owner's scarce attention**, measured on a representative distribution of the owner's real tasks and constrained by reliability, forbidden side effects, elapsed time, and cost. That conclusion follows from four independent bodies of evidence:

1. Real-work randomized trials show that assistance can improve speed and quality in some task distributions and degrade them in others. Noy and Zhang measured 40% lower completion time and 18% higher quality on bounded professional writing tasks, while METR measured a 19% slowdown for experienced maintainers on their own repositories with early-2025 tools; the BCG experiment found gains inside an uneven capability frontier and worse correctness outside it. The intervention, task distribution, user expertise, and outcome therefore matter more than the label “AI-assisted.” [S1] [S2] [S3]
2. Subjective belief is not a trustworthy primary outcome. In METR's early-2025 RCT, developers expected a 24% speedup and retrospectively believed they had received a 20% speedup, while measured completion time was 19% worse. [S2]
3. Agent success is stochastic and average one-shot accuracy hides operational flakiness. τ-bench introduced `pass^k`, the probability of succeeding on every one of *k* trials, and reported retail `pass^8` below 25% even when individual success was much higher. NIST likewise treats repeated attempts on an item as clustered Bernoulli trials and warns that naive standard errors are invalid. [S4] [S5]
4. Benchmark accuracy is not equivalent to generalized or deployed usefulness. NIST distinguishes accuracy on a fixed benchmark from accuracy over a target population; Kapoor et al. show that agent benchmarks commonly omit cost, adequate holdouts, and reproducibility; METR explicitly warns that benchmark tasks can either overestimate or underestimate real-world impact. [S5] [S6] [S2]

The concrete recommendation is therefore a thin, local, versioned evaluation loop riding the existing CLI/hook bus: a small task bank sampled from real work; executable end-state and side-effect graders; repeated paired trials with pinned conditions; trace and cost capture; a small amount of blinded owner review; and a change gate based on a scorecard, not a scalar. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to identify materially harmful or clearly helpful harness changes after enough representative trials, because it combines the outcome, reliability, efficiency, and human-impact signals validated separately in [S2], [S4], [S5], [S7], and [S8]. It is **UNVERIFIED** for this owner until a prospective experiment runs the old and candidate harness on the same stratified task sample and checks whether offline results predict subsequent accepted real-work outcomes.

## 1. What is actually established

### 1.1 The unit of evaluation is the whole system in context

An agent result is produced jointly by the model, instructions, scaffold/harness, tools, environment, and grader. Anthropic explicitly defines agent evaluation as evaluation of the harness and model together. Changing the model while claiming a harness improvement, or changing tools while claiming a model improvement, does not identify the cause. [S7]

NIST's evaluation guidance is consistent with this system view: evaluation objectives determine the appropriate target quantity, and contextual factors determine which measurements are meaningful. Its statistical report distinguishes performance conditional on a fixed benchmark from performance over a broader population of similar items. [S5] [S9]

**Consequence:** every comparative run must record at least harness revision, model/version, model parameters, task revision, environment/container revision, permissions, tool/MCP availability, grader revision, trial seed when exposed, start/end times, and raw trace. Without that provenance, a score difference is not attributable to the harness. This is a direct methodological implication of [S5], [S7], and [S10].

### 1.2 Real usefulness is task-distribution-specific

There is no externally established universal proxy that maps a generic agent score to personal usefulness:

- On incentivized professional writing tasks, ChatGPT reduced average completion time by 40% and increased rated quality by 18%. [S1]
- In a preregistered experiment with 758 consultants, AI users completed 12.2% more tasks and were 25.1% faster on tasks inside the studied capability frontier, while the study also found degraded correctness on a task outside that frontier. [S3]
- In METR's early-2025 RCT, 16 experienced open-source developers completed 246 real issues from repositories they knew well; allowing AI increased completion time by 19%. METR explicitly says benchmark realism and missing human interaction can bias capability estimates in either direction. [S2]
- A field study of customer-support work found an average 15% productivity increase, with heterogeneous effects by worker experience and skill. [S11]

These studies do not contradict one another: they use different tasks, users, tools, dates, and dependent variables. Together, they establish that the target distribution must resemble the owner's actual work and that results should be stratified by task family and difficulty rather than pooled blindly. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S11]

### 1.3 Direct outcomes dominate activity proxies

For tool-using agents, a statement that an action succeeded is weaker evidence than the resulting state. τ-bench grades the final database state against an annotated goal state. WebArena uses functional state checks, including backend state for tasks that modify data. Anthropic recommends end-state verification and notes that a conversational agent can claim a booking exists even when the database says otherwise. [S4] [S7] [S12]

For coding, tests provide a strong but incomplete outcome signal. Anthropic recommends combining executable tests with code-quality or transcript checks where needed; METR later found that many SWE-bench-passing patches would not be merged by maintainers, demonstrating that passing benchmark tests and being acceptable work products are not identical. [S7] [S13]

For research, NIST's report-evaluation framework decomposes quality into completeness, accuracy, and verifiability, using required information “nuggets” and mappings from claims to sources. Anthropic similarly recommends groundedness, coverage, source quality, and human-calibrated rubrics. [S14] [S7]

**Established ordering of evidence for this use case:** executable end-state checks and forbidden-side-effect checks first; owner/expert acceptance second; human-calibrated rubric judgments for irreducibly subjective properties third; trace/activity measures only as diagnostics. This ordering is supported by the strengths and limitations of deterministic, model, and human graders catalogued in [S7], plus the state-based designs in [S4] and [S12].

### 1.4 Productivity is multidimensional

The SPACE framework's central result is that developer productivity cannot be represented by a single activity measure or dimension. It includes satisfaction/well-being, performance, activity, communication/collaboration, and efficiency/flow. [S15]

Agent-specific research reaches the same conclusion from another direction. “AI Agents That Matter” finds that accuracy-only optimization favors unnecessarily complex and costly systems and recommends jointly considering accuracy and cost. Anthropic's examples track turns, tool calls, token use, latency, outcome state, and quality, while keeping capability and regression suites separate. [S6] [S7]

For a personal harness, the minimum useful scorecard therefore needs:

- **Outcome:** did the requested real-world state or artifact become correct and acceptable?
- **Reliability:** does it succeed repeatedly, not merely once?
- **Owner effort:** how many active owner minutes were spent prompting, clarifying, monitoring, reviewing, and repairing?
- **Elapsed time:** how long until an acceptable result existed?
- **Cost/resources:** tokens, API dollars where available, tool calls, and retries per accepted outcome.
- **Risk:** forbidden or destructive side effects, security-policy violations, fabricated completion, and silent corruption.
- **Experience:** owner-rated cognitive load, trust calibration, and willingness to delegate the same task again.

Only the first five need to be present on every routine run; risk is a hard gate, and experience can be sampled periodically. The need for multiple dimensions is established; the exact owner-specific weighting is not externally established. [S6] [S7] [S15]

### 1.5 Repeated trials and uncertainty are mandatory

Agent output remains stochastic even under nominally deterministic settings. NIST notes that temperature zero does not guarantee full determinism and models repeated attempts as outcomes clustered within tasks. It warns that a naive standard error over all trial rows violates independence assumptions and that uncertainty is necessary to distinguish an actual difference from chance. [S5]

Two metrics answer different operational questions:

- `pass@k`: probability that **at least one** of *k* attempts succeeds. It measures the value of search/retry budget.
- `pass^k`: probability that **all** *k* attempts succeed. It measures repeatable reliability. τ-bench introduced the latter specifically for agent deployment reliability. [S4]

A personal harness normally needs `pass@1` and a repeated-success measure such as empirical `pass^3`. Reporting only `pass@k` rewards brute-force retry strategies and can conceal flakiness; reporting only a mean success rate conceals whether failures concentrate in high-value task families. This interpretation follows directly from the definitions and results in [S4] and the item-clustering analysis in [S5].

### 1.6 Holdouts, fresh tasks, and grader validation prevent self-deception

Kapoor et al. identify inadequate or absent holdouts, benchmark overfitting, cost omission, and non-reproducible comparisons as recurring agent-evaluation defects. [S6] SWE-rebench reports that continuously refreshed tasks can reveal performance inflation on static public benchmarks due to contamination. [S16]

Anthropic reports concrete grader failures: ambiguous requirements, overly rigid numeric matching, stochastic targets, and thresholds that contradicted task instructions materially changed scores. Its operating rule is to read traces and investigate failures rather than accept scores at face value. [S7]

This establishes three separate controls:

1. Keep a **development set** visible for iteration and a **holdout set** unavailable to the change author until the decision run.
2. Add **fresh real tasks** over time, because a frozen set gradually becomes a prompt-tuning target.
3. Validate every grader with known-pass, known-fail, and plausible-alternative controls, then inspect a sample of traces and grader disagreements.

### 1.7 Human judgment is necessary but must be instrumented

Human review is the gold standard for expert-user acceptability, but it is slow and can be inconsistent. Anthropic recommends calibrating model graders against human graders and tracking inter-annotator agreement. [S7]

LLM judges can scale subjective assessment: the MT-Bench study found over 80% agreement with human preferences in its setting. The same study documents position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning biases, and found that reference-guided judging reduced a studied failure rate from 70% to 15%. [S17]

Therefore, an LLM judge is evidence only after local calibration on the exact rubric and task family. It must not be the sole gate for destructive actions, factual correctness, or whether an executable outcome exists. This is supported independently by [S7] and [S17].

## 2. What is promising but unproven

### 2.1 “Verified useful task yield per owner-hour” as the north star

**very likely (80-90%)**, based on the divergence between self-report and elapsed-time outcomes in METR [S2], the simultaneous speed-and-quality effects in Noy/Zhang and BCG [S1] [S3], and the accuracy/cost critique in Kapoor et al. [S6], a metric centered on **accepted outcomes per owner-active hour** will predict this owner's experienced usefulness better than benchmark accuracy alone.

Define, without collapsing the displayed components:

`useful_yield = Σ(task_value_weight × verified_acceptance × no_harm) / owner_active_hours`

Record `elapsed_hours` and `direct_cost` alongside it, not inside it. `task_value_weight` should use a small ordinal scale fixed before the run (for example 1 = convenience, 3 = routine value, 9 = high value) and results must also be shown unweighted. A weighted metric can be gamed by changing weights, so weights are governance, not learned truth.

This metric is **UNVERIFIED** because no study located validates this exact formula for a single-owner agent harness. The settling experiment is a prospective eight-week study: collect the formula and independent end-of-week owner rankings of which harness version produced more valuable work, then test rank correlation and inspect disagreements.

### 2.2 Correction burden as a leading indicator

**likely (55-80%)**, because METR observed a gap between perceived and measured productivity [S2] and Anthropic recommends trace inspection to diagnose genuine versus grading failures [S7], the fraction of tasks requiring material clarification, takeover, repair, or re-run will predict whether apparent autonomy actually saves attention.

Measure separately:

- clarification turns caused by missing or incorrect agent assumptions;
- owner interventions during execution;
- post-run review minutes;
- repair minutes;
- abandoned runs;
- false completion claims;
- repeat of an already-failed action or error fingerprint.

This is **UNVERIFIED** as a predictor for this owner. The settling experiment is to regress final owner-active minutes and accept/reject outcomes on these trace-derived counts over at least 50 heterogeneous real tasks, with tasks clustered by family.

### 2.3 Reliability-adjusted capability (`pass^k`) for delegation decisions

**very likely (80-90%)**, based on τ-bench's large collapse from individual success to repeated success [S4] and NIST's treatment of trial variance [S5], `pass^3` or the lower confidence bound on per-task-family success will better predict “can I safely delegate this repeatedly?” than a single successful demo.

This remains **UNVERIFIED** for personal-harness tasks because τ-bench is simulated customer service, not this owner's workflow. The settling experiment is to run three independently reset trials per task on a stratified suite, then compare `pass@1` and `pass^3` with the failure rate of the next ten naturally occurring tasks in each family.

### 2.4 Human-in-the-loop switchback trials on live work

**likely (55-80%)**, because randomized assignment identified effects that forecasts and retrospective impressions missed [S2], alternating stable and candidate harness configurations across naturally arriving tasks will estimate net personal impact better than retrospective before/after comparisons.

The design is promising but operationally difficult: tasks are heterogeneous, learning carries over, blinding is incomplete, and model/provider drift can coincide with the trial. It is **UNVERIFIED** for a single owner. The settling experiment is a preregistered switchback: for safe, reversible tasks, randomly assign A/B before seeing the task in full; pin model and tools; record baseline difficulty before reveal; analyze paired or stratified outcomes; and stop immediately on a harm gate.

### 2.5 A small, discriminating task subset for frequent checks

**likely (55-80%)**, based on item-response logic in NIST [S5] and recent agent-evaluation work reporting that historically intermediate-difficulty items preserve rankings with fewer tasks [S18], a small smoke suite composed mainly of non-saturated tasks can provide fast directional feedback between full runs.

The cited efficient-agent-benchmark result is recent and does not establish validity for a shifting personal task distribution. This proposal is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by backtesting whether rankings from the proposed smoke subset match full-suite rankings across at least six historical harness/model configurations. The full suite and fresh holdout remain authoritative.

### 2.6 LLM-assisted qualitative grading

**likely (55-80%)** to reduce review cost on style, instruction following, research coverage, and maintainability, based on MT-Bench human agreement [S17], Anthropic's applied guidance [S7], and PaperBench's explicit meta-evaluation of its judge [S19]. It is not established as a replacement for owner review.

It is **UNVERIFIED** until the exact judge prompt is tested on locally double-scored examples. The settling experiment is at least 50 anonymized, order-swapped pairwise comparisons, measuring agreement with the owner, false-pass rate, false-fail rate, and position consistency. Keep the judge only if its errors are acceptable for the use and recalibrate whenever the judge model changes.

## 3. What is hype, and how to tell

### 3.1 “One benchmark score tells us which harness is best”

This is unsupported. NIST distinguishes fixed-benchmark accuracy from generalized accuracy [S5]; METR documents the uncertain translation from benchmarks to real-work impact [S2]; Kapoor et al. document absent holdouts, cost omission, and reproducibility failures [S6]. A claim is hype when it omits the target task population, scaffold/model versions, cost, repeated trials, uncertainty, and the raw trajectories needed to audit failures.

### 3.2 “The agent solved it once, so it is dependable”

This confuses capability with reliability. τ-bench's `pass^8` result demonstrates that repeated dependable success can be far lower than one-trial success. [S4] A demo without repeated independently reset trials is evidence of possibility, not delegation readiness.

### 3.3 “Pass@k proves production quality”

`pass@k` measures whether retry/search budget can produce at least one success. It can increase when a system emits more attempts without becoming more reliable per attempt. τ-bench's `pass^k` exists to answer the opposite question: whether all repeated attempts succeed. [S4] A report that advertises pass@k but omits attempt cost, selection method, pass@1, and repeated-success reliability is optimizing a showcase rather than the default user experience.

### 3.4 “Users feel faster, therefore they are faster”

METR mechanically falsified this inference in its early-2025 RCT: measured slowdown coexisted with expected and retrospective perceived speedups. [S2] Satisfaction matters as one component of experience, but self-report cannot be the sole productivity measure.

### 3.5 “LLM-as-judge is objective”

LLM judges are scalable, not objective. Controlled research documents position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning biases. [S17] A judge result is hype when the report omits the judge version, prompt/rubric, references, position swaps, human calibration set, agreement, and error matrix.

### 3.6 “More tool calls, more autonomy, or more tokens means better work”

Turns, tokens, tool calls, and latency are diagnostic or efficiency measures; none proves a correct end state. Anthropic explicitly separates transcript metrics from outcome graders. [S7] They become Goodhart targets if promoted to success measures. A harness that uses fewer calls for the same verified outcome is generally preferable on cost and attack-surface grounds, but correctness and owner effort must remain primary. [S6] [S7]

### 3.7 “A giant evaluation platform is necessary”

No evidence located shows that a personal harness needs a new daemon, vector store, workflow engine, or observability SaaS to evaluate itself. Official Claude Code supports non-interactive JSON/stream-JSON output with session, usage, and cost metadata, and lifecycle hooks receive structured event data and transcript paths. [S20] [S21] Inspect AI demonstrates that local, file-backed eval logs and dataframe analysis are sufficient for substantial evaluation work. [S22]

The anti-overfit counterexample is concrete: the official mini-SWE-agent repository describes an approximately 100-line agent core scoring above 74% on SWE-bench Verified, while emphasizing simple linear history and a shell-only interface. [S23] That score does not prove mini-SWE-agent is best for this owner, but it disproves the premise that architectural bulk is required for strong benchmark capability.

### 3.8 “Tests passed, therefore the change is done”

Executable tests are high-value evidence, but incomplete specifications can accept undesirable patches or reject valid alternatives. Anthropic documents grader-induced score swings [S7], and METR found that many test-passing SWE-bench patches would not be merged by maintainers [S13]. “Done” requires outcome checks plus no regression, no forbidden effects, and acceptance criteria appropriate to the task.

## 4. Specific apparatus this owner's harness should adopt

### 4.0 Design constraints and anti-overfit gate

Every proposal below rides the existing CLI, file, hook, skill, or MCP bus. None introduces a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The apparatus is deliberately file-backed and disposable. This satisfies the anti-overfit gate and is supported by the native structured-output/hook surfaces [S20] [S21], the local-log precedent in Inspect [S22], and the minimal-agent counterexample [S23].

### 4.1 UNVERIFIED — Native trace and run-envelope capture — **NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Enable programmatic runs with JSON or stream-JSON output and capture native session ID, result, usage, cost metadata, retry events, and timestamps. Use command hooks only to append lifecycle/tool envelopes that are not already present. Claude Code documents structured JSON output, cost metadata, and retry events; hooks receive `session_id`, `transcript_path`, `cwd`, event name, tool name, and tool input. [S20] [S21]

Store one immutable directory per trial:

```text
runs/<run-id>/
  manifest.json        # harness/model/task/grader/environment revisions
  prompt.txt
  events.jsonl         # native/hook envelopes
  transcript.ref       # native transcript path or copied stable artifact
  stdout.json
  stderr.txt
  outcome.json         # machine graders
  human.json           # optional owner review
```

**very likely (80-90%)** to supply enough provenance to debug and compare trials because those fields are native and match the trace requirements in Anthropic's evaluation taxonomy [S7]. The remaining premise—whether every provider/tool event needed by this harness is exposed—is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a canary session containing a known successful tool call, a known tool failure, a retryable API error, and a normal stop, then reconcile native output, hook log, and transcript.

### 4.2 UNVERIFIED — Versioned owner-task bank — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Create a file-backed JSONL catalog, not a service. Each task should contain:

```json
{
  "id": "family-immutable-id",
  "family": "coding|research|ops|writing|planning|computer-use",
  "prompt": "...",
  "fixture": "fixtures/...",
  "value_weight": 1,
  "estimated_owner_minutes_without_agent": 45,
  "success_checks": ["..."],
  "forbidden_effects": ["..."],
  "max_owner_interventions": 1,
  "max_elapsed_minutes": 30,
  "grader_version": "...",
  "split": "dev|regression|holdout",
  "source": "sanitized-real-failure|fresh-real-task|synthetic-control"
}
```

Start with 30–50 tasks spanning actual high-frequency and high-value families, not generic trivia. Keep three logical suites:

- **Regression:** previously reliable behavior and repaired real failures; target near-100% no-harm success.
- **Capability:** valuable tasks near the current frontier; deliberately non-saturated.
- **Holdout/fresh:** withheld tasks and newly sampled real work; used only for release decisions.

Anthropic independently recommends separate capability and regression suites [S7]; NIST emphasizes realistic scenarios [S9]; Kapoor et al. and SWE-rebench support holdouts and fresh tasks [S6] [S16].

**very likely (80-90%)** to improve external validity relative to generic leaderboards, provided task-family weights mirror real use. The representativeness premise is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it quarterly by comparing suite family/value/difficulty proportions with a blinded sample of the prior month's natural tasks and refreshing mismatched strata.

### 4.3 UNVERIFIED — Deterministic outcome and harm graders — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build a small grader adapter that runs task-declared commands against a reset fixture and emits only typed assertions:

```json
{
  "task_id": "...",
  "trial_id": "...",
  "required": {"passed": 7, "failed": 0},
  "forbidden": {"triggered": 0},
  "acceptance": true,
  "evidence": [{"check": "tests", "exit": 0, "artifact_hash": "..."}]
}
```

Prefer, in order: end-state assertions; pass-to-pass plus fail-to-pass tests; schema/type/static checks; citation-to-claim checks for research; exact checks only where the answer is genuinely exact. Never infer success from the agent's final prose. This ordering follows τ-bench, WebArena, Anthropic, and NIST report evaluation. [S4] [S12] [S7] [S14]

Every grader must pass two mechanically distinct controls before use: a known-good artifact/state and a deliberately wrong or harmful artifact/state. Add a plausible alternative solution to detect over-strict grading. Anthropic's documented grader bugs make this a release requirement, not polish. [S7]

**very likely (80-90%)** to reduce false completion and grader ambiguity on executable tasks. Coverage for open-ended tasks is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by double-scoring 20 outputs per open-ended family with the deterministic checks and owner review, then catalog false passes and false fails.

### 4.4 UNVERIFIED — Thin paired eval runner — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Implement one script with four verbs: `validate`, `run`, `compare`, and `promote`. It should invoke the existing agent CLI, never become an agent runtime. For each comparison:

1. Pin model/version, permissions, tools, task/grader revisions, working fixture, and time/token budget.
2. Randomize A/B execution order within task to reduce time/provider-order bias.
3. Reset the fixture independently for every trial.
4. Run at least three trials per task for candidate release comparisons; a one-trial smoke run is diagnostic only.
5. Capture native trace/cost envelopes.
6. Run deterministic graders, then optional blinded qualitative grading.
7. Emit per-task paired rows and an aggregate scorecard with uncertainty.

Repeated trials and clustering are required by [S4] and [S5]; provenance and trace inspection by [S7]; reproducible configuration by [S6].

**very likely (80-90%)** to detect large regressions and gross efficiency changes on covered task families. Sensitivity to small improvements is **UNVERIFIED** and depends on variance and sample size; settle it by estimating variance from the first full baseline and publishing the minimum detectable effect before the next comparison.

### 4.5 UNVERIFIED — Scorecard and release gate — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Do not compute a single hidden composite. Report this table for the whole suite and every task family:

| Dimension | Primary metric | Required companion |
|---|---|---|
| Correctness | strict verified acceptance rate | partial-credit rubric only as diagnostic |
| Reliability | empirical `pass^3` and per-family lower bound | `pass@1`; failure concentration |
| Owner effort | median and total active owner minutes per accepted task | clarify/review/repair breakdown |
| Time | median elapsed time to accepted result | timeout/abandon rate |
| Cost | tokens and dollars per accepted result | attempts/tool calls/retries |
| Harm | forbidden-side-effect count and severity | near misses/blocked actions |
| Experience | blinded owner preference; willingness to delegate again | confidence and cognitive-load rating |

Default release gate:

1. **Hard stop:** any new severe forbidden side effect, data loss, secret exposure, or false-success state.
2. **Regression:** no previously stable regression family may show a material decline outside its preregistered tolerance.
3. **Usefulness:** candidate must improve verified acceptance, owner-active time, or cost on the holdout/fresh suite without a material decline in the other two.
4. **Reliability:** improvement must not be purchased by lower repeated success.
5. **Audit:** manually inspect all A/B disagreements, all harms, and a random sample of agreements before promotion.

The scorecard structure is well supported by [S4], [S6], [S7], and [S15]. Exact tolerances are **UNVERIFIED** and owner-dependent. **likely (55-80%)**, based on these sources, explicit Pareto-style gates will make decisions more robust than a weighted scalar; settle this by recording both the gate's choice and the owner's blinded holistic choice over six candidate comparisons, then revising tolerances only from preregistered disagreement analysis.

### 4.6 UNVERIFIED — Statistics that match the design — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

The comparison report should include:

- paired per-task A-minus-B differences;
- bootstrap confidence intervals resampling **tasks/families**, not individual trials as independent rows;
- exact counts and intervals for strict success and harm rates;
- median plus distribution for skewed time/cost, not just means;
- task-family breakdowns and the worst-family change;
- both fixed-suite accuracy and explicitly labeled target-population estimates;
- model/harness/task/grader versions and every exclusion.

NIST establishes that attempts cluster within tasks, naive standard errors can be invalid, uncertainty is necessary, and fixed-benchmark versus generalized accuracy are different estimands. [S5] For a small personal suite, a task-cluster bootstrap is simpler than making a generalized linear mixed model the operational dependency; the raw paired table should remain the primary audit surface.

**likely (55-80%)** that this small statistical layer is adequate for large practical changes while preserving interpretability. It is **UNVERIFIED** at the eventual sample size; settle it with simulation using observed task/trial variance, checking interval coverage and false-promotion rate under a null A/A experiment. If the A/A false-positive behavior is poor, use the NIST-described hierarchical/GLMM approach rather than inventing a custom significance rule. [S5]

### 4.7 UNVERIFIED — Lightweight owner review and calibrated judge — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For subjective tasks, render A/B artifacts anonymously with randomized left/right position. Ask the owner only:

1. Accept A, accept B, accept both, or accept neither?
2. Which required less repair, and estimated repair minutes?
3. Which would you delegate again?
4. Optional one-sentence reason/failure label.

Use a model judge only after calibration, as a triage signal and for routine regression scoring. Run position swaps, keep the judge/model/prompt version, and sample human rechecks. MT-Bench documents both scalability and bias [S17]; Anthropic requires human calibration [S7]; PaperBench explicitly evaluates its judge separately [S19].

**likely (55-80%)** to reduce the owner's grading burden without losing large preference differences after calibration. It is **UNVERIFIED** for this owner's standards; settle it with the 50-pair calibration experiment described in §2.6 and require a prespecified maximum false-pass rate.

### 4.8 UNVERIFIED — Failure taxonomy and eval flywheel — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Every failed or owner-corrected real task should receive one primary label and may become a sanitized regression task:

```text
spec/clarification | context/retrieval | planning | tool-selection | tool-execution
environment/provider | permission/policy | verification | premature-stop
false-success | output-quality | grader-bug | harness-infrastructure
```

Record the earliest causal failure, not every downstream symptom. Promotion into the suite requires a reproducible fixture and a validated grader; otherwise retain it as qualitative evidence. Anthropic recommends trace inspection and treating eval suites as maintained living artifacts [S7].

**very likely (80-90%)** to make recurring failure modes visible and convert production corrections into regression coverage, because this is the standard eval-maintenance loop described in [S7]. The taxonomy's categories are **UNVERIFIED**; settle them after 100 labeled failures by measuring inter-review consistency, “other” frequency, and whether labels predict a distinct remedy. Merge categories that do not.

### 4.9 UNVERIFIED — Live-work switchback after offline qualification — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

After a candidate clears deterministic offline gates, evaluate it on safe natural work:

- Randomly assign eligible tasks to stable or candidate before the task is solved.
- Exclude destructive/high-stakes work from experimentation.
- Pin the model and provider where possible; record unavoidable drift.
- Measure owner-active minutes, elapsed time, accepted outcome, repair, abandonment, cost, and preference.
- Review weekly, but use a preregistered stop/promotion rule rather than stopping when a favorable result appears.

Randomized real-work assignment is the strongest available design for net productivity in this domain; it revealed effects that self-report missed in METR [S2] and underlies the writing and BCG results [S1] [S3].

**likely (55-80%)** to give the most decision-relevant evidence available to a single owner, but it is **UNVERIFIED** under small samples, learning, and provider drift. Settle it with an initial A/A period to validate timing and assignment instrumentation, followed by a minimum 30-task A/B pilot stratified by family. Treat estimates as provisional until intervals exclude the owner's practical-indifference range.

### 4.10 UNVERIFIED — Small external sentinel suite, not leaderboard governance — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Keep a small set of external tasks only to detect gross capability drift and compare model/scaffold changes under a common interface. Candidates include a handful of GAIA-style tool/research tasks [S24], SWE-bench or fresh SWE-rebench coding tasks [S16] [S25], and τ-bench-style stateful tool-policy tasks [S4]. Do not mix their scores into the owner-usefulness gate.

**likely (55-80%)** to catch broad regressions missed by a narrow personal suite, because these benchmarks exercise complementary capabilities. It is **UNVERIFIED** whether the added runtime predicts this owner's outcomes; settle it by measuring correlation with owner-suite changes over at least six versions and retire sentinels with no diagnostic value.

### 4.11 UNVERIFIED — Explicitly reject a new eval daemon/platform — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Record an architecture decision that evaluation remains local, file-backed, and invoked through existing CLI/hooks until one of these falsifiable triggers occurs:

- trial concurrency or artifact volume exceeds reliable local processing;
- more than one owner needs shared access control and concurrent annotation;
- trace querying demonstrably dominates evaluation labor;
- retention/compliance requirements cannot be met by files.

**very likely (80-90%)**, based on the existing native JSON/hooks surfaces [S20] [S21], Inspect's local log model [S22], and mini-SWE-agent's simplicity/performance counterexample [S23], that a daemon would add more failure surface than value at the initial personal scale. This is **UNVERIFIED** at future volume; the triggers above are the settling experiment. This proposal explicitly avoids all previously rejected components named in the anti-overfit gate.

## 5. A concrete first 30-day measurement program

This section is a proposed execution sequence, not an externally established protocol.

### Days 1–3: define outcomes before instrumenting everything

1. Select six task families that cover most valuable actual work.
2. Write five tasks per family: two regression/failure-derived, two current-frontier, one holdout.
3. Specify required end state, forbidden effects, owner-time fields, and task value before any candidate is run.
4. Validate each grader on known-good and known-bad controls.

**very likely (80-90%)** to yield more signal than exhaustive event telemetry without task definitions, because external guidance prioritizes evaluation objectives and outcomes over activity metrics. [S7] [S9]

### Days 4–7: baseline the stable harness

1. Run the stable harness three times per task with reset fixtures.
2. Capture native JSON/trace/cost data.
3. Owner-review every failure, every grader disagreement, and 20% of passes.
4. Freeze baseline task/grader versions.
5. Compute variance, saturation, and family-level failure concentration.

Do not tune the harness during baseline collection. **very likely (80-90%)** this is necessary for an interpretable comparator because changing the system during measurement destroys attribution. [S5] [S7]

### Days 8–14: run one candidate, not a bundle

Change one coherent mechanism at a time. Run A/B in randomized order on the same tasks and budgets. A bundle may be appropriate when components are inseparable, but then the conclusion is only about the bundle.

**very likely (80-90%)** that controlled comparisons improve causal attribution; this follows from the system-confounding problem described in §1.1 and the randomized designs in [S1], [S2], and [S3].

### Days 15–21: validate the measurement itself

1. Run an A/A comparison to estimate false alarms.
2. Inject one known tool failure, one known harmful outcome, one valid alternative output, and one deliberately overlong/retry-heavy trajectory.
3. Confirm the instrumentation sees each event and that scorecard dimensions react only where expected.
4. Blind and position-swap qualitative comparisons.

**very likely (80-90%)** to catch detector and grader defects before they become harness conclusions, based on Anthropic's documented eval bugs and trace-audit recommendation. [S7]

### Days 22–30: safe live-work pilot

Randomize at least 30 eligible natural tasks between stable and candidate, stratified by family. Record owner-active minutes contemporaneously, not from end-of-week recall. Maintain the hard harm gate. At the end, compare offline ranking, live accepted outcomes, active time, and blinded preference.

**likely (55-80%)** to reveal whether the offline suite transfers, based on real-work RCT evidence [S1] [S2] [S3], but 30 tasks will often leave wide uncertainty. The result should promote only a large, consistent benefit; otherwise continue sampling rather than declare equivalence.

## 6. Minimal report contract for every claimed improvement

No harness change should be called “better” unless its report includes:

1. The exact claim and target task population.
2. Stable and candidate revision identifiers.
3. Pinned model, tools, permissions, environment, task, and grader versions.
4. Inclusion/exclusion rules chosen before results.
5. Per-task raw outcomes for all trials.
6. Verified acceptance, repeated reliability, owner-active time, elapsed time, cost, harms, and preference.
7. Task-clustered uncertainty and family breakdowns.
8. All grader disagreements and severe failures.
9. Manual trace-audit sample and known-good/known-bad grader controls.
10. A statement of what changed besides the harness.
11. Development-set, regression-set, and holdout/fresh-set results kept distinct.
12. A decision: promote, reject, or insufficient evidence—never “promising” without a next experiment.

This contract synthesizes reproducibility and holdout requirements [S6], statistical validity [S5], grader/trace validation [S7], and multidimensional productivity [S15].

## 7. What metrics actually predict usefulness?

The external evidence supports the following priority order:

### Tier 1 — closest to usefulness

1. **Accepted, verified task completion on representative real work.** Directly measures whether useful work exists; supported by state-based agent benchmarks and real-work experiments. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S4] [S12]
2. **Net owner-active time per accepted outcome.** Measures the scarce human input after prompting, supervision, review, and repair; direct timing outperformed subjective impressions in METR. [S2]
3. **Forbidden-side-effect-free success.** Prevents an accuracy gain from concealing damage; state-based benchmarks and Anthropic's multidimensional graders support explicit side-effect checks. [S4] [S7] [S12]
4. **Repeated reliable success (`pass^k` or lower-bound success).** Predicts whether delegation works consistently rather than once. [S4] [S5]

### Tier 2 — important constraints and mediators

5. **Elapsed time to acceptable outcome**, separated from owner-active time.
6. **Cost/tokens per accepted outcome**, not per raw run. [S6] [S7]
7. **Correction, intervention, retry, and abandonment burden**, derived from traces and owner timestamps. [S7]
8. **Blinded owner preference and willingness to delegate again**, used as an acceptance/experience signal rather than a replacement for timing and correctness. [S7] [S15] [S17]

### Tier 3 — diagnostic, not targets

9. Tool-call error rates, repeated error fingerprints, context growth, tool-selection confusion, clarification count, turns, token counts, and latency decomposition. Anthropic treats these as transcript metrics useful for diagnosis, not proof of outcome. [S7]
10. Public benchmark scores. Useful as external sentinels and capability probes, not direct estimates of this owner's net benefit. [S2] [S5] [S6]

No located source establishes a universal numerical threshold at which any of these guarantees personal usefulness. The only defensible answer is a validated chain: representative task → verified outcome → measured owner effort → repeated reliability → prospective transfer to live work.

## 8. Gaps and what could not be determined

1. **No validated universal “personal agent usefulness” metric was found.** The proposed useful-yield formula is **UNVERIFIED**; the eight-week prospective correlation experiment in §2.1 would settle whether it tracks this owner's holistic judgment.
2. **The number of trials and tasks required for this owner cannot be determined ex ante.** NIST establishes the need to model uncertainty but sample size depends on observed variance, effect size, clustering, and acceptable error. [S5] An A/A baseline plus simulation is required.
3. **The current native event coverage of every model provider, MCP, subagent, compaction, and failure mode was not verified.** Official Claude Code documents broad hooks and structured output [S20] [S21], but a canary reconciliation experiment is required before claiming complete telemetry.
4. **The transfer validity of τ-bench `pass^k` to heterogeneous personal tasks is unknown.** Its logic is strong and its domain evidence is direct [S4], but three-trial personal-suite results must be prospectively compared with natural-work failures.
5. **The long-term effect of harness assistance on maintenance burden was not established.** The located RCTs measure bounded tasks or relatively short horizons [S1] [S2] [S3] [S11]. A 30/90-day rework and defect follow-up is needed.
6. **The best owner-specific task-family weights are unknown.** Frequency, value, risk, and annoyance may imply different priorities. Report unweighted and weighted results until prospective owner rankings validate a weighting scheme.
7. **LLM-judge calibration will drift with judge updates.** The exact recalibration frequency is not established. Version changes and observed disagreement spikes should trigger a fresh calibration set. [S7] [S17]
8. **Whether an external benchmark sentinel adds decision value is unknown.** Correlation across multiple harness versions is the settling experiment; absent correlation, remove it.
9. **Late-2025/2026 developer-productivity estimates are affected by participation/selection concerns.** METR itself changed its experiment design after developers declined no-AI work, so those newer estimates should not be treated as a clean universal effect. [S26]
10. **No evidence was found that an evaluation daemon, vector database, trained critic, observability SaaS, or durable workflow engine improves evaluation validity at personal scale.** This is not proof of absence; it is a research gap. A build-large proposal would require a measured local bottleneck and a comparative pilot, neither currently exists in this external lens.
