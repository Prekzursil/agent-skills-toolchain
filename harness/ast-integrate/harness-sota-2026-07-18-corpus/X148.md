# X148 — Cross-domain borrow: cartographic generalisation → harness observability without a baseline

## Unit boundary and result

This is an ideation unit. It deliberately does **not** inspect the local harness, corpus, audit findings, or repository; other units own that grounding work. It researches a mechanism from cartography, tests whether the structural analogy transfers, and specifies a falsifiable harness design. The pinned statement that the target situation has “no baseline” is treated as the problem premise, not as a locally re-measured absence.

**Verdict:** the borrow transfers usefully, but only to the *representation and diagnosis* half of observability. It does **not** solve causal attribution. Cartography's specific useful mechanism is an **anchor-scale multi-representation database (MRDB) with explicit, constraint-preserving generalisation rules**. It keeps a detailed source representation, derives purpose- and scale-specific representations through named operations, and tests the derived representations against preservation and legibility constraints. That can turn raw harness events into several honest levels of detail without letting aggregation silently erase important failures. It cannot tell us whether a harness change helped when no historical measurement or counterfactual exists.

That negative boundary is strong: it is **almost certain (90-99%)**, because cartographic MRDB work explicitly derives intermediate representations from a most-detailed database, cartographic quality assessment compares initial and final states, and causal-impact literature independently says a post-treatment observation cannot identify the missing counterfactual ([Girres & Touya, ICC 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/638.html); [Bard, *Quality Assessment of Cartographic Generalisation*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9671.2004.00168.x); [World Bank, *Impact Evaluation in Practice*, pp. 47–58](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstream/handle/10986/25030/9781464807794.pdf)). “Almost certain,” rather than 100%, leaves room for a narrow retrospective design that constructs a credible counterfactual from replayable historical tasks; that would be a newly created baseline, not something generalisation itself supplies.

## 1. What cartography has actually solved

### 1.1 The problem in its own field

A map has a finite display area. As scale decreases, faithfully drawing every source object produces clutter, collisions, and illegibility. The United States Geological Survey defines generalisation as reducing information and detail so complex features can be displayed in limited map space, using operations that select the proper amount and manner of content at a particular scale ([USGS, “Generalization”](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/science-topics/generalization)). Ordnance Survey gives a production-grade formulation: reduce scale and complexity while maintaining important elements and characteristics, using selection/omission, simplification, exaggeration, aggregation, symbolisation, and displacement ([OS VectorMap District product guide, “Generalisation,” pp. 6–7](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/product-support/user-guide/os-vectormap-district-product-guide.pdf)).

The important point is not “show fewer things.” It is **deliberate, typed loss under preservation constraints**:

- *Selection/omission* removes source features from the smaller-scale representation.
- *Simplification* reduces geometric detail while attempting to retain characteristic shape.
- *Aggregation* combines many small features into a larger representation.
- *Symbolisation/collapse* changes the representational type: a complex entity may become a point or standard symbol.
- *Exaggeration* enlarges a small but important feature so it survives scale reduction.
- *Displacement* moves a portrayal so overlapping features remain discernible.

Those are not interchangeable transformations. Each changes the claims a reader can safely make from the map. Ordnance Survey's operational definitions and examples provide direct evidence for all six ([OS VectorMap District product guide, pp. 6–7](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/product-support/user-guide/os-vectormap-district-product-guide.pdf)); its public explanation adds elimination, amalgamation, simplification, exaggeration, displacement, and collapse, including the polygon-to-point example ([Ordnance Survey, “What is Cartography?”, §4](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/what-is-cartography)).

### 1.2 The specific mechanism: anchor scales + multiple representations + explicit rules

An MRDB stores representations of the same real-world entities at different levels of detail. Girres and Touya state that an MRDB supports movement between scales, while generalisation derives intermediate representations from the most detailed database. Their ScaleMaster 2.0 formalises rules by scale range; for each land-use class, it can specify merging and minimum-area thresholds, then apply class merging, elimination, and line simplification ([Girres & Touya, ICC 2015, abstract and method](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/638.html)). ScaleMaster research similarly describes anchor databases compiled at specific resolutions and selection/elimination/symbol design across the scale range ([Brewer et al., “ScaleMaster: Multi-Scale Mapmaking…”](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2007/abstracts/html/10_Oral4_4_SCAL.htm)).

This mechanism has four parts:

1. **A source representation.** Generalised views are derived from detailed data; omission is not deletion from the source.
2. **Named anchor scales.** Certain representations are stable reference products, with intermediate levels derived by rules.
3. **A rule manifest indexed by feature class, scale, and purpose.** Different classes may be merged, retained, or removed differently; Girres and Touya's isolated urban polygon example shows why a single global threshold is inadequate ([ICC 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/638.html)).
4. **Preservation constraints.** Selection must reduce feature count while preserving hierarchy, density differences, and characteristic patterns. The ICA Commission gives road/river selection as a concrete example and describes simultaneous generalisation as optimizing competing analytical constraints ([ICA Commission on Multi-Scale Cartography, “Examples of results”](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html)).

Töpfer's Radical Law is a narrower, quantitative sub-mechanism: it estimates how many source objects to retain after a scale change with a square-root relationship between scale denominators. An applied hydrography study describes it as an empirically based estimate of feature count and also reports limited applicability at some large scales ([Wilmer & Brewer, “Application of the Radical Law…”](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVIII/part4/files/Wilmer.pdf)). It is useful here as evidence that cartography makes an *omission budget* explicit. It is not the main borrow because an event stream has no physical map denominator and because importance is not a simple function of display area.

### 1.3 Quality is purpose-relative, not “maximum detail”

Constraint-based cartographic generalisation explicitly trades readability against preservation. Harrie's method models simplification, smoothing, exaggeration, and displacement as a compromise among constraints; not all can be completely satisfied ([Harrie, “Weight-Setting and Quality Assessment in Simultaneous Graphic Generalization”](https://doi.org/10.1179/000870403225012925)). ISO 19157 likewise defines a structure for describing, evaluating, and reporting geographic-data quality, while explicitly declining to set universal minimum acceptable quality levels; sufficiency depends on product specification and application ([ISO 19157:2013 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)).

This is the field's most valuable conceptual move for observability: **a lower-detail representation is not inherently lower quality if it preserves the invariants needed for its declared purpose and exposes what it omitted.** Conversely, a numerically exact aggregate can be a poor map if it destroys the structure needed for diagnosis.

## 2. Structural correspondence to harness observability

| Cartography | Harness observability translation | Structural match |
|---|---|---|
| Real-world geographic entity | One task/run/tool call/retry/error/correction/outcome | Both are entities with identity and attributes. |
| Most-detailed source database | Append-only canonical event records plus outcome annotation | Both permit derived views without destroying source detail. |
| Map purpose | Decision question: release gate, incident triage, tool reliability, cost, owner-friction | Both require purpose-specific selection. |
| Map scale / display resolution | Analysis extent and detail budget: event → run → cohort → release/estate | Partial match: it is an analytic scale, not a physical ratio. |
| Generalisation operator | Retain, classify, aggregate, collapse, highlight, or separate events | Strong operational match if every transform is declared. |
| Landmark | Rare catastrophic event, owner correction, task failure, safety violation | Both are low-frequency features whose importance can dominate size/frequency. |
| Preservation constraint | Conservation, provenance, severity-survival, topology/retry-chain survival, cohort-stratification | Both prevent a readable view from becoming a misleading one. |
| Legend and scale statement | View manifest: population, window, filters, denominator, transform version, omissions | Both tell the reader what the representation means. |
| Drill from small to large scale | Summary → stratum → run → raw event | Both restore omitted detail on demand. |

The correspondence is **very likely (80-90%)** to be useful for *finding where aggregation changed the story*, because cartography already addresses the same information-shaping problem—bounded attention, multiple levels of detail, deliberate omission, and preservation of rare/structural features—and the proposed harness transforms are direct analogues of documented operators ([USGS generalisation operators](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/generalisation-operators); [ICA examples](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html)). This confidence does not extend to causal attribution.

## 3. Concrete translation

### UNVERIFIED recommendation A — Build a “Run Atlas” generalisation layer

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

UNVERIFIED premise: a canonical per-run event artifact already exists or can be emitted by an existing end-of-run hook without altering the agent runtime. The experiment that settles it is **E1 — Source-Layer Feasibility**: instrument 30 representative runs, verify event IDs, timestamps, operation/tool, result status, latency, retry-parent ID, task ID, harness-version ID, and outcome/correction labels are present, and report field completeness plus join success. If E1 fails, the proposal remains BUILD-SMALL only if those fields can be added within the existing hook bus; otherwise its tag must be re-estimated rather than silently retained.

Build one deterministic generaliser that reads canonical event JSON/JSONL and emits four linked representations plus a manifest:

1. **L0 — event/source scale.** Loss-minimal events, immutable IDs, raw error fingerprint references, retry edges, timestamps, harness version, task/cohort keys, and outcome annotations. This is the source database, not a user-facing dashboard.
2. **L1 — run scale.** A task timeline with contiguous successful calls collapsed, retry loops preserved as chains, severe/novel failures exaggerated into visible “landmarks,” and explicit counts for omitted routine events.
3. **L2 — cohort/change scale.** Stratified distributions by task family, tool/operation, complexity proxy, environment, and harness version. Show outcome, correction, error, retry, latency, and resource distributions—not only means. Every cell links to contributing run IDs.
4. **L3 — release/estate scale.** A small set of fixed outcome and harm measures, each with denominator, population, time window, uncertainty interval, and the largest heterogeneous strata. This is an index map, not the evidence itself.

The manifest is the analogue of scale, projection, and legend. For every representation it records: source schema/version; population and exclusion rule; time window; denominator; grouping keys; operator applied to each field; severity-retention rule; threshold version; count of source events represented and omitted; and links/checksums back to the source. A report with no manifest fails closed as “uninterpretable,” not “green.”

The required generalisation operators are:

- **select:** retain fields/events needed for the view's decision purpose;
- **classify:** map raw error fingerprints to versioned classes while keeping the fingerprint link;
- **aggregate:** compute counts, rates, quantiles, and confidence/credible intervals per declared stratum;
- **collapse:** turn repeated equivalent calls into one retry-chain glyph with length and terminal status;
- **exaggerate:** pin all catastrophic outcomes, owner corrections, and novel fingerprints even if their frequency would otherwise round to zero;
- **displace/separate:** split results by important strata when an aggregate overlaps conflicting regimes—for example, an overall improvement paired with a regression in one task family;
- **omit:** remove routine detail only while emitting an omitted-event count and reversible membership mapping.

The design confidence is **likely (55-80%)** to remain under roughly 200 lines for the deterministic transform and invariant checker, because it requires JSONL grouping, versioned rules, and linked Markdown/JSON outputs rather than a service or UI; this is UNVERIFIED until **E2 — 200-Line Spike** implements the narrowest version and measures nonblank source lines. If it exceeds the threshold, split configuration from executable lines but do not mislabel a materially larger implementation.

### Preservation constraints: the non-negotiable part

The Run Atlas is only a cartographic borrow if the following constraints are machine-checked at every scale:

1. **Population conservation:** `represented source IDs ∪ explicitly omitted source IDs = eligible source IDs`; sets must be disjoint.
2. **Count conservation:** total calls, failures, corrections, and terminal outcomes reconcile between adjacent levels.
3. **Landmark survival:** every event above a versioned harm threshold and every novel fingerprint appears at all coarser levels until explicitly acknowledged.
4. **Topology survival:** retry parent/child structure and terminal outcome cannot be replaced by a flat error count at L1.
5. **Denominator visibility:** a rate never appears without numerator, denominator, population, and window.
6. **Stratum audit:** L3 shows the largest direction-reversing or materially divergent strata rather than allowing an overall mean to hide them.
7. **Provenance reversibility:** each aggregate resolves to exact contributing run/event IDs.
8. **Rule stability:** scale rules are versioned and frozen during a comparison; changing the legend is a measurement change, not a performance change.

These constraints translate cartography's dual goals of readability and characteristic preservation ([Harrie](https://doi.org/10.1179/000870403225012925); [ICA network-selection example](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html)). Population conservation and provenance go beyond ordinary paper maps; the digital event domain permits loss accounting that physical portrayal often cannot offer.

### UNVERIFIED recommendation B — Pair the atlas with a prospective comparison protocol

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The atlas must label a change **“effect unknown—no counterfactual”** until one of these comparison designs exists:

1. **Preferred: randomized interleaving.** Assign eligible, comparable tasks to old versus candidate harness versions during the same time window, keeping model and environment fixed where possible.
2. **Fallback: paired replay.** Run a frozen task packet under both versions with randomized order, multiple repetitions for stochastic agents, and blinded outcome adjudication.
3. **Weaker: matched concurrent cohorts.** Compare simultaneously observed cohorts and disclose matching variables and residual imbalance.
4. **Descriptive only: pre/post window.** Show it, but never call the difference causal; the World Bank explicitly calls simple before/after comparisons risky “counterfeit” counterfactuals because time-varying factors can explain the change ([*Impact Evaluation in Practice*, pp. 53–58](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstream/handle/10986/25030/9781464807794.pdf)).

UNVERIFIED premise: enough eligible work can be routed or replayed without unacceptable owner cost or contamination. **E3 — Counterfactual Pilot** settles it: pre-register one change, primary outcome, guardrails, eligible-task definition, assignment/replay method, and stopping rule; collect at least the pre-registered sample; then report balance, cross-version contamination, outcome-label agreement, and effect uncertainty. No fixed sample size is asserted here because the required size depends on baseline event rate and minimum effect of interest.

Randomized interleaving is **very likely (80-90%)** to give the strongest attribution among these options when assignment is implemented correctly, based on experimental-design principles distinguishing correlation from causality and on the counterfactual framework ([NIST, “Experiments and Experimental Design”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section1/ppc136.htm); [World Bank, chapter 3](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstream/handle/10986/25030/9781464807794.pdf)). It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that randomized live routing will be operationally acceptable for this owner, because this unit has no evidence about traffic volume, task risk, or willingness to expose work to a candidate harness; E3 is the settling experiment.

## 4. What this would catch that ordinary flat summaries miss

The following are forward-looking hypotheses, not observed defects in the subject harness:

1. **Aggregation reversal.** Overall error rate falls while a high-value task family worsens. The stratum-audit constraint is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose a direction reversal present in recorded cohort fields, because L2 retains strata and L3 is required to surface divergent strata. It cannot expose an unrecorded task attribute; E1 tests coverage.
2. **Retry laundering.** Ten failed attempts followed by one success appears as “success.” Retry topology plus run-scale collapse is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose this when retry-parent or temporal-equivalence data are joinable, because the terminal success and chain length coexist instead of flattening to one status. E1 tests joinability.
3. **Rare-harm disappearance.** A severe one-off failure rounds to 0.0% or is omitted by top-N reporting. Landmark survival is **almost certain (90-99%)** to retain any event satisfying the configured threshold, because retention is a deterministic invariant; **threshold adequacy remains UNVERIFIED**, settled by **E4 — Omission Red Team**, which injects known severe, novel, low-frequency, and boundary-value fixtures and verifies survival at L1–L3.
4. **Taxonomy drift masquerading as improvement.** Reclassification makes an error class shrink. Frozen rule versions and raw-fingerprint provenance are **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal a mapping-version change, because comparisons reject differing manifests or display them as incomparable. E4 includes a deliberate taxonomy change.
5. **Denominator drift.** A rate improves because eligible calls or tasks changed. Population conservation and manifest denominators are **very likely (80-90%)** to expose recorded exclusions, since included and omitted IDs must reconcile. They cannot reveal events never captured; E1's independent field-completeness and join checks address that boundary.
6. **Tail-cost regression hidden by a mean.** Median latency/cost improves while p95/p99 or worst retry chains worsen. Multi-resolution distributions are **likely (55-80%)** to expose this if sufficient sample exists, because L2 retains quantiles and L3 carries guardrail tails; small samples may make tail estimates unstable, which E3 must show as uncertainty rather than a point claim.
7. **Metric-definition change.** A renamed field, changed window, or new filter produces a discontinuity presented as harness progress. Manifest comparison is **almost certain (90-99%)** to flag an encoded definition change deterministically; it cannot flag undocumented producer behavior, which requires fixture-based E4 and source checksums.
8. **No-baseline overclaim.** A post-change dashboard looks healthy and is narrated as improvement. The mandatory “effect unknown—no counterfactual” state is **almost certain (90-99%)** to block the report generator from emitting an improvement verdict when comparison metadata are absent, assuming the verdict is produced through this layer; E4 must remove comparison metadata and assert fail-closed behavior.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 5.1 A map preserves a state; an improvement claim compares possible worlds

Cartographic generalisation starts with geographic data and asks how to portray it at another scale. Harness evaluation asks whether intervention `H1` caused better outcomes than `H0` would have caused for comparable work. Those are different questions. The first is representational; the second is causal. The World Bank defines the counterfactual as the outcome that would have occurred without the intervention and explains why it must be estimated rather than directly observed ([*Impact Evaluation in Practice*, chapter 3](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstream/handle/10986/25030/9781464807794.pdf)).

Therefore the statement “the new harness has 3% errors” can be mapped faithfully at multiple detail levels, but no generalisation operator can convert it into “the new harness reduced errors.” If task mix, model version, environment, or owner behavior changed, a post-only atlas remains descriptive. This disanalogy is **almost certain (90-99%)**, supported independently by the cartographic requirement for source/initial data and the causal requirement for a counterfactual ([Girres & Touya](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/638.html); [World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/650951468335456749/pdf/520990PUB0EPI1101Official0Use0Only1.pdf)).

### 5.2 “Scale” is only a metaphor in telemetry

Map scale has a geometric ratio and imposes physical resolution limits. “Run → cohort → estate” has no equivalent denominator. Töpfer's square-root retention law therefore does not transfer as a formula. A fixed “retain √n events” rule could suppress a single catastrophic event or over-display repetitive noise. The rejection of a direct Radical Law transplant is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the law's scale-denominator inputs and reported limited applicability even within hydrographic mapping ([Wilmer & Brewer](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVIII/part4/files/Wilmer.pdf)). The useful transfer is explicit omission budgeting and scale-indexed rules, not the square-root equation.

### 5.3 Geography has spatial topology; traces have causal/temporal topology

Road connectivity and neighborhood are not the same as retry ancestry, tool dependency, or phase order. A geometric simplifier cannot be reused blindly. The analogue transfers only after defining trace-specific invariants: parent/child relationships, ordering, terminal status, and task outcome. It is **likely (55-80%)** that topology-aware trace generalisation will outperform flat counts for incident diagnosis, because retry chains retain mechanistically relevant structure, but this is UNVERIFIED until **E5 — Diagnosis Trial** randomizes reviewers across flat summaries versus atlas views on seeded incidents and compares correct root-cause identification, time, and false-confidence rate.

### 5.4 Deliberate omission can become institutionalized blindness

Cartographers omit details according to purpose; observers can likewise encode current beliefs into a rule manifest and repeatedly hide unknown failure classes. “Novel fingerprint always survives” and periodic random raw-event samples mitigate this, but cannot guarantee discovery of fields never captured. It is **likely (55-80%)** that invariant checks plus random omission audits reduce silent summary loss compared with unversioned dashboards, based on deterministic reconciliation, but the size of the reduction is UNVERIFIED until E4 measures injected-fixture recall and false-negative rate.

### 5.5 A faithful map can still optimize the wrong objective

ISO 19157 supplies quality description and evaluation structure but no universal acceptance level ([ISO 19157:2013](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)). Likewise, an atlas cannot decide whether task completion, owner corrections, cost, latency, or safety deserves priority. Those values must be pre-registered for each harness change. Allowing post hoc metric selection would turn multiple scales into multiple opportunities to cherry-pick. This is why the comparison protocol freezes the primary outcome, guardrails, and manifests before observing results.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and rejected expansions

The proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate by design:

- It rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus as a deterministic end-of-run transformation and comparison report.
- It adds no second orchestration runtime.
- It adds no vector database or knowledge graph.
- It adds no durable-execution engine.
- It adds no trained critic.
- It adds no observability SaaS.
- It adds no guardrail framework.
- It keeps raw events and derived JSON/Markdown as ordinary artifacts; a UI is optional and out of scope.

## 7. Falsification plan and adoption gate

Do not adopt the Run Atlas because the analogy is elegant. Run these experiments:

### E1 — Source-Layer Feasibility

Capture 30 representative runs through the existing bus. Use two mechanically independent checks for each necessary linkage: (a) field-level completeness and type validation in event records, and (b) reconstruction of run totals/retry chains from IDs and timestamps against terminal run summaries. The proposal fails if canonical events cannot be loss-accounted or joined without a new runtime.

### E2 — 200-Line Spike

Implement L0→L1 and L1→L2 transformation, manifest generation, and eight invariants using ordinary files. Measure executable nonblank/noncomment lines, runtime, artifact size, and deterministic checksum stability. The BUILD-SMALL label fails if the minimal useful implementation exceeds approximately 200 executable lines or needs a resident service.

### E3 — Counterfactual Pilot

Pre-register one harness change and compare old/new versions via randomized interleaving or paired replay. Use two mechanically independent outcome signals—for example, task-result tests and blinded human adjudication—before declaring a task successful. Report effect uncertainty and guardrails; do not “graduate” on a point estimate alone.

### E4 — Omission Red Team

Inject known-present fixtures: one catastrophic rare event, one novel fingerprint, a retry loop ending in success, a denominator exclusion, a taxonomy-version change, a direction-reversing stratum, and a missing-comparison manifest. First prove detectors recover these known-present controls, then measure recall across L1–L3. Separately reconcile source-ID sets and aggregate counts; these are mechanically independent signals. Any fixture silently lost is a design failure, not a tuning opportunity hidden in limitations.

### E5 — Diagnosis Trial

Seed realistic incidents into trace packets. Randomize reviewers to flat summary versus atlas, blind them to condition intent, and measure correct root cause, time, and confidence calibration. Adopt the diagnosis claim only if atlas users improve accuracy without materially increasing false confidence or time.

### Adoption decision

Adopt the small generalisation layer only if E1, E2, and E4 pass. Permit “helped/harmed” language only if E3 supplies a credible comparison. Treat E5 as the test for whether the atlas is worth retaining as a human diagnostic interface. This staged recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)** to distinguish representation value from causal value because each is tested separately, with known-present controls and independent outcome signals; the evidence is the explicit separation between cartographic derivation/evaluation and counterfactual impact evaluation in the cited fields.

## 8. Final answer to the cross-domain question

Cartography has solved how to make **multiple purpose-specific, scale-dependent, deliberately incomplete representations of one detailed source while preserving declared invariants and exposing the transformation rules**. The concrete borrow is an MRDB/ScaleMaster-style Run Atlas: canonical events, fixed analytic levels, named generalisation operators, versioned legends/manifests, landmark retention, count/provenance conservation, and drill-down.

It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch hidden heterogeneity, retry laundering, rare-harm omission, taxonomy and denominator drift, and tail regressions, because the proposed invariants preserve the necessary recorded structure; E1, E4, and E5 settle that claim. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** to catch the narrower epistemic failure of missing comparison metadata, because the report rule deterministically refuses to label a post-only view as improvement; E4's missing-manifest fixture settles the implementation claim.

But cartography has **not** solved “did the change help when there is no baseline?” Its mechanism assumes a source representation, and causal improvement requires an estimated counterfactual. The analogy therefore transfers as a disciplined observability *lens and loss-accounting layer*, not as an impact estimator. The correct ambitious move is not a large new observability platform; it is a small, falsifiable generalisation layer paired with prospective randomized or paired comparison. If the counterfactual protocol is absent, the only honest output is: **current state mapped; effect unknown**.
