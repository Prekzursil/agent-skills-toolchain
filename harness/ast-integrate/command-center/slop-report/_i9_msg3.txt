WHAT HAPPENED. Section 5 described the extractor's claim form and, to show the shape, wrote a
backticked example of it. The extractor read the example as an assertion, looked the name up,
found a real receipt carrying that name with different values, and correctly called it a

instance of use-vs-mention in this lane -- census outputs, a round-1 sentinel, the commands
gate's own prose, the bucket classifier's producer signal, and now this -- and like the
round-1 instance it landed inside the paragraph warning about it.

WHY IT SLIPPED THROUGH, which is the part that is mine and not the gate's.

Two independent things had to line up.

1. The claim was inert until I committed evidence. At the previous commit claims_found was 42
   and receipts_indexed 85; at 54c57a88 they are 43 and 88. My own three new receipts made a
   previously unanswerable name answerable, so a line that no receipt could speak to became
   checkable the moment I committed the receipts. A document can therefore be clean when
   written and violating when its sibling evidence lands, with no edit to the document.

2. I ran the pre-commit check and then kept editing. The clean --no-write reading in section
   3.3 was true for the bytes I checked. The section-5 paragraph was added AFTER it, and I
   committed without re-running. The tool worked; I stopped using it one edit too early. That
   is exactly the failure mode the --no-write flag was added to remove, committed by the
   person who added it, in the commit that added it.

THE FIX. The sentence now describes the shape without instantiating it. Verified by
re-extracting every claim the gate finds in this file -- 1 before, 0 after -- rather than by
re-reading the paragraph, and then by the gate itself: rc 0, TRANSCRIPTION-CLEAN, 42 claims.

NOT fixed with the REFUTED marker, which was available and would have been wrong. That marker
means "a superseded number, preserved on purpose". This was never a number, so suppressing it
that way would have made the marker mean "anything I do not want flagged" -- the mute-button
decay the baseline convention exists to prevent.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE GATE. It has teeth on real content, not only on injected fixtures:
its first live catch was its own author's document, one commit after wiring. It also confirms
the limitation already recorded in the residuals -- the claim form is narrow, and narrowness
cuts both ways, because the one shape it does recognise it recognises everywhere, including
in prose that is only naming it.

Verified after this commit rather than before it, which is the whole point.
