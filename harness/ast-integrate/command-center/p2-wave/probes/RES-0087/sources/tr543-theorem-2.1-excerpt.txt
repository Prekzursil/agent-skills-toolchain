Abstract: 'any nontrivial weighted average of two or more distinct, calibrated probability forecasts is necessarily uncalibrated and lacks sharpness.'

Section 2 / Theorem 2.1 (the journal version numbers this as Theorem 1):
Suppose that p1,...,pk are calibrated for the binary event Y and such that pi ≠ pj with strictly positive probability for at least one pair i ≠ j. Consider the linear opinion pool p = w1 p1 + ... + wk pk where wi > 0 and weights sum to 1. Let q = P(Y=1|p). Then:
(a) The linear opinion pool p lacks calibration, in that q ≠ p with strictly positive probability.
(b) The linear opinion pool p lacks sharpness.
(c) The recalibrated forecast q is calibrated and outperforms p for every strictly proper scoring rule.

Baseline: each source marginally calibrated, P(Y=1|p_i)=p_i a.s.
