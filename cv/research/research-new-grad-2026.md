Research date: 2026-09-23. ## 1. Bottom line for the owner (INFERRED from the evidence in sections 2-10)

1. The market is genuinely bad at the entry gate and not because of him: new-grad hiring at large US tech companies is down roughly 65% versus 2019 and new grads were about 1 in 10 engineering hires in 2025 (versus ~3 in 10 in 2023); Romania is the weakest tech-hiring market of 42 countries surveyed in Q3 2026 and its junior segment is explicitly described as squeezed. He should size the campaign accordingly (hundreds of targeted applications over months, multi-country) and treat silence as a base rate, not a verdict.
2. The rebound that exists is in senior and AI-titled roles. His portfolio (AI/agent tooling, automation, research pipelines) maps onto the only growing new-grad title segment ("AI Engineer", "Founding Engineer", forward-deployed roles). He should position as an AI-native systems engineer, not as a generic junior developer.
3. Automated screening will not reject him for having an unconventional profile; it will make him invisible if the CV does not parse cleanly, does not contain the literal terms recruiters search, or arrives after the shortlist is full. The auto-reject fear is a myth; the volume problem is real.
4. The one-page rule is a convention, not an enforced rule, and the empirical evidence is split. Default to one dense page for US/Big-Tech-style and high-volume applications; use two full pages for EU/Romanian employers and for roles where project depth is the pitch; never 1.5 pages and never tiny fonts.
5. Employers still prefer internship experience as a tie-breaker, but substantive, verifiable projects are the accepted substitute. What makes a project count is external evidence: users, downloads, merged PRs into projects other people depend on, a live demo, tests/CI, and a commit history that shows real iteration. Volume (68 repos) without curation reads as noise and, per hiring managers, half-baked public repos can hurt.
6. In 2026 the artifact is no longer proof; the ability to defend it live is. Interview loops have shifted to project deep-dives, debugging unfamiliar code, and AI-fluency rounds. His AI-assisted workflow is an asset only if he can show verification discipline (tests, honest commits, PR discussions, debugging notes) and explain every decision.
7. The 14-15 months since graduation must be presented as a dated, counted work entry ("Independent Software Engineer / open-source maintainer, Jul 2025-present", with outputs), mirrored on LinkedIn to the month. The most-flagged label is a vague "personal projects".
8. Conversion levers with the best evidence: apply within 24-72 hours of posting; tailor per role (roughly 2x interview rate in the largest dataset found); build warm channels (referred candidates reach interview ~40% of the time versus a ~0.2% offer rate for cold inbound); quantify every project; make links good or omit them.

## 3. Is AI the cause? (why the owner should not adopt either extreme narrative)

- **Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" revision 2026-08-12 (ADP payroll data through June 2026).** VERIFIED: "employment of young workers (ages 22-25) in AI-exposed occupations now stands 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with that of their less-exposed peers" (15% at the July 2025 vintage); "operates primarily through reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations"; software developers 22-25 declined nearly 20% from late-2022 peak by Sept 2025 (Nov 2025 version). Caveats in the authors' own words: "These patterns attenuate when controlling for education, show some divergent trends predating generative AI, and are more pronounced in the ADP analysis sample than in national survey benchmarks"; "early, descriptive indicators ... rather than causal estimates"; with firm-time fixed effects the decline "becomes significant only in 2024". https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/
- **Yale Budget Lab, 2026-05-07.** VERIFIED: using synthetic differences-in-differences on CPS microdata, "we generally find no statistically or economically significant effects as of yet" on employment or wages of AI-exposed occupations; mixed evidence for workers 34 and under. https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market
- **Economic Innovation Group (Iscenko & Curto Millet, Jan 2026).** VERIFIED: "The most plausible explanation is ... the sharpest monetary policy tightening cycle in four decades"; postings for the most AI-exposed quintile "peaked in March-April 2022 and declined sharply throughout the remainder of the year", six months before ChatGPT; "no evidence that job postings for junior roles within occupations most exposed to AI have declined more than postings for senior positions"; LinkedIn July 2025: entry-level hiring rates -23% vs pre-pandemic (overall -18%). https://eig.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TAWP-Iscenko-Millet.pdf
- **SIEPR policy brief (2026).** VERIFIED: "isolating AI's impact is empirically challenging. This remains an open and active area of research"; two papers find hiring in AI-exposed occupations declined after the Fed pivot but before ChatGPT.
- **ITIF (2026-06-30) citing Kharazian et al.** VERIFIED: high-intensity AI adopters increased entry-level headcount by 12.0% after adoption (contrarian to displacement).

INFERRED: the honest reading is "cyclical over-hiring correction plus a structural shift in what junior work looks like, with AI's causal share unresolved". For the owner, the practical consequence is identical under both readings: employers are automating "the checkable, process-intensive tasks that historically justified entry-level headcount" (Stanford's phrasing) and are paying for people who can direct and verify AI output. That is precisely the capability his portfolio demonstrates, if he presents it as verification discipline rather than volume of generated code.

## 4. Automated screening: what ATSs and AI screeners actually do

### 4.1 The auto-reject myth (high confidence it is false as commonly stated)

- The "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before a human sees them" statistic traces to a 2012 sales pitch by Preptel (defunct 2013) with no published methodology; no peer-reviewed study supports any specific auto-rejection percentage. VERIFIED in five independent debunks (Enhancv 2025; ApplyMate 2026; StartupKit 2026; Workplacea 2026; ResumeGeni 2026, whose author "spent 12 years at ZipRecruiter"). It is still being recycled in 2026 with fabricated attributions (a "Stealth Agents" page attributes "75% rejected" to "ResumeGo 2026", which does not exist as such).
- **Enhancv recruiter study (Sept-Oct 2025, n=25 US recruiters across Workday, iCIMS, Greenhouse, Lever, Bullhorn, BambooHR, Teamtailor, Phenom, SuccessFactors; companies 120-50,000+ staff).** VERIFIED: 92% (23/25) say their ATS does not auto-reject for formatting, content, or design; 8% (2/25, Bullhorn and BambooHR) configured content thresholds (e.g., "fewer than 7 of 10 required skills"); 100% use knockout questions (work authorization, licence, location); 44% have an AI "fit score", 36% use it as guidance, 8% as a definitive filter, 56% ignore or lack it; 52% review applications in arrival order; shortlists typically close at "250-300 candidates"; recruiters' checklist includes "One page for entry-level, two pages maximum"; "32% of recruiters said a warm InMail beats 100 cold applications". Stated 90% CI on the 8% figure: 2-21%. https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/
- **Vendor documentation (primary).** Greenhouse Talent Matching FAQ: "Talent Matching does not auto-reject or auto-advance any candidate ... All disposition and hiring decisions are made by the hiring team"; Greenhouse Talent Filtering: recruiters search "job titles, skills, locations, and other keywords in the full text of resumes" and "the keyword from your search must exactly match the keyword in the application" with Preferred (OR) and Required (AND) terms (https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/27104809835291-Talent-Filtering; https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41131886674075-Talent-Matching-FAQ). Independent platform research (sunnypatell/ats-screener; atsgrader 2026-08): Lever "does NOT score or rank candidates" and its search uses word stemming but "cannot identify abbreviations"; Workday's base recruiting "does not assign an automated match score" (HiredScore is an optional layer) and relies on the structured form fields the applicant types; Taleo supports disqualification questions and exact/related/conceptual search modes. Greenhouse application rules can auto-reject on custom application-question answers, not on resume prose (ResumeGeni Greenhouse guide, 2026-02).
- **Harvard Business School / Accenture "Hidden Workers" (2021, 2,250 executives).** VERIFIED via three secondary sources: 88% of employers say qualified high-skill candidates are filtered out because they do not exactly match criteria; ~49% of companies automatically eliminate candidates with 6+ month employment gaps. The authors' point: human-configured criteria, not autonomous software, do the excluding.

### 4.2 AI in screening: adoption is real, measurement is inconsistent

- Resume Genius 2026 Hiring Trends (Pollfish, 1,500 US hiring managers, June 2026): 87% use AI somewhere in recruitment; 58% use AI to screen resumes (up from 35% in 2025); 81% have encountered candidate AI use; "heavily AI-generated application materials" is the #2 red flag (49%). Resume Genius 2026 Hiring Insights (Jan 2026, n=1,000): 71% use an ATS; 19% "use AI to purposefully screen out applications before they get a human review"; 32% say AI ranks but humans decide; 80% claim they can spot an AI-written resume; 86% hire for experience over education. https://resumegenius.com/blog/job-hunting/hiring-trends-report-2026
- SHRM State of AI in HR 2026 (n=1,722, Dec 2025; via two secondary summaries): 27% of organizations use AI in recruiting; of those, 44% for resume screening (~12% of organizations overall); 19% of organizations using hiring automation report it has screened out qualified candidates.
- HireVue 2026 Global AI in Hiring (3,100+ hiring managers): 77% of HR teams use AI weekly or daily; 71% of candidates use AI to write resumes; "the resume is losing its value in hiring"; recommendation to "evaluate skills not resumes" via assessments.
- CV-Library UK survey (March 2026; 424 recruiters, 1,067 candidates): 75% of candidates believe AI screens CVs versus 21% of recruiters who say they use it there; 79% of recruiters report more AI-generated CVs; 81% say CVs "lack personality or distinction"; 35% say AI has caused them to miss strong candidates.
- Bullhorn GRID 2026 (2,300 recruitment professionals, via Refolk): applications up 51%, job orders flat; cause named: candidates using AI to mass-apply.
- Gartner (via toolixlab): 26% of candidates trust AI to evaluate them fairly; 52% assume they are already screened by AI.

INFERRED for the owner: the mechanics that decide his fate are (a) parse fidelity (single column, standard headings, real text), (b) literal keyword visibility (recruiters search exact terms; include both "Kubernetes" and "K8s"-style forms only where true), (c) arrival time, (d) knockout answers (work authorization, location, years-of-experience questions), and (e) whether a human skimming for 7-30 seconds can immediately see a title, a stack, dates and one credible number. An "unconventional heavy-portfolio profile" is not penalized by the software; it is penalized by a human who cannot find the expected fields in it. The design response is to make the profile look conventional in structure while being unconventional in evidence.

## 5. Resume length: is the one-page rule still enforced?

- **Stated preference vs revealed preference.** ResumeGo's 2024 survey (418 hiring professionals): 92% recommend one page for the average job seeker; but 63% say resumes are generally too short and 89% say too little information is worse than too much; >50% say an irregular length (1.5 pages) hurts the candidate's image and 84% recommend adjusting to avoid it (https://www.resumego.net/research/how-long-should-a-resume-be/). ResumeGo's 2018 simulation (482 recruiters/HR/executives, 7,712 resumes): two-page resumes chosen 2.3x overall; at entry level 58% vs 42% (1.4x); two-page resumes scored 8.6 vs 7.1 on summarizing credentials; review time 2:24 (one page) vs 4:05 (two pages) (CNBC 2018-12-19; ERE 2019). Caveats (resumx.dev analysis): run by a resume-writing service; measured simulated choice, not callbacks; "the preference may reflect information volume rather than page count".
- **Novorésumé HR survey 2025:** 68.3% of HR professionals consider two pages ideal, 21.8% one page; 59.9% would consider longer than two only if relevant at a glance. (Vendor survey; methodology not shown in excerpt.)
- **Blackburn-Brockman & Belanger (2001, Journal of Business Communication, mailed to 570 Big-Six recruiters):** entry-level accounting candidates with two-page resumes were ranked more favorably. Old and field-specific, but the only peer-reviewed controlled comparison found.
- **Google (official "Create your resume for Google" video and recruiter interviews):** "Try to keep your resume to one page for business and internship roles and no longer than two pages for engineering roles"; PDF "of no more than two pages"; XYZ bullets; bold the programming language per project; include open source, hackathons, competitions.
- **Gergely Orosz (hiring manager at Uber/Skyscanner; The Tech Resume Inside Out, ~2 dozen recruiter contributors):** new-grad structure "Resume Length: Keep it to one page"; a top mistake is "'Abusing' the one-page resume ... tiny fonts, so that two pages of projects with intricate detail could be listed on that one page ... Project #6 is not that important"; two-column layouts disfavored; no photo, no birth date; "EUROPASS ... avoid".
- **Enhancv's 25 recruiters:** "One page for entry-level, two pages maximum"; the eye-tracking "7.4 seconds" comes from a Ladders study of n=30 (2018) and measures the initial scan, not the read.
- **Europe/Romania:** Europass instructions: "in most cases, two pages are enough"; Romanian guides: "1-2 pages is standard" (taurusid 2026-09-14; Academia Programatorilor; Jobera).
- **hireresume.ai (2026)** asserts "researchers submitted identical profiles ... two-page received 2.3x more callbacks for 10+ years; no significant difference for entry level" and a "CareerBuilder 2024, 2,200 hiring managers" split (39/25/36) — UNVERIFIED, no primary found; do not cite.

Conclusion (INFERRED, high confidence): nobody enforces one page; recruiters say one page and choose two. For this owner: one dense page for US/Big-Tech-style and high-volume applications (recruiters at those companies expect it and Orosz/Google/Enhancv converge on it for new grads), a full second page for EU/Romanian employers and for roles where the project narrative is the pitch, never an orphan half page, never sub-10pt fonts, and the first page must stand alone (name, target title, stack, dated "Independent Software Engineer" entry with numbers, top 3 projects).

## 6. Self-directed projects versus internships and jobs

- **Employers' stated hierarchy (NACE 2026):** "when deciding between two equally qualified job candidates, employers overwhelmingly choose the candidate who has internship experience"; nearly all respondents value US internships, ~75% co-ops, 40%+ on-campus work/apprenticeships; "College graduates should demonstrate skills, not just list them" — 59% want "listing of specific skills with examples". INFERRED for the owner: he cannot manufacture an internship after the fact, but he can make projects satisfy the same three tests employers apply to internships — an external party, a schedule, and something produced (cvbooster's rule for self-directed work). ## 7. What converts a project-heavy resume into interviews (mechanics with evidence)

1. **Apply early.** 52% of recruiters review in arrival order; shortlists close at 250-300; popular roles draw 400-2,000+ applications (Enhancv, n=25; corroborated by Resume Genius volume figures and Bullhorn +51%). Y Combinator startups reportedly ~3,000 applications per role in 30 days (Nox, UNVERIFIED). Action: alerts on target boards; apply within 24-72h.
2. **Tailor.** Huntr Q1 2026 (139,927 applications, 25,635 job seekers): 4.23% interview rate for tailored versus 2.07% untailored (vendor data, self-selection likely, but the largest dataset found). Enhancv recruiters: "Relevant experience and skills visible immediately" (88%), "Natural use of keywords" (76%), generic applications that forget to change the job title are a red flag. Google Romania: uncalibrated CV is the #1 first-round failure. Orosz: tailor and cut a master resume per posting. Action: one master document, per-role cuts, mirror the posting's true vocabulary, include full forms and abbreviations once where true.
3. **Warm channels.** Ashby (38M applications, 93K jobs, 2021-2024): 93.8% of applications are inbound and 1% referrals; 40% of referred candidates reach interview; inbound offer rate fell ~70% to ~2 per 1,000; Ashby 2026: 52% of referred candidates pass initial screens vs 35% overall. LinkedIn: 3.6x more likely to be hired if connected to an employee before applying. Burks et al. (QJE 2015) and Pallais & Sands (2015): referrals carry information and lower the hiring bar. Romanian training providers: direct recommendation is the best route. Action: before applying to any company, find one engineer there (alumni, OSS maintainers he has interacted with, meetups) and get a referral or at least a conversation; recruiters say a warm InMail beats 100 cold applications (32% of Enhancv's sample).
4. **Quantify.** Google's XYZ formula ("Accomplished X as measured by Y by doing Z"); Orosz's example: "Built an open source JavaScript component for date display. The project has 5 contributors, 120 stars and 4 known production use cases"; interviewchamp: one usage metric or one depth indicator per project, "50-200 users is plenty" for a new grad; NACE: examples, not lists. Action: instrument every flagship repo (downloads, stars, users, issues closed, PRs merged, latency/perf numbers, test coverage) and write exactly one impact-or-depth bullet each.
5. **Links must be good or absent.** Orosz: "Links on the resume pointing to poor work" (a GitHub with no README) is a top-9 mistake; techinterview.org: a repo "with a barebones README, no commits in 18 months ... is worse than not listing the project", "Reviewers don't follow links from resume PDFs reliably. Describe what it is"; interviewchamp: verify every link from a logged-out browser; live demo first, GitHub second.
6. **Curate the GitHub as a landing page.** Pin 3-6 repos; one-line descriptions; archive tutorial/half-baked repos; real name and one-line bio ("An empty profile with an avatar placeholder reads as abandoned"); profile README optional (Priodemy); "Four polished projects beat six random ones" (underdog.io); reviewers scan for ~90 seconds (Engineering Dad). HN: "what you have publicly available in your GitHub can only hurt you unless you are the owner of a successful open source project" — i.e., unfinished public repos are a liability.
7. **Keep LinkedIn aligned and alive.** Missing/stale LinkedIn is a bigger negative than missing GitHub (HN 2024); recruiters cross-check dates to the month (Shortlisted 2026); LinkedIn's own data on connections (3.6x). Action: mirror the CV's dated entries exactly; add the flagship projects with links; turn on open-to-work for recruiters.
8. **Expect and prepare for verification.** See section 9: the interview is the product; the resume is the prompt.

## 9. GitHub and AI-generated work: the 2026 evidence layer has changed

- Volume signals have inflated: GitHub Octoverse 2025 via instalent (2026-08): 180M+ developers, 121M new repos in 2025, commits up 25%, "80 percent of new developers on GitHub use Copilot in their first week"; "Anything one person can generate went up. Anything that requires other engineers to grant something did not" — review comments on others' code, maintainer/release rights, work "that survived contact with time", and written reasoning (RFCs, post-incident write-ups, talks) are the signals that still discriminate.
- Sourcing practice (noon.ai 2025-11; Pin 2025-03; Vamo 2026-04; recruiter.daily.dev 2026-03): recruiters do source on GitHub with `language:`/`location:`/`followers:`/`pushed:` qualifiers and read pinned repos, READMEs and commit consistency; they discount stars, streaks and follower counts; 82% of contributions are private so "absence is not negative evidence". A Beamery claim that "83% of technical hiring managers trust GitHub profiles over resumes" appears only in one vendor article — UNVERIFIED.
- Hiring managers now verify authorship: Resume Genius 2026: 29% of hiring managers have seen AI-generated portfolio projects; nextmantra rubric: single-commit repos "suggest code was written elsewhere and imported", "AI-style prose in README ... reduces the evidentiary value"; Saral (2026-05): "Anyone can generate Profile A over a weekend with Cursor or Windsurf. Nobody can fake Profile B" (18-month maintained repos, reverts, reviewed PRs, closed issues with root causes). TechScreen (2026-06): the post-submission live review "is the single most reliable AI detector"; graders analyze commit history for unnatural patterns. zandigital (2026-09): Anthropic rebuilt its performance-engineering take-home three times because each new Claude model solved it; interviewing.io survey of 67 interviewers: 81% suspected AI cheating; 67% of startups changed process; Gartner: 72.4% of recruiting leaders now interview in person against fraud; Canva replaced its CS-fundamentals screen with "AI-Assisted Coding"; Google's reported pilot scores "AI fluency, including prompt engineering, output validation, and debugging skills" and gives negative feedback to candidates who "rely heavily on AI without demonstrating their own understanding"; DoorDash runs a 60-minute AI-assisted working session (StartupKit 2026-06). Learnist (2026-05): for an AI-coded portfolio project, bring "README, commit history, screenshots, test notes, prompt notes, and a short explanation of what you changed after the AI draft"; never say "The AI built it, so I am not sure."

INFERRED for the owner: his portfolio was built with heavy AI tooling (agentic workflows are the subject of much of it). That is not a liability if the evidence layer shows judgment: honest incremental commits, PR discussions, tests/CI, incident/debugging write-ups, and the ability to explain any decision and extend any file live. It is a liability if repos look like one-shot generations. He should (a) audit each flagship repo's commit history and README for "Profile B" signals, (b) write short engineering notes (what broke, why this design) — these double as blog posts, which two practitioner sources list as a distinct positive signal, and (c) rehearse 10-minute deep-dives and a constraint-reversal exercise per project.

## 10. EU / Romania specifics

- **CV norms.** Romanian private-sector/tech: modern 1-2 page CV, GitHub + LinkedIn links essential, photo "less critical" for technical roles and some multinationals ask for none; Europass mostly public sector/academia (taurusid 2026-09-14 claims eJobs data that "over 60% of private-sector recruiters prefer a concise, visually clean CV over the Europass template" — UNVERIFIED figure). Orosz (EU-based): Europass "encourages half of the problems ... Sharing of far too much personal information ... avoid"; no photo, no birth date, no marital status. Older guides (Jobera 2023) still say a photo is "standard" in Romania — contested and dated. Languages with CEFR levels are valued in Europe (taurusid; ShapeCV 2026).
- **GDPR Article 22 (in force).** VERIFIED text: right "not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects ... or similarly significantly affects him or her", with exceptions for contract necessity/consent and a minimum right "to obtain human intervention ... to express his or her point of view and to contest the decision". Ropes & Gray (2026-04): "if a manager only interviews candidates from an AI-generated shortlist at which they have merely glanced, that is likely to be ADM"; lexICT (CJEU SCHUFA reasoning): where scoring "substantially determines what follows, it becomes part of the decision"; the ICO lists "e-recruiting practices without human intervention" as a similarly significant effect.
- **EU AI Act.** VERIFIED across EC, DLA Piper, Gibson Dunn, Jones Walker, Ogletree, CSA, lawandtechnology.eu, jobiqo: the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) was published 24 July 2026 and entered into force 27 July 2026; Annex III high-risk obligations (which include "AI systems intended to be used for the recruitment or selection of natural persons, in particular to place targeted job advertisements, analyse and filter job applications, and evaluate candidates") now apply from 2 December 2027 (embedded Annex I systems: 2 August 2028); Article 50 transparency obligations applied on schedule from 2 August 2026 and cover chatbots/AI interaction disclosure and synthetic content, "not silent back-office automation" (findskill). Article 26 deployer duties (inform affected workers/candidates, human oversight, logs) and the Article 86 right to an explanation belong to the deferred high-risk regime. One vendor (MintHCM) asserts Art. 50 already requires informing candidates that AI evaluated their application — this conflicts with the law-firm readings and is marked contested. Emotion-recognition in recruitment is already prohibited (since Feb 2025).

INFERRED for the owner: legal protections against opaque automated rejection exist on paper (GDPR Art. 22 now; AI Act Annex III from Dec 2027) but are a low-yield lever for a job seeker; the practical value is knowing that EU employers are under pressure to keep humans in the loop and to disclose candidate-facing AI (chatbot pre-screens), and that he may lawfully ask for human review of a solely automated rejection.

## 11. Contested claims and widely repeated advice with weak or no evidence

1. "75% (or any fixed share) of resumes are auto-rejected by ATS." No study; Preptel 2012 marketing; contradicted by vendor docs and the Enhancv sample. Still recycled in 2026 with false attributions.
2. "Recruiters spend 6-7 seconds on a resume." Ladders eye-tracking, n=30, measures the first scan (7.4s); simulation data shows 2-4 minutes when a resume is actually evaluated. Both can be true; the number is misused as a length argument.
3. "One page is mandatory for new grads." Recruiters recommend it (92%) and choose two pages in simulations (1.4x at entry level, 2.3x overall); studies are run by resume services and do not measure callbacks; Google allows two for engineering; Europe treats two as normal. Verdict: a convention with real downside only for padding, tiny fonts, or 1.5 pages.
4. "AI is eliminating entry-level software jobs." Stanford (descriptive, 19% relative gap, attenuates with controls, pre-trends) vs Yale Budget Lab (no significant effect), EIG (rate-hike timing), LinkedIn ("not yet"), Indeed (normalization), Handshake (no differential decline for AI-exposed roles), ITIF/Kharazian (adopters hire more juniors). Open question; do not build a narrative on either extreme.
5. "AI screens most resumes now." 58% of US hiring managers (Resume Genius, Pollfish) vs ~12% of organizations (SHRM) vs 21% of UK recruiters (CV-Library) vs 75% candidate belief. Definitions differ; ranking, not rejecting, dominates.
6. "Recruiters look at your GitHub." Sourcing vendors say yes; hiring managers on HN/Reddit mostly say rarely, and when they do it is to disconfirm; Orosz says links are not clicked under volume. Net: a curated GitHub can only help and an uncurated one can hurt; the "83% trust GitHub over resumes" figure is a single vendor claim.
7. "Exact keyword matching / keyword stuffing beats the ATS." Greenhouse's filter is exact-match but its matching layer is semantic; Lever stems but ignores abbreviations; Workday relies on typed form fields; stuffing is a stated red flag. Use the posting's vocabulary where true; write for a human.
8. "Referrals get you 40% of hires" / "10x conversion." Ashby: 40% of referred candidates reach interview but referrals are 1% of applications and inbound still produces most hires; the "29 days vs 45 days" and "45% two-year retention" numbers are Jobvite 2012 data recycled in 2026 vendor posts.
9. "Employers hire for skills, not degrees." NACE: ~70% say skills-based hiring, but the top screening factors for GPA-ignoring employers are major (94%) and internships; LinkedIn: only 13-14% of recruiter searches filter by skill (though <3% filter by degree). The degree is still a knockout criterion in many postings; what changed is GPA weight (42% screen it vs 73% in 2019).
10. "Romania no longer hires entry-level IT" (HR consultant, Ziare.com) vs "junior jobs are everywhere if you are prepared" (wellcode.ro, a training company). Both are opinion; the hard data points are Experis -11%, Cognizant 600/35, and undelucram's "essentially frozen" quotes.
11. Romanian IT headcount: -27% 2022-2024 (Organisationsdiplomaten, 146k) vs ~200k today (ANIS via undelucram). Different definitions; unreconciled.
12. "Europass is required / a photo is standard in Romania." Dated guides say so; 2025-2026 tech-specific guidance and Orosz say use Europass only when explicitly requested and omit the photo for tech/multinational roles.
13. "Tailored resumes double your interview rate." Huntr's own users (vendor); plausible, single dataset, self-selection uncontrolled.
14. "Employers auto-reject 6+ month gaps" (HBS 2021, 49%). Executive self-report from 2021; for a first job after graduation the relevant framing evidence is about labeled vs unlabeled time, not gap length per se.
15. "Article 50 of the AI Act already forces employers to tell you AI screened your CV" (MintHCM). Law-firm readings: Art. 50 covers interaction with AI systems and synthetic content; candidate-information duties sit in the deferred high-risk regime (Dec 2027). GDPR transparency/Art. 22 rights, not the AI Act, are the live lever in 2026.
