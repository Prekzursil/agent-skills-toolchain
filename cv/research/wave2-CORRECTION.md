## 6. The strategic question that the research cannot answer

**Question.** Does "bachelor's from a private Romanian university (Titu Maiorescu), master's from a state one (University of Bucharest)" carry weight — positive or negative — with Romanian IT employers or with the SDI admission committee?

Nothing else in the market file touches university brand. **COULD NOT DETERMINE** from the six files.
- The academic wave's institution-related warning (Harchol-Balter: publications in venues "we are not familiar with" get discounted) is about *venues*, not degrees.

**What the parallel `conflict-resolution.md` (written after the owner's correction, same day) established — reported, not re-fetched:**
- Titu Maiorescu is accredited (Legea 239/2002), ARACIS "grad de încredere ridicat" 2023–2028, with ARACIS-accredited doctoral schools — VERIFIED there.
- Employers: dated press evidence (2009–2011) of a mild, informal, contested preference for state graduates (one HR specialist and one research project say "preference for state"; one recruiter says the distinction ended "five or six years ago"; a 2011 UECNCFPA-study report says 80 % of employers have no institutional preference, with a conflicting unverifiable 96 % snippet); nothing measured after 2015; IT-specific sources say a bachelor's diploma is often not a hiring necessity at all (Codecool, 47 IT leaders; WEF 2023 via Ziare.com: under 30 % of Romanian companies emphasise a degree). Its conclusion: current general practice COULD NOT DETERMINE beyond "no recent evidence that it matters, least of all in IT".
- Doctoral admission: neither the SDI page nor the UB regulation carries any criterion on the type of institution that awarded earlier degrees (VERIFIED there and confirmed by my read of UB Art. 12 and the SDI scan: eligibility is the master's + 300 ECTS; tie-breaks are averages and publications). Informal committee weighting: COULD NOT DETERMINE.

**Position.** On paper the combination is neutral for the doctorate: the regulation is institution-blind, his *most recent* degree is from the admitting university itself, and the tie-break uses his licence *average*, not its issuer. That his master's is at UB is, if anything, the favourable half of the combination for the SDI (INFERRED: the gate is a UB supervisor's agreement, which is easier to earn from inside the faculty). For employers, the honest answer is that nothing on disk measures it after 2015 and the IT-specific evidence points to portfolios and experience dominating degrees. Whether an individual recruiter or committee member discounts a private bachelor's: **COULD NOT DETERMINE**, and I decline to fill it from general knowledge.
