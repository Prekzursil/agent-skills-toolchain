# X029 — Aviation safety → finite-horizon agent context

**Unit:** X029  
**Lens:** aviation-safety mechanisms for deciding what enters, remains in, or leaves a finite context window  
**Research boundary:** external aviation sources only; no inspection of the local harness, repository, audit, or corpus  
**Date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive conclusion

Aviation has **not** solved the general problem of optimal context selection. It has solved a narrower, structurally similar problem well enough to borrow: **positive transfer of an operational picture when responsibility moves from one bounded attention-holder to another**. The best source mechanism is the FAA air-traffic-control position-relief process, not the popularized idea of “just use a checklist.” Its essential elements are:

1. continuously externalize operationally relevant status instead of reconstructing it at handoff;
2. let the incoming operator preview the external state and live operation before hearing a narrative;
3. use a position-specific checklist plus a verbal exception briefing;
4. make the incoming operator resolve questions before explicitly accepting responsibility;
5. retain a short post-transfer overlap in which the outgoing operator checks for omissions, updates, and inaccuracies.

The FAA’s current standard operating practice says that memory-only relief is vulnerable to forgotten or incomplete information, defines a Status Information Area and written notes, assigns equal responsibility for completeness to both controllers, and specifies preview, verbal briefing, explicit assumption, and post-transfer review. It also requires operationally relevant status to be recorded as soon as feasible rather than waiting for relief ([FAA Order JO 7110.65BB, Appendix A](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/atc_html/appendix_a.html)).

The concrete harness translation is a **Context Relief Protocol**: a tiny, typed “Context Status Area” maintained during work; a compaction/handoff checklist; an explicit before/after context manifest; a one-turn overlap allowing rehydration; and hold/resume markers for interrupted procedures. This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce high-severity losses of constraints, unresolved commitments, active-process state, and interrupted-step location, because each proposed control has the same causal job as the aviation control—externalizing volatile state and detecting omission at a responsibility boundary—but the claim is **UNVERIFIED; the paired replay-and-live shadow experiment in “Validation program” would settle it**.

The borrow must remain small. A second orchestration runtime, a vector database, a knowledge graph, a durable-execution engine, a trained critic, an observability SaaS, or a generic guardrail framework is neither required nor justified. The proposal rides existing compaction, hook, skill, and tool buses. The minimal architecture is deliberate: most raw history should still be evicted; only a small operational kernel and evidence-addressable pointers stay live.

## 1. What aviation actually solved

### 1.1 Primary mechanism: positive transfer of position responsibility

Air traffic control is continuous while individual controllers rotate. The outgoing controller has a rich, time-sensitive mental model; the incoming controller has finite attention and must safely assume control without replaying the whole shift. That is close to an agent crossing a context-compaction, model-handoff, or session-resumption boundary.

The FAA mechanism works as a four-stage protocol:

**Preview.** The relieving controller follows a checklist, reads the Status Information Areas, inspects equipment and the work environment, listens to communications, observes current and pending traffic, and correlates those observations with recorded flight information. Only then does the relieving controller indicate readiness for the verbal briefing.

**Exception-oriented verbal briefing.** The outgoing controller briefs abnormal status not already represented in the status area, items needing explanation, weather, traffic, and communication status. The incoming controller asks questions; the outgoing controller must answer them.

**Positive acceptance.** Transfer is not inferred from silence or from completion of a summary. The incoming controller explicitly indicates that responsibility has been assumed; the outgoing controller releases the position and notes the time.

**Post-transfer review/overlap.** The incoming controller checks and updates received information. The outgoing controller reviews the checklist, status displays, notes, and other sources; calls out omissions, updates, or inaccuracies; observes the operation; and assists if needed.

These are not informal best practices; they are the stepwise structure in the FAA’s published SOP ([FAA Order JO 7110.65BB, Appendix A](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/atc_html/appendix_a.html)). The same order explains the design motivation: pertinent information was often forgotten or incompletely covered when practices varied and memory lacked systematic reminders. It also tells controllers to record information when it becomes operationally relevant, which makes handoff a validation of accumulated state rather than an end-of-shift act of recollection.

Two details matter especially for context engineering:

* The status display and narrative are **complementary**. Stable or inspectable facts live in the status area; abnormalities and special-interest items get verbal explanation. A giant prose summary is not the aviation design.
* Responsibility is **bilateral**. The sender must provide a complete brief, but the receiver must reach its own satisfaction and resolve questions before acceptance. A summarizer declaring itself “done” is not positive transfer.

The transfer mechanism is not perfectly sufficient. An FAA retrospective study found that operational errors historically occurred at a higher percentage early in a controller’s time on position and tapered later. It concluded that the position-relief checklist, though grounded in human-factors principles, was insufficient to account for differing states of mind, transfer types, and time pressure ([Bailey, Pounds & Scarborough, *En Route Operational Errors: Transfer of Position Responsibility as a Function of Time on Position*, DOT/FAA/AM-08/16](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/data_research/research/med_humanfacs/oamtechreports/200816.pdf)). This negative result is central: borrow the protocol, not the belief that a checklist completes the safety case.

### 1.2 Supporting mechanism: checklist interruption has an explicit hold point

A context boundary is not the only danger. Tool calls, user interjections, subtask switches, errors, and long outputs interrupt multi-step procedures. Aviation treats an unmarked interruption as a memory hazard.

NASA’s flight-deck checklist research describes an explicit “hold at [item]” call when a checklist is interrupted and a corresponding continuation call. Without a special marker, the hold point exists only in low-capacity short-term memory and is vulnerable to interference. The report includes an incident in which an interrupted engine-start checklist led a crew to miss the only fuel check ([Degani & Wiener, *Human Factors of Flight-Deck Checklists: The Normal Checklist*, NASA CR-177549](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). A later NASA investigation likewise describes airline practice: avoid starting a checklist that cannot be completed without interruption, or explicitly mark “Hold … at [item]” and resume at that location ([Latorella, *Investigating Interruptions: Implications for Flightdeck Performance*, NASA/TM-1999-209707](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20000004291/downloads/20000004291.pdf?attachment=true)).

The transferable unit is not a general to-do list. It is a **resumption token** containing procedure identity, last verified step, next step, blocking condition, and whether an intervening state change invalidates earlier steps.

### 1.3 Supporting mechanism: phase-of-operation relevance (“sterile cockpit”)

US regulation forbids nonessential duties and distracting activity during critical phases of flight; critical phases include taxi, takeoff, landing, and other operations below 10,000 feet except cruise ([14 CFR §121.542](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/121.542%20Flight%20crewmember%20duties.pdf)). The mechanism is a phase-dependent admission rule: information or work that is acceptable during low-workload cruise is not automatically acceptable during a critical transition.

This maps only partly to token context. It supports reserving working-context capacity during destructive changes, credential/security operations, deployments, migrations, or final verification. It does **not** justify discarding unexpected input. In aviation, a safety-relevant cabin call must still penetrate a sterile flight deck; analogously, a user correction, new constraint, tool failure, or safety signal must preempt the gate. “Sterile” should mean defer nonessential material and tool verbosity, never make the agent deaf.

### 1.4 Supporting mechanism: immediate actions versus reference procedures

Aviation does not expect the flight crew to keep an entire operations manual in working memory. It separates time-critical immediate actions (“memory items”) from a readily available checklist or Quick Reference Handbook used for subsequent diagnosis and management. FAA training guidance explicitly distinguishes read/do, do/verify, memory items, flows, and QRH/emergency checklist use ([FAA AC 61-138A, §14.3.3](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_61-138A_CHG_1.pdf)). FAA accident-learning material similarly explains that pilots perform immediate memorized actions and then consult the applicable checklist ([FAA lesson on Socata TBM 700 N900KN](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/small_airplane/accidents/N900KN)).

The useful translation is a very small always-live **operational kernel**, backed by addressable source material. The kernel contains facts whose absence could immediately corrupt the next action; it does not contain whole manuals, raw transcripts, or every rationale.

### 1.5 Supporting mechanism: mode awareness prevents automation surprise

Automation surprise occurs when automation behaves as designed but differently from what the operator expects. NASA’s research reports mode-awareness failures involving knowledge gaps, data-entry mistakes, missed mode transitions, and crew coordination; pilots can know what the aircraft is doing while not knowing the automation state that produced it ([Palmer et al., *Aiding Pilot Understanding of Automation Modes*, NASA/TM-1998-112217](https://humanfactors.arc.nasa.gov/publications/20051025132410_aiding_vg_understanding_tm.pdf)). A later NASA study says automation surprise and mode confusion remain prevalent and evaluates displays designed to expose automation path and configuration, including “Automation Does What?” and an automation-function configuration display ([Sherry et al., 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205002530/downloads/DASC%202020%20ADW%20AFC%20Paper%201044%20Final.pdf)).

Agent compaction is an automation mode transition. If the model cannot see that a summary replaced raw turns, which facts were retained, what was evicted, and what retrieval path remains, it can confidently act from the wrong mental model. The analogous control is an annunciator, not another hidden summarizer.

### 1.6 Learning mechanism: confidential, non-punitive incident capture

NASA’s Aviation Safety Reporting System accepts voluntary reports of actual or potential safety deficiencies, de-identifies them, protects confidentiality, and—within defined limits—provides enforcement-use and penalty-waiver protections. The explicit purpose is to make safety information available that would otherwise remain hidden ([NASA ASRS confidentiality and incentives](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/confidentiality.html); [ASRS policy detail](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/immunity.html)). ASRS reports are analyzed and disseminated as safety information rather than treated only as individual blame events ([NASA ASRS overview](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/)).

The context analogue is not a human post-mortem bureaucracy. It is a local record of **context-loss near misses**: a user repeats a constraint, corrects a forgotten decision, asks why an already-rejected path returned, or identifies that the agent resumed at the wrong step. Record the failed context decision and its cause category, not merely “model gave bad answer.” This allows policy tuning against real omissions.

## 2. Structural mapping

| Aviation element | Context-window analogue | What remains deliberately different |
|---|---|---|
| Controller position | One active agent/model context | The agent’s “mental state” is token-conditioned and discontinuous, not a persistent human mind. |
| Status Information Area | Small typed Context Status Area | Software state can often be re-read exactly; user intent and tacit rationale cannot. |
| Written notes recorded when relevant | Event-time updates to commitments, hazards, and active processes | Automatic extraction is probabilistic and must retain provenance. |
| Incoming preview of displays and live traffic | Re-read authoritative files/tool state plus status area before summary | Re-reading every source is expensive; preview must be scoped. |
| Verbal exception briefing | Compaction summary of abnormalities, ambiguity, and rationale | It is generated by the same model family and is not an independent witness. |
| Questions before acceptance | Consistency checks and explicit unresolved-fields gate | A same-model self-question can reproduce the same omission. Deterministic/source checks are more independent. |
| Explicit assumption of responsibility | Context-generation ID and “handoff accepted” marker | No legal responsibility changes; the value is mode clarity and auditability. |
| Outgoing controller overlap | Keep old manifest/critical tail addressable for one validation turn | Full duplicate context is too costly; overlap must be pointer-based or narrowly retained. |
| Sterile cockpit | Critical-operation admission/reserve policy | Token occupancy is not human distraction; an apparently irrelevant fact can become causally important later. |
| Memory item + QRH | Always-live kernel + just-in-time source retrieval | Retrieval can fail or retrieve a stale version, so identity, version, and provenance matter. |
| ASRS near-miss report | Local context-loss/correction event | There may be no organizational fear of punishment, while privacy and secret leakage are stronger concerns. |

## 3. Proposed harness design

All proposals below pass the anti-overfit gate: they use an existing compaction/hook/skill/tool path, do not require a second orchestrator, and do not introduce any previously rejected component class.

### 3.1 UNVERIFIED proposal — Context Status Area

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Maintain a compact, typed sidecar that is rendered into context as a bounded block and updated at event time. It should be inspectable text (for example, JSON plus a concise rendering), not an embedding store.

Minimum fields:

```text
context_generation: stable ID + compaction parent
mission: current user outcome and explicit definition of done
authority: allowed writes/external effects; approval requirements
hard_constraints: exact user/system prohibitions and mandatory output contract
active_phase: explore | decide | edit | verify | destructive/irreversible
commitments: promised deliverables, target paths, recipients, due conditions
open_loops: unresolved questions, blockers, TODOs, pending follow-ups
active_operations: process/tool ID, purpose, state, expected completion signal
working_set: exact files/objects being changed and last verified state
decisions: decision, rationale pointer, rejected alternatives, invalidation condition
hazards: risk, trigger, mitigation, owner
holds: procedure ID, last verified step, next step, blocker, state fingerprint
evidence_index: claim -> source pointer/version/hash, not source body
known_unknowns: explicit uncertainty and experiment/check that resolves it
```

Each item should carry `source`, `observed_at`, `last_verified_at`, and where meaningful `expires_when`. Those fields distinguish a user constraint from a model inference and a current tool result from a stale belief.

Admission rule: an item enters the live status area if at least one condition holds:

1. it constrains the legality, safety, or user acceptability of the next plausible action;
2. it represents an unresolved obligation or pending external operation;
3. losing it would force expensive or impossible reconstruction;
4. it is needed to interpret an active mode, procedure, or artifact;
5. it has already caused a correction or near miss.

Eviction rule: remove an item from the live area only when it is explicitly discharged, superseded with a pointer to the superseding item, expired by a named condition, or cheaply reconstructible from an authoritative source whose identity is retained. “Old” alone is not an eviction criterion. Raw successful tool output, completed conversational scaffolding, duplicate explanations, and superseded plans are first to leave; user constraints, unresolved commitments, active-operation handles, and irreversible-action boundaries are last.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to preserve constraints and commitments better than prose-only summaries because typed required fields make omission mechanically visible and event-time recording avoids end-of-window reconstruction; the evidence is the FAA status-area-plus-checklist design and NASA’s interruption findings. The harness benefit is **UNVERIFIED; experiment V1 will settle it**.

What it catches that an ordinary rolling summary can miss:

* an active background command disappears while its output is still awaited;
* a user’s “do not modify X” constraint is compressed into vague prose;
* a rejected alternative reappears because its rejection condition was lost;
* a promised artifact or recipient vanishes after the technical task completes;
* evidence remains in context but its freshness or provenance is forgotten;
* a completed fact occupies tokens even though it is cheaply re-readable.

### 3.2 UNVERIFIED proposal — Positive Context Relief at compaction or handoff

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

At every compaction, model handoff, session resume, or long-running task continuation, run this protocol:

1. **Record before relief.** Flush event-time changes into the Context Status Area. Do not ask the summarizer to reconstruct all state from scratch.
2. **Preview from independent sources.** Re-read the bounded status area and inspect authoritative live signals relevant to active work: current file metadata/diff for edited files, process state for active commands, goal/plan state, and existence/identity of required artifacts. This is the detector control.
3. **Brief exceptions.** Generate a short narrative only for abnormal state, ambiguities, rationale that cannot be encoded tersely, and recent changes.
4. **Run a relief checklist.** Mechanically require every schema category to be present, explicitly empty, or marked unknown. Validate pointers and freshness for critical items.
5. **Resolve conflicts.** When narrative, sidecar, user instruction, and live state disagree, do not silently choose. Retain the conflict as an open loop, with authority precedence where known.
6. **Positive acceptance.** Emit a new `context_generation`, parent generation, timestamp, and manifest: `retained`, `summarized`, `pointer_only`, `evicted_as_reconstructible`, `unknown/lost`.
7. **Post-transfer overlap.** For the next action-selection turn, keep the prior critical tail and old manifest addressable. Compare the proposed next action against both generations. Rehydrate on discrepancy, then release the overlap.

The manifest is the automation-mode annunciator. It should be visible to the agent and optionally to the owner in a terse form such as:

```text
CONTEXT RELIEF g41 <- g40
retained: mission, 7 constraints, 3 open loops, active process p18
pointer-only: 12 evidence items, 4 completed tool outputs
evicted: 31 reconstructible/duplicate items
unknown: rationale for decision d7 (rehydrate before revisiting)
```

The protocol is **likely (55-80%)** to catch silent compaction damage before it changes an external action, because the preview uses live state and required fields rather than relying only on the outgoing narrative; the evidence is FAA positive transfer plus mode-awareness research. It is not “two mechanically independent signals” when the same model merely summarizes and critiques itself. Independence comes from comparing model-produced state with file/process/tool facts and deterministic schema checks. The outcome is **UNVERIFIED; experiments V1 and V2 will settle it**.

### 3.3 UNVERIFIED proposal — Hold/resume tokens for interrupted procedures

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

When a multi-step procedure is interrupted, write a hold token before switching focus:

```text
procedure: release-checklist/v3
last_verified_step: 4
next_step: 5
blocked_by: build process p18
preconditions_fingerprint: git HEAD, dirty paths, config version
must_restart_if: HEAD or scoped files change
```

On resumption, compare the fingerprint to current state. Continue only when prerequisites still hold; otherwise restart from the earliest invalidated step. The token is removed only after explicit completion or cancellation.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce skipped or duplicated steps after interruptions because it externalizes precisely the fragile resumption point identified in NASA checklist research. Its effect size in an agent harness is **UNVERIFIED; interruption-injection experiment V2 will settle it**.

### 3.4 UNVERIFIED proposal — Critical-phase context reserve, not blanket “sterility”

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

During a declared critical phase—destructive action preparation, production/deployment change, credential handling, migration, final acceptance, or any user-defined high-risk stage—reserve a fixed context slice for mission, authority, hazard, rollback, verification, and current-state evidence. Defer low-priority retrieval expansion, verbose successful tool output, tangential suggestions, and unrelated housekeeping until the phase ends.

Hard bypasses must admit user messages, permission changes, errors, safety/security signals, state conflicts, and tool results required by the active operation. Deferred material is queued with a pointer; it is not deleted merely because it arrived during the phase.

This is **roughly even (45-55%)** to improve overall task outcomes. The aviation evidence supports attention gating during critical phases, but tokens do not distract a model in exactly the way conversation distracts a pilot, and suppression can remove a weak signal needed later. The proposal is **UNVERIFIED; phase-stratified experiment V3 will settle whether reduced irrelevant occupancy outweighs missed-signal risk**. Until V3 succeeds, this feature should run in shadow mode and annotate what it would defer.

### 3.5 UNVERIFIED proposal — Context-loss near-miss ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Record a local, redacted event when a strong signal indicates context loss:

* the owner repeats or corrects a prior constraint or decision;
* an accepted handoff field is later found unknown or wrong;
* an interrupted procedure resumes at an invalid step;
* a pointer cannot rehydrate its source;
* the agent acts in a different context mode than the manifest declares;
* a post-transfer overlap catches an omission before external effect.

Store event category, context generations, the item’s prior disposition (`retained`, `pointer_only`, `evicted`, or `never_recorded`), detection signal, severity, and redacted repair. Do not store hidden reasoning, secrets, or full conversation by default. Aggregate failures should tune admission/eviction rules; an isolated event should not automatically become a permanent prompt rule.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal systematic context-policy failures that answer-quality scoring conflates with other errors, because it records the decision boundary where information was lost. The inference from ASRS is partial: a single-owner harness lacks aviation’s reporting disincentives, while local telemetry creates privacy risk. The recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; experiment V4 will settle whether detected events have acceptable precision and lead to replay-measurable policy improvements**.

## 4. What the combined design catches

The mechanisms form a small error-trapping chain:

```text
event becomes operationally relevant
        ↓
typed status update + provenance
        ↓
admission/eviction policy (kernel / pointer / discard)
        ↓
compaction relief: preview → exception brief → checklist → acceptance
        ↓
manifest annunciates what changed
        ↓
one-turn overlap checks next action against old and new state
        ↓
later correction/near miss feeds policy evaluation
```

The combined system is **likely (55-80%)** to detect these failure classes, with evidence from the analogous aviation controls but **UNVERIFIED until V1–V4**:

1. **Silent constraint eviction.** A required status field becomes missing or `unknown`, rather than disappearing inside fluent summary prose.
2. **State/narrative divergence.** The summary says a process finished, but the process signal says it is running or failed; the relief preview surfaces the conflict.
3. **Stale evidence.** The pointer’s version/hash no longer matches the live source; the system requires re-verification.
4. **Lost open loop.** An unanswered question or promised follow-up cannot be silently dropped because relief requires an explicit category disposition.
5. **Interrupted-procedure skip.** Resume begins from a named step and checks whether prior state changed.
6. **Compaction mode confusion.** The model sees that raw history was replaced, knows what became pointer-only, and can rehydrate instead of assuming full recall.
7. **Token pollution by completed detail.** Reconstructible raw output leaves while its result, provenance, and retrieval pointer remain.
8. **Premature declaration of completion.** Mission, commitments, and verification fields remain active until explicitly discharged.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 5.1 Aviation state is more externally observable

Aircraft positions, clearances, weather, equipment status, and communications have standardized representations and can often be cross-checked against the world. Agent context includes ambiguous intent, aesthetic preference, implicit social commitments, and reasons that may exist only in conversation. A typed status area can create false confidence by representing only what fits its schema.

Consequence: never treat “all fields populated” as proof of complete context. Preserve an `unknown` state, source pointers, and a bounded recent conversational tail. The protocol is a detector for known categories, not a proof that nothing was lost.

### 5.2 Sender and receiver may not be independent

ATC relief uses two people with partly independent observation. Agent compaction often uses the same model or model family to create and consume the summary. It may reproduce the same blind spot during “review.” Calling a second prompt a cross-check would overstate independence.

Consequence: reserve independence claims for deterministic schema validation and different data channels—file state, process state, tool exit status, owner text, and pointer integrity. A second agent or critic is not proposed; adding one would consume context and reintroduce orchestration complexity without evidence.

### 5.3 Controller relief has a clean responsibility boundary; context decays continuously

Information can become stale or irrelevant between formal compactions. A protocol that runs only at the boundary will miss event-time loss and may be forced to reconstruct the whole shift, the exact anti-pattern the FAA status area avoids.

Consequence: maintain the Context Status Area incrementally. Compaction validates and packages it; compaction is not its sole author.

### 5.4 Checklist completion is not operational understanding

The FAA’s own 2008 study found the relief checklist insufficient to eliminate elevated early-on-position error patterns and pointed to time pressure, transfer type, and operator state ([DOT/FAA/AM-08/16](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/data_research/research/med_humanfacs/oamtechreports/200816.pdf)). A harness can fill required fields with vague or wrong text and pass syntax.

Consequence: measure downstream retrieval and action correctness, not checklist completion. Require provenance and freshness. Test under time/token pressure. A checklist is a forcing function, not a truth machine.

### 5.5 “Sterile cockpit” can become harmful censorship

Aviation’s rule controls human attention during phases whose hazards are relatively well characterized. Software tasks have shifting relevance; a tangential user remark can invalidate the whole plan. Model context occupancy also differs from conscious distraction.

Consequence: critical-phase gating must be reversible, transparent, and bypassable. Defer rather than erase. The confidence for this borrow is only **roughly even (45-55%)**, based on this disanalogy; V3 must show benefit before enforcement.

### 5.6 Memory items can become prompt bloat

Aircrew memory items are tightly curated for a specific aircraft and emergency. A general harness serves heterogeneous tasks. Declaring every rule “critical” recreates the context-overload problem and makes truly critical constraints less salient.

Consequence: the kernel must have a hard size budget and an explicit promotion/demotion test based on next-action hazard, irreconstructibility, and unresolved obligation. Domain procedures remain just-in-time references.

### 5.7 Post-mortem incentives differ

ASRS addresses fear of discipline and loss of safety information across a large industry. A private agent harness may not need immunity, but it can leak secrets through telemetry or overfit policy to a vocal subset of corrections.

Consequence: keep the ledger local, minimize and redact payloads, separate event capture from rule promotion, and require replay evidence before changing policy.

## 6. Validation program

No recommendation above should move from shadow mode to enforcement on analogy alone.

### V1 — Paired long-horizon replay: context relief versus baseline

Sample completed long sessions with at least one compaction or resumption. Construct a future-use gold set mechanically: items are “needed later” when a later user message, tool argument, file edit, verification assertion, or correction refers to them. Manually adjudicate a stratified sample to measure detector precision.

Replay each prefix with the same model, tool fixtures, and token budget under:

* baseline rolling summary;
* typed status area only;
* relief protocol without overlap;
* full relief protocol;
* placebo checklist of equal token cost but no typed fields/provenance.

Measure high-severity omission rate, incorrect retained facts, owner-correction proxies, task success, external-action divergence, rehydration count/latency, and total tokens. Stratify by context pressure and task domain. The placebo arm distinguishes checklist structure from simply spending more tokens.

Promotion criterion is **UNVERIFIED; preregister a minimum reduction in severe omission and a maximum token/latency cost before running V1 rather than selecting thresholds after observing results**. The experiment settles the central claim that positive relief adds value beyond summary quality and token spend.

### V2 — Interruption and handoff fault injection

Create deterministic multi-step tasks, interrupt them after each step with user redirection, long tool output, failed command, compaction, and state mutation. Inject omissions into outgoing status, stale pointers, falsely completed processes, and mismatched file fingerprints.

Measure skipped/duplicated steps, unsafe continuation after precondition change, detection before external effect, false alarms, and recovery time. Include a same-model self-review arm and a deterministic/live-state review arm to test the independence claim.

V2 settles whether hold tokens and source-based preview catch errors rather than merely describe them.

### V3 — Critical-phase gate shadow trial

Do not suppress anything initially. Mark candidate critical phases and log which context/tool material the gate would defer, which bypass admitted it, and whether deferred material was needed in the next N decisions. Compare token occupancy and action quality with counterfactual replay.

V3 settles the **UNVERIFIED** premise that phase-based reserve improves signal-to-noise without hiding weak but important signals. Given the **roughly even (45-55%)** confidence, failure of this test should delete the proposal rather than invite more scaffolding.

### V4 — Near-miss detector precision and policy utility

Run candidate detectors on historical and live shadow traffic. Validate each detected correction against the source turns: was it genuinely caused by eviction, by failure to admit information, by stale information, or by an unrelated reasoning/tool error? Then alter one admission/eviction rule and replay matched cases plus unaffected controls.

Measure precision, secret/redaction failures, storage volume, repeat-event reduction, and regressions from retaining too much. V4 settles whether an ASRS-like ledger yields actionable context evidence rather than a generic error log.

### V5 — Boundary stress and deletion test

Stress the design with heterogeneous tasks, rapid goal changes, very short windows, very large tool outputs, multiple active processes, and deliberately ambiguous user intent. Ablate every component. If removal of a component does not measurably worsen severe omissions or recovery cost, remove it.

This deletion test enforces the anti-overfit gate. The design succeeds only if the smallest surviving set beats baseline under matched budgets.

## 7. Implementation order and stop rules

1. **Shadow manifest only.** Produce admission/eviction manifests without changing context. Validate that the manifest can find known-present constraints and processes before making any absence claim.
2. **Context Status Area and hold tokens.** These have the closest structural match and the highest confidence bands.
3. **Positive relief with source validation.** Add explicit generation IDs, pointer checks, and one-turn overlap.
4. **Near-miss ledger.** Add only after context dispositions exist, otherwise it cannot attribute loss to a decision.
5. **Critical-phase reserve.** Last, shadow-first, and delete if V3 is neutral.

Stop or roll back when any of the following occurs:

* the status block grows without bound or displaces task evidence;
* schema completion rises but downstream omission does not fall;
* false “unknown/lost” alarms cause excessive rehydration;
* same-model review is being described as independent verification;
* sensitive content enters the near-miss ledger;
* a component cannot show incremental benefit in ablation;
* implementation begins to require a daemon, second orchestration runtime, vector/KG memory, durable workflow engine, trained critic, SaaS telemetry, or guardrail framework.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Keep everything”

Rejected. It does not solve a finite window and retains duplicated, stale, and reconstructible material. Aviation’s status-area/reference split points in the opposite direction: keep operational state live, keep detailed procedure accessible.

### “Use a better prose summarizer”

Rejected as the sole mechanism. Prose has no mechanically required disposition for active operations, unresolved questions, constraints, or unknowns, and it does not announce mode changes. It may remain the exception briefing.

### “Run a second critic agent at every compaction”

Rejected. It is not guaranteed independent, adds token and orchestration cost, and violates the minimal-architecture bias. Deterministic checks plus authoritative live sources provide clearer independence.

### “Store all history in a vector database or knowledge graph”

Rejected. Retrieval storage does not decide what must remain immediately active, does not guarantee recall at the right moment, and is a previously rejected architecture class under this program’s anti-overfit gate. Source-addressable pointers are sufficient for the proposed experiment.

### “Train a learned eviction policy immediately”

Rejected. There is no validated label set yet, it risks encoding current-model quirks, and it makes failure explanations harder. First collect disposition-linked near misses and test transparent rules.

### “Apply sterile cockpit globally”

Rejected. The disanalogy is too strong: relevance changes unpredictably, and suppressing unexpected user input is dangerous. Only a reversible, bypassable, shadow-tested critical-phase reserve remains as a low-confidence candidate.

## 9. Final recommendation

Borrow **positive transfer**, not aviation aesthetics. Build a small operational status area, explicit hold/resume tokens, a compaction relief protocol with positive acceptance, and a visible before/after manifest. Validate against authoritative live state and retain a brief overlap. Treat raw history as evictable when reconstructible, but treat mission, authority, constraints, unresolved obligations, active operations, procedure holds, hazards, and evidence identity as the protected kernel until discharged.

This bounded borrow **likely (55-80%)** transfers because both domains involve continuity of safety-relevant work across a finite-attention handoff, and because the controls address state externalization, omission detection, interruption recovery, and mode awareness rather than human-specific motor skills. It is not more than likely because agent intent is less observable, sender and receiver may share failure modes, and FAA evidence shows checklist-based relief is not sufficient. The recommendation remains **UNVERIFIED until V1 and V2 demonstrate fewer severe omissions under equal model and token budgets**.

The “sterile context” and ASRS analogies are secondary. Critical-phase gating is **roughly even (45-55%)** and should be deleted if shadow trials do not show net benefit. A near-miss ledger is **likely (55-80%)** to improve diagnosis but should not promote rules without replay evidence.
