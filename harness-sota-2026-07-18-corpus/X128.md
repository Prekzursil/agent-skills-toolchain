# X128 — forensic-science → trust-calibration

## Executive result

The strongest transferable mechanism is **casework-embedded blind proficiency testing, stratified by task and difficulty, coupled to controlled information exposure and blind independent checking**. It transfers because forensic examination and agent work share the relevant causal structure: a fallible decision-maker operates on heterogeneous cases; knowing that a case is a test changes behavior; ordinary cases usually lack immediately observable ground truth; irrelevant context can move a judgment; and performance on one task or difficulty does not license confidence on another. The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to produce more honest per-agent/per-task trust estimates than self-reported confidence, generic benchmark scores, or aggregate success rates, because forensic guidance explicitly distinguishes blind operational testing from declared testing, requires representative difficulty, and separates examiner performance from the whole pipeline; the remaining uncertainty is whether hidden probes can be made indistinguishable from this owner's real work without distorting that work.

This is not a recommendation to import a forensic laboratory bureaucracy. The minimum useful borrow is a small protocol on the existing hook/agent/skill bus: occasionally route a hidden, casework-like task with known outcome; record the exact agent configuration and task stratum; score the outcome and abstention; keep uncertainty visible; and use an independently produced answer before exposing a reviewer to the first answer. No vector database, knowledge graph, second orchestrator, trained critic, durable workflow engine, guardrail framework, or observability SaaS is justified.

The literal “double-blind” analogy does **not** transfer cleanly. A scorer of a ground-truth task must usually know the truth, and a router must know which item is synthetic. What transfers is narrower: the worker is blind to test status, the independent checker is blind to the first result and outcome-favoring context, and any subjective adjudicator is blind to agent identity. Calling that whole arrangement “double-blind” would overclaim.

Scope note: this is an external ideation lens as requested. I did not inspect or make claims about the local subject tree. Therefore, “what we currently miss” below means failure modes missed by ordinary production telemetry, self-evaluation, declared benchmarks, or non-independent review. Whether this particular harness already implements any element is **UNVERIFIED — settle by an independent configuration inventory plus a known-present detector control and a second signal from executed event logs**.

## 1. What forensic science solved

### 1.1 Blind proficiency testing measures operational behavior, not test-taking behavior

In forensic science, proficiency testing gives an examiner or laboratory a specimen whose source or expected result is known to the test administrator. A declared/open test tells the examiner it is a test. A blind test enters the normal case stream and is made to resemble ordinary evidence, so the examiner does not know until after completion. The purpose is not merely certification; it is to estimate how the examiner and the surrounding process behave under operational conditions.

The OSAC Human Factors Committee's technical publication says blind specimens should enter ordinary casework in a way that makes them indistinguishable, recommends checking whether examiners detect the supposedly blind specimens, and explains that blind studies can reveal whether open-study performance matches actual practice. It also states the limiting conditions: blind studies are easiest where items are uniform, case size is small, and little contextual information is needed; otherwise retesting or non-blind black-/white-box studies may be more viable. This is guidance, not a binding standard ([NIST/OSAC, *Human Factors in Validation and Performance Testing of Forensic Science*, 2020](https://www.nist.gov/adlp/spo/organization-scientific-area-committees-forensic-science/human-factors-validation-and)).

An OSAC friction-ridge proficiency-testing document is more prescriptive: test administration should avoid even subtle cues to expected results; it defines blind and non-blind formats; calls blind testing more robust; requires evaluation criteria and statistical methods to be documented before administration; and evaluates agreement, supporting documentation, and procedural compliance, not just the final categorical answer ([OSAC 2022-S-0012, sections 4.4.6–4.5.1](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2022/08/15/OSAC%202022-S-0012%20Standard%20for%20Proficiency%20Testing%20in%20FR%20Exam_REGISTRY%20VERSION.pdf)).

Operational implementations support the distinction. A peer-reviewed account of blind proficiency programs describes known-ground-truth samples submitted through the normal pipeline as real cases and notes that declared commercial tests may differ from casework in both task and difficulty or test only one stage of the pipeline. It also records concrete unblinding failures: a crowbar carried implausible print patterns and submission handwriting was “too neat” ([Mejia et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/)). A separate implementation at the Harris County Institute of Forensic Sciences deliberately tested the complete process from evidence receipt through report release and reports that doing so was feasible but operationally difficult ([Wickenheiser et al., 2020 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31922611/)). These are two mechanically different signals for the proposition that hidden, realistic tests can expose pipeline behavior that a declared component test does not: a cross-laboratory methods analysis and a named laboratory implementation.

The forensic mechanism is therefore:

1. Establish ground truth independently of the examiner.
2. Make the test item and its administrative wrapper representative of routine work.
3. Insert it into the normal workflow without telling the examiner which item is a test.
4. Predefine scoring, including procedural conformance and all outcome categories.
5. Measure the examiner and, where intended, the whole intake-to-report system.
6. Check for test detection; a test that is recognized is not evidence of ordinary-case performance.
7. Feed results into improvement, not merely a pass/fail badge.

### 1.2 Performance must be conditioned on task, specimen mix, and difficulty

Forensic science has also learned that “the error rate” is not a portable scalar. The OSAC human-factors publication says test items should represent casework and that difficulty must be considered; it requires both false inclusions and false exclusions to be reported and says inconclusive/not-suitable rates matter to interpretation ([NIST/OSAC 2020](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2023/10/26/OSACTechSeriesPub_HF%20in%20Validation%20and%20Performance%20Testing%20of%20Forensic%20Science_March2020.pdf)). NIST's handwriting human-factors review likewise warns that proficiency tests designed for accreditation may not represent casework, says comparison types should sometimes be considered separately, and insists that challenging material is needed to find system limits ([NISTIR 8282r1, section 4.2.6](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2021/NIST.IR.8282r1.pdf)).

Empirical work illustrates why. In a large latent-print study, examiners differed substantially along largely independent skill dimensions; limited presentations gave wide uncertainty for individual estimates, and simple observed variation overstated true examiner variation because of sampling error ([Ulery et al., 2011](https://www.nist.gov/document/113uleryfullibpcpdf)). A more recent bullet-comparison study found decisions varied with bullet quality, ammunition/firearm conditions, and participant ([Hicklin et al., 2024](https://www.nist.gov/publications/accuracy-and-reproducibility-bullet-comparison-decisions-forensic-examiners)). A blind latent-print program found print quality significantly associated with conclusions and accuracy and, importantly, exposed performance of the AFIS-plus-examiner system rather than the human alone ([Gardner, Neuman & Kelley, 2021 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34004529/)).

The solved principle is not “rank examiners.” It is **estimate performance for a defined decision, population of cases, difficulty range, workflow, and time window, while retaining sampling uncertainty and abstentions**.

### 1.3 Sequential unmasking and blind checking prevent conclusion contamination

Forensic examiners sometimes need contextual information, but some context is irrelevant to the analytical judgment and can push it toward an expected outcome. Linear sequential unmasking (LSU) addresses this by making the examiner analyze the trace first, record observations and confidence, then expose reference or contextual material in a controlled order; substantive revisions after exposure are documented and can trigger additional review. The original LSU proposal describes trace-first analysis, confidence recording, bounded revision, and blind review for suspicious high-confidence changes ([Dror et al., 2015 abstract](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/letter-editor-context-management-toolbox-linear-sequential)).

The UK Forensic Science Regulator's guidance independently supports the same causal control: give the practitioner only analysis-relevant information, use a case manager when broader context is needed, make suitability judgments before reference comparison, and document later changes to prevent post-comparison rationalization or circular reasoning ([FSR-G-217, issue 2, section 1.4](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/470549/FSR-G-217_Cognitive_bias_appendix.pdf)). A New Zealand protocol operationalized independent checking by having the checker interpret evidence without case information or the original examiner's notes/conclusion ([Osborne & Taylor, 2018 abstract](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/contextual-information-management-example-independent-checking-review)). Thus the evidence is not merely a repeated recommendation: one regulator describes the control and a separate laboratory workflow implemented blind peer review.

### 1.4 Contamination controls diagnose the process, not merely the specimen

In DNA work, a negative control contains the reagents and processing steps but no sample; an unexpected result flags contamination introduced by reagents, disposables, or handling and may implicate other samples processed with it ([NIST OSAC Lexicon, “Negative Control”](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/36651)). The transferable insight is that a control should travel through the same process as the evidence while omitting the target signal. That localizes a failure to the pipeline rather than treating every bad answer as examiner incompetence.

This mechanism is useful but transfers only partially. A blank prompt is not an agent-work control: language models will still generate text, and many tasks legitimately require priors. The closer analogue is a task whose supplied evidence deliberately does **not** support the requested claim, with the correct action defined as “insufficient evidence/abstain.” Such controls test whether the pipeline fabricates support or imports a conclusion from contaminated context.

## 2. Structural transfer test

### 2.1 Where the analogy holds

| Forensic structure | Agent-harness structure | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Examiner identity is not enough; discipline and specimen difficulty change performance. | Agent name/model is not enough; task family, tool regime, ambiguity, and environment change performance. | **Very likely (80-90%)**, supported by the task/difficulty-dependent forensic studies above and the direct heterogeneity of agent tasks. |
| Knowing one is tested changes care, thresholds, and checking. | A benchmark label, evaluation prompt, or obvious canary can elicit extra deliberation or benchmark-specific behavior. | **Likely (55-80%)** for deployed agents; forensic operational evidence is strong, but the magnitude for this harness is **UNVERIFIED — settle with randomized paired hidden-vs-declared probes drawn from the same item generator**. |
| Routine casework often has unknown ground truth. | Many real agent outputs are accepted without a later objective outcome. | **Very likely (80-90%)** as a general structural fact; exact prevalence here is **UNVERIFIED — settle by sampling completed tasks and coding whether an independent outcome became observable**. |
| Outcome-favoring context can influence a subjective examination. | Prior agent answers, owner preference, expected patch, or judge rationale can anchor a worker/reviewer. | **Likely (55-80%)**; the forensic causal mechanism is established, but agent-specific effect size is **UNVERIFIED — settle with randomized answer-first versus evidence-first review trials**. |
| A pipeline can fail even when the examiner's narrow analytic step is correct. | Routing, retrieval, tool execution, transformation, and reporting can fail around an otherwise capable model. | **Almost certain (90-99%)**, because both systems are multi-stage pipelines and the blind latent-print work explicitly measured AFIS-plus-examiner behavior. |
| Abstention/inconclusive rates trade off against error rates. | An agent can appear accurate by refusing hard work, or productive by guessing. | **Very likely (80-90%)**, a direct decision-theoretic equivalence; whether current agents exploit either extreme is **UNVERIFIED — settle by measuring accuracy and coverage jointly on hidden probes**. |

### 2.2 Where it breaks

1. **Ground truth is scarcer and more perishable.** Forensic proficiency samples can be manufactured with known source. Many software/design/research tasks have plural acceptable outputs, evolving dependencies, or delayed success criteria. A hidden test with a weak “gold answer” can calibrate agreement with the curator rather than truth. This is the central disanalogy.

2. **Agents are versioned artifacts, not stable practitioners.** Model upgrades, system prompts, tool availability, context windows, and skill revisions can change the effective agent overnight. A long-lived personal proficiency score is therefore invalid. The correct unit is an agent-configuration fingerprint over a bounded time window.

3. **Agents may share priors or training contamination.** Multiple “independent” agents using the same base model can make correlated errors. Blind peer agreement is not independent evidence in the statistical sense. The reviewer can also reconstruct likely answers from conventions even without seeing the first result.

4. **Digital probes are copyable and detectable.** Exact benchmark items can leak, hashes can be recognized, and stylometric wrappers can reveal synthetic work. Forensic programs also face realism problems, but machine agents can detect patterns at scale and retain them through prompt/tool artifacts.

5. **The ethical boundary differs.** A forensic blind test consumes laboratory effort but is designed not to affect a real person’s case. Injecting synthetic tasks into a live owner workflow could waste time, mutate files, send messages, or pollute repositories. Agent probes must be shadowed, sandboxed, or restricted to non-side-effecting work.

6. **Literal double blinding is impossible for objective scoring.** The worker can be blind to test status, but the deterministic scorer necessarily encodes expected properties. For subjective output, an identity-blind adjudicator can be used, but if it sees a reference answer it is not blind to the expected conclusion.

7. **Trust is not probability of correctness alone.** Security boundaries, reversibility, cost, latency, disclosure of uncertainty, and procedural compliance matter. A single accuracy number would erase precisely the dimensions the borrow is meant to expose.

These disanalogies do not defeat the mechanism, but they sharply limit its claim. The borrow is **likely (55-80%)** to transfer for repeatable task strata with independently checkable outcomes and safe shadow execution, supported by the shared causal structure and operational forensic implementations. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to yield defensible calibration for singular strategy, taste, novel research synthesis, or owner-preference tasks without later outcome evidence; the difficulty of defining non-circular ground truth is the evidence.

## 3. Concrete harness translation

### Proposal A [UNVERIFIED — settle with a 200-item retrospective labeling study]: define the object being trusted

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Never store “agent X = 87% trustworthy.” Define an immutable execution identity:

`model/version + system-prompt hash + agent definition hash + enabled tool/skill set + relevant policy/config hashes + environment class`

Define a small task taxonomy based on observable work, not persona names. A starting schema:

- task family: fact lookup, repo navigation, diagnosis, implementation, test repair, shell operation, code review, research synthesis, planning;
- consequence class: read-only, reversible write, destructive/external side effect;
- verification regime: exact oracle, property tests, delayed production outcome, rubric/adjudication only;
- difficulty indicators: input size, dependency breadth, ambiguity, novelty, tool count, cross-platform requirement;
- context condition: fresh, long-context, prior-answer exposed, owner-correction exposed;
- date/config epoch.

The taxonomy is **likely (55-80%)** to produce useful strata if retrospective labeling shows reasonable inter-rater agreement; evidence is the forensic need to separate comparison types and difficulty. It is **UNVERIFIED — settle with two independent labelers on 200 historical tasks, report per-field agreement, collapse fields below a preregistered threshold, and confirm predictive separation on held-out outcomes**. This heading inherits the tag because all later per-task calibration depends on the taxonomy being reproducible.

### Proposal B [UNVERIFIED — settle with a no-side-effect pilot and test-detection audit]: embed blind proficiency probes

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a small probe broker at the existing task-routing boundary. It samples from a versioned bank of casework-like tasks with independently known outcomes and submits them through the same agent route and tools as ordinary work. Hidden metadata lives outside the agent-visible envelope. The first phase must be shadow-only and read-only: disposable repository snapshots, local fixtures, mocked external services, or tasks with no mutations.

Each probe record contains:

- probe/version ID and creation lineage;
- task-stratum labels and intended difficulty;
- objective oracle or predeclared scoring properties;
- allowed tools and side effects;
- agent-configuration fingerprint;
- declared confidence or explicit abstention, captured before feedback;
- final output plus tool trace and procedural violations;
- whether the agent suspected a test and why;
- scorer version, score, and adjudication status.

The broker must mix ordinary-difficulty items with boundary/stress items. It must include both positive controls (the supplied evidence supports a determinate answer) and negative controls (the supplied evidence is insufficient, so the correct behavior is to abstain or request information). It must not reuse exact public benchmark text. A post-task “did you suspect this was a probe?” field, plus a classifier comparing probe wrappers with sampled real-task wrappers, supplies two mechanically different signals of unblinding: agent report and distributional distinguishability. If either shows easy detection, results are labeled declared-like and excluded from operational calibration.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to catch ordinary-case failures that declared evals miss, based on blind forensic programs measuring full pipelines, but realism in agent work is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a 50-probe pilot, randomized declared counterparts, zero persistent side effects, and preregistered thresholds for detection and outcome differences**.

What it catches:

- agents that perform well on recognizable evals but skip checks in routine-looking work;
- routing/retrieval/tool/reporting faults that component benchmarks miss;
- task-specific weaknesses hidden by a global score;
- unsupported-answer behavior exposed by negative controls;
- “accurate” agents whose coverage is low because they abstain on most difficult cases;
- productive agents whose low abstention masks costly false claims;
- drift after a model, prompt, skill, tool, or environment change;
- correlated failure across several nominal agent roles using the same base configuration.

### Proposal C [UNVERIFIED — settle by backtesting coverage and calibration]: produce task-conditioned trust cards, not ranks

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Append probe outcomes to a local JSONL ledger and generate a compact trust card at routing time. No service or database is needed. For each exact configuration and task stratum, report:

- sample count and time/config window;
- correct, incorrect, abstain/inconclusive, and unscorable counts;
- false-claim and false-refusal rates where applicable;
- coverage (fraction answered) alongside conditional accuracy;
- procedural-conformance rate;
- confidence calibration by coarse bins only when enough observations exist;
- a binomial/Beta interval or conservative lower bound, not just a point estimate;
- nearest broader stratum used for fallback and the distance/fields that differ;
- probe-detection rate;
- status: `CALIBRATED`, `SPARSE`, `STALE`, `SHIFTED`, or `UNSCORABLE`.

Routing policy should treat missing evidence as `UNKNOWN`, not average it across unrelated work. A new configuration inherits no personal score; at most it receives a clearly labeled prior from the nearest configuration and is shadow-tested. High-consequence operations require either adequate lower-bound evidence in the matching stratum or independent verification.

The trust card is **likely (55-80%)** to improve routing decisions over a leaderboard because forensic evidence shows multidimensional examiner skill and strong task/difficulty dependence. The exact estimator and thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle by chronological backtesting: compare predicted bands with held-out probe outcomes, measure empirical interval coverage, Brier score for confidence, answer coverage, and decision cost; reject any scheme that does not beat a task-family base-rate baseline**.

Sparse-data warning: individual forensic error estimates can have wide uncertainty even with dozens of presentations. Therefore, a card with small `n` must say `SPARSE`; zero observed errors is not zero risk. This prevents the most dangerous cosmetic result: a precise-looking trust percentage derived from a handful of easy probes.

### Proposal D [UNVERIFIED — settle with randomized answer-first vs evidence-first trials]: sequentially unmask review

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For high-consequence or weakly calibrated strata:

1. The primary agent receives task-relevant evidence but not expected-answer hints, another agent's conclusion, or outcome-favoring owner commentary unless operationally necessary.
2. It records a structured commitment: conclusion, decisive evidence references, uncertainty, and missing information.
3. The checker independently receives the original task and raw artifacts, not the primary conclusion, rationale, confidence, or identity.
4. The checker commits its own result.
5. Only then does a reconciliation step unmask both outputs and compare claims/evidence.
6. Any revision is logged with the newly revealed information that caused it. Agreement is not counted as two independent votes when both workers share the same base model/configuration.

This is not a new orchestration runtime; it is a context-release policy on the existing agent bus. It is **likely (55-80%)** to expose anchoring and rubber-stamp review on subjective tasks, supported by forensic LSU and blind-checking protocols, but the effect size in agent review is **UNVERIFIED — settle with randomized paired trials on matched tasks, scoring defect discovery, false alarms, latency, and whether disagreement predicts downstream failure**.

What it catches that ordinary review misses:

- a checker repeating the primary answer's framing rather than re-examining evidence;
- a high-confidence conclusion that changes only after exposure to a target answer;
- missing-evidence disagreements hidden by polished shared prose;
- prompt/context leakage that causes supposedly independent agents to reproduce the same unsupported claim.

### Proposal E [UNVERIFIED — settle with planted contamination and clean controls]: add provenance and negative controls

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Attach a lightweight provenance manifest to evaluation and high-consequence runs: which inputs were available at each phase, hashes of reference answers/previous outputs, when each was revealed, and which tools returned the decisive evidence. Add paired negative-control tasks in which a plausible answer is present only in task-irrelevant context or a previous agent output, while the authoritative evidence does not support it.

The control is successful only if the worker refuses to treat the contaminant as evidence and the blind checker remains unaffected before unmasking. If both repeat the planted claim, the result diagnoses context-path contamination rather than simply “two agents agreed.”

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal answer laundering and unsupported consensus because it instantiates the same-process/absent-signal logic of forensic negative controls. Its ecological validity is **UNVERIFIED — settle by randomized planted-contaminant trials with semantically matched clean controls and independent deterministic evidence checks**.

## 4. Scoring and decision rules

### 4.1 Predeclare what counts

Scoring must be fixed before a probe runs. Prefer, in order:

1. deterministic tests, exact extraction, schema/property validation, or externally observed outcome;
2. blinded human adjudication against a predeclared rubric, with agent identity removed;
3. multiple independent adjudications with disagreement reported;
4. LLM judging only as a noisy secondary label, never silently promoted to ground truth.

Using the same or closely related model as worker and sole judge is **very likely (80-90%)** to under-detect correlated errors, based on shared-model dependence as a direct mechanism; the magnitude is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a disagreement study against deterministic or human-adjudicated outcomes**.

### 4.2 Preserve the whole decision surface

For binary decisions record true positive, false positive, true negative, and false negative. For open work, define claim-level supported/unsupported, task completion, and abstention. Always report coverage. A harness should not reward an agent for converting difficult tasks into refusals, nor for converting uncertainty into confident guesses.

For self-reported confidence, calibration means: among outcomes assigned approximately 80% confidence, approximately 80% should satisfy the oracle in that task stratum. Do not use confidence as evidence of competence until this relation is demonstrated. LSU's confidence recording makes changes auditable; it does not establish that confidence is accurate.

### 4.3 Route on evidence with asymmetric consequences

Use consequence-specific loss, not a universal threshold. A false claim in read-only brainstorming and an incorrect destructive command do not have equal cost. The routing rule can be simple:

- `CALIBRATED` and adequate conservative lower bound for the matching stratum: normal route;
- `SPARSE` or `STALE`: route but require blind check for consequential work and collect shadow probes;
- `SHIFTED`: freeze inheritance from the prior configuration and requalify;
- repeated negative-control contamination: withhold prior-answer context and require evidence-first checking;
- `UNSCORABLE`: display that trust is judgmental; do not synthesize a number.

This policy is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce high-cost errors if thresholds are fitted to actual consequence weights; it is **UNVERIFIED — settle with offline replay of historical decisions followed by a reversible A/B pilot measuring weighted error cost, latency, and owner overrides**.

## 5. Experiments that can falsify the borrow

The design should be rejected or narrowed if any of these occur:

1. **Blindness failure:** agents identify hidden probes materially above chance from wrapper/style cues, confirmed both by their suspicion reports and a separate distributional detector.
2. **No operational gap:** randomized hidden and declared versions produce equivalent behavior within a preregistered smallest effect of interest. In that case declared tests are cheaper and sufficient for that stratum.
3. **Taxonomy failure:** independent labelers cannot reproduce task/difficulty strata, or strata do not predict held-out error after accounting for sample size.
4. **Oracle failure:** adjudicators disagree so often that “ground truth” mainly measures taste, or deterministic properties fail to predict downstream success.
5. **Nonstationarity dominates:** configuration changes happen faster than enough safe probes can accumulate. Then only system-level or short-lived cohort estimates are defensible.
6. **Review independence failure:** blind independent checking does not improve defect detection over ordinary review, or shared-model agreement remains uncorrelated with correctness.
7. **Behavioral distortion:** the known presence of occasional hidden tests changes all production behavior enough to harm cost/latency without reducing errors.
8. **Safety failure:** probes create persistent side effects, reach external recipients, pollute owner data, or consume material resources. One such event should stop live embedding pending redesign.

A staged evaluation is **very likely (80-90%)** to determine whether the mechanism is worth retaining because each causal link has an observable rejection criterion; evidence is the measurable sequence of blindness, label reliability, oracle validity, predictive calibration, and decision utility. Whether available task volume is enough for tight per-agent/per-task estimates is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a power analysis using observed probe base rates after the initial shadow pilot**.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and implementation size

The proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate:

- It rides the existing routing/hook/agent/skill bus.
- Storage is append-only JSONL plus generated summaries.
- Evaluation uses deterministic existing tests first.
- Context control is a release policy, not a daemon.
- No second orchestration runtime is introduced.
- No vector database or knowledge graph is introduced.
- No durable-execution engine is introduced.
- No trained critic is introduced.
- No observability SaaS is introduced.
- No guardrail framework is introduced.

The ambitious part is the experimental discipline, not code volume. A plausible minimum is one small probe-selection hook, one small scoring/ledger script, one trust-card renderer, and a context-envelope convention. Whether each can remain under roughly 200 lines in this harness is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a throwaway implementation spike against documented extension points; if the bus cannot carry hidden metadata or phased context without a new runtime, stop rather than build the runtime**.

Do not begin with automated routing changes. Begin with read-only shadow probes and retrospective trust cards. Automated gating is warranted only after held-out calibration and decision-utility tests beat a task-base-rate baseline. This sequencing is **very likely (80-90%)** to contain risk because shadow execution separates measurement validity from production mutation; the remaining uncertainty is probe realism.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Generic benchmark leaderboard

Rejected as the primary trust signal. Declared benchmarks invite test-aware behavior, rarely reproduce the exact tool/context pipeline, and average across task mix. They remain useful for broad method validation. The chance that a generic leaderboard alone gives well-calibrated trust for a particular agent/task/configuration is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, supported by forensic evidence on blindness, difficulty, and multidimensional examiner performance.

### Self-reported confidence only

Rejected. Confidence is a useful recorded observation and a potential abstention signal, not proof of accuracy. It becomes actionable only after task-conditioned calibration against outcomes. Confidence-only routing is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be reliable, because it has no external ground-truth anchor.

### Majority vote among agents

Rejected as a calibration mechanism. Shared base models, prompts, retrieved sources, or leaked prior answers make errors correlated; vote count can manufacture certainty. Blind independent outputs are still valuable as defect detectors, but agreement must not be interpreted as independent likelihood multiplication. Majority voting is **unlikely (20-45%)** to improve trust consistently without measured diversity, supported by the dependence mechanism; exact correlation here is **UNVERIFIED — settle with pairwise residual-error correlations on hidden probes**.

### One global “trust score” per named agent

Rejected. It confounds configuration drift, task mix, difficulty, abstention, and consequence. Forensic studies directly show multidimensional and specimen-dependent performance. A global score is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** sufficient for the target problem as stated (“per agent and per task type”).

### Full double-blind evaluator service

Rejected. Literal double blindness is not coherent when a scorer must apply known truth, and a new service would violate the anti-overfit gate unless independently justified. Identity-blind adjudication plus test-blind execution captures the useful controls on the existing bus.

### Continuous hidden testing on real owner tasks

Rejected for the initial phase. It risks side effects and cannot guarantee ground truth. Safe shadow fixtures and delayed-outcome sampling are the appropriate first population. Expanding later is **roughly even (45-55%)** to be worthwhile; it depends on whether shadow performance predicts real outcomes, **UNVERIFIED — settle with a prospective correlation study before any live mutation-capable probe**.

## 8. What this would catch that ordinary harness telemetry misses

Ordinary telemetry can show that a tool errored, a task ended, or an owner corrected an answer. It cannot by itself estimate the counterfactual correctness of accepted outputs with no observed correction. The proposed controls add known outcomes under ordinary-looking conditions and therefore can reveal:

- silent false claims that pass because nobody knew the answer;
- false confidence and poor confidence–accuracy calibration;
- skill that is real for one task class but wrongly generalized to another;
- apparent accuracy purchased by excessive abstention;
- test-taking uplift that makes declared evals optimistic;
- degradation tied to a precise configuration epoch;
- pipeline errors outside the model's narrow reasoning step;
- contamination from prior answers or outcome-favoring context;
- rubber-stamp reviews that agree only after seeing the first conclusion;
- test fixtures whose difficulty mix does not resemble the route they are supposed to validate;
- correlated failures across nominally distinct agents.

Whether these are presently unmeasured in the owner's harness is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the two-signal inventory described in the scope note**. The claim here is narrower and evidence-backed: these failure modes are not inferable from completion counts, aggregate error rates, self-confidence, or non-blind agreement alone.

## 9. Gaps and boundaries

- I did not inspect local repository/configuration state, by explicit unit scope. I make no claim that blind probes, context gating, or trust cards are absent.
- The external literature is strongest for bounded comparison/classification work. Transfer to open-ended creative or strategic tasks remains **unlikely (20-45%)** without delayed outcome measures, because no stable non-circular oracle is available.
- Published forensic implementations establish feasibility, not that their measured error rates transport to agents. No forensic rate was imported as an agent prior.
- Per-agent estimates may remain too sparse for rare catastrophic errors. Zero observed failures must never be rendered as zero risk.
- “Independent checker” means informationally independent at the time of judgment, not statistically independent when configurations share a base model.
