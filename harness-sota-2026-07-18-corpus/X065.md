# X065 — Cross-domain borrow: epidemiology → finite-horizon context control

## Executive finding

The useful epidemiological borrow is **a purpose-specific, staged case definition coupled to sensitivity, specificity, and base-rate-aware predictive value**. Epidemiology does not pretend that one universal threshold can simultaneously find every real case and exclude every look-alike. In an outbreak investigation it defines observable criteria, applies them consistently, permits graded states such as suspected/probable/confirmed, deliberately starts loose when missing a case is expensive, and later tightens the definition when false positives would corrupt analysis. CDC states this sequence directly: an early investigation may use a loose, sensitive definition including confirmed, probable, and possible cases, while later analytic work drops weaker categories because false positives can mislead results ([CDC, *Principles of Epidemiology*, Lesson 6, §2, lines 87–126](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).

That mechanism **partially transfers** to context selection. The transfer is **likely (55–80%) to improve the inspectability and calibration of context admission/eviction**, because both settings allocate scarce follow-up capacity under asymmetric false-negative and false-positive costs, and because the epidemiological source explicitly varies the operating point with the decision phase. Its effect on end-task success is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a preregistered, task-stratified replay A/B comparing the protocol below with the current context policy on token cost, later-needed-item recall, false-inclusion rate, owner corrections, and task success**. I assign only “likely,” rather than “very likely,” because context relevance lacks the independent, contemporaneous disease state that makes diagnostic accuracy well-defined.

The correct translation is therefore not “diagnose every message.” It is a small, existing-bus **context surveillance protocol**: define relevance against the next decisions; place context candidates into suspect/probable/confirmed tiers; keep a compact line list with stable locators for material outside the prompt; use a broad screen before a narrow admission decision; stratify thresholds by the base rate and consequence of later need; and estimate errors from hindsight, including a random audit of evictions. It is **likely (55–80%)** to catch three failures that undifferentiated recency/salience policies miss: low-base-rate false positives that consume many tokens, weak early clues that deserve temporary monitoring but not full prompt residence, and old constraints whose low recency conceals a high cost of omission. This is **UNVERIFIED; the same replay A/B, with those three error classes labeled before scoring, settles the claim**.

The recommendation stays inside the anti-overfit gate: flat records, deterministic rules, one small hook/compaction component, and ordinary retrieval by stable locator. It proposes no second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. The epidemiological mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 A case definition is an operational decision rule, not a metaphysical diagnosis

CDC defines a case definition as a standard set of criteria used to decide whether a person is classified as having the condition of interest. In outbreak work, the criteria combine clinical observations with restrictions by time, place, and person, and must be applied consistently ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 87–94](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)). The important features are:

1. **The target is declared.** Investigators identify the health condition and the purpose of the investigation.
2. **Criteria are observable and operational.** CDC recommends simple, objective measures and explicit bounds on time, place, and person ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 90–92](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).
3. **Criteria are kept separate from the hypothesized cause.** CDC warns not to include the exposure or risk factor being tested in the case definition, because doing so would bake the desired association into the sample ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 93–94](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).
4. **Classification can be graded.** Suspected, probable, and confirmed states represent different evidence strengths. In CDC’s meningococcal example, a suspected case satisfies clinical criteria, a probable case adds cerebrospinal-fluid or epidemiological-link evidence, and a confirmed case adds laboratory confirmation ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 95–116](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).
5. **The definition is allowed to evolve.** Classification is a working instrument. A case may remain probable when confirmation is expensive or unavailable; not all useful surveillance records need the strongest evidence ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 116–118](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).

This matters because a case definition is optimized for a decision, not merely for semantic resemblance. CDC’s field manual says investigators should decide up front which control decisions must be made and what information is needed to support them; it also asks for data that are “necessary and sufficient” for those measures ([CDC, *Field Epidemiology Manual*, “Collecting Data,” lines 75–83](https://www.cdc.gov/field-epi-manual/php/chapters/collecting-data.html)).

### 1.2 Sensitivity and specificity are an explicit trade, not a single quality score

Let the condition be “truly a case,” as established by a reference standard:

- sensitivity = `TP / (TP + FN)`: among true cases, the fraction the rule finds;
- specificity = `TN / (TN + FP)`: among true non-cases, the fraction the rule rejects;
- positive predictive value (PPV) = `TP / (TP + FP)`: among items classified positive, the fraction actually positive;
- negative predictive value (NPV) = `TN / (TN + FN)`: among items classified negative, the fraction actually negative.

These definitions and the need to choose a threshold by weighing false-negative against false-positive consequences are reviewed in Monaghan et al. ([2021, *Medicina*, PMCID PMC8156826](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8156826/)).

CDC is explicit about the phase-dependent trade. Early in an investigation, a loose and sensitive definition helps characterize extent, find affected populations, and generate hypotheses. Later, a tight and specific definition is preferred for analytic testing, because included false positives can produce misleading results ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 119–126](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)). CDC’s surveillance-evaluation guidance makes the resource consequence equally explicit: improving sensitivity can reduce PPV and increase cost; high sensitivity in syndromic surveillance can create false alarms that drain limited resources ([CDC, Lesson 5, §7, lines 38–50](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section7.html)).

The mechanism is therefore a **changing operating point**:

| Investigation phase | Dominant error to avoid | Case definition | Operational reason |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Initial finding / hypothesis generation | False negative | Loose, high sensitivity | An uncollected case may be unrecoverable or expensive to revisit. |
| Confirmation / analytic comparison | False positive | Tight, high specificity | Contaminating the analytic set can distort associations and decisions. |
| Continuing surveillance | Purpose-dependent balance | Periodically evaluated | Usefulness, resources, timeliness, and error costs change over time. |

The table is a paraphrase of the cited CDC guidance, not a claim that epidemiology always uses exactly three temporal phases.

### 1.3 Base rate determines whether a “positive” is worth acting on

For prevalence `p`, sensitivity `Se`, and specificity `Sp`, Bayes’ rule gives:

`PPV = (Se × p) / ((Se × p) + ((1 − Sp) × (1 − p)))`.

PPV and NPV depend strongly on the pre-test probability/prevalence, even when a test’s nominal sensitivity and specificity are held fixed; Monaghan et al. explain this relationship and show the corresponding contingency tables ([2021, PMCID PMC8156826](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8156826/)). A large meta-epidemiological analysis adds an important caveat: measured sensitivity and specificity themselves can vary with prevalence and case spectrum in real diagnostic studies, so assuming transportability across populations is unsafe ([Lin et al., 2023, *CMAJ*, PMCID PMC10356012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10356012/)).

A simple arithmetic illustration shows why base rates matter. With `Se = 0.90`, `Sp = 0.90`, and prevalence `p = 0.01`, PPV is `0.009 / (0.009 + 0.099) = 0.0833`: only about 8.3% of positives are true. At `p = 0.10`, PPV becomes `0.09 / (0.09 + 0.09) = 0.50`. These arithmetic results are **almost certain (90–99%)**, supported by direct substitution into the cited Bayes formula; the residual uncertainty is only transcription or rounding error. The lesson is not that 90/90 is a suitable context classifier. The lesson is that the same apparent screen can flood a low-prevalence stratum with false positives.

### 1.4 Surveillance keeps a line list and separates detection from the full record

After defining cases, outbreak investigators find them systematically and record key fields in a line listing. CDC describes one row per case and columns for critical variables such as identifiers, dates, classification, and diagnostic evidence; new cases are appended and the list can be scanned and updated ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 189–206](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)). This is not merely “memory.” It is a compact control surface that preserves status and provenance without placing every source record in every analyst’s immediate view.

Surveillance also selects its collection method according to objective and resource constraints. CDC notes that passive surveillance is provider-initiated, active surveillance solicits reports and is usually restricted to specific periods or exposures, and sentinel reporting uses a stable, prearranged sample when comprehensive reporting is impractical ([CDC, Lesson 5, §4, lines 66–101](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section4.html)). I do **not** recommend importing those labels wholesale into an agent harness; they establish that epidemiology separates acquisition coverage, follow-up intensity, and record storage rather than treating “collect everything” as the only path to sensitivity.

## 2. Structural mapping to finite context

The mapping is:

| Epidemiology | Context control |
|---|---|
| Person/event under surveillance | Atomic context item with a stable source locator |
| True disease/case state | Item later shown necessary for a correct, efficient future decision |
| Case definition | Explicit relevance rule for the current task phase |
| Suspected / probable / confirmed | Candidate / active dependency / pinned obligation-or-fact |
| Laboratory or reference evidence | Direct owner requirement, accepted decision, observed tool result, live dependency, or hindsight outcome |
| Case line list | Compact context ledger outside the prompt body |
| Loose early definition | Broad capture into ledger during exploration |
| Tight analytic definition | Narrow admission into the model’s active prompt before action |
| Sensitivity | Fraction of later-needed items retained or recoverably indexed |
| Specificity | Fraction of later-unneeded items correctly kept out of active context |
| PPV | Fraction of active-context items that become decision-relevant before expiry/refresh |
| Prevalence | Base rate that an item in this task/source/age stratum will be needed before refresh |
| False negative | Needed item absent and not recovered in time |
| False positive | Unneeded item consuming prompt/attention budget |
| Changed case definition | Changed task phase, decision frontier, or error-cost ratio |

“True context relevance” in this table is explicitly retrospective and horizon-bounded. It is not an intrinsic property of a message. Define an item as later-needed only when a preregistered grader can link it to a later decision, error, retrieval, owner correction, or counterfactual outcome inside horizon `H`. Without a declared horizon and outcome rule, sensitivity and specificity are undefined.

### Why the mapping has real structure

1. **Both systems screen many candidates for scarce downstream attention.** Public-health follow-up has staff, testing, and time costs; active context has token and model-attention costs. CDC explicitly frames surveillance characteristics and costs as interrelated and conflicting ([CDC, Lesson 5, §7, lines 38–50](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section7.html)).
2. **Both have asymmetric error costs that change by phase.** Missing an early clue can block hypothesis discovery; retaining every clue during final action can dilute the decision state. The epidemiological sequence is directly evidenced by CDC’s loose-early/tight-later guidance ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 119–126](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).
3. **Both need stable operational criteria.** “Interesting” is no more reproducible than “looks ill.” Explicit time/source/evidence criteria make classifications auditable.
4. **Both face low-base-rate strata.** Most old conversational detail is plausibly not needed for the next action, whereas an unresolved owner constraint or currently imported API contract has a higher prior probability of near-term use. The direction of this statement is **likely (55–80%)**, based on the finite-horizon definition and ordinary task structure, but its magnitude is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to label future need by source/age/dependency stratum over a representative replay set and estimate empirical prevalence with confidence intervals**.

## 3. Recommended translation — UNVERIFIED pending the specified replay A/B

The overall recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because no experiment cited here tests this mechanism in an agent context. The settling experiment is specified in §5. Every component below carries exactly one required NATIVE-vs-BUILD value.

### Proposal A — task-phase “context case definition” — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

At task start and whenever the decision frontier changes, require a short machine-readable definition:

```json
{
  "horizon": "until the next externally visible action or verification result",
  "phase": "discover | decide | execute | verify",
  "positive_event": "item is cited by, constrains, or prevents error in a decision inside horizon",
  "high_cost_false_negative": [
    "owner requirement or prohibition",
    "irreversible-action target",
    "accepted decision and rationale",
    "unresolved blocker or failing invariant"
  ],
  "high_cost_false_positive": [
    "superseded result",
    "uncorroborated hypothesis during execute/verify",
    "large raw output with a stable source locator"
  ]
}
```

The values above are a starter schema, not universal truth. The phase controls the operating point:

- `discover`: admit candidates to the ledger broadly; retain only compact representations in active context;
- `decide`: promote competing hypotheses plus discriminating evidence; demote background narrative;
- `execute`: pin obligations, exact targets, accepted decisions, and safety constraints; require stronger evidence for everything else;
- `verify`: pin expected outcomes, observed results, and unresolved discrepancies; evict implementation chatter that cannot affect verdicts.

This definition must not contain the conclusion the agent hopes to prove. That is the direct translation of epidemiology’s warning not to include the hypothesized exposure in the case definition. For example, if deciding whether library X caused a regression, “mentions library X” may be a discovery signal but must not itself be confirmation-quality evidence.

It is **likely (55–80%)** that a phase declaration will prevent some stale discovery material from being carried into execution as if confirmed, because CDC’s analogous phase shift exists specifically to reduce false-positive contamination. Harness impact is **UNVERIFIED; the §5 experiment must score stale-hypothesis carry-through separately**.

### Proposal B — a three-tier context line list — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Maintain an append/update flat JSONL or equivalent small record set at compaction and major decision boundaries. Do not put the complete ledger in the prompt. Each record contains:

```text
id, source_locator, observed_at, last_used_at, task_phase,
tier={suspect|probable|confirmed}, evidence_codes[],
claim_or_constraint, supersedes[], contradicted_by[],
next_decision, omission_cost={low|medium|high},
token_cost, expiry_or_review_trigger, disposition_reason
```

Tier semantics:

- **suspect**: matches the broad screen but has no live dependency. Keep the locator and a one-line claim outside active context. Examples: an early hypothesis, an unused alternative, a potentially related file, or a large output with one plausible signal.
- **probable**: has a live link to the next decision. Keep a concise representation in active context. Evidence includes an unresolved tool failure, a referenced-but-unread source, a pending test, or a decision branch that has not closed.
- **confirmed**: is authoritative or directly observed and has high omission cost. Pin a minimal exact representation. Examples: explicit owner constraint, approved scope, accepted decision, destructive-action target, verified result, or unresolved contradiction between two authoritative observations.

Promotion requires a new evidence code; demotion requires a reason. Supersession must be explicit rather than inferred from age. Every non-active item keeps a stable locator, so eviction means “remove from prompt residence,” not “destroy source evidence.” Raw tool output stays at its existing source; the ledger stores only the locator, digest, classification, and reason.

The ledger would catch a currently easy-to-miss state: a weak clue can remain **suspect and recoverable** without occupying active context or being discarded. Conversely, a long, vivid output cannot remain active merely because it is recent; without live decision evidence it has low PPV and is represented by a pointer. It is **likely (55–80%)** that this reduces both irrevocable forgetting and prompt bloat relative to a binary keep/drop policy, because graded epidemiological classifications explicitly preserve uncertainty without treating all possible cases as confirmed. The agent outcome is **UNVERIFIED; the §5 experiment compares binary and tiered policies on later retrieval and active tokens**.

### Proposal C — stratified, two-pass admission and eviction — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

At a context boundary, perform two logically separate passes:

**Pass 1: high-sensitivity candidate capture.** Scan the just-completed interval for explicit requirements, prohibitions, accepted decisions, changed assumptions, unresolved errors, future commitments, exact identifiers/targets, contradictions, and evidence-bearing results. Append candidates to the ledger even if they will not enter active context.

**Pass 2: high-specificity active admission.** Admit only records whose next-decision link, omission cost, evidence tier, and freshness/review trigger justify prompt residence under the current token budget. Prefer the smallest faithful representation; retain a source locator for expansion.

Do not set one global threshold. Estimate and update separate empirical base rates for at least:

- explicit owner requirements and prohibitions;
- current accepted decisions and rationales;
- unresolved failures/blockers;
- exact tool observations;
- untested hypotheses;
- completed-work narrative;
- raw outputs with stable locators;
- old conversational detail.

Use cost-sensitive admission, not PPV alone. A rare destructive-action prohibition can merit pinning even if its stratum has few later uses, because one false negative is expensive. A large raw output can be excluded despite plausible relevance when it is cheaply recoverable. A conceptual decision rule is:

`admit if P(needed before refresh | stratum, evidence, phase) × C_FN > (1 − P) × C_FP + token_cost + retrieval_adjustment`

This is a decision-theoretic translation, not an epidemiological formula. The probability and costs are initially rough ordinal estimates and must be replaced with measured calibration where possible. It is **roughly even (45–55%)** that a hand-set version beats a strong model-only context summary, because misestimated costs and probabilities may erase the benefit. It is **UNVERIFIED; a three-arm §5 experiment—model-only, fixed rules, empirically calibrated rules—settles this**.

The base-rate step catches the “rare-positive flood”: a broad keyword or semantic match over a huge pool of old material can produce mostly false inclusions even when it appears accurate. It also catches the inverse: a small, high-risk stratum such as explicit owner prohibitions should not share the eviction threshold of routine narrative. The direction is **almost certain (90–99%) as a mathematical consequence of the PPV formula when strata have different prevalence**, supported by the formula and arithmetic in §1.3; whether the proposed strata actually differ enough in this owner’s workloads to matter is **UNVERIFIED; estimate their empirical prevalence in the replay experiment**.

### Proposal D — hindsight surveillance evaluation with negative sampling — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

At the end of horizon `H`, assign outcomes without asking the selector to grade itself:

- `needed`: later cited, retrieved, implicated in an error/correction, or judged necessary by a blinded task-outcome grader;
- `not-needed-within-H`: no such link inside the declared horizon;
- `indeterminate`: outcome cannot be adjudicated.

Compute by task and stratum:

- context sensitivity = needed-and-retained-or-timely-recovered / all adjudicated needed;
- active specificity = unneeded-and-excluded / all adjudicated unneeded;
- active PPV = needed active items / all adjudicated active items;
- recovery latency and tokens spent restoring an evicted item;
- active-context tokens by tier;
- task success and owner correction rate.

Audit a random sample of apparent true negatives (evicted and never retrieved), not only retrieved/corrected items. Otherwise the system observes labels disproportionately where its policy or the user already suspected a problem. Because counterfactual necessity is not directly observable, replay sampled evictions into otherwise matched runs and compare outcomes. This is **likely (55–80%)** to reveal some silent false negatives that correction-only evaluation misses, based on the direct fact that unobserved evictions receive no ordinary downstream label; the magnitude is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the sampled counterfactual replay just described**.

Periodically re-estimate thresholds. CDC recommends evaluating surveillance against its purpose, usefulness, costs, and attributes rather than assuming the original design remains optimal ([CDC, Lesson 5, §7, lines 15–50](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section7.html)). Here the equivalent is a small local calibration report, not a new observability platform.

## 4. What this catches that simpler context heuristics miss

### 4.1 The low-base-rate false-positive swamp

A selector can look “90% accurate” on sensitivity and specificity yet have a poor PPV when screening a large, low-relevance pool. Recency, lexical overlap, and semantic similarity can each nominate many plausible items. The protocol asks the operational question: among admitted items from this stratum, how many actually influence a decision before refresh? It then lowers active admission or stores only locators when PPV is poor.

Expected benefit is **likely (55–80%)**, because the base-rate effect is mathematical but the proposed features’ achieved sensitivity/specificity are unknown. This is **UNVERIFIED; label active items by stratum in the §5 replay and test whether PPV and task outcomes improve at equal token budget**.

### 4.2 Weak clues lost by binary keep/drop compaction

An early clue may be too uncertain to pin and too important to erase. The suspect tier records it cheaply until evidence promotes, expires, contradicts, or supersedes it. Epidemiological case categories exist precisely because evidence is incomplete and confirmation can be delayed or costly ([CDC, Lesson 6, §2, lines 95–118](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).

Expected benefit is **likely (55–80%)** for tasks with extended discovery phases and **unlikely (20–45%)** for short, single-step tasks, based on how often delayed evidence can arise in each task shape. Both estimates are **UNVERIFIED; stratify the §5 replay by task length and number of decision-boundary changes**.

### 4.3 Old but high-consequence constraints

Age is weak evidence against relevance when the item is an unresolved owner prohibition, accepted architectural decision, or exact destructive target. The protocol stratifies by omission cost and requires explicit supersession. This can keep a ten-hour-old prohibition while evicting a ten-second-old verbose output.

Expected reduction in constraint-loss errors is **likely (55–80%)**, based on the asymmetric-cost decision rule; actual frequency is **UNVERIFIED; seed and naturally sample long-horizon tasks with old constraints, then measure violation/correction rates**.

### 4.4 Stale hypotheses masquerading as facts

The tier and evidence code distinguish “suspected explanation” from “observed result.” Phase changes can tighten the definition before execution, and contradictions block confirmation. This catches summaries that compress away epistemic status.

Expected benefit is **likely (55–80%)** on investigative tasks and **unlikely (20–45%)** on deterministic transformation tasks, based on the presence or absence of hypothesis generation. This is **UNVERIFIED; the §5 suite must include both strata and score unsupported-cause carry-through**.

### 4.5 Silent false negatives hidden by selective feedback

If evaluation counts only user corrections and explicit re-retrievals, it cannot see an evicted fact whose absence caused a plausible but inferior route. Sampling evictions for counterfactual replay creates a second observation channel. It will **likely (55–80%)** detect at least some errors missed by ordinary correction logs, because correction logs require the user or agent to recognize the omission; this is **UNVERIFIED; sampled counterfactual replay is the settling experiment**.

## 5. Experiment that can falsify the recommendation

### 5.1 Preregistered hypotheses

The primary hypothesis is **UNVERIFIED**: at equal maximum context tokens, the staged protocol improves task success without increasing owner corrections. The settling experiment is the A/B/C replay below.

Secondary hypotheses, all **UNVERIFIED**, are:

1. The protocol increases recall of later-needed items while reducing active-context tokens.
2. Tiering reduces both unrecoverable omissions and full-text prompt residence.
3. Base-rate stratification improves active PPV over a global threshold.
4. Random negative audits expose false negatives not visible in normal retrieval/correction logs.

### 5.2 Design

Use a preregistered set of long-horizon agent sessions, stratified by task family, length, tool intensity, number of explicit constraints, and number of phase changes. Freeze model, temperature, tools, task inputs, and maximum context budget. Compare:

- **A — baseline:** existing/model-native context policy;
- **B — fixed protocol:** Proposals A–D with hand-set ordinal thresholds;
- **C — calibrated protocol:** same mechanism with thresholds fitted only on a disjoint training fold.

Replay each condition with multiple seeds where nondeterminism exists. Blind outcome graders to condition. Declare horizon `H` before labeling. Adjudicate later-needed items using two mechanically independent signals before calling an omission harmful: (1) a trace link such as later retrieval, citation, correction, failing check, or changed action; and (2) a blinded counterfactual replay or independent grader judgment tied to the final outcome. A detector control must include known-present needed and unneeded items so both label paths are shown to work.

The two-signal rule is intentionally stricter than ordinary offline scoring. A later mention alone may reflect repetition rather than necessity; a grader judgment alone may be subjective.

### 5.3 Primary and guardrail metrics

Primary:

- externally checked task success;
- owner correction/constraint-violation rate;
- active tokens integrated over turns.

Mechanism:

- sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV by stratum and task phase;
- recovery latency for evicted items;
- tier promotion/demotion precision;
- fraction of active tokens with no demonstrated use inside `H`;
- silent false-negative yield from random eviction audits.

Guardrails:

- extra latency per boundary;
- ledger bytes and update cost;
- number of erroneous pins caused by stale/contradicted records;
- summary-induced factual distortion;
- outcome degradation on short tasks.

Set minimum worthwhile effects and stopping rules before inspecting results. If fixed or calibrated rules do not improve task success/corrections at equal tokens, retire them even if their intermediate classification metrics look attractive. A classifier can optimize the surrogate and harm the agent.

### 5.4 Falsification outcomes

Reject the borrow as a harness recommendation if any of these replicate:

- no task-success or correction benefit at equal context budget;
- ledger/tiering overhead consumes the saved tokens or latency;
- model attention is harmed by compact classified records more than by ordinary summaries;
- relevance prevalence is too unstable across tasks for useful stratification;
- hindsight labels have low inter-rater agreement and counterfactual replays do not stabilize them;
- suspected/probable/confirmed tiers create false confidence rather than preserving uncertainty.

It is **roughly even (45–55%)** that a hand-set implementation will clear all falsification gates, because the mechanism has a sound structural correspondence but no agent-domain validation and relevance labels are endogenous. This is **UNVERIFIED; only the preregistered experiment above settles it**. A calibrated implementation has a **likely (55–80%)** chance of improving at least classification/token metrics, based on the ability to learn stratum-specific empirical base rates, but improved task outcome remains **UNVERIFIED; the same held-out experiment settles it**.

## 6. Adversarial analogy audit

### 6.1 Disanalogy: disease state is more independent than relevance

A person may have a pathogen whether or not surveillance includes them. Context relevance is counterfactual and policy-dependent: retaining an item may cause the model to use it, while omitting it may change the plan so the item never appears necessary. There is no clean “laboratory test” for future relevance at selection time. This is the largest break in the analogy.

Consequence: do not call the tiers truth states. They are evidence and disposition states. Report retrospective, horizon-specific outcomes and retain an `indeterminate` label. The transfer is **almost certainly not (1–5%) valid as a literal diagnostic classifier with a stable gold standard**, because the target changes with policy and future action; the evidence is the definition of relevance above and the causal dependence of future traces on context selection.

### 6.2 Disanalogy: context items interact non-additively

Diagnostic cases are usually counted as units. Context items can be complements, substitutes, or contradictions. Two individually low-value facts may jointly unlock a decision; ten redundant facts may add no value beyond one. Token position and formatting also affect model attention.

Consequence: item-level sensitivity/PPV are diagnostics, not sufficient objectives. Task success, constraint adherence, and token-integrated cost remain primary. The item-independent version of the analogy is **very unlikely (5–20%)** to be adequate, because bundles and redundancy violate additivity; this assessment is based on the logical structure of multi-premise decisions, not an empirical harness measurement.

### 6.3 Disanalogy: storage and attention are different resources

An epidemiological line list can preserve a record without requiring every investigator to hold it in working memory. An agent can likewise keep a locator outside its immediate prompt, but retrieval has latency and may fail. “Evicted but indexed” is not equivalent to “retained.”

Consequence: measure timely recovery separately from residence, and never count an unreachable pointer as a true positive. The line-list translation is **likely (55–80%)** to be useful only when stable-locator retrieval is reliable; this premise is **UNVERIFIED; inject known-present locator probes and measure successful recovery and latency in the §5 experiment**.

### 6.4 Disanalogy: epidemiological error costs are social; context error costs are task-local

Public-health case definitions may be selected around transmission control, patient burden, resource use, and population harms. Context selection trades token cost, latency, distraction, and task error. The cost scales cannot be copied.

Consequence: import the discipline of declaring error costs, not epidemiology’s numeric thresholds. A universal sensitivity target is **almost certainly not (1–5%)** justified, because task-specific irreversible actions and cheap reversible lookups have plainly different consequences.

### 6.5 Disanalogy: prevalence and spectrum drift faster across agent tasks

Diagnostic populations can also shift—Lin et al. empirically found associations between prevalence and measured sensitivity/specificity across 6,909 diagnostic studies ([2023, PMCID PMC10356012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10356012/)). Agent tasks may switch domains and phases within minutes, so a global base rate is even less defensible.

Consequence: estimate by coarse task/source/phase strata, expose uncertainty, and fall back to cost rules when samples are sparse. Stable global calibration is **unlikely (20–45%)**, based on heterogeneous task definitions; exact stability is **UNVERIFIED; time-split calibration error by stratum in §5 settles it**.

### 6.6 Disanalogy: surveillance seeks population inference; context seeks one successful trajectory

Surveillance may tolerate incomplete but stable sentinel samples to estimate trends. A single agent trajectory can fail because one unique constraint was absent; representativeness does not rescue it.

Consequence: do not use sentinel sampling as the main eviction policy. Random sampling belongs only in evaluation of apparent negatives, while high-consequence unique constraints are pinned. A literal sentinel-context scheme is **very unlikely (5–20%)** to meet the target, because the goal is not estimating the distribution of old context but selecting the exact information needed for the next action.

### 6.7 Verdict after the adversarial audit

The analogy survives as an **operating discipline**—explicit target, tiered uncertainty, phase-dependent threshold, base-rate reasoning, and measured error matrix. It fails as an ontology of memory or as proof that a context item has an objective disease-like state.

The constrained transfer is **likely (55–80%) to be useful enough to test**, supported by the shared screening/resource/error-cost structure and CDC’s explicit phase-dependent mechanism. It is **UNVERIFIED as an improvement to agent outcomes; the §5 preregistered replay is the settling experiment**. This confidence would fall below “roughly even” if stable locators are unreliable, future-need labels have low agreement, or task-level outcomes do not track the proposed mechanism metrics.

## 7. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

The smallest testable architecture is:

1. a short configuration block declaring horizon, phase, and high-cost errors;
2. one boundary hook that emits/updates flat ledger records;
3. deterministic admission and eviction rules using tier, evidence, omission cost, token cost, and expiry;
4. existing source retrieval by stable locator;
5. an offline evaluator that computes the confusion matrix and samples evictions for replay.

The combined live-path implementation is targeted at fewer than roughly 200 lines; the offline experiment may be separate test code. This size claim is **likely (55–80%)**, based on the limited schema and deterministic operations, but is **UNVERIFIED; the settling test is an implementation spike that counts non-test logical lines while exercising all record transitions**.

Explicitly excluded:

- no second orchestrator;
- no vector database or knowledge graph;
- no durable-execution runtime;
- no trained critic;
- no observability SaaS;
- no generic guardrail framework;
- no model fine-tuning;
- no full transcript duplication in a new store.

If the flat ledger cannot beat ordinary model summaries, the result is to stop, not to escalate into a new memory service. That preserves the anti-overfit gate.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Literal epidemic models (SIR/SEIR) — rejected

“Relevant context spreading between agents” is rhetorically attractive but structurally weak. Relevance does not reproduce through susceptible hosts with stable transmission/recovery parameters. An SIR-style context manager is **almost certainly not (1–5%)** justified, based on the absence of corresponding conserved populations or transmission dynamics.

### Keep everything, summarize repeatedly — rejected as the borrowed mechanism

This optimizes initial sensitivity but provides no measured specificity or PPV and risks compounding summary distortion. It may still be a baseline. Its inferiority is **UNVERIFIED; the §5 experiment must include the native/model-summary baseline rather than assume failure**.

### One learned relevance score — rejected for the first build

A learned score can hide phase, base rate, and asymmetric costs in an opaque scalar, and would require representative labels that do not yet exist. It may eventually outperform deterministic rules, but that is **UNVERIFIED; after collecting held-out labels, compare it as a later arm**. Starting there risks fitting the biased feedback produced by the existing policy.

### Capture–recapture estimation of missing context — rejected

Capture–recapture can estimate unseen populations from overlapping samples under demanding independence/homogeneity assumptions. Multiple context selectors are likely correlated and the “population” of relevant items changes with the trajectory. Transfer is **very unlikely (5–20%)** without strong corrections, based on those violated assumptions, and it adds estimation machinery without directly deciding what enters the prompt.

### Sentinel context kept permanently — rejected

A representative sample can reveal trends but cannot guarantee presence of a unique high-cost constraint. Use random samples to audit evictions, not to populate active context. A permanent sentinel sample improving single-trajectory success is **unlikely (20–45%)**; this is **UNVERIFIED, but it is not recommended because the structural objective mismatch already fails the anti-overfit gate**.

## 9. Gaps and evidence boundaries

- No cited study evaluates epidemiological case-definition staging for LLM context management. Every agent-impact recommendation is therefore marked **UNVERIFIED** with the §5 experiment that would settle it.
- This ideation unit intentionally did not inspect the local harness, repository, session corpus, defects, or prior audit. The unit brief explicitly assigned local grounding to other units. Therefore this artifact makes no claim that any proposed bus, hook event, ledger path, or native context feature currently exists.
- Stable source-locator retrieval is a required premise and is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with known-present positive controls plus independently observed successful fetches during the §5 experiment**.
- The empirical prevalence of later-needed context by source, age, phase, or task family is unknown and **UNVERIFIED; settle it by blind retrospective labeling and sampled counterfactual replay on a representative task set**.
- “Needed” remains counterfactual even after hindsight. Two independent signals and an indeterminate class reduce, but do not eliminate, this identification problem.
- The CDC self-study pages are archived and were last reviewed in 2012, but they directly state the established field mechanism being borrowed. The current CDC Field Epidemiology Manual (updated August 8, 2024) independently supports purpose-first data collection, standard case definitions, necessary-and-sufficient data, and resource-aware protocol design ([CDC, “Collecting Data,” lines 54–99](https://www.cdc.gov/field-epi-manual/php/chapters/collecting-data.html)). This is not a claim that all contemporary outbreak practice is uniform.
