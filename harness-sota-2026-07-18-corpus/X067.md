# X067 — Epidemiology → synthesis: treat findings as a surveillance population, not a reading queue

**Unit:** X067  
**Lens:** cross-domain borrow from epidemiology  
**Target:** collapse thousands of findings into a decision without requiring an agent to read the full finding corpus  
**Scope boundary:** external ideation only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness or repository. The mechanism below is grounded in public-health sources, then translated into a proposed harness mechanism.  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive result

The epidemiological mechanism that transfers is **case-based surveillance built around a versioned case definition, a line list, staged case status, and measured surveillance performance**. Public health does not ask a central decision-maker to reread every clinical note. It makes every candidate event addressable as one row, applies an explicit definition, preserves uncertain cases rather than silently discarding them, aggregates the rows into decision views, and measures both detection and false-alert behavior.

The transfer is **very likely (80-90%) to be structurally valid**, because both domains have (1) a large population of heterogeneous reports, (2) expensive confirmation, (3) asymmetric false-negative and false-positive costs, (4) duplicated/correlated reports, and (5) a decision-maker who needs trends and actionable clusters rather than every source document. The evidence for the epidemiology side is the CDC's explicit separation of case-level sensitivity, outbreak-detection sensitivity, and predictive value positive, plus WHO's case-definition → line-list → refine-definition workflow ([CDC, 2001](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm); [WHO outbreak investigation stages](https://www.who.int/emergencies/outbreak-toolkit/standardized-data-collection-tools/investigating-outbreak-of-unknown-disease)). The remaining uncertainty is whether a compact implementation retains enough semantics for this owner's decisions; that premise is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with a blinded historical replay in which the owner makes decisions from the proposed decision packet and from the full finding set, then compare missed/changed decisions, time, and confidence**.

The transfer is deliberately narrow. Epidemiology supplies a disciplined registry and calibration model; it does **not** justify treating agent votes as independent tests, calling consensus “confirmed,” calculating a universal truth probability, or automating final architectural decisions. Those extensions do not survive the analogy.

## 1. What epidemiology has already solved

### 1.1 The specific mechanism: case-based surveillance

The relevant epidemiological object is not merely a “case definition.” It is a pipeline:

1. Define what counts as a reportable candidate using observable criteria.
2. Classify candidates into staged statuses such as suspected, probable, confirmed, and discarded/ruled out.
3. Record candidates in a standardized case-level **line list**.
4. Aggregate the line list by dimensions relevant to action (classically person, place, and time), while retaining links to individual cases.
5. Investigate and confirm selected cases.
6. Evaluate the surveillance system itself for sensitivity, predictive value positive, representativeness, timeliness, stability, simplicity, and related attributes.
7. Revise the definition as knowledge changes, while logging the revision so old and new counts remain interpretable.

WHO's current outbreak-investigation workflow says an outbreak definition should specify person, time, place, clinical and epidemiological criteria, and laboratory diagnosis; where relevant, it should distinguish suspected, probable, and confirmed cases. The same workflow instructs investigators to find cases systematically, collect standardized case-investigation data, maintain a line list, and refine the definition as evidence changes. Crucially for synthesis integrity, WHO says changes to the definition should be evidence-based, communicated, and logged ([WHO outbreak investigation stages](https://www.who.int/emergencies/outbreak-toolkit/standardized-data-collection-tools/investigating-outbreak-of-unknown-disease)).

WHO's yellow-fever definitions show how the stages work in their own field rather than as abstract labels. A suspected case is based on a broad clinical pattern; a probable case adds immunological evidence, histopathology, or an epidemiological link; a confirmed case requires specified stronger evidence and controls for plausible cross-reactivity or recent vaccination ([WHO yellow-fever surveillance case definitions](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/case-definitions-for-public-health-surveillance)). Thus, “probable” and “confirmed” are evidence rules, not confidence adjectives or vote totals.

The CDC defines surveillance sensitivity at two levels: the fraction of actual cases detected, and the ability to detect outbreaks or changes over time. It defines predictive value positive (PVP) as the fraction of reported cases that actually have the event. The CDC also warns that low PVP wastes investigative resources and can produce a “pseudo-outbreak”; PVP depends on the case definition's sensitivity and specificity **and on prevalence**. Finally, it says sensitivity can be assessed only with validation or data external to the surveillance system and notes that increasing sensitivity can reduce PVP and increase complexity ([CDC, Updated Guidelines for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems, sections D.2.e–D.2.f and recommendations, 2001](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

Those details are the solution to the synthesis problem:

- **Do not equate intake with truth.** A report is a suspected case until it meets stronger evidence criteria.
- **Do not equate repetition with independence.** Epidemiological linkage is data to preserve; it does not magically turn linked cases into independent confirmation.
- **Do not optimize only recall.** Track the cost of false alerts through PVP.
- **Do not optimize only precision.** Audit what the intake definition fails to catch through sensitivity estimation.
- **Do not destroy the denominator.** Retain all candidates and statuses so rates, omissions, and definition drift remain measurable.
- **Do not make a summary the database.** The line list is the canonical evidence index; situation reports are disposable projections of it.

### 1.2 Why base rates matter when thousands of findings arrive

Sensitivity and specificity describe a classifier conditional on truth; PVP describes what the decision-maker experiences after a positive report. PVP changes with the underlying prevalence of real, decision-relevant findings. The CDC explicitly makes this dependence part of surveillance-system evaluation ([CDC, 2001](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

A mechanical example shows why “many detections” is not equivalent to “many defects.” Suppose 10,000 candidate findings contain 100 genuinely decision-relevant findings (1% prevalence). A screen with 90% sensitivity and 95% specificity produces:

- 90 true positives;
- 10 false negatives;
- 495 false positives; and
- a PVP of 90 / (90 + 495) = **15.4%**.

The screen sounds good in isolation, yet roughly five and a half of every six alerts sent for review are false positives. If multiple audit agents share prompts, evidence, or model failure modes, counting their repeated detections as independent positives makes the apparent evidence stronger without improving specificity. This is an exact consequence of the stated hypothetical rates, not a claim about this harness's actual prevalence or classifier performance.

### 1.3 Why the mechanism compresses without erasing

The compression unit is a **case row and its status**, not a prose summary. That distinction is essential. A line list supports compact aggregation while preserving:

- the original report and evidence locator;
- the definition version under which it was classified;
- the evidence used for escalation;
- its relationship to duplicate or linked reports;
- the reason it was ruled out or deferred; and
- the decision/action attached to it.

The decision-maker can therefore read a one-page situation view and drill into the handful of case clusters that drive a decision. The full source corpus remains available for audit but is not the interface for routine synthesis. This is analogous to surveillance, where aggregate counts do not replace case investigation records.

## 2. Concrete translation to an agent harness

## Recommendation — UNVERIFIED: build a Finding Surveillance Register and decision packet

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build one small, deterministic synthesis step that accepts normalized finding records, maintains an append-only JSONL/SQLite line list, applies a versioned case definition, clusters linked findings, and emits a short Markdown/JSON decision packet. It should be a skill or hook on the existing bus, not a second orchestration runtime.

The recommendation is **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is a preregistered replay over several completed audits: compare (a) decisions made from the generated packet, (b) decisions made with unrestricted access to all findings, and (c) known eventual outcomes; measure decision agreement, harmful omissions, false escalations, review time, and drill-down count**. It is **likely (55-80%) to reduce decision reading load without a material loss of decision quality**, based on the structural fit above and the fact that the canonical records remain drillable; the wide band reflects the lack of a harness-specific replay.

This recommendation passes the anti-overfit gate: it rides the existing skill/hook/MCP bus, consists primarily of a schema plus deterministic transforms, and introduces none of the previously rejected categories (no second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework).

### 2.1 One row per candidate finding

Each input becomes one immutable finding record. A minimal schema is:

```yaml
finding_id: stable content-derived or assigned identifier
source_id: audit unit / run / detector
source_family: shared prompt/model/tool/evidence lineage
observed_at: timestamp or run identifier
scope: component or decision domain
phenotype: externally observable failure or opportunity
claim: one atomic proposition
evidence_locators: [URL/path:line/command-output-id]
signal_methods: [runtime observation, static read, corpus count, control test, ...]
definition_version: semver/hash
case_status: suspected | probable | confirmed | ruled_out | unresolved
status_reason: machine-readable rule plus short prose
severity: impact class, explicitly separate from confidence
novelty: singleton | linked | duplicate
linkage_cluster: shared root-cause/evidence/provenance cluster
independence_groups: [mechanically independent signal groups]
recommended_action: verify | act | monitor | archive
action_owner: optional
decision_id: optional
```

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Define the record as a versioned JSON Schema and require producers to emit it. Preserve arbitrary prose as an attachment, not as the primary merge key.

It is **very likely (80-90%) that explicit `source_family`, `linkage_cluster`, and `independence_groups` fields will prevent at least some false corroboration**, because they make dependence visible before aggregation; the evidence is the direct logic of correlated reports plus WHO's explicit use of epidemiological linkage as a classification attribute rather than laboratory confirmation. The harness-specific frequency and impact are **UNVERIFIED — settle them by relabeling a stratified sample of past finding clusters and comparing naive source counts with independence-group counts**.

### 2.2 Use domain-specific case definitions, not one universal rubric

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Store a small versioned definition for each finding family (reliability defect, security risk, documentation mismatch, capability opportunity, and so on). A starting template is:

- **Suspected:** one atomic, falsifiable claim; a resolvable evidence locator or named settling experiment; scope and possible decision impact identified. This stage is deliberately sensitive.
- **Probable:** the suspected definition plus either two mechanically independent supporting signals, or one direct operational observation whose detector has a known-positive control, with plausible alternatives considered.
- **Confirmed:** a direct reproduction or authoritative reference test satisfies a finding-family-specific confirmation rule. A source count, agent majority, or repeated paraphrase can never satisfy this tier by itself.
- **Ruled out:** the reference test contradicts the claim, or evidence proves the observation is an expected state. Record the reason; do not delete the row.
- **Unresolved:** evidence is contradictory, unavailable, or outside authority. Record the exact experiment or authority needed.

These labels intentionally describe **evidentiary state**, not severity. A suspected catastrophic risk can outrank a confirmed cosmetic defect for immediate verification. They also do not claim medical-grade probabilities.

The initial thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle them by double-adjudicating a stratified sample under candidate definitions and choosing thresholds against explicit false-negative/false-escalation costs**. It is **likely (55-80%) that per-family definitions will calibrate better than a universal rubric**, because confirmation evidence differs materially by claim type (a runtime failure, a policy mismatch, and a speculative opportunity do not share a reference test); the evidence is WHO's disease-specific criteria and CDC's statement that sensitivity/PVP reflect the chosen case definition.

### 2.3 Link and deduplicate without throwing reports away

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Normalize obvious exact duplicates deterministically, then propose clusters using conservative keys such as `(scope, phenotype, normalized evidence locator, proposed root cause)`. Preserve every source record beneath the cluster.

The cluster must separately show:

- raw report count;
- unique evidence-locator count;
- unique source-family count;
- mechanically independent signal count;
- status of the strongest case; and
- contradictions/ruled-out reports.

Do not let an LLM silently merge records. It may propose a link, but a deterministic rule or explicit adjudication must commit it. A cluster with 40 reports derived from one shared bad detector is one signal family, not 40 confirmations.

It is **very likely (80-90%) this representation will expose correlated-consensus inflation when it exists**, because raw and independent counts are displayed side by side; the evidence is mechanical rather than empirical. Whether that changes any owner decision is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by recomputing historical cluster rankings with raw-count and independence-count scoring and adjudicating the disagreements**.

### 2.4 Emit a decision packet, not a mega-summary

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Generate a bounded packet with five sections:

1. **Decisions now:** intervention clusters that cross an explicit action rule, with impact, strongest case status, independent signals, contrary evidence, cost/reversibility, and drill-down IDs.
2. **Verify next:** high-impact suspected/probable cases, ordered by expected value of information; each has one settling experiment.
3. **Watch:** trends or recurring phenotypes that have not crossed an action threshold.
4. **Novel singletons:** high-severity or high-novelty cases protected from frequency-based suppression.
5. **Surveillance health:** intake volume, status funnel, PVP estimate, sensitivity/escape estimate, unresolved age, definition version changes, and strata with no coverage.

The packet should enforce a hard item budget but never silently truncate: overflow is represented by counts and stable query/drill-down IDs. A decision row should point to the case cluster and underlying records so a reviewer can inspect evidence on demand.

It is **likely (55-80%) that this packet will prevent frequency from dominating severity and novelty**, because it reserves distinct lanes for decisions, verification, and novel singletons. Its actual ranking utility is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the historical replay and measure whether known consequential singletons survive the packet budget**.

### 2.5 Measure the synthesizer as a surveillance system

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a periodic calibration job, not a trained evaluator:

- Draw a stratified random sample from promoted, unresolved, ruled-out, and never-promoted candidates.
- Have an adjudicator apply a stronger reference procedure without seeing the synthesizer's status where practical.
- Estimate PVP among promoted cases.
- Estimate an “escape rate” among non-promoted cases and use the sampling weights to estimate sensitivity over the sampled population.
- Report these by finding family and source family, not only as a misleading global mean.
- Track turnaround time and unresolved age.
- Place known-positive and known-negative controls in detector tests.
- Re-run the same frozen calibration set whenever a case definition changes, while also sampling new material to detect overfitting.

Sensitivity cannot be inferred by reviewing positives alone. CDC specifically says its measurement needs validation and/or data external to the surveillance system ([CDC, 2001](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm)). Therefore, the design's negative/never-promoted sample is not optional.

It is **very likely (80-90%) that negative sampling will reveal false-negative classes that positive-only review cannot observe**, because sensitivity's denominator includes missed positives; the evidence is the metric's definition and CDC's external-validation requirement. The achievable precision of the estimate is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with a pilot power/precision analysis using observed adjudicated prevalence and disagreement rates**.

### 2.6 Version definitions and make trend breaks explicit

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Every row records its definition version. Definition changes require a changelog containing rationale, expected direction of sensitivity/PVP change, and the range of rows reclassified. Trend charts mark the boundary; where feasible, reclassify a frozen overlap sample under both versions.

WHO explicitly requires evidence-based, communicated, logged case-definition changes so analytical findings remain interpretable ([WHO outbreak investigation stages](https://www.who.int/emergencies/outbreak-toolkit/standardized-data-collection-tools/investigating-outbreak-of-unknown-disease)). CDC warns that changes in diagnostic methods or awareness can produce surveillance artifacts and that searching for these artifacts is an early outbreak-investigation step ([CDC, 2001](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

It is **very likely (80-90%) that versioning plus overlap reclassification will distinguish at least some real finding-rate changes from rubric changes**, because the same records can be compared under both definitions. The amount of historical backfill needed is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by changing one pilot definition and measuring reclassification rates on a frozen sample**.

## 3. What this would catch that a flat synthesis can miss

The following are predicted detection classes, not claims that this harness currently exhibits each one.

### 3.1 Pseudo-outbreaks of findings

Many reports can trace to one faulty detector, one shared prompt, or one copied interpretation. Raw frequency presents them as consensus; the register presents the shared lineage and independent-signal count. This class is **very likely (80-90%) to be caught when provenance is populated correctly**, because linkage is an explicit aggregation dimension. Correct provenance population is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by tracing a random cluster back to prompts, tools, and evidence sources and scoring lineage completeness**.

### 3.2 Severe singletons buried by majority voting

A rare, high-impact finding may never become frequent. The explicit novel-singleton lane and severity/confidence separation keep it visible for verification. This class is **likely (55-80%) to be caught more often than under count-ranked synthesis**, because the packet reserves capacity independent of frequency; the comparative baseline is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with seeded singleton findings of varied severity in a blinded replay**.

### 3.3 Broken detectors masquerading as absence

A detector that returns zero can look like “no cases.” Known-positive controls, source-level sensitivity tracking, and coverage strata make detector failure distinguishable from a clean population. This class is **very likely (80-90%) to be caught when a representative known-positive control exists**, because failure to find the control directly invalidates the negative result. Control representativeness is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by mutation testing each detector with several known-present variants, not one fixture**.

### 3.4 High-recall intake consuming all verification capacity

A broad definition can flood reviewers with false positives. Per-source/per-family PVP exposes which screens consume investigation time without producing confirmed cases. This class is **very likely (80-90%) to become visible after adjudicating a representative positive sample**, directly from the PVP numerator and denominator. Whether retuning saves net time is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by A/B replaying old and proposed thresholds and counting adjudication minutes plus escaped consequential cases**.

### 3.5 Silent false negatives in the synthesis funnel

Reviewing only the final shortlist cannot reveal what was filtered out. Stratified sampling of ruled-out and never-promoted records can discover missed decision-relevant classes. This class is **very likely (80-90%) to become measurable**, based on the definition of sensitivity, but the discovery rate is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the negative-sample pilot**.

### 3.6 Apparent trends caused by rubric drift

If a definition becomes broader, finding counts rise even when the underlying system does not change. Definition versions and overlap samples expose the discontinuity. This class is **very likely (80-90%) to be caught for documented definition changes**, because the reclassification effect is measured directly. Undocumented upstream changes remain a gap; detecting those requires source-distribution monitoring and controls.

### 3.7 Confidence laundering during compression

A prose synthesis can turn “three agents speculated” into “three audits confirmed.” The status transition log shows which criterion moved a case from suspected to probable or confirmed, and prohibits source count alone from confirming it. This class is **very likely (80-90%) to be caught when every promoted claim has a machine-checkable transition record**, because unsupported transitions are schema violations. Semantic evidence quality still needs adjudication and is not solved by the schema.

### 3.8 Coverage deserts hidden by a polished report

The decision packet reports strata with no eligible detector/source and the status funnel by domain. It does not infer “no problems” from “no reports.” This class is **likely (55-80%) to reveal meaningful coverage gaps**, because explicit denominators make unobserved strata visible; whether a named stratum is truly relevant is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by owner review of the scope taxonomy and by seeding known cases in each stratum**.

## 4. Adversarial assessment: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 There is no stable pathogen or universal laboratory test

Diseases often have biological reference tests, even if imperfect. Architectural findings range from reproducible defects to value judgments about maintainability or future capability. “Confirmed” therefore cannot mean one universal truth standard. It must mean “satisfies the predeclared reference procedure for this finding family.” Some opportunity claims may never progress beyond probable until an experiment is run.

This disanalogy is substantial but not fatal to the registry mechanism. It blocks universal probability scores and universal thresholds. It is **very likely (80-90%) that a universal confirmation rule would miscalibrate heterogeneous finding types**, based on the visibly different reference procedures required for runtime defects versus design opportunities; no harness-specific rate is asserted.

### 4.2 Audit reports are not independent people

Epidemiological cases can be linked through transmission; reports about a harness can be far more tightly correlated because agents share the same model, prompt, files, prior claims, or detector. “Ten reports” may be one copied observation. Conventional count aggregation is therefore even less trustworthy here.

The transfer works only if dependence is first-class. `source_family` and `independence_groups` are not optional metadata. Independence must be justified mechanically (for example, runtime reproduction versus static configuration inspection), not inferred merely from different agent IDs.

### 4.3 The observed population is selected, not natural

Audit findings come from prompts designed to seek problems, so their “prevalence” is conditional on audit selection and scope. A global PVP cannot be transported across source families or task types. Calibration must be stratified; prevalence and performance estimates apply only to the defined intake population and time window.

This means the registry may compare detector performance within stable strata, but **must not** state “the harness has X% true defects” from the finding stream. That conclusion would require a sampling frame over the harness itself, not merely over reports.

### 4.4 Findings can interact and decisions are causal

A line list encourages atomic cases, while architecture decisions often depend on interactions: three individually minor issues may share a root cause, and one intervention may create new risks. Epidemiological surveillance detects patterns but does not by itself establish which intervention is best.

The decision packet must therefore aggregate by proposed intervention/root cause and show conflicts, dependencies, reversibility, and contrary evidence. It remains a decision aid. It is **unlikely (20-45%) that case classification alone would select the best intervention**, because classification establishes evidence state, not causal effect or implementation trade-offs; the evidence is the logical distinction between “is this finding supported?” and “which change has best expected consequences?”

### 4.5 Adversarial or malformed findings differ from clinical reporting

Agent outputs can contain prompt injection, fabricated locators, schema gaming, or confident nonsense. A valid row is not a valid claim. The proposal therefore uses syntax/schema only for intake and reserves confirmation for resolvable evidence and reference procedures. Security isolation and content trust are outside this unit's epidemiological borrow.

### 4.6 Compression can erase weak signals and explanatory context

Case definitions necessarily discard detail. Novel cross-cutting ideas may not fit the taxonomy, and strict schemas can make them invisible. The design mitigates this with raw attachments, unresolved status, novelty lanes, overflow counts, and negative sampling, but cannot eliminate the risk.

It is **roughly even (45-55%) whether the first schema will omit at least one decision-relevant semantic field**, because initial taxonomies are rarely complete and no harness replay has been performed. This is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by having reviewers reconstruct decisions from packet rows and record every required return to raw prose; add fields only for repeated, decision-relevant omissions**.

### 4.7 Sensitivity is hard to identify without an external reference

If the same model generates findings and adjudicates them, apparent sensitivity can be circular. CDC's requirement for external data/validation is directly relevant. Calibration needs a stronger procedure: direct reproduction, authoritative documents, owner adjudication, or deliberately seeded cases. Without such a reference, report “escape discoveries in the sample,” not an authoritative sensitivity percentage.

### 4.8 Surveillance thresholds do not encode owner utility

Public-health action thresholds reflect disease severity, transmissibility, and intervention costs. Harness decisions have their own loss function: a false positive may waste minutes or cause a dangerous rewrite; a false negative may be harmless or catastrophic. PVP and sensitivity are diagnostics, not the objective function. Action rules must explicitly include impact, reversibility, verification cost, and owner preferences.

## 5. Transfer verdict

**Adopt the epistemic infrastructure; reject the biological metaphor as an ontology.**

The narrow mechanism transfers **very likely (80-90%)**: a versioned case definition, canonical line list, staged evidence status, linkage-aware aggregation, decision packet, negative sampling, PVP/sensitivity evaluation, and logged definition changes. That conclusion rests on the close workflow match and on authoritative epidemiological guidance explicitly addressing the same information problems: costly confirmation, missed cases, false reports, correlated/linkable cases, changing definitions, and aggregate action ([CDC, 2001](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm); [WHO outbreak investigation stages](https://www.who.int/emergencies/outbreak-toolkit/standardized-data-collection-tools/investigating-outbreak-of-unknown-disease); [WHO yellow-fever case definitions](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/case-definitions-for-public-health-surveillance)).

End-to-end benefit in this harness remains **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the preregistered historical replay, stratified adjudication sample, known-positive detector mutations, and definition-overlap test described above**. Until then, the design should report measured funnel statistics and sampled error rates, not claim comprehensive truth.

Reject these forced transfers:

- agent agreement as independent epidemiological cases;
- “confirmed” as a majority vote;
- a single global prevalence or truth probability;
- surveillance counts as causal evidence for an intervention;
- deletion of ruled-out/duplicate cases;
- an LLM-generated executive summary as the canonical record; and
- a new daemon, knowledge graph, vector memory, trained critic, or orchestration runtime.

The radical part of the recommendation is not more agents or more summarization. It is changing the unit of synthesis from prose documents to a calibrated, auditable population of claims, while keeping the implementation deliberately small.

## 6. Minimal acceptance protocol

The following is a proposal for validation, not a claim of completed work.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement only the schema validator, registry transform, deterministic aggregation, and packet renderer for the pilot.

Predeclare acceptance criteria before replay:

1. Select several completed audit episodes spanning different finding families; freeze all source records and eventual known outcomes.
2. Seed known-positive and known-negative cases, including correlated duplicates and one severe singleton.
3. Have one process generate the surveillance packet without access to eventual outcomes.
4. Randomize reviewers to full-corpus-first or packet-first; require decisions, confidence, time, and drill-down IDs.
5. Independently adjudicate stratified samples of promoted and non-promoted records using direct tests or authoritative evidence.
6. Compare harmful decision omissions, false escalations, decision agreement, time, drill-down count, PVP by stratum, sampled escape rate, singleton retention, and correlated-duplicate handling.
7. Change one case definition, rerun a frozen overlap sample, and verify that the packet identifies the trend break.
8. Reject or revise the mechanism if it saves reading time only by hiding consequential disagreements.

It is **likely (55-80%) that this protocol is sufficient to falsify the main benefit claim**, because it tests decision equivalence, time, false positives, false negatives, dependence, novelty, and definition drift. It is **unlikely (20-45%) to establish long-run generalization from one replay batch**, because future finding distributions and owner priorities can change; ongoing small calibration samples remain necessary if the pilot succeeds.
