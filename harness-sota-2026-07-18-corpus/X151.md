# X151 — Archival science → use-versus-mention guards

## Scope and conclusion

This unit is an ideation lens. Per the assignment, it does **not** inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. The only target facts assumed are those supplied in the X151 brief: five detectors have produced at least twelve false fires because they match payload text that merely describes a guarded thing. Those counts are assignment inputs, not independently verified findings in this unit.

The archival mechanism that transfers is the **archival bond, implemented through provenance-preserving, event/role-aware description**. Its useful lesson is narrow but powerful: do not infer what an information object *is doing* from its lexical content. Determine its status from the activity in which it participates, the creator/agent, its relationship to surrounding objects, and its role in an event. Archival science says that identical documents can become distinct records because their archival bonds differ; PREMIS makes the same idea machine-actionable by recording Events, Agents, Objects, and the roles linking them ([InterPARES, “The Archival Bond” citations](https://interparestrust.org/terminology/citations/758); [PREMIS 3.0 Data Dictionary, pp. 153–158](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v3/premis-3-0-final.pdf)).

The analogy transfers at the harness event boundary, not as a general theory of language. A hook receives structured facts—hook name, tool, field, source, and impending action—that are analogous to archival context. It can decide whether suspicious text is the **object of an action**, merely an **input/source**, or simply a **quotation/document under inspection**. It cannot reliably recover speaker intent from arbitrary prose after those boundaries have been flattened.

**Verdict:** build a small role-bearing event envelope and make guards authorize on typed event fields, using text only to refine a decision inside an actionable field. This is **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to eliminate deterministic mention-only fires where the hook already exposes field boundaries, because the brief identifies payload-wide matching as the failure mode and PREMIS demonstrates an implementable separation of object, event, agent, and role. The exact reduction is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying the twelve-plus known false-fire fixtures and a matched set of true-positive fixtures through old and new guards**.

## 1. What archival science solved in its own field

### 1.1 Provenance: meaning follows origin and function, not subject words

The Society of American Archivists defines provenance as origin or source and explains that archival significance depends heavily on context of creation; arrangement and description should relate to original purpose and function. It also states the two traditional operational rules: do not mingle records from different provenances, and preserve their original order ([SAA Dictionary, “provenance”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/provenance.html)). This displaced subject-based rearrangement: two papers mentioning the same topic need not belong together, while papers with different vocabulary may belong together because they arose from the same activity.

That is already a use-versus-mention distinction in structural form. A memo approving deletion, a policy quoting “delete,” and a report documenting an accidental deletion may contain the same token. Their archival identity and treatment depend on who created them, for which function, and where they sit in the sequence of business—not the shared token.

NARA operationalizes this in scheduling. A schedule describes both informational content **and** “the context or function of the records,” and the scheduling process begins with functional or business-process analysis. NARA also warns that one agency-wide schedule is inappropriate unless records have the same content **and uses** as well as the same retention/disposition periods ([NARA, “Scheduling Records”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). Thus archival appraisal does not treat topical resemblance as sufficient authority for the same disposition.

### 1.2 The archival bond: a document’s role in an activity determines record identity

The more precise mechanism is Luciana Duranti’s archival bond. InterPARES summarizes it as the network of relationships between a record and the other records in its aggregation. The bond arises when a document is set aside for action or reference, is qualified by its function in that aggregation, and develops as the activity proceeds. InterPARES further records the crucial claim that several identical documents become distinct records after acquiring distinct archival bonds, and that the bond expresses the direction of the cause-effect relationship in the activity ([InterPARES, citations to Duranti, “The Archival Bond”](https://interparestrust.org/terminology/citations/758)).

This is stronger than attaching a generic `context` label. It says the relation is part of the record’s identity. “Reference copy of a deletion policy,” “request to delete,” and “execution receipt for deletion” are not three bags of similar words; they occupy different positions and roles in an activity graph.

### 1.3 Archival diplomatics: authenticity uses identity, integrity, and context together

InterPARES defines authenticity as a record being what it purports to be and free from tampering/corruption. Its cited diplomatic test assesses identity and integrity and asks what the record is, who created it, when, what action or matter it participated in, and its administrative, cultural, documentary, and other contexts ([InterPARES Trust, “authenticity”](https://interparestrust.org/terminology/term/authenticity)). This does not reject content inspection; it refuses to let content stand alone. Internal and external evidence are combined.

That combination matters for guard design. Provenance alone can be forged or mislabeled; content alone confuses quotation with action. The archival answer is triangulation among form/content, declared identity, relationships, and custody/process history.

### 1.4 PREMIS: the machine-readable pattern

PREMIS is the most concrete borrowing. Its model separates Objects, Events, Agents, Rights, and Intellectual Entities ([Library of Congress, PREMIS maintenance activity](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/)). PREMIS 3.0 gives an Agent a role *in relation to an Event* (examples include authorizer, implementer, validator, executing program), and gives an Object a role in an Event (examples include source and outcome). It explicitly says object-role metadata distinguishes an Object’s role and that digital provenance requires links between Objects and Events ([PREMIS 3.0, pp. 153–158](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v3/premis-3-0-final.pdf)).

The key design fact is relational: role is not inferred from an object’s bytes. The same object or agent can occupy different roles in different events. That is the archival-science answer most directly applicable to a hook seeing suspicious text.

## 2. Translation into an agent harness

### 2.1 Proposal: Provenance-and-Role Guard Envelope

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

At every guardable hook event, normalize existing hook input into a compact envelope:

```json
{
  "event": "tool.invoke",
  "agent": {"id": "assistant", "role": "initiator"},
  "tool": "shell",
  "objects": [
    {"field": "command", "role": "action", "media": "shell"},
    {"field": "description", "role": "annotation", "media": "text"}
  ],
  "effect": {"kind": "filesystem.delete", "target": "/resolved/path"},
  "provenance": {"hook": "PreToolUse", "schema": 1}
}
```

The controlled roles should be deliberately small:

* `action`: bytes interpreted by an executor or used as an operation parameter;
* `target`: resource upon which an effect will operate;
* `source`: data read, summarized, transformed, or inspected;
* `annotation`: explanation, title, log, or diagnostic text;
* `quoted`: delimited example/test/documentary material that is not interpreted at this event;
* `outcome`: executor result or newly produced object;
* `unknown`: boundary could not be established.

This borrows PREMIS’s Event–Object–Agent separation and role links, but not its preservation vocabulary wholesale. The harness is processing live actions, so it needs execution-specific roles.

### 2.2 Decision rule

A guard should evaluate in this order:

1. **Event applicability.** Is this a pre-action event for a tool capable of the guarded effect? If not, do not block; optionally record a diagnostic.
2. **Role applicability.** Inspect only `action`, `target`, and effect metadata. Never scan `source`, `annotation`, `quoted`, or `outcome` as if those fields were executable.
3. **Structural interpretation.** Parse the action with the narrowest available native parser or tool schema. For example, resolve a shell command’s command/arguments, or use the MCP method name and typed arguments. Do not use a payload-wide regex as the primary classifier.
4. **Policy.** Apply the guard to the resolved operation/effect and target.
5. **Uncertainty.** If an action-capable tool has `unknown` role or parsing fails, choose the detector’s explicit failure policy. High-impact destructive operations may fail closed; advisory style checks should fail open with telemetry. Do not silently treat `unknown` as `quoted`.

The action-field restriction is **almost certain (90-99%)** to stop a detector from firing solely because a non-action field contains its trigger text, because that field is excluded by construction. Evidence is the supplied failure definition (payload text is scanned without semantic role) plus PREMIS’s explicit source/outcome and agent-event role separations. End-to-end behavior remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it with field-permutation tests that move an identical trigger string through every envelope role while holding the actual event constant**.

Structural parsing is **likely (55-80%)** to catch real operations that keyword matching misses—aliases, encoded arguments, structured MCP calls, and operations whose method conveys the effect while payload text lacks a trigger—because the operation is represented in syntax/schema rather than vocabulary. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with mutation tests covering aliases, quoting, redirection, nested interpreters, encoded commands, and equivalent MCP methods, scored against manually labeled effects**.

### 2.3 “Archival bond” as an event-local relationship, not durable memory

The harness analogue of an archival bond should be a short-lived `event_id` plus explicit links:

```text
user/assistant intent event
    └── proposed tool event
          ├── action object: command or typed method arguments
          ├── target object: resolved resources
          ├── source objects: files/documents being inspected
          └── outcome event: success, denial, or error
```

A guard judges the proposed tool event. A string inside a source object does not acquire action status merely because the source is attached to the event. Conversely, a dangerous method remains actionable even if its arguments are semantically bland.

No vector database, knowledge graph, durable execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, guardrail framework, or second orchestration runtime is proposed. The relationship exists only in the existing hook message and optional local diagnostic record. This passes the anti-overfit gate: the mechanism rides the hook/tool schema bus and can be a small normalizer plus detector helpers.

### 2.4 Concrete detector conversion pattern

Convert each of the five affected detectors from:

```text
if regex(trigger, JSON.stringify(payload)): fire
```

to:

```text
if event_is_applicable(envelope)
   and actionable_role(field)
   and parsed_effect_matches(field, policy): fire
```

Every firing should emit an explanation tuple:

```text
(detector_id, event, field, role, parsed_effect, resolved_target, policy_rule)
```

That tuple is the harness counterpart of documenting provenance and disposition authority. It makes a false fire attributable to a bad field role, parser, or policy rule instead of leaving only a matched substring.

Adding the explanation tuple is **very likely (80-90%)** to shorten diagnosis of future false fires because it exposes the exact decision inputs rather than a payload-level match. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by timing blinded diagnosis of a fixed sample of seeded failures using old logs versus tuple-bearing logs**.

## 3. What this catches or avoids

### 3.1 Mention-only false positives it avoids

The new rule distinguishes these cases even when the bytes are identical:

| Suspicious text location | Event role | Guard behavior | Archival analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| User asks “why is `rm -rf` dangerous?” | `source`/instruction under analysis | Do not fire deletion guard | Document retained for reference |
| Tool description says “blocks `git push --force`” | `annotation` | Do not fire push guard | Finding aid/description is not the described record action |
| A file being reviewed contains destructive examples | `source` or `quoted` | Do not fire unless a later event executes it | Content under appraisal, not a disposition act |
| Shell `command` contains a real destructive invocation | `action` | Parse, resolve target, apply policy | Instrument participating in the business act |
| MCP method is `delete_item` with an opaque ID | method + `target` | Fire even without the word “delete” in free text | Function and event role establish meaning |
| Tool output repeats the submitted command | `outcome` | Do not re-fire pre-action guard | Execution record/receipt is not another execution |

The first three are direct repairs for use-as-mention confusion. The fourth preserves true positives. The fifth is the important expansion: role/event-based guards can detect an action that keyword search misses. The sixth prevents recursive false fires on logs and results.

### 3.2 Cases current payload matching can miss

Assuming typed tool metadata is available, the proposal can detect:

* destructive semantics encoded in a method name plus structured parameters, even if no trigger word appears in user-visible text;
* a dangerous target after path/ID resolution, where the raw action uses an alias or variable;
* action laundering, where dangerous text first appears in a source document and is later copied into an executable action—the first event is mention, the second is use;
* replays and retries as separate events linked to one proposed operation, allowing a detector to avoid treating repeated outcome text as repeated intent.

These gains are **likely (55-80%)**, conditional on retaining typed tool boundaries and implementing parsers for the affected tools. Evidence is PREMIS’s ability to distinguish source/outcome roles and the target brief’s diagnosis that current matching ignores semantic role. They are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with a labeled replay corpus containing source→action transitions, opaque structured methods, aliases, and duplicated outcomes**.

## 4. Adversarial test of the analogy

### 4.1 Why it transfers

The structural isomorphism is real at a narrow level:

* An archive has records participating in activities; a harness has objects/fields participating in tool events.
* Archival identity depends on creator, function, relationships, and custody; guard meaning depends on agent, event type, field role, and execution boundary.
* Identical documents can have different archival bonds; identical strings can be inert documentation in one field and executable action in another.
* PREMIS already demonstrates that object/event/agent links and controlled roles can be represented in a small machine-readable model.

Therefore the transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** sound for structured hook payloads whose field-to-executor mapping is known. Evidence is the direct correspondence above and PREMIS’s explicit `linkingAgentRole` and `linkingObjectRole` semantics ([PREMIS 3.0, pp. 153–158](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v3/premis-3-0-final.pdf)).

### 4.2 Where it breaks

Archival science normally analyzes records after or during their creation and can tolerate professional interpretation, hierarchical description, and delayed appraisal. A guard must decide before an effect, in milliseconds, under adversarial input. Provenance metadata in archives is evidence to evaluate; harness role metadata may be attacker-controlled if copied from untrusted payloads. Therefore roles must be assigned by trusted adapters from tool schemas/hook positions, never accepted from free text.

Original order also does **not** map to “trust conversation order.” Digital/groupware records already challenge stable original order because components can come from many sources and authors ([SAA Dictionary, “original order”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/original-order.html)). Agent conversations are even more compositional: retrieved text, tool output, system instructions, and user content are interleaved. A chronological transcript does not by itself reveal execution role.

The archival bond does not solve pragmatic language. A user can mention a command while implicitly asking the agent to execute it (“that cleanup command from above, please”). The later tool call—not prose classification—provides the decisive event. If a guard must police natural-language intent before any typed action exists, this proposal is **unlikely (20-45%)** to classify all use-versus-mention cases reliably because the relevant relationship has not yet crystallized into a tool event. Evidence is the disanalogy between retrospective archival context and prospective, ambiguous speech. This limitation is **UNVERIFIED at the harness level; settle it with a blinded pragmatic-language benchmark labeled for mention, request, quotation, indirect request, and action, compared with event-boundary classification**.

Finally, archival provenance protects context but does not guarantee truth or safety. An authentic deletion request may still be unauthorized; an accurately typed `action` may still be malicious. Policy authorization remains a separate layer. The borrow solves **what role does this text/object play in this event?**, not **should the requested event be allowed?**

### 4.3 Failure modes introduced by the borrow

1. **Role misassignment.** A wrapper may label executable bytes as `annotation`. Mitigation: adapters derive roles from immutable schema paths and unit-test each field permutation.
2. **Flattening before the guard.** If an upstream component serializes all fields to one string, provenance is lost. Mitigation: guard before flattening; treat flattened action-capable input as `unknown`, not inert.
3. **Nested interpreters.** A shell action may launch Python, PowerShell, or another shell whose actionable text is nested. Mitigation: bounded parser recursion for supported interpreters, with an explicit unknown policy.
4. **Dual-use fields.** Some APIs interpret a “description” as a template or query. Mitigation: role comes from actual executor semantics, not field names.
5. **Forged provenance.** Untrusted documents may contain JSON resembling the envelope. Mitigation: envelope exists out-of-band and is created by trusted hook adapters.
6. **Over-broad `quoted`.** Quoted text can be executed by `eval`, command substitution, templates, or interpreters. Mitigation: `quoted` is event-relative; once passed to an executor it becomes `action` in the child event.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that role misassignment and nested interpreters are the dominant new correctness risks, because they are the points where the typed event boundary diverges from actual execution. This ranking is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by fault-injecting each listed failure mode and comparing false-negative/false-positive counts**.

## 5. Verification and acceptance design

### Experiment A — known false-fire replay

Replay all twelve-plus known false fires through the five converted detectors. Preserve the exact structured payloads. Acceptance: every case whose trigger exists only in `source`, `annotation`, `quoted`, or `outcome` is silent; its paired action fixture still fires. This is the experiment required to verify the main recommendation.

### Experiment B — field-permutation metamorphic test

For each trigger, place identical bytes in every envelope role while holding event/tool constant. Expected invariant: only actionable roles can lead to blocking, and changing text in non-actionable roles cannot change the decision. This catches regressions without needing a large learned benchmark.

### Experiment C — semantic mutation test

Generate meaning-preserving action variants: aliases, reordered flags, quoting, variable indirection, nested interpreter, encoded command, structured API method, and opaque target identifier. Include harmless lexical near-matches. Measure precision and recall against resolved effects rather than trigger strings.

### Experiment D — provenance-integrity test

Attempt to inject fake envelope JSON, role labels, and event IDs through user messages, files, tool descriptions, and tool results. Acceptance: trusted adapters overwrite/ignore them; no untrusted object can self-declare `quoted` or `annotation` at the enforcement boundary.

### Experiment E — two-signal completion gate

Do not declare the defect fixed from unit tests alone. Require two mechanically independent signals:

1. deterministic replay/metamorphic tests show the twelve-plus false fires removed with paired true positives preserved; and
2. shadow-mode event telemetry on real sessions shows old-only firings attributable to non-action roles, while manually reviewed actionable firings remain covered.

Before interpreting “zero false fires,” prove the shadow detector can find known-present seeded mention fixtures and known-present action fixtures. This follows the assignment’s detector-validation requirement and avoids confusing a silent/broken detector with a successful one.

If the existing hook payload retains field boundaries, completing this implementation and test suite is **likely (55-80%)** within the `BUILD-SMALL` budget. This estimate is **UNVERIFIED because this ideation unit intentionally did not inspect the local harness; settle it by a spike that maps the five detector inputs to the envelope and counts changed lines excluding fixtures**.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Improve the regex with quotation detection” — rejected

Quotation is syntax-dependent, nesting-sensitive, and not equivalent to mention. A quoted string passed to `eval` is use; an unquoted prose sentence may still be mention. This treats a relational problem as punctuation classification and loses the tool schema evidence.

### “Ask an LLM whether this is use or mention” — rejected as the enforcement core

An LLM classifier could be advisory for ambiguous natural language, but it adds nondeterminism and latency where the hook already has a stronger execution-boundary signal. A trained critic is also explicitly rejected by the anti-overfit gate. The proposal does not require one.

### “Keep a knowledge graph of provenance” — rejected

The useful archival bond is local to a proposed event. A durable knowledge graph would introduce a previously rejected component, expand failure surface, and not improve field-role classification at the enforcement boundary.

### “Apply retention schedules to detector logs” — not the answer to this target

Retention schedules govern how long classes of records are kept and disposed of. They reinforce function-based classification, but they do not distinguish executable use from mention at decision time. Borrowing them directly would force the analogy.

### “Full archival description standard in every hook” — rejected

PREMIS and archival description are evidence that typed roles work, not schemas to copy wholesale. The six or seven roles above are sufficient for this defect class. Importing a full preservation model would violate the minimal-architecture principle and create fields with no enforcement value.

## 7. Recommended decision

Adopt the Provenance-and-Role Guard Envelope as a small compatibility layer, then convert only the five known payload-scanning detectors first. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Keep authorization policy separate from role classification, derive roles from trusted schema adapters, and require action-aware parsing before blocking.

This recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)** better than adding regex exceptions because it removes the identified category error—confusing document content with event role—rather than enumerating textual symptoms. Evidence comes from the assignment’s stated failure mechanism, archival provenance’s rejection of subject-only arrangement, the archival bond’s function-relative identity, and PREMIS’s machine-readable event/object roles. Its realized precision, recall, and line-count remain **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the five-part replay, permutation, mutation, provenance-integrity, and shadow-mode program above**.

The borrow should stop there. Archival science provides a robust model for preserving contextual relationships; it does not provide a complete authorization system, shell parser, or pragmatic-intent oracle. For those problems, forcing the analogy would be actively misleading.
