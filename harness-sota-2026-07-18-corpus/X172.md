# X172 — Experimental design → use-versus-mention guards

## Executive result

The cross-domain borrow is valid, but only in a constrained form. The specific mechanism worth borrowing is the **matched negative control (falsification control)**, supplemented by preregistered detector contracts and blinded outcome adjudication. Experimental design does not merely tell researchers to “test more examples.” It asks them to construct a condition in which the hypothesized causal mechanism cannot operate while the suspected nuisance mechanisms remain as similar as possible. If the outcome still appears, the method has detected bias rather than the intended effect.

That maps unusually cleanly to use-versus-mention. A guard that is supposed to react to an attempted action has a hypothesized causal eligibility condition: the sensitive phrase occurs in an action-bearing semantic role. A document that quotes, explains, forbids, or reports that same phrase preserves the lexical nuisance features but removes the action-bearing role. Such documents are matched negative controls. If the guard fires on them, it has measured token presence rather than attempted use.

The resulting proposal is a small **role-gated guard contract plus paired falsification suite**, not another policy engine or orchestration layer. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** It would place a deterministic semantic-role gate before the five payload-text detectors, then continuously test each detector with minimal positive/negative pairs that share the same dangerous-looking text. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose lexical coupling before deployment, because the control deliberately preserves the exact feature a substring/regex detector uses while changing the policy-relevant role. It is **likely (55-80%)** to eliminate the reported class of mention-only false fires when event provenance and typed fields are available, because the guard can then decline to evaluate non-action fields before inspecting their text. Both confidence bands rest on the causal structure of matched negative controls and on the task premise that the observed failures are guards matching payload text rather than semantic role; they are not claims that every one of the five implementations has already been inspected.

This result does **not** support using negative controls as proof that a guard is safe, using an LLM to guess whether text is a use, or applying ordinary independent-sample power formulas blindly to clustered harness events. Those are important limits, detailed below.

## The source-field mechanism in its own field

### 1. Matched negative controls remove the proposed mechanism while retaining bias pathways

In laboratory experiments, a negative control repeats an experiment under a condition expected to yield a null result—for example, leaving out an essential ingredient, inactivating the hypothesized active ingredient, or checking an outcome impossible through the proposed mechanism. The important point is not merely that the control is “negative.” It is that it remains exposed to the same procedural errors, contaminants, and measurement artifacts as the primary condition. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen formalize the same idea for epidemiology: the control should be incapable of the causal effect of interest but share the primary outcome’s potential sources of bias ([Epidemiology paper, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).

Their useful structural criterion is *U-comparability*: a negative-control outcome should have incoming causes as similar as possible to the primary outcome except for the treatment’s causal arrow. An association with the negative control then reveals a pathway that can create a result even when the proposed causal mechanism is absent. The authors are equally explicit about the limitation: practical controls are only approximately comparable, so a positive control failure diagnoses possible bias but does not uniquely identify its source; a null control does not rule out biases the control does not share ([same source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).

Arnold and Ercumen state the operational definition succinctly: a negative-control outcome shares the primary outcome’s potential bias sources but cannot plausibly be affected by the treatment. They also explain placebo as a negative-control exposure that is susceptible to the same bias structure while omitting the active ingredient ([JAMA viewpoint, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428075/)). This is the precise experimental-design solution that transfers.

### 2. Preregistration fixes the estimand and decision rule before results invite retuning

Preregistration is a time-stamped, read-only study plan posted before data collection or analysis; it records hypotheses, methods, and analysis decisions before outcomes are known ([OSF documentation](https://help.osf.io/article/152-withdraw-a-registration)). Its relevant contribution is not bureaucracy. It separates planned confirmatory tests from later exploratory work and makes deviations visible. The Center for Open Science describes predefined plans as a way to reduce outcome-driven method changes and selective reporting ([OSF benefits](https://help.osf.io/article/603-benefits-of-registration)); the broader methodological account defines preregistration as fixing research questions and analysis plans before observing outcomes ([PNAS, “The preregistration revolution”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/)).

For a detector, the analogue is to state what event is eligible, what counts as a true fire, what costs matter, which fixtures are confirmatory, and what threshold permits rollout *before* looking at the detector’s results on the frozen holdout. This prevents the harness equivalent of moving endpoints: relabeling inconvenient mention cases as “unsafe anyway,” weakening the false-positive metric, or adding one-off exceptions until the observed suite passes.

### 3. Blinding protects subjective outcome assessment, not the intervention itself

Blinding is relevant when a person must adjudicate an ambiguous label. Cochrane distinguishes bias in outcome measurement and notes that differential measurement errors are less likely when outcome assessors do not know intervention assignment; the concern is strongest for outcomes requiring judgment and weaker for objective automated outcomes ([Cochrane Handbook, chapter 8](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-08)).

Translated carefully, the reviewer who decides whether a payload is “actual use,” “quoted mention,” or “ambiguous” should not see which detector fired, the detector version, or which side of a generated pair the test author expected to fail. Blinding does not help the runtime detector itself: code has no expectancy bias. Its value is preventing detector output from leaking into the ground-truth label used to evaluate that detector.

### 4. Power analysis asks whether a null result was capable of revealing a meaningful defect

Power is the probability of detecting a real effect of a specified magnitude under a specified design; required sample size depends on effect size, significance level, variability, and desired power. A priori power planning is intended to control false-negative risk, while post-hoc power derived from an observed null result is uninformative or misleading ([sample-size primer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10133938/); [discussion of a priori versus post-hoc power](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13246433/)).

For guards, this means that “zero false fires in ten hand-picked examples” is not evidence of a low operational false-positive rate. The evaluation must specify the smallest unacceptable false-fire rate and collect enough *independent clusters* to bound it. However, classical formulas assume a statistical sampling model that harness examples often violate. Multiple snippets from one document, template, session, or generator are correlated. Therefore, power analysis transfers as a requirement to define detectable risk and sampling units, not as permission to paste a clinical-trial sample-size formula onto fixture counts.

## Structural translation: from causal control to semantic-role control

The mapping is:

| Experimental-design element | Guard analogue |
|---|---|
| Active treatment or exposure | An action-bearing event containing the guarded operation |
| Outcome | Guard fires, warns, blocks, or rewrites |
| Hypothesized causal mechanism | The operation is being requested/executed in a role the policy governs |
| Nuisance pathway / bias | Lexical token, regex shape, serialization, Markdown fence, quoted command, filename, error echo |
| Matched negative control | Same dangerous-looking string in documentation, quotation, denial, output, error report, fixture, or historical transcript |
| Positive control | A real eligible action encoded in the same surface form and hook envelope |
| Blinded outcome adjudication | Human labels semantic role without seeing detector output/version |
| Preregistered analysis plan | Versioned detector card and frozen acceptance criteria |
| Power / sample planning | Enough independent source clusters and mutation families to detect an unacceptable false-fire rate |

The key counterfactual is not “remove the dangerous word.” That creates an easy and nearly useless negative. It is “keep the dangerous word and all feasible formatting constant; remove only action eligibility.” For example, the following should be one pair family:

```text
POSITIVE / tool-argument role:
  target = <guarded operation text>

NEGATIVE / documentation role:
  The string `target = <guarded operation text>` is an example of what the guard must reject.
```

A stronger pair serializes both through the same outer JSON/hook envelope and changes a typed role discriminator or field provenance rather than wrapping one in obviously different prose. An even stronger metamorphic family holds the phrase constant while moving it among roles: user instruction, quoted user instruction in a bug report, assistant proposal, tool argument, tool result echo, stderr, source-code comment, test fixture, retrieved web page, and policy document. The expected outcome changes only where the harness has explicitly declared that role action-bearing.

This would catch several defects that ordinary lexical tests miss:

1. A detector that searches every string field in an event rather than only the action-bearing field.
2. A detector that correctly ignores Markdown fences in user text but still scans the same quotation when it appears inside JSON, XML, YAML, logs, or tool output.
3. A detector that gates on role before serialization but loses provenance after concatenating messages, retrieved content, and tool output.
4. A detector whose allowlist handles one exact mention example but fails under whitespace, escaping, case, Unicode, or nesting mutations.
5. A detector that fires on its own warning, on a prior detector result, or on an error echo, creating recursive false-fire loops.
6. A regression that changes which event types are sent to a detector even though the detector regex itself is unchanged.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that paired role-migration tests would catch defects 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6 when the test harness can preserve and vary provenance, because each defect predicts a non-null guard response in a condition where action eligibility has been removed. It is **likely (55-80%)** that mutation families would catch defect 4, because their yield depends on how representative the chosen transformations are. These bands follow from the direct observability of guard output in controlled pairs and from negative-control theory’s requirement to preserve shared nuisance pathways.

## UNVERIFIED proposal — role-gated guard contract and falsification suite

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

This recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because it assumes the existing bus exposes, or can attach, trustworthy event provenance; experiment ROLE-ENVELOPE-INVENTORY would settle that premise. If events arrive only as one flattened undifferentiated string, stop at test instrumentation and do not pretend a reliable role gate can be reconstructed from prose.

### A. Define an event-role algebra outside individual detectors

Use a small closed set such as:

```text
ACTION_CANDIDATE  = typed tool input or explicit execution request governed by this hook
INSTRUCTION       = user/owner instruction not yet mapped to a governed action
QUOTED_CONTENT    = text explicitly represented as data, quotation, fixture, or document
OBSERVATION       = tool output, stdout/stderr, fetched content, diagnostics
POLICY            = guard/rule text
UNKNOWN           = provenance missing or invalid
```

Each detector declares eligible roles and eligible typed fields. The dispatcher checks event provenance first and passes only eligible fields to the detector. A destructive-operation guard might inspect a typed command argument at a pre-tool-use boundary; it should not scan the command’s later stderr echo. A secret-exfiltration guard may have a different eligibility matrix. “Semantic role” must therefore be policy-specific rather than one global `is_mention()` heuristic.

Do not use an LLM classifier as the first-line role gate. The role should come from the event boundary, typed field, AST node, or explicit content provenance whenever possible. An LLM may triage `UNKNOWN` events for telemetry, but making it the blocker would replace an observable category error with a probabilistic one.

This dispatcher is **likely (55-80%)** to fit within the BUILD-SMALL bound if the hook bus already carries event type and field provenance, because the work is a role enum, eligibility map, and adapter shared by five detectors. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to fit that bound if provenance has already been destroyed upstream, because recovering it would require changes at multiple producers or a parser for flattened mixed-origin text. Evidence for both conditional bands is architectural: routing existing typed metadata is local, whereas reconstructing lost causality is not. ROLE-ENVELOPE-INVENTORY is the settling experiment.

`UNKNOWN` must not be silently equated with “mention” or “safe.” Its policy should be explicit per detector: fail closed only at the actual action boundary for high-consequence operations; otherwise emit a non-blocking diagnostic and preserve evidence. This is a design choice, not a universal safety theorem.

### B. Add matched pair and role-migration fixtures for every detector rule

Every positive rule fixture must ship with at least:

- one lexical negative with the trigger absent (basic sanity control);
- one matched mention negative with the exact trigger present;
- one role-migration family moving the same payload through all supported origins;
- one positive control at the actual action boundary;
- one recursive negative containing the detector’s own warning/error text;
- one malformed/unknown-provenance case with an explicitly preregistered expected policy.

The suite should assert both final behavior and stage behavior: whether the dispatcher admitted the event, whether the detector matched, and whether enforcement occurred. That separates role-routing defects from pattern defects without pretending the negative control alone identifies the cause.

This suite is **very likely (80-90%)** to reproduce at least the supplied mention-only failure class once the twelve known examples are converted into frozen controls, because those examples are direct observed counterexamples by task premise. Whether it reveals additional production families is **likely (55-80%)**, contingent on the diversity of source clusters and role migrations. Experiment ROLE-MIGRATION-PILOT would settle the latter by running all five detectors over a cross-product of trigger families × provenance roles × serializations and reporting unique new failure mechanisms, not just failing strings.

### C. Preregister a detector card before tuning

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Each detector version gets a machine-readable card containing:

```yaml
policy_target: attempted action that warrants intervention
unit_of_analysis: independent source document/session, not snippet
eligible_event_types: [...]
eligible_roles_and_fields: {...}
positive_controls: [...]
matched_negative_control_families: [...]
primary_metrics: false blocks per independent mention cluster; missed blocks per action cluster
minimum_unacceptable_rates: owner-selected values
release_rule: frozen before holdout execution
unknown_provenance_policy: explicit per event type
exploratory_findings_policy: add to next version, never relabel current holdout silently
```

This is the useful part of preregistration: it freezes the estimand and decision rule. It does not forbid repair after a failure. A repair creates a new detector-card version and a new untouched holdout family; the old failure remains in regression tests.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that detector cards reduce endpoint drift during iterative tuning, because they make eligible roles, primary metrics, and deviations diffable before holdout results are viewed. The confidence is not higher because preregistration can become ceremonial if reviewers permit vague role definitions or retroactive amendments; OSF’s benefits depend on committing to a sufficiently specific predefined plan ([OSF benefits](https://help.osf.io/article/603-benefits-of-registration)).

### D. Blind only the subjective labeling step

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

For ambiguous historical cases, present annotators with the event envelope and necessary preceding context but hide detector name, version, output, enforcement result, and expected pair polarity. Collect independently:

- semantic role;
- whether an actual governed action is attempted;
- whether intervention is warranted;
- confidence and “insufficient context.”

Reveal detector outputs only after labels are frozen. Disagreements become an ambiguity stratum, not forced ground truth. Fully objective typed tool-boundary cases need no human blinding; their labels follow from the declared event contract.

Blinding is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce confirmation-driven relabeling in subjective cases, because it removes knowledge of the detector’s assignment from outcome assessment, the same pathway Cochrane identifies for differential measurement error. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to materially improve objective typed-boundary cases, because there is little subjective assessment for blinding to protect. These are conditional mechanism claims, not measured effects in this harness.

### E. Use cluster-aware assurance rather than a magic fixture count

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Define the independent sampling unit as the source document, session, or generator seed. Report confusion matrices by role and detector, plus cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals or conservative zero-event bounds at the cluster level. Choose the minimum unacceptable false-fire rate before sampling, then determine how many independent clusters are needed to make a null result informative. Keep generated mutations grouped with their seed; one seed with 500 whitespace variants is not 500 independent observations.

An exact sample size cannot be responsibly proposed here because the unacceptable false-fire rate, role prevalence, intracluster correlation, and desired confidence are **UNVERIFIED; experiment CLUSTER-PILOT-POWER would settle them by labeling a pilot of independently sampled sessions/documents, estimating cluster sizes and between-cluster variance, and then simulating power for owner-selected false-fire thresholds**. A conventional 80% target may be common in research, but the cited primer explicitly warns against assuming it without domain justification ([sample-size primer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10133938/)). Safety-critical action blocking may warrant a different threshold.

## Acceptance experiment

The full evaluation can be run as a staged experiment without deploying new enforcement:

1. **ROLE-ENVELOPE-INVENTORY:** validate the detector by proving it finds known-present role metadata in at least one typed tool-input event, then inventory every relevant event type through a separately recorded producer schema or hook declaration. Do not claim metadata absent from one empty probe. This explicitly satisfies the two-mechanically-independent-signals rule for any later absence claim: runtime capture is signal one; producer schema/declaration is signal two.
2. **CONTROL CONSTRUCTION:** convert each of the twelve-plus supplied false fires into a frozen negative-control cluster. For each, create a matched positive at the genuine action boundary and at least one role migration preserving the trigger text.
3. **BLINDED LABEL FREEZE:** have reviewers label ambiguous cases without detector output. Resolve only through written policy; retain disagreements.
4. **PREREGISTRATION:** freeze detector cards, primary metrics, minimum unacceptable rates, unknown-role policy, and release rule before opening the holdout results.
5. **BASELINE:** run current detectors over the frozen suite and record dispatcher admission, raw match, and final enforcement.
6. **ROLE GATE:** add the common role dispatcher without changing regexes. This isolates the effect of provenance gating.
7. **HOLDOUT:** evaluate on independent source clusters and unseen role/serialization combinations. Generated children remain clustered by seed.
8. **NEGATIVE CONTROL INTERPRETATION:** any mention-control fire falsifies role specificity. A clean negative suite supports only the bias pathways represented by those controls; it is not a proof of absence.
9. **POSITIVE CONTROL INTERPRETATION:** any failure on the action-bearing mate reveals over-filtering or a role-contract mismatch. Negative-only success is unacceptable.
10. **SHADOW PHASE:** observe decisions without blocking and compare typed role, detector output, and actual downstream action. Promotion occurs only under the preregistered rule.

The paired design is **very likely (80-90%)** to distinguish “regex is too broad” from “wrong event/field reached the regex” when stage-level assertions are captured, because admission and match are separate observable outcomes. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that the first role taxonomy will cover every ambiguous mixed-content payload, because retrieved documents can contain instructions that later influence an agent even though they are observations at ingestion time. ROLE-MIGRATION-PILOT and shadow evaluation are the settling experiments.

## Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### Why it transfers

1. **The counterfactual intervention is real and cheap.** We can hold the trigger bytes fixed and move them between typed semantic roles. In many biological studies, comparability is approximate; a harness can often make the negative control byte-identical except for provenance.
2. **The nuisance mechanism is known.** The target premise identifies payload-text matching. Mention controls intentionally retain payload text, serialization artifacts, escaping, and detector warning strings.
3. **The outcome is observable.** Admission, regex match, warning, block, and rewrite can be logged as distinct deterministic outcomes in a test run.
4. **The failure interpretation is appropriately narrow.** A fire on a matched mention control proves the detector is not specific to the declared role for that case. It does not require inferring intent from a production incident.

For these reasons, it is **very likely (80-90%)** that the negative-control concept transfers as a falsification-test design. The confidence rests on the ability to perform a closer intervention than the epidemiological sources generally can: preserve the payload and alter typed provenance.

### Where it does not transfer

1. **“Mention” is not always causally inert.** A retrieved page that merely contains a command at ingestion can become an instruction through prompt injection or later model copying. It is a negative control for an immediate pre-tool-use detector only if the detector’s policy target is immediate attempted execution; it may be a positive case for a content-boundary or untrusted-instruction detector. Controls must be detector-specific.
2. **Semantic role can be mixed.** A user can quote a command and then say “run it.” The quotation is data locally but participates in an action request compositionally. Field-level provenance alone may not establish intent.
3. **Negative controls detect, but do not identify, the bias.** A false fire could arise in routing, flattening, parsing, normalization, recursion, or the detector. Stage instrumentation is needed to localize it, matching the epidemiological warning that negative controls are sensitive but blunt.
4. **A null does not prove safety.** A control suite covers only its shared nuisance pathways. New serializations, hook types, and mixed-origin concatenations can evade it.
5. **Blinding is not a runtime mechanism.** There is no value in “blinding” deterministic code. It applies only to subjective labeling and review.
6. **Power assumptions are weaker here.** Mutation cases are correlated and production distributions drift. Fixture count is not sample size, and a nominal power calculation on non-independent snippets would overstate assurance.
7. **Preregistration cannot replace policy judgment.** Freezing an underspecified or wrong role contract merely makes the mistake reproducible.

The analogy therefore supports the test and routing architecture, but **does not** justify a universal semantic classifier that labels all quoted text safe. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that such a universal rule would be structurally wrong, because the same text can be inert documentation at one hook boundary and an action-bearing argument at another; the evidence is the boundary-dependent causal target above, not an empirical claim about current code.

## Anti-overfit gate

The recommended design passes the stated gate:

- It rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus: a shared pre-detector role adapter, declarative detector cards, and tests.
- It does not introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector/KG memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- Its complexity is bounded: typed role routing is centralized; individual detectors declare eligibility rather than each inventing a semantic parser.
- It degrades honestly: missing provenance becomes explicit `UNKNOWN`, not guessed “mention.”
- It remains valuable as models improve because it tests deterministic harness boundaries rather than modeling general language intent.

The BUILD-SMALL line budget applies to the reusable adapter/reporting logic, not to fixture data. If ROLE-ENVELOPE-INVENTORY finds that trustworthy provenance is absent upstream, the recommendation must be re-estimated; hiding multi-producer reconstruction inside a nominal 200-line label would be dishonest.

## Final recommendation

Adopt matched negative controls as the governing verification mechanism for use-versus-mention guards. For every dangerous-looking positive, preserve the payload and migrate it through non-action semantic roles; require null enforcement there and retained enforcement at the actual action boundary. Freeze the detector’s eligible roles and acceptance rule before holdout evaluation, and blind humans only when labels require judgment.

Proceed with the role-gated implementation only if ROLE-ENVELOPE-INVENTORY confirms trustworthy provenance using both runtime captures and producer schemas. Under that condition, **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** and it is **likely (55-80%)** to eliminate the stated mention-only category while retaining positive controls. Without that condition, do not infer semantic role from payload prose and do not force a small-build label onto a cross-runtime provenance reconstruction.
