# X127 — Cross-domain borrow: forensic science → harness observability without a baseline

## Executive finding

The borrow transfers, but only in a bounded form. Forensic science has a strong answer to **“is the operating system producing defensible results under ordinary conditions?”**: insert realistic, known-answer proficiency items into the normal case pipeline without alerting the examiner; protect those items with positive/negative contamination controls; and delay potentially biasing context until an observation has been recorded. It does **not** by itself answer the causal question **“did change B improve on A?”**. That requires an added paired, randomized A/A-then-A/B design.

The concrete recommendation is a small **blind proficiency packet runner**: a versioned set of hidden, known-answer and invariant-bearing tasks; interleaved runs of incumbent and candidate harnesses; opaque treatment labels; scoring before the labels are revealed; provenance and negative controls to detect benchmark contamination; and an A/A phase that measures stochastic noise before any improvement is claimed.

This is a legitimate transfer because the source and target share four structural properties: a multi-stage pipeline rather than a single test function, behavior that changes when the operator knows it is being assessed, sparse real-world ground truth, and consequential contamination by contextual information. It is only a partial transfer because open-ended agent work often has no unique known answer, model behavior is stochastic and can drift without a harness change, and realistic synthetic tasks can leak their test status.

**Overall confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)**, based on (1) field implementations showing that blind cases can traverse an ordinary forensic pipeline and reveal differences from declared tests, (2) a direct mapping from “examiner knows it is a test” to agent/evaluator test awareness, and (3) standard paired experimental controls. The remaining uncertainty is primarily ecological validity for heterogeneous agent work, not whether blinding itself is coherent.

## 1. What forensic science has actually solved

### 1.1 Test-blind proficiency testing: a known-answer case disguised as routine work

In forensic laboratories, an ordinary case generally has unknown truth: the examination is being performed precisely because the answer is not already known. A proficiency test creates a sample whose expected answer is controlled. A **declared** proficiency test tells the examiner it is a test. A **blind** proficiency test submits the item through the ordinary intake and analysis pipeline as if it were a real case; the examiner learns that it was a test only after completing the work.

Mejia, Cuellar, and Salyards describe the mechanism explicitly: proficiency testing permits evaluation of an examiner or an entire laboratory pipeline against known ground truth; blind samples enter “the normal analysis pipeline” as real cases; and only after completion is their test status disclosed. They also document the reason for masking. Commercial or declared tests can differ from casework in task and difficulty, examiners can devote special effort to known tests, and historical comparisons in drug and blood-lead laboratories found different error rates on blind versus declared samples. Their review reports that blind testing can expose examiner errors, process failures, and even misconduct that open testing may miss. Source: [Mejia, Cuellar & Salyards, *Implementing blind proficiency testing in forensic laboratories: Motivation, obstacles, and recommendations* (2020), full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/) and [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsisyn.2020.09.002).

The Houston Forensic Science Center implementation is unusually informative because it exercised whole workflows, not only isolated analytical judgments. Its program covered toxicology, seized drugs, firearms, latent prints, biology, and multimedia. Quality staff created samples with known expected answers after studying each discipline’s evidence-submission workflow. From 2015–2018, 901 of 973 submitted blind samples were completed and analysts discovered only 51 as tests. Source: [Wightman et al., *Implementation of a Blind Quality Control Program in a Forensic Laboratory* (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1556-4029.14259). A later firearms report makes the contrast precise: open tests measure examiner competence but may not represent actual casework; mock blind cases instead mimic routine work and traverse normal microscopic examination and comparison. Its 51 blind cases generated 570 determinations and exposed a large, evidence-type-dependent inconclusive pattern despite no “unsatisfactory” result. Source: [Wightman et al., *Blind testing in firearms: Preliminary results from a blind quality control program* (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1556-4029.15031).

The key forensic mechanism is therefore not merely “use a test set.” It is this five-part protocol:

1. Establish an assigned truth or a defensible expected-result envelope before submission.
2. Make the sample and intake metadata realistic enough to enter the normal pipeline.
3. Withhold test status from the operating examiner.
4. Exercise intake, routing, analysis, verification, and reporting rather than only the central analytic step.
5. Reconcile the completed result and process trace against the assigned truth, then investigate deviations.

**Transfer confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that these five elements are accurately characterized, because the peer-reviewed implementation and the open-access methods/recommendations paper independently describe the same workflow.

### 1.2 Proficiency testing creates a baseline where routine cases have none

Proficiency testing replaces unavailable truth on ordinary cases with truth assigned to specially constructed cases. ISO/IEC 17043 defines competence requirements for providers of proficiency testing, while ISO/IEC 17025 defines competence requirements for testing and calibration laboratories; together they institutionalize external comparison and controlled test items rather than pretending routine work supplies its own truth. Sources: [ISO/IEC 17043:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/80864.html) and [ISO/IEC 17025:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/66912.html).

This baseline is **local and operational**, not a universal estimate of field accuracy. It says how the laboratory performed on specified controlled challenges under specified conditions. The firearms example is important: the program could characterize inconclusive-result rates by ground truth and evidence type, but the authors did not turn those cases into a universal error rate for all firearms work. That restraint maps directly to harness evaluation: a proficiency packet can establish repeatable performance on its challenge distribution, but it cannot establish performance on all future owner work.

**Transfer confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that controlled packets can supply a useful local baseline for an agent harness, based on the exact match in the missing-data structure: truth is unavailable on most natural cases but can be assigned on designed cases. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that any fixed packet remains representative for long without maintenance, based on the forensic literature’s documented gap between commercial proficiency items and routine casework.

### 1.3 Contamination control: controls and provenance, not “be careful”

Forensic DNA practice treats contamination as something to make detectable. The UK Forensic Science Regulator’s laboratory guidance has distinct controls for prevention and detection. Its detection regime explicitly includes positive and negative controls, within- and between-batch comparisons, and comparison against elimination databases; it also separates incident management from preventive practice. Sources: [official publication page, *DNA contamination controls: laboratory (FSR-GUI-0018)*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dna-contamination-controls-laboratory) and [the issued guidance PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4fbd8a2467584757371c90/FSR-GUI-0018_-_contamination_controls_-_lab_issue_2.pdf).

The conceptual move matters: a negative control has no target material, so a positive result indicates contamination somewhere in preparation or processing; an elimination database can associate a detected profile with staff or other expected handlers rather than misattributing it to the evidentiary source; batch comparison can reveal carry-over across items. The analogous harness problem is benchmark leakage and evaluation-context leakage: the task, expected answer, version label, or prior solution may enter the agent’s or judge’s context and make a result look better than the deployed system is.

**Transfer confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that explicit provenance plus negative controls will detect some harness-evaluation leakage that ordinary pass/fail scoring misses, because the mechanism detects forbidden material in both domains. The confidence is not higher because semantic leakage into a pretrained model is much harder to identify than a DNA profile and may have no attributable “elimination” match.

### 1.4 Sequential unmasking: record the evidence before revealing the hypothesis

Linear Sequential Unmasking (LSU) requires the examiner to inspect and document the questioned evidence before seeing the known reference material that could anchor interpretation. LSU-Expanded generalizes the sequencing rule: begin with the raw evidence, prioritize information by biasing power, objectivity, and relevance, and document information exposure and any opinion changes as the sequence unfolds. Source: [Dror & Kukucka, *Linear Sequential Unmasking–Expanded* (2021), full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8385162/) and [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsisyn.2021.100161).

For harness observability, the “known reference” is the treatment identity and the designer’s theory that a change is beneficial. An evaluator should first see the raw task, outputs, process trace, and predeclared rubric under opaque labels; record scores and reasons; and only then learn which output came from the candidate. This is more precise than asking reviewers to “be objective.”

**Transfer confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that delayed version disclosure reduces evaluator anchoring, based on the direct identity between the information-flow structures and the forensic method’s explicit sequencing rationale. It will not remove rubric bias or hindsight in task selection.

## 2. The proposed harness mechanism

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED until a 20-item spike proves bus integration and line count

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Build one thin runner and use data manifests for the packet. No second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is introduced. This passes the anti-overfit gate: it is an evaluation fixture and adapter on the existing execution bus, not a runtime that attempts to make the agent smarter.

The premise that this can remain below roughly 200 executable lines is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to implement one adapter, run 20 packet items through the existing hook/skill/MCP entry point, and count non-test executable lines plus integration-specific branches**. If it exceeds the threshold because each agent surface requires a bespoke adapter, reclassify it as BUILD-LARGE rather than hiding complexity in scripts.

Call the artifact a **Blind Proficiency Packet (BPP)**. Each packet item is declarative:

```yaml
id: stable opaque identifier
stratum: e.g. retrieval | edit | shell | planning | external-tool
input_fixture: task plus isolated workspace snapshot
truth:
  executable_oracle: optional
  required_invariants: [...]
  forbidden_effects: [...]
  rubric: optional, fixed before execution
controls:
  positive: expected detectable success signal
  negative: expected absence of a seeded signal
provenance:
  authorship_and_source: ...
  content_hashes: ...
  forbidden_context_hashes: [...]
realism_notes: ...
```

The runner performs this protocol:

1. **Freeze the estimand.** Before any result is seen, choose one primary task-level endpoint (for example, oracle-confirmed completion without forbidden effects), guardrails (data loss, policy violation, owner correction, unrecovered tool failure), and secondary efficiency measures (cost, latency, tool-call count). This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce post-hoc metric shopping, because the permitted primary comparison is fixed before treatment identities and outcomes are known.
2. **Run A/A first.** Execute the incumbent twice on the same stratified items, with independent stochastic replicates and randomized order. This measures repeatability, evaluator disagreement, and infrastructure noise. With no historical baseline, the A/A distribution is the baseline.
3. **Run paired A/B.** Execute incumbent and candidate on the same item snapshots. Randomize order and opaque labels per item. Where a provider permits control of seeds or model snapshots, hold them fixed; where it does not, use multiple paired replicates and record the provider/model identifier and time.
4. **Keep the agent test-blind where feasible.** Present the same task envelope and tool surface used in ordinary work. Do not add “benchmark,” expected-answer, or candidate-version text to the model-visible context. Synthetic live submissions must be confined to an isolated account/workspace and must never contact real people or mutate owner data.
5. **Keep the evaluator treatment-blind.** Run executable oracles first. Give any human or model judge only the raw task, the two labeled outputs/traces, the rubric, and labels such as X/Y. Lock the score and rationale before revealing the version map. A second adjudicator receives the same blinded material when the first judge marks ambiguity.
6. **Run contamination controls.** Include a no-op/placebo change, a negative-control task containing a tempting but forbidden leaked answer, and a positive-control task with an intentionally detectable known defect. Hash and provenance-check packet content against any prompt/example bundle distributed to the harness. Separate packet authorship from final adjudication.
7. **Unmask once.** After scores are immutable, reveal treatment identity, compute paired effect sizes and uncertainty by stratum, and inspect guardrail regressions. Report item-level results, not only an aggregate score.
8. **Refresh by retirement, not mutation.** When an item is exposed, learned, or ceases to resemble work, retire it with its old results intact and add a newly versioned item. Never silently edit a failed test into a passing one.

The protocol’s central causal claim is **very likely (80-90%)**: paired A/B comparison after an A/A noise estimate will distinguish real harness effects from ordinary run-to-run variance more reliably than an unpaired before/after dashboard. Evidence is the paired control structure plus the forensic demonstration that test awareness changes behavior. The exact replication count and decision threshold are **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a pilot that increases replicates until the A/A interval for the predeclared primary endpoint is narrower than the smallest improvement the owner considers worth shipping**.

### Optional live canary layer — UNVERIFIED until isolation and recognizability tests pass

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** If the existing bus already supports scheduled isolated evaluation runs, configure a small random sample of packet tasks to traverse the deployed routing and tool stack. Do not inject fake work into real owner sessions or external services.

The premise that a synthetic canary can look like ordinary agent work without unsafe side effects is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a red-team recognizability study in which operators and an independent classifier try to distinguish 50 canaries from 50 matched ordinary, fully de-identified tasks, followed by an isolation audit that proves all writes and outbound actions terminate in disposable fixtures**. This layer is **likely (55-80%)** to catch deployment-only faults—routing, permissions, missing tools, stale configuration—that an offline replay misses, because it crosses the deployed pipeline. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to provide an unbiased estimate of overall owner-task success, because safe canaries necessarily omit many high-stakes and interpersonal tasks.

## 3. What this would catch that ordinary telemetry misses

1. **Special effort or altered behavior under declared evaluation.** A blind packet is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose at least some test-awareness effects if the ordinary evaluator announces benchmark status, because forensic comparisons found declared and blind tests can yield different behavior and error rates.
2. **Pipeline failures outside the central model answer.** Whole-path items are **likely (55-80%)** to catch intake, routing, permission, tool-selection, state-transfer, and reporting defects that answer-only grading misses, based on Houston’s explicit whole-quality-system design. The settling evidence for this harness would be a fault-injection matrix with one seeded defect at each stage.
3. **False improvement from stochastic noise.** A/A variance plus paired replicates are **very likely (80-90%)** to reject some apparent wins produced by ordinary randomness, because the same incumbent is expected to generate a nonzero empirical difference distribution. The magnitude is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the A/A pilot**.
4. **Evaluator confirmation bias.** Opaque labels and score-lock-before-unmasking are **likely (55-80%)** to reduce version-favoring judgments, based on LSU-E’s information-sequencing mechanism. The harness-specific effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by randomly revealing candidate identity to half of evaluators and comparing score deltas and rationale changes**.
5. **Benchmark or answer leakage.** Provenance hashes, forbidden-context checks, answer-lure negative controls, and exposed-item retirement are **likely (55-80%)** to catch direct leakage. They are **very unlikely (5-20%)** to detect all semantic memorization from opaque pretrained-model data, because there may be no inspectable source or exact string match.
6. **A metric that passes while behavior degrades.** Positive controls, negative controls, forbidden-effect checks, and item-level strata are **likely (55-80%)** to expose some aggregate-score masking (for example, efficiency gains purchased with unsafe side effects). They cannot guarantee detection of an unmeasured harm.
7. **A proficiency test that is too easy or unlike practice.** Discovery rate, item difficulty, inconclusive/abstention patterns, and periodic recognizability studies are **likely (55-80%)** to flag gross ecological-validity failure. This follows the forensic finding that commercial proficiency samples can be easier and cleaner than casework, but subtle distribution mismatch will remain.
8. **Change-independent infrastructure drift.** Interleaving incumbent and candidate in the same time blocks is **likely (55-80%)** to separate a candidate effect from shared provider or network drift. It cannot separate treatment from drift if the two treatments cannot be run contemporaneously.

## 4. Adversarial transfer analysis

### Where the analogy holds

| Structural feature | Forensic laboratory | Agent harness | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground truth is absent in ordinary work | Submitted evidence has an unknown answer | Novel owner tasks have unknown counterfactual “best” output | Strong: designed known-answer items create a local reference |
| Assessment awareness changes behavior | Examiners can spend extra effort on declared tests | Agents and judges can react to benchmark framing/version identity | Strong: masking removes the same contextual variable |
| Quality is pipeline-wide | Intake, custody, preparation, analysis, verification, report | Prompt/context, routing, tools, state, answer, side effects | Strong: submit at the boundary and score the trace |
| Contamination creates false attribution | Foreign DNA or carry-over can be attributed to evidence | leaked answers/version labels can be attributed to capability | Moderate: controls transfer, signatures are weaker in software |
| Reference information can anchor judgment | Known comparison material/context alters evidence interpretation | candidate identity/design intent alters evaluation | Strong: score before unmasking |

### Where it fails or weakens

**No stable physical analyte.** DNA profiles, drug concentrations, and manufactured comparison marks permit assigned values. Many agent tasks permit several equally good outputs, and “best” may depend on owner taste. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that executable oracles alone would overfit the packet toward narrow coding and retrieval tasks. Evidence: this is a direct mismatch between assigned physical quantities and open-ended language/action outcomes. Therefore the packet must combine exact oracles, invariants, forbidden effects, and blinded adjudication, and must report coverage by task stratum.

**Synthetic evidence can announce itself.** The forensic literature reports blind items discovered because physical handling or submission details were implausible; the same failure can occur through sterile prompts, tiny repositories, artificial filenames, or missing conversational history. It is **likely (55-80%)** that a naïvely authored harness canary will be recognizable. Evidence: documented discovery problems in the blind-testing implementation literature. This is why recognizability is a measured property, not an assumption.

**A forensic proficiency test monitors competence; it does not identify the cause of a change.** Passing after a harness edit does not prove the edit caused the pass. Provider updates, model stochasticity, evaluator drift, and time-varying tools are competing causes. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that blind proficiency testing without concurrent controls is insufficient for “did B help A?”; the evidence is the absence of a counterfactual in the proficiency protocol itself. Paired randomized A/B and interleaving are necessary additions, not forensic imports.

**True double-blindness is incomplete.** The packet controller must know the treatment mapping and expected answers; executable oracles encode the expected result; the harness may expose its own version through behavior or files. The defensible target is role-separated masking: the operating agent does not receive test status, and the subjective evaluator does not receive treatment identity until scoring is locked. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that perfect double-blinding is achievable across an introspective agent filesystem. The protocol must log every visible version cue and treat guessed identity as a covariate.

**Nonstationarity is faster.** Laboratory methods and staff change, but hosted models, web content, tool APIs, and rate limits can change between runs. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a baseline measured once will decay. Evidence: the target system includes externally versioned components, whereas a controlled physical sample can remain stable. Interleaved contemporaneous controls are more important here than in the source domain.

**The canary can cause harm.** A forensic laboratory can route mock evidence under controlled agreements. An agent task might send email, modify repositories, spend money, or contact people. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that unrestricted blind injection into real owner work is unacceptable. The transfer stops at isolated fixtures unless an action is provably reversible and contained.

**Gaming migrates.** Once developers know packet families, they can optimize to the packet even without seeing item identities. It is **likely (55-80%)** that long-lived packets will induce Goodhart effects. Evidence: the forensic literature’s observed gap between standardized proficiency material and casework. Rotation, held-out authors, and immutable retirement history mitigate but do not solve it.

## 5. Minimum viable experiment and falsification criteria

The borrow should be rejected, not elaborated, if the following pilot fails.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Construct 20 items across at least four task strata, including five exact-oracle items, five invariant/forbidden-effect items, five tool-path fault items, and five blinded-rubric items. Add one positive control, one negative leakage control, and one placebo harness change. Run incumbent/incumbent first, then incumbent/candidate, with treatment labels concealed from adjudicators.

Predeclare these falsifiers:

- If blinded adjudicators cannot reproduce their own rubric decisions above the owner’s predeclared acceptable agreement level, the packet has no stable measurement surface; improve or drop those items.
- If canaries are identified as tests materially more often than matched ordinary tasks, do not claim ecological validity; redesign or keep the packet explicitly offline.
- If the positive control is not detected, the detector is unvalidated and no “absence of regression” claim is allowed.
- If the negative control passes as genuine capability, contamination control failed and the affected results are invalid.
- If A/A variation is as large as the smallest worthwhile A/B effect, increase replication or concede that the harness cannot resolve changes of that size.
- If candidate identity can be inferred reliably before unmasking, report the masking failure and analyze identity-guess strata separately.
- If integration exceeds the BUILD-SMALL boundary, do not create a second runtime. Prefer a thinner adapter or stop.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this pilot will yield a usable measurement surface for bounded, tool-using tasks, based on the forensic implementations and the availability of executable/invariant outcomes in software. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that the same packet design will yield stable judgments for highly subjective strategy, prose, or relationship-sensitive work, because those tasks lack assigned truth and often depend on owner-specific context.

## 6. Decision rule and reporting format

Do not report “B is better” from a single aggregate. Report:

- packet version, item provenance, model/provider/tool versions, and run window;
- A/A paired-difference distribution and evaluator agreement;
- A/B paired effects by task stratum with uncertainty intervals;
- pass/fail of the positive, negative, and placebo controls;
- guardrail regressions and forbidden side effects item by item;
- test-discovery/identity-guess rate;
- excluded items and the exclusion rule fixed before unmasking;
- the exact practical-improvement threshold chosen before execution.

A shipping rule based on “primary endpoint exceeds the predeclared practical threshold while no guardrail crosses its predeclared non-inferiority boundary, and all controls validate” is **likely (55-80%)** to resist false wins better than dashboard inspection. The exact statistical interval, sample size, and non-inferiority margins are **UNVERIFIED; settle them from the A/A pilot and the owner’s cost of false promotion versus false rejection, not from a generic 95% convention**.

## 7. Bottom line

The high-value forensic borrow is a **measurement intervention**, not an observability platform: blinded, realistic, known-answer cases; whole-pipeline traversal; contamination controls; and score-before-unmasking information flow. It gives the harness a deliberately manufactured baseline when natural work has none. The necessary non-forensic addition is a concurrent paired A/A/A-B causal design.

This proposal is deliberately small. It adds a packet, a thin runner, and a protocol on the existing bus. It does not add a second agent runtime or a heavyweight memory/telemetry system. Its success condition is not “more traces”; it is the ability to falsify an improvement claim under masking, controls, provenance, and measured run-to-run noise.
