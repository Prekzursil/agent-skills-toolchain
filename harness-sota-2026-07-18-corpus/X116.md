# X116 — Cross-domain borrow: reliability engineering → cost control

**Unit:** X116  
**Source field:** reliability engineering (FMEA/FMECA, fault trees, probabilistic risk assessment, risk-importance measures, redundancy/diversity, common-cause failure)  
**Target problem:** spend agent compute and quota where it can change the answer, rather than uniformly  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19 UTC  
**Scope discipline:** external research and ideation only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness/repository. I used the pinned measurements as givens only to understand the overall program, not as evidence for this cross-domain lens.

## Executive judgment

The borrow **does transfer, but only as a decision architecture, not as literal reliability arithmetic**.

The most useful reliability-engineering mechanism is not a conventional FMEA risk-priority number. It is the combination of:

1. a **fault tree** that starts from a defined unacceptable system outcome and represents the combinations of basic events that produce it;
2. **counterfactual risk-importance measures** that distinguish “where would improvement reduce total risk most?” from “what must remain reliable because losing it raises risk most?”; and
3. **common-cause analysis**, which refuses to count nominally redundant channels as independent when one cause can defeat them together.

In an agent harness, this becomes a small **risk-directed compute allocator**. It gives every task a cheap baseline attempt, identifies live answer-failure pathways, estimates which available next action has the largest *marginal expected loss reduction per unit cost*, discounts repeated evidence that shares a likely failure cause, and stops when no eligible action has sufficient marginal value. It also protects a small “reliability assurance floor” for functions whose loss makes every downstream answer unsafe, such as evidence validation for high-consequence claims.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this policy can beat uniform “always use the expensive model / always spawn N reviewers / always search K sources” at equal cost on a heterogeneous owner-task distribution. Evidence: input-adaptive test-time computation improved the quality/cost frontier in an ICLR 2025 study, while reliability importance measures are expressly designed to prioritize improvements and assurance work; however, neither source validates this proposed harness policy on the owner’s task mix ([ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1b623663fd9b874366f3ce019fdfdd44-Paper-Conference.pdf), [NRC NUREG/CR-3385 landing page and abstract](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/nuregs/contract/cr3385/index)). **UNVERIFIED: settle with the matched-budget, task-stratified shadow/A-B experiment specified below.**

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that blindly multiplying same-model samples or reviewers overstates the amount of independent checking in at least some tasks. Evidence: reliability sources warn that redundancy is susceptible to common-mode failure and prescribe diversity to address it; the transfer to model copies is structurally plausible because copies can share model, prompt context, tools, retrieval results, and verifier. This is not a measured claim about this owner’s harness ([NIST SP 800-160 Vol. 1 Rev. 1, principle E.26](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-160v1r1.pdf), [NIST UTC reliability implementation](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/time-realization/utcnist-time-scale/reliability-utcnist)). **UNVERIFIED: settle by measuring pairwise conditional error correlation for verifier channels on a labeled task suite, as specified below.**

The design is deliberately not a second orchestration runtime, learned router, trained critic, durable workflow engine, vector database, knowledge graph, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It is a compact policy function and event record riding the existing model/tool/hook bus.

## 1. What reliability engineering actually solved

### 1.1 Start from loss, then decompose how it can happen

Fault-tree analysis is deductive. The analyst first specifies an undesired “top event,” then models the logical combinations of more elementary events that can produce it. NASA’s software engineering handbook describes the procedure as assuming the hazard has occurred and expanding its possible causes through AND/OR relationships until the leaves have computable probabilities or cannot be analyzed further ([NASA Software Fault Tree Analysis](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVC/pages/140640490/8.7%2B-%2BSoftware%2BFault%2BTree%2BAnalysis)). NASA’s aerospace fault-tree handbook explains that the top-event probability can be propagated from basic-event probabilities or evaluated from minimal cut sets—the smallest combinations sufficient to produce the top event ([NASA Fault Tree Handbook with Aerospace Applications, chapter 7](https://extapps.ksc.nasa.gov/reliability/Documents/Fault_Tree_Handbook_with_Aerospace_Applications_August_2002.pdf)). The NRC’s handbook codified this method for system-safety and reliability analysis ([NRC NUREG-0492](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/nuregs/staff/sr0492/index)).

This matters for resource allocation because raw component failure frequency is not system importance. A frequent component fault may sit behind a successful fallback and contribute little to the top event; a rare fault may be the only barrier against a catastrophic outcome. The NIST bottom-up reliability guide makes the structural assumption explicit: series, parallel, r-out-of-n, and standby models combine component failure behavior differently, and simple multiplication is valid only under stated independence conditions ([NIST Engineering Statistics Handbook §8.1.8](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr18.htm)).

### 1.2 Use two counterfactuals, because “improve this” and “protect this” are different questions

Probabilistic risk assessment uses importance measures to ask counterfactual questions about a component or feature:

- **Risk Reduction Worth (RRW)** asks how much total risk could fall if a feature were made perfectly reliable. The NRC says it is useful for prioritizing improvements that can most reduce risk.
- **Risk Achievement Worth (RAW)** asks how much total risk would rise if a feature were unavailable or failed. The NRC says it is useful for prioritizing reliability-assurance and maintenance activities.

Those purposes are stated in the abstract of the NRC’s NUREG/CR-3385 ([NRC, *Measures of Risk Importance and Their Applications*](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/nuregs/contract/cr3385/index)). In simplified notation, for baseline top-event risk \(R\), a feature \(i\), \(R_{i=0}\) with its failure probability set to zero, and \(R_{i=1}\) with it forced failed:

\[
RRW_i = R / R_{i=0}, \qquad RAW_i = R_{i=1} / R.
\]

The exact equations depend on the PRA convention, but the conceptual split is the important transferable mechanism. “Spend new resources where improvement has leverage” and “do not economize away a critical existing barrier” are different portfolios.

The NRC also cautions that importance measures provide different insights and that categorization should be an integrated decision using plant information beyond the measures ([NRC Advisory Committee letter, 2000](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/acrs/letters/2000/4691872.html)). A contemporaneous NRC meeting transcript is even more adversarial: importance measures are relative, can be misleading for flat risk profiles or special cut-set structures, and should be an initial screening step when direct change in risk can be evaluated ([NRC joint subcommittee transcript, 1999](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/acrs/tr/subcommittee/1999/pr990924)). Thus the source field itself does not license “sort one score and obey it.”

### 1.3 Allocate mitigation by marginal system-risk reduction, not activity volume

The operational consequence is to rank candidate mitigations by how much they move the system outcome. One concrete government application removes each risk from a Monte Carlo schedule model, measures the marginal change in the project finish-date distribution, and prioritizes the risks with the largest marginal impact ([GAO-10-189, “Prioritizing risks and risk mitigation”](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a299357.html)). GAO’s cost-estimating guide similarly says uncertainty analysis should identify the work-breakdown elements harboring the most risk so contingency reserve can be allocated where it can mitigate risk quickly ([GAO-09-3SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a77186.html)).

For a finite budget, the natural engineering extension is not merely \(\Delta R\), but marginal reduction per cost:

\[
priority(a) = \frac{R_{before} - E[R_{after}\mid a]}{incremental\ cost(a)}.
\]

That ratio is a design translation, not a claim that the cited NRC formulas prescribe this exact harness equation. Cost-effectiveness is nevertheless consistent with reliability-centered maintenance: DOE defines it as scheduled maintenance optimized through the relationship of preventive maintenance to reliability and the benefits of reliability to safety and cost reduction ([DOE O 433.1B self-study module, p. 16](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2013/06/f1/O-433-1B_ssm_0.pdf)).

### 1.4 Redundancy only buys reliability when failures are sufficiently independent

Reliability engineering distinguishes redundancy from diversity. NIST defines redundancy as replication that can support failover, voting, agreement, backup, or load balancing, but warns that redundant solutions remain susceptible to common-mode failure; diversity addresses that concern ([NIST SP 800-160 Vol. 1 Rev. 1, E.26](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-160v1r1.pdf)). NIST’s UTC service demonstrates the principle concretely: monitors differ in computers, buildings, operating systems, and administrators; alternate and contingency time scales are designed with progressively fewer common failure points; the emergency system accepts inferior performance in exchange for zero common points of failure, including geographic diversity ([NIST, Reliability of UTC(NIST)](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/time-realization/utcnist-time-scale/reliability-utcnist)).

This is not a slogan that “more diversity is always better.” Diversity has cost and may introduce new failure modes. The usable rule is: before crediting a second channel as independent protection, enumerate shared causes and discount the claimed risk reduction accordingly.

## 2. Concrete translation to an agent harness

### 2.1 The harness top events

The harness should not optimize token count directly. It should minimize owner-weighted answer loss subject to cost and latency constraints. A compact generic fault tree can begin with three top events:

1. **Wrong consequential action:** an incorrect or unauthorized external mutation is executed.
2. **Wrong consequential answer:** a material claim or recommendation is accepted despite being false, stale, unsupported, or misaligned with the owner’s objective.
3. **Failure to deliver:** the task exhausts time/quota, loops, or terminates without the requested artifact/outcome.

These are design categories, not an exhaustive empirical taxonomy. It is **likely (55-80%)** that these three cover enough loss mass to support an initial allocator because they separate integrity, action safety, and availability; evidence is conceptual alignment with fault-tree practice, which requires a specifically defined top event before causal expansion ([NASA Software Fault Tree Analysis](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVD/pages/102695720/8.07%2B-%2BSoftware%2BFault%2BTree%2BAnalysis)). **UNVERIFIED: settle by independently labeling 300 stratified historical owner tasks for realized and near-miss losses, then measuring the fraction that maps unambiguously to these categories and inter-rater agreement.**

Example pathways—not claims about current local implementation—include:

- wrong consequential answer = high-impact claim **AND** claim not externally checkable by the recipient **AND** evidence channel absent, failed, stale, or correlated with the generator;
- wrong consequential action = mutation attempted **AND** target resolution ambiguous or scope authorization absent **AND** no independent precondition check;
- failure to deliver = repeated action failure **AND** retry policy fails to change a causal variable **AND** budget is consumed before fallback or escalation.

The AND/OR structure is more useful than a flat “task difficulty” score. A trivial-looking task can carry high consequence; a hard but reversible brainstorming task can tolerate uncertainty. Difficulty predicts effort. Risk pathways predict the value of effort.

### 2.2 Candidate interventions, not fixed tiers

For each live pathway, the allocator considers a small catalog of existing-bus interventions:

- retrieve an authoritative source;
- execute a deterministic check or test;
- inspect the target state with an independent tool;
- ask the owner one focused question;
- switch model/configuration;
- generate one more candidate;
- commission a reviewer with a genuinely different evidence channel;
- narrow scope, use a safe fallback, or abstain;
- stop and return the baseline answer.

Each catalog entry has six fields:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `eligible_when` | Observable pathway predicates, not free-form model confidence alone |
| `cost` | Expected tokens, dollars/quota, latency, tool calls, and owner interruption |
| `effect` | Empirical probability of catching or repairing this failure class |
| `shared_causes` | Model, prompt, context, source, tool, verifier, network, or operator dependencies shared with existing evidence |
| `side_effects` | New failure modes, including contradictory edits or false-positive blocking |
| `evidence_grade` | Deterministic/externally observed, independent model judgment, same-model judgment, or unvalidated prior |

The catalog is intentionally small and auditable. It is not an ontology of every task.

### 2.3 The selection rule: risk-reduction worth per marginal cost

Let pathway \(j\) have estimated probability \(p_j\), owner-weighted consequence \(L_j\), and candidate action \(a\) have pathway-specific detection/repair effectiveness \(e_{aj}\). Let \(d_{aj}\in[0,1]\) be a diversity factor that discounts shared-cause evidence, and let \(s_a\) represent expected side-effect loss. A screening score is:

\[
MRW(a) = \frac{\sum_j p_j L_j e_{aj} d_{aj} - s_a}{cost(a)}.
\]

`MRW` means **marginal risk-reduction worth** here; it is a harness term, not an established reliability-engineering measure. The action with the highest positive score is eligible, subject to hard safety invariants and a per-task budget ceiling. After the action returns, update observable predicates and rescore. Stop when:

- no action clears a configured minimum marginal value;
- a deterministic success criterion is met;
- residual owner-weighted risk is below the task-class threshold;
- the budget ceiling is reached, in which case return the safest useful result with explicit uncertainty rather than silently overspending.

The initial \(p\), \(L\), \(e\), and \(d\) values must be coarse ordinal buckets or conservative tables, not fake decimal precision. The formula is a forcing function: it exposes why compute is being spent and what failure it is expected to change.

### 2.4 The paired portfolio: improvement budget and assurance floor

The RRW/RAW split maps to two budgets:

- **Improvement pool (RRW analogue):** optional extra compute goes to actions expected to reduce current residual answer loss most per unit cost.
- **Assurance floor (RAW analogue):** never remove cheap barriers whose absence sharply increases loss, even if they rarely fire. Examples include target identity checks before destructive action, schema validation before fan-out, and source/date validation for high-consequence unstable claims.

This avoids a common cost-control failure: optimizing away a verifier because its observed intervention rate is low. In reliability terms, low intervention frequency does not imply low RAW; the barrier may be the reason failure remains rare.

### 2.5 Common-cause discount: diversity is about failure mechanism, not headcount

Two model calls should receive little independence credit when they share the same model family, system prompt, context summary, retrieved source set, and judge. A useful diversity vector is:

`[generator, context lineage, evidence source, tool path, verifier, execution environment]`.

For each added channel, the allocator records which dimensions differ. The strongest economical pattern is often heterogeneous:

- model proposal + compiler/test;
- model factual claim + authoritative primary source;
- shell target resolution + filesystem metadata/API confirmation;
- generated structured output + independent schema validator;
- same-model second pass only when stochastic search, not independent verification, is the intended mechanism.

The allocator should not automatically buy another large-model opinion when a cheaper deterministic or external check attacks the failure cause more directly.

## 3. Proposals

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: hook-bus risk-directed compute gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a stateless or task-local policy function invoked at three existing lifecycle points: after task intake, after a failed/ambiguous tool result, and before an expensive escalation. Inputs are already observable task/action metadata; output is one of `continue-baseline`, `run-check:<id>`, `escalate:<id>`, `ask-owner`, or `stop-with-uncertainty` plus a reason record.

Minimal pseudocode:

```text
classify consequence and reversibility from explicit task/action facts
activate fault-path predicates from tool status, evidence status, and success criteria
enumerate eligible catalog actions
apply hard invariants (authorization, destructive target identity, mandatory evidence classes)
score remaining actions by coarse MRW bucket / cost bucket
discount shared-cause channels
take one action; observe; rescore
stop on verified success, low residual risk, low marginal value, or ceiling
```

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a rule/table implementation can capture useful gains without a learned router. Evidence: the relevant engineering mechanism is structured counterfactual prioritization, and the ICLR 2025 adaptive-compute result establishes that prompt-dependent allocation can outperform static allocation; uncertainty remains because this policy uses pathway signals rather than the paper’s reward-distribution predictor ([ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=6qUUgw9bAZ&noteId=wqcLJgfeyf)). **UNVERIFIED: settle with the shadow/A-B protocol in §6; adoption requires non-inferior quality with lower median and tail cost, or higher quality at matched cost.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It rides lifecycle hooks and the existing action bus; it adds no second runtime, durable scheduler, trained critic, vector/KG memory, SaaS, or guardrail framework. Keep policy data in a small versioned file and each decision in the ordinary task event stream.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: common-cause ledger for expensive verification

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Attach `failure_domain` metadata to existing verifier/action catalog entries: model family, context lineage, source origin, tool/backend, judge, and environment. Configure the allocator so a second channel advertised as “verification” must differ on at least one failure-relevant dimension; otherwise it is labeled “additional sampling” and evaluated on that basis.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this will prevent some quota from being spent on correlated agreement while preserving useful search. Evidence: NIST explicitly distinguishes redundant replication from diversity against common-mode failure; uncertainty remains because the effective correlation dimensions for this harness have not been measured ([NIST SP 800-160 Vol. 1 Rev. 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-160v1r1.pdf)). **UNVERIFIED: settle by the correlation experiment in §6, estimating conditional joint error by diversity vector and task class.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is metadata and a selection constraint on existing channels, not a multi-agent framework.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: RAW-style assurance minima

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Define a short list of high-RAW barriers by task/action class. Examples: destructive mutations require independent target resolution; medical/legal/financial and other unstable high-consequence claims require current authoritative evidence; fan-out structured responses require schema preflight; a retry after a repeated identical error must change a causal variable or choose a fallback. These minima consume a reserved slice of task budget before optional exploration.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicitly reserving budget for critical barriers is safer than allocating solely by observed firing frequency. Evidence: NRC defines RAW specifically to prioritize features important to reliability assurance and maintenance, while NIST reliability models show system outcomes depend on series/parallel structure, not component event counts alone ([NRC NUREG/CR-3385](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/nuregs/contract/cr3385/index), [NIST §8.1.8](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr18.htm)). **UNVERIFIED: settle by barrier-ablation replay on labeled historical tasks plus a prospective canary where barriers log counterfactual decisions but cannot block execution.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes if the list remains short, evidence-based, and implemented through existing pre-action/pre-response configuration. A sprawling universal guardrail taxonomy would fail the gate.

## 4. What this catches that uniform cost control misses

These are prospective failure classes, not claims that the current harness exhibits them.

1. **High token use with no live risk pathway.** A verbose or apparently difficult request may be safely reversible and already satisfy deterministic criteria. Uniform deep reasoning continues spending; MRW stops when the next action cannot plausibly change the result.

2. **Low apparent difficulty but high consequence.** A one-line destructive command or factual answer can have a short solution and severe loss. Difficulty routing under-spends; consequence/pathway routing buys target or source validation.

3. **Repeated same-cause verification.** Three agreeing model copies can inherit the same stale source, prompt assumption, context corruption, or judge. Headcount routing calls this three checks; the common-cause ledger discounts it and prefers a compiler, test, API, filesystem probe, or independent primary source.

4. **Frequent but buffered faults.** A noisy tool may fail often yet have a cheap reliable fallback. Call-count ranking may overinvest there; a fault tree can show low contribution to owner-visible loss.

5. **Rare barrier loss.** A validator may almost never reject output because upstream behavior is good. Frequency-only pruning removes it; RAW-style analysis protects it if forced loss would sharply raise top-event risk.

6. **Retry loops that do not alter a cause.** Resubmitting the same action after the same fingerprint is nominal activity but not risk reduction. The intervention catalog requires a causal-variable change, alternative channel, or stop.

7. **Verifier or evidence-channel degradation.** A generator can appear healthy while the independent check path is unavailable. The assurance floor treats check-path health as a protected function and chooses a diverse fallback or reports residual uncertainty.

8. **Budget spent after answer saturation.** Adaptive-compute research reports that optimal test-time strategy varies by prompt difficulty and budget rather than uniformly scaling every prompt ([ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1b623663fd9b874366f3ce019fdfdd44-Paper-Conference.pdf)). The harness analogue stops on marginal value, not a fixed reviewer count.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that at least classes 1, 2, 3, and 8 occur in any sufficiently heterogeneous agent workload; evidence is structural plus adaptive-compute research, not owner-specific observation. **UNVERIFIED: settle by coding one month of task traces for each class using two independent reviewers and measuring cost/loss contribution.**

## 5. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 5.1 Why the structure transfers

The analogy has four genuine structural correspondences:

| Reliability system | Agent harness |
|---|---|
| Finite maintenance/test/design budget | Finite tokens, quota, latency, tool calls, and owner attention |
| Top event is a system outcome, not a component metric | Owner-visible wrong action/answer/non-delivery, not token count or individual tool error |
| Mitigations differ in effect on causal pathways | Search, tests, reviewers, model escalation, and clarification address different answer-failure mechanisms |
| Redundant components may share a cause | Multiple samples/reviewers may share model, context, source, tool, or judge failures |

Those correspondences are enough to borrow the questions and bookkeeping: define loss, expose pathways, estimate counterfactual effect, preserve high-RAW barriers, discount common causes, and allocate marginally.

### 5.2 Why literal PRA does not transfer

The disanalogies are substantial:

1. **Probability data are weak.** Engineered components can accumulate controlled test and field-failure data. Agent tasks are open-ended, labels are sparse, and the distribution shifts with models, tools, prompts, and owner behavior. NIST notes that even conventional reliability inference becomes difficult when failures are rare; precision depends strongly on observed failures, not merely sample size ([NIST §8.1.3.2](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr132.htm)). Harness probabilities will often be less defensible.

2. **Basic events are not stable components.** A prompt, tool result, model policy, and context interact. An “intervention” can change the reasoning trajectory and introduce new faults, violating a clean component counterfactual.

3. **Independence is usually unknown.** The NASA fault-tree handbook’s simple product/sum approximations require small probabilities and suitable assumptions; it warns to use more accurate treatment when cut-set probabilities are high ([NASA Fault Tree Handbook, chapter 7](https://extapps.ksc.nasa.gov/reliability/Documents/Fault_Tree_Handbook_with_Aerospace_Applications_August_2002.pdf)). Model/verifier dependence is both strong and hard to observe.

4. **Loss is normative and task-specific.** Hardware safety analyses can anchor on physical harm metrics. An agent harness must encode owner preference, reversibility, opportunity cost, latency, and the value of creativity. A universal scalar loss can conceal rather than resolve tradeoffs.

5. **The allocator can Goodhart its signals.** If model-written “confidence” unlocks extra compute, the system may learn or drift toward producing the signal. Observable checks and capped coarse buckets reduce but do not eliminate this.

6. **Verification is not repair.** Finding disagreement can consume more compute without identifying truth. Reliability maintenance often has a concrete component action; open-domain adjudication may remain unresolved.

7. **Novel tasks may have no known fault tree.** FTA begins with a known top event and credible pathways. NASA explicitly says the top hazard must be known before analysis ([NASA Software Fault Tree Analysis](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVD/pages/102695720/8.07%2B-%2BSoftware%2BFault%2BTree%2BAnalysis)). Creative research can fail in ways no catalog anticipates.

Therefore the design must not claim calibrated “probability of correctness” for each live answer. It should use coarse, revisable screening categories and direct deterministic evidence where available. The score recommends the next check; it does not certify safety.

### 5.3 Rejected borrows

- **Rejected: multiply FMEA severity × occurrence × detectability into one Risk Priority Number.** Different triples can yield the same product, ordinal categories do not justify arithmetic precision, and the score ignores system cut-set structure. This would create a neat but brittle compute router.
- **Rejected: MTBF as the primary harness objective.** Agent tasks and repairable tool services do not share one meaningful lifetime distribution. NIST explicitly distinguishes rate of occurrence of failures for repairable systems from first-failure/hazard terminology for non-repairable populations ([NIST §8.1.2.1](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr121.htm)). Cost control needs owner loss per budget, not time between heterogeneous incidents.
- **Rejected: N-version agents as default redundancy.** Merely cloning an agent does not establish independent failure modes. Diversity must target an identified common cause and justify its incremental cost.
- **Rejected: full quantitative fault-tree service.** It would require taxonomy maintenance, calibration infrastructure, and likely a new runtime while offering spurious precision under distribution shift. That violates the anti-overfit gate.
- **Rejected: learned/trained critic or router for v1.** It introduces training, drift, and evaluation machinery before demonstrating that a transparent coarse policy has value. This is one of the explicitly rejected component classes in the program brief.

## 6. Verification program

### 6.1 Phase 0: detector controls and labeling

Before claiming any pathway, verifier outage, or common-cause condition is absent or present, validate each detector against:

1. a known-positive synthetic/canary event it must find; and
2. a mechanically independent signal, such as provider status plus tool error, compiler result plus test result, or filesystem metadata plus API response.

Re-running the same query is not independent. This directly implements the unit’s required two-signal rule.

Build a stratified evaluation set spanning low/high consequence, reversible/irreversible actions, factual research, coding, open-ended design, tool-heavy execution, and known retry/error cases. Labels need externally checkable success criteria where possible and two independent human adjudicators for normative cases.

### 6.2 Shadow mode

Run the allocator without changing execution. For every point where it would spend or stop, record:

- active pathways and evidence signals;
- chosen action and next-best alternative;
- estimated cost/effect/diversity buckets;
- actual downstream cost;
- whether the proposed action later proved necessary, useful, useless, or harmful.

Promotion criteria must be pre-registered. Suggested minimum: adequate coverage of each major task stratum; bounded false-stop rate on consequential tasks; stable decisions under harmless prompt paraphrase; and no reliance on an unvalidated detector.

### 6.3 Matched-budget experiment

Randomize tasks within strata among:

- current/default policy;
- uniform extra-compute policy at the same aggregate budget;
- risk-directed policy at the same aggregate budget.

Primary outcomes: externally scored task success and severity-weighted loss. Secondary outcomes: tokens/dollars, wall time, tool calls, owner interruptions, catastrophic/irreversible near misses, and p95 cost. Report Pareto frontiers, not a single blended score. The key causal contrast is whether allocation changes *which tasks* receive compute and improves quality at fixed total budget.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that matched aggregate budget and task stratification are necessary to distinguish better allocation from simply spending more. Evidence: the target question is explicitly allocation under finite budget, and the adaptive-compute literature evaluates prompt-dependent strategies under a given test-time compute budget ([ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=4FWAwZtd2n)). The exact sample size is **UNVERIFIED: settle with a power analysis using shadow-mode outcome variance and the pre-registered minimum practically important effect.**

### 6.4 Common-cause experiment

For labeled tasks with a known answer or executable oracle, obtain paired verifier judgments under controlled variations:

1. same model, prompt, context, sources, and judge;
2. different sample only;
3. different model family;
4. different source/evidence channel;
5. deterministic executable oracle where available.

Estimate individual error rates and joint conditional error, not just disagreement. A diverse channel is valuable when it reduces joint false acceptance after accounting for cost and its own side effects. This experiment calibrates `d`, and can reveal that some nominal diversity dimensions do not matter.

### 6.5 Barrier ablation

Replay labeled tasks with each proposed high-RAW barrier logically removed while leaving other policy constant. Measure the change in severity-weighted top-event loss. In prospective operation, use canary/shadow decisions rather than removing safety barriers from real consequential actions. Require two mechanically independent signals before declaring a barrier unused, broken, or redundant.

### 6.6 Kill criteria

Stop or substantially redesign the borrow if any of these occurs:

- coarse risk scores are not stable across independent labelers;
- the allocator’s gain disappears at matched aggregate budget;
- false stops or missed high-consequence checks exceed the pre-registered bound;
- common-cause metadata does not predict joint error better than a simple cost-only baseline;
- policy evaluation overhead consumes the savings;
- maintenance grows beyond a small hook-bus implementation;
- task distribution shift makes calibration decay faster than it can be cheaply refreshed.

This is important: the experiment can validate “adaptive allocation helps” while falsifying the reliability-shaped mechanism. If a simpler observable rule—such as deterministic-check-first plus a hard consequence gate—matches it, choose the simpler rule.
