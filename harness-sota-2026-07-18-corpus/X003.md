# X003 — Clinical medicine → verification

## Executive result

The borrow transfers, but only in a narrower and more useful form than “make the agent think like a doctor.” The strongest mechanism is a **diagnostic-accuracy protocol for claims**: treat the agent's assertion as an *index test*, define an external reference standard before looking at the assertion, preserve a differential of plausible failure modes, select mechanism-independent tests, and continue surveillance after acceptance. This combines four established clinical mechanisms:

1. differential diagnosis and “can't-miss” hypothesis elimination;
2. prior-aware interpretation of diagnostic tests;
3. controls against incorporation, review, and partial-verification bias;
4. pharmacovigilance plus absolute-benefit accounting (NNT), so rare and interaction-driven failures feed back into future checks and verification cost is measured against errors actually prevented.

The transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce false “done/correct/verified” claims if limited to consequential, mechanically checkable claims. Evidence: the clinical source mechanisms directly address premature closure, imperfect tests, circular reference standards, selective verification, and harms discovered only after deployment; these have exact structural counterparts in agent verification. This is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; settle it with the preregistered replay and prospective shadow experiment specified in §9.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to establish truth for subjective, normative, or open-world claims. Evidence: medicine can often define a target condition and a best-available reference standard, whereas “best design,” “complete research,” and many semantic software claims have no stable gold standard. Those claims need scoped warrants and explicit residual uncertainty, not a binary verified badge.

The recommended architecture is deliberately small: a claim-evidence contract in existing instructions/configuration, one hook-bus validator, and an append-only adverse-verification registry. It adds no second orchestrator, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. The clinical mechanisms in their own field

### 1.1 Differential diagnosis is a maintained set of rival explanations, not a decorative list

Clinical diagnosis is an iterative process of information gathering and reasoning, and the diagnosis may remain a working explanation rather than a final fact. The National Academies' consensus report describes diagnosis as a complex, collaborative activity and emphasizes that an inaccurate or delayed diagnosis can cause harm through delayed appropriate treatment or unnecessary/harmful treatment ([National Academies, *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care*](https://www.nationalacademies.org/projects/IOM-HCS-13-03/publication/21794)). Its patient-facing material makes the anti-closure move concrete: ask “What else could it be?”, ensure test results are received, and monitor whether the course matches the diagnosis ([National Academies interactive report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/21794/interactive/)).

Emergency medicine adds a useful asymmetry. For high-risk decisions, clinicians may use hypothesis elimination: rule out dangerous “can't miss” diagnoses rather than merely accumulate support for the most likely diagnosis ([AHRQ, ED-to-hospital transitions](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxsafety-care-transitions2.html)). This matters because a common benign explanation can be most probable while a less probable dangerous explanation still controls the next test or disposition.

The important mechanism is therefore:

- maintain multiple candidate causes;
- distinguish “most likely” from “must exclude”;
- choose observations that discriminate among candidates;
- revise the working diagnosis when new evidence is discordant;
- arrange follow-up, because a snapshot can be wrong even when the initial process was reasonable.

This is not equivalent to asking for more chain-of-thought. It is a control over *which rival hypotheses remain live* and *what evidence could falsify the leader*.

### 1.2 Diagnostic evidence changes a prior; a test result is not truth by itself

Clinical test interpretation combines pretest probability with test performance. The classic probabilistic account states that pretest probability is required to calculate posttest probability and that a test is valuable when it changes management; a result that barely changes the probability may not be worth obtaining ([NCBI Bookshelf, *The Use of Diagnostic Tests: A Probabilistic Approach*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)). Formally:

`post-test odds = pretest odds × likelihood ratio`

A positive result can occur without disease and a negative result with disease. The same source also states a critical assumption: sequential multiplication is unsafe when tests are not conditionally independent. Two correlated tests do not supply two independent updates merely because they are two commands.

This produces three clinically important disciplines:

1. specify what condition is being tested;
2. know the test's false-positive and false-negative behavior in a relevant population;
3. stop testing when the result cannot change the decision.

### 1.3 Diagnostic-accuracy research has named the circularity traps

Medicine distinguishes the **index test** under evaluation from a **reference standard**, the best available way to determine the target condition. QUADAS-2 and AHRQ's medical-test methods guide expose several biases that map unusually cleanly to agent claims:

- **Incorporation bias:** the index test is used to establish the final diagnosis. If an agent's own statement, generated artifact, or self-authored expectation participates in deciding that the statement is true, verification is circular.
- **Review bias:** interpretation of one test is influenced by knowing the result of the other. If a verifier is shown the agent's conclusion and rationale before inspecting raw evidence, it can anchor on the proposed answer.
- **Partial verification bias:** only a selected subset receives the reference standard. Checking only successful-looking outputs, only changed files, or only agent-flagged risky claims systematically hides false negatives.
- **Differential verification bias:** different cases receive different reference standards. A weak check for inconvenient cases and a strong check for easy cases makes aggregate accuracy uninterpretable.
- **Arbitrary-threshold bias:** choosing a cutoff after seeing results gives optimistic performance that may fail out of sample.
- **Indeterminate-result exclusion:** silently dropping inconclusive cases makes a test appear more accurate.

These definitions and their effects are explicit in the [AHRQ Methods Guide for Medical Test Reviews](https://effectivehealthcare.ahrq.gov/products/methods-guidance-tests-bias/methods) and its [QUADAS-2 question table](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK98233/table/ch5.t2/?report=objectonly). The table asks whether index and reference results were interpreted without knowledge of each other, whether thresholds were prespecified, whether all participants received a reference standard, whether they received the same one, and whether all were included in analysis. STARD 2015 similarly requires enough detail to replicate both index test and reference standard, the rationale for choosing the reference standard, prespecified versus exploratory cutoffs, what information each assessor saw, and handling of indeterminate and missing results ([STARD explanation and checklist](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5128957/)).

This is the highest-value borrow. “Use two checks” is insufficient. Medicine asks whether the checks are blind, independent of the claim, consistently applied, and evaluated against an external outcome.

### 1.4 Iatrogenics and contraindication registries treat combinations as first-class hazards

Clinical safety does not assume an individually useful intervention remains safe in every context. Prescription-drug labeling separates contraindications and drug interactions, and FDA maintains specific guidance for both ([FDA drug-interaction labeling resources](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-interactions-labeling/drug-interactions-relevant-regulatory-guidance-and-policy-documents)). The conceptual move is to encode dangerous *combinations and contexts*, not just defective components.

The failure mode of this mechanism is equally instructive. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 studies estimated that 90% of drug-drug interaction alerts were overridden, with extreme heterogeneity (`I² = 100%`) ([Felisberto et al., PubMed PMID 38899788](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38899788/)). This does not prove every system will have a 90% override rate; it shows that broad, poorly contextualized registries can create alert fatigue and lose authority. AHRQ therefore emphasizes patient- and situation-specific alerts rather than simple pair matching ([AHRQ, meaningful DDI decision support](https://digital.ahrq.gov/program-overview/research-stories/creating-meaningful-decision-support-reduce-drug-drug)).

### 1.5 Pharmacovigilance separates a signal from causation

Some harms are too rare, delayed, or context-dependent to appear before approval. FDA explains that preapproval testing occurs in relatively small groups and some problems emerge only after broad use, motivating postmarket safety reporting ([FDA postmarketing adverse-event compliance](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/surveillance-post-drug-approval-activities/postmarketing-adverse-event-reporting-compliance-program)). Just as important, an adverse event does not by itself establish that the drug caused it. FDA's current safety-signal page says a listed product-risk pair is a potential signal under evaluation, not a causal conclusion; later evaluation can lead to no action, labeling changes, risk controls, or more data ([FDA adverse-event safety signals](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-adverse-event-monitoring-system-aems/new-safety-information-or-potential-signals-serious-risks-identified-fda-adverse-event-monitoring)).

The mechanism is a state machine:

`report → signal → evaluate against alternatives and exposure → confirm / refute / gather data → update controls`

It preserves weak evidence without prematurely converting correlation into a hard contraindication.

### 1.6 NNT forces absolute, comparator-specific benefit accounting

Number needed to treat (NNT) is the expected number treated with an intervention rather than a comparator for one additional beneficial or harmful outcome over a stated time. It is derived from absolute risk difference, is comparator-specific, and is not a timeless property of the treatment ([Cochrane Handbook §15.4](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)). Cochrane warns that relative effects can look dramatic while absolute effects are small, and that absolute effects depend on baseline risk. It recommends clear direction and timeframe and prefers NNTB/NNTH terminology over ambiguous “NNT/NNH.”

The transferable discipline is not the reciprocal formula alone. It is: compare against a baseline, count outcomes in natural frequencies, include harms and burden, state the time horizon, and do not generalize an absolute benefit across populations with different baseline risk.

## 2. Concrete translation: the Claim Diagnostic Protocol

For a consequential agent claim such as “the defect is fixed,” instantiate the clinical roles as follows:

| Clinical role | Harness role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Patient/case | task execution and resulting workspace/external state | the exact checkout, environment, inputs, and produced changes |
| Target condition | sharply defined proposition | “with input X, version Y returns Z and preserves invariant Q” |
| Index test | agent's claim and cited evidence | “tests pass; therefore fixed” |
| Differential diagnosis | rival explanations for the evidence | genuine fix; stale binary; wrong target; skipped test; mocked path; cached output; hidden regression |
| Can't-miss diagnosis | low-frequency, high-damage failure mode | data loss, security bypass, action against wrong account/repository |
| Reference standard | best available outcome not derived from the claim | fresh black-box reproduction, direct state query, independent parser, externally observed side effect |
| Contraindication | evidence combination that cannot justify the claim | test fixture copied from implementation; same cached artifact read twice; verifier shown expected answer before adjudication |
| Follow-up | delayed outcome check | rerun after clean rebuild/restart, inspect downstream consumer, monitor later failure reports |
| Adverse event | later contradiction or harm | user correction, rollback, failed deployment, wrong-recipient action |

The operational sequence is:

1. **Atomize the claim.** Split “done” into propositions with explicit subject, predicate, scope, environment, and time. “All tests pass” is different from “the defect is fixed.”
2. **Declare the decision and harm.** What action will rely on the claim? What is the worst plausible false acceptance?
3. **Write the differential before inspecting supportive evidence.** Include the genuine-success hypothesis, common artifact hypotheses, and at least one task-specific can't-miss hypothesis for high-impact actions.
4. **Choose the reference method before seeing the claimed result.** Prespecify pass/fail/indeterminate criteria. Prefer a black-box observable or authoritative state over the implementation's own representation.
5. **Apply at least two mechanically independent signals for absence/broken/done claims.** Independence is by failure mechanism and source of truth, not command count. A test and its log parser are one causal channel; a test plus direct downstream observation can be two.
6. **Blind where practical.** Give the reference checker the target proposition, environment, and raw inputs, but withhold the agent's conclusion and persuasive rationale until it records its result.
7. **Record discordance and indeterminacy.** Do not coerce “could not inspect,” timeout, missing permission, or ambiguous semantics into pass.
8. **Decide using risk.** High-damage can't-miss hypotheses require exclusion or explicit owner acceptance; low-impact claims can use sampling.
9. **Follow up and learn.** Later contradictions become signals, not instant proof of verifier failure. Adjudicate, then update a scoped contraindication or test-performance estimate.

## 3. What this catches that ordinary “run tests and review” misses

### Circular success

Example: the implementation emits a structured file; the test imports the same serializer and compares against an expected structure produced by that serializer. Both agree because they share the defect. The protocol labels this incorporation/common-method bias and requires a separately implemented parser, black-box consumer, or fixed external fixture.

This catch is **very likely (80-90%)** when provenance is available, because shared generators/imports are mechanically detectable and QUADAS-2's independence rule maps directly. This remains **UNVERIFIED** for actual harness traces; settle it by seeding shared-oracle mutants in the §9 replay.

### “Two signals” that are really one

Example: a command exits 0 and its own log says PASS. Both descend from the same process and can fail together. The protocol records one causal channel, then asks for an orthogonal observation such as inspecting the produced artifact with another implementation or querying the consumer.

This catch is **likely (55-80%)**, because explicit provenance can identify direct dependencies, but hidden common libraries, caches, and environment state can preserve correlation. Evidence: the clinical probabilistic source explicitly warns that sequential tests require conditional independence. **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with fault injection against shared cache, shared parser, and stale-build scenarios.

### Premature closure after a plausible pass

Example: a unit test passes, so the agent stops considering “tested the wrong worktree,” “test was deselected,” or “deployed binary is stale.” A precommitted differential keeps these hypotheses alive until discriminating observations address them.

This catch is **likely (55-80%)** for recurrent, enumerable artifacts and **unlikely (20-45%)** for genuinely novel failure modes. Evidence: differential diagnosis counters premature closure but cannot enumerate unknown unknowns. **UNVERIFIED**; settle both estimates by comparing seeded known-pattern and novel-pattern defects in §9.

### Selective verification and success-only metrics

Example: only claims that agents label uncertain receive expensive checks, while confident false claims escape; or only completed tasks are scored, excluding timeouts and indeterminate runs. The protocol requires a random audit sample across passes, failures, and confidence bands, and retains indeterminate outcomes.

This catch is **very likely (80-90%)** if sampling is enforced outside the claimant, because the selection path is directly observable. Evidence: AHRQ identifies partial verification and removal of indeterminate results as named sources of optimistic bias. **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by measuring false-accept rate on a random reference-standard sample versus claimant-selected samples.

### Interaction-specific verification hazards

Example pairs for a scoped contraindication registry:

- claim “remote change succeeded” + evidence “local CLI exited 0,” without direct remote-state read;
- claim “absence” + evidence from a detector that has not demonstrated a known-positive control;
- claim “content is current” + evidence from a cache without freshness metadata;
- claim “safe deletion target” + path resolved through an unvalidated variable/glob;
- claim “all cases” + reference check applied only to agent-selected cases;
- claim “independent confirmation” + both checks share the same parser, generated fixture, model-produced oracle, or cached artifact;
- claim about an external side effect + verifier cannot observe the external system.

A contextual registry is **likely (55-80%)** to catch repeated known combinations and **very unlikely (5-20%)** to catch a new semantic failure before any rule or signal exists. Evidence: drug-interaction systems are designed for known combinations, while pharmacovigilance exists precisely because predeployment knowledge is incomplete. Both forecasts are **UNVERIFIED**; settle them by replaying known fingerprints and a held-out set of newly adjudicated failures.

## 4. Proposal A — Claim-evidence contract — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Require consequential completion claims to emit a compact manifest:

```json
{
  "claim": "subject + predicate + scope + environment + time",
  "impact": "low|medium|high|irreversible",
  "decision_if_accepted": "...",
  "differential": [
    {"hypothesis": "genuine success", "status": "supported"},
    {"hypothesis": "wrong target", "status": "excluded", "evidence_id": "e2"},
    {"hypothesis": "stale artifact", "status": "indeterminate"}
  ],
  "evidence": [
    {
      "id": "e2",
      "source": "direct remote query",
      "mechanism": "external-state-read",
      "observed": "...",
      "freshness": "2026-07-18T...Z",
      "reference_standard": true,
      "claim_blinded": true
    }
  ],
  "residual_uncertainty": ["stale artifact not excluded"],
  "verdict": "verified|supported|indeterminate|contradicted"
}
```

The manifest does not request hidden reasoning. It requests auditable propositions, observations, provenance, and unresolved alternatives. A verdict of `verified` means the prespecified reference check passed and required contraindications are absent; `supported` means evidence raises confidence but no adequate reference standard exists.

This contract is **likely (55-80%)** to improve reviewability and reduce semantic inflation from “supported” to “verified,” because missing scope, provenance, and indeterminate alternatives become explicit fields. Boilerplate compliance without substantive evidence is also **likely (55-80%)**, because the fields can be populated textually without changing the underlying observations. Both claims are **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the blinded replay in §9, including a measure of manifest completion without substantive evidence.

Anti-overfit gate: passes. It is configuration on the existing instruction/output path. It introduces no orchestration runtime or external memory system.

## 5. Proposal B — Contextual verification-contraindication hook — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build one deterministic validator on the existing completion/claim event. It reads the manifest and a versioned, human-readable registry. It does not judge truth with another model. Initial rules should be few and high-specificity:

1. `verified` + no reference-standard evidence → block the label, downgrade to `supported`;
2. absence/broken/done + fewer than two distinct mechanism classes → require another signal or `indeterminate`;
3. evidence source equals generated oracle/source-of-truth → incorporation-bias warning or block for high impact;
4. claim concerns external state + no direct external observation → block `verified`;
5. high/irreversible impact + no can't-miss hypothesis → require explicit hypothesis;
6. missing/timeout/inaccessible result encoded as pass → block;
7. reference criteria created after result timestamp → mark exploratory, not confirmatory.

Each registry entry should have: scoped claim type; evidence pair/context; severity; action (`note`, `downgrade`, `block`); rationale; examples; first/last observed; status (`signal`, `confirmed`, `retired`); owner; and expiry/review date. Only `confirmed` high-severity rules may interrupt. `signal` entries remain noninterruptive and collect outcomes.

The hook is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent a useful subset of circular or under-evidenced completion labels because its initial rules are structural and deterministic. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to verify substantive correctness by itself; it validates the evidence design, not the world. Both claims are **UNVERIFIED**; settle them with seeded structural violations and held-out real claims in §9.

Alert-fatigue constraint: if override/downgrade rates are high, do not add friction or a password. Measure precision, suppress low-value rules, scope by claim type and impact, and convert noisy rules to noninterruptive telemetry. The 90% pooled DDI override result is a warning against an encyclopedic registry, not a performance target.

Anti-overfit gate: passes. One small hook and a flat registry ride the existing bus. No daemon, classifier, guardrail framework, or second runtime is proposed.

## 6. Proposal C — Adverse-verification surveillance and “number needed to verify” — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Append later contradictions to a local event stream keyed by claim type and verification pattern. Candidate sources include owner corrections, rollbacks, downstream test/deploy failures, and direct state contradictions. Preserve them as `signal`, not `caused_by_verifier`. A small adjudication record asks:

- Was the original proposition false at the claimed time and scope?
- Did the reference check fail, was it absent, or was the target condition ill-defined?
- Is there a plausible competing cause (environment drift, later change, external outage)?
- Does this recur with the same evidence combination?
- Should the registry entry be confirmed, narrowed, retired, or left as a signal?

For each claim class and verification bundle, report natural frequencies over a fixed horizon:

- false acceptances per 1,000 accepted claims under baseline policy;
- false acceptances per 1,000 under the new policy;
- additional indeterminate/delayed completions per 1,000;
- median tool calls and wall time added;
- high-impact false acceptances separately from low-impact ones.

Define **NNV-B** (number needed to verify for one additional false acceptance prevented) as `1 / absolute reduction in false-accept probability`, with direction, comparator, claim population, and time window always stated. Define an analogous **NNV-H** for one additional materially delayed or wrongly blocked completion. Do not pool NNVs across claim classes with different baseline risk, and do not compute it when randomization/adjustment does not support a causal comparison. This follows Cochrane's warning that NNT is comparator- and baseline-risk-dependent.

Risk-tier the policy using absolute outcomes:

- high/irreversible impact: verify every claim against the strongest available standard;
- medium impact: verify required structural rules plus a random reference audit;
- low impact: sample, unless a confirmed contraindication fires.

This feedback loop is **likely (55-80%)** to identify recurrent post-acceptance failure patterns that predeployment checks miss, because it explicitly collects delayed contradictions. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to establish causality from passive reports alone, because reporting and exposure are selective. Evidence: FDA explicitly separates adverse-event signals from causal conclusions. Both are **UNVERIFIED**; settle them with adjudicated cohorts and randomized/stepped-wedge policy comparisons in §9.

Anti-overfit gate: passes if implemented as an append-only local file plus existing-hook summarizer. It fails the gate if expanded into a durable workflow service or observability platform; that expansion is not recommended.

## 7. Why the analogy transfers—and where it breaks

### Structural correspondences that hold

1. **Both domains decide under incomplete observation.** A claim and a diagnosis are propositions about latent state inferred through fallible observations.
2. **Tests have characteristic blind spots.** Unit tests, logs, exit codes, screenshots, and state queries can each produce false reassurance, just as clinical tests have false positives and negatives.
3. **Evidence channels can be dependent.** Shared parsers, fixtures, caches, models, credentials, and targets are the harness equivalent of correlated clinical tests.
4. **Premature closure is costly.** Once a plausible conclusion is accepted, contradictory explanations stop receiving tests.
5. **Rare/contextual harms appear after release.** Some errors emerge only in a particular tool sequence, environment, or downstream consumer; a signal registry fits this temporal structure.
6. **Verification has harms and opportunity costs.** Extra checks consume time/tool calls and can block correct work, so absolute benefit versus burden matters.

These correspondences make transfer **very likely (80-90%)** at the level of experimental design and evidence hygiene. Evidence: each maps a named clinical bias or surveillance mechanism to an isomorphic information-flow failure. This claim is still **UNVERIFIED** in harness outcomes; the §9 experiments test whether structural similarity produces practical benefit.

### Disanalogies that prohibit a literal transfer

1. **No stable disease ontology.** Clinical conditions and endpoints are imperfect but often standardized. Agent claims are open-ended and can change meaning with task context.
2. **No validated likelihood ratios yet.** Clinical tests can be studied across many patients. Harness evidence types vary by repository, tool, and claim. Assigning “90% confidence” to a passing test would be fabricated precision.
3. **Reference standards are often absent.** A remote state query can ground “file exists,” but no black-box test fully grounds “best architecture” or “complete research.”
4. **The agent can shape both specimen and test.** It writes implementation, tests, fixtures, and explanation, creating stronger incorporation bias than is typical in clinical diagnostics.
5. **Cases are nonstationary and adversarial.** Software and external services change rapidly; prevalence and test accuracy drift.
6. **A second model is not an independent laboratory.** Same-family models share training, prompts, and reasoning tendencies. Multi-agent voting is therefore not automatically independent evidence.
7. **Verification can change the artifact.** Some checks mutate state or consume one-shot operations, unlike many observational medical tests.

Consequences:

- Do not compute Bayesian posteriors until claim-specific priors and likelihood ratios have been prospectively calibrated on held-out outcomes.
- Do not equate two commands, two agents, or two textual explanations with two independent signals.
- Do not issue `verified` for claims without an operational target condition and best-available external reference.
- Do not force binary verdicts; `indeterminate` is a first-class safe outcome.
- Do not build a universal contraindication catalog. Entries must be scoped, evidence-linked, expiring, and monitored for alert fatigue.

The numeric-Bayes extension is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** justified at initial deployment. Evidence: no claim-class priors, likelihood ratios, independence estimates, or calibration cohort are supplied in this unit. It becomes eligible only after the prospective experiment produces those quantities.

## 8. Rejected borrows and dead ends

### “Differential diagnosis prompt” alone — rejected

A generic instruction to list three alternatives is **likely (55-80%)** to generate plausible prose without changing evidence collection. Evidence: AHRQ's review of clinical-reasoning checklists concludes that evidence is thin and diagnostic checklists were not ready for clinical practice ([AHRQ issue brief on clinical-reasoning checklists](https://www.ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/wysiwyg/patient-safety/reports/issue-briefs/diagnostic-error-reduction.pdf)). The harness extrapolation is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by comparing evidence actions, not list completeness, in the §9 replay. The useful control is not list length; it is prespecified rival hypotheses tied to discriminating, independent observations.

### “Two independent agents agree” — rejected

Agreement is an index test, not a reference standard. Same-family agents are **very likely (80-90%)** to share important model priors, supplied context, and persuasive framing because those inputs are literally common; hidden correlation magnitude is **UNVERIFIED** and is settled by Experiment 3. Without blinding and an external outcome, consensus is **likely (55-80%)** to amplify incorporation and review bias when the original rationale is wrong; evidence is the shared information path, and the settling experiment is blinded versus unblinded replay.

### Universal Bayesian truth score — rejected

The equation transfers; the inputs do not. Without measured base rates and test characteristics, a posterior is numerology. Use categorical warrants and calibration data first.

### Block every registry match — rejected

Clinical DDI evidence shows severe override/alert-fatigue problems. Only confirmed, high-severity, contextual rules should interrupt; signals should collect telemetry. A high override rate is evidence to narrow or demote a rule, not to add a stronger modal.

### Verify everything exhaustively — rejected

It ignores NNT's absolute-benefit discipline and the anti-overfit gate. Low-risk claims need sampling; high-impact and irreversible claims justify stronger coverage. Selective sampling must be random or policy-defined rather than claimant-selected, to avoid partial-verification bias.

### New verifier daemon / knowledge graph / trained critic — rejected

Those components violate the stated anti-overfit gate and are unnecessary for the proposed information-flow controls. A flat registry, manifest, and existing hook are sufficient to test the hypothesis.

## 9. Experiments that settle the UNVERIFIED premises

### Experiment 1: preregistered retrospective claim replay

1. Randomly sample consequential completion claims across claim types and confidence language; do not select on known outcome.
2. Freeze a reference-standard rubric per claim type before exposing adjudicators to agent conclusions.
3. Give two adjudicators raw workspace/external evidence without the agent's verdict or rationale; resolve disagreements with a third adjudicator.
4. Label target condition as true, false, or indeterminate at the original time and scope.
5. Run baseline policy and the proposed manifest/hook in replay.
6. Seed additional mutants for wrong target, stale artifact, skipped test, shared oracle, missing external observation, and coerced indeterminate.
7. Report sensitivity for false claims, specificity for true claims, indeterminate rate, results by claim class, and confidence intervals. Include all sampled cases.

Success criterion should be prespecified rather than chosen after results. A reasonable initial decision rule is **UNVERIFIED**: adopt only if the lower confidence bound shows a material reduction in high-impact false acceptances without an owner-defined unacceptable increase in delay/indeterminate outcomes. The experiment must set “material” and “unacceptable” before replay.

### Experiment 2: prospective shadow mode, then randomized or stepped-wedge activation

1. Run the hook noninterruptively for a fixed horizon; collect would-block events, overrides, latency, and later contradictions.
2. Adjudicate a random sample of non-alerted claims as well as alerts; this prevents partial verification.
3. Estimate rule precision and false-negative rate. Retire or narrow noisy entries.
4. Randomize eligible sessions or roll out by prespecified stepped wedges, stratified by claim impact/type.
5. Compare absolute false-accept and delay rates; compute NNV-B/NNV-H only where causal comparison is defensible.
6. Re-evaluate after environment/model changes; do not assume transportability.

The shadow-first sequence is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal noisy rules without adding blocking burden, because it records counterfactual alerts while leaving execution unchanged. Whether the observed labels are accurate remains dependent on blinded adjudication. **UNVERIFIED**; the prospective audit itself settles this.

### Experiment 3: mechanical-independence fault injection

Build a small matrix in which pairs of evidence channels share or do not share a parser, cache, fixture generator, target, credential, and process. Inject one fault at a time and observe joint failure. Promote a pair to an “independent mechanism class” only when it resists the relevant shared faults on held-out cases.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent superficial independence labels because causal dependencies become executable tests. Hidden dependencies will remain. **UNVERIFIED**; the experiment directly measures joint failure under known faults.

## 10. Minimal implementation order

1. Deploy Proposal A in nonblocking form for high-impact completion claims.
2. Implement Proposal B with only the seven structural rules above in shadow mode.
3. Run Experiment 1 before any blocking behavior.
4. Add Proposal C's signal states and natural-frequency report; do not compute NNV until a valid comparator exists.
5. Activate only high-precision, high-severity rules through a randomized or stepped-wedge evaluation.
6. Recalibrate by claim class and environment; expire rules that lack recent support.

This order is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve the minimal architecture while producing enough evidence to decide whether stronger enforcement earns its cost. Evidence: it uses configuration and one existing-bus hook, stages alerts noninterruptively, and conditions expansion on absolute outcomes. **UNVERIFIED**; Experiment 2 settles operational burden and benefit.

## 11. Final recommendation

Adopt the clinical borrow as **evidence-design discipline**, not medical theater:

- claims become explicit target conditions;
- the agent's assertion is an index test, never its own reference standard;
- rivals include both likely artifacts and high-harm “can't miss” failures;
