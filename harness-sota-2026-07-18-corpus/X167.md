# X167 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → failure recovery

**Unit:** X167  
**Lens:** military doctrine (commander's intent, mission command, OODA, after-action review, red teaming)  
**Target:** resuming useful work after a worker, session, or machine dies mid-task  
**Scope discipline:** external research and cross-domain design only. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect or make claims from the local harness repository.

## Executive verdict

The analogy **partially transfers, and the transferable unit is a bundle rather than a single military idea**:

1. **Commander's intent and mission orders** preserve *why the work exists, what success means, and the boundaries for initiative* when the original issuer cannot provide further instructions.
2. **Succession of command** predesignates who may take over and requires that successor to remain informed.
3. **Running estimates and a common operational picture** preserve a continuously revised account of facts, assumptions, options, and current position relative to the end state.
4. **PACE (primary, alternate, contingency, emergency)** requires recovery paths that do not share a single failure mode and gives explicit triggers/order for switching paths.
5. **OODA** supplies the correct restart discipline: re-observe and re-orient before deciding and acting. It does not supply persistence.
6. **After-action review (AAR)** converts a recovery event into a standards-based correction after useful work has resumed. It does not perform the resume.
7. **Red-team premortem and rehearsal** test the recovery design by deliberately removing the worker, session, storage route, or authority source before production failure does.

The central design borrow is therefore a **Recovery Order plus rehearsed succession**, backed by **fault-domain-distinct recovery routes**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this bundle will improve worker- and session-death recovery over transcript-only resumption because Army doctrine independently specifies (a) intent that enables action without further orders, (b) a successor kept current, (c) current running estimates, and (d) independent degraded-communications routes; the supporting evidence is ADP 6-0 paragraphs 1-45 through 1-50, 2-24, 4-12 through 4-13, and 4-90 through 4-92, plus ATP 6-02.53 paragraphs 10-24 through 10-27 ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0), [ATP 6-02.53 PDF](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf)). That confidence is not higher because trained human subordinates possess tacit shared context that a fresh agent process does not.

Machine-death recovery has a hard boundary: **no doctrine, prompt, or local checkpoint can recover state from a lost machine unless at least one usable copy crosses that machine's failure domain**. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a same-disk “backup” cannot meet machine-death recovery, based on the elementary common-cause fact that loss of the disk removes both copies and on ATP 6-02.53's requirement that a viable PACE method reflect actual availability/capability rather than merely be listed (paragraphs 10-25 through 10-27, [ATP 6-02.53 PDF](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf)).

This is not a proposal for a second orchestration runtime, durable-execution engine, vector database, knowledge graph, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The useful design rides the existing hook/session/agent bus and uses small structured records. The anti-overfit gate is satisfied.

## Research method and evidentiary limits

I used U.S. Army and U.S. Air Force/Department of the Air Force sources. The key doctrinal source is *ADP 6-0, Mission Command: Command and Control of Army Forces* (31 July 2019). The copy linked here is hosted by the Army Judge Advocate General's Legal Center and School and contains the relevant numbered paragraphs ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)). For current publication status, the Army Publishing Directorate lists *TC 7-0.1, After Action Reviews*, dated 13 February 2025, as active ([APD record for TC 7-0.1](https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1030481)).

The Army's Applied Critical Thinking/Red Teaming handbook explicitly says that it is **not doctrine or a school solution** (Chapter I, page 7 of the handbook pagination). I therefore use it as an Army-developed practice source, not as doctrinal authority ([Applied Critical Thinking Handbook v8.1](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/ARIManagingComplexProblems/downloads/The_Applied_Critical_Thinking_Handbook_v8.1.pdf)).

John Boyd's OODA construct is theory, not a recovery protocol. The Air University correction sheet shows the full model: observation, orientation, decision as hypothesis, action as test, environmental interaction, feedback, and orientation shaped by culture, prior experience, analysis/synthesis, new information, and unfolding circumstances ([Air University, Boyd OODA errata diagram](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf)). I use this original-form diagram rather than the popular four-box caricature.

No external source directly tests this exact agent-harness design. Every projected harness outcome below therefore carries a confidence band and an experiment where material uncertainty remains.

## What the source field actually solved

### 1. Commander's intent: purpose that survives loss of contact

In its own field, commander's intent is not a compressed task list. ADP 6-0 defines it as a clear, concise expression of the operation's purpose and desired end state. It exists so subordinate and supporting commanders can act toward the desired result **without further orders**, including when the operation does not unfold as planned (paragraph 1-45). The intent may include purpose, key tasks, and end-state conditions; it also communicates constraints and why the mission exists (paragraphs 1-46 through 1-48). It is supposed to be brief enough for lower-level leaders to remember and understand even without the order (paragraph 1-50) ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

Mission orders complete the mechanism. The standard order format is situation, mission, execution, sustainment, and command-and-signal. A mission order emphasizes the result rather than prescribing the method; it includes who, what, when, where, and especially why, with only the detail required for coordination (paragraphs 1-52 through 1-56) ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

**Concrete translation — BUILD-SMALL:** a successor agent needs a compact, owner-authored or owner-ratified statement of purpose, observable end-state tests, key tasks, constraints, non-goals, and authority boundaries. A transcript summary that only says “what I was doing next” is not equivalent: it can preserve method while losing purpose.

**What it catches:** local optimization after restart; continuation of an obsolete implementation plan; satisfying a proxy instead of the user's acceptance condition; and a successor taking actions outside the authority granted for the task.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit end-state tests plus authority boundaries reduce semantically wrong resumes, because ADP 6-0 makes purpose/end state the basis for initiative when orders or plans fail, while the proposed record gives a fresh agent the analogous decision boundary ([ADP 6-0, paras. 1-45–1-50](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)). The remaining uncertainty is the model's ability to apply concise intent without the shared training and trust assumed by mission command.

### 2. Succession of command: authority is designated before the casualty

In its own field, succession is explicit. ADP 6-0 says a second in command's responsibilities include temporary absence and succession; if a commander is killed, wounded, medically incapacitated, or relieved, the second normally assumes command. Where rank does not make succession obvious, the operations order specifies it. Crucially, the successor “must be able to assume command at any time,” must stay informed, and must be told of changes in the commander's visualization or intent (paragraphs 4-7 through 4-13) ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

This solves two distinct problems that software recovery often conflates:

- **Liveness:** somebody notices that control has disappeared and takes over.
- **Legitimacy:** that somebody is allowed to decide and act.

**Concrete translation — BUILD-SMALL:** every recoverable task receives a stable task identity, an active-worker lease, a declared successor policy, and explicit authority limits. Expiry of a lease permits a successor to *attempt recovery*; it does not silently expand the successor's authority. Any action requiring fresh user approval remains blocked after succession.

**What it catches:** two workers both believing they own a task; an orphaned task nobody is empowered to resume; a replacement agent repeating an external side effect because it cannot tell whether the prior worker completed it; and a replacement treating task recovery as permission for a materially broader action.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a lease plus explicit successor policy reduces orphaning and split-brain work, based on the direct structural match to predesignated succession and continuously informed seconds in command ([ADP 6-0, paras. 4-12–4-13](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)). Confidence is limited because distributed lease correctness depends on the host's clock, atomicity, and storage semantics, which are **UNVERIFIED; settle with the split-brain experiment in the test plan below**.

### 3. Running estimates and shared operational picture: intent is insufficient without current state

In its own field, staffs do not rely on the original order as the state of the world. ADP 6-0 requires staffs to keep running estimates current, continually replace outdated facts and assumptions with new information, and revise conclusions and recommendations. Those current estimates help the commander understand feasible options and decide during execution (paragraph 2-24). The doctrine also describes feedback as the comparison between actual situation and plan; conflicting new information must cause validation or revision of expectations (paragraphs 3-11 through 3-13) ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

**Concrete translation — BUILD-SMALL:** the recovery record must be a running estimate, not a final farewell note. Update it at meaningful state transitions with:

- verified facts and their evidence references;
- assumptions, each with validation state and invalidation condition;
- decisions and brief rationale;
- completed effects, not merely attempted commands;
- partial/non-idempotent operations and reconciliation instructions;
- current risks and unknowns;
- artifact locations, content hashes or equivalent version identifiers, and last verification result;
- next safe action and actions that must not be replayed;
- position relative to each observable end-state test.

**What it catches:** “command returned” being mistaken for “effect exists”; stale assumptions surviving a restart; partial files mistaken for complete artifacts; lost rationale causing the successor to undo a deliberate choice; and blind replay of a non-idempotent external call.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that effect-oriented running estimates improve recovery correctness relative to command histories, because the doctrine explicitly replaces stale facts/assumptions and compares actual state to desired end state, while the proposed fields force the same distinction ([ADP 6-0, paras. 2-24 and 3-11–3-13](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

### 4. PACE: redundancy must cross failure modes and be rehearsed

In its own field, a PACE plan is specific to a mission or task and orders the available methods to use until contact is re-established. ATP 6-02.53 requires a plan for each operational phase and requires the receiving unit to evaluate asset availability and compatibility with the higher plan. It warns that listing a system that is not trained, equipped, or actually available does not ensure continuity; units must report shortfalls and mitigation (paragraphs 10-24 through 10-27) ([ATP 6-02.53 PDF](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf)). ADP 6-0 adds the key common-cause rule: alternate and contingency methods must not rely on the primary (paragraph 4-91) ([ADP 6-0 PDF](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

**Concrete translation — CONFIG:** define recovery routes by failure domain, not by product name. Availability of the required independent existing routes is **UNVERIFIED; settle with the three failure-domain trials specified under the inherited UNVERIFIED proposal heading below**:

- **Primary:** atomic task-owned recovery record in the working environment, updated at state transitions.
- **Alternate:** harness/session event storage managed outside the worker process.
- **Contingency:** supervisor/parent-agent receipt of the compact Recovery Order and last transition sequence.
- **Emergency:** a human-readable recovery brief in an owner-accessible, machine-independent task location, sufficient to identify intent, last verified effect, hazards, and evidence pointers.

The routes must include switch triggers, freshness timestamps/sequence numbers, integrity identifiers, read tests, and a statement of which failures they survive. Four names on the same disk, authenticated by the same expiring credential, are one route—not four.

#### **UNVERIFIED proposal: fault-domain-distinct PACE persistence — CONFIG**

The proposal assumes the existing harness/session/agent bus exposes at least one owner-accessible persistence route outside the worker and, for machine-death coverage, outside the machine. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle by killing the worker, terminating the session, and destroying/replacing the machine in three separate trials, then proving a fresh process can enumerate and read each promised route without credentials or state held only on the dead target**. Until that experiment passes, advertise only the narrower recovery class actually demonstrated.

If such an existing route exists, it is **very likely (80-90%)** that configuring independent PACE copies materially improves recovery availability, based on ATP 6-02.53's viability test and ADP 6-0's common-cause independence rule ([ATP 6-02.53, paras. 10-24–10-27](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf), [ADP 6-0, para. 4-91](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)). If no independent native route exists, this unit does **not** recommend building a new durability daemon: that would violate the anti-overfit gate and turn a small recovery protocol into a second runtime.

### 5. OODA: the successor must re-orient, not blindly continue

In its own field, Boyd's full OODA model is not a simple serial loop. Orientation is influenced by previous experience, new information, unfolding circumstances, cultural traditions, and analysis/synthesis; decisions are hypotheses and actions are tests whose feedback changes later observation and orientation ([Air University OODA diagram](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf)). Air University analysis also cautions that OODA is not solely about speed: orientation shapes observation, decision, and action ([Air University, “Finding the Right Model”](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/JIPA/Display/Article/3371164/finding-the-right-model-the-joint-force-the-peoples-liberation-army-and-informa/)).

**Concrete translation — BUILD-SMALL:** after takeover, the successor performs a bounded **reorientation gate**:

1. Read intent, end-state tests, constraints, sequence number, and hazards.
2. Independently observe the minimum decisive external state: artifact existence/version, working-tree state where relevant, pending external operation receipts, and validity of the assumptions that govern the next action.
3. Compare observations with the running estimate.
4. Classify each mismatch as stale record, changed environment, partial effect, or corruption.
5. Decide a safe next action; act as a test; update the estimate from feedback.

It must never “resume at line N” merely because line N is recorded. Code locations and tool-call offsets are hints, not ground truth.

**What it catches:** a checkpoint that was flushed before the corresponding side effect; an environment that changed during downtime; an artifact modified by another actor; and an old plan whose premise is no longer true.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a bounded reorientation gate reduces stale-state continuation, because the full OODA model explicitly integrates new information and feedback before action, and the gate independently observes effect state rather than trusting the dead worker's last belief ([Air University OODA diagram](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf)).

### 6. AAR: recover first, then institutionalize the lesson

In its own field, an AAR is a guided analysis conducted during or after training/operations to improve future performance. Army FM 7-0 Appendix K describes it as immediate, direct, standards-based feedback and a professional discussion designed for participants to discover what happened and develop an improvement plan ([FM 7-0 Appendix K PDF](https://www.first.army.mil/Portals/102/FM%207-0%20Appendix%20K.pdf)). An official 1987 *ARMOR* article gives the durable four-question form: what happened, why, what should have happened, and how to make the desired behavior happen next time ([ARMOR, September–October 1987 PDF](https://www.moore.army.mil/armor/eARMOR/content/issues/1987/SEP_OCT/ArmorSeptemberOctober1987web.pdf)). The Army Publishing Directorate lists the dedicated 2025 TC 7-0.1 as active ([APD record](https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1030481)).

**Concrete translation — BUILD-SMALL:** after the task is safely resumed or terminated, automatically create a small recovery AAR record:

- expected recovery behavior and recovery service level;
- observed failure and exact recovery timeline;
- divergence from the standard;
- cause category (missing intent, stale estimate, corrupt write, common-cause PACE failure, authority ambiguity, non-idempotent effect, detector failure);
- one accountable corrective experiment or record-format change;
- evidence pointers and whether the correction later passed rehearsal.

An AAR is not the checkpoint and must not delay the first safe recovery action.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that structured recovery AARs reduce repeated recovery defects if corrective actions are assigned and later rehearsed, based on FM 7-0's standards-based improvement model and the four-question method ([FM 7-0 Appendix K](https://www.first.army.mil/Portals/102/FM%207-0%20Appendix%20K.pdf), [ARMOR AAR article](https://www.moore.army.mil/armor/eARMOR/content/issues/1987/SEP_OCT/ArmorSeptemberOctober1987web.pdf)). The conditional matters: storing unowned narratives without a test would be process theater.

### 7. Red teaming: kill the plan before the environment does

In its own field, the Army's Applied Critical Thinking Handbook defines red teaming as an independent capability to explore alternatives and perspectives, and says its common work includes challenging facts, assumptions, problem frames, and groupthink. Its premortem technique asks what would cause a proposed course of action to fail and is deliberately applied before execution (Chapter I, pages 1–7; Chapter VI, handbook pages 165–166) ([Applied Critical Thinking Handbook v8.1](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/ARIManagingComplexProblems/downloads/The_Applied_Critical_Thinking_Handbook_v8.1.pdf)). Again, the handbook itself says it is not doctrine.

**Concrete translation — BUILD-SMALL:** recovery red teaming is a deterministic fault-injection matrix, not an LLM role that merely critiques prose. It kills components at adversarial moments and asks a fresh agent to recover without hidden conversational context.

**What it catches:** fake backups; recovery dependent on the dead worker's credentials; checkpoints that parse but omit decisive state; completion inferred from intent rather than evidence; split brain; and a resume procedure understood only by its author.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that fault injection discovers defects that static schema review misses, because the PACE source explicitly distinguishes listed from operationally viable methods, while premortem analysis is designed to challenge assumptions before execution ([ATP 6-02.53, paras. 10-25–10-27](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf), [Applied Critical Thinking Handbook, premortem](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/ARIManagingComplexProblems/downloads/The_Applied_Critical_Thinking_Handbook_v8.1.pdf)).

## Proposed harness design

### Proposal A — Recovery Order record — BUILD-SMALL

Implement one small, versioned record per active task. The minimal conceptual schema is:

```yaml
recovery_order_version: 1
task_id: stable-id
owner_intent:
  purpose: why this task matters
  end_state_tests: [observable acceptance conditions]
  key_tasks: [outcomes, not detailed method]
  constraints: [must/must-not/approval boundaries]
  non_goals: [explicit exclusions]
authority:
  permitted_effects: [scope]
  approval_still_required: [actions]
succession:
  active_worker: worker-id
  lease_generation: integer
  lease_expiry: timestamp
  successor_policy: policy-id
running_estimate:
  phase: label
  verified_facts: [{claim, evidence_ref, observed_at}]
  assumptions: [{claim, status, invalidation_condition}]
  decisions: [{decision, rationale, evidence_ref}]
  effects: [{operation_id, intended_effect, state, receipt_ref, replay_safe}]
  artifacts: [{uri, version_or_hash, state, last_verified_at}]
  risks_unknowns: [item]
  end_state_progress: [{test, state, evidence_ref}]
resume:
  next_safe_action: description
  do_not_replay: [operation_id]
  reobserve_before_action: [probe]
pace:
  routes: [{rank, fault_domain, locator, sequence, integrity, switch_trigger}]
record:
  sequence: monotonic-integer
  previous_integrity: hash-or-version
  written_at: timestamp
```

This is intentionally a semantic record, not a serialized chain-of-thought. Rationale is short and decision-relevant; private hidden reasoning is neither needed nor reliable recovery state.

Write it atomically (temporary file plus same-filesystem replace, or the host's equivalent transaction), with a monotonic sequence and integrity link. Update it at **meaningful state transitions**, not on every token or tool call: intent acceptance, pre-side-effect, post-side-effect reconciliation, artifact verification, assumption invalidation, phase change, and completion evidence.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the hook and schema can remain under roughly 200 lines if the existing bus offers task IDs and lifecycle events; this is **UNVERIFIED; settle by a spike that implements only record validation, atomic replace, and six transition handlers, then count non-test source lines and measure per-transition latency**. The recommendation remains BUILD-SMALL because it must be abandoned or reduced—not promoted to a daemon—if the bus cannot support it compactly.

### Proposal B — Succession lease and takeover CAS — BUILD-SMALL

Use a generation number or compare-and-swap equivalent so exactly one successor claims a stale task. Takeover steps are:

1. Read the highest valid sequence from all reachable PACE routes.
2. Prove lease expiry according to the bus's authoritative clock/state.
3. Claim a new generation atomically.
4. Re-read after claim; if another generation won, stand down.
5. Perform the reorientation gate.
6. Reconcile any operation in `unknown` or `in_flight` state before retry.
7. Continue within existing authority.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this prevents ordinary duplicate takeover if the existing store provides atomic generation updates; the atomic primitive is **UNVERIFIED; settle with two simultaneous successors attempting 10,000 takeovers while checking that no generation has two accepted owners and no task has zero owners after the recovery deadline**.

### Proposal C — Recovery reorientation gate — BUILD-SMALL

Provide a reusable recovery skill/hook that turns `reobserve_before_action` entries into bounded read-only probes and blocks blind replay of any effect whose state is not `not_started` or explicitly `replay_safe`. The gate emits one of:

- `continue` — record matches decisive external state;
- `repair_record` — effect state is sound but the record is stale;
- `reconcile_effect` — partial/unknown external effect;
- `replan_within_intent` — plan invalid, intent still valid;
- `escalate` — intent/authority ambiguity or irreversible-risk threshold crossed.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this classification improves operator understanding during recovery because it separates epistemic mismatch, partial effect, obsolete plan, and authority ambiguity rather than collapsing them into “retry failed”; evidence is the OODA feedback/orientation structure and ADP 6-0's requirement to replace outdated facts and assumptions ([OODA diagram](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf), [ADP 6-0, para. 2-24](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

### **UNVERIFIED proposal D — Cross-machine PACE route — CONFIG**

Configure, do not build, one already-available owner-controlled route whose durability and credentials are independent of the worker machine. The route must pass actual write/read/freshness/integrity tests from a replacement machine. Availability of such a native route is **UNVERIFIED; settle with the machine-loss test described below**.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that true machine-death recovery requires this or an equivalent independent route, because local process state, local session state, and local disk state can all disappear with the machine, and Army PACE explicitly rejects unavailable or dependent alternates ([ATP 6-02.53](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf), [ADP 6-0, para. 4-91](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)).

### Proposal E — Recovery COMMEX and AAR — BUILD-SMALL

Add a test script/skill that injects failures, scores recovery, and emits the four-question AAR. It rides the existing test/hook bus; it is not a persistent monitor.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a small deterministic fault injector is more useful here than an elaborate learned critic because recovery correctness has concrete invariants—single owner, valid intent, readable record, verified effects, no unsafe replay, and acceptance evidence—and because the Army sources emphasize operational viability and rehearsal rather than mere plan existence ([ATP 6-02.53](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/0C45D378-25E0-438E-8881-749EF51DE080-1452191121290/atp6_02x53.pdf)).

## Adversarial test plan

### Detector validation before absence claims

This unit did not inspect the current harness, so it does not claim any listed feature is absent. To test absence without repeating the detector failures described in the program brief:

1. Seed a known-present synthetic recovery record, known-present lease, and known-present external receipt.
2. Prove the detector finds each control.
3. Probe real tasks through two mechanically independent signals, for example structured task-state enumeration and direct artifact/event-store enumeration.
4. Only then report a missing record, lost owner, or absent receipt.

Any claim that “the current harness misses” one of the classes below is **UNVERIFIED; settle with the validated detector procedure plus corpus replay of known worker/session termination cases**.

### Failure-injection matrix

| Injection | Timing | Required observation | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worker `SIGKILL`/hard termination | Before an effect | Record says `not_started` | Successor performs it once |
| Worker termination | After request sent, before receipt saved | Effect is `unknown` | Successor reconciles; does not blind retry |
| Worker termination | During artifact write | Old complete or new complete record | No parseable-but-torn “success” state |
| Session destruction | Immediately after phase change | Alternate route has latest acknowledged sequence | Fresh session resumes from verified state |
| Machine destruction/replacement | After local-only update | Remote route freshness gap is visible | Recovery is either correct or explicitly bounded to older state; never falsely “current” |
| Two successors start simultaneously | After lease expiry | One generation winner | Exactly one active owner |
| Primary and alternate share credential | Credential revoked | Both fail together | Test labels plan common-cause invalid |
| Checkpoint tampering/staleness | Before takeover | Integrity/version mismatch | Successor rejects or repairs after independent observation |
| Intent changes during outage | Before takeover | New owner intent has higher generation | Successor replans; does not continue stale goal |
| Artifact modified by another actor | Before takeover | Hash/version mismatch | Reorientation detects divergence |
| Completion marker written before tests | Just before failure | Acceptance evidence absent | Task remains incomplete |
| Recovery route parses but omits authority | Before risky action | Missing boundary | Successor escalates rather than infers permission |

### Metrics

- **Semantic recovery rate:** fraction of injected failures that reach the original observable end state without owner correction.
- **Unsafe duplicate-effect rate:** non-idempotent effects performed more than once.
- **False-completion rate:** completion asserted without all end-state evidence.
- **Time to first useful safe action:** exclude time spent rereading irrelevant transcript.
- **Recovery-point loss:** acknowledged state transitions missing from the best surviving route.
- **Split-brain duration:** time with more than one accepted active worker.
- **Common-cause PACE score:** number of advertised routes surviving each independent failure domain.
- **Record burden:** bytes and write latency per meaningful transition, plus agent tokens needed to interpret the record.
- **AAR closure rate:** corrective actions that later pass the exact failed injection.

Suggested acceptance thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; settle them by baselining transcript-only recovery and then choosing thresholds that show statistically and operationally material improvement without increasing unsafe effects**. A reasonable experimental comparison uses the same task set and seeded failure points, randomized between baseline and Recovery Order conditions.

## Failure classes this design is intended to expose

The following are target classes, **not claims that validated detectors have found them in the present harness**:

1. Purpose missing although next step is present.
2. End state described in prose but not tied to observable evidence.
3. Plan survives even though its governing assumption changed.
4. Attempted command recorded as completed effect.
5. External effect is ambiguous and automatically retried.
6. Successor exists but has no authority boundary.
7. Lease expiry creates two active workers.
8. Checkpoint is syntactically valid but torn, stale, or from an older generation.
9. All “backup” routes share process, disk, network, or credential failure.
10. Fresh session can read a summary but cannot locate/version the actual artifacts.
11. Machine recovery is advertised although every record is machine-local.
12. A recovery defect produces a narrative but no owned corrective experiment.

## Where the analogy breaks

### 1. Humans carry tacit state; replacement model processes do not

Mission command depends on competence, mutual trust, shared understanding, training, and habitual relationships. ADP 6-0 explicitly says shared understanding grows from familiarity, trust, shared philosophy, and experience, and that successors must continually stay informed ([ADP 6-0, paras. 1-44 and 4-13](https://tjaglcs.army.mil/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=-Yv035hB3rw%3D&portalid=0)). It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a fresh agent lacks material parts of the dead worker's latent context, based on the loss of the worker's in-memory state and the doctrine's explicit dependence on accumulated familiarity and experience. Therefore, “write commander's intent and trust initiative” does **not** transfer by itself. The running estimate and evidence ledger are necessary compensations.

### 2. Military action tolerates bounded ambiguity; software side effects can be exactly-once sensitive

A subordinate can often act from purpose under uncertainty. A successor agent facing “payment/API mutation probably happened” cannot safely improvise. Doctrine supplies risk acceptance and initiative, not transaction receipts, idempotency keys, or reconciliation APIs. The harness must represent effect state explicitly and refuse blind replay.

### 3. Succession of authority is not reconstruction of computational state

Naming a second in command solves legitimacy and organizational continuity. It does not restore registers, memory, open file handles, unflushed buffers, ephemeral credentials, or an interrupted external transaction. Software recovery still requires persistence boundaries and effect reconciliation.

### 4. PACE is a planning pattern, not proof of independence

Military doctrine itself warns that a named but unavailable method is not viable. It is **likely (55-80%)** that two nominally different software endpoints share at least one consequential dependency in a typical deployment, because identity, region, DNS, billing, and control planes commonly sit below multiple endpoints; the settling experiment is the common-cause matrix above. Independence must be demonstrated by fault injection, not inferred from different labels.

### 5. OODA can become an empty four-step ritual

The popular four-box loop encourages “go faster.” The useful piece is reorientation from new evidence and feedback. If every restart mechanically performs Observe/Orient/Decide/Act without a decisive-state checklist, OODA adds vocabulary but no reliability.

### 6. AAR happens too late to be the recovery mechanism

An AAR improves the next operation; it cannot reconstruct the current operation's missing state. Making AAR completion a precondition for resuming useful work would invert its role and increase recovery time.

### 7. Red teams can become a second orchestration layer

The Army handbook calls for an independent perspective but also warns that red teaming must be tailored and is not a one-size-fits-all template ([Applied Critical Thinking Handbook v8.1](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/ARIManagingComplexProblems/downloads/The_Applied_Critical_Thinking_Handbook_v8.1.pdf)). It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a permanent “critic agent” attached to every action adds inference cost, latency, and another stateful actor, because each invocation consumes execution and the critic itself must be scheduled and supplied context. Deterministic fault injection at defined test points is the narrower transfer.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just preserve the transcript” — rejected

A transcript is useful evidence but mixes intent, speculation, superseded plans, tool chatter, and effects. It does not inherently designate authoritative current facts, idempotency status, or acceptance evidence. It can be an alternate source, not the sole running estimate.

### “Checkpoint every tool call” — rejected

Tool-call frequency is not semantic progress. It creates volume and still permits a checkpoint between request dispatch and effect reconciliation. Meaningful-transition checkpoints plus explicit pre/post effect states are smaller and safer.

### “Use OODA as the whole recovery algorithm” — rejected

OODA explains adaptive decision activity. It neither persists state nor assigns authority nor creates an independent recovery route.

### “Spawn a standing red-team/critic agent for every task” — rejected

This approaches a second orchestration runtime and reintroduces a previously rejected trained-critic pattern without necessity. A small deterministic kill/recovery test catches the relevant invariants with less machinery.

### “Build a durable workflow engine” — rejected by the anti-overfit gate

That is BUILD-LARGE and a previously rejected component. It would require its own correctness, migration, scheduling, identity, and observability story. The evidence gathered here supports a small protocol on the existing bus, not a new runtime.

### “Put four copies on the same machine” — rejected

That can help process death but cannot support a machine-death claim. It fails the PACE independence principle and the stated target.

### “Automatically resume every interrupted action” — rejected

Some actions are non-idempotent, approval-gated, or made obsolete by changed conditions. Recovery means restoring useful agency, not blindly replaying execution.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Define the semantic Recovery Order and invariants — BUILD-SMALL.** Start with intent, end-state evidence, authority, effect state, running estimate, and next safe action.
2. **Implement atomic state-transition writes — BUILD-SMALL.** Support only meaningful transitions and measure burden.
3. **Implement generation-based succession and reorientation — BUILD-SMALL.** Do not add cross-machine claims yet.
4. **Run worker-death and session-death rehearsals — BUILD-SMALL.** Baseline against transcript-only recovery.
5. **Configure one independent cross-machine route if already available — CONFIG.** This step is **UNVERIFIED; settle with actual replacement-machine read and credential-independence tests**.
6. **Run the full PACE/common-cause matrix and publish the demonstrated recovery envelope — BUILD-SMALL.** Say “worker,” “session,” or “machine” only for classes that pass.
7. **Attach a four-question AAR to failed rehearsals and production recoveries — BUILD-SMALL.** Close each lesson with a rerun of the failed injection.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this sequence yields most of the available reliability benefit without overfitting because it begins with the smallest semantic state needed for safe succession, validates each broader failure domain before claiming it, and refuses the second-runtime path. The evidence is the combined Army pattern of concise intent, current estimates, designated succession, viable PACE routes, and rehearsal; the residual uncertainty is specific bus capability and workload overhead, both addressed by the spike and randomized baseline experiments.

## Bottom line

Military doctrine did **not** solve crash consistency or exactly-once side effects. It did solve the organizational half of continuity under lost leaders and degraded communication: preserve purpose, predesignate legitimate succession, keep the successor current, maintain a live operational estimate, provide independent communication routes, rehearse their loss, and learn against a standard.

The borrow transfers when translated into a compact Recovery Order, effect-aware running estimate, generation-based successor claim, reorientation gate, and genuinely independent PACE route. It fails when reduced to inspirational “commander's intent,” a four-box OODA loop, an after-the-fact retrospective, or several copies inside one failure domain.

The decisive criterion is empirical: kill the worker, kill the session, kill the machine, and require a fresh successor with no hidden context to produce the intended end state without duplicated effects or expanded authority. Anything less is continuity theater.
