# X138 — Cross-domain borrow: systems-safety → observability without a baseline

**Unit:** X138  
**Source field:** systems-safety (STAMP/STPA, leading/lagging process-safety indicators, safety/assurance cases; with Swiss-cheese and normal-accident theory as rejected or limited alternatives)  
**Target problem:** deciding whether an agent-harness change actually helped when no historical baseline exists  
**Research posture:** ideation lens only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, repository, or pinned corpus. External sources are cited as URLs with page or paragraph locations.

## Executive finding

The strongest transferable mechanism is a **change-specific, assumption-based leading-indicator program embedded in a living assurance case**. STPA supplies a disciplined chain from unacceptable loss → hazard → safety constraint → control action → causal scenario → safety-critical assumption. Leveson's assumption-based method then turns each vulnerable assumption into a monitor with a check method, cadence, responsible party, trigger, and precommitted response. A safety case keeps the top-level claim, reasoning, evidence, counterarguments, and context auditable as the system changes.

This transfer is **very likely (80-90%) to improve the validity and diagnostic value of harness observability**, because (a) agent harnesses are feedback-control systems with controllers, controlled processes, actuators, feedback, delayed signals, human controllers, and changing environments, matching STAMP's central structural objects; and (b) both MIT's method and HSE's independently developed process-safety guidance derive indicators from particular controls rather than from generic dashboard fashion. Evidence: Leveson defines the method and its control-loop basis in pp. 17-34, especially pp. 20-21 and 31-34 ([MIT-hosted paper](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/papers/2014_A_systems_approach_t_n1.pdf)); HSE independently requires leading indicators against critical controls, tolerances, follow-up, and paired lagging outcomes in paras. 57-68, pp. 23-26 ([HSG254](https://books.hse.gov.uk/gempdf/hsg254.pdf)).

It is **almost certainly not (1-5%) sufficient by itself to establish that a harness change caused an improvement**. The evidence is structural: a safety case organizes an argument and evidence, while a leading indicator detects control degradation; neither creates the unobserved counterfactual “what would have happened to the same workload without the change.” NASA defines an assurance case as evidence and rationale supporting claims, not as a causal estimator ([NASA, paras. 144-154](https://sma.nasa.gov/news/articles/newsitem/2020/09/22/new-tool-for-developing-safety-assurance-cases)); FAA defines safety assurance as evaluating continued control effectiveness via acquisition, analysis, and assessment, again without specifying a counterfactual design ([FAA SMS components, lines/section “Safety Assurance”](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/sms/explained/components)).

Therefore the honest design is a hybrid:

1. Borrow STPA/assumption-based indicators to decide **what must be true**, **what evidence would reveal weakening**, and **what action follows**.
2. Borrow a living assurance case to preserve the claim-evidence-context chain and its defeaters.
3. Add a small, explicit experiment-design element—concurrent canary, randomized interleaving, or paired replay—to answer the causal “helped” question when historical baseline data do not exist.

The third element is not a systems-safety invention and must not be mislabeled as one. It is the missing counterfactual instrument.

## 1. First disambiguate “no baseline”

“No baseline” hides three different problems:

1. **No historical telemetry.** Old sessions exist but were not measured with the new outcome definition.
2. **No stable reference distribution.** Workload, models, tools, owner behavior, and external services drift, so last month's rate is not today's counterfactual.
3. **No explicit acceptability model.** Even if a metric moves from 10% to 8%, nobody stated which loss it proxies, whether 8% is acceptable, or what regression elsewhere would invalidate the win.

The systems-safety borrow directly addresses (3), partially addresses (1), and detects some forms of (2). It does not magically reconstruct lost pre-change data. A concurrent control or paired replay addresses (1) and (2) prospectively.

A useful distinction follows:

- **Control-adequacy claim:** “The changed harness satisfies its constraints under stated contexts.” This can be supported without a historical baseline through tests, invariants, canaries, leading signals, and outcome evidence.
- **Causal-improvement claim:** “The change is better than the alternative.” This requires a comparison that approximates a counterfactual. Post-change health alone cannot answer it.

This distinction is the main guard against cargo-culting a safety case into an “improvement proof.”

## 2. The mechanism in its own field

### 2.1 STAMP/STPA: model safety as enforced constraints, not just failed parts

STAMP treats safety as a system property maintained by constraints in a hierarchical control structure. STPA analyzes control loops for unsafe control actions and causal scenarios, including software, human, organizational, coordination, and feedback defects—not merely component failure chains. Leveson's paper states that STPA rigorously examines control loops and that accidents occur when system safety constraints are violated; the analysis derives more detailed constraints and requirements (pp. 27-29; PDF text around pp. 10-12 in the web rendering: [MIT-hosted paper](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/papers/2014_A_systems_approach_t_n1.pdf)).

The practical sequence is:

1. Define unacceptable losses.
2. Identify system states/hazards that, with a worst-case environment, could lead to those losses.
3. State constraints that must hold to prevent or control each hazard.
4. Draw the control structure: controllers, control actions, actuators, controlled processes, feedback, and process models.
5. Identify unsafe control actions: a necessary control is not supplied; an unsafe one is supplied; timing/order is wrong; or duration/cessation is wrong.
6. Build causal scenarios explaining how the unsafe action or inadequate feedback could occur.
7. Eliminate the scenario if possible; otherwise add controls and monitor the assumptions on which those controls depend.

This is relevant to observability because measurement is not an afterthought in a control loop. Feedback must tell the controller whether the controlled process remains within constraints. Missing, delayed, aggregated, or misleading feedback is itself a causal mechanism.

### 2.2 Assumption-based leading indicators: monitor why the control is believed to work

The key mechanism is more specific than “use leading metrics.” Leveson defines a leading indicator as a warning that a safety-related assumption is broken, dangerously weak, or changing in validity/vulnerability (p. 20, section 3.1: [paper](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/papers/2014_A_systems_approach_t_n1.pdf)). The program monitors assumptions that were wrong initially and those that become wrong as operations or environment change (p. 20).

The field procedure is:

- Identify assumptions underlying the analysis, design, construction, operation, maintenance, coordination, management structure, and environment.
- Ask whether each is vulnerable and what change would invalidate it.
- Create a specific signpost for the vulnerable assumption.
- Record the associated assumption, how it is checked, when it is checked, and the hedging action taken if it fails (p. 31, section 5.1).
- Assign responsibility and predefine action plans and triggers, preferably before failure so denial and goal pressure cannot rewrite the decision rule (p. 33, section 5.3).
- Revisit the indicator set. If a serious event was not presaged, determine whether the indicator was absent, unchecked, ignored, or ineffective, then repair both the indicator and the derivation process (p. 33).

This differs from mining historical correlations. It begins from the causal justification for a control. That is precisely why it is useful with no historical baseline: one can inspect a new control's assumptions before enough failures accumulate to estimate a stable rate.

Leveson is appropriately cautious about evidence. The paper calls its feasibility experience anecdotal rather than scientific proof and says more careful evaluations are needed (p. 33). Accordingly, the claim that this method will improve this particular harness is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the prospective 20-change trial in section 7**. The outcome is **likely (55-80%) to be positive**, based on the method's direct traceability and one reported industrial experiment in which a warning-analysis process produced 43 signs, 37 new, with 14 adopted by management (pp. 32-33), tempered by the paper's own admission that broader proof is incomplete.

### 2.3 HSE's paired leading/lagging control indicators: triangulate mechanism and outcome

HSE's process-safety guidance independently reaches a compatible operational pattern:

- relying on failure data means learning only after something has gone wrong (p. 1);
- select critical risk-control systems rather than collecting an exhaustive KPI raft (paras. 31-34, pp. 13-14);
- attach a leading indicator to each critical control to show whether it operates as designed (para. 57, p. 23);
- set a tolerance that triggers management attention (paras. 58-60, p. 24);
- follow deviations and connect each leading indicator to a lagging desired outcome (paras. 61-66, pp. 25-26); and
- use disagreement diagnostically: poor leading/good lagging suggests the leading signal is too remote; good leading/poor lagging suggests the control is ineffective (paras. 67-69, pp. 26-27). Source: [HSE HSG254](https://books.hse.gov.uk/gempdf/hsg254.pdf).

That last rule is unusually valuable. It turns metric disagreement into model criticism instead of averaging everything into a “health score.”

### 2.4 Living assurance case: force claims, evidence, context, and defeaters to remain linked

NASA describes an assurance case as a comprehensive, auditable artifact recording safety risks and mechanisms used to reduce them; it includes why evidence supports claims, why verification is appropriate, and how counterarguments were addressed. NASA also argues that it should be a living document updated as knowledge of hazards and mitigation effectiveness changes ([NASA, paras. 144-154](https://sma.nasa.gov/news/articles/newsitem/2020/09/22/new-tool-for-developing-safety-assurance-cases)).

In its own field, the safety case does not replace hazard analysis, testing, or operational monitoring. It is the structured connective tissue:

> claim → argument/strategy → subclaim → evidence, all bounded by explicit context, assumptions, justifications, and rebuttals.

For a change, stale or contradicted evidence should weaken the case rather than remain a green checkmark detached from the context in which it was collected.

## 3. Concrete harness translation

### 3.1 Minimal control structure

Map an agent harness as follows:

| Systems-safety object | Harness instance |
|---|---|
| Loss | owner harm: wrong external action, corrupted work, unrecoverable deletion, privacy leak, unusable artifact, or excessive cost/time |
| Hazard | system state that can lead to loss: action without validated target, retry storm, silent tool failure, success declared without evidence, context overflow, or wrong controller acting |
| Safety constraint | invariant such as “a destructive action occurs only after target resolution and scope validation” |
| Controller | model, hook, policy/rule, subagent coordinator, owner, tool wrapper |
| Control action | permit/block/retry/escalate/delegate/write/execute |
| Controlled process | tool call, filesystem, remote service, conversation, task plan |
| Feedback | tool result, exit status, artifact check, owner correction, evaluator result, latency/cost signal |
| Process-model assumption | “exit 0 means intended artifact exists,” “retry is transient,” “validator detects malformed output,” “workload mix is comparable” |

The observability design should be derived from the loss and constraint, not from whatever fields are easiest to count.

### 3.2 A worked example

Suppose the change adds automatic retry after a tool error.

- **Claim:** retry improves task recovery without creating loops or masking terminal errors.
- **Losses:** cost/time exhaustion; duplicate side effects; false success.
- **Hazard:** retry proceeds when the failure is permanent or when the first attempt may already have changed external state.
- **Constraint:** retry only idempotent actions and only for classified transient failures, within a bounded budget; ambiguous side effects escalate.
- **Control action:** retry / stop / escalate.
- **Safety-critical assumptions:** error classification is accurate; idempotency metadata is correct; each attempt is observable; the budget terminates every path; “success” is independently verified.
- **Leading indicators:** unknown-class error fraction; retries lacking idempotency metadata; attempts within 10% of budget; duplicate operation identifiers; verifier not executed after recovered success.
- **Lagging outcomes:** owner-corrected false successes; duplicate side effects; task completion; total tokens and wall time; unrecovered transient failures.
- **Defeaters/counter-signals:** completion increases only because costs explode; retry rate falls because errors are swallowed; verifier passes because it shares the same parser defect.
- **Precommitted action:** disable retry for the implicated class if any ambiguous-side-effect duplicate occurs; reduce budget if tail cost breaches tolerance; return the case to hazard analysis if leading is green while lagging is red.

This catches failure modes a generic “error rate after release” graph misses: successful-looking retries that duplicate effects; a classifier that silently maps unknown errors to transient; budget erosion before a storm; a verifier sharing the producer's defect; and metric improvement caused by relabeling rather than recovery.

## 4. Proposed build

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: change assurance card — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For every behavior-changing harness patch, require one versioned Markdown or YAML “change assurance card” with:

```yaml
change_id: retry-v2
claim: bounded transient retry improves completed tasks without duplicate effects
losses: [duplicate-side-effect, false-success, budget-exhaustion]
constraints:
  - only idempotent and transient-classified calls may retry
contexts: [tool_class, model, task_type, external_service_state]
assumptions:
  - id: A1
    text: idempotency metadata is correct
    vulnerability: metadata can be missing or stale
    leading_signal: retry_without_verified_idempotency
    source: retry-decision hook
    cadence: every decision
    tolerance: 0
    action: block retry and escalate
outcomes:
  - task_completion
  - duplicate_side_effect
  - owner_correction
tradeoffs: [tokens, latency]
defeaters:
  - apparent gain comes from changed workload mix
  - producer and verifier share one failure mode
comparison: randomized_interleave
decision_rule: promote only if ...
expiry: 2026-08-19
```

A small linter rejects a card when a claim has no loss, constraint, assumption, independently sourced outcome, tolerance, action, comparison, or expiry. It emits/consumes ordinary JSONL hook events; it does not introduce a service.

This proposal rests on an **UNVERIFIED premise that authors can produce useful cards at acceptable cost; the settling experiment is to time card creation and score inter-reviewer agreement for 20 changes**. It is **likely (55-80%) to expose missing measurement logic before deployment**, because every observation is traceable to a control assumption and because HSE and Leveson independently require explicit control-linked signals and actions. It is **roughly even (45-55%) that teams will maintain cards without an automated expiry gate**, based on NASA's warning that static, approved-then-forgotten cases are a known failure mode and on the nontrivial work NASA reports for case creation ([NASA, paras. 149-154](https://sma.nasa.gov/news/articles/newsitem/2020/09/22/new-tool-for-developing-safety-assurance-cases)).

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: prospective concurrent comparator — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

When there is no historical baseline, route eligible tasks or replayable decision points between old and changed policy during a bounded observation window:

- randomize at the task/session boundary when contamination between variants is low;
- stratify by task/tool/model/context declared in the card;
- for irreversible actions, use shadow decisions or recorded replays rather than executing both variants;
- preserve assignment, exposure, outcome, and exclusion reasons in append-only JSONL;
- compare both desired outcomes and safety constraints; and
- promote only under the precommitted decision rule.

If the old behavior cannot remain available, use a paired corpus replay with identical inputs, then require a small live canary for external-validity checks. Replay alone is vulnerable to changed services, nondeterminism, hidden state, and owner interaction.

This proposal is **UNVERIFIED for the subject harness; the settling experiment is a two-week randomized interleave on one reversible change with a predeclared sample-size/sensitivity analysis and audit of contamination**. It is **very likely (80-90%) to yield a more credible improvement estimate than a post-only dashboard**, because concurrent assignment supplies a counterfactual under the same time-varying environment. It is only **likely (55-80%) to be operationally feasible across a broad set of harness changes**, because side effects, learning across sessions, model nondeterminism, and cross-variant contamination can prevent clean randomization. These probabilities are design judgments, not claims from the systems-safety sources.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: two-channel evidence and disagreement alarms — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

For each important claim, configure at least:

1. a **leading/control signal** at the decision point (for example, unsafe retry blocked), and
2. a **lagging/outcome signal** from a mechanically independent observation path (for example, downstream artifact verifier or owner correction, not another label from the retry classifier).

Never collapse the pair into one score. Alert on the four states:

| Leading/control | Lagging/outcome | Interpretation/action |
|---|---|---|
| good | good | provisional support; continue watching context and defeaters |
| poor | good | signal may be too remote/noisy, or safety margin is being consumed; inspect |
| good | poor | control or detector is ineffective; reopen hazard analysis |
| poor | poor | control degradation with realized harm; rollback/contain per card |

This directly adapts HSE paras. 67-69. The proposal is **UNVERIFIED for harness telemetry; the settling experiment is retrospective blinded classification of 100 known incidents followed by a prospective miss-rate comparison**. It is **very likely (80-90%) to catch shared-detector and proxy failures that a single metric misses**, provided the sources are genuinely mechanically independent; evidence is HSE's explicit diagnostic use of leading/lagging disagreement and the logical independence created by distinct collection paths.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: expiry and management-of-change trigger — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Invalidate or mark evidence stale when any declared context changes: model version, tool schema, hook version, permission mode, OS/shell, MCP availability, workload stratum, or relevant owner policy. A stale case blocks the strong wording “helped” and falls back to “not yet reassured in this context.” The card's signposts automatically open a re-evaluation task; they do not silently tune thresholds.

This is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is mutation testing in which declared context fields are changed and the gate's detection and false-alarm rates are measured**. It is **likely (55-80%) to prevent evidence collected under one operating context from being reused after material drift**, because Leveson's three assumption classes explicitly include construction/operation as designed and non-violation by system or environmental change (p. 31). The residual uncertainty is whether the context declaration is complete.

## 5. What this would catch that post-only observability misses

The following are structural blind spots of post-only dashboards, not claims that each currently exists in the subject harness:

1. **Risk migration without an incident.** Budget headroom, exception-path coverage, approval latency, or unknown classification can deteriorate before a loss. Assumption-linked leading signals expose that drift.
2. **A green proxy with a red outcome.** The hook fires exactly as designed, yet false success or owner correction rises. Paired evidence points to an ineffective control.
3. **A red proxy with a green outcome.** A compliance metric worsens while outcomes remain sound; the proxy may be too remote, preventing needless architecture built to optimize the wrong number.
4. **Metric relabeling.** Errors “decline” because failures become unknown, suppressed, or reclassified. Negative/defeater signals and independent outcomes reveal the change.
5. **Shared-mode detector failure.** Producer and verifier rely on the same parser, schema, log path, or success flag. Mechanical independence is made an acceptance criterion.
6. **Context-invalid evidence.** A result from one model/tool/workload is treated as timeless. Evidence expiry ties claims to declared context.
7. **Tradeoff displacement.** Completion improves while latency, tokens, side effects, or owner interventions worsen. The assurance claim requires explicit loss/tradeoff coverage.
8. **Actionless telemetry.** A threshold is breached repeatedly but nobody owns the response. Every leading indicator has an owner and precommitted action.
9. **Survivorship and workload drift.** Easy tasks dominate the post-change window. Concurrent stratified assignment records exposure and exclusions.
10. **Safety through non-use.** A dangerous feature looks perfect because the changed path never activates. Exposure/coverage is evidence, not an optional dashboard field.

## 6. Adversarial transfer analysis

### What transfers

1. **Feedback-control structure:** a harness chooses actions based on an internal process model and receives partial feedback. That is a genuine structural homology, not metaphor.
2. **Emergent failures:** many harness losses arise from interactions among model, rule, hook, tool, OS, service, and owner rather than a single broken component. STAMP was designed to extend component-failure chains (Leveson, pp. 17-18).
3. **Rare severe outcomes:** waiting for catastrophic lagging events is statistically and operationally weak in both fields. HSE explicitly motivates early warnings because failure-only learning is too late (p. 1).
4. **Goal pressure and adaptation:** agents retry, route around restrictions, compress context, and satisfy proxy goals; operators alter procedures under schedule pressure. Monitoring assumption vulnerability is applicable.
5. **Change invalidates assurance:** tool schemas, models, services, and policies change quickly. A living case and change signposts match this reality.

### What does not transfer cleanly

1. **Loss severity and ethics differ.** Chemical/aviation safety prioritizes rare catastrophic physical harm; many harness outcomes are frequent, reversible, subjective, and multidimensional. Importing heavyweight certification rituals would be disproportionate.
2. **System boundary is less stable.** A general agent's task, tools, and success criteria can change each session. A single global STPA model would become vague. Analysis must be change-specific and risk-tiered.
3. **Human judgment defines many outcomes.** “Useful,” “best,” and “done” are partly owner-relative. Constraints can encode observable proxies but cannot eliminate preference elicitation.
4. **Fast model drift shortens evidence life.** Safety cases for relatively stable engineered artifacts can amortize analysis; harness evidence may expire quickly.
5. **Leading indicators are not causal estimators.** A warning sign can be diagnostic even when the proposed change did not cause its movement. The comparator remains necessary.
6. **STAMP can generate too many scenarios.** Leveson reports 250 potential risks in one organizational application and acknowledges feasibility questions (p. 33). Without risk-tiering, this would recreate the elaborate scaffolding the anti-overfit gate forbids.

The analogy therefore survives only in a deliberately narrow form: use the causal-control vocabulary and assumption monitors for the handful of loss-relevant controls affected by a change. Do not build an enterprise safety-management bureaucracy around every prompt edit.

### Why Swiss cheese is not the primary borrow

The Swiss-cheese model is useful for asking whether multiple barriers have aligned weaknesses, but it is too underspecified for the target question. It does not define a comparison, an indicator-selection procedure, a tolerance, evidence freshness, or causal attribution. “Add another layer” can also create correlated layers whose detectors share one blind spot. It is **unlikely (20-45%) to materially improve no-baseline evaluation if adopted alone**, because it supplies a barrier metaphor rather than an evaluation design; the evidence favoring the more specific alternative is HSE's explicit control/indicator/tolerance procedure and STPA's assumption derivation.

Its one retained idea is independent barrier verification: the outcome signal must not share the same detector or implementation path as the control signal.

### Why normal-accident theory is not the primary borrow

Normal-accident theory is a warning about interactive complexity and tight coupling, not a method for measuring a change's effect. It usefully predicts that adding couplings can make behavior harder to interpret, but it offers no operational counterfactual. Its implication here is negative: do not add a second orchestration runtime merely to observe the first.

### Safety cases can become persuasive theater

A structured case can make weak evidence look orderly. NASA's description emphasizes counterarguments and living updates, but structure alone cannot validate premises or eliminate confirmation bias. It is **roughly even (45-55%) that an ungated, author-reviewed assurance card would degrade into compliance theater**, based on the documented workload of cases and the familiar “approved and forgotten” criticism NASA explicitly discusses (paras. 149-154). Independent outcome sources, evidence expiry, explicit defeaters, and occasional blinded review are therefore essential.

## 7. Settling experiment: prospective 20-change trial

This experiment resolves the central **UNVERIFIED** recommendation premise.

### Design

1. Select 20 reversible harness changes spanning tool routing, retry/error handling, context management, verification, and delegation. Exclude changes whose side effects cannot be safely shadowed or rolled back, but record all exclusions.
2. Before implementation results are observed, randomly assign 10 changes to ordinary instrumentation and 10 to the assurance-card process. If only one change is available at a time, alternate assignment using a concealed random schedule.
3. For every change, use a concurrent comparator where safe; otherwise paired replay plus live canary.
4. Have a reviewer blind to condition enumerate measurement defects before deployment: missing exposure, shared detector, absent outcome, no action threshold, untracked tradeoff, context mismatch, or non-identifiable causal claim.
5. During the observation window, collect:
   - defects discovered pre-deploy;
   - incidents/owner corrections and whether they were presaged;
   - false alarms and actionability;
   - time to diagnose;
   - time to author/maintain instrumentation;
   - causal-estimate sensitivity to workload/model strata;
   - reviewer agreement on whether the promotion claim is supported.
6. Seed a small set of known mutations: suppress exposure events, make producer/verifier share a parser, relabel one error class, shift workload mix, and invalidate a context field. Verify detector sensitivity before interpreting an absence.
7. Precommit pass criteria. Example: assurance-card changes find at least 30% more seeded/real measurement defects before deployment, do not increase median change lead time by more than 15%, and yield no worse false-promotion rate. These numbers are proposed decision thresholds, not evidence-based universal constants.

### Interpretation

- If cards improve defect detection but not causal certainty, retain the card and strengthen assignment/comparison.
- If causal comparison works but cards add no diagnostic value, retain the comparator and simplify the card to claim/outcome/context/defeater.
- If both fail cost/benefit thresholds, reject the borrow rather than expanding it.
- If leading indicators are green and outcomes red, reopen the control model; do not tune the outcome away.
- If no incidents appear, do not claim safety improvement. Report exposure, sensitivity to seeded defects, constraint satisfaction, and the maximum effect size the sample could reasonably detect.

It is **likely (55-80%) that this trial will distinguish useful structure from paperwork**, because it measures both detection gain and authoring cost and includes known mutations to validate detectors. The range remains broad because 20 heterogeneous changes may have limited statistical power; the experiment should report uncertainty rather than binary “proven” language.

## 8. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

All proposals ride existing hooks/skills/MCP/logging and use Markdown/YAML plus append-only JSONL. There is:

- no second orchestration runtime;
- no vector database or knowledge graph;
- no durable-execution engine;
- no trained critic;
- no observability SaaS; and
- no guardrail framework.

The entire borrow should remain a schema, linter, event convention, and comparison wrapper. If implementation exceeds roughly 200 lines per component or requires a daemon, the design must return to review because the administrative mechanism may have become more complex than the control it evaluates.

Risk tiering is mandatory for proportionality:

- **Tier 0:** documentation/UI-only change — claim, direct check, no STPA worksheet.
- **Tier 1:** reversible behavioral change — short card plus replay/canary.
- **Tier 2:** destructive/external/permission or autonomous change — explicit losses, constraints, unsafe actions, two-channel evidence, concurrent shadow where possible, and independent review.

This tiering is **likely (55-80%) to keep the borrowed method below its overfit threshold**, because HSE recommends a few critical controls rather than KPI overload (paras. 31-34), but the exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to measure authoring time and escaped measurement defects by tier over the 20-change trial**.

## 9. Recommendation

### Adopt a thin “assumption-to-evidence” protocol, not a safety bureaucracy — UNVERIFIED

**BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Adopt the assurance card, two mechanically independent evidence channels, explicit tolerances/actions, evidence expiry, and a concurrent comparator for reversible changes. Start with one high-value reversible change, then run the 20-change trial before universal enforcement.

This recommendation is **very likely (80-90%) better grounded than either a generic KPI dashboard or a safety-case-only approach**, because it combines two independently supported systems-safety mechanisms with the counterfactual element they lack. It remains **UNVERIFIED for this harness; the settling experiment is the prospective trial in section 7**.

Do not claim “the change helped” unless the card contains a credible comparison. Without one, use narrower language: “the change satisfied these constraints under these observed contexts; these assumptions remain supported; no effect relative to the old behavior was estimated.” That sentence is less exciting and much more honest.

## 10. Gaps and boundaries

1. I did not inspect the subject harness or corpus, by explicit unit instruction. Consequently, examples of what this would catch are structural predictions, not findings that those blind spots are present locally.
2. The systems-safety sources do not establish that assumption-based indicators improve agent-harness outcomes. That premise is marked **UNVERIFIED** wherever it drives a recommendation, with the settling experiment named inline.
3. Leveson's reported applications support feasibility and indicator discovery, not a controlled estimate of accident reduction; the paper itself calls some experience anecdotal (p. 33).
4. HSE guidance concerns major-hazard process safety. Its paired-indicator logic transfers, but its organizational governance and consequence scale should not be copied wholesale.
5. Assurance cases provide disciplined argumentation, not truth. Weak premises, shared-mode evidence, omitted defeaters, and stale context can still yield a polished wrong case.
6. Concurrent A/B execution is unsafe for some external side effects. Shadow decisions and replay reduce but do not eliminate external-validity problems.
7. No claim of absence, brokenness, or completion in the subject system is made here, so the two-independent-signal absence rule was not invoked. The proposed trial nonetheless validates detectors with known-present seeded mutations before interpreting zero events.
