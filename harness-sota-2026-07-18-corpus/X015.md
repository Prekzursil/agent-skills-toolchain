# X015 — Cross-domain borrow: intelligence tradecraft → agent-claim verification

## Executive result

The intelligence-tradecraft borrow transfers, but only after a substantial narrowing.

The useful mechanism is a three-part discipline:

1. **Keep source reliability separate from the credibility of this particular report.** The NATO/UK “Admiralty Code” records these as two independent judgments rather than allowing a trustworthy source to make every statement trustworthy.
2. **Separate observations, assumptions, and judgments; express uncertainty in controlled terms.** ICD 203 requires analysts to explain source quality, distinguish information from assumptions and judgments, explain uncertainty, consider alternatives, and identify indicators that would change a judgment.
3. **For consequential ambiguous claims, seek diagnostic disconfirmation across explicit alternatives.** Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) asks which evidence is inconsistent with each reasonable hypothesis, rather than merely accumulating support for the favored one.

The concrete harness translation is a small **claim-evidence gate** riding the existing tool/hook bus. It records tool evidence outside the agent’s prose, decomposes consequential completion claims into bounded assertions, maintains independent provenance and claim-support grades, runs claim-type-specific postcondition checks, and invokes a very small ACH-like alternative test only when deterministic checks cannot settle a high-impact claim. This compound recommendation is decomposed into individually tagged proposals in Section 3. It is **not** a second agent runtime, a generic “LLM judge,” a knowledge graph, or a promise to prove arbitrary natural-language truth.

My principal recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce false “done,” false “absent,” citation-laundering, stale-state, and same-origin-corroboration errors, because the default path relies on external tool receipts and observable postconditions while published experiments show that unassisted LLM self-correction can fail ([Huang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)) and self-evaluation can exhibit self-preference ([Panickssery et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Paper-Conference.pdf)). This effectiveness estimate is **UNVERIFIED;** the settling experiment is the shadow-mode, seeded-fault evaluation specified below. The recommendation does not inherit confidence from ACH: controlled ACH studies are mixed or negative, so full ACH is explicitly rejected as the default.

## Scope and evidence posture

This is the requested ideation lens. I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness repository. I used the measured ground truth supplied in the unit brief only to understand the target problem, not as evidence for the cross-domain mechanism. All descriptions of intelligence practice below cite public sources. All harness effects are predictions and therefore carry the mandated Sherman-Kent-style confidence bands and, where not yet tested, inline **UNVERIFIED** labels naming the settling experiment.

“Proof” here means a bounded acceptance result: a claim of a specified type, scope, object, and observation time satisfies an independently executed predicate over captured evidence. It does not mean that arbitrary prose has been proven true. Any system that collapses those two meanings would merely manufacture confidence.

## 1. What intelligence tradecraft solved in its own field

### 1.1 The Admiralty Code: do not confuse a reliable source with credible information

UK Joint Doctrine Publication 2-00 describes intelligence grading as a shared shorthand for two judgments: reliability of the source and credibility of the information. It represents them as an A–F / 1–6 pair. Crucially, the dimensions need not match: doctrine gives examples of a usually reliable source providing improbable information (`B5`) and confirmed information from an unreliable source (`E1`). It also says that an unjudgeable grade is not the same as useless information and that grades should be reviewed as more information arrives ([UK Ministry of Defence, JDP 2-00, pp. 59–60](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/311572/20110830_jdp2_00_ed3_with_change1.pdf)).

In its own field, this prevents two common reasoning errors:

- **Halo transfer:** “this source has been good before, therefore this report is true.”
- **Reverse halo:** “this source is poor or unknown, therefore this report is false.”

The mechanism is deliberately compact. It preserves two facts that a single trust score destroys: who/what produced the information, and how well this individual item is supported. It also preserves “cannot judge” as an epistemic state rather than silently mapping it to false.

This part of the analogy is **very likely (80-90%)** to transfer because an agent/model/tool can have a good historical record and still misread one output, while an unfamiliar tool can produce a directly checkable result; treating model reputation, agent identity, or past task success as the truth value of the current claim reproduces the same source/item halo structure. Its practical benefit is **UNVERIFIED;** the settling experiment is the separate-grade versus single-trust-score ablation in Section 6.

### 1.2 ICD 203: distinguish evidence, assumptions, judgments, likelihood, and confidence

ODNI’s ICD 203 requires analytic products to describe the quality and credibility of sources, explain uncertainty, distinguish underlying intelligence from assumptions and judgments, incorporate alternatives, and identify indicators that would alter a judgment. It says confidence rests on the logic and evidentiary base, including source quantity and quality and subject understanding. It also provides standardized likelihood ranges and warns against mixing likelihood terminology; importantly, it says not to combine a confidence level and degree of likelihood in the same sentence because they describe different things ([ODNI, ICD 203, especially pp. 3–6](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

Sherman Kent’s original argument arose from the ambiguity of words such as “possible” and “probable”: readers assigned materially different numerical meanings to the same estimative language. His remedy was a limited vocabulary tied to understood odds ([Sherman Kent, “Words of Estimative Probability”](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-8-no-4/words-of-estimative-probability/)).

In intelligence work this is not cosmetic hedging. It prevents a factual observation (“imagery shows a runway”), an interpretation (“it is military”), and a forecast (“it will become a staging base”) from being presented as though they have the same epistemic status. It also makes the linchpin assumption inspectable: ICD 203 calls for stating assumptions that bridge key gaps and explaining what happens if they fail.

This distinction is **very likely (80-90%)** to transfer because agent answers can contain four objects with different verification predicates:

- an **observation** made by a tool;
- an **action report** about what the agent caused;
- an **inference** from outputs;
- a **forecast or recommendation** about what will happen after handoff.

A verifier cannot apply the same acceptance rule to all four. A file-existence claim may be checked directly at a time and path. A causal diagnosis requires competing explanations. A prediction cannot honestly be marked “verified” before the event.

### 1.3 ACH: test alternatives with discriminating and inconsistent evidence

Richards Heuer’s ACH requires analysts to enumerate reasonable competing hypotheses and evaluate them together rather than test a favorite sequentially. Its central insight is that evidence consistent with a hypothesis can be weak: the same evidence may also be consistent with every alternative. More useful evidence discriminates among hypotheses, and analysts should focus on evidence inconsistent with a candidate explanation ([Heuer, *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*, Chapter 8](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/9a5f1162fd0932c29bfed1c030edf4ae/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The CIA tradecraft primer similarly describes ACH as a way to prevent premature closure and highlight the most discriminating evidence, while a Key Assumptions Check makes premises explicit and asks what information would force them to be reconsidered ([CIA, *A Tradecraft Primer: Structured Analytic Techniques for Improving Intelligence Analysis*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)).

In its own field, ACH changes the question from “what supports my explanation?” to “what observation would be surprising if this explanation were true, and which alternative explains the whole evidence set with fewer contradictions?” It also leaves an audit trail of alternatives considered, evidence judgments, and sensitivity to decisive evidence.

That logic transfers to ambiguous agent claims. For example, after an agent says “the defect is fixed,” reasonable alternatives include:

- H1: the implementation is correct and the relevant behavior now passes;
- H2: only the reported test passes, while the original failure path remains;
- H3: the command did not run against the intended artifact/environment;
- H4: the result was cached, stale, truncated, or misread;
- H5: the change fixes the symptom but creates a prohibited side effect.

Ten repetitions of the same test are not ten diagnostic observations. A postcondition probe through a different mechanism may discriminate H1 from H2–H4 much better.

## 2. Adversarial transfer test: where the analogy breaks

The borrow survives, but full transplantation does not.

### 2.1 Agent verification often has accessible ground truth; intelligence often does not

Intelligence estimates concern partially observed, strategic, sometimes deceptive worlds. Many agent claims concern machine state that can be queried: a path exists, a process listens, a test has a recorded exit status, a diff contains a requested change, or an API reports an object. It is **likely (55-80%)** that turning those claims into an ACH matrix would add subjective judgment where a predicate can settle the issue; this is **UNVERIFIED,** and the settling experiment is the full-ACH ablation in Section 6.

Therefore the harness should use **deterministic postconditions first, alternatives second**. Full ACH as a universal gate is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to outperform claim-type-specific checks, based on the disanalogy above and controlled evidence that ACH-trained analysts did not reliably beat controls. One randomized study of 50 intelligence analysts found mixed evidence on bias reduction and observed possible increases in inconsistency and error ([Dhami et al., 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.3550)); another experiment found the ACH-style matrix did not reduce confirmation bias while a differently oriented matrix did ([Dhami et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11169332/)). The exact harness comparison is **UNVERIFIED;** the settling experiment is the ablation `deterministic gate` versus `deterministic gate + full ACH on every claim` below.

### 2.2 A source grade is not truth and must not become a global agent reputation score

Human sources and collection systems can have somewhat persistent identity and access. Agents can change model version, prompt, context, tool availability, environment, and task domain between runs. A global score such as “Agent A is 94% reliable” would hide these regime changes and invite automation bias.

The transferable unit is therefore not “the agent.” It is a scoped provenance tuple such as:

`{producer model/version, verifier version, claim type, tool adapter, environment, acquisition path, time window}`.

Even that tuple’s history must remain advisory. The current report’s evidence grade remains separate. A scoped registry is **likely (55-80%)** to be more informative than a global agent score because it preserves capability and version boundaries, but this is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it by comparing calibration/Brier score and false-pass rate for global, per-claim-type, and full-tuple histories over held-out seeded tasks.

### 2.3 Corroboration is not independence

Two analysts may rely on the same underlying report. Likewise, two agents may quote two pages copied from one press release, use the same search index, read the same tool output, or share a model family with correlated errors. Counting voices rather than provenance roots manufactures corroboration. The NeurIPS study on LLM evaluators shows a concrete independence concern: evaluated models favored their own outputs and self-recognition correlated with self-preference ([Panickssery et al., 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

Consequently, “ask a second agent” is not a second mechanically independent signal. Independence must be earned by a different observation mechanism or independently rooted source. A shell exit code plus the same shell’s prose is one chain; a test exit receipt plus an independently queried artifact postcondition can be two. Perfect independence detection is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** attainable from URLs and model names alone because copied content and shared upstream systems can be opaque. That impossibility claim is bounded to automatic provenance inference; a controlled provenance registry can establish independence for harness-owned tools.

### 2.4 The claimant can game a verifier that trusts claimant-authored evidence

An intelligence analyst normally does not manufacture the sensor record being analyzed. An autonomous agent can choose commands, summarize outputs, omit failures, or cite an irrelevant but real receipt. A claim manifest emitted only by the agent is therefore an assertion, not evidence.

The bus must capture raw receipts outside the model-controlled prose. The agent may point to receipt IDs but may not create or edit them. This separation is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch fabricated command execution and incorrect exit-status reports, because the acceptance predicate reads a hook-generated event rather than the claimant’s account; it will not by itself catch a real but irrelevant command. This prediction is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it with seeded fabricated, replayed, wrong-working-directory, truncated-output, and irrelevant-command cases.

### 2.5 Natural-language decomposition is itself fallible

A claim extractor can miss an embedded assertion, split away a qualifier, or turn a recommendation into a fact. Intelligence tradecraft assumes an analyst consciously identifies the key judgments; an automatic harness must infer them from prose.

The safe consequence is not to pretend extraction is complete. Require explicit manifests for high-impact action claims, run a high-recall extractor over the final answer as a cross-check, and sample unmatched sentences. High-recall extraction plus explicit completion claims is **likely (55-80%)** to improve coverage, but **UNVERIFIED;** settle it on a human-annotated corpus of final answers with atomic-claim recall, qualifier preservation, and false-escalation metrics.

### 2.6 Verification is time- and scope-bounded

“The server is running” can be true at observation time and false one second later. “Tests pass” may mean a named subset, not the suite. Intelligence grading preserves uncertainty and re-evaluation as new reporting arrives; JDP 2-00 explicitly calls for revisiting source and information grades as new intelligence arrives ([UK MOD, JDP 2-00](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/311572/20110830_jdp2_00_ed3_with_change1.pdf)).

Every accepted claim therefore needs object, scope, environment, and observation timestamp. “Verified” without those fields is invalid. The gate proves only the recorded predicate at that boundary.

## 3. Concrete harness design

### 3.1 Proposal A — Evidence receipts on the existing bus

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add a hook-side receipt writer for consequential tool results. It stores append-only records outside model-authored output:

```json
{
  "receipt_id": "r-…",
  "tool": "shell|read|edit|mcp|browser|test",
  "invocation_digest": "…",
  "cwd_or_target": "…",
  "started_at": "…",
  "observed_at": "…",
  "exit_or_status": "…",
  "stdout_digest": "…",
  "stderr_digest": "…",
  "output_pointer": "…",
  "adapter_version": "…"
}
```

The receipt is not a truth certificate. It proves that a particular adapter observed a particular result. Content predicates still decide whether that observation supports the claim.

The `<~200 lines` classification is **roughly even (45-55%)** because adapter normalization and Windows edge cases may exceed the budget; it is **UNVERIFIED,** and the settling experiment is a two-day implementation spike covering shell, file read/write, and test events. If the spike exceeds the threshold, split adapters into existing hook-local helpers rather than creating a daemon; do not silently relabel it BUILD-LARGE.

Expected catches, all **UNVERIFIED** until the seeded-fault experiment:

- **very likely (80-90%)** to catch “command ran” when no matching event exists, because receipt existence is outside the agent’s prose;
- **very likely (80-90%)** to catch falsely reported exit status, because the hook records it directly;
- **likely (55-80%)** to catch wrong-directory and stale-replay errors when the predicate checks target and timestamp;
- **unlikely (20-45%)** to catch semantic misinterpretation by itself, because a genuine receipt can be irrelevant or misunderstood.

### 3.2 Proposal B — Typed claim manifest and epistemic labels

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Require a compact sidecar for consequential claims, either emitted explicitly by the agent or derived and then confirmed before finalization:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "c-…",
  "exact_text_span": "…",
  "type": "observation|action|state|absence|causal|forecast|recommendation",
  "object": "…",
  "scope": "…",
  "environment": "…",
  "observation_time": "…",
  "evidence_receipts": ["r-…"],
  "assumptions": ["…"],
  "falsifier": "…",
  "status": "verified|supported|unresolved|contradicted|not-yet-verifiable"
}
```

Rules:

- `verified` is permitted only for an acceptance predicate with independently captured evidence.
- forecasts are `not-yet-verifiable` and use the owner’s specified likelihood bands with evidence;
- causal claims must expose alternatives or be `supported`, never promoted merely because a fix and symptom change co-occurred;
- absence/broken/done claims require two mechanically independent signals, and absence probes require a demonstrated known-present control;
- qualifiers in `exact_text_span` must survive decomposition.

Configuration alone is **likely (55-80%)** to improve epistemic clarity because it makes type and scope explicit, but is **unlikely (20-45%)** to enforce truth without Proposal A and predicates; both predictions are **UNVERIFIED,** settled by the manifest-only ablation in the evaluation plan.

### 3.3 Proposal C — Two-axis provenance/support grade, never one trust score

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For each evidence item, retain two fields:

**Provenance reliability (`R`)**

- `R-A`: harness-owned direct observer with validated adapter and intact receipt;
- `R-B`: primary external source or stable API with known acquisition path;
- `R-C`: secondary source with attributable origin;
- `R-D`: model/agent assertion or transformed summary;
- `R-U`: cannot judge.

**Claim-specific support (`S`)**

- `S-1`: independently confirmed by a distinct observation root and directly entails the bounded claim;
- `S-2`: direct single-root observation;
- `S-3`: indirect but discriminating support;
- `S-4`: merely consistent / non-discriminating;
- `S-5`: inconsistent with the claim;
- `S-U`: cannot judge.

These are harness translations, not NATO labels, and must not be described as an official Admiralty scale. The system must store the reasons and provenance roots, not only `R-A/S-2`. It must never arithmetically average R and S. A historically strong API returning an unrelated field can be `R-A/S-4`; a new deterministic probe may be `R-U/S-2` until its adapter is validated.

This scheme is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent reputation halo and “two citations means confirmed” counting when acceptance rules use provenance roots, based on the structural match to the separate dimensions in JDP 2-00. Its thresholds and labels are **UNVERIFIED;** settle them by measuring inter-rater agreement, calibration, and false-pass rate against simpler `{receipt/no receipt}` and single-score baselines.

### 3.4 Proposal D — Claim-type predicate registry

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Use small, explicit predicates, for example:

| Claim type | Minimum admissible check | Distinct second signal where required | Reject/caveat condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| file created/edited | hook receipt plus read-after-write content/hash | independent path listing or parser where “done” is asserted | target mismatch, stale time, missing requested content |
| tests pass | test receipt, exact command/scope, exit status | requested-behavior probe or artifact-level check for “fixed” | subset represented as suite; cached/stale/wrong environment |
| service/API healthy | process or request receipt | independent endpoint/state query | one transient success represented as durable health |
| item absent | negative probe whose detector first finds a known-present control | different mechanism/data field | uncontrolled detector, permission/filter ambiguity |
| causal diagnosis | observations mapped against at least two plausible mechanisms | intervention or discriminating probe | correlation only, alternatives not tested |
| citation supports text | resolved passage and exact claim span | independent source only when “confirmed” is claimed | citation exists but does not entail claim |
| forecast/recommendation | evidence, assumptions, likelihood band | indicator/falsifier for later update | labeled “verified” before outcome |

This registry is **very likely (80-90%)** to be more auditable than a generic scalar LLM score because each acceptance decision exposes a predicate and evidence record. It is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it by replaying the same claim/evidence pairs through both systems and comparing false-pass explanations and reviewer agreement. The line-count classification is **roughly even (45-55%)** if many claim types are included; the initial spike should implement only file, test, process, absence, and citation predicates.

### 3.5 Proposal E — ACH-lite only for unresolved high-impact claims

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

When a consequential claim is not deterministically settled, prompt a verifier to produce no more than four records:

1. the exact claim and its linchpin assumptions;
2. the strongest plausible alternative explanations (normally two to four, including tool/provenance failure);
3. the currently available evidence that is inconsistent with each alternative;
4. the cheapest next observation with the greatest discriminating power.

The verifier does not vote on prose quality and does not sum consistency marks. It requests a discriminating observation; the external predicate consumes the resulting receipt. If no affordable observation discriminates, the output remains `unresolved` with the alternatives visible.

This narrowed use is **likely (55-80%)** to catch premature causal closure and irrelevant-support accumulation, because it operationalizes ACH’s strongest insight without relying on ACH’s empirically weak scoring/integration rule. It is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it on ambiguous causal and research tasks with an ablation against deterministic checks alone and full ACH.

### 3.6 Proposal F — Indicators for claims that can decay

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

For state claims whose truth can expire, attach an expiry or invalidating indicator: process exit, file modification, dependency version change, remote response change, or elapsed TTL. CIA’s public description of “Signposts and Indicators” is to define observable events and review them periodically for change ([CIA, “Ask Molly: SATs Advice”](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-sats-advice/)); ICD 203 likewise calls for indicators that would alter uncertainty ([ODNI, ICD 203](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

No new monitor or daemon is proposed. Existing events invalidate cached acceptance; otherwise the final answer carries its observation time. Event-driven invalidation is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce stale claims in workflows that already emit relevant events, but **UNVERIFIED;** settle it by replaying mutation-after-verification scenarios and measuring stale-claim survival.

## 4. Decision policy: what the gate actually does

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

The gate returns one of five results, never an ungrounded “confidence score”:

- **ACCEPT-BOUNDED:** predicate passed; render the exact scope and observation time.
- **SUPPORT-WITH-CAVEAT:** evidence is relevant but not independently decisive; render assumptions and the required next check.
- **RECHECK:** a cheap, discriminating probe exists; execute it through the existing tool bus.
- **HUMAN-ESCALATE:** impact is high, alternatives remain live, and no admissible automatic check exists.
- **REJECT/CONTRADICTED:** evidence conflicts with the claim or required receipts are absent.

The LLM may help extract claims, generate alternatives, or identify a discriminating check. It may not mint receipts, declare independence, or override deterministic predicate failure. Human reading is reserved for high-impact unresolved cases and a random audit sample, not every answer.

This policy is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce total human review while preserving detection on high-impact claims, but **UNVERIFIED;** settle it by measuring review minutes, false-pass rate, and escalation precision in shadow mode. There is no claim that all falsehoods become automatically decidable.

## 6. Evaluation that could falsify the recommendation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

### 6.1 Shadow-mode benchmark

Run the gate without blocking production behavior. Construct a stratified set of at least 300 consequential claims from real tasks plus injected variants. The count 300 is a design target, not a power calculation; final sample size should be set from desired confidence intervals after a pilot.

Seed at least these fault families:

- fabricated execution and false exit status;
- wrong working directory, environment, branch, object, or scope;
- stale and replayed outputs;
- partial suite represented as full suite;
- real but irrelevant receipt;
- copied sources and same-root multi-agent corroboration;
- citation presence without entailment;
- disabled, permission-blind, or pattern-blind absence detector;
- passing narrow test with failing behavioral postcondition;
- multiple plausible causes with a tempting favored explanation;
- changed state after initial verification;
- embedded qualifier dropped by claim extraction.

Ground truth should come from task-specific deterministic fixtures where possible. Two human reviewers adjudicate only semantic cases and disagreements; they are not asked to reread every normal trace.

### 6.2 Required ablations

Compare:

- baseline agent self-report;
- generic second-pass LLM judge;
- manifest only;
- receipts + typed predicates;
- receipts + predicates + separate R/S grades;
- recommended system with ACH-lite escalation;
- full ACH on every claim.

Measure atomic-claim recall, qualifier preservation, false-pass rate, false-block rate, unsupported-`verified` rate, provenance-independence errors, escalation precision/recall, reviewer minutes, tool calls, token overhead, and wall-clock overhead. For predictions, measure calibration/Brier score separately; never merge it with factual verification accuracy.

### 6.3 Predeclared adoption gate

Adopt only if the recommended system materially lowers false passes on high-impact seeded faults without an unacceptable increase in false blocks and review time. “Materially” and “unacceptable” must be set by the owner before results are revealed. If receipts + predicates perform as well as the R/S or ACH-lite layers, delete the non-contributing layers. If the generic LLM judge matches the deterministic system on held-out adversarial cases, this unit’s architectural recommendation is falsified.

The recommended system beating baseline on the seeded set is **likely (55-80%)**, because several seeds are directly detectable from external receipts; generalization to unseeded real failures is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because claim extraction and semantic relevance remain open. Both are **UNVERIFIED;** the held-out real-failure slice, not the seeded training slice, settles generalization.

## 7. Anti-overfit gate

Every proposal above rides the existing hook/skill/MCP/tool bus. None introduces a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The design stores a small append-only receipt stream and ephemeral claim records; it does not build generalized memory.

The default path is intentionally mechanical:

`claim type → bounded predicate → external receipt(s) → accept/caveat/recheck`

ACH-lite is an exception path for consequential ambiguity, not a ceremony imposed on every sentence. Source history remains advisory and separable from the current item. If an ablation shows that a layer does not reduce held-out false passes, remove it.

A new daemon/service/runtime is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be justified for the initial version because existing hooks can capture the relevant events and the acceptance logic is small; this is **UNVERIFIED,** settled by the implementation spike’s adapter coverage, latency, and line-count findings. If hooks cannot provide tamper-resistant receipts or cross-tool identifiers, that is a gap to report—not automatic permission to build a second runtime.

## 8. Rejected alternatives and dead ends

### Full ACH for every claim — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Rejected because it treats observable software state like uncertain geopolitical inference, increases subjective scoring, and has mixed or negative experimental support. The useful residue is explicit alternatives plus a request for discriminating evidence on high-impact unresolved claims.

### A single “trust score” for each agent — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Rejected because it recombines the exact dimensions the Admiralty system separates and ignores model/version/domain/context changes. It would invite halo transfer.

### A second LLM as proof — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Rejected as the acceptance authority. A second model may generate alternatives or map a claim to a predicate, but published self-correction and self-preference evidence makes model agreement insufficient as proof ([Huang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798); [Panickssery et al., 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

### Majority vote / multi-agent jury — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Rejected unless voters consume mechanically independent evidence roots. Correlated repetition is not corroboration, and more model calls create cost without necessarily creating a new observation.

### Universal knowledge graph of claims and evidence — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)**

Explicitly rejected under the anti-overfit gate. Claim/evidence edges can live in a per-run JSON record; a durable graph adds a previously rejected component without evidence that the target predicates need it.

### Cryptographic signing as truth — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

A digest can make receipt tampering evident, but it cannot make an irrelevant observation entail a claim or make a broken sensor accurate. Hashes may protect integrity after the basic experiment; they are not the verification mechanism.

### Human review of every answer — rejected

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

It violates the target constraint and does not scale. Human attention belongs on high-impact unresolved cases, disagreements, and random quality-control samples.

## 10. Bottom line

Intelligence tradecraft did not solve automatic truth. It solved a narrower institutional problem that maps well: how to stop provenance reputation, report plausibility, analyst inference, and forecast uncertainty from collapsing into one unexamined assertion—and how to force attention toward evidence that could disprove the favored explanation.

The transferable harness principle is:

> Never trust the claimant’s confidence, the source’s reputation, the number of agreeing voices, or the existence of a citation as proof. Capture observations outside the claimant, preserve provenance independence, apply a claim-specific predicate, and seek discriminating disconfirmation when direct checking runs out.

The proposed receipt-and-predicate core is **likely (55-80%)** to provide useful verification at small architectural cost, with evidence from the structural match and from known limitations of model self-evaluation; the net benefit in this owner’s harness remains **UNVERIFIED** until the specified shadow-mode seeded-fault and held-out-real-failure experiments are run. Full ACH, a universal trust score, and an LLM jury do not earn adoption from this analogy.
