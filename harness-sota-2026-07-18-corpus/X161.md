# X161 — military doctrine → use-versus-mention guards

## Executive judgment

The borrow transfers, but only in a narrow and useful form. The transferable mechanism is **engagement control**: do not let a raw observation directly trigger a consequential action; first characterize the candidate in context, validate that acting on it serves the commander's objective, and apply an authority rule proportionate to the cost of a mistake. US joint targeting doctrine explicitly distinguishes detection from combat identification, defines combat identification as characterization sufficient to support an engagement decision, requires that it occur before engagement, and separately requires target validation against current objectives ([JP 3-60, pp. II-20–II-21](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/jp3_60.pdf)). Army targeting doctrine likewise puts observations into “target” versus “suspected target” categories; suspected targets must be confirmed, and stale information must be verified before declaring a target ([FM 3-60, paras. 2-16–2-20](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/B0FC2CCE-1D6D-41EB-B5F3-AEAD9147638E-1692033323348/fm3_60.pdf)).

That maps directly to the reported defect: a lexical match is a **nomination**, not proof that the matched text is performing the prohibited action. The guard must identify the candidate's semantic role—executed instruction, state-changing payload, quoted example, historical log, policy text, or unknown—before it “engages” by blocking or rewriting. This unit's assigned brief reports five detectors and at least 12 false fires caused by matching payload text that merely describes a thing; that count is accepted as pinned program input, not independently re-measured here, per the IDEATION-only instruction.

I assess it **very likely (80-90%)** that this architecture will remove the described class of false fires without needing a second orchestration runtime. Evidence: the military mechanism explicitly inserts characterization and validation between detection and action, while the defect is explicitly that the present detectors collapse those stages. The remaining uncertainty is implementation-specific: the brief intentionally withholds the five detector implementations from this lens, so the experiment that settles the claim is a replay of all known false-fire fixtures plus matched true-positive fixtures through the proposed gate.

This is not a claim that military doctrine solved the philosophy-of-language distinction between use and mention. It did not. It solved a structurally similar control problem: an indicator can be associated with a target without yet licensing action against it. The translation succeeds at the decision architecture level and fails if taken as a literal equivalence between battlefield identification and language understanding.

## The specific source-field mechanism

### 1. Engagement is a staged decision, not a reflex from observation

JP 3-60 defines positive identification as identification derived from observation and analysis of target characteristics. It then defines combat identification as attaining a sufficiently accurate characterization of detected objects to support an engagement decision, and says that combat identification is acquired before engagement. The same passage requires validation to determine whether a planned target still contributes to objectives, is accurately located, and will not conflict with friendly operations ([JP 3-60, pp. II-20–II-21](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/jp3_60.pdf)). This is a three-part separation:

1. **Detect / nominate:** something may be relevant.
2. **Identify / characterize:** establish what the observed thing is in the operational situation.
3. **Validate / authorize:** decide whether acting on that identified thing advances the objective under present constraints.

Army doctrine makes the uncertainty state explicit. Target-selection standards divide nominations into targets and suspected targets. Targets satisfy engagement accuracy and timeliness requirements; suspected targets require confirmation. Source reliability, accuracy, target status, and information age are evaluated before action ([FM 3-60, paras. 2-16–2-20](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/B0FC2CCE-1D6D-41EB-B5F3-AEAD9147638E-1692033323348/fm3_60.pdf)). This matters because the useful borrow is not merely “add context.” It is **preserve epistemic state**: candidate, confirmed target, non-target, and unknown must remain different states.

Direct-fire doctrine adds an authority policy. Under WEAPONS HOLD, engagement occurs only after being engaged or ordered; under WEAPONS TIGHT, only positively identified enemy targets may be engaged; under WEAPONS FREE, anything not positively identified as friendly may be engaged. The doctrine explicitly relates more restrictive status to greater friendly-fire risk ([ATP 3-21.8, “Weapons Control Status”](https://www.benning.army.mil/Infantry/DoctrineSupplement/ATP3-21.8/appendix_b/DirectFireControl/FireControlMeasures/index.html)). The relevant abstraction is that **decision thresholds depend on the consequence of misclassification**. It would be a category error to copy the military labels or their lethal defaults into software; only the risk-proportional threshold transfers.

### 2. Commander's intent supplies the objective against which a match is validated

ADP 6-0 defines commander's intent as a concise expression of operational purpose and desired end state. It may include purpose, key tasks, and end-state conditions; it is intended to guide subordinate judgment when events do not follow the plan ([ADP 6-0, paras. 1-45–1-51](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/1FE33715-CFD1-4614-A489-B3E0480C3F80-1428688882108/adp6_0.pdf)). The significant feature is not inspirational wording. It is that a subordinate action is judged against a purpose and desired outcome, rather than against an isolated token from an earlier order.

For guards, “intent” becomes a detector contract:

- **Protected outcome:** what harm is this detector preventing?
- **Semantic target:** what event, not what word, constitutes that harm?
- **Required evidence:** which trusted event fields establish that the event is occurring?
- **Non-target roles:** documentation, quotation, logs, tests, policy discussion, and other referential contexts that must not be treated as the act.
- **Unknown policy:** warn, ask, or block when characterization cannot be completed.

A detector whose contract says only “match this string” has a trigger but no commander's intent. It cannot validate whether intervention contributes to the protected outcome.

### 3. OODA contributes the orientation barrier

Air Force material describes Boyd's OODA loop as observe, orient, decide, and act, and describes it as collecting, interpreting, and reacting to battlefield information ([US Air Force, “University course teaches principles of information operations”](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/122663/university-course-teaches-principles-of-information-operations/)). For this problem, the valuable detail is simply that **orientation lies between observation and decision**. A regex match belongs in Observe. Event provenance, parser position, data flow, destination, and detector purpose belong in Orient. Suppress, warn, ask, or block belongs in Decide/Act.

OODA alone is too generic to design the guard. “Add an orientation phase” does not specify what evidence is sufficient. Engagement control and commander's intent provide that missing specificity. I assess it **likely (55-80%)** that naming the runtime stages `nominate → characterize → validate → intervene` will produce clearer implementations than naming them after OODA. Evidence: JP 3-60 gives each stage a decision-specific responsibility, whereas the Air Force summary only supplies four broad cognitive phases. The experiment that settles this naming/design claim is a blinded design review: give maintainers both API sketches and score whether they place provenance, confidence, and intervention policy in the correct stage.

### 4. After-action review makes false engagements training evidence

Army training doctrine gives the AAR a fixed comparison: review what was supposed to happen, establish what actually happened using multiple perspectives, determine what was right or wrong against guidance and measures, and determine how to perform to standard next time ([FM 7-0, Appendix K, paras. K-6–K-10](https://www.first.army.mil/Portals/102/FM%207-0%20Appendix%20K.pdf)). The mapping is a small, local incident record for every appealed or overridden guard decision:

- intended detector outcome;
- actual event role and actual intervention;
- decisive evidence fields;
- adjudicated classification;
- smallest fixture or contract change that prevents recurrence.

The AAR is not runtime memory and must not become one. It produces regression fixtures and manifest corrections, then exits. That distinction keeps the proposal inside the existing harness bus and outside the rejected durable-memory and observability-platform designs.

### 5. Red teaming challenges the classification assumptions

The US Army Red Team Handbook describes red teaming as challenging facts, the problem frame, and assumptions; seeking alternative perspectives; and mitigating groupthink. Its Assumption Sensitivity Analysis asks participants to assign confidence to assumptions so effort can focus on the least-supported ones ([Red Team Handbook, summary and pp. 105–106](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/arimcpii/downloads/Red_Team_Handbook_v9.pdf)). Applied here, red teaming does not mean adding a trained critic model. It means generating adversarial fixtures across semantic roles and asking which classifier assumption each fixture breaks.

Examples of assumption attacks:

- “Quoted text is always mention” fails for `printf 'dangerous-command' | sh`, where quoted bytes flow to an execution sink.
- “A command-looking string is always use” fails in a Markdown policy document explaining what must never be executed.
- “Tool input is always active” fails when the tool input is a search query, a test fixture, or content being written to documentation.
- “Tool output is always inert mention” fails if an agent copies an untrusted output into a later executable field.
- “Code fences are mention” fails when a runner extracts and executes fenced code.

These cases show why syntax-only exclusions are inadequate. The role is determined jointly by **source, field path, destination/sink, and data flow**, not typography.

## Concrete translation: the Semantic Engagement Gate — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The line-size and available event-envelope premise are **UNVERIFIED** because this IDEATION unit was instructed not to inspect the local harness. The settling experiment is a one-hour spike against one existing detector: implement the router and one manifest using only the hook event's documented fields, count executable lines, and record whether tool name, field path, event direction, and destination are available without a new runtime. If that spike cannot remain approximately 200 operational lines or cannot obtain trusted provenance, reject the BUILD-SMALL classification rather than silently expanding it into BUILD-LARGE.

Build one shared gate in front of the five detectors, not five independent “ignore docs” regexes. Each existing detector continues to nominate candidates. The gate receives a structured envelope and returns a typed decision with evidence.

### Event envelope

```text
candidate_text       exact span nominated by detector
detector_id          which protected outcome is at issue
event_kind           tool request | tool result | user message | file mutation | other
tool_name            trusted tool identity, when applicable
field_path           structured field containing the span
destination          execution sink, file path/class, network sink, display-only, unknown
parser_context       argv | shell program | JSON value | prose | code block | log | unknown
provenance           user-authored | model-authored | tool-returned | generated | unknown
```

The envelope must come from trusted harness metadata where possible. Asking an LLM to infer `tool_name` or `field_path` from the candidate string would recreate the original payload-text error in probabilistic form.

### Semantic roles

```text
EXECUTE_NOW     bytes are being supplied to an active interpreter/action sink
MUTATE_ACTIVE   bytes change executable policy, hooks, credentials, or active config
REFERENCE       bytes are displayed, searched, logged, quoted, tested, or documented
PASSIVE_DATA    bytes are data for a non-executing sink
UNKNOWN         available evidence cannot safely establish a role
```

These roles are deliberately operational rather than philosophical. A string can change role as it flows: a quoted example in a document is `REFERENCE`; extracting it and sending it to a shell makes the later event `EXECUTE_NOW`. Classification therefore occurs at each consequential sink, not once at text creation.

### Detector contract (“commander's intent”)

Each detector receives a small declarative manifest:

```yaml
protected_outcome: prevent destructive filesystem execution
targets: [EXECUTE_NOW]
candidate_patterns: [...existing patterns...]
positive_evidence:
  - event_kind == tool_request
  - tool_name in [shell, powershell]
  - field_path in executable_fields
non_targets: [REFERENCE, PASSIVE_DATA]
unknown_policy: ask
intervention: block
```

Not every detector should share the same targets. A prompt-injection detector may legitimately inspect untrusted data even when it is `PASSIVE_DATA`, because its protected outcome concerns information crossing a trust boundary. A destructive-command detector should care about an execution sink. The manifest forces that distinction into reviewable configuration.

### Decision procedure

1. **Nominate:** existing cheap lexical/structural rules produce a candidate. No intervention is permitted at this stage.
2. **Characterize:** deterministic adapters derive source field, sink, parser context, and role from the event envelope. The classifier preserves `UNKNOWN`; it never converts uncertainty into “use” merely because a string looks dangerous.
3. **Validate:** compare the role and evidence to the detector contract. Ask whether intervention prevents the named outcome in this event.
4. **Apply risk-proportional authority:** high-cost blocking requires positive semantic evidence. `REFERENCE` is suppressed. `UNKNOWN` follows an explicit per-detector policy, normally ask or warn for high-impact user work, never an undocumented default.
5. **Emit a decision trace:** `{candidate, role, evidence_codes, contract_version, action}`. Keep it local and compact; do not build a telemetry service.
6. **AAR on appeal:** an override or owner correction becomes a minimized positive or negative fixture and, when needed, a manifest correction.

I assess it **very likely (80-90%)** that the deterministic envelope plus per-detector contract is the highest-value first implementation. Evidence: the defect is semantic-role collapse, and the hook bus already necessarily knows at least the event type and detector invocation point in order to run a detector at all; the unit brief also mandates proposals ride the existing bus. Whether all required provenance fields are exposed is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the one-detector event-envelope spike described above.

I assess it **unlikely (20-45%)** that a general-purpose LLM role classifier should be in the first version. Evidence: the five known detectors represent a small bounded set, while structured tool identity and field paths provide stronger, cheaper evidence than free-text judgment; an LLM would also introduce latency and a new failure mode. The experiment that could overturn this recommendation is a fixture benchmark where deterministic adapters fail on materially many true semantic ambiguities and a constrained classifier improves balanced error cost without weakening reproducibility.

## What this catches that lexical guards miss

The following are design-level expected outcomes, not measured results:

| Event | Lexical detector failure | Gate classification | Expected intervention |
|---|---|---|---|
| A safety document says “never run `rm -rf …`” | False fire on dangerous token | `REFERENCE`, prose/code example, non-executing destination | Suppress |
| A session log contains a credential command from yesterday | False fire on historical payload | `REFERENCE`, tool result/log | Suppress; a separate secret-exposure detector may still act under its own contract |
| A search query asks for examples of a prohibited call | False fire on query text | `REFERENCE` or `PASSIVE_DATA`, search field | Suppress |
| A shell tool receives the prohibited call in its executable program field | May be caught today, but with no role proof | `EXECUTE_NOW`, trusted executable field | Block |
| `printf '<prohibited call>' | sh` | Naive “quoted means mention” exclusion would miss a real use | `EXECUTE_NOW`, data flow terminates at interpreter sink | Block if the adapter resolves the pipeline |
| Text is written into an enabled hook or active policy file | A command-only detector may miss delayed activation | `MUTATE_ACTIVE`, active destination | Warn/block only for detectors whose protected outcome includes active-policy mutation |
| A fenced code block is extracted by a runner | Code-fence exemption would miss execution | first event `REFERENCE`; runner event `EXECUTE_NOW` | Intervene at the runner sink |
| Candidate occurs in an unrecognized custom tool field | Either false block or unsafe allow | `UNKNOWN` | Follow explicit detector policy and expose missing adapter |

I assess it **almost certain (90-99%)** that trusted field-path classification will correctly suppress straightforward prose, log, and search-query mentions when those are structurally distinct event fields. Evidence: these roles are encoded by the host event schema rather than inferred from wording. The residual uncertainty is schema coverage; the settling experiment is fixture replay across every event type observed for the five detectors.

I assess it **likely (55-80%)** that a small first version can catch direct interpreter fields and common pipe-to-interpreter forms while staying BUILD-SMALL. Evidence: direct fields are schema lookups and common shell pipelines can be recognized with the harness's existing shell parser if one exists. The existence and suitability of that parser are **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the spike plus fixtures for Bash, PowerShell, nested `-c`/`-Command`, pipelines, and quoting. Without an existing parser, defer pipeline data-flow detection rather than implementing a shell parser inside this unit's proposed hook.

I assess it **very unlikely (5-20%)** that a sub-200-line gate can soundly resolve arbitrary obfuscation, command substitution, generated scripts, aliases, and multi-step data flow. Evidence: those require language-specific parsing and cross-event provenance. The experiment that would revise this claim is a corpus benchmark demonstrating that existing native parsers expose normalized sinks and provenance through the hook envelope. Until then, those are explicit `UNKNOWN` cases, not promised catches.

## Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### Where the structure genuinely transfers

1. **Both domains separate indicators from authority to act.** JP 3-60's detection/characterization/validation stages directly correspond to lexical nomination/semantic role/guard decision ([JP 3-60, pp. II-20–II-21](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/jp3_60.pdf)).
2. **Both domains pay asymmetric error costs.** Direct-fire doctrine tightens engagement authority when friendly-fire risk is greater ([ATP 3-21.8, “Weapons Control Status”](https://www.benning.army.mil/Infantry/DoctrineSupplement/ATP3-21.8/appendix_b/DirectFireControl/FireControlMeasures/index.html)). Blocking an owner's legitimate work and permitting a dangerous act have different costs by detector and context; a single threshold is therefore inappropriate.
3. **Both domains operate on changing context.** Joint doctrine requires revalidation when objectives, location, or surrounding operations change ([JP 3-60, p. II-20](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/jp3_60.pdf)). Text likewise changes from mention to use when moved into an execution sink.
4. **Both domains benefit from explicit uncertainty.** FM 3-60 retains “suspected target” rather than forcing a binary decision ([FM 3-60, para. 2-16](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/B0FC2CCE-1D6D-41EB-B5F3-AEAD9147638E-1692033323348/fm3_60.pdf)). A guard needs `UNKNOWN` for the same control reason.

### Where the structure does not transfer

1. **Physical identity is not semantic role.** Military PID uses observable target characteristics. Use-versus-mention often depends on compositional syntax, speech act, and downstream execution. A string is not a stable entity: identical bytes can be inert prose now and executable input later. Therefore “better regex PID” is not the answer.
2. **Military false-positive costs are vastly different.** Lethal engagement doctrine is designed around irreversible human harm. A harness block is reversible and can ask the owner. Copying a weapons-tight default everywhere would over-block and exploit the analogy emotionally rather than structurally.
3. **There is no adversary in most false fires.** The document is not disguising itself as a command. Red-team language can tempt designers to assume hostile intent and scan more aggressively, which worsens the exact defect.
4. **Commander/subordinate hierarchy is only partial.** The owner supplies intent, but the guard is deterministic infrastructure, not a trained subordinate exercising broad judgment. The harness should encode narrow contracts, not claim autonomous “disciplined initiative.”
5. **OODA speed is not the objective.** A faster lexical loop produces faster false fires. Orientation quality and intervention cost matter more than cycle time here.
6. **AARs do not automatically establish truth.** An owner override is strong feedback but may be situational. The AAR must preserve the event envelope and protected outcome; blindly turning every override into a global exemption would create the inverse failure.

The analogy therefore passes only with these constraints: borrow staged evidence, explicit uncertainty, intent validation, proportional authority, and fixture-producing review. Reject martial vocabulary in user-facing guard messages, reject assumptions of hostility, and reject any implication that semantic classification can be reduced to target recognition.

## Anti-overfit gate

The proposed gate passes the program's anti-overfit constraint only if it remains a shared adapter plus declarative detector contracts on the existing hook/skill/MCP bus. It adds no second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or general guardrail framework. It converts five independently over-literal triggers into clients of one small evidence-and-policy boundary.

The correct stopping rule is important: if the spike reveals that trusted provenance requires whole-program taint tracking or a daemon, stop. Preserve `UNKNOWN`, improve the native event envelope if possible, and reassess. Do not let a useful military analogy become justification for a command-and-control superstructure. The program brief's pinned reminder that a roughly 100-line loop can score 74% on SWE-bench is the architectural prior here: add the smallest decision boundary that fixes the evidenced class, then demand replay evidence before expansion.

## Bottom line

Borrow **engagement discipline**, not military aesthetics. A match nominates. Trusted context characterizes. A detector contract validates against the protected outcome. Risk determines intervention authority. Unknown stays unknown. Appeals become AAR fixtures. Red teaming attacks role transitions. That is a concrete, bus-native response to use-versus-mention false fires; everything beyond it must earn its complexity with replay evidence.
