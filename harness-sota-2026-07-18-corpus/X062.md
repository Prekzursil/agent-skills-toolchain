# X062 — Epidemiology → use-vs-mention guards

## Unit result

The epidemiologic mechanism that transfers is **a staged surveillance case definition: a sensitive “suspected case” screen, followed by a more specific and mechanically different confirmation criterion, with positive predictive value (PPV) evaluated separately in populations with different base rates**. It transfers **very likely (80-90%)**, because both domains must find rare consequential events in a large stream of superficially similar non-events, must spend follow-up effort only after a signal, and can attach explicit context to a case; the evidence is the unit's pinned observation of 12+ mention-triggered false fires across five detectors and CDC's treatment of surveillance case definitions, sensitivity, PPV, false-positive investigations, and pseudo-outbreaks [CDC, *Updated Guidelines for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

The transfer is **not** “run the same text matcher twice.” WHO warns, in its own domain, that ostensibly different rapid diagnostic tests may be fundamentally the same and therefore share false-positive reactions [WHO, *Guidelines on Hepatitis B and C Testing*, §7.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/#ch7.s9). In the harness, confirmation must therefore use evidence with a different failure mode: typed event provenance, semantic role, parsed command/action structure, resolved target, or the actual tool sink. A second keyword list, a paraphrase matcher, or an LLM rereading the same undifferentiated payload is not independent confirmation.

The practical design is a three-state detector contract:

1. **SUSPECTED** — a high-recall lexical or structural screen saw a candidate. Record it; do not block merely because the bytes occurred.
2. **PROBABLE / INDETERMINATE** — the candidate is near an action boundary but its role cannot be resolved safely (for example, dynamically assembled shell code). Apply a constrained action, approval, or narrow clarification appropriate to impact.
3. **CONFIRMED USE** — a mechanically independent signal shows that the candidate occupies an operative role at a real sink. Only this state can trigger the detector's full intervention.

This design is **likely (55-80%)** to remove most of the described use-vs-mention false fires without reducing protection at typed action sinks; evidence is the structural match to staged case ascertainment and serial confirmation, but the harness-specific effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the stratified replay experiment in “Verification protocol” before enforcement**. The confidence is not higher because natural language can change role during an agent trajectory, detector errors can be correlated, and there is no biological assay serving as a natural reference standard.

## What epidemiology solved, in its own field

### 1. Define a case, not a token

Public-health surveillance starts with an operational case definition. CDC says that a definition may combine clinical manifestations, laboratory results, epidemiologic information such as person/place/time, specified behaviors, and levels of certainty such as confirmed, probable, and suspected. CDC also says that a standard definition increases reporting specificity and comparability [CDC, *Updated Guidelines*, “Describe the public health importance…”](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

The central move is ontological: “case” means a specified event under specified conditions, not the presence of one symptom. Fever can be evidence for a case but is not itself the disease. Context is part of the definition rather than an after-the-fact exception list. Surveillance definitions also explicitly serve a declared purpose; CDC notes that the system's purpose, objectives, and planned uses establish the frame for evaluating components [CDC, *Updated Guidelines*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

This is directly relevant to use-vs-mention. A dangerous string is analogous to a symptom: potentially sensitive, rarely dispositive. The harness “case” must instead be an **operative event**, such as “a delete operation is about to execute against resolved target T,” “a credential-bearing value is about to cross an external network boundary,” or “untrusted instructions are being promoted into an instruction-bearing control channel.” A document saying that such an event exists is evidence about the world, not necessarily the event itself.

### 2. Separate suspected, probable, and confirmed cases

Tiered certainty lets surveillance cast a broad net without pretending every report is confirmed. A current CDC example, the MIS-C surveillance definition, combines clinical, laboratory, and epidemiologic criteria and classifies reports as confirmed, probable, or suspect [CDC, *Case Definitions and Reporting: MIS*](https://www.cdc.gov/mis/hcp/case-definition-reporting/index.html). CDC's general surveillance guidance likewise lists levels of certainty as a normal part of case definitions [CDC, *Updated Guidelines*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

The mechanism preserves information. A sensitive candidate is not discarded, but neither does it immediately receive the consequences reserved for a confirmed case. Follow-up can promote or exclude it as evidence arrives.

### 3. Use serial confirmation selectively, especially when prevalence is low

WHO's hepatitis B guidance is a concrete instance. It defines a two-assay strategy in which reactive specimens from the first assay receive a second test; WHO says this generally improves PPV and reduces false-positive referrals. It recommends considering confirmation in low-prevalence populations and explicitly makes the choice depend on prevalence, sensitivity, specificity, costs, and operational constraints [WHO, *How to test for chronic hepatitis B infection*, §§7.1–7.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/).

The base-rate effect is large in WHO's worked model. At analyte prevalence 0.1%, the displayed PPV is 4.7% for assay 1 and 90.7% for assay 1 plus assay 2 in series; at 1%, the displayed values are 33.3% and 99% [WHO, *Guidelines on Hepatitis B and C Testing*, Table 5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442287/table/annex5.tab35/). These values are properties of that model and those assay assumptions, **not** performance estimates for a harness.

WHO is also unusually useful for the analogy's failure conditions. The two-assay recommendation was conditional and based on low-quality evidence and predictive modelling rather than direct comparative studies. WHO further cautions that two nominally different rapid tests may be fundamentally the same and share inaccuracies, and that poor sensitivity in the first assay cannot be repaired by applying a second test only to first-stage positives [WHO, §§7.3 and 7.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/#ch7.s5). Thus, staging is useful only when the first screen has adequate recall and confirmation contributes genuinely different information.

### 4. Measure PPV, not just sensitivity, and stratify it

CDC defines surveillance sensitivity as the proportion of real cases detected and PPV as the proportion of reported cases that actually have the event. It states that low PPV misdirects investigation resources, can generate pseudo-outbreaks, and reflects the case definition's sensitivity, specificity, and the event prevalence. CDC also recommends that more than one PPV measurement may be needed—for example, by data source, combinations of sources, or event type [CDC, *Updated Guidelines*, §§D.2.e–D.2.f](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

This matters because a detector's “accuracy” is not portable across payload roles. The prior probability of active use is low in quoted documentation and incident reports, higher in an action proposal, and highest in a typed tool invocation. Even an excellent generic matcher can have poor PPV in the documentation stratum. One aggregate false-positive rate conceals this.

### 5. Calibrate follow-up cost to harm and require an alternative reference for misses

CDC's early-outbreak framework says threshold choice must balance sensitivity against investigation burden, that the locally acceptable false-alarm level varies with risk and resources, and that measuring sensitivity requires a high-quality alternative data source to find outbreaks the surveillance system missed [CDC, *Framework for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems for Early Detection of Outbreaks*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5305a1.htm). CDC's broader guidance says surveillance systems should remain as simple as possible while meeting their objectives [CDC, *Updated Guidelines*, §D.2.a](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

This prevents a false success criterion. Eliminating visible false fires is not enough; a detector that never fires has perfect apparent PPV on the alerts it never emits. Misses require a separate labeled corpus, seeded active cases, or shadow comparison, just as surveillance sensitivity needs an external reference.

## Structural translation to an agent harness

| Epidemiology | Harness translation | Concrete consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Event/case definition | Define the prohibited or approval-requiring **operative event** | Match `operation + semantic role + sink + target/context`, not raw payload text |
| Symptom/screening assay | Existing keyword/regex/pattern detector | Produces `SUSPECTED`, telemetry, and evidence capture only |
| Person/place/time or exposure context | Event provenance: actor, turn, channel, field, trust boundary, tool, phase, resolved resource | The same bytes classify differently in quoted source text and an executable argument |
| Suspected/probable/confirmed | `SUSPECTED`, `INDETERMINATE`, `CONFIRMED_USE` | Consequence increases with evidence strength and action impact |
| Different confirmatory assay | Typed sink evidence or parser/AST/resolver evidence | Prevents a second correlated text matcher from laundering suspicion into confirmation |
| Exclusion criterion / alternative diagnosis | Explicit `MENTION` evidence: quote, code fence under inspection, tool output, documentation field, escaped literal | Suppresses intervention at that stage, but does not permanently bless the bytes |
| Prevalence/base rate | Active-use rate by semantic-role stratum and detector | Compute PPV separately for documentation, chat instruction, generated proposal, and tool invocation |
| Case investigation | Replay/adjudication of candidate plus full event envelope | Produces a labeled outcome and a reason code, not just “allowed/blocked” |
| Pseudo-outbreak | Burst of alerts caused by scanning a document that lists many forbidden examples | Collapse related suspects into one document-level mention episode; do not treat it as many attacks |
| Case-definition revision | Versioned detector policy and labeled replay suite | Compare old/new confusion matrices and preserve auditability |

The key unit of classification is an **event envelope**, not a string. A minimal envelope is:

```json
{
  "detector": "destructive-operation",
  "candidate": "rm -rf /example",
  "source_role": "quoted_documentation",
  "source_trust": "untrusted",
  "phase": "read",
  "sink": null,
  "action_kind": null,
  "resolved_target": null,
  "evidence": ["lexical_match", "markdown_fence"],
  "case_state": "SUSPECTED_MENTION"
}
```

If those same bytes are later copied into a shell call, that is a **new event** with `phase: "act"`, `sink: "shell"`, a parsed action kind, and a resolved or unresolved target. Mention status must not be transitive. This “classify at each role transition” rule is **very likely (80-90%)** necessary, because documents can contain instructions that an agent later operationalizes; the evidence is the harness's multi-stage read/reason/act structure stated in the target problem plus the direct disanalogy to a stable patient label. It prevents a benign source classification from becoming an execution bypass.

## Proposed implementation — UNVERIFIED until event-bus schema and replay fit are tested

### Proposal A: staged detector adapter — UNVERIFIED event-envelope availability

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Place a thin adapter around each of the five text detectors. The adapter accepts the existing match plus the event envelope and returns one of `mention`, `suspected_use`, `indeterminate_use`, or `confirmed_use`, with reason codes. It does not add an orchestration runtime.

Decision order:

1. Preserve the original matcher as the sensitive screen.
2. Check whether the candidate is in an action-bearing field at a real sink.
3. If it is not at a sink, record a mention/suspect result and continue without the full guard consequence.
4. If it is at a sink, ask a detector-specific parser/resolver for independent evidence: operation kind, destination boundary, target, or authority transition.
5. If independent evidence confirms use, apply the existing guard consequence.
6. If parsing or resolution is impossible, retain `indeterminate_use`; choose approval, sandboxing, or a constrained denial based on harm rather than silently treating ambiguity as safe.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this adapter fits within the small-build bound for five detectors if the bus already exposes typed tool name, argument field, and phase; evidence is the deliberately narrow state machine and reuse of existing matchers. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by instrumenting 100 representative events and confirming that tool, field, phase, and provenance are present without reconstructing them from prose**. If those fields are absent, the line estimate is invalid and the proposal must be rescoped rather than mislabeled BUILD-SMALL.

### Proposal B: detector-specific case definitions — UNVERIFIED maintainability effect

Give each detector a declarative case definition containing:

Example for a destructive-operation guard:

```yaml

  mention: observe
  suspected_use: observe
  indeterminate_use: require_approval
  confirmed_use: apply_existing_policy
```

It is **likely (55-80%)** that declarative definitions will make drift and cross-detector inconsistency easier to review; evidence is CDC's rationale for standardized case definitions and comparable reporting [CDC, *Updated Guidelines*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm). The harness effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by having two maintainers independently classify the same 50 replay events using the schema and measuring agreement and unresolved fields**.

## What this catches that payload matching misses

### 1. Active use expressed without the detector's favorite literal

A typed sink can establish use through operation identity even when spelling changes: a filesystem delete API, a PowerShell cmdlet, a shell alias, or a structured MCP mutation can all map to the same action kind. The proposed confirmation stage is **likely (55-80%)** to catch some active operations missed by exact-string detectors because it keys on sink semantics rather than one lexeme; evidence is the availability in principle of typed tool/action fields, not local harness inspection. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with mutation tests that paraphrase or alias each known active example while preserving the same parsed operation and target**.

### 2. Role transitions from inert text to executable action

The model catches a document phrase as a mention during reading and then creates a fresh case when that phrase enters a shell argument, SQL query, outbound request, or privileged instruction field. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to close the obvious “mention exemption becomes a laundering channel” failure mode; evidence is event reclassification at the sink and WHO's warning that shared failure modes invalidate serial confirmation [WHO, §7.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/#ch7.s9). The frequency of this trajectory in the owner's workloads is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying histories and counting mention-classified spans later copied into action-bearing fields**.

### 3. Dangerous structure assembled dynamically

If the sink sees dynamic evaluation or an unresolved target, the event becomes `indeterminate_use` rather than escaping because no complete forbidden string existed upstream. It is **likely (55-80%)** that this catches split-string and templated constructions that literal scans miss, because ambiguity is preserved at the action boundary. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with generated adversarial cases that concatenate, interpolate, encode, or indirectly reference the same prohibited action**.

### 4. False “outbreaks” produced by security and harness documents

A document enumerating five dangerous examples can yield one mention episode with five screen hits, rather than five actionable incidents. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce repeated guard firing on documents that describe policies or failures; evidence is the target problem's 12+ observed false fires and CDC's account of pseudo-outbreaks caused by erroneous reports [CDC, §D.2.f](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm). The exact reduction is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying all 12+ known false-fire cases through the staged adapter and requiring zero full interventions while retaining all suspect telemetry**.

## Adversarial assessment of the analogy

### Where the structure genuinely matches

1. **Rare-event screening:** both systems inspect many noncases to find a smaller set of consequential cases. This match is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by the pinned false-fire observation and CDC's explicit sensitivity/PPV framework.
2. **Follow-up has cost:** public-health case investigation consumes staff; a harness false intervention consumes turns, approvals, retries, and owner attention. This match is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by CDC's discussion of misdirected resources and the target problem's repeated false fires [CDC, §D.2.f](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).
3. **Context changes prior probability:** population prevalence changes diagnostic PPV; semantic role changes the active-use rate. This match is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by WHO's prevalence-stratified test strategy and CDC's recommendation to measure PPV by source/event [WHO, §§7.2 and 7.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/); [CDC, §D.2.f](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).
4. **Certainty is staged:** suspected and confirmed are useful operational states in both fields. This match is **likely (55-80%)**, because the classification structure transfers, though harness state can change within milliseconds rather than over a clinical investigation.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **There is no natural pathogen or stable patient truth.** “Use” is an event-relative semantic property. The same span can be a mention at read time and use at execution time. Therefore a permanent label on the text is invalid. This disanalogy is **almost certain (90-99%)**, evidenced by the distinct source and sink roles in the target problem.
2. **The detector can affect the event.** Blocking a tool call prevents the harmful action, so eventual outcome cannot naïvely serve as ground truth. Epidemiologic observation generally does not erase the pre-existing infection merely by recording it. This disanalogy is **very likely (80-90%)**; it requires counterfactual labels or safe simulation for blocked cases.
3. **Adversaries adapt.** Attackers can intentionally place active instructions in documentation-shaped text, unlike a pathogen deliberately crafting a markdown fence. Mention evidence therefore cannot be a blanket allowlist. This disanalogy is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the control/data boundary inherent in agent execution; the actual adversarial rate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with red-team role-confusion cases**.
4. **Test errors are highly correlated.** Regex, semantic classifier, and LLM judge may all consume the same text and share training or representation biases. WHO's warning about nominally different but fundamentally identical assays maps exactly here [WHO, §7.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/#ch7.s9). Mechanically different sink evidence is mandatory.
5. **Sensitivity cannot be inferred from alert logs.** Logs contain detected candidates, not undetected active uses. CDC explicitly requires an alternative high-quality source to measure missed outbreaks [CDC, *Early Detection Framework*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5305a1.htm). A replay gold set, seeded cases, and safe shadow execution are necessary.
6. **Consequences are not symmetric.** A false negative on catastrophic deletion can dominate many false-positive documentation warnings. Thresholds must be detector- and impact-specific; there is no single acceptable sensitivity/PPV point. CDC likewise treats calibration as dependent on risk, timeliness, resources, and acceptable false alarms [CDC, *Early Detection Framework*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5305a1.htm).

The analogy therefore transfers as a **measurement and classification discipline**, not as a claim that semantic guards behave like laboratory assays. That narrower conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**; evidence is that the useful elements—case definitions, staged certainty, independent confirmation, base-rate-aware PPV, and external sensitivity measurement—do not require biological causality, while the rejected elements do.

## Verification protocol

The recommendation remains **UNVERIFIED until this experiment is run**:

1. Build a blinded replay set containing all 12+ known mention false fires, a representative random sample of detector negatives, every available true intervention, and seeded active uses for each detector. Preserve complete event envelopes.
2. Establish reference labels with two mechanically independent signals: (a) human adjudication from the full trajectory and intended operation, and (b) a safe sink-level oracle such as parsed operation/target or sandboxed dry-run trace. Disagreements remain indeterminate rather than being forced.
3. Prove both detectors before absence claims: demonstrate that the lexical screen finds a known-present literal and that the sink oracle finds a known-present active operation. Only then use either to report misses.
4. Replay the current detector and staged detector in shadow mode. Do not let the new classifier alter execution during measurement.
5. Stratify by detector and source role: documentation/quoted artifact, user instruction, assistant proposal, generated code, tool input, tool output, and dynamic/opaque execution.
6. Report confusion matrices, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, indeterminate rate, and intervention cost per stratum. Do not collapse them into one accuracy value.
7. Gate enforcement on two conditions: all known mention false fires lose their full intervention, and seeded/known active cases retain detection at the real sink. For catastrophic sinks, any observed sensitivity regression blocks rollout regardless of PPV improvement.
8. Run adversarial transitions: mention → copy → execute; quoted instruction → privileged prompt field; split/encoded string → dynamic sink; benign-looking wrapper → destructive resolved target.
9. Canary one detector first, version the case definition, and retain instant config rollback. Expand only after new live events reproduce the replay direction.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a replay set of several hundred carefully stratified events will reveal the dominant classification errors for these five detectors; evidence is the narrow target problem and known false-fire seeds. Sample adequacy is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with binomial confidence intervals per detector/role stratum and continue sampling until the interval around the required sensitivity and PPV thresholds is decision-useful**.

## Rejected alternatives and dead ends

### Blanket-ignore quotes, code fences, and documents

Rejected. It would fix visible false fires by creating an obvious laundering channel when text crosses into execution. The classification belongs to each event and transition, not permanently to the span. This rejection is **very likely (80-90%)** correct, based on the role-transition disanalogy.

### Add more negative-lookaround regexes

Rejected as the main mechanism. Handwritten exclusions can reduce individual symptoms but cannot express source provenance, tool sink, target resolution, or later role changes. They also produce detector-specific exception drift. This rejection is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the mismatch between raw text and the operative-event case definition; a small local regex may still be justified as one feature, not as confirmation.

### Ask an LLM whether every match is use or mention

Rejected as default confirmation. It adds latency and nondeterminism and is not mechanically independent when it reads the same payload without typed sink evidence. A model may assist only for the `indeterminate_use` queue, with its uncertainty retained. This rejection is **likely (55-80%)**, based on WHO's correlated-assay warning by analogy; comparative harness evidence is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by testing an LLM judge against the sink oracle on the blinded replay set**.

### Require two lexical detectors to agree

Rejected. Two regexes over the same bytes are analogous to two assays with the same underlying false reaction. Agreement can multiply confidence without adding information. This rejection is **very likely (80-90%)**, directly supported by WHO's caution that “different” assays may be fundamentally the same [WHO, §7.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442276/#ch7.s9).

### Build a new policy daemon or semantic guardrail platform

Rejected. It violates the anti-overfit gate and is unnecessary for the three-state contract. There is no evidence in this unit that a new runtime yields enough incremental signal to justify its operational surface. This rejection is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the specified existing-bus constraint and CDC's simplicity principle [CDC, §D.2.a](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm).

### Optimize only for zero known false fires

Rejected. That objective can be met by disabling the detectors and says nothing about sensitivity. CDC's requirement for an alternative reference source to find misses makes this a measurement error, not merely a conservative trade-off [CDC, *Early Detection Framework*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5305a1.htm).
