# X156 — Archival science → synthesis

**Unit:** X156  
**Lens:** cross-domain borrow from archival science  
**Target:** collapse thousands of findings into an auditable decision without requiring a deciding agent to read the entire corpus  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** This is an ideation unit. I did not inspect or make claims about the local subject repository. References to current conditions below are restricted to the pinned inputs in the unit brief. The harness hooks section in the corpus is explicitly treated as no data, not evidence that hooks did not fire.

## Executive conclusion

The borrow transfers, but only as a **decision-control and navigation mechanism**, not as an automatic truth engine.

The specific archival mechanism worth borrowing is the linked combination of:

1. **aggregate appraisal and disposition schedules** — decide what class of material warrants permanent retention, temporary retention, review, or disposal, using declared functions and values rather than rereading every item;
2. **provenance and original order** — preserve who produced a record, in what activity, and where it sat in the producing sequence instead of flattening everything into topic snippets;
3. **multilevel description / finding aids** — describe the whole first, then series and items only as needed, with linked levels and no gratuitous repetition; and
4. **authenticity and chain-of-preservation controls** — preserve fixity and transformation history so a reader can distinguish an original finding from a later synthesis, while not confusing authenticity with truth.

The concrete harness translation is a **Decision Fonds**: immutable finding envelopes plus an automatically generated, progressively disclosed finding aid; a versioned appraisal schedule; a decision register; and a mandatory exception dossier containing contradictions, minority evidence, unverified premises, scope gaps, and discarded-class samples. The decision agent reads the decision register and exception dossier first and drills down through series descriptions to individual evidence only when required.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce synthesis context volume materially without a corresponding loss of decision-relevant evidence, because archival multilevel description is explicitly designed to represent a whole and its parts without repeating parent information, while NARA uses schedule-level appraisal to separate permanent from temporary records. This remains **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a blinded replay benchmark comparing decisions made from the Decision Fonds against decisions made from the full artifacts, with decisive-evidence recall, contradiction recall, decision agreement, token count, and elapsed time measured.** The probability is not higher because archival appraisal assumes comparatively stable institutional functions and trained human custodians; an agent-research run has unstable questions, correlated model errors, and evidence whose importance may emerge only after synthesis.

The design deliberately does **not** introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It rides ordinary files, JSONL, existing hooks/skills, and deterministic validation. It therefore passes the stated anti-overfit gate.

## 1. What archival science actually solved

### 1.1 Appraisal: control the mass by selecting at an aggregate level

Archival appraisal is not ordinary summarization. It is a governed decision about which records possess enough continuing value to be retained and which may be destroyed after a defined period. The Society of American Archivists (SAA) defines appraisal as “the process of identifying materials offered to an archives that have sufficient value to be accessioned.” Its cited archival literature frames appraisal as evaluation of a mass of source material and selection of the portion to keep; it also identifies functional characteristics, information quality, relationship to parallel sources, likely use and access constraints, and preservation cost as distinct appraisal considerations ([SAA Dictionary: appraisal](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/appraisal.html)).

NARA’s operational model makes the mechanism unusually concrete. Agencies identify and describe classes of records, propose dispositions and retention periods, and submit records schedules. NARA appraisers then assess the agency’s functions, practices, evidential value, and informational value, classify records as temporary or permanent, check that schedules are clear and implementable, and subject temporary dispositions to review and public comment ([NARA: NARA Appraisal and Approval](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/nara-review)). NARA states the two-sided purpose directly: preserve important records while avoiding unnecessary maintenance of too much documentation.

The key scale mechanism is the **record series / aggregate schedule item**. The rule is attached to a class generated by a function, not improvised item by item. Retention periods are set for record series, while exceptions such as litigation or unexpected archival value can require a particular record to be held longer ([SAA Dictionary: retention period](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/retention-period.html)). NARA likewise warns that aggregate schedules need local file plans mapping actual series into each aggregate item; a schedule alone can be too abstract for staff to apply ([NARA: Implementing Schedules](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/implementation)).

That exception principle matters. Appraisal achieves scale by defaulting at class level, but it is not supposed to make a class rule blind to an exceptional item.

### 1.2 Provenance and original order: preserve the context that makes an item intelligible

The archival principle of provenance keeps records of one creator together and interprets them through the activity and function that produced them. The SAA definition explains that archival significance depends heavily on creation context and that arrangement and description should relate to original purpose and function. Its cited formulation has two components: do not mix records of different provenance, and maintain the order in which the records were created and kept ([SAA Dictionary: provenance](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/provenance.html)).

Original order is not mystical. It is the organization and sequence established by the creator, retained because that order can reveal relationships and make records usable. The archival literature also explicitly cautions that original order is not sacrosanct and becomes difficult for virtual records assembled from many sources ([SAA Dictionary: original order](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/original-order.html)). This caveat is directly relevant to multi-agent research: a finding can have more than one meaningful context—unit, question, evidence source, correction chain, and final decision.

The important archival move is therefore not “never reorder.” It is “never destroy the producing context when adding access-oriented arrangements.” Topic indexes can coexist with provenance; they must not replace it.

### 1.3 Multilevel description and finding aids: describe the whole, then permit selective descent

The General International Standard Archival Description, ISAD(G), formalizes description from the general to the specific. Its four rules are: describe from general to specific; record information relevant to the level being described; link each description to the next higher unit and identify its level; and avoid repeating information already supplied at a higher level ([International Council on Archives: ISAD(G), second edition landing page and standard](https://www.ica.org/resource/isadg-general-international-standard-archival-description-second-edition/)).

SAA’s concise definition describes multilevel description as separate, interrelated descriptions of a whole and its parts that reflect the hierarchy of the materials. Each level carries information relevant only to that level, and subordinate descriptions link upward rather than repeating the parent ([SAA Dictionary: multilevel description](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/multilevel-description.html)). A finding aid then supplies intellectual control and acts as a road map into the collection; the SAA discussion specifically notes that encoding structure is necessary so a search hit reveals how the described material fits into the whole ([SAA Dictionary: finding aid](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/finding-aid.html)).

This is the closest direct answer to “how can a decision maker use 12 MB without reading 12 MB?” The archival answer is not one lossy mega-summary. It is a hierarchy of linked descriptions whose upper levels state scope, context, and selection logic, with lower levels available for audit and exceptions.

### 1.4 Authenticity: establish identity and integrity, not truth

Archival authenticity asks whether a record is what it claims to be and is free from tampering. SAA describes authenticity as inferred from internal and external evidence including physical characteristics, structure, content, and context. Crucially, it says authenticity does not automatically imply that a record’s content is reliable ([SAA Dictionary: authenticity](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/authenticity.html)). InterPARES’ preservation framework likewise treats identity and integrity metadata, custody, and documented preservation actions as requirements for maintaining authentic digital records ([InterPARES 2, *A Framework of Principles for the Development of Policies, Strategies and Standards for the Long-term Preservation of Digital Records*](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/ip2_book_appendix_14.pdf)).

That distinction prevents a dangerous analogy error. A hash can show that a finding has not changed since intake. It cannot show that its cited URL supports its conclusion, that its detector was valid, or that two agents did not repeat the same hallucination.

## 2. The translation: a Decision Fonds

“Fonds” is used here as a design metaphor for the body of findings organically produced by one research program. The implementation should use ordinary, unsurprising names; no operator should need archival vocabulary to run it.

### 2.1 Proposal A — immutable finding envelopes and a provenance-preserving manifest

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Require every unit artifact to emit, or be deterministically wrapped in, one or more newline-delimited finding envelopes. A minimal envelope would contain:

```json
{
  "finding_id": "X156:F03",
  "run_id": "2026-07-19-...",
  "producer": {"unit": "X156", "lens": "archival-science", "agent": "codex"},
  "created_at": "2026-07-19T...Z",
  "sequence": 3,
  "claim": "...",
  "claim_type": "proposal|verified-fact|gap|counterevidence|decision",
  "scope": {"includes": ["..."], "excludes": ["..."]},
  "evidence": [{"locator": "https://...", "kind": "primary-source", "supports": "..."}],
  "confidence_band": "likely (55-80%)",
  "verification": {"state": "unverified", "settling_experiment": "..."},
  "relations": {
    "supports": ["decision:..."],
    "contradicts": ["X042:F07"],
    "supersedes": [],
    "derived_from": []
  },
  "proposal_class": "BUILD-SMALL",
  "anti_overfit": {"existing_bus": true, "rejected_component": null},
  "content_sha256": "..."
}
```

Keep the authored artifact as the record; the envelope is its catalog entry, not a replacement. Preserve original unit grouping and sequence as the primary arrangement. Build topic, defect, mechanism, and decision indexes as secondary access paths.

This would catch several synthesis failures that a flat folder or concatenated report does not catch reliably:

- an orphan conclusion with no evidence locator;
- a proposal lacking the required NATIVE-vs-BUILD value;
- an uncertain claim without the mandated confidence vocabulary;
- an UNVERIFIED premise without a settling experiment;
- a correction that exists but is not linked to the finding it supersedes;
- two textually similar findings that came from the same producer or source and therefore are not independent corroboration;
- a decision whose support graph contains only derivative summaries and no primary evidence leaf.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a deterministic envelope validator would catch missing required fields, because those are syntactic invariants and the pinned corpus already reports schema-related failures as a measurable class. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to recover correct semantic links such as contradiction or supersession when authors omit them, because text similarity is not dependable evidence of logical relation. The latter is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by hand-labeling relations among 300 stratified findings and measuring precision/recall of proposed links, with auto-linking prohibited unless both exceed a predeclared threshold.**

Do not force deeply nested fan-out schemas at generation time. Accept a small stable core, place optional extensions in a permissive object, validate locally, and quarantine malformed envelopes rather than retrying the same over-strict model call. This design directly respects the pinned ground truth that StructuredOutput had a 22.9% error rate and 294 schema mismatches.

### 2.2 Proposal B — a versioned appraisal and retention schedule for research outputs

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a human-readable `appraisal-schedule.yaml` that assigns defaults to *series*, not individual files. A plausible initial schedule is:

| Series | Default disposition | Rationale | Mandatory exception / hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pinned measured ground truth and owner corrections | Permanent | Governs later interpretation and prevents rediscovery | Never silently supersede; link replacement and preserve old version |
| Primary-evidence extracts and verification reports | Permanent through decision and audit horizon | Allows claims to be rechecked | Hold if cited by any live decision or contradiction |
| Unit-level findings and counterfindings | Permanent through decision and audit horizon | Core intellectual record | Hold minority, contradictory, high-impact, or UNVERIFIED findings |
| Generated series summaries and decision briefs | Versioned permanent | Shows what a decision maker actually saw | Preserve derivation manifest and exact input set |
| Exact duplicate downloads and convenience copies | Temporary after fixity/dedup audit | Low marginal information | Hold if annotations, different custody, or use in a decision makes the copy unique |
| Failed probes and detector diagnostics | Selective; retain fingerprints plus representative examples | Useful for reliability without retaining every repetition | Hold first occurrence, terminal occurrence, new fingerprint, owner correction, and detector-control result |
| Raw retry-loop repetitions | Temporary after loop digest | Repetition is usually operational noise | Hold any retry with a changed input, changed state, or novel error |
| Secrets or restricted payloads | Reference-only metadata; content governed separately | Minimize unnecessary exposure | Legal/security policy overrides research schedule |

Each schedule rule needs: series identifier, functional scope, inclusion/exclusion examples, retention trigger, disposition, review date, authority/owner, and exception predicates. Any item not matching a schedule goes to `UNSCHEDULED`, never to deletion. Changes are prospective; previously synthesized decisions retain the schedule version used.

This would catch “unknown unknown records” in a mundane but valuable way: an unscheduled series becomes visible instead of disappearing into an `other` bucket. It would also expose retention rules that are too broad to apply, a failure mode NARA addresses by requiring file plans that map local records to aggregate schedules. NARA further requires periodic review and identifies unclear descriptions, unimplementable disposition instructions, and too-long or too-short retention as revision triggers ([NARA: Updating Schedules](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/updates)).

It is **likely (55-80%)** that series-level schedules will reduce duplicate/noise material presented to synthesis while retaining auditability, because this is the scale advantage of archival scheduling and because retry repetitions and convenience copies are identifiable classes. It is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by applying the proposed schedule to a frozen historical run, having two reviewers independently classify a stratified sample, measuring inter-reviewer agreement, and then checking whether any disposed candidate was uniquely decisive in a full-corpus reference decision.** No automatic physical deletion is authorized by this proposal; the first implementation should alter the synthesis view, not the underlying artifacts.

### 2.3 Proposal C — generate a multilevel finding aid, not a single mega-summary

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Generate four linked levels from the manifest:

```text
L0  Decision register
    one row per decision: status, chosen option, decisive support, strongest objection,
    confidence, unresolved experiment, accountable owner

L1  Program / phase / lens descriptions
    mandate, scope, dates, producer set, methodology, coverage counts, exclusions,
    appraisal schedule version, conflict counts, evidence-quality distribution

L2  Series and finding cards
    claim, evidence leaves, independence class, relations, confidence, verification state,
    proposals and anti-overfit status

L3  Original artifacts and source captures
    immutable authored reports, command outputs where retained, external sources
```

Apply ISAD(G)’s rules literally where they fit: go general to specific; store information at the level where it applies; link upward; do not repeat parent context in every child. The output should be static Markdown plus JSONL so both humans and agents can navigate it. The L0 register is not permitted to omit the strongest objection or unresolved settling experiment. Every L0 evidence reference must resolve through L2 to an L3 source or be marked `BROKEN-LINK`.

The deciding agent’s reading protocol becomes:

1. Read the pinned ground truth, known operating defects, L0 decision register, and exception dossier.
2. For each high-impact decision, read the L1 descriptions of all contributing and dissenting series.
3. Descend to L2 for decisive support, strongest objection, low-independence clusters, and every UNVERIFIED premise.
4. Descend to L3 whenever evidence support is disputed, the claim is negative/absence-based, authenticity fails, or the decision is irreversible/high cost.
5. Record the exact IDs and versions consulted in the decision record.

This would catch what a one-shot summary misses: whether “ten findings agree” means ten independent investigations or ten descendants of one source; whether an entire lens produced no findings or merely was never run; whether a minority counterexample was appraised away; whether a high-level statement silently widened the scope of its children; and whether the deciding agent ever opened the evidence behind a high-impact negative claim.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that deterministic link and count checks will reveal broken references, uncovered series, and disagreement counts omitted from prose, because the manifest makes these mechanically enumerable. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that the L0+exception-first reading protocol alone will preserve expert-quality decisions on novel architecture questions, because the archival sources establish navigability and context, not decision equivalence under lossy abstraction. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the blinded replay benchmark in section 5, and require explicit drill-down rather than raising the confidence estimate by assertion.**

### 2.4 Proposal D — mandatory exception dossier (“archival hold” for synthesis)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Appraisal’s dangerous edge is that class-level selection can erase the item that later overturns the decision. The harness should therefore generate a dossier that bypasses normal compression. Include every item matching at least one of these predicates:

- contradicts a candidate decision or a high-confidence finding;
- is the sole support for a high-impact decision;
- has low or unknown source independence;
- is UNVERIFIED or has a failed/incomplete settling experiment;
- makes an absence, brokenness, completeness, or “done” claim without two mechanically independent signals and a known-present detector control;
- records an owner correction;
- reports scope exclusion, inaccessible source, detector failure, or sampling limitation;
- is a minority result in a series otherwise summarized as consensus;
- was proposed for disposition but has unusually high novelty, severity, or downstream degree;
- fails authenticity/fixity checks;
- comes from an unscheduled series;
- depends on a rejected anti-overfit component.

For each excluded/disposed class, retain its count, stable IDs, rule invoked, and a deterministic random sample for audit. This is appraisal accountability: the synthesis agent need not read every repeated item, but it can see what kinds and how much material were kept out.

This would specifically catch the failure pattern emphasized in the unit brief: a broken detector masquerading as an absence, or a single unvalidated probe becoming a confident conclusion. A qualifying negative claim cannot disappear into a majority summary; it is promoted to the exception dossier until its detector control and independent signals are present.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit predicates will surface formally marked UNVERIFIED findings, owner corrections, broken links, and missing detector-control fields, because each is mechanically testable. It is only **likely (55-80%)** that the dossier will surface every substantive minority insight, because novelty and contradiction detection depend on author-supplied relations or fallible semantic classification. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a stratified human audit of 300 findings, oversampling low-frequency series and comparing dossier inclusion to reviewer judgments of “could reverse or materially qualify a decision.”**

### 2.5 Proposal E — fixity and derivation manifests for synthetic products

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For each authored artifact, calculate a content hash and record size, producer, creation time, and source path. For every synthesis product, record:

- exact input finding IDs and hashes;
- appraisal schedule and generator versions;
- exclusions and their rule IDs;
- prompt/model identity when an LLM produces prose;
- deterministic counts computed before prose generation;
- output hash; and
- supersession links if regenerated.

The validator should recompute hashes, resolve every input, and prove that the number of included, excluded, exception, malformed, and unscheduled items sums to the intake total. A synthetic brief is authentic only relative to explicit claims—e.g., “this is the output generated from these inputs under schedule v3”—not because its conclusions are true.

This would catch silent post-hoc edits, a summary regenerated from a different input set under the same filename, missing findings, double counting, and a decision based on a stale brief. It would not catch a source that was false when first captured.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that fixity checks will detect byte-level alteration after hashing, absent hash collisions or compromise of both artifact and manifest; that confidence is supported by the deterministic property being tested, not by archival analogy. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that reconciliation totals will catch accidental omission or duplication when every intake item receives exactly one state. Whether the current production bus exposes all lifecycle events needed to automate capture is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by instrumenting one shadow run and reconciling filesystem enumeration, unit completion records, and manifest intake as three separate counts.**

### 2.6 Proposal F — an explicit decision record as the archival “accession” outcome

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

The final synthesis must not merely output recommendations. It must accession decisions into a register with:

- decision ID and exact question;
- alternatives considered, including “do nothing” and “replace” where applicable;
- chosen alternative and owner-visible rationale;
- decisive finding IDs;
- strongest counterfinding IDs;
- independence assessment of supporting signals;
- confidence band and evidence for the band;
- inherited UNVERIFIED premises and their experiments;
- NATIVE-vs-BUILD value;
- anti-overfit gate result;
- reversibility, blast radius, and rollback evidence;
- expiry/review trigger; and
- supersedes/superseded-by links.

This catches a recurrent synthesis pathology: polished prose that contains no inspectable commitment, or a proposal whose uncertainty and dependencies vanish between research and roadmap. It also enables later appraisal: a finding cited by a live decision is automatically held.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a structured decision register will make disagreements and stale premises easier to review, because it exposes the relation between decision and evidentiary record. Whether it improves the substantive choice is **UNVERIFIED; settle it in the replay benchmark by scoring decision correctness/acceptability separately from traceability and reviewer time.**

## 3. What this would catch that the present synthesis brief says is being missed

## 4. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 4.1 Structural correspondences that do transfer

1. **Both domains face superabundance.** Archives cannot preserve and item-describe every record at equal depth; a synthesis agent cannot attentively read every artifact. Aggregate control and selective descent address the same resource constraint.

2. **Both require later accountability.** An archival researcher must know where a description came from; an owner must know which finding and evidence justified a harness decision. Provenance and derivation manifests serve this shared need.

3. **Both contain organic groupings.** Records arise from functions and offices; research artifacts arise from program phases, units, lenses, prompts, tools, and sources. Those groupings carry information that topical flattening destroys.

4. **Both need class rules plus exceptions.** Retention schedules scale because they apply to series, while holds protect exceptional items. Synthesis needs the same separation between default compression and “must surface” predicates.

5. **Both benefit from intellectual control before item-level treatment.** A series description can establish scope and context before item review; a lens/phase description can tell a deciding agent what was and was not investigated before it reads individual claims.

These correspondences make it **likely (55-80%)** that multilevel, provenance-aware control transfers as a navigation and accountability layer. The evidence is the close match between the resource constraint and the operations described in ISAD(G), SAA definitions, and NARA scheduling practice. The remaining uncertainty must be resolved by the replay benchmark, because no cited archival source tests agent-harness decisions.

### 4.2 Disanalogies that can make the borrow fail

#### A. Archival value is not technical truth or optimality

Appraisal decides what is worth retaining under a mandate. It does not adjudicate whether a claim is correct or which architecture will perform best. Provenance may increase interpretability without increasing truth.

**Design consequence:** never turn retention class, citation count, producer prestige, or authentic hash into a truth score. Evidence support and experiment results remain separate fields.

#### B. Archival functions are more stable than research questions

Governmental series often recur under known functions. A frontier research program can discover a new decision dimension late, making an earlier “temporary” class suddenly decisive.

**Design consequence:** first apply disposition only to the *synthesis view*, preserve underlying artifacts through the audit horizon, version schedules, and treat new/unscheduled series as exceptions. Physical deletion is outside this unit.

#### C. Original order is ambiguous for born-digital, multi-agent findings

A finding may be authored in one unit, cite another unit, correct an earlier run, and support several decisions. One tree cannot express all of these relations. Archival literature itself notes the problem for virtual records assembled from many authors and data sources ([SAA Dictionary: original order](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/original-order.html)).

**Design consequence:** preserve one primary production order and add typed cross-links and secondary indexes. Do not force a single topical hierarchy, and do not infer independence from folder separation.

#### D. Archivists traditionally apply human judgment; automated appraisal can amplify model bias

If the same model writes a finding, appraises it, summarizes it, and decides, correlated errors can become invisible. A class rule can systematically suppress unconventional evidence.

**Design consequence:** deterministic predicates handle formal properties; humans or separately instructed review passes audit semantic appraisal; minority and random disposed-class samples are mandatory. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that fully automatic semantic appraisal is safe enough for irreversible harness decisions without audit, because no independent evidence here establishes its recall and the correlated-error structure is unlike a neutral cataloging operation. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the stratified appraisal audit and decision replay, not deployment optimism.**

#### E. “More context” can be the evidence

In archives, surrounding files may reveal meaning. In agent traces, the exact failed command, quoting, environment state, and retry sequence can reveal a root cause that an error fingerprint omits. Overaggressive deduplication destroys process evidence.

**Design consequence:** deduplicate only byte-identical or normalized-safe classes in the synthesis view; preserve first, terminal, state-changing, and novel-error events and maintain counts plus source pointers.

#### F. Retention and synthesis are different time horizons

Archival retention answers what survives over time. Synthesis answers what must be presented now. A record can deserve long retention but have no bearing on the current decision, or be ephemeral yet immediately decisive.

**Design consequence:** keep separate fields for `retention_disposition` and `synthesis_priority`. Never overload one as the other.

### 4.3 Bottom line of the adversarial test

The analogy **does transfer** for:

- preserving producing context;
- governing selection transparently;
- progressive disclosure;
- exception/hold handling;
- authenticity and derivation; and
- auditing what was excluded.

It **does not transfer** for:

- truth verification;
- source independence by itself;
- technical ranking;
- automatic discovery of every important relation;
- proof that a summary is decision-equivalent to full reading; or
- justification for deleting original research immediately.

Any implementation that claims the second list on the strength of archival precedent would be a forced analogy and should be rejected.

## 5. Required experiment: Decision-Fonds replay benchmark

This experiment settles the central UNVERIFIED premise rather than asking the owner to trust the analogy.

### 5.1 Corpus and sampling

Select 20-30 historical decision questions stratified by:

- replacement versus incremental change;
- operational reliability versus new capability;
- high versus low finding count;
- strong consensus versus known conflict;
- presence versus absence of owner corrections;
- availability versus absence of executable verification; and
- reversible versus costly/irreversible decision.

Freeze the complete artifact set before generating any finding aid. Have a reference team construct a gold evidence map after unrestricted full-corpus access. “Gold” should mean adjudicated, not assumed infallible.

### 5.2 Arms

1. **Full corpus:** deciding agent gets all artifacts and the original brief.
2. **Flat mega-summary:** deciding agent gets a conventional bounded summary plus on-demand search.
3. **Decision Fonds:** deciding agent gets pinned inputs, L0, L1, exception dossier, and on-demand L2/L3 drill-down.

Rotate models or reviewers across arms to reduce reviewer-specific effects. Prevent an agent from seeing the same decision in multiple arms. Equalize total elapsed-time limits, but record tokens and files opened.

### 5.3 Predeclared primary metrics

- recall of adjudicated decisive evidence;
- recall of adjudicated counterevidence;
- rate of unsupported claims in final decisions;
- decision agreement with the adjudicated reference outcome;
- correct propagation of UNVERIFIED premises;
- correct identification of non-independent support;
- context tokens consumed and artifacts opened;
- time to an auditable decision; and
- traceability: proportion of final rationale statements resolving to evidence leaves.

### 5.4 Safety thresholds

Do not promote the approach merely because it saves tokens. A reasonable precommitment is:

- no statistically or practically meaningful degradation in decisive-evidence or counterevidence recall relative to full corpus;
- zero silent loss of owner corrections in the test set;
- zero absence/brokenness claims accepted without required detector controls;
- at least 95% final rationale-to-evidence link resolution;
- material median context reduction, defined before seeing results; and
- every omitted gold-decisive item examined to determine which appraisal or description rule failed.

The exact acceptable decision-agreement margin is **UNVERIFIED; settle it before the benchmark through owner risk tolerance and the reversibility class of each decision, rather than selecting a favorable threshold after results are known.**

### 5.5 Detector validation and independence

Before claiming the finding aid omitted or found any class, validate each detector on a known-present control. Use two mechanically independent reconciliation signals:

1. enumerate authored artifacts from the frozen filesystem/input manifest; and
2. independently total unit completions or run-controller receipts.

Then compare both with manifest intake and state reconciliation. Re-running the same enumeration is not a second signal.

### 5.6 Failure interpretation

- If the Decision Fonds saves context but misses minority counterevidence, keep provenance/fixity but reject or widen semantic appraisal.
- If relation linking has low recall, require author-declared links and human exception review; do not add a knowledge graph.
- If L0 decisions drift while L2/L3 contains the right evidence, revise the reading protocol and surface triggers.
- If the full-corpus arm also misses evidence, do not count that as proof that compression is safe; report both absolute performance and relative difference.
- If performance depends on one model, classify portability as **UNVERIFIED; settle it with at least one materially different model family or human reviewer.**

## 6. Minimal implementation shape and anti-overfit gate

### 6.1 Files, not a new platform

```text
run/
  artifacts/                    # authored originals, unchanged
  control/
    intake.jsonl                # one immutable envelope per finding/artifact
    appraisal-schedule.yaml     # versioned class rules
    derivations.jsonl           # input/output hashes and transformations
    malformed.jsonl             # quarantined records; never silently dropped
  access/
    00-decision-register.md      # L0
    01-exception-dossier.md      # bypasses compression
    series/                      # L1
    findings/                    # L2 cards or indexes
    indexes/                     # topic, mechanism, decision, producer
  decisions/
    decision-records.jsonl
```

One small validator/generator can:

1. parse the stable envelope core;
2. hash originals;
3. reconcile counts;
4. apply deterministic appraisal and exception rules;
5. generate Markdown indexes and tables;
6. flag unresolved links and malformed items; and
7. exit nonzero when mandatory controls fail.

LLM-generated prose may populate candidate series descriptions, but all counts, IDs, hashes, missing-field flags, and exception membership must be computed deterministically. Generated descriptions remain derived records with their own provenance.

### 6.2 Explicit anti-overfit evaluation

| Candidate component | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| JSONL envelope and static Markdown finding aid | Adopt | Existing file/hook/skill bus; inspectable and replaceable |
| Small deterministic validator/generator | Adopt | Bounded mechanism, no orchestration authority |
| Versioned YAML schedule | Adopt | Configuration, human-reviewable |
| Vector database | Reject | Previously rejected class; hierarchy plus indexes and direct links suffice for the initial test |
| Knowledge graph database | Reject | Typed relations fit JSONL; a database adds operational surface before link quality is established |
| Second agent orchestration runtime | Reject | The mechanism is post/intake control, not scheduling |
| Durable workflow engine | Reject | File reconciliation and rerunnable generation suffice |
| Trained critic | Reject | Benchmark deterministic checks and review protocol first |
| Observability SaaS | Reject | Static local artifacts provide the required audit evidence |
| General guardrail framework | Reject | Narrow validators enforce the actual invariants |

No rejected component is reintroduced. If scale later proves static generation inadequate, that premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring generator wall time, peak memory, artifact count, and index-open latency on the largest run before proposing BUILD-LARGE.**

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### A. One enormous abstractive summary

Rejected. It has no stable relation between omitted material and selection rationale, tends to erase minority evidence, and cannot expose whether ten claims share one provenance. It borrows none of archival science’s key control mechanisms.

### B. Pure retrieval over all raw artifacts

Rejected as the primary mechanism. Retrieval is useful for L3 access, but a query cannot reliably retrieve a dimension the decision maker does not know to ask about. It also does not provide appraisal accountability, coverage totals, or a record of what was excluded. A secondary full-text index is compatible with this design; a vector database is not justified.

### C. Rank findings only by confidence or vote count

Rejected. Confidence is not evidentiary independence; vote count can multiply a shared source; both can suppress rare counterexamples. Provenance and contradiction handling must precede aggregation.

### D. Have a “chief archivist agent” read and curate everything

Rejected. That recreates the original context bottleneck in one agent and adds a single correlated judgment point. The useful archival borrow is explicit schedule, description hierarchy, and inspectable exceptions—not an archival persona.

### E. Delete aggressively according to a retention schedule

Rejected for initial adoption. Synthesis priority and long-term retention are not the same, the transfer is unverified, and importance may emerge late. The initial schedule filters views while retaining underlying artifacts through an audit horizon.

### F. Build Records in Contexts as a full ontology/knowledge graph

Rejected for now. Rich contextual modeling is intellectually attractive, but it would cross the anti-overfit gate into infrastructure before relation-extraction quality and decision benefit are proven. Typed JSONL relations preserve the useful idea without a graph runtime.
