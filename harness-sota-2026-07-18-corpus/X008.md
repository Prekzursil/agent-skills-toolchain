# X008 — Cross-domain borrow: clinical medicine → failure recovery

**Unit:** X008  
**Lens:** differential diagnosis, iatrogenics, contraindication records, and number-needed-to-treat  
**Target:** resuming useful work after a worker, session, or machine dies mid-task  
**Scope discipline:** external clinical evidence and translation only. Per the unit brief, this report does not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation.

## Executive conclusion

The analogy transfers, but only in a constrained form. The useful clinical borrow is a **recovery reconciliation protocol**: reconstruct the best possible pre-failure task history from more than one source; compare it with the present state; maintain a short differential for what may have happened during the observation gap; use low-risk discriminating probes; then screen the proposed continuation against a small, severity-tiered registry of replay contraindications. This is stronger than “load the last checkpoint and continue,” because a checkpoint is analogous to an old medication list: it records what somebody believed or intended, not necessarily what the patient actually took or what is safe now.

That conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on a strong structural match between clinical transitions and agent restarts: both involve a change of actor, incomplete observations, stale representations of state, and interventions whose repetition can itself cause harm. The evidence for the clinical mechanisms is substantial: WHO defines medication reconciliation as building a best possible history, comparing it with current orders, resolving discrepancies, documenting changes, and communicating the result; a nine-hospital I-PASS study found an associated 23% relative reduction in medical errors and 30% relative reduction in preventable adverse events after a standardized handoff bundle. The exact benefit in an agent harness is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is a randomized replay study over real interrupted tasks comparing baseline recovery with reconciliation-assisted recovery, scored for correct continuation, duplicate side effects, time-to-useful-work, and unnecessary blocks**.

The analogy does **not** justify a second orchestration runtime, event-sourced durable-execution engine, universal checkpoint daemon, vector database, or autonomous “doctor agent.” Those components would exceed what is borrowed. The minimal implementation rides the existing hook/skill/tool bus and writes a small recovery capsule; on restart, a skill performs read-only reconciliation before any risky replay. This passes the anti-overfit gate because it adds a bounded decision aid rather than another executor.

The most important adversarial finding is that **differential diagnosis alone is not enough**. Medicine reasons about a largely hidden biological state, whereas software and tool state is often directly queryable. Generating many recovery hypotheses when a definitive read-only query exists wastes time and creates model confabulation surface. Differential reasoning transfers only for the residual ambiguity after direct state inspection.

## 1. What clinical medicine actually solved

### 1.1 Differential diagnosis is iterative hypothesis management, not list generation

The National Academies’ model of diagnosis describes an iterative cycle of information gathering, integration and interpretation, and generation of a working diagnosis. A working diagnosis can be a single hypothesis or a differential; hypotheses are refined as more information arrives. It also says verification checks whether the lead diagnosis adequately explains the evidence and fits the patient’s context ([National Academies, *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care*, chapter 2](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21794/chapter/4); the same chapter is preserved by [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)).

AHRQ’s probability-based description makes the decision mechanics explicit: begin with pre-test probabilities, update them using history and examination evidence, and select tests in light of both informativeness and the possibility that testing can help or harm ([AHRQ, “Probability and the Diagnostic Pathway”](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/probabilistic-thinking3.html)). The clinically mature mechanism therefore has four parts:

1. Preserve multiple plausible explanations when the evidence does not warrant closure.
2. Rank them using prior prevalence and case-specific evidence.
3. Choose tests that discriminate among them.
4. Update or abandon hypotheses as evidence arrives.

This mechanism exists partly to counter premature closure and confirmation bias. AHRQ identifies premature closure, confirmation bias, base-rate neglect, and availability bias among diagnostic-error-associated biases; it also notes that evidence for generic diagnostic checklists is mixed and that content-specific checklists appear more promising, although the evidence is thin ([AHRQ current state of diagnostic safety](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxsafety-current-state3.html); [AHRQ on content-specific versus process-focused checklists](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)). A diagnostic timeout is a deliberate pause to reassess the diagnosis when uncertainty or error risk is high, rather than a mandate to reconsider everything on every encounter ([AHRQ Patient Safety Learning Lab](https://www.ahrq.gov/patient-safety/resources/learning-lab/improving-safety-diagnosis-long-desc.html); [AHRQ PSNet review of diagnostic timeouts](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/diagnostic-time-outs-improve-diagnosis)).

The key lesson for recovery is consequently not “always brainstorm five causes.” It is “do not collapse an ambiguous observation gap into a single story, and spend probes in proportion to uncertainty and consequence.”

### 1.2 Medication reconciliation repairs state at vulnerable transitions

WHO’s medication-safety report calls transitions of care a priority because medication discrepancies occur when patients move between settings. Its protocol has three concrete steps:

1. Build a **Best Possible Medication History (BPMH)** by a systematic interview and verification against at least one other reliable source.
2. Compare the BPMH with prescribed medication, identify and resolve discrepancies, and document changes.
3. Communicate the accurate current list and reasons for changes to the patient and the next care provider.

WHO further distinguishes intentional documented discrepancies, intentional but undocumented discrepancies, and unintentional discrepancies; the latter include omission and commission errors ([WHO, *Medication Safety in Transitions of Care*, pp. 12–13 in the PDF](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/who-uhc-sds-2019-9-eng.pdf); [WHO publication landing page](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-UHC-SDS-2019.9)). This distinction matters: a difference between the old list and the new order is not automatically an error. It may be an intentional treatment change whose rationale was lost.

The Joint Commission makes the corresponding anti-pattern explicit: transitions require a process to ensure coordination and use of the most recent orders, but that process may not use blanket orders to resume medications ([Joint Commission FAQ on cancelling and rewriting orders after transitions](https://www.jointcommission.org/en-us/knowledge-library/support-center/standards-interpretation/standards-faqs/000002044)). That is almost exactly the dangerous recovery pattern in agent work: replaying all pending actions or treating the last plan as current merely because it was current before interruption.

Clinical handoff standardization supplies outcome evidence, although it is not specific to machine failures. In a prospective nine-hospital pre/post intervention study covering 10,740 pediatric admissions, the I-PASS bundle standardized oral and written handoffs and was associated with a fall in medical errors from 24.5 to 18.8 per 100 admissions (23% relative reduction) and preventable adverse events from 4.7 to 3.3 per 100 admissions (30% relative reduction), without a significant increase in handoff duration. The authors explicitly note that the study design cannot definitively establish causality and that the bundle prevents attribution to any single component ([Starmer et al., NEJM 2014](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556)). This is evidence that structured transition information can matter; it is not evidence that an agent recovery capsule will produce the same effect size.

### 1.3 Iatrogenics: recovery actions can create the next failure

In medicine, an adverse event may arise from care rather than from the underlying disease. The Joint Commission distinguishes serious patient-safety events from the natural course of illness and requires investigation of causes and corrective actions for sentinel events ([Joint Commission Sentinel Event policy](https://www.jointcommission.org/en-us/knowledge-library/support-center/standards-interpretation/sentinel-event-policy-and-procedures)). The transferable idea is causal separation:

- primary failure: the worker/session/machine died;
- latent task damage: an action was left incomplete or its result became unknown;
- recovery-induced damage: the replacement worker duplicated, reversed, or contradicted an action while trying to resume.

This taxonomy matters because a recovery system evaluated only on “did work eventually continue?” can look successful while silently causing duplicates or state corruption. Clinical safety treats benefit and treatment-caused harm as separate outcomes; an agent recovery evaluation should do the same.

### 1.4 Contraindications are patient-specific action guards, not a universal blacklist

FDA labeling guidance says a contraindication is appropriate when risk in a defined scenario outweighs potential benefit and rechallenge would be clinically inappropriate. It requires precise wording, the affected situation, and the observed or anticipated consequence; it also recognizes contraindications caused by dangerous drug combinations ([FDA, *Warnings and Precautions, Contraindications, and Boxed Warning Sections of Labeling*, especially pp. 8–10](https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Warnings-and-Precautions--Contraindications--and-Boxed-Warning-Sections-of-Labeling-for-Human-Prescription-Drug-and-Biological-Products-%E2%80%94-Content-and-Format.pdf)).

Clinical systems combine general knowledge with patient-specific records at the point of action. CMS’s EHR certification material describes real-time drug-drug and drug-allergy contraindication notifications based on the medication list, allergy list, and computerized order entry, with adjustable notification behavior ([CMS drug-interaction-check specification](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/legislation/ehrincentiveprograms/downloads/2013definition_2_drug_interaction_checks.pdf)).

There is an important terminology correction: clinical medicine does not rely on one universal “contraindication registry” containing every prohibited act for every patient. The operational pattern is distributed: authoritative product labeling and interaction knowledge are combined with the individual’s allergy, medication, condition, and prior-reaction records. The harness translation should preserve that two-level structure: general action classes plus task/resource-specific “do not replay” facts.

Clinical evidence also exposes the failure mode of naive alerting. A retrospective study of 611,192 drug-allergy alerts in two academic hospitals found override rates increasing from 83.3% to 87.6%; more than half of override reasons pointed to irrelevant alerts, such as prior tolerance, and repeated alerts were overridden more often ([Topaz et al., *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11740751/)). An expert-panel study recommends making low-priority interactions non-interruptive to reduce alert fatigue ([Phansalkar et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628052/)). Thus, “add a warning for every risky tool” is directly contradicted by the source field’s experience.

### 1.5 Number needed to treat forces absolute, comparator-specific evaluation

The original NNT paper is Laupacis, Sackett, and Roberts’ 1988 “An assessment of clinically useful measures of the consequences of treatment” ([PubMed record and DOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3374545/)). Cochrane defines NNT as the expected number receiving an experimental rather than comparator intervention for one additional beneficial or harmful outcome over a stated time frame. It emphasizes that NNT is comparative, is not an intrinsic property of an intervention, depends on baseline risk, and must distinguish benefit (NNTB) from harm (NNTH). It is derived from the absolute risk difference and conventionally rounded up ([Cochrane Handbook, §15.4](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

This is valuable for harness architecture because relative improvements can hide low absolute value. If a recovery feature reduces a rare 0.2% failure to 0.1%, that is a 50% relative reduction but an absolute reduction of 0.1 percentage points: 1,000 interrupted tasks would need the feature for one additional success, before counting feature-caused harm or latency. Conversely, a modest relative effect in a high-risk stratum can be worth deploying.

NNT also supplies a warning against pooling incomparable populations. Cochrane notes that absolute effects differ with baseline risk and time horizon, and that NNT must be interpreted for a specific comparator and population ([Cochrane Handbook, §15.4.2–15.4.4](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)). An all-task “number needed to recover” would therefore be misleading if it blended read-only research, local edits, deployments, payments, and external messages.

## 2. Structural mapping and its limits

| Clinical structure | Recovery structure | Strength of transfer | Critical disanalogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient moves between care teams | Task moves from dead worker/session to replacement | Strong | Human handoff often has a live sender; crash recovery usually does not. Therefore the written capsule and independent state inspection matter more than oral exchange. |
| Medication history may differ from prescriptions and actual use | Plan/checkpoint may differ from tool calls and external effects | Strong | Software state can often be queried exactly; do not substitute prose reconciliation for authoritative state APIs. |
| Differential retains competing explanations for symptoms | Recovery differential retains competing explanations for the last ambiguous action | Moderate | Clinical hypotheses concern causal disease; recovery hypotheses often concern transaction state and can be settled by deterministic IDs, hashes, or status queries. |
| A test updates probabilities but can cause cost or harm | A probe distinguishes recovery states but can mutate state, consume quota, or disclose data | Strong | Many software probes can be truly read-only; prefer them before probabilistic reasoning. |
| Re-administering a drug can be contraindicated for this patient | Replaying a tool action can be contraindicated for this resource/task | Strong | Contraindication knowledge in software can often be mechanically derived from idempotency and operation IDs, so the registry should not remain free text only. |
| Treatment can produce iatrogenic harm | Recovery can duplicate a send/deploy/delete/charge or overwrite newer work | Strong | Software harms may be exactly reversible; severity must consider reversibility, not merely action category. |
| NNT compares treatment with control over time | Number-needed-to-reconcile compares a recovery mechanism with baseline over a task window | Moderate | Task populations drift rapidly as models and tools improve, undermining stable effect estimates. Continuous stratified measurement is required. |

### Why the core analogy survives adversarial review

The analogy survives because its invariant is not “agents are patients.” The invariant is **decision-making across an information discontinuity where an intervention based on stale state can cause additional harm**. WHO’s reconciliation protocol does not require biological causality to work; it requires multiple imperfect representations of prior and current state, a transition, and consequences for acting on discrepancies. Agent recovery has all three.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that reconciliation before replay will reduce at least some duplicate or contradictory recovery actions, based on the direct match between ambiguous execution status and medication-list discrepancies plus the I-PASS association between structured handoffs and fewer errors. The magnitude and net benefit are **UNVERIFIED; settle with the randomized interrupted-task experiment defined in §5**.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **Medicine lacks authoritative direct inspection for much internal state.** A patient’s physiological state is only partially observable; many agent-side systems expose exact transaction IDs, commit hashes, message IDs, deployment status, or filesystem contents. When an authoritative read-only query exists, use it. A “differential” is not permission for the model to narrate possibilities instead of checking facts.

2. **Clinical urgency and software urgency differ.** Delay can itself harm a patient, so clinicians sometimes treat before certainty. Most coding/research recoveries tolerate seconds of inspection. The harness default can be “inspect before mutate,” with an explicit urgency override rather than a medical-style presumption of immediate intervention.

3. **Disease hypotheses and transaction states have different probability models.** Disease priors can be estimated epidemiologically. The frequency of “request committed but response lost” depends on tool protocol, vendor, platform, and incident type. Global priors would be brittle; empirical per-tool incident counts are needed.

4. **A task is not a stable patient identity.** Branches, worktrees, cloud resources, and external conversations can be shared across tasks and changed by other actors. Recovery must bind evidence to resource identity and version, not just a task name.

5. **Clinical handoff evidence is not crash-recovery evidence.** I-PASS was a bundled, non-randomized pre/post intervention in pediatric units; it cannot isolate the written summary component and cannot establish the harness effect size. Any recommendation that cites “23% fewer errors” as the expected harness outcome would be an invalid extrapolation.

6. **Alerts can become the harm.** Clinical alert fatigue demonstrates that a high-volume registry of vague “be careful” warnings can desensitize operators. Only high-specificity, action-time checks should interrupt; everything else belongs in the recovery context or a non-interruptive note.

## 3. Concrete design proposals

## Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: Best Possible Task History reconciliation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Create one compact, human-readable **Recovery Capsule** per active task. It is not a second event log and not a full transcript. It contains only the fields needed to reconstruct and safely continue:

```yaml
recovery_capsule_version: 1
task_identity: <stable task/run id>
objective: <current user outcome>
owner_constraints:
  - <do-not-do / approval / scope constraints>
last_verified_state:
  observed_at: <timestamp>
  resource_versions:
    - resource: <path, branch, PR, message thread, deployment, etc.>
      version: <hash/revision/status id>
completed_effects:
  - action_signature: <normalized action + target>
    evidence: <receipt, commit, message id, status query, file hash>
pending_or_ambiguous:
  - intended_action: <what was being attempted>
    last_observation: <request issued / response seen / verification absent>
    operation_id: <if available>
    potential_external_effect: <what may already have happened>
plan_changes:
  - change: <old -> new>
    reason: <why>
next_safe_probe: <read-only command or API query>
```

The capsule should be updated atomically at meaningful boundaries: objective/constraint change; before a non-idempotent or high-impact tool call; after a verified effect; and when the plan changes. Updating after every token or every read-only call would add noise and may increase failure surface.

On restart, a recovery skill builds a **Best Possible Task History (BPTH)** from at least two mechanically independent sources when available:

- capsule and tool receipt/log;
- capsule and authoritative current-state query;
- tool receipt and resource version/hash;
- checkpoint and user-visible external state.

It then classifies discrepancies using the WHO-inspired categories:

- **documented intentional:** the plan changed and the reason is present;
- **undocumented intentional:** state changed in a way consistent with an actor’s deliberate action, but the reason is missing;
- **unintentional omission:** intended required work is absent;
- **unintentional commission:** duplicated, conflicting, or unintended effect exists;
- **unresolved:** evidence cannot distinguish these states.

The recovery skill must not claim successful completion or absence from one signal. Its detector should first demonstrate that it can find a known-present control of the same type, then require a second independent signal before declaring “absent,” “broken,” or “done.” This directly instantiates the unit’s honesty instrumentation rather than relegating it to prose.

**What it catches that a checkpoint alone misses:**

- a side effect completed although the tool response was lost;
- the plan changed but the checkpoint still describes the old plan;
- a different actor changed the target resource during downtime;
- a pending item is actually complete according to a receipt or current-state query;
- an intended action was omitted despite optimistic checkpoint prose;
- the checkpoint refers to the correct path but an old version/hash.

The claim that these cases occur often enough to justify always-on capsules is **UNVERIFIED; settle by injecting crashes at pre-call, post-request/pre-response, post-response/pre-capsule, and between-plan-update boundaries across a representative task suite, then measure the observed ambiguity and duplicate-action base rates**. The proposal is nevertheless **likely (55-80%)** to have positive net value for medium/high-impact tasks because it stores bounded facts at precisely the transition risk points; evidence is WHO’s reconciliation design and the direct transactional-state mapping. For low-impact, read-only tasks, net value is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because reconstruction from the existing transcript may be cheaper than maintaining a capsule; the same crash-injection experiment should stratify by task risk.

### Minimality and anti-overfit gate

The capsule is a lossy safety summary, not durable execution. It does not schedule work, own retries, guarantee exactly-once semantics, or replace native sessions. A small hook writes it; a skill reads and reconciles it. It rides existing buses and introduces none of the rejected components. If implementation exceeds roughly 200 lines because every tool needs custom state machinery, stop and split the first pilot to only high-impact action classes rather than growing a recovery runtime.

## Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: Recovery differential with discriminating probes

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For each ambiguous action, instantiate a fixed, content-specific differential rather than a generic “think harder” checklist:

| Hypothesis | Meaning | Preferred discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H0: not issued | Worker died before sending the request | tool request log or operation ID absent, with a known-present control proving detector coverage |
| H1: rejected/failed before effect | Request reached the tool but no target mutation occurred | explicit error plus authoritative unchanged target version/status |
| H2: effect committed, response lost | External effect happened but worker never recorded success | receipt lookup, transaction/message/deployment ID, target-state query |
| H3: partial effect | Some sub-effects committed | per-resource hashes/status, not overall process exit alone |
| H4: effect committed then superseded | Another actor changed state afterward | version history, audit log, timestamps/actor IDs |
| H5: recorded success is stale or wrong-target | Log says success but current intended target differs | resource identity plus version and scope comparison |

The next step is chosen by **expected discrimination per unit risk**, not by which hypothesis sounds most plausible. Priority order:

1. authoritative, read-only, identity-bound status query;
2. receipt/audit-log lookup using an existing operation ID;
3. independent comparison of resource version/hash;
4. narrow reversible probe;
5. ask the owner when ambiguity concerns a high-impact irreversible action;
6. replay only after contraindication screening.

Use a diagnostic timeout only when a trigger fires: ambiguous non-idempotent call, state-version mismatch, missing receipt, capsule/current-state discrepancy, or failed verification. Do not run a differential after an ordinary read-only failure whose absence of effect is guaranteed by the tool contract.

**What it catches:** premature closure on “the failed call did nothing,” premature closure on “the success text means the intended target is current,” and blind replay after a lost response.

The fixed differential is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform open-ended recovery reasoning on ambiguous non-idempotent actions, because it maps the finite transaction-state space and AHRQ reports that content-specific checklists appear more promising than generic process checklists. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle with a blinded evaluation in which recovery agents diagnose injected H0–H5 incidents using either baseline transcript context, a generic reflection prompt, or the fixed differential, measuring correct state classification and risky replays**.

### Why this is not a diagnostic-agent architecture

No extra agent is needed. The differential is a short schema or skill section invoked only by a trigger. Creating a standing critic/diagnostician would add coordination cost and reintroduce an orchestration layer; the clinical literature cited here does not justify that architecture.

## Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: Severity-tiered replay contraindication record

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Maintain two small sources of contraindication knowledge:

1. **General action profile**, defined with the tool/action: idempotent or non-idempotent; read-only or mutating; reversible or irreversible; receipt/operation-ID support; authoritative verification query.
2. **Task/resource-specific reaction record**, written to the recovery capsule when evidence shows that replay is unsafe or requires special conditions.

A task-specific record should be precise:

```yaml
action_signature: "send:<channel-or-thread>:<content-hash>"
target_identity: <stable resource id>
contraindication: "Do not replay while prior send status is unresolved"
consequence: "duplicate external message"
severity: hard_stop
evidence: <request id / log position / current-state uncertainty>
clears_when: <receipt lookup proves absent, or owner explicitly authorizes duplicate>
override: <who may override and what evidence/reason is recorded>
expires: <optional>
```

Tier behavior:

- **Hard stop:** exact same target/action, severe or irreversible consequence, and unresolved prior effect. Require authoritative evidence or explicit owner authorization.
- **Interruptive warning:** meaningful but reversible consequence or probable signature match. Require a recorded reason to continue.
- **Non-interruptive context:** broad class similarity, low severity, or speculative cross-match.
- **No alert:** read-only action or action with a verified idempotency key and safe server-side semantics.

Candidate hard-stop classes include sending an external message, issuing a payment, publishing/deploying, deleting, merging, or mutating an external record when a prior identical attempt has unknown status. These are examples, not claims about installed tools. Whether each class is actually non-idempotent is tool-contract-specific and must be verified before classification.

**What it catches:** recovery-induced duplicate effects, cross-task collision on the same resource, replay of an action that a prior incident proved unsafe, and “retry because error” loops where the error reports only response failure rather than absence of side effect.

The tiered record is **very likely (80-90%)** to be safer than a flat warning list, based on FDA’s scenario-specific contraindication requirements, CMS’s patient-list-plus-general-knowledge model, and the observed 83–88% override rates in noisy clinical allergy alerting. Its net effect in this harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle with shadow-mode logging of would-fire alerts, blinded adjudication of alert correctness/severity, and then a limited trial measuring duplicate effects, appropriate overrides, false blocks, and alert override rate**.

### Explicit contraindication to the proposal itself

Do not launch interruptive mode until shadow data show high precision in the high-severity tier. If more than a small prespecified fraction of hard stops are judged safely overridable, the system is recreating clinical alert fatigue. The acceptable fraction must be set before examining results; selecting it afterward would overfit the pilot.

## Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: Number Needed to Reconcile as an architecture admission gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Do not make “Number Needed to Reconcile” (NNR) a runtime feature. Make it an evaluation and admission rule for recovery features. For a predefined task stratum and recovery window:

```text
baseline correct-recovery rate = CBR
intervention correct-recovery rate = IBR
absolute recovery benefit = IBR - CBR
NNR-benefit = ceil(1 / absolute recovery benefit)

baseline recovery-induced-harm rate = BHR
intervention recovery-induced-harm rate = IHR
absolute harm increase = IHR - BHR
NNR-harm = ceil(1 / absolute harm increase), when IHR > BHR
```

Report natural frequencies alongside the reciprocal: “per 1,000 interrupted deployment tasks, reconciliation yielded X additional correct recoveries and Y additional false blocks within 30 minutes.” Never report NNR without:

- comparator;
- task/risk stratum;
- failure injection or incident type;
- outcome definition;
- time horizon;
- confidence interval;
- benefit and harm endpoints;
- latency and operator-attention cost.

Suggested strata are read-only/local reversible, local mutating, external reversible, and external irreversible. Tool family and failure boundary should be secondary stratifiers. Pooling all tasks into one number is contraindicated because baseline risk differs drastically.

**Admission rule:** retain a recovery mechanism only if its absolute benefit is nontrivial in at least one prespecified risk stratum, its harms/latency are acceptable, and the benefit persists on a later holdout window. Prefer targeted enablement for the benefiting stratum over global enablement.

This metric is **likely (55-80%)** to improve architecture decisions by exposing tiny absolute gains and subgroup dependence; evidence is Cochrane’s comparator-, baseline-risk-, and time-specific interpretation of NNT. It is **UNVERIFIED; settle by applying the rule prospectively to at least two candidate recovery interventions and checking whether holdout results preserve the ranked decision**. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that one stable all-harness NNR will exist, because task mix, model competence, tool contracts, and base failure rates drift; evidence is the direct disanalogy with more stable trial populations and Cochrane’s warning that absolute effects change with baseline risk.

## 4. Recommended combined recovery flow

The proposals compose into one bounded flow:

```text
worker/session/machine loss detected
        |
        v
load Recovery Capsule + identify exact target resources
        |
        v
build Best Possible Task History from >=2 independent sources where available
        |
        v
compare intended/recorded/current state; classify discrepancies
        |
        +-- no ambiguity, read-only or verified idempotent next action --> continue
        |
        v
instantiate H0-H5 differential only for ambiguous action(s)
        |
        v
run lowest-risk authoritative discriminating probe
        |
        v
screen proposed continuation against general + task-specific contraindications
        |
        +-- hard stop unresolved --> request evidence or owner authorization
        +-- safe/cleared ----------> continue and record verification receipt
        |
        v
score outcome later as benefit, recovery-induced harm, false block, and latency
```

This flow is **likely (55-80%)** to resume useful work more safely than either blind replay or transcript-only continuation on high-impact ambiguous failures, because each stage addresses a distinct transition hazard documented in the clinical mechanisms: stale history, premature closure, treatment-caused harm, and patient-specific contraindication. Its superiority is **UNVERIFIED; the §5 experiment is required before default-on deployment**.

## 5. Verification program that would settle the borrowed premises

### 5.1 Phase 0: retrospective incident labeling

Select a representative, time-bounded sample of interrupted tasks. Independently label:

- last action type and target;
- interruption boundary (before request, after request, after effect, after response, after verification);
- whether current state is directly queryable;
- whether replay is idempotent by contract, merely believed idempotent, or non-idempotent;
- actual H0–H5 state using receipts/audit logs/current state;
- recovery-induced harms and false completions.

Two adjudicators or two mechanically independent evidence sources should resolve high-impact labels. Before accepting an “absent” label, validate the detector on a known-present control. This phase estimates the baseline population and tells whether the recovery problem is large enough for intervention.

### 5.2 Phase 1: crash-injection replay benchmark

Use real task shapes with sandboxed targets. Inject termination at four minimum boundaries:

1. before tool request;
2. after request transmission but before response;
3. after response but before capsule update;
4. after verified effect but before plan update.

Cross those boundaries with read-only, reversible mutation, non-idempotent external mutation, and multi-resource partial-effect actions. Preserve hidden ground truth for scoring.

Randomize recovery condition:

- baseline available context;
- recovery capsule only;
- fixed differential only;
- reconciliation + differential + contraindication screen.

Primary benefit outcome: correct useful continuation without owner repair within a fixed time. Primary harm outcome: duplicate/contradictory/unsafe recovery mutation. Secondary outcomes: time-to-first-useful-action, number and cost of probes, false hard stops, incorrect “done” declarations, owner interventions, and context tokens.

### 5.3 Phase 2: shadow contraindication study

Run proposed checks without interrupting. For each would-fire alert, record exact signature, severity, supporting evidence, and what the worker actually did. Blind reviewers to proposed severity when judging whether replay was unsafe. Calculate precision by tier and repeat-alert rate. Promote only precise severe cases to interruptive mode; downgrade speculative cross-matches to context.

### 5.4 Phase 3: prospective controlled rollout

Randomize or use a stepped-wedge rollout across comparable interrupted tasks. Pre-register strata, outcome window, minimum important absolute benefit, maximum false-block rate, and analysis. Report absolute events per 1,000 plus NNR-benefit and NNR-harm with confidence intervals. Re-evaluate on a later holdout window because model/tool changes can invalidate earlier base rates.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this program can determine whether the mechanism reduces duplicate actions in the tested task strata, because the injected boundary supplies ground truth and randomization supplies a comparator. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to establish universal performance across all future tools and models, because idempotency contracts and recovery behavior change; evidence is the heterogeneous target population and Cochrane’s baseline-risk dependence. Therefore the result should govern scoped enablement, not be marketed as a permanent global constant.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Rejected: checkpoint after every tool call

This treats more documentation as equivalent to accurate state. It does not solve the crucial post-request/pre-response window, can persist an incorrect success interpretation, and increases write load. A bounded capsule at risk boundaries plus authoritative reconciliation is preferable. It is **likely (55-80%)** that per-call checkpointing would create more overhead than benefit on read-heavy tasks; this is **UNVERIFIED; settle by comparing capsule write volume, recovery accuracy, and latency under per-call versus risk-boundary checkpointing**.

### Rejected: automatically retry every failed action

A transport or response failure does not prove absence of external effect. Automatic replay is contraindicated for unresolved non-idempotent actions. This is a logical consequence of ambiguous commit status, not a probabilistic claim.

### Rejected: ask another agent for a second opinion on every restart

Clinical consultation helps when expertise differs, but a second language-model sample does not provide mechanically independent evidence about external state. It can reproduce the same mistaken inference. Use another agent only when it brings a distinct capability or evidence source; otherwise use a receipt, audit log, hash, or status API.

### Rejected: universal hard-stop warnings

High override rates and repeated-alert fatigue in clinical systems are direct evidence against undifferentiated interruptive warnings. Severity and match specificity must determine interruption.

### Rejected: full event sourcing / durable-execution service

Such a service could solve a broader class of execution guarantees, but it is a previously rejected component under this program’s anti-overfit gate and is not required for this borrow. The proposal deliberately diagnoses and reconciles state; it does not guarantee exactly-once execution. No clinical evidence cited here justifies the operational cost of a new runtime.

### Rejected: train a recovery critic

The data need is currently causal ground truth about transaction state, not a learned preference score. A trained critic would add drift, evaluation, and maintenance burden while remaining unable to see an external effect without the same receipts/probes. Fixed content-specific logic is the appropriate first test.

### Rejected: one global NNR score

NNT is population-, comparator-, outcome-, and time-specific. A single global reciprocal would conceal the high benefit expected in ambiguous external mutations and the likely low benefit in read-only tasks.

## 7. Implementation boundaries and failure modes

1. **Capsule becomes stale.** Treat it as one source, never ground truth. Bind each state statement to a timestamp and resource version.
2. **Capsule write tears during machine death.** Use atomic replace and retain the last parseable version. This prevents corruption but cannot capture an effect that occurred after the last durable write; reconciliation remains necessary.
3. **“Read-only” probe is not actually read-only.** Tool profiles must derive from documented contracts and empirical verification, not action names. A status endpoint that refreshes or triggers work must be classified accordingly.
4. **Operation signatures collide.** Include stable target identity, normalized parameters or content hash, actor/task identity, and time window. Broad fuzzy matching should never produce a hard stop.
5. **External state changes after verification.** Carry the observed version into the next mutation as a precondition where the tool supports conditional writes. Otherwise re-check immediately before high-impact mutation.
6. **Override becomes routine.** Record structured override reasons, monitor tier-specific override rates, and retire or downgrade low-value alerts. Repeated overrides are a signal to improve the rule, not proof that users are careless.
7. **Recovery capsule leaks sensitive content.** Store identifiers, hashes, and minimal evidence rather than message bodies or secrets. Inherit the task’s access controls and retention, and never print credentials into the capsule.
8. **The model fabricates a missing source.** The schema should distinguish observed evidence, inference, and unknown. Recovery decisions must link to receipts/queries rather than unsupported prose.
9. **A multi-step effect is represented as binary.** Record per-resource or per-subaction receipts; H3 partial effect is a first-class state.
10. **Measurement rewards speed over safety.** Correct continuation and recovery-induced harm must be co-primary or separately gated; time-to-resume alone encourages blind replay.

## 9. Gaps

- No local harness files were read, by explicit instruction. Therefore this report does not claim that a recovery capsule, hook trigger, idempotency profile, operation receipt, or existing native session mechanism is absent or present.
- The medical sources establish transition reconciliation, diagnostic reasoning, contraindication alerting, and treatment-effect evaluation in their own field. They do not validate an agent implementation.
- I-PASS’s effect estimate comes from a bundled pre/post study in pediatric inpatient care. It supports plausibility, not causal attribution to a written handoff field or transportability of the numerical effect.
- No source found establishes “contraindication registry” as a single canonical clinical artifact. The verified operational mechanism is the combination of general drug/interaction knowledge, patient-specific medication/allergy state, precise contraindication labeling, and point-of-order checking.
- Tool-specific idempotency, receipt availability, and authoritative verification queries require a separate local/tool-contract unit. This ideation unit intentionally does not infer them.
- The threshold for “acceptable” hard-stop false positives is a governance choice and should be pre-registered for the pilot; no universal clinical percentage transfers cleanly.

## 10. Bottom line

Clinical medicine’s best contribution to failure recovery is not a metaphor and not a larger agent organization. It is a disciplined restart rule:

> **Reconstruct what was actually true, reconcile it with what is true now, keep competing explanations only where direct inspection cannot decide, and do not re-administer an ambiguous action until task-specific contraindications are cleared. Measure both benefit and recovery-caused harm in absolute terms.**

The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally sound, based on the shared transition/uncertainty/intervention-harm structure and authoritative clinical mechanisms. The operational payoff is **UNVERIFIED; the decisive experiment is the stratified randomized crash-injection benchmark followed by shadow alert adjudication and a prospective holdout rollout**. Until those results exist, use the design as a small, scoped pilot for ambiguous high-impact actions, not as justification for an always-on recovery platform.
