# X158 — Cross-domain borrow: archival science → harness observability without a baseline

## Unit scope and result

This unit used an ideation lens only. It did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, repository, corpus, audit findings, or subject trees. That is deliberate: the unit brief assigns local grounding to other units. The only local facts assumed are the problem statement and architectural constraints supplied in the unit prompt.

**Result:** the archival-science mechanism transfers, but only in a bounded, prospective form. The useful mechanism is a **function-first documentation strategy, enforced by an appraisal/retention schedule and trustworthy-record controls**. In archives, this mechanism decides what evidence of a function must be created or selected, how long each record class survives, and what context and integrity evidence must travel with it. In a harness, it becomes a small, versioned “change evidence schedule” plus an append-only evidence packet captured at every change boundary.

This mechanism does **not** recover a baseline that was never recorded and does **not** establish that a change caused an improvement. That boundary is central, not a caveat. Archival science can make future comparisons possible and defensible; experimental design must supply a counterfactual. The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce unsupported “this change helped” conclusions when promotion is conditioned on a complete evidence packet; evidence: documentation strategy explicitly starts by defining what must be documented and examining available documentation, NARA schedules explicitly bind record content/function to disposition, and NARA trustworthy-record guidance requires content, context, structure, and controlled alteration. Whether it reduces such conclusions in this particular harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the 30-change shadow-mode experiment specified below**.

## The specific archival mechanism in its own field

### 1. Documentation strategy: begin with the function, not with whatever survived

A documentation strategy is proactive appraisal. Instead of treating the accumulated pile as a neutral representation of reality, the archivist first defines the process/event/topic that society needs to document. Participants and repositories are identified; existing documentation is examined for form, substance, duplication, and gaps; then records are selected and placed so that an adequate record survives. The SAA definition is explicit that this can span creators and institutions, which matters when evidence of one activity is dispersed across many systems. [SAA documentation strategy](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/documentation-strategy.html)

Institutional functional analysis strengthens the first step. The unit of thought is an activity or function, not a fashionable document type or the output of the easiest system to query. This guards against a classic selection failure: preserving abundant low-value artifacts while losing sparse but decisive evidence. [SAA institutional functional analysis](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/institutional-functional-analysis.html)

Mechanically, the sequence is:

1. State the function or event to be evidenced.
2. Identify actors, systems, and record-producing transactions around it.
3. Inventory available record series and their gaps.
4. Appraise series by evidentiary value, not merely volume or convenience.
5. Assign custody/placement so selected evidence is actually retained and discoverable.
6. Reappraise when functions, record systems, or user needs change.

The crucial archival insight is that **the archive is a designed sample, not reality itself**. Selection decisions shape every conclusion later users can draw. NARA’s appraisal policy makes this explicit in practice: appraisal is informed judgment, not a rote rule, and its objectives focus on evidence of policy, process, action, decision, and effects. [NARA appraisal policy](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)

### 2. Records schedule: make selection and deletion explicit before convenience decides them

A records schedule turns appraisal into enforceable lifecycle instructions. It identifies a record class by content, format, and context/function; states whether it is temporary or permanent; and says when it may be destroyed or transferred. [NARA scheduling and appraisal](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/sch-appraisal)

This solves two coupled problems in archives:

- **Unprincipled loss:** evidence is deleted because storage pressure, system migration, or local habit acts before appraisal.
- **Indiscriminate accumulation:** everything is kept forever, making significant evidence expensive to preserve and difficult to retrieve.

Implementation is more than a time-to-live value. NARA calls for filing instructions, treatment of non-record material, restrictions, explicit authority, replacement of obsolete schedule items, and updates to file plans. [NARA implementing schedules](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/implementation)

The schedule is therefore a policy over record *series*, trigger events, retention periods, disposition actions, and exceptions. It is intelligible before deletion and reviewable afterward. Reappraisal is part of the discipline: NARA’s appraisal policy permits revisiting an earlier judgment when compelling evidence changes the assessment.

### 3. Provenance, authenticity, integrity, and fixity: preserve the ability to interpret and challenge

Kept bytes are not necessarily evidence. NARA separates four qualities:

- **Reliability:** the record is a full and accurate representation of the transaction or facts.
- **Authenticity:** it is what it purports to be and has the purported creator/sender.
- **Integrity:** it is complete and unaltered, or authorized changes are explicitly marked.
- **Usability:** it can be found, retrieved, presented, interpreted, and connected to its producing business activity.

NARA further states that content, context, and sometimes structure must be preserved, and that links among records in a sequence should remain. [NARA electronic-signature records guidance](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/policy/electronic-signature-technology.html)

Fixity is narrower. A hash comparison can show that bits changed; it cannot show that the captured bits truthfully represented the event. The Library of Congress describes fixity information as ranging from file counts and sizes to checksums and cryptographic hashes. [Library of Congress fixity guidance](https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2014/10/we-want-you-just-the-way-you-are-the-what-why-and-when-of-fixity/)

Chain of custody records who controlled the material from creation onward. [SAA chain of custody](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/chain-of-custody.html) NARA’s operational preservation assumptions combine these ideas: record fixity, track actions, separate access copies, and audit regularly. [NARA digital preservation program](https://www.archives.gov/preservation/digital-preservation/about)

These controls do not turn false source data into truth. They make later alteration, decontextualization, and undocumented transformation harder to confuse with an original record.

## Structural mapping to harness observability

| Archival construct | Harness analogue | Concrete field(s) or action | Failure exposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Documented function | Owner outcome the change is meant to improve | `target_function`, `decision`, `failure_mode`, `population` | Proxy metrics that move while the owner outcome does not |
| Record creator | Producer of the observation | tool/hook/test ID, process identity, model/tool version | Numbers whose generating system is unknown |
| Record series | Repeated class of comparable observations | task attempts, tool calls, correction events, latency/cost samples | Mixing unlike units or comparing changed schemas |
| Documentation inventory | Map of available and missing evidence | source list plus explicit `missing_sources` | Treating an unobserved signal as zero |
| Appraisal | Decision about evidentiary value | required/optional/transient class with rationale | Keeping easy telemetry while dropping decisive outcomes |
| Retention schedule | Lifecycle policy | trigger, duration, aggregation, deletion, legal/privacy exception | Losing the only comparison window or keeping raw sensitive content forever |
| Provenance/context | Conditions of production | harness/config/model/tool hashes, environment, task cohort, sampling rule | Attributing model or workload drift to a harness change |
| Original order/archival bond | Relationship among events | trace/run IDs, monotonic sequence, parent event, causal link | Reconstructing a misleading sequence from unordered logs |
| Fixity | Bit-level change detection | manifest hash per evidence packet and raw member | Silent post hoc editing or corruption |
| Chain of custody | Transformation history | append-only capture/aggregate/redact/delete events with actor and code version | “Cleaned” metrics whose exclusions cannot be audited |
| Reappraisal | Scheduled schema/policy review | review on new function, metric version, incident, or quarterly trigger | A once-useful metric becoming a ritualized target |
| Finding aid | Human/machine discovery layer | small manifest/index pointing to packets, definitions, and reports | Evidence exists but cannot be located or joined |

The mapping is strongest where both domains face **selective survival under constrained retention** and **later interpretation by a party not present at creation**. It is weaker where observability seeks causal attribution: archives preserve traces of one realized history, while causal evaluation needs a defensible comparison to an unrealized or concurrently controlled alternative.

## Proposed harness translation

### Proposal A — Change evidence schedule [UNVERIFIED harness benefit; settle in the 30-change shadow trial] — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Create one small, versioned declarative schedule for evidence classes. It is a function-based contract, not a dashboard configuration. A minimal schema should include:

```yaml
schedule_version: 1
record_series:
  - id: task_attempt
    function: end_to_end_owner_outcome
    unit: one accepted task attempt
    population: explicit inclusion rule
    producer: harness boundary hook
    required_fields: [attempt_id, cohort_id, outcome, correction_count, started_at, ended_at]
    exclusions: explicit rules plus excluded_count
    retention:
      raw: 30d
      redacted: 180d
      aggregates: 2y
    disposition: delete_raw_after_verified_aggregate
    rationale: supports pre/post or concurrent-cohort comparison
    owner: named role
    review_trigger: [metric_schema_change, producer_change, 90d]
```

Required cross-series metadata should include:

- immutable `change_id` and parent change;
- exact harness/config/rule/skill versions or content hashes;
- model, tool, operating environment, and evaluation-code versions;
- target function and hypothesized mechanism;
- metric name **and semantic version**;
- numerator, denominator, unit of analysis, exclusions, and missingness count;
- sampling frame/cohort assignment and workload descriptors;
- source producer and capture time;
- whether the evidence is pre-change, post-change, concurrent control, replay, or synthetic;
- retention trigger, disposition action, disposition reason, and schedule authority/version;
- privacy/secrets class and allowed transformations.

This is deliberately a “big bucket” schedule with a few function-level series, not one rule per tool event. The schedule is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent metric-definition drift from masquerading as improvement because comparisons can reject incompatible semantic versions; evidence: archival schedules bind content and context to lifecycle rules, and the proposed contract makes metric semantics part of that context. Effectiveness in this harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by mutation-testing 20 evidence packets with changed denominators, exclusions, and producer versions and measuring gate rejection**.

The schedule is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce survival bias from asymmetric retention because raw pre- and post-change members share a record-series rule; evidence: records scheduling explicitly applies disposition by identified series rather than ad hoc file deletion. This harness-specific benefit is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by simulating 90 days of rollovers under storage pressure and checking whether paired cohorts remain reconstructable**.

### Proposal B — Boundary evidence packet and append-only custody log [UNVERIFIED premise: the existing hook bus exposes a stable change/promotion boundary; settle with a no-op hook that records exactly one event for each of 20 staged changes] — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

At the existing harness change boundary, write one compact packet rather than introducing a telemetry service. The packet should contain:

1. `manifest.json`: identifiers, versions, target function, metric definitions, cohort/sampling description, missing sources, retention class, and member hashes.
2. `observations.jsonl` or references to already-retained records: sufficient statistics plus raw-member locators where policy permits.
3. `custody.jsonl`: append-only events for creation, validation, aggregation, redaction, migration, access-copy generation, and disposition. Each event records actor/process, timestamp, action, input hash, output hash, and code/config version.
4. `assessment.json`: claim level, uncertainty, known confounders, incompatible comparisons, and promotion decision.

The hook validates required fields, hashes packet members, then appends a custody event. It must never “repair” a missing baseline by relabeling post-change observations. A missing required source is recorded as `missing`; zero is valid only when the producer ran successfully and observed zero events. This distinction directly imports documentation inventory: absence of documentation and documentation of absence are different records.

The packet is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal silent after-the-fact edits to retained evidence when hashes and custody events are stored outside the edited member; evidence: Library of Congress fixity guidance explains that hashes detect alteration and NARA requires actions on preserved files to be tracked. End-to-end detection under the actual storage permissions is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by tampering with one member, one manifest, and one access copy in a 30-packet test and verifying detection from an independently retained custody head**.

It is only **likely (55-80%)** to reveal misleading metric transformations, because a complete transformation log enables review but reviewers or gates may still accept a bad method; evidence: NARA’s authenticity guidance requires authorized annotations and contextual links, but does not guarantee analytical validity. Harness impact is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with five seeded transformations—dropped failures, denominator change, retry collapse, cohort filter, and clock-window shift—and blind reviewer detection**.

### Proposal C — Claim ladder and promotion gate [UNVERIFIED premise: a current promotion path can call a local validator without becoming a second orchestrator; settle by tracing one staged change through the existing path and proving the validator can fail closed in dry-run] — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

The validator assigns the strongest claim the evidence can support:

| Level | Evidence available | Permitted wording |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | No comparable prior or concurrent evidence | “Instrumented from version X; effect unknown.” |
| E1 | Post-change observations only | “Observed rate/value after change”; no “improved” claim |
| E2 | Historical pre/post evidence with stable definitions and documented cohort drift | “Associated with change”; confounding stated |
| E3 | Comparable concurrent control, randomized crossover, or other predeclared causal design | “Estimated effect” with interval and assumptions |
| E4 | Replication across time/task strata plus rollback or independent confirmation | “Replicated estimated effect” with scope |

The archival mechanism supplies the evidence inventory, context, integrity, and retention behind the ladder. It does not justify moving from E2 to E3; that requires evaluation design. NIST describes deliberate factor changes and planned experiments as the route to valid objective conclusions, while the What Works Clearinghouse separately requires assessing baseline similarity of intervention and comparison groups. [NIST experimental design](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section1/pri11.htm) [WWC baseline equivalence](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED579502)

For a change with no baseline, the gate should permit deployment if other safety criteria pass, but it should prohibit the specific claim “helped” and immediately start a prospective record series. If feasible, the evaluation owner separately chooses a randomized/crossover/holdout design; that design is not smuggled in as an archival feature.

The claim ladder is **very likely (80-90%)** to make “no baseline” visible instead of silently converting it to a zero or an anecdote, because `missing`, E0, and E1 are explicit schema states and the validator can reject stronger wording. Evidence: documentation strategy begins by examining available documentation and gaps, while the ladder mechanically binds claims to evidence classes. Behavioral compliance by agents is **UNVERIFIED; settle it in shadow mode on 30 real change assessments, seed 10 with unsupported improvement language, and measure validator recall, precision, override frequency, and owner acceptance**.

The gate’s net benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, not “almost certain,” because false blocks may teach users to bypass it and because some valuable low-risk changes cannot afford a controlled evaluation. Evidence for the risk is structural: archival appraisal itself is informed judgment rather than rote selection, per NARA; a rigid schema can reproduce the over-strictness the owner’s brief warns against. The correct threshold is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by comparing advisory-only and blocking modes across matched sets of 15 changes, measuring unsupported claims caught, legitimate promotions delayed, and overrides**.

### Proposal D — Scheduled reappraisal and verified disposition [UNVERIFIED premise: evidence storage has a stable scheduler or periodic hook; settle with a dry-run enumeration that emits due actions without deleting anything] — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Run a periodic local job that:

1. identifies packets due for review or disposition under the versioned schedule;
2. verifies that required aggregates, manifests, and member hashes exist;
3. emits a disposition plan, never an unresolved wildcard;
4. records the applicable schedule version and exception/hold;
5. requires the normal safe deletion authority outside this proposal;
6. appends a tombstone containing packet ID, disposed member classes, time, authority, and pre-disposition hashes;
7. triggers reappraisal when a metric producer, schema, target function, or privacy classification changes.

This proposal does not authorize deletion. It designs the evidentiary preconditions for later, separately authorized disposal.

Scheduled reappraisal is **likely (55-80%)** to catch stale metrics whose generating function or schema changed; evidence: NARA explicitly provides for reappraisal and instructs implementers to replace obsolete/superseded authorities and update file plans. Harness effectiveness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting producer/schema/function changes into a six-month schedule simulation and measuring whether each produces a review task before the next comparison**.

Verified disposition is **likely (55-80%)** to retain enough evidence for later comparison while bounding raw-data growth, because it conditions raw deletion on verified derivative records rather than indefinite retention. The storage/utility tradeoff is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a replay study that answers 20 past-style evaluation questions from full raw data and from scheduled derivatives, measuring answer equivalence, privacy exposure, and bytes retained**.

## What this catches that ordinary telemetry commonly misses

These are prospective hypotheses, not claims about the current harness:

1. **Missing denominator disguised as improvement.** Error counts fall because total calls fell or a producer stopped. Required numerator, denominator, producer heartbeat, and missingness distinguish improvement from non-observation. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** for schema-visible omissions; evidence is the proposed mechanical validator. It is **UNVERIFIED; settle with denominator-removal and producer-silence mutations**.
2. **Metric drift across versions.** “Success” changes definition, exclusions, or retry collapsing. Semantic versions and transformation custody make the comparison incompatible rather than silently comparable. Detection is **likely (55-80%)** because only declared drift is detectable; **UNVERIFIED; settle with declared and undeclared definition mutations**.
3. **Workload/model/tool drift attributed to the harness.** Provenance binds outcomes to cohort and environment. Exposure is **likely (55-80%)** when those covariates are captured; **UNVERIFIED; settle by swapping model/tool/workload labels in a blinded comparison test**.
4. **Survivorship bias caused by retention.** Failures or pre-change records expire sooner than successes or post-change records. Series-level retention and disposition tombstones expose asymmetric survival. Detection is **likely (55-80%)**; **UNVERIFIED; settle with simulated selective expiry**.
5. **Post hoc cleaning.** A report silently drops timeouts, corrections, or retries. Input/output hashes and transformation events expose that the analytical object changed, though not necessarily why. Exposure is **likely (55-80%)**; **UNVERIFIED; settle with the five seeded transformations in Proposal B**.
6. **Dashboard without reconstructability.** Aggregate is visible but source, cohort, formula, and evaluation code are gone. The manifest keeps the archival bond between result and generating activity. Reconstruction is **likely (55-80%)** for explicitly scheduled questions; **UNVERIFIED; settle with the 20-question replay study**.
7. **Baseline laundering.** First post-change measurements are mislabeled “baseline,” or absence becomes numeric zero. Evidence state and claim level prohibit that representation. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** for packet-mediated reports; **UNVERIFIED; settle by seeding mislabeled periods and absent producers**.
8. **Permanent metrics with obsolete purpose.** A metric continues because it exists, not because it evidences an owner function. Review triggers force a new appraisal rationale. Detection is **likely (55-80%)**; **UNVERIFIED; settle by changing three target functions in simulation and checking review creation**.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Where the analogy holds

1. **Both domains observe traces, not the underlying activity directly.** A tool log is to a harness run what a transaction record is to an institutional function: partial evidence produced by a system with its own incentives and failure modes.
2. **Both have selection pressure.** Storage, privacy, cost, and queryability prevent keeping every representation forever. Appraisal is therefore unavoidable even when unstated.
3. **Context decays faster than bytes.** A metric without its cohort, producer version, definition, and exclusions may remain readable but become uninterpretable. This maps closely to NARA’s content/context/structure requirements.
4. **Later users were not present at creation.** Future agents and owners need provenance and custody to challenge a conclusion rather than trust a dashboard screenshot.
5. **Distributed creation matters.** Harness evidence is dispersed across model calls, tools, hooks, evaluators, owner corrections, and artifacts; documentation strategy was expressly conceived for documentation dispersed across creators/repositories.

Given those shared structures, the bounded transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to be conceptually valid; evidence: each mapped archival control operates on record selection, context, lifecycle, or integrity, all of which have a direct harness record analogue. Operational payoff remains **UNVERIFIED; the combined shadow-mode experiment below settles it**.

### Where it fails

1. **An archive preserves one realized history; effect estimation needs a counterfactual.** Two mechanically independent signals establish this boundary. First, the archival definitions describe selection, retention, disposition, context, and authenticity—not treatment assignment or counterfactual construction. Second, NIST independently defines experimental design as deliberate factor manipulation planned to yield valid objective conclusions, and WWC separately evaluates baseline equivalence of treatment and comparison groups. Therefore it is **almost certain (90-99%)** that archival controls alone cannot establish causal improvement; evidence: the independent disciplinary scopes just cited.
2. **Authentic is not true.** A faithfully preserved biased evaluator output remains biased. NARA’s definitions distinguish authenticity (what it purports to be) from reliability (accurate representation). A hash authenticates stability, not correctness.
3. **Original order transfers imperfectly.** Digital harness events are concurrent, retried, buffered, and distributed; one total order may be fictional. The concrete translation must preserve partial order (`trace_id`, parent link, monotonic producer sequence, timestamps) rather than impose a single file order. That translation is **likely (55-80%)** to retain the meaningful execution relationship; **UNVERIFIED; settle by replaying traces with parallel branches and delayed delivery**.
4. **Archival appraisal tolerates enduring cultural value and expert judgment; promotion gates need fast, repeatable operational decisions.** An elaborate appraisal committee would be too slow. The borrow must be a compact schema plus exception path, not an institutional bureaucracy.
5. **Archives often stabilize records after activity; observability must capture during activity.** Late archival acquisition can document a function from distributed sources, but ephemeral runtime state is gone if capture was not prospective.
6. **Retention can conflict with privacy and secrets handling.** “Keep enough context” is unsafe if it means raw prompts, credentials, or personal data. The schedule must support redacted derivatives and short raw retention; archival value does not override security policy.
7. **Goodhart pressure is stronger in operational metrics.** Once a metric controls promotion, producers may optimize it. Provenance and reappraisal expose definition and lineage but do not eliminate gaming.

The analogy therefore transfers as **evidence governance**, not as causal inference, monitoring infrastructure, or truth machinery.

## Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

The proposals pass the stated anti-overfit gate conditionally: they are one declarative file plus small validators/hooks on the existing bus. They do not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Rejected alternatives:

- **“Keep everything forever.”** Rejected. It avoids explicit appraisal only by making cost, privacy risk, and discoverability the accidental appraisers. Archival schedules exist precisely because selection and disposition must be deliberate.
- **Retroactively infer a baseline from surviving logs.** Rejected as a general answer. Surviving records can support a qualified historical reconstruction, but missing producers, denominator drift, and changed cohorts prevent them from becoming a measured baseline by declaration.
- **Hash every event and call the result trustworthy.** Rejected. Fixity detects alteration; it does not establish faithful capture, valid metrics, or causal effect.
- **A blockchain or external transparency ledger.** Rejected. This would introduce infrastructure disproportionate to the threat model. A local append-only custody log with independently retained heads is enough to test the mechanism first.
- **A new observability daemon or SaaS.** Rejected under the anti-overfit gate. The archival contribution is the evidence contract and lifecycle, not another collection runtime.
- **Vector-DB/KG “institutional memory.”** Rejected as both explicitly disallowed and unnecessary. Packets need stable IDs, manifests, and a simple index; semantic retrieval adds no causal validity.
- **One over-strict schema for every change.** Rejected. Archival appraisal is function-sensitive. Required core fields plus function-specific record series and an explicit `not_applicable` reason avoid forcing irrelevant evidence into every fan-out.
- **An LLM critic judging whether evidence is adequate.** Rejected. The core checks—presence, version compatibility, hashes, denominators, claim level, retention class—are deterministic. Human/agent judgment may explain exceptions, but it should not be a trained gatekeeper component.
- **Fully automatic deletion in the first implementation.** Rejected. Initial disposition must be plan-only until replay demonstrates that scheduled derivatives answer expected questions and deletion authority is separately established.

## Falsifiable combined experiment

### Thirty-change shadow trial

Run Proposals A–C in advisory shadow mode for 30 naturally occurring harness changes; do not block promotion during the trial.

Predeclare:

- 10 changes expected to have no usable pre-change baseline;
- 10 with historical pre/post evidence;
- 10 where a concurrent or crossover comparison is feasible;
- a human-reviewed gold label for permitted claim level, created without seeing validator output;
- mutations distributed across packets: missing denominator, silent producer, changed metric definition, cohort shift, environment change, selective expiry, post hoc exclusion, altered file, and mislabeled post-change “baseline.”

Measure:

1. unsupported improvement claims caught (recall);
2. supportable claims incorrectly downgraded (false-positive rate);
3. seeded provenance/fixity/retention defects detected;
4. time and bytes per packet;
5. reviewer time to reproduce numerator, denominator, cohort, and result;
6. override rate and override reason;
7. proportion of packets that remain answerable after simulated scheduled disposition;
8. whether E0/E1 labeling changes actual report wording.

Decision rule, to be fixed before data collection: proceed to a blocking pilot only if recall on unsupported “helped” claims is at least 90%, false blocking is at most 10%, every tampered member is detected, and median packet-generation overhead is below a locally chosen operational budget. The numerical thresholds are **roughly even (45-55%)** to be the correct tradeoff because no harness-specific cost curve was provided; evidence: they are intentionally test thresholds, not archival doctrine. They are **UNVERIFIED; settle by owner review of shadow-trial errors and an explicit cost-of-false-claim versus cost-of-delay decision**.

Then run Proposal D in plan-only mode over a six-month synthetic timeline. No deletion occurs. Compare full raw evidence with scheduled derivatives on 20 evaluation questions sampled from future decision types. Only authorize any automatic disposition mechanism if answer equivalence and privacy/storage results meet predeclared thresholds.

### Detector validation and the two-signal rule

For every “missing evidence” decision in the trial:

1. **Positive control:** prove the detector finds a known-present record from the same producer and schema.
2. **Signal A:** inspect the expected record-series membership/producer output.
3. **Signal B:** inspect an independent producer heartbeat, denominator ledger, or custody event—not a rerun of Signal A.

Only if the positive control passes and both independent signals agree may the packet say `missing`. Otherwise it says `detector_unknown`.
