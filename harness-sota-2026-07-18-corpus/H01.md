# H01 — Calibrated confidence as honest signalling

## Executive verdict

**The Sherman–Kent bands are UNVERIFIED as calibrated in practice.** The experiment that settles this is a prospective, immutable forecast-to-outcome study: record a band before its resolution is known, settle it only from a later external signal, and compare each band’s stated interval with its observed correctness frequency. The present harness mandates bands and lints their presence, but does not settle them. This conclusion has two mechanically independent local signals:

1. The behavior contract requires a Sherman–Kent/WEP band and evidence for uncertain claims (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:80-83`), while the implementation accepts any percentage, the word `confidence`, evidence words, or a receipt reference as satisfying `_BAND` and suggests `~70%`; it does not check the number against outcomes (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2044-2049,2251-2265`). A controlled search first found the known-present strings `confidence`, `_BAND`, and `Sherman-Kent`, then found no `brier`, `log score`, `reliability diagram`, `calibration error`, or forecast/outcome join in the shared harness, Claude hooks, or behavior contract. Command: `rg -n -i 'confidence|_BAND|Sherman.Kent' ...`, followed by `rg -n -i 'brier|log.?score|reliability.?diagram|calibration.?error|forecast.{0,30}outcome|outcome.{0,30}forecast' ...`; the second search returned no matches.
2. The distilled corpus detector was validated on a known-positive control (`tool_usage` contains 533 tool records and begins with Bash), but its top-level schema contains no calibration/forecast/confidence/outcome table. More importantly, `hooks.DETECTOR_STATUS` says the hook detector is broken and must be treated as no data (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:1`, JSON pointers `/_meta`, `/tool_usage`, `/hooks`). Therefore the corpus cannot establish either calibration or miscalibration; treating zero hook fires as evidence would violate the brief.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the explicit band-presence regex and the controlled absence checks above, that the deployed rule enforces confidence *syntax*, not empirical calibration. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that any one current band is numerically well calibrated, because no prospective local outcomes have been joined to bands; the experiment above is what settles that claim.

The best architecture is not a confidence hook. It is a small **forecast-settlement lane inside the existing shared dispatcher and event ledger**. It adds zero hook processes, zero owner prompts in normal operation, zero orchestration runtimes, and no trained critic. It reuses the existing normalized `last_assistant_message` and tool-result fields (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/models.py:26-39`; `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/adapters.py:44-62,109-145`), the already-wired Stop/PostToolUse/PostToolUseFailure events (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json:310-378,484-504`), and additive append-only ledger fields (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:817-880`).

## Grounding and limits

The corpus covers 11,633 transcript files, 10.62 GB, 1,564,084 parsed lines, zero failed files, one bad line, and 32 oversized lines (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:1`, `/_meta`). Its tool and failure counts are strong aggregate ground truth, but samples are capped at 12 and the correction miner is recall-biased. In particular, the corpus reports 2,268 correction candidates among 8,441 apparent owner messages but warns that 41/41 items in an unfiltered smoke sample were false positives because agent-authored prompts were misclassified (`corpus.json:1`, `/owner_correction_candidates`). Consequently, correction candidates are useful for constructing a review sample, not as automatic truth labels.

The real corpus shows abundant external outcomes with which a prospective evaluator can settle claims: Bash has 108,121 calls and 6,461 errors; StructuredOutput has 3,694 calls and 845 errors; Glob has 6,222 calls and 826 errors; WebFetch has 8,042 calls and 454 errors (`corpus.json:1`, `/tool_usage`). It also contains typed retry episodes—53 Read runs, 14 Bash runs, and smaller loops for TaskOutput, deep-research checking, ToolSearch, PowerShell, and StructuredOutput (`corpus.json:1`, `/retry_loops`). These counts do **not** imply that an agent predicted the outcomes; they show that later execution results exist and can serve as settlement oracles after a forecast has been precommitted.

The higher-confidence operational evidence records seven confident conclusions caused by unvalidated single probes and states the mechanical remedy: prove a detector can find a known-present control before reporting absence (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). It separately records a fabricated orchestrator constraint propagated into roughly 15 prompts and drove use of a 13.6%-failure path instead of a 0.0%-failure path (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`). These are directly relevant: calibration must score precommitted claims against external outcomes, and inherited prompt assertions must remain falsifiable.

### Measurement contract

A **scorable forecast** is a binary, externally resolvable claim stated before the resolving evidence arrives. It must have: `forecast_id`, redacted/canonical `claim`, exact Sherman–Kent `band`, fixed `p_score`, `claim_family`, `provider`, `model_id`, `evidence_state`, `created_seq`, `resolution_predicate`, and `resolve_by`. The fixed scoring points are the interval midpoints: almost certain .945, very likely .85, likely .675, roughly even .50, unlikely .325, very unlikely .125, almost certainly not .03. The owner still sees words, not spurious decimal precision.

A **settlement** has: `forecast_id`, `outcome` in `{1,0,censored,disputed}`, `oracle_class`, `oracle_ref`, `settled_seq`, and `adjudicator`. Only objective execution/readback or explicit owner adjudication enters the primary score. A fresh-context second model may nominate a settlement, but its result stays `provisional` until an objective receipt or owner decision agrees. This enforces the law that same-context self-review is never an oracle.

Primary reporting is per-band empirical accuracy with a Wilson interval, Brier score using the fixed point, resolution coverage, censoring rate, and selective risk/coverage. A band is called locally calibrated only after its empirical interval is contained within the stated Sherman–Kent interval **and** a minimum sample threshold is met. `n < 30` is displayed as `insufficient evidence`, not good or bad calibration. The choice of 30 is **UNVERIFIED**; the experiment that settles it is a power analysis or bootstrap simulation using the observed per-band prevalence and desired detectable gap before any enforcement threshold is enabled.

This is consistent with proper-scoring-rule theory: a proper score rewards honest probabilistic forecasts in expectation ([Gneiting & Raftery, JASA 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). It also follows current LLM evidence rather than assuming prompt wording is trustworthy: verbal confidence elicitation remains an empirical problem ([Xiong et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6733cf15e10e2cd1d59af033c3bb8507-Abstract-Conference.html)), while linguistic calibration is defined in terms of enabling calibrated downstream human predictions, not merely emitting percentages ([Band et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/band24a.html)).

## Findings — inferred-surprise first

### Finding 1 — UNVERIFIED: make every useful hedge a forecast that can mature into evidence

- **Improvement:** Add an immutable forecast→settlement lane to the existing event ledger, in SHADOW mode first.
- **What it is:** On Stop/TaskCompleted, parse only exact Sherman–Kent terms in `last_assistant_message`. Admit a claim only if a deterministic predicate can resolve later: exact command exit, test count, file/readback state, server health, owner adjudication, or an independently executed acceptance check. Append `kind="forecast"` before any later resolving event. On PostToolUse/PostToolUseFailure/TaskCompleted, a deterministic matcher appends `kind="forecast_settlement"` referencing the prior event. Never rewrite the forecast. Never ask the same context whether it was right.
- **Why inferred:** The owner mandates confidence bands because they want honest signalling, but the local rule currently rewards the label rather than the calibration (`modules.py:2044-2049,2251-2265`). The operational failures show that confident single-probe conclusions cause real damage (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). The corpus has many later execution results but no calibration join (`corpus.json:1`, `/tool_usage`, `/retry_loops`). The surprising move is to treat a hedge as a small testable commitment, not as prose style.
- **How to build:** Add two event kinds and three bounded ledger queries (`unsettled_forecasts`, `settle_forecast`, `calibration_rows`) to `ledger.py`; add a `ForecastSettlement` module to the shared dispatcher’s existing Stop and result paths; use exact lexicon regex plus conservative binary templates; cap at three forecasts per answer; hash/redact claim text; use ledger sequence ordering to reject any resolution whose sequence is not later than `created_seq`. Wrap each entry point in `try/except` and return allow/exit 0 on error. Shadow for at least 50 externally settled claims before any communication changes. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to produce a valid local calibration sample because the normalized event already carries assistant text and later tool outcomes, and the ledger accepts additive fields; the remaining uncertainty is whether enough naturally phrased claims are machine-resolvable. The experiment is the 50-settlement shadow run with manual audit of admission and join precision.
- **External signal:** A later typed receipt, exact failing-command rerun, independent file/state readback, or explicit owner adjudication. Two-signal release check: (1) synthetic known-positive/known-negative transcript fixtures prove extraction and temporal ordering; (2) blinded manual review of 100 shadow joins measures admission and settlement precision. The feature is not “done” unless both pass.
- **SOTA:** Proper scoring rules make truthful probability reports optimal in expectation ([Gneiting & Raftery](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)); prompt-level confidence is not established merely by elicitation ([Xiong et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6733cf15e10e2cd1d59af033c3bb8507-Abstract-Conference.html)); intrinsic self-correction can degrade without external feedback ([Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Abstract-Conference.html)).
- **Value × effort:** transformative × medium.
- **Novelty:** inferred-surprise.
- **Autonomy:** just-do-it for shadow instrumentation; confirm-first before changing owner-visible wording or enforcement.
- **NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).
- **Anti-overfit gate:** Passes. It extends the existing dispatcher/ledger and introduces none of the rejected components.

### Finding 2 — UNVERIFIED: issue a calibration passport, not one global trust score

- **Improvement:** Produce a versioned, domain-conditional “calibration passport” for each provider/model/evidence state.
- **What it is:** A compact table such as `provider × model_id × claim_family × evidence_state × band → n, observed accuracy, Wilson interval, Brier, coverage`. Claim families should be low-cardinality and deterministic: code execution, file/state existence, web factual, reverse-engineering anchor, schedule/estimate, and open-ended judgment. Pool sparse cells upward; never invent an embedding cluster or global reputation token.
- **Why inferred:** The owner uses a heterogeneous harness—78 skills, 22 agent types, 93 MCP servers, and 400 MCP tools appear in the corpus (`corpus.json:1`, `/capability_use`). A single marginal score could hide a model that is well calibrated on test exits and poor on web or RE claims. The corpus’s very different failure rates across tools further argues against one undifferentiated confidence prior (`corpus.json:1`, `/tool_usage`).
- **How to build:** Add a pure report function over settled ledger rows. Key by explicit metadata already present in normalized events plus deterministic claim family. Require `n>=30` per displayed leaf, otherwise pool to model+family, then provider+family, and label the result `insufficient evidence` if still sparse. On model/version change, start a new cohort; retain old cohorts for comparison. Do not automatically transfer a calibration curve across versions. It is **likely (55-80%)** that conditional passports will reveal materially different calibration between at least two claim families, because local task/tool error profiles vary sharply and multicalibration research finds value in subgroup checks; the exact local effect is UNVERIFIED and is settled by likelihood-ratio or bootstrap comparison after 100+ settlements per compared family.
- **External signal:** Only externally settled forecast rows. Validate the grouper with a known-positive fixture in every family and a known-negative “unknown family” fixture, then compare the passport’s counts to a separate SQL/JSONL aggregation implementation. These are mechanically independent code paths.
- **SOTA:** Multicalibration evaluates calibration across intersecting groups and reports improved fine-grained calibration in LLM benchmarks ([Detommaso et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/detommaso24a.html)). The recommendation deliberately uses categorical metadata, not their embedding option, to avoid the rejected vector-memory architecture.
- **Value × effort:** high × medium.
- **Novelty:** inferred-surprise.
- **Autonomy:** just-do-it in an offline/shadow report; confirm-first before using a passport to route or suppress work.
- **NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).
- **Anti-overfit gate:** Passes; bounded grouping over the existing JSONL ledger, no vector DB or new service.

### Finding 3 — separate truth state from probability so “verified” is not a 99% synonym

- **Improvement:** Use a two-axis output contract: `evidence_state` plus Sherman–Kent band.
- **What it is:** `VERIFIED` means a named external check established the exact claim at a specific state/time; `FALSIFIED` means an external check refuted it; `UNVERIFIED` means no adequate external resolution. A band is reserved for residual or forward uncertainty. Example: `VERIFIED: tests pass at commit X (receipt #Y). likely (55-80%) the deployment will remain healthy, because staging passed but production has not been exercised.`
- **Why inferred:** The current `_BAND` regex treats `[verified]`, `[plausible]`, `receipt`, `proven`, and percentages as interchangeable ways to silence the same hedge lint (`modules.py:2044-2049`). Yet the ledger already distinguishes receipt classes and freshness (`ledger.py:98-121,1065-1117`). The owner wants assertion when verified and hedging when uncertain; conflating those axes makes both less legible.
- **How to build:** Change the behavior rule and message template, not the hook topology. Add `evidence_state` to forecast events; do not score `VERIFIED` claims as forecasts unless they include a separately resolvable future component. Require a receipt reference for `VERIFIED`, and downgrade to `UNVERIFIED` if the receipt is absent or predates the last relevant mutation. Preserve the exact Sherman–Kent words for genuine forecasts. It is **very likely (80-90%)** this will reduce semantic ambiguity because the current regex explicitly conflates proof words and probability form; whether it improves owner decisions is UNVERIFIED, settled by a blinded A/B review of 30 paired completions measuring interpretation accuracy and reading time.
- **External signal:** Typed fresh receipt or independent readback for evidence state; later outcome for probability. The A/B acceptance result comes from the owner or blinded fresh-context readers, never the originating context.
- **SOTA:** Linguistic calibration is about downstream decision quality, which supports distinguishable, actionable uncertainty language rather than decorative confidence ([Band et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/band24a.html)). Human reliance research distinguishes calibrated confidence cues from merely high confidence ([Fregosi et al., AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38798)).
- **Value × effort:** high × low.
- **Novelty:** inferred-surprise.
- **Autonomy:** just-do-it for schema/shadow output; confirm-first for changing the global behavior contract.
- **NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus). The communication-rule portion is configuration-sized, while receipt-backed state validation makes the finding as a whole a small bus extension.
- **Anti-overfit gate:** Passes; it simplifies semantics and reuses receipts.

### Finding 4 — calibration debt must become an open thread, not a silent discard

- **Improvement:** Track every admitted but unresolved forecast as a settlement thread with censoring, TTL, and supersession.
- **What it is:** An unresolved prediction is neither correct nor incorrect. It remains `open` until its predicate resolves, is explicitly superseded, or reaches `resolve_by`, after which it becomes `censored`. The ledger projection exposes the oldest/highest-value unresolved items to the existing OPEN-THREADS workflow; routine overdue items stay silent and appear only in the periodic calibration report.
- **Why inferred:** “Never drop a thread” is a hard owner requirement. The current ledger already stores open failures and tombstones superseded failures (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/note.py:2-12,25-42`; shared reducer support at `ledger.py:741-746,802-805`). Forecasts that vanish when a session ends would recreate the discussed-then-forgotten failure in a measurement system intended to prevent it.
- **How to build:** Add `resolve_by`, `status`, and `supersedes_forecast` fields; project `open_forecasts` with a bound of 20, like current decisions/failures; append settlements instead of editing rows. Fold high-value resurfacing through the already-built calibrated-silence `resurface` type, whose design learns from external owner behavior and caps fatigue (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:2-53,75-124`). Do not add a button or a new reminder hook. It is **very likely (80-90%)** the append-only thread model will prevent measurement loss because the existing failure/supersession reducer already demonstrates the state pattern; the exact resurfacing threshold remains UNVERIFIED and is settled by acted/ignored/annoyed reactions over at least 30 resurfacing events.
- **External signal:** Later receipt/readback/owner adjudication closes the thread. A separate ledger replay must reproduce the same open set as the incremental state reducer; synthetic known-open and known-superseded controls validate both detectors before any “no open forecasts” claim.
- **SOTA:** Human-AI guidelines recommend remembering recent interactions, adapting cautiously, supporting correction, and timing proactive services to context ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019 guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)).
- **Value × effort:** high × medium.
- **Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

### Finding 5 — treat owner attention as a scarce calibration channel

- **Improvement:** Replace per-turn calibration nagging with a low-frequency trust card and exception-only escalation.
- **What it is:** Normal answers keep the required band next to the uncertain claim and cite its evidence, but the harness does not ask the owner to rate it. After at least 50 primary settlements or once monthly, whichever is later, provide a compact card: `almost certain: 18/20 correct (90%); likely: 12/22 (55%); unresolved: 7; largest gap: web factual`. Surface immediately only when a load-bearing current band is known to be materially overconfident and the action is irreversible/high-stakes.
- **Why inferred:** Prompt fatigue is explicitly a regression in this unit. Existing calibrated-silence already models useful versus annoying interjections from the owner’s next-turn behavior and raises the bar with correction rate, terse prompts, late hour, and recent density (`calibrated_silence.py:2-53,94-124`). Its companion reaction logger records acted/annoyed/ignored from external behavior rather than self-judgment (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/log_reaction.py:2-28,95-151`). The corpus correction candidates are too noisy to justify more automatic questions (`corpus.json:1`, `/owner_correction_candidates`).
- **How to build:** Generate the card from the offline passport; route it through the existing `gem` or `resurface` calibrated-silence call; cap at five lines; include one actionable implication; offer an existing command/file view rather than a new UI button. Owner adjudication is requested only for disputed, high-value settlements, batched to at most three questions via existing AskUserQuestion. It is **likely (55-80%)** this cadence will preserve information while reducing fatigue compared with per-claim prompts, because the existing system already uses context-sensitive suppression; the local effect is UNVERIFIED and is settled by the reaction ledger’s acted/ignored/annoyed rate plus owner interruption count across an A/B month.
- **External signal:** Owner next-turn behavior and explicit dismissals, already logged externally; card usefulness is measured by action/ignore/annoyance, not by the agent rating its own prose.
- **SOTA:** Human-AI guidance recommends timing services based on context, efficient dismissal/correction, granular feedback, cautious adaptation, and global control ([Amershi et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)). ### Finding 6 — build an oracle ladder that refuses pseudo-ground-truth

- **Improvement:** Formalize which external signals may settle a band and how conflicts are handled.
- **What it is:** Priority order: (A) deterministic task-specific oracle—exit/status plus assertion, exact readback, or test count; (B) two mechanically independent state observations; (C) explicit owner adjudication; (D) fresh-context, preferably cross-family model review, marked provisional. A model vote alone never enters the primary Brier/reliability computation. Conflicts become `disputed`, not majority-voted away.
- **Why inferred:** The law rejects same-context self-correction. The local behavior contract still tells the agent to run an *internal* generator-critic pass (`claude/AGENTS.md:74-75`), which is incompatible with using that pass as verification. The known defects document shows the cost of single unvalidated probes (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). The current receipt map and freshness checks provide a useful base but use ANY-of receipt classes (`ledger.py:109-121,1093-1117`), so settlement must be claim-specific rather than merely turn-fresh.
- **How to build:** Store `oracle_class`, `predicate_hash`, `subject_digest`, and `oracle_ref`. For code claims, bind the predicate to exact argv or changed paths; for absence claims, require a known-positive detector control plus a second independent data field/tool; for research claims, require source-level evidence and owner/fresh reviewer adjudication if binary settlement is impossible. Exclude `provisional`, `disputed`, and `censored` rows from the primary score but report their rates. It is **very likely (80-90%)** this ladder will prevent same-context outputs from masquerading as outcomes because the admission rule is deterministic; its false-rejection rate is UNVERIFIED and is settled by blinded audit of 100 candidate settlements.
- **External signal:** By definition, only the ladder’s external oracles. Completion requires two independent test signals: adversarial fixtures that try to self-settle or use unrelated green receipts must fail, and a separate reviewer must reproduce settlement labels from the raw exhibits.
- **SOTA:** Intrinsic correction can worsen answers without external feedback ([Huang et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8b4add8b0aa8749d80a34ca5d941c355-Abstract-Conference.html)); appropriate reliance studies measure outcomes rather than trusting confidence appearance ([Fregosi et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38798)).
- **Value × effort:** transformative × medium.
- **Novelty:** inferred-surprise.
- **Autonomy:** just-do-it for settlement policy in shadow; confirm-first before excluding previously accepted evidence from completion gates.
- **NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).
- **Anti-overfit gate:** Passes; deterministic policy over existing receipts, no critic service.

### Finding 8 — create a golden calibration replay from real failures before trusting live scores

- **Improvement:** Build a small, versioned golden-adversarial suite for forecast extraction, temporal commitment, oracle joining, and reporting.
- **What it is:** Seed cases from the seven validated single-probe errors, the fabricated orchestrator constraint, representative tool failures, successful controls, unresolved claims, contradictory receipts, and deliberately unresolvable prose. Every case includes assistant text, band, timestamp/sequence, external exhibit, expected admission, expected outcome, and expected score contribution.
- **Why inferred:** The owner wants evidence, not a confidence dashboard that can silently score the wrong rows. The correction miner’s 41/41 false-positive smoke sample proves weak text classifiers can manufacture labels (`corpus.json:1`, `/owner_correction_candidates`). The hook detector itself returned zero on a known-positive control and is explicitly marked broken (`corpus.json:1`, `/hooks`). A calibration system without detector controls would reproduce the exact failure it is meant to measure.
- **How to build:** Start with 40 fixtures: 10 true/10 false externally resolvable forecasts across bands, 5 post-outcome statements that must be rejected as non-prospective, 5 same-context self-settlements that must be rejected, 5 censored/disputed cases, and 5 known-positive/negative detector controls. Tests assert immutable ordering, exact oracle class, no leakage into primary score, deterministic rerun, and report totals. Then conduct a blinded live audit of 100 shadow rows. It is **very likely (80-90%)** this catches high-impact join and detector regressions because it directly encodes every locally observed detector-control class; coverage of novel future failure modes is necessarily incomplete and is expanded only from externally confirmed misses.
- **External signal:** Fixture oracles come from recorded command outputs/readbacks and the independently diagnosed known-defect exhibits; the live acceptance sample is labeled blind by a different model/context or the owner, with disagreements left disputed.
- **SOTA:** Finite-sample forecast evaluation can have poor discrimination even with proper scores, motivating explicit power/reliability testing rather than metric worship ([Marcotte et al., ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/marcotte23a.html)). Test-based calibration work likewise emphasizes interpretable, statistically grounded error measurement ([Matsubara et al., UAI 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/matsubara23a.html)).
- **Value × effort:** high × medium.
- **Novelty:** user-asked.
