# X075 — Cross-domain borrow: law of evidence → agent memory

## Executive result

**Verdict: transfer the procedural skeleton, not the legal outcomes.** Law of evidence has not solved “memory” in the database or cognitive sense. It does not prescribe what an institution should retain, how quickly a mutable fact becomes stale, or when a record should be physically deleted. It has, however, developed a coherent answer to a narrower and highly relevant question: *when may a decision-maker rely on a proposition whose source is outside the decision-maker’s direct present perception, and how must that proposition remain contestable?* The current Federal Rules of Evidence are an appropriate concrete source system for this lens; the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts describes them as governing admission or exclusion in most federal proceedings and links the current rules ([U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Evidence](https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure/federal-rules-evidence)).

The transferable unit is an **evidentiary memory lifecycle**:

1. capture near the event and identify who or what had knowledge;
2. authenticate the record as the thing claimed;
3. retain provenance through every transformation, requiring a reliability basis at every nested layer;
4. retrieve the completing context and make the underlying material inspectable when relying on a summary;
5. preserve later contradictions as impeachment instead of silently overwriting them;
6. apply a higher proof requirement when an erroneous reliance would be more costly;
7. retire mutable claims from automatic reliance when they pass a domain-specific review boundary, while retaining them as historical evidence unless an independent retention policy requires deletion.

This lifecycle is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce provenance loss, summary laundering, silent contradiction, and reliance on stale mutable state in a harness, because each target failure is intercepted by a directly corresponding admission check described below. This effectiveness estimate is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a shadow-mode replay of a stratified set of historical cross-session memory retrievals, scored for erroneous admission, correct admission, abstention, latency, and token cost against the existing memory path**. The estimate is below “almost certain” because the corpus supplied to this program does not measure memory errors as a distinct class, this unit was instructed not to inspect the local harness, and model compliance with metadata is not guaranteed.

The proposal is deliberately small. It is a schema plus capture/retrieval policy riding the existing memory/hook/skill bus, not a second orchestrator, knowledge graph, vector database, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability service, or guardrail framework. It therefore passes the anti-overfit gate. No SUBJECT tree was read or changed; this is the requested external ideation lens.

## What the source field has actually solved

### 1. Recorded recollection: trustworthy capture is time- and adoption-sensitive

Federal Rule of Evidence 803(5) admits a recorded recollection only when the witness once knew the matter but cannot now recall it well enough, the record was made or adopted while the matter was fresh, and the record accurately reflects the witness’s knowledge. Even then, the proponent normally reads it into evidence rather than admitting the document as an exhibit; only the adverse party may offer the exhibit ([FRE 803(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

In its own field, this is not a claim that all contemporaneous notes are true. It is a foundation for substituting a fresh record for degraded human recall, coupled to a witness who can establish prior knowledge, freshness, and accuracy. “Made or adopted” matters: a witness can validate a record prepared by another, but mere presence in a file is not adoption. The rule also limits the form of use, which prevents the proponent from turning the document itself into a rhetorically over-weighted exhibit by default.

**Transfer:** a candidate long-term memory should identify the direct observer/tool result, `observed_at`, `recorded_at`, and whether the owner or agent explicitly adopted the proposition. Capture-time proximity is positive evidence for an event memory; it is not a perpetual freshness warranty. An agent-generated inference written after the event is not relabeled “owner fact” merely because it was saved in the owner’s memory file.

**What this catches:** post-hoc reconstructions presented as direct observations; model inferences promoted to user preferences; notes whose author/source is unknown; and a later session paraphrasing a prior session as though it directly observed the underlying event.

**Disanalogy:** the legal foundation depends on a human witness who once knew and can attest to accuracy. An agent record usually has no conscious declarant and cannot meaningfully swear that it “remembers.” The transfer therefore uses machine-verifiable source references and explicit owner adoption, not a fiction that the model is a witness. Copying the exception literally would fail.

### 2. Regularly conducted records: routine process plus a knowledgeable source, with a trustworthiness escape hatch

Rule 803(6) requires a record made at or near the time by, or from information transmitted by, someone with knowledge; kept in a regularly conducted activity; made as a regular practice; supported by a custodian/witness/certification; and not shown by an opponent to be untrustworthy in source, method, or circumstances ([FRE 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)). The Advisory Committee explanation is especially useful: systematic checking, continuity, operational reliance, and a duty of accuracy can supply reliability, but an outsider’s statement does not become reliable merely because an employee copied it accurately into a regular record. The Committee’s example is a police report that scrupulously records a bystander’s account: the officer’s recording process does not cure the bystander link ([FRE 803 advisory notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

In its own field, the mechanism separates **recording integrity** from **source reliability**. It also makes the presumption defeasible: an opponent may show that the source or preparation circumstances indicate a lack of trustworthiness.

**Transfer:** distinguish a mechanically captured tool result from the truth of statements inside it. A clean transcript proves “the API returned X,” not necessarily “X is true.” Routine memory-writing hooks earn trust only for faithfully recording fields; they do not confer trust on unverified model text or third-party content. Store both the recording link and the originating source link.

**What this catches:** “the memory file says it, therefore it is true”; a web snippet laundered through an agent summary; repeated copies of the same false claim counted as independent confirmation; and a generated status report mistaken for direct system state.

**Disanalogy:** organizations subject to Rule 803(6) have human duties, incentives, and established practices that may not exist in an autonomous harness. Regular automated generation can produce regular automated error. Routine production is therefore evidence about process consistency, not a sufficient truth score.

### 3. Authentication and chain of custody: identity and integrity, not truth

Rule 901(a) makes the proponent produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item is what the proponent claims. Illustrative methods include a knowledgeable witness, distinctive characteristics considered with circumstances, and evidence describing a process or system and showing it produces an accurate result ([FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). This is the doctrinal home for authentication; chain of custody is one method of establishing that an offered item has not been substituted or materially altered.

The limit is crucial. Authentication establishes identity sufficiently for use; it does not establish that every assertion inside the item is true. Nor does every imperfect custody record force exclusion. In *Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts*, the Supreme Court observed that gaps in a chain normally bear on evidentiary weight rather than admissibility and that the prosecution need not call every person who handled an item ([557 U.S. 305, 311 n.1 (2009)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/557/305/)).

**Transfer:** give a memory stable identity and a derivation ledger: content digest, source locator, capture time, producer, transformation, and parent memory IDs. A broken link lowers reliance or requires revalidation; it need not always delete the item. A hash can show that bytes did not change after capture, but it cannot show that the captured proposition was true or remains current.

**What this catches:** provenance detached by summarization; wrong-version references; accidental mutation; a claim attributed to the owner that originated with the agent; and a derived recommendation whose source record can no longer be located.

**Disanalogy:** physical evidence usually concerns identity through a bounded sequence of handlers. Agent knowledge is copied, merged, rephrased, and semantically transformed; byte hashes cannot authenticate semantic equivalence. The necessary translation is a derivation DAG-like list of parent IDs, but this is a list stored on ordinary records—not a graph database or knowledge-graph service.

### 4. Hearsay within hearsay: every transformation layer needs its own warrant

Rule 805 permits nested hearsay only when **each part** of the combined statements conforms to an exception. Its own example distinguishes a routine hospital record from a family member’s embedded statement: the regular-record basis for the outer record does not automatically validate the inner declarant, which needs an independent basis ([FRE 805 and advisory note](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805)).

In its own field, this blocks trust laundering through a reliable container. An admissible outer layer is not a blanket license for embedded assertions.

**Transfer:** every derived memory needs a typed chain such as `tool observation → agent extraction → agent inference → compressed summary`. Each layer records its operation and warrant. The retrieval gate may use an extraction as evidence of what a source says while refusing to use the source’s claim as evidence of real-world truth. A chain fails at the weakest unsupported link for the intended use.

**What this catches:** “session summary says the test passed” when the summary derives from an assistant’s unverified claim; a memory distilled from a prior memory with no path to raw evidence; and source-count inflation when five summaries descend from one observation.

**Disanalogy:** hearsay doctrine is full of historically and constitutionally contingent categories; some exceptions pursue litigation practicality rather than optimal epistemic accuracy. The harness should transfer the recursive-link principle, not implement a catalog of legal hearsay exceptions.

### 5. Completeness and inspectable summaries: compression must not sever context

Rule 106 lets an adverse party require contemporaneous introduction of another part or related statement when fairness requires it. The Advisory Committee identifies two rationales: excerpts can create misleading impressions, and delayed repair can be inadequate ([FRE 106](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)). Rule 1006 separately permits summaries of voluminous admissible material, but requires the underlying originals or duplicates to be available for examination or copying; the court may order their production ([FRE 1006](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1006)). The 2024 committee note emphasizes that parties need a fair opportunity to evaluate a summary and that an inaccurate or argumentative summary remains subject to exclusion.

In its own field, these rules accept compression as necessary while making it contestable. The summary is useful evidence, not a replacement that makes its basis disappear. Completion is narrower than “include everything”: it targets material needed to correct the misimpression created by the excerpt.

**Transfer:** a memory summary must retain resolvable parent references. Retrieval should co-load directly linked qualifications, scope boundaries, contrary findings, and superseding records when omission would change the proposition’s practical meaning. Raw material can live in the ordinary session/artifact store; it need not be injected into every prompt.

**What this catches:** “always use X” extracted from “for this repository only, use X”; a green test result without the skipped-test qualification; a preference without the owner’s later exception; and a compacted summary that drops uncertainty or a failed verification.

**Disanalogy:** a courtroom has an adversary motivated to demand completion. A single-agent harness does not. The retrieval mechanism must mechanically search linked qualifications and contradictions, or assign an explicit adversarial check to the same model. Self-review is correlated with the original error, so it is weaker than real adversarial testing.

### 6. Impeachment: later inconsistency changes weight without erasing history

Rule 806 allows the credibility of an admitted hearsay declarant to be attacked as if the declarant had testified. It expressly permits an inconsistent statement or conduct regardless of when it occurred ([FRE 806](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_806)). The rule treats an out-of-court declarant as functionally present for credibility assessment rather than shielding the statement from challenge merely because the speaker is absent.

**Transfer:** when a later source conflicts with a memory, append the conflict and move the mutable proposition to `contested` or `superseded`; never overwrite the old record as though it never existed. At retrieval, show the conflict and prefer a later, better-authenticated observation only for the scope and time it actually covers. Source-level reliability incidents can “impeach” other records from the same method, but they must not automatically negate unrelated content.

**What this catches:** stale owner preferences silently surviving a correction; old endpoint/tool availability; status assertions contradicted by live probes; and oscillation where sessions alternately overwrite each other and lose the reason for change.

**Disanalogy:** legal impeachment often attacks a human declarant’s character or capacity. A harness should prefer claim- and method-specific reliability evidence. Global “source reputation” is prone to overgeneralization and feedback loops.

### 7. Burdens and standards of proof: allocate the risk of an erroneous reliance

In *Addington v. Texas*, the Supreme Court explained that a standard of proof allocates the risk of error and expresses the importance attached to the decision; it selected “clear and convincing” evidence for involuntary civil commitment rather than either ordinary civil preponderance or criminal reasonable doubt ([441 U.S. 418, 423–33 (1979)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep441/usrep441418/usrep441418.pdf)). The mechanism is not “more evidence is always better.” It deliberately assigns more risk of error to one side when the interests at stake justify it.

**Transfer:** memory admission should be use-sensitive. A weak, explicitly labeled memory may help generate a search query. The same memory should not authorize deletion, credential changes, external publication, or a claim that something is absent. For high-cost actions, put the burden on the remembered proposition: require live revalidation and, where the decision is an absence/broken/done claim, two mechanically independent signals with a known-present control as required by this program’s honesty rules.

**What this catches:** one stale memory driving an irreversible action; a historical “service unavailable” result being treated as current; and one broken detector becoming evidence that a capability is absent.

**Disanalogy:** legal verbal standards are not calibrated probabilities and should not be translated into fake numerical confidence thresholds. Courts allocate error between litigants under substantive law; an agent balances heterogeneous harms, reversibility, time, and owner intent. The transferable mechanism is asymmetric risk allocation, not the legal labels or their supposed percentages.

### 8. Age can change admissibility, but evidence law supplies no general freshness clock

Rule 609(b) creates a special presumption against using convictions more than ten years old for impeachment unless their probative value, supported by specific facts and circumstances, substantially outweighs prejudicial effect and notice is given ([FRE 609(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_609)). This shows a valid abstract pattern: age can shift the default and the burden for a particular use.

It does **not** establish that ten years, or any universal TTL, is epistemically meaningful. The rule is limited to conviction evidence used to attack truthfulness and is designed partly to prevent unfair prejudice. Evidence law offers no general doctrine that detects when an API endpoint, project status, owner preference, or factual statement has become stale.

**Transfer:** only the pattern transfers: after a type-specific review boundary, automatic use ends and the proponent must provide fresh, specific support. The boundary must be chosen from the volatility of the proposition and cost of error, not copied from Rule 609.

## Concrete harness design

### Recommendation 1 — UNVERIFIED: Evidentiary Memory Envelope — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Wrap each durable memory in a small typed envelope rather than saving free-floating prose. **UNVERIFIED premise: the existing harness exposes a capture hook or skill boundary capable of adding and validating these fields; settle it by enumerating the actual memory write/read entry points and running one synthetic record through each.** If no such bus exists, the size tag must be revisited; this unit does not claim that local fact.

Minimum envelope:

```yaml
id: stable-id
proposition: concise, scoped claim
kind: observation | owner_instruction | quotation | extraction | inference | summary
scope: repository/task/account/world and any limiting predicate
source:
  locator: session/tool/artifact/URL identifier
  producer: owner | agent | tool | external-author
  content_digest: optional digest of captured bytes
observed_at: timestamp-or-null
recorded_at: timestamp
adoption: owner_explicit | agent_only | mechanical_capture
derivation:
  parent_ids: []
  operation: direct | extract | infer | summarize | merge
validity:
  temporality: immutable_event | mutable_state | preference | policy | prediction
  valid_from: optional
  review_after: optional, type-specific
  invalidation_keys: optional paths/versions/services/people
status: active | due_for_review | contested | superseded | quarantined
relations:
  qualifies: []
  contradicts: []
  supersedes: []
verification:
  signals: [{method, source_id, observed_at}]
  known_present_control: optional result
sensitivity_retention:
  class: ordinary | secret | personal | regulated
  disposition: session_only | retain_until | owner_managed
```

Do **not** store a scalar “truth score.” Independent dimensions—source identity, directness, freshness, adoption, contradiction, and action risk—must remain visible. Five descendants of one source count as one signal, not five. Content digests authenticate bytes only. `review_after` changes admissibility for mutable uses; it does not rewrite the historical claim.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit `kind`, `parent_ids`, and `adoption` would prevent some agent inferences from being retrieved as owner statements, because these fields preserve the distinction that free prose erases. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an adversarial fixture set containing owner quotes, agent paraphrases, nested summaries, and deliberately mislabeled records, measuring classification and retrieval errors with and without the envelope**.

### Recommendation 2 — UNVERIFIED: Capture Foundation Gate — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

At durable-memory write time:

1. accept direct event observations only with a source locator and capture time;
2. accept owner instructions with the exact owner statement or resolvable transcript span plus scope; mark paraphrases as `extraction` or `summary`, not `owner_instruction` unless explicitly adopted;
3. require every inference/summary to name parent IDs and its transformation;
4. reject or quarantine a mutable proposition with neither `observed_at` nor a current verification basis;
5. attach nearby qualifications and detected conflicting active memories rather than resolving conflict silently;
6. prevent secrets and personal data from entering ordinary durable memory unless the explicit retention class permits it.

**UNVERIFIED premise: a write-time gate will not cause unacceptable friction or induce the model to fabricate metadata; settle it by shadow-capturing 200 candidate memories, comparing metadata to raw sessions, and measuring false rejection, fabricated lineage, added tokens, and latency.** The gate is **likely (55-80%)** to improve memory quality because contemporaneous automated metadata is less reconstruction-prone than metadata invented at later retrieval, with Rule 803’s freshness rationale and the direct source references providing the evidence for that direction. The broad band reflects unknown local capture surfaces and model behavior.

### Recommendation 3 — UNVERIFIED: Use-Sensitive Admission and Staleness Gate — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Before a retrieved memory influences an answer or action, classify the proposed use rather than treating retrieval as admission:

| Use tier | Examples | Minimum disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Exploratory | search terms, hypotheses, candidate files | May use contested or due-for-review memory only with an uncertainty label; never present it as fact. |
| Ordinary/reversible | explanation, local reversible edit, low-cost choice | Require traceable source, supported derivation links, in-scope proposition, no unresolved material contradiction, and current validity or a live refresh. |
| High-cost/irreversible/external | deletion, security or credential change, publication/message, legal/financial claim, assertion that something is absent/broken/done | Require live revalidation against primary state; for absence/broken/done, require two mechanically independent signals and a known-present control; request owner authority where the action itself needs it. |

For `mutable_state`, compare current time and invalidation signals to `review_after`. If due, retrieve the record as a search lead, perform a live probe, and append a new observation. For `immutable_event` (“test X failed at time T”), age does not make the historical event false; ensure only that the system does not silently generalize it into a current-state claim. For preferences, a later explicit owner instruction can supersede an earlier one within overlapping scope, while the earlier record remains historical.

The review boundary should be assigned by type and environment: a process ID may require session-only validity, branch status may require validation before every use, tool availability may require validation after environment change, and a stable owner preference may remain active until contradicted. These example policies are **likely (55-80%)** to be directionally appropriate because their volatility differs, but their actual intervals are **UNVERIFIED; settle them by measuring observed change intervals and the harm/cost of stale use per memory type, then selecting boundaries from that empirical distribution**.

The admission gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch a stale mutable memory before a high-cost action when the record is correctly typed and the gate is actually invoked, because mandatory live revalidation directly tests current state. End-to-end benefit is only **likely (55-80%)**, because typing errors and bypass paths may dominate; **UNVERIFIED, settle with fault injection that seeds expired endpoints, changed branches, corrected preferences, and false “done” memories across every retrieval path**.

### Recommendation 4 — UNVERIFIED: Completeness and Impeachment Bundle — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

When one memory is selected, load a compact “adversarial bundle” before reliance:

- the selected proposition and intended use;
- its direct parents, or locators sufficient to inspect them;
- active `qualifies`, `contradicts`, and `supersedes` relations;
- distinct verification roots after collapsing shared ancestry;
- the newest observation for the same scoped predicate;
- an explicit statement when a parent cannot be resolved.

Only relations material to the intended use should enter context. This is Rule 106-style completion, not wholesale prompt stuffing. If a later contradiction arrives, append it and atomically mark the older mutable record `contested` or `superseded`. Never delete contrary evidence merely because the current system selects one version.

This bundle is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent qualification loss during compaction, supported by Rule 106’s precise account of misleading excerpts and Rule 1006’s inspectable-source requirement. It is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by creating paired source passages whose omitted qualifiers reverse the correct action, then compare retrieval decisions and context cost with plain top-k retrieval versus relation completion**. The benefit may be limited if relation extraction misses the qualifier, which is why raw-parent inspection remains available.

### Recommendation 5 — UNVERIFIED: Retire, Quarantine, Supersede; Delete Only Under an Independent Retention Rule — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Define “forget” in three distinct ways:

1. **Recall retirement:** exclude an item from default retrieval because it is irrelevant, out of scope, superseded, or due for review.
2. **Reliance quarantine:** retain it as evidence/history but forbid factual use until revalidated or conflict-resolved.
3. **Physical deletion:** remove content because an owner instruction, privacy/security rule, or bounded retention policy requires it.

Evidence law supports the first two only by analogy; it does not supply the third policy. A superseded record is often valuable for explaining why behavior changed and for detecting oscillation. A secret that should never have been retained should be deleted rather than preserved for “chain of custody.” If deletion accountability is needed, retain only a non-sensitive tombstone such as ID, deletion time, and policy reason; do not retain a digest where even a digest would violate the applicable policy.

**UNVERIFIED premise: the existing memory store supports status filters and retention classes as configuration; settle it by inspecting the store schema and executing expiry, quarantine, and deletion fixtures.** If code is required, this recommendation becomes BUILD-SMALL rather than CONFIG. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that separating recall retirement from physical deletion prevents the common design error of choosing between “prompt forever” and “erase all history,” because these are technically independent operations. Whether it reduces owner-visible errors is **UNVERIFIED; settle with a longitudinal test measuring stale recalls, missing useful history, sensitive-data exposure, and storage growth**.

## Worked examples: what the borrow catches

### A. “The MCP is dead” survives for weeks

An old session recorded a connection failure. Under the envelope it is an immutable event—“connection attempt failed at T”—and a mutable inference—“service unavailable”—with a short review boundary. It remains admissible as historical evidence but not as current status. Before skipping or removing the MCP, the high-cost gate requires a live probe; before declaring it absent/broken, the harness also needs two mechanically independent signals and a known-present control. This design would **very likely (80-90%)** block reliance on the old result if all status decisions pass the gate, because the old inference is explicitly expired. That is **UNVERIFIED; settle by seeding a recovered service after a failure memory and replaying status/removal decisions across all call paths**.

### B. An agent paraphrase becomes an owner preference

Session 1: owner says, “For this migration, avoid library Q until its Windows bug is fixed.” A summary stores “Owner never wants Q.” The capture gate marks the summary as an agent transformation with a parent transcript span and migration scope. Completeness retrieval supplies the limitation; a later “use Q for the Linux service” instruction supersedes only overlapping scope. This would **likely (55-80%)** prevent preference overgeneralization, because the source and qualifier become retrievable together; **UNVERIFIED, settle with scoped-instruction/summary adversarial pairs and score whether later sessions obey the correct scope**.

### C. Five memories appear to confirm one false report

A web page says X; two agents summarize the page; a session compactor summarizes both; a later report cites two summaries. Parent ancestry collapses all five records to one source root. The admission gate reports one external assertion, not five independent signals. This would **almost certainly (90-99%)** detect shared ancestry when every transformation correctly records parent IDs, because graph traversal of explicit IDs is deterministic. Metadata completeness is **UNVERIFIED; settle by injecting missing and false parent links and measuring ancestry-collapse precision and recall**.

### D. “Tests passed” loses the qualification “integration tests skipped”

Rule 106-style relations link the pass result to the skipped-test qualification and the raw command result. Any use of “tests passed” to declare work done retrieves both. The record can support “the executed subset passed,” not “all relevant tests passed.” The bundle would **very likely (80-90%)** prevent the exact overclaim when the skip is linked, because the qualification changes the admitted proposition; **UNVERIFIED, settle with test outputs containing skips, expected failures, flaky retries, and partial suites, then score final status claims**.

### E. A memory’s bytes are intact but its conclusion is obsolete

A content digest validates a saved tool configuration. A software update changes the valid schema. Authentication still succeeds: this is the same old configuration. Freshness fails for the intended current-use claim. This illustrates why chain of custody alone cannot solve staleness. The gate’s separate identity and validity dimensions would **almost certainly (90-99%)** distinguish “unaltered” from “current” when temporality and review state are populated, because no inference from digest to freshness exists in the policy. Population accuracy is **UNVERIFIED; settle with version-change fixtures and assert that unchanged hashes never bypass current-schema validation**.

## Adversarial transfer audit

### Structural matches

1. **Both domains decide under indirect information.** Courts often receive records and out-of-court statements; later agent sessions consume summaries and tool traces rather than re-experiencing prior events.
2. **Both domains suffer transformation chains.** A person tells a recorder who creates a report that another person summarizes; an agent pipeline performs an analogous series of semantic transformations.
3. **Both domains compress large records.** Trials use summaries because underlying records are voluminous; agents compact sessions because context is bounded.
4. **Both domains must distinguish admissibility from weight/use.** A record can be retained and inspectable while receiving little weight; a memory can remain historical while being barred from current-state action.
5. **Both domains face asymmetric error costs.** A wrong search lead and a wrong destructive action should not require the same support, just as law varies standards with the interests at stake.

These similarities make the procedural skeleton **very likely (80-90%)** to transfer at the level of design invariants, based on the one-to-one failure interception described in the examples. That claim remains **UNVERIFIED; settle with the shadow replay and fault-injection experiments specified above**.

### Structural mismatches and rejected borrows

1. **Adversary mismatch.** Evidence doctrine expects opposing parties to object, impeach, and demand completeness. A harness often has one model producing and reviewing the same claim. Therefore “the opponent can challenge it” is not a mechanism unless retrieval automatically exposes contrary material.
2. **Objective mismatch.** Evidence rules optimize a mixture of accuracy, fairness, constitutional rights, jury management, efficiency, and policy. They are not a general theory of truth. Legal admissibility must not become a proxy truth label.
3. **Declarant mismatch.** Human witnesses perceive, remember, adopt, lie, and face cross-examination. Tools and models have different error modes. Perjury incentives and demeanor evidence do not transfer.
4. **Custody mismatch.** Physical custody is mostly linear; information is copied and semantically merged. Byte integrity is necessary in some cases but insufficient for semantic fidelity.
5. **Temporal mismatch.** Litigation ordinarily adjudicates bounded past events and eventually reaches finality. Agents repeatedly act in an open, changing environment. A once-admitted fact may need current revalidation indefinitely.
6. **Retention mismatch.** Courts’ evidence rules concern what is usable at a proceeding, not comprehensive records retention, privacy deletion, minimization, or secret handling. “Keep everything for impeachment” would be unsafe and costly.
7. **Threshold mismatch.** “Preponderance,” “clear and convincing,” and “beyond a reasonable doubt” are normative legal standards, not calibrated machine probabilities. Assigning them percentages would create precision theater.

Accordingly, the following tempting designs are rejected:

- **Reject a universal memory TTL.** Rule 609’s ten-year boundary is use- and evidence-specific; copying it, or any single interval, has no structural basis.
- **Reject hash-equals-truth.** Authentication shows identity/integrity, not truth or currency.
- **Reject all-or-nothing admissibility.** A stale record can be a useful search lead or historical fact while being insufficient for a destructive action.
- **Reject deletion on contradiction.** Impeachment requires retaining the inconsistent history unless privacy/security retention policy independently requires deletion.
- **Reject legal hearsay categories as software policy.** Transfer recursive provenance, not doctrinal exceptions developed for courts.
- **Reject a second “evidence engine” runtime.** A daemon, vector database, knowledge graph, trained critic, or durable workflow system would violate the anti-overfit gate and is unnecessary for stable IDs plus ordinary relations and hooks.
- **Reject model-generated confidence as the burden itself.** The burden is satisfied by required evidence operations—source trace, live probe, independence, control—not by confident prose.

## Minimal implementation sequence and falsification plan

This is a design, not a build authorization. If a build unit takes it forward, the smallest responsible sequence is:

1. define the envelope and five states;
2. shadow-write metadata without changing retrieval;
3. audit metadata against raw sources;
4. shadow-run the admission decision on actual retrievals;
5. test targeted faults (stale state, missing parent, shared ancestry, scope qualifier, contradiction, sensitive retention);
6. enable warnings for high-cost uses before blocking ordinary uses;
7. enable hard gating only where bypass coverage is measured;
8. tune review boundaries from observed volatility, not intuition.

Success metrics should include erroneous factual admissions, stale-action attempts blocked, qualification/contradiction recall, owner-attribution errors, false quarantines, missing-history errors, sensitive-data retention, context tokens, latency, and bypass rate. “More memories rejected” is not a success metric.

The composite mechanism should be retained only if it produces a meaningful reduction in stale or provenance-defective reliance without materially increasing false abstention and context cost. A reasonable initial falsification criterion is **UNVERIFIED and intentionally provisional: reject or redesign the mechanism if the shadow replay shows no reduction in known-bad admissions, or if correct-admission loss and task latency outweigh the avoided error cost; settle the actual thresholds with the owner’s risk preferences and a representative replay set**. No legal doctrine supplies those product thresholds.
