# X024 — Cross-domain borrow: intelligence tradecraft → agent trust calibration

**Unit:** X024  
**Source field:** intelligence tradecraft (source grading, Sherman-Kent-style probability language, analytic confidence, ACH/structured analytic techniques)  
**Target:** knowing how much to believe a particular agent output, conditional on agent identity and task type  
**Research posture:** external ideation only; no local-repository claims or inspection  
**Date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive verdict

The full intelligence-production analogy does **not** transfer. Its useful kernel does.

The strongest transferable mechanism is not “give every agent a reputation score.” It is the intelligence discipline of keeping four different questions separate:

1. How reliable has this source been in the relevant domain?
2. How credible is this particular report, given its evidence and provenance?
3. How likely is the report's proposition to be true?
4. How sound and stable is the foundation for that likelihood judgment?

UK defence doctrine explicitly separates source reliability from information credibility, says the two ratings need not match, and gives the examples of a usually reliable source reporting improbable information and an unreliable source reporting confirmed information. It also requires re-evaluation over time and warns about circular reporting ([UK MOD, *Joint Doctrine Publication 2-00*, pp. 67–69](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653a4b0780884d0013f71bb0/JDP_2_00_Ed_4_web.pdf)). US ICD 203 separately requires source-quality descriptions, uncertainty explanations, explicit separation of evidence from assumptions and judgments, and analysis of alternatives ([ODNI, *ICD 203 Analytic Standards*, pp. 3–5](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). The UK intelligence assessment community separately distinguishes probability from analytical confidence; its latter rating evaluates the information base, analytic rigor, and complexity/volatility ([UK Government, *Explaining Uncertainty in UK Intelligence Assessment*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)).

That four-way separation is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve an agent harness's trust decisions when compared with a single global agent score, because the doctrine directly addresses two structurally matching errors: a good source can produce a bad report, and a weak source can produce a well-corroborated report. The settling experiment is a preregistered replay in which risk decisions made from the proposed four-field trust card are compared with decisions made from one global agent score on held-out, independently resolved tasks.

The proposed implementation is deliberately small: add a claim/evidence envelope, maintain a versioned `agent × task-type` performance ledger from independently observable outcomes, and invoke an alternative-hypothesis challenge only for consequential claims whose foundations are weak. It rides the existing output/config/hook/skill buses and adds no second orchestration runtime, vector database, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability service, or guardrail framework.

The strongest negative finding is equally important: canonical Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) is not justified as a default evaluator or an automatic numeric trust score. A randomized study of 50 intelligence analysts found mixed evidence of debiasing and found that ACH may increase inconsistency and error; it also identifies underspecified hypothesis selection, evidence ratings, and the absence of prior-probability updating as weaknesses ([Dhami et al., 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3550)). Therefore, default-on ACH is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be worth its latency and complexity in routine agent work, based on that controlled result plus the mismatch between open-ended intelligence hypotheses and many executable software tasks. The settling experiment is an agent-task randomized trial of canonical ACH, a lightweight “state one rival and its discriminator” challenge, and no challenge, stratified by task risk and task type.

## 1. What intelligence tradecraft has actually solved

### 1.1 The Admiralty/NATO two-axis grade: source reliability is not report credibility

In its own field, the NATO Intelligence Grading System (often called the Admiralty Code) encodes two judgments in one digraph:

- source reliability: A (completely reliable) through E (unreliable), with F meaning reliability cannot be judged;
- information credibility: 1 (confirmed by other sources) through 5 (improbable), with 6 meaning truth cannot be judged.

The doctrine's operational point is independence between the axes, not the typography of the code. Source reliability depends on such factors as motivation, expertise, access, and reporting history. Information credibility is assessed from the individual report's factual/logical plausibility and, preferably, corroboration. Doctrine says a B5 and E1 are both coherent grades: a usually reliable source can supply improbable information, while an unreliable source can supply information confirmed elsewhere. Grades are revisited as new information arrives ([UK MOD, *JDP 2-00*, pp. 67–69](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653a4b0780884d0013f71bb0/JDP_2_00_Ed_4_web.pdf)).

This solves a recurring epistemic category error: substituting biography or reputation for evaluation of the present claim. It also prevents the inverse error of permanently ignoring a source after poor history. A report remains evaluable on its own evidence.

The doctrine adds a second indispensable detail: corroboration is not mere agreement. Analysts must remain alert to circular reporting, where apparently separate reports derive from a common origin ([UK MOD, *JDP 2-00*, p. 68](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653a4b0780884d0013f71bb0/JDP_2_00_Ed_4_web.pdf)). This is directly relevant when multiple agents share a base model, prompt context, retrieval result, or generated intermediate.

### 1.2 Sherman Kent's estimative language: make likelihood terms mean something

Sherman Kent's “Words of Estimative Probability” distinguished statements close to knowable fact from analytic judgments and future-facing estimates, then argued for a small, consistently understood vocabulary of odds ([CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, Sherman Kent, *Words of Estimative Probability*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf)). Modern ICD 203 institutionalizes a seven-band vocabulary tied to numerical ranges and requires consistent use ([ODNI, *ICD 203*, p. 4](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). The exact bins differ among institutions; the transferable mechanism is a controlled vocabulary that prevents “possible,” “likely,” and “almost certain” from functioning as unscored rhetoric.

The UK formulation makes the companion distinction especially clear:

- **probability** is the assessed likelihood that a statement is true or an event occurs;
- **analytical confidence** is the soundness and stability of the foundations for that probability judgment.

Its confidence assessment considers information base, analytical rigor, and complexity/volatility, and includes a statement describing the specific remaining uncertainty and how confidence could be increased ([UK Government, 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)). ICD 203 likewise says confidence can depend on logic, quantity and quality of sources, and topic understanding; it requires causes and effects of uncertainty and indicators that would change the judgment ([ODNI, *ICD 203*, p. 4](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

This solves another category error: “I think X is very likely” does not entail “I have a strong basis for thinking X is very likely.” A sparse but decisive observation can support high probability; a turbulent or poorly observed domain can make even a directionally reasonable judgment fragile.

### 1.3 Forecasting tournaments: keep score by topic, not by eloquence

IARPA's Aggregative Contingent Estimation program sought probabilistic forecasts, mathematical aggregation, and weighting by attributes including past performance and expertise ([IARPA, *ACE Program*](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/ace)). Forecasting tournaments create resolvable questions and compare individuals, teams, and algorithms on common ground ([Tetlock et al., 2014](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/0963721414534257/)). Brier's original probability score provides a concrete way to penalize distance between forecast probability and resolved outcome ([Brier, 1950](https://journals.ametsoc.org/doi/10.1175/1520-0493%281950%29078%3C0001%3AVOFEIT%3E2.0.CO%3B2)).

The borrowed lesson is not “all agent quality is forecasting.” It is that trust claims become auditable only when predictions are recorded before resolution, questions have resolution criteria, and performance is compared within relevant topics. An agent that is calibrated on dependency-version questions has not thereby earned trust on security review or causal diagnosis.

Using Brier score for **binary, genuinely probabilistic, independently resolvable claims** is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal overconfidence that simple pass rates conceal, based on Brier's direct penalty for probability error and intelligence forecasting's use of scored questions. The broad range reflects a major transfer risk: much agent work is not naturally a binary forecast. The settling experiment is to pre-register claim-resolution rules for one narrow task family, collect at least enough cases for stable reliability diagrams, and compare Brier score, calibration error, and selective accuracy against raw pass rate.

### 1.4 ACH: force alternatives to compete, but do not confuse procedure with proof

Heuer's canonical ACH asks analysts to identify reasonable hypotheses, list evidence for and against them, construct a hypothesis-by-evidence matrix, assess evidence diagnosticity, reject hypotheses with the most inconsistent evidence, test sensitivity, report conclusions, and identify future indicators. Its key conceptual move is to ask which evidence discriminates among explanations instead of accumulating evidence merely consistent with a favored explanation ([CIA, Richards Heuer, *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*, ch. 8](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/9a5f1162fd0932c29bfed1c030edf4ae/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). ICD 203 requires plausible alternatives particularly under major uncertainty, complexity, or low-probability/high-impact conditions ([ODNI, *ICD 203*, p. 5](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

ACH solves an *auditability and search-direction* problem better than it solves a statistical inference problem. It records alternatives, contradictions, and discriminating observations. The controlled evidence does not support treating its matrix total as ground truth: Dhami et al. found inconsistent adherence, mixed debiasing results, and possible increases in inconsistency and error ([Dhami et al., 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3550)).

## 2. Structural transfer test

### 2.1 What genuinely matches

The transfer has five real structural correspondences.

| Intelligence setting | Agent-harness setting | Why the relation matters |
|---|---|---|
| A source has a reporting history | An agent configuration has a history on task families | Historical reliability is a prior, not a verdict on the next claim. |
| A report carries claim-specific evidence | An answer carries tool observations, citations, tests, or artifacts | Present evidence can dominate weak or strong source history. |
| Sources can share a hidden origin | Agents can share model, context, retrieval, tools, or copied intermediate text | Agreement is not independent corroboration. |
| Analysts distinguish facts, assumptions, and judgments | Agent outputs mix observations, inferences, plans, and guesses | Consumers need claim type and provenance before assigning weight. |
| Estimates remain uncertain after analysis | Agent answers can remain uncertain after tool use | Probability and foundation strength communicate different information. |

The source/report separation is **very likely (80-90%)** to transfer because these correspondences preserve the causal structure: historical error propensity and present evidence are different variables in both fields. The settling experiment is the held-out trust-decision study specified in the executive verdict, with ablations for source history alone, report evidence alone, and both axes.

### 2.2 Where the analogy breaks

1. **An agent is not merely a source.** It is simultaneously collector, analyst, writer, and sometimes executor. A self-authored statement that “two sources confirm this” is not independent evidence. Only harness-observed provenance, separate tool results, or independently produced artifacts can support corroboration.

2. **Agent identity is nonstationary.** A human-source grade assumes some continuity of access, competence, and motivation. An “agent” can change when model version, system prompt, tool permissions, context composition, temperature, dependency state, or task scaffold changes. A global name-level reputation would pool unlike systems.

3. **Many software outcomes admit stronger verification than intelligence assessments.** Compilation, a deterministic test, schema validation, and a reproduced failure can directly adjudicate parts of an output. Substituting a confidence label for an available executable check would be epistemically weaker.

4. **Many agent outputs are acts or artifacts, not propositions.** A patch, refactor, or UI design cannot be reduced to a single true/false claim. Its assertions can be scored; the artifact additionally needs task-specific acceptance tests.

5. **Correlated failure is unusually dense.** Two agents using the same base model and prompt may generate the same error without copying. Independence must include model lineage and shared context, not merely different process IDs or agent names.

6. **Outcome labels are selectively missing.** Easy tasks resolve quickly; strategic advice and plans often lack a clean outcome at evaluation time. Naïvely learning only from resolved cases creates selection bias when resolved and unresolved work differ.

7. **The agent can game self-reported confidence.** If confidence controls whether expensive verification runs, a system optimized on completion or latency has an incentive to sound certain. Analytical confidence therefore needs to be computed primarily from harness-observed evidence features, not accepted as self-attestation.

8. **ACH's candidate set is not naturally exhaustive.** Real software faults can have compositional causes, and an LLM generating both hypotheses and evidence ratings can omit the true cause from every column. The matrix cannot recover an absent hypothesis.

9. **Tests are evidence, not infallible truth.** A test suite may be incomplete, coupled to the implementation, flaky, or irrelevant to the user's real acceptance criterion. “All tests passed” increases report credibility only for the behaviors those tests exercise.

These disanalogies rule out a literal Admiralty digraph as the final trust decision and rule out agent self-grading. They do not defeat the narrower separation-of-concerns borrow.

## 3. Concrete harness translation

### Proposal 1 — Claim-and-evidence envelope — **UNVERIFIED** (settle with schema A/B replay)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Require consequential outputs to expose a small, permissive envelope. Do not force every sentence into a rigid fan-out schema. A minimal record is:

```json
{
  "claim": "The failure is caused by an expired credential",
  "claim_type": "diagnosis",
  "task_type": "runtime_diagnosis",
  "likelihood_band": "likely (55-80%)",
  "evidence": [
    {"kind": "tool_observation", "ref": "event-id", "supports": true},
    {"kind": "tool_observation", "ref": "event-id", "supports": false}
  ],
  "assumptions": ["the observed process used the displayed credential set"],
  "alternatives": ["network policy blocked the request"],
  "discriminator": "repeat with a newly issued credential on the same route",
  "impact_if_wrong": "medium"
}
```

The harness, not the agent, adds immutable provenance: agent fingerprint, model/prompt/tool versions, task identifier, evidence event hashes, shared-context lineage, time, and verification outcome. “Likelihood” describes the proposition. “Analytical confidence” is not accepted from the envelope; Proposal 2 derives it from evidence quality and task conditions.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to make unsupported assumptions and unsupported certainty easier to detect, because ICD 203's evidence/assumption/judgment distinction directly maps to the mixed content of agent answers. It is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; the settling schema A/B replay measures extraction success, schema-failure rate, token overhead, and whether reviewers catch more seeded unsupported claims than with free-form output.

What it catches that an undifferentiated answer misses:

- a confident causal claim supported only by correlation;
- a cited source that supports context but not the key conclusion;
- a recommendation whose linchpin assumption is unstated;
- “verified” language with no referent to a tool event or artifact;
- an answer that never states the plausible rival explanation or the observation that separates them.

### Proposal 2 — Versioned agent × task reliability ledger — **UNVERIFIED** (settle with prospective scored-task trial)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a post-task hook that appends resolution records to a flat, inspectable log and periodically emits a compact trust card. The identity key is not an agent display name. It is a fingerprint over at least:

`agent role + model/version + governing prompt/version + tool set/version + evaluation epoch`.

The task key begins coarse and is allowed to split only when held-out data demonstrates a stable difference. Initial families can be:

- factual retrieval;
- citation entailment;
- causal diagnosis;
- code generation/editing;
- code review/security review;
- planning/design;
- probabilistic forecasting;
- action execution/state change.

Each resolution record contains the pre-resolution claim, likelihood band if applicable, verifier identity/type, resolution criterion, outcome, evidence independence lineage, and whether the case was selected for verification. The trust card reports rather than hides sample size and missingness:

```text
agent-fingerprint: …
task-type: runtime_diagnosis
resolved n: 43 / emitted n: 117
exact/accepted: 30 / 43
abstained: 9 / 117
verification selection: risk-triggered
probability calibration: insufficient n
recent regime change: none observed
```

For binary probability claims, retain a Brier score and a reliability diagram. For artifacts, retain task-native outcomes rather than fabricating a probability score: build/test results for code, citation-entailment checks for research, reproduced cause/intervention for diagnosis, and explicit customer acceptance or later outcome for plans. Never merge unlike task types into a single league table.

Historical source reliability becomes a prior with visible uncertainty, not a permission bit. A low-sample agent is “untested,” not bad. A version change starts a new cohort while retaining the older record for comparison. Results from self-verification are recorded separately from independent verification.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to identify agents that are strong in one task family and weak in another, based on IARPA ACE's use of past performance/expertise in aggregation and the direct domain dependence of competence. It is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; the settling prospective trial records all eligible tasks before outcome selection and tests whether task-conditional cards predict held-out failures better than global agent identity, model identity, and a no-history baseline.

It catches:

- a generally successful agent whose security reviews have a poor false-negative history;
- a new or historically weak agent whose present claim is independently confirmed;
- a model/prompt upgrade that invalidates an old reputation;
- an agent that achieves high apparent accuracy by abstaining or by receiving easier tasks;
- an agent whose self-checks look strong but fail under independently authored tests;
- overconfident probability language despite an acceptable binary pass rate.

### Proposal 3 — Per-claim credibility and analytic-confidence derivation — **UNVERIFIED** (settle with blinded human/action prediction study)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Do not copy NATO's A–F/1–6 labels literally; “probably true” in a credibility scale risks being confused with claim probability. Preserve the two-axis logic with explicit names.

**Source reliability field**

- `relevant-history-strong`, `mixed`, `weak`, or `untested`;
- always accompanied by task type, resolved/total counts, evaluation epoch, and selection policy.

**Current-claim evidence field**

- `independently-adjudicated`: a task-appropriate independent check directly tests the claim;
- `independently-corroborated`: at least two provenance-independent observations support it;
- `single-direct-observation`: one harness-visible observation supports it;
- `reasoned-unverified`: internally plausible argument, no adjudicating observation;
- `contradicted`: a relevant observation conflicts;
- `cannot-judge`: evidence or provenance is insufficient.

**Analytical confidence field**

- `high`, `moderate`, or `low`, plus a mandatory reason statement;
- derived from information coverage/quality, independence, analytic method, unresolved contradictions, task volatility, and availability of a decisive check;
- never inferred from prose tone, token count, or self-declared confidence alone.

The output is a vector, not a scalar:

```text
relevant source history: strong (n=81, code-edit tasks, current epoch)
current claim evidence: reasoned-unverified
claim likelihood: very likely (80-90%)
analytic confidence: low — no runtime reproduction; one linchpin environment assumption
independence: 1 root evidence lineage
next discriminator: reproduce under the reported environment
```

This proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent at least some reputation substitution and confidence/likelihood confusion, because the exact distinctions are explicit requirements in both UK doctrine and ICD 203. It is **UNVERIFIED** as an action-routing aid; the settling study blinds reviewers to agent names, compares vector cards with single scores, and measures whether reviewers choose the correct verification/action policy on held-out resolved claims.

It catches the particularly dangerous combination “high historical reliability + weak present evidence.” It also preserves “weak history + independently adjudicated present result,” which a global reputation gate would wrongly suppress.

### Proposal 4 — Risk-triggered competing-explanation challenge — **UNVERIFIED** (settle with stratified randomized trial)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Implement a small challenge skill, not a universal ACH runtime. Trigger it only when at least one of these conditions holds:

- the proposed action is irreversible, external, security-sensitive, or expensive;
- a causal diagnosis controls the action and lacks direct adjudication;
- evidence lineages conflict;
- the answer is high-likelihood but low analytical confidence;
- a low-probability alternative has high impact;
- a verifier shares too much model/context provenance with the producer to count as independent.

The challenge asks for at most three items:

1. the strongest plausible rival explanation or failure mode;
2. the existing evidence most inconsistent with the favored explanation;
3. the cheapest discriminating observation or reversible test.

Only exceptionally consequential, ambiguous cases expand into a full hypothesis-by-evidence matrix. Matrix totals never become probabilities, and missing hypotheses remain an explicit limitation.

A lightweight alternative challenge is **likely (55-80%)** to catch confirmation-shaped diagnoses at acceptable cost, because it preserves ACH's discriminating-evidence move while avoiding most of its procedural burden. It is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling randomized trial compares no challenge, this three-question challenge, and canonical ACH on seeded and naturally resolved cases, measuring false acceptance, false rejection, latency, token/tool cost, hypothesis coverage, and user-correction rate.

This catches:

- “credential expired” when the same symptoms also fit network policy;
- a patch that makes the observed test pass while leaving the competing race-condition explanation untouched;
- two agreeing agents whose conclusions trace to the same retrieved document;
- a reviewer that lists supporting evidence but never names evidence against its favored conclusion.

### Proposal 5 — Decision policy uses risk and evidence, not prestige — **UNVERIFIED** (settle with shadow-mode routing experiment)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Use the trust vector to choose a verification action, not to produce an ornamental badge:

| Impact/reversibility | Current-claim evidence | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low and reversible | any non-contradicted grade | proceed; sample some cases for calibration |
| Medium | reasoned-unverified or cannot-judge | run the cheapest discriminator |
| High or irreversible | below independently adjudicated | require independent task-native verification and human authorization where policy already requires it |
| Any | contradicted | stop the dependent action; resolve the contradiction |

Historical reliability changes sampling intensity and which agent is routed a task; it never waives claim-level evidence for a consequential action. Preserve a random audit sample even for high-performing agents, both to detect regime shifts and to avoid a self-confirming routing loop in which favored agents receive all easy work and disfavored agents never generate fresh evidence.

This policy is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce both blind trust and wasteful universal review because it connects verification effort to consequence and present evidence. It is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling shadow-mode experiment logs what the policy would route, compares it with eventual outcomes and actual cost, and only then enables actions for a randomized low-risk slice.

## 4. End-to-end mechanism

The resulting flow is intentionally short:

1. **Before work:** fingerprint the agent configuration and assign a coarse task type.
2. **During work:** keep tool/artifact provenance as harness events; do not trust prose descriptions of those events.
3. **At answer:** request claim, evidence references, assumptions, likelihood band where meaningful, rival, discriminator, and impact.
4. **At evaluation:** attach task-conditional source history; derive current-claim evidence grade and analytical confidence; collapse common-origin evidence lineages.
5. **At decision:** route by impact, reversibility, contradiction, and present evidence. Use historical reliability only as a prior and sampling input.
6. **After resolution:** append the independently defined outcome, including unresolved/missing status; update the relevant cohort, not a global score.
7. **Periodically:** test calibration, selection bias, regime change, and task-taxonomy splits on held-out records.

The minimal data model is:

```text
AgentEpoch(agent_fingerprint, valid_from, lineage)
Task(task_id, task_type, risk, acceptance_criterion)
Claim(claim_id, task_id, proposition, type, likelihood_band, emitted_at)
Evidence(evidence_id, kind, immutable_ref, root_lineage, supports_or_conflicts)
Assessment(claim_id, source_history, report_credibility, analytic_confidence, reason)
Resolution(claim_id, criterion, verifier_lineage, outcome, resolved_at)
```

This is a flat event ledger plus derived summaries, not a knowledge graph or semantic memory system. It can be JSON Lines and a deterministic report script.

## 5. What present trust heuristics fail to see

The following are target failure classes, not claims that this specific local harness currently exhibits. Whether they occur locally is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is to label a stratified sample of past outputs using independent task-native checks and search for each pattern.

| Weak heuristic | Missed case | Proposed signal |
|---|---|---|
| “This agent is usually good” | one unsupported high-impact claim | current-claim evidence grade |
| “Two agents agree” | both inherit the same retrieval/model/context error | root-lineage independence |
| “The answer sounds certain” | rhetoric exceeds evidence | controlled likelihood plus derived confidence |
| “Tests pass” | tests do not cover the claimed behavior | claim-to-test adjudication relation |
| “Accuracy is 90%” | task mix is easy or resolved cases are selected | task-conditioned cohort and resolved/emitted denominator |
| “The new agent has no history” | its current output is directly verified | present evidence can override an untested prior |
| “The verifier approved it” | producer and verifier share failure lineage | verifier lineage and independent-test flag |
| “The diagnosis fits all observations” | observations fit several causes | rival explanation and discriminator |
| “Low confidence means low probability” | soundness of basis is confused with likelihood | separate probability and analytical-confidence fields |
| “No contradiction was reported” | contradicting evidence was discarded | explicit supporting/conflicting evidence references |

## 6. Calibration, scoring, and statistical cautions

### 6.1 Never publish one “trust score”

A single score would erase precisely what the borrow contributes. At minimum expose task type, evaluation epoch, resolved sample size, outcome-selection policy, current evidence grade, likelihood where meaningful, analytic confidence with reason, and independence lineage.

Ranking agents by one global score is **very likely (80-90%)** to be misleading when task distributions differ, based on the definition of the target problem itself (trust varies per agent and task type) and on the source doctrine's emphasis on access, expertise, and reporting history. The settling experiment is a held-out comparison of global, task-conditioned, and hierarchical models with calibration curves and decision-cost metrics.

### 6.2 Distinguish calibration, discrimination, accuracy, and coverage

- **Calibration:** among claims assigned a band, how often do they resolve true?
- **Discrimination:** does the system assign higher likelihood to true claims than false ones?
- **Accuracy/acceptance:** does the output satisfy a task-native criterion?
- **Coverage:** for what proportion of emitted outputs is there a valid resolution?
- **Selective accuracy:** how accurate is the system at each abstention/verification threshold?

Brier score combines aspects of probability error; it is not a complete description of trust. Report a reliability diagram and task-native error costs alongside it. High calibration can coexist with weak discrimination if an agent always predicts the base rate.

### 6.3 Independence is a graph property

Count root evidence lineages, not agent messages. Two reports are not independent if one copied the other, both depend on the same retrieved page, both use the same generated test, or both use the same model/context in a failure mode where that commonality matters. Independence is graded, not magically binary.

Automatically detecting all meaningful common-cause dependence is **unlikely (20-45%)** with metadata alone, because shared model priors and correlated training errors are partly latent. The settling experiment is a labeled set of corroboration pairs with known shared and independent origins, scored for precision/recall under provenance-only and provenance-plus-diverse-check policies.

### 6.4 Missing outcomes are data

Always retain emitted count, resolved count, time to resolution, verification-selection reason, and abstentions. Do not compare agents whose outcomes were selected differently without adjustment. Random audit sampling provides an empirical bridge across routing policies.

### 6.5 Use task-native adjudicators

Examples:

- retrieval: source existence, currency, and citation entailment;
- diagnosis: reproduce the failure and change the proposed causal factor while holding plausible rivals stable;
- code edit: compile, targeted tests, regression tests, and acceptance criteria;
- security review: seeded known findings plus independently confirmed natural findings, with false-negative accounting;
- action execution: externally observed state transition and idempotence/reversibility checks;
- plan/design: explicit forecasted milestones and later acceptance, not immediate stylistic review.

No adjudicator is universally privileged. A tool-produced observation can still be irrelevant or generated under the wrong environment.

## 7. Adversarial risks and failure modes of the borrow

### Risk A: false precision

Mapping seven verbal bands onto sparse agent samples can create a scientific-looking fiction. Use the owner's required bands in reports, but display counts and interval uncertainty around empirical calibration. Do not assign a narrow empirical rate from a handful of cases.

### Risk B: Goodharting

Agents performing cheap irrelevant checks in response to a credibility incentive is **likely (55-80%)**, because the proposed grade explicitly rewards observed evidence and therefore creates that optimization pressure. This is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling red-team experiment is described below. Evidence must be linked to the specific claim and classified as adjudicating, supporting, conflicting, or merely contextual.

Gaming pressure is **likely (55-80%)** once trust fields affect routing, based on the general incentive created by the mechanism rather than evidence from this harness. This is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling red-team experiment rewards agents for maximizing trust grade while hiding a seeded defect, then measures irrelevant-evidence inflation and false adjudication.

### Risk C: reputation lock-in

Routing more tasks to the current winner creates more evidence for that winner while starving alternatives. Version cohorts, uncertainty-aware summaries, exploration traffic, and random audits mitigate this feedback loop.

### Risk D: verifier monoculture

Adding a second agent of the same model is cheap but can create false assurance. Prefer orthogonal evidence: executable test, different retrieval origin, independent specification, or a differently generated test. Agent diversity is useful only when it changes relevant failure lineage.

### Risk E: taxonomy explosion

Overly fine task types produce tiny samples; overly broad types hide specialization. Begin coarse, predefine split criteria, and validate splits out of sample. Do not let every agent invent its own category.

### Risk F: ACH theater

An agent can populate a polished matrix after deciding its conclusion. The useful check is whether the rival predicts a different observable and whether that observable is actually sought. The matrix itself earns no confidence bonus.

### Risk G: replacing verification with narration

Confidence language and source grades are decision aids. Where direct verification is safe and cheap, run it. Intelligence tradecraft is designed for information that often cannot be directly tested; software work frequently offers a stronger option.

## 8. Anti-overfit gate

All five proposals pass the required gate:

- they ride output/config/hook/skill mechanisms;
- the state is an append-only flat record plus deterministic summaries;
- no new orchestration runtime is introduced;
- no vector database or knowledge graph is introduced;
- no durable-execution engine is introduced;
- no trained critic is introduced;
- no observability SaaS is introduced;
- no guardrail framework is introduced;
- routine work does not require multi-agent fan-out;
- expensive alternative analysis is risk-triggered.

The deliberate non-proposals are:

1. **No “trust daemon.”** A hook and report script are enough until measured throughput or consistency proves otherwise.
2. **No learned meta-critic.** That would create another opaque agent whose own calibration must be established.
3. **No vector/KG evidence memory.** Immutable references and lineage identifiers solve the narrow provenance problem without semantic retrieval infrastructure.
4. **No universal debate panel.** Shared-model correlation and cost make agreement a weak proxy unless evidence lineages differ.
5. **No literal Admiralty A1–F6 UI.** Preserve the conceptual axes with names meaningful to software tasks.
6. **No default full ACH.** Controlled evidence does not justify it, and many tasks admit direct tests.

## 9. Minimum viable experiment program

These experiments are themselves recommendations and therefore carry implementation tags.

### Experiment 1 — Historical shadow labeling — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Select a stratified sample by agent fingerprint, task type, risk, and apparent confidence. Independently label claim type, evidence lineage, present credibility, task-native outcome, and resolvability. Validate the detector on seeded known-present evidence references before interpreting absence. Use two mechanically independent signals before marking a claim contradicted or a provenance field absent: for example, transcript event plus artifact/test output, not two parses of the same transcript.

Success criterion: inter-rater agreement is adequate for the specific fields and at least one trust-vector component adds held-out predictive or decision value beyond agent identity. The value is **roughly even (45-55%)** before data, because historical outputs may lack pre-resolution claims and reliable outcome labels. The experiment settles this by measuring label coverage and agreement rather than imputing missing truth.

### Experiment 2 — Prospective low-risk instrumentation — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Collect the envelope and immutable provenance on low-risk tasks without changing routing. Report schema compliance, added tokens, latency, evidence-reference validity, resolution coverage, and agent/user correction rates.

Success criterion: the permissive envelope parses reliably and reviewers detect more seeded unsupported claims without a material increase in task failure. Benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, conditional on keeping the schema small; the experiment directly settles the conditional.

### Experiment 3 — Trust-vector ablation — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

On held-out resolved cases compare:

- no metadata;
- global agent history;
- task-conditioned history;
- present evidence grade;
- full vector;
- full vector without lineage collapsing.

Measure decision-weighted error, calibration, abstention, verification cost, and false assurance from correlated evidence. The full vector outperforming every simpler ablation is **roughly even (45-55%)**; additional fields can add noise. This experiment is the deciding test and permits deleting fields that do not earn their cost.

### Experiment 4 — Alternative-analysis randomized trial — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Randomize eligible high-uncertainty cases among no challenge, three-question rival/discriminator challenge, and canonical ACH. Stratify by diagnosis, research synthesis, code review, and planning. Seed some cases with a known rival and retain natural cases with later resolution.

Success criterion: lower decision-weighted error net of time/tool cost. The lightweight challenge beating canonical ACH is **likely (55-80%)**, based on ACH's mixed controlled evidence and the smaller method preserving its most diagnostic move. The trial settles it.

### Experiment 5 — Shadow routing and random audit — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Run the decision table in shadow mode. Compare predicted route, actual action, eventual outcome, and verification expense. Keep a randomized audit sample across all trust strata. Enable only a reversible low-risk stratum after predeclared thresholds are met.

Success criterion: lower expected decision loss at equal or lower verification cost, with no trust stratum becoming unobservable. Net benefit is **likely (55-80%)** if task-native outcomes are available; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** if most outcomes remain unresolved. The shadow phase measures that prerequisite.

## 10. Adoption and rejection criteria

Adopt the narrow borrow only if prospective evidence shows all of the following:

- present-claim evidence predicts outcome or reviewer action beyond agent reputation;
- task conditioning improves held-out calibration or decision cost;
- provenance collapsing reduces false corroboration;
- the envelope has acceptable parse/latency overhead;
- missing outcomes and selection policy remain visible;
- a risk-triggered challenge adds net value in at least one task family.

Reject or reduce it if:

- task-native direct checks dominate every metadata field;
- reviewers interpret confidence as probability despite presentation changes;
- historical reliability is unstable across versions or too sparse to use;
- evidence-lineage detection has unacceptable false-independence rates;
- the envelope encourages irrelevant tool theater;
- the lightweight challenge does not outperform no challenge;
- operational cost exceeds decision-weighted error reduction.

Full replacement with an intelligence-style assessment workflow is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** justified by the present evidence, because the disanalogies are fundamental and direct executable verification is often stronger. A narrow augmentation is **likely (55-80%)** justified enough to test, based on multiple primary doctrines independently converging on the same separations and on forecasting research demonstrating the value of precommitted, scored judgments. Both claims remain conditional on the experiments above.
