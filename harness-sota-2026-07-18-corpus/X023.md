# X023 — Cross-domain borrow: intelligence tradecraft → cost control

## Executive judgment

The useful borrow is **not ACH-as-a-matrix** and not “make every agent perform intelligence analysis.” The useful mechanism is **diagnostic collection management**: define the decision, retain plausible competing answers, identify the next observation whose possible outcomes would discriminate among those answers, discount it for source quality and dependence, and collect it only when its expected decision impact warrants its cost. In intelligence, this is how analysts avoid spending scarce, risky collection capacity on more evidence that merely agrees with the leading story. In an agent harness, it becomes a small, task-local **answer-change budget gate**: spend extra searches, model calls, context, branches, reviewers, and verification runs on unresolved facts that can change the answer or its required confidence—not on generic “more thinking.”

My transfer judgment is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the structural match between costly intelligence collection under uncertainty and costly agent tool/model calls under uncertainty, plus direct evidence that input-adaptive LM compute allocation can preserve measured quality while reducing computation. The wide band is deliberate: the particular harness mechanism proposed here is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the logged shadow-mode randomized evaluation specified below**, and full ACH has failed to improve probabilistic coherence in at least one controlled experiment.

The analogy transfers only for **epistemic work** (research, diagnosis, choice among plausible explanations, and verification). It does not transfer reliably to open-ended construction, taste, negotiation, or tasks where another tool call creates the artifact rather than reveals evidence. A harness must apply an eligibility gate, not a universal ritual.

## 1. What intelligence tradecraft has already solved

### 1.1 Analysis of Competing Hypotheses: diagnosticity, not evidence volume

Richards Heuer’s *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis* defines evidence as diagnostic to the extent that it changes the **relative likelihood of alternative hypotheses**. Evidence consistent with all alternatives can have no diagnostic value even if it looks strongly supportive in isolation. His ACH procedure asks analysts to enumerate alternatives, place evidence against each, remove non-diagnostic evidence, seek disconfirmation, test sensitivity to critical evidence, and identify future milestones that would indicate a different course. See the CIA text, especially the definition and fever/illness example on PDF pp. 72–73 and the eight-step outline on pp. 123–124: [CIA, *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/9a5f1162fd0932c29bfed1c030edf4ae/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf).

The key move is relational. “This source supports answer A” is not enough. The analyst asks whether that same observation was also expected under B and C. If so, collecting ten more versions of it adds confidence theater rather than discrimination. Conversely, a cheap observation that is expected under A but surprising under B may be worth much more than a long general report.

The current U.S. Intelligence Community analytic standard preserves the same logic without mandating an ACH worksheet. ICD 203 requires products to explain source quality, uncertainty and its causes, linchpin assumptions, plausible alternatives, and indicators that would alter uncertainty or the likelihood of alternatives: [ODNI, ICD 203, §§4(e)(1)–(4), PDF pp. 3–5](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf). This is important: the durable mechanism is an explicit relationship among evidence, assumptions, alternatives, and answer-changing indicators—not a particular matrix UI.

### 1.2 Collection is chosen for answer change, with cost and risk in the denominator

Intelligence collection is not free. It consumes scarce assets, time, access, and sometimes human safety. A value-of-information treatment in an intelligence-analysis setting therefore defines information as valuable when it can change the analysis output, explicitly warns that merely reducing uncertainty around the leading hypothesis can reinforce confirmation bias, and argues that collection value must be balanced against cost and risk: [Robinson, “Value of Information for Argumentation based Intelligence Analysis,” §§1 and 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08180).

That paper is research rather than binding doctrine, and its formal argumentation model assumes independent evidence in places where real reporting may not be independent. It is nevertheless concrete evidence that the **cost-aware answer-change formulation exists inside the source field**, rather than being retrofitted from generic economics.

### 1.3 Source reliability is separate from claim credibility

UK joint intelligence doctrine distinguishes the reliability of a source from the credibility of a particular report. It warns about circular reporting, requires contradictory information to be analyzed rather than discarded, and notes that reliable sources can be wrong while credible information can come from usually unreliable sources. Its NATO/“Admiralty Code” expresses those two dimensions separately: [UK MOD, JDP 2-00 (4th ed.), §§3.39–3.40, PDF pp. 67–69](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653a4b0780884d0013f71bb0/JDP_2_00_Ed_4_web.pdf).

This separation matters to cost control. Three web pages syndicating one press release are not three mechanically independent confirmations. Two model branches using the same model, prompt, context, and retrieved snippet are not two independent analytic signals. A harness that counts outputs but ignores provenance can pay three times for one evidentiary lineage.

### 1.4 Sherman Kent bands communicate uncertainty; they do not themselves allocate compute

Sherman Kent’s “words of estimative probability” addressed the finding that the same phrase could communicate radically different odds to different readers and argued for a consistent vocabulary: [CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, Sherman Kent, “Words of Estimative Probability”](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-8-no-4/words-of-estimative-probability/) and its [archival PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf). ICD 203 now supplies probability ranges and explicitly distinguishes likelihood from confidence in the evidentiary basis: [ODNI, ICD 203, §4(e)(2), PDF p. 4](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf).

The transfer is modest but useful: the harness needs coarse, shared bands to state the current answer and an independently stated evidence-confidence assessment. It should not confuse “A is likely” with “we have high confidence in that likelihood,” and should spend compute on the latter only when better evidence could matter. Bands are a communication and stopping interface, not a magic calibration mechanism.

## 2. Concrete translation to an agent harness

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: Decision-Diagnostic Compute Gate — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

**UNVERIFIED premise:** a cheap first-pass model can identify useful competing answers and answer-changing probes often enough that the gate’s own cost is lower than the compute it saves. **Settling experiment:** replay a stratified task set in shadow mode, then randomize eligible live tasks between fixed budgets and the gate; measure task success, owner corrections, total model/tool cost, latency, and missed escalations as specified in §5.

This should be a task-local skill plus a thin pre-escalation hook/state record, not a second orchestration runtime. It rides the existing model/tool/hook bus and stores only a small per-task JSON record. No daemon, vector database, trained critic, durable workflow engine, or observability service is needed.

### 2.1 Eligibility gate

Run the mechanism only when all of the following are true:

1. The task contains an outcome-sensitive factual or causal judgment: research, diagnosis, root cause, recommendation among alternatives, safety check, or verification.
2. At least two materially different answers or failure explanations are plausible, or a single answer depends on a fragile assumption.
3. An additional observation/tool action could discriminate between them.
4. The marginal action is optional; mandatory tests, user-required sources, security checks, and acceptance criteria may not be skipped merely because the gate predicts low value.

Skip it for direct transformations, formatting, deterministic file operations, tiny factual lookups with an authoritative source, and creative tasks whose quality cannot be represented as competing factual answers. This applicability classifier is **likely (55-80%)** to be more important than fine-grained scoring, because ACH’s logic requires plausible alternatives and diagnostic evidence; Heuer explicitly notes that diagnosticity cannot be evaluated without a set of alternatives ([CIA, pp. 72–73](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/9a5f1162fd0932c29bfed1c030edf4ae/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The exact classifier performance is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is manual labeling of 300 stratified historical tasks followed by precision/recall measurement**.

### 2.2 Cheap framing pass: a five-field uncertainty ledger

Before an eligible task requests an expensive escalation, record:

```text
decision: what output choice could change?
alternatives: H1..H4 (including “requirements misunderstood” when plausible)
current_judgment: leading answer + Sherman-Kent likelihood band
evidence_confidence: low / medium / high, stated separately
linchpins: assumptions or evidence whose reversal changes the answer
```

Cap alternatives at four by default. This is not an exhaustive ontology; it is enough structure to expose whether the next action is discriminating. An “other/unknown” alternative prevents premature closure. The four-hypothesis cap is **roughly even (45-55%)** to be the best default; evidence supports keeping multiple alternatives in view, but not this exact number. **UNVERIFIED; settle it by an ablation across caps 2, 4, and 6 measuring framing cost, alternative recall, and final correctness.**

Do not force precise probabilities. Use the owner-required bands for likelihood and a separate confidence label tied to evidence quality. Numerical likelihood ratios produced by an LLM would look scientific without calibration data.

### 2.3 Candidate probe cards

For each plausible next action—another search, opening a primary document, running a focused test, reading a specific file, asking the user one question, sampling another solution, invoking a stronger model, or requesting an independent review—the agent emits a compact card:

```text
probe: concrete action
outcomes: 2–4 anticipated result classes
discriminates: which alternatives each outcome favors/disfavors
flip: can any plausible outcome change the answer, required confidence, or chosen action?
lineage: source/model/context lineage; independent, partially dependent, or duplicate
quality: expected source reliability and claim credibility, separately
cost: low / medium / high tokens, quota, latency, and external risk
```

Rank probes lexicographically rather than with invented decimals:

1. mandatory acceptance/safety checks;
2. plausible answer-flipping probes;
3. confidence-threshold-crossing probes required by task consequence;
4. among ties, higher independence and source quality;
5. among remaining ties, lower cost.

This is a qualitative approximation of expected value of information. It deliberately avoids a learned router and a probabilistic argument graph. The answer-flip criterion follows the intelligence paper’s recommendation to value information that changes the output rather than merely reinforces the current favorite ([Robinson, §§1, 5, 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08180)).

### 2.4 Execute one probe, update, and stop

Execute the top probe, update the ledger, then re-rank. Stop optional collection when any of these holds:

- no plausible probe outcome changes the answer or crosses the confidence threshold required by consequence;
- the next probe is a duplicate lineage and cannot resolve a known contradiction;
- marginal expected impact is lower than cost;
- a task-specific hard cap is reached, in which case report the unresolved gap rather than silently upgrading confidence.

Escalate budget when a cheap probe reveals a new plausible alternative, contradicts a linchpin, exposes low source quality/circular reporting, or leaves a high-consequence choice below its required evidence-confidence threshold.

The stop rule is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce “comfort collection”—additional calls that repeat the same support—because it makes the agent name an answer-changing outcome before spending. **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to label post-hoc tool calls as answer-changing, confidence-changing, mandatory, or redundant and compare redundant-call share between policies.**

### 2.5 Consequence sets the confidence threshold, not task length

Use a small policy table:

| Consequence if wrong | Minimum evidence posture before acting | Typical escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Reversible/local | One credible line or deterministic check; disclose uncertainty | Cheap model and one focused tool call |
| Material but recoverable | Independent corroboration or executable verification | Primary source/test plus one different-lineage check |
| Destructive, security-sensitive, legal/financial/medical, or externally consequential | Mandatory domain checks and explicit user/authority gates override compute savings | Stronger model, authoritative sources, deterministic validation, human approval where required |

This table allocates compute to consequence and reversibility, not merely perceived “difficulty.” The exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; settle them by risk-stratified replay and review of false-stop severity**, and they must never weaken existing safety or authorization rules.

## 3. What this catches that uniform budgeting misses

1. **Confirmatory search spirals.** The agent already favors H1, repeatedly queries H1-shaped terms, and receives more H1-compatible pages. The gate asks whether the proposed search could plausibly favor H2; if not, it is low diagnosticity.

2. **Syndication mistaken for corroboration.** Several pages repeat one press release or dataset. Source/claim separation and lineage marking prevent paying for fake independence, following JDP 2-00’s warning about circular reporting ([§3.39(b), PDF p. 68](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653a4b0780884d0013f71bb0/JDP_2_00_Ed_4_web.pdf)).

3. **False multi-agent consensus.** Three samples share a model, prompt, retrieved context, and failure mode. Their agreement is dependent evidence. The gate favors a different modality—test execution, primary document, or differently conditioned review—when it can discriminate.

4. **High-confidence but fragile answers.** A fluent answer rests on one linchpin assumption. Sensitivity analysis identifies a cheap probe that can overturn it even if the model’s verbal confidence is high.

5. **Low-confidence but irrelevant escalation.** The model feels uncertain, but all plausible answers imply the same user action. More compute will not change the decision, so the harness can disclose uncertainty and stop.

6. **Expensive verification of non-critical details.** A detail is uncertain but cannot change acceptance, recommendation, or safety posture. It is recorded as a gap instead of consuming quota.

7. **Uniform “strong model for hard-looking task” routing.** Difficulty alone does not say whether more computation is useful. ICLR 2025 work predicts reward as a function of input and budget and allocates compute where useful; across its programming, mathematics, and dialog suite, it reports up to 50% computation reduction at no quality loss: [Damani et al., “Learning How Hard to Think,” ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ff414825df833edb8b1839e3d5d495e9-Abstract-Conference.html). Generalization from those benchmarks to a heterogeneous owner harness is **likely (55-80%)**, not certain; task mix and tool costs differ. The proposed experiment tests this locally without assuming the reported effect size.

8. **Retry loops with no new information.** Repeating a failed call with unchanged parameters and lineage has no new diagnostic outcome. A retry must change the hypothesized failure cause, input, tool, or verification channel—or stop with the gap.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 Full ACH is not empirically earned

A controlled study with 227 participants and uncertain evidence found no coherence advantage for ACH over a no-technique control; both groups were subadditive, unreliable, and overly conservative, and the ACH group showed pseudo-diagnostic weighting: [Karvetski & Mandel, 2020, *Judgment and Decision Making*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/coherence-of-probability-judgments-from-uncertain-evidence-does-ach-help/903C3CE8556F3EC7DE80BBC040DBB18B). A Defence Research and Development Canada critique likewise says SATs lack sustained scientific validation and that decomposition may add rather than reduce noise: [Chang et al., 2017, Government of Canada catalogue and paper](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.852936/publication.html).

Therefore it is **unlikely (20-45%)** that importing mandatory ACH matrices wholesale would improve a general agent harness, based on the negative controlled result, weak validation base, and overhead of decomposing every task. The recommendation intentionally imports only diagnostic collection questions and sensitivity, then demands an experiment.

### 4.2 Intelligence collection observes; agent compute often constructs

An intelligence sensor usually tries to reveal a world state that exists independently of the collection act. In coding, writing, planning, and design, another model call may create a better artifact rather than reveal which hypothesis was already true. “Would this observation flip the answer?” is ill-posed when the action itself expands the solution space. The transfer is therefore **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be useful for purely generative steps, based on this causal disanalogy. Such steps should use acceptance criteria, tests, or user taste—not pseudo-ACH.

### 4.3 Hypotheses may be non-exclusive, incomplete, or reframed mid-task

Root causes can coexist; requirements can change; a tool result can reveal a new objective. ACH’s tidy columns invite premature closure. The harness must allow “multiple causes” and “other/unknown,” and any surprising probe reopens framing. It must never interpret the leading of four generated hypotheses as an exhaustive truth set.

### 4.4 Agent self-reports are not calibrated sensors

An LLM can produce polished alternatives, confidence labels, and probe scores without those labels tracking reality. Sherman-Kent vocabulary standardizes communication; it does not calibrate the speaker. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that ungated self-reported confidence alone would be an unsafe budget controller, based on ICD 203’s insistence that confidence be grounded in logic and evidentiary quality and on ACH’s negative probabilistic-coherence result. External outcomes—tests, owner corrections, authoritative-source agreement, and task success—must calibrate the policy.

### 4.5 “Independent” calls frequently share hidden causes

Two vendors may train on overlapping data; two sites may copy an undisclosed upstream source; two agents may inherit the same prompt assumption. Lineage labels can detect obvious dependence but not hidden dependence. Claims of independence should therefore be “different known lineage,” never “independent” without evidence.

### 4.6 Collection adversaries and agent environments differ

Intelligence doctrine assumes denial, deception, access constraints, and risk to assets. Some agent research shares adversarial sources; many local coding tasks do not. Conversely, agent actions can mutate the environment, making observations stale. The analogy supports provenance and contradiction handling, but it does not justify importing secrecy classifications, military collection bureaucracy, or Admiralty alphanumeric grades wholesale.

## 5. Required experiment before enforcement

### Phase A — shadow mode (no routing effect)

1. Sample at least 300 tasks stratified by research, diagnosis, coding/debugging, direct transformation, recommendation, and high-consequence advice.
2. Log the eligibility decision, ledger, candidate probe cards, chosen probe, predicted answer-change class, cost, and stop point while the existing harness proceeds normally.
3. Have blinded reviewers label whether each extra call changed the final answer, materially changed justified confidence, satisfied a mandatory criterion, or was redundant. Use executable tests/owner corrections where available rather than model judgment alone.
4. Validate the detector with known-positive controls: seeded tasks containing a decisive contradictory source, circularly syndicated sources, and an irrelevant uncertainty. A second mechanically independent signal is the actual downstream answer delta or test outcome. Do not claim the detector works merely because it emitted cards.

### Phase B — randomized policy evaluation

Randomize eligible tasks among fixed current budget, simple difficulty routing, and the diagnostic gate. Pre-register:

- primary utility: task success/acceptance and severe-error rate;
- cost: tokens, model dollars/quota, tool calls, wall time, and user interruptions;
- epistemic outcomes: owner corrections, missed answer flips, unresolved gaps disclosed, and primary-source use;
- policy behavior: false stops, useless escalations, duplicate-lineage calls, and gate overhead;
- risk strata: reversible versus consequential tasks.

Adopt only if the upper confidence bound on severe-quality regression remains within a pre-agreed non-inferiority margin and cost falls materially. “Up to 50%” from ICLR 2025 is a research result on its task suite, not this harness’s expected saving. A local 10–20% reduction with no quality loss is **likely (55-80%)** given the structural prevalence of heterogeneous task difficulty shown by adaptive-compute research, but this numerical expectation is **UNVERIFIED; the randomized experiment settles it**.

### Phase C — calibrate, do not train a critic

Use a small table of observed success by task class, confidence band, and selected escalation—not a trained critic. Retire probe patterns that do not predict answer changes. Re-evaluate quarterly or after major model/tool changes because routing value is non-stationary. This remains CONFIG plus BUILD-SMALL; if accurate allocation requires a learned router or persistent service, stop and reassess rather than quietly crossing into BUILD-LARGE.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

The proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate:

- It rides the existing skill/hook/model/tool bus.
- It is task-local and disposable.
- It adds a compact record and a pre-escalation decision, not a new orchestration runtime.
- It requires no vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- Its value is measurable through ordinary task outcomes and cost logs.

Rejected alternatives:

1. **Full ACH matrix for every task — rejected.** The applicability assumptions fail on generative work, controlled evidence does not show a probabilistic-coherence benefit, and ritual overhead would itself consume compute.
2. **Probabilistic argument graph / Bayesian network — rejected.** Dependencies are hard to elicit, LLM probabilities are uncalibrated, and the structure trends toward a second reasoning runtime. Robinson’s paper explicitly notes an independence assumption that may not hold in real analysis ([§5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08180)).
3. **Learned budget critic/router — rejected for first build.** It would require training data, drift management, and its own evaluation surface. The cheap ordinal gate can establish whether the signal exists before any larger build.
4. **Uniform confidence threshold — rejected.** Consequence, reversibility, mandatory verification, and source quality matter; verbal confidence alone is not an allocation objective.
5. **Count-source corroboration — rejected.** It rewards syndication and shared failure modes. Known lineage and claim-level credibility are the relevant dimensions.
6. **Always use more test-time compute on “hard” tasks — rejected.** Difficulty and marginal usefulness are different. A hard task may be unsolvable with available tools; an easy-looking task may contain one decisive hidden assumption.

## 7. Minimal implementation contract

If Phase A validates the signal, the implementation contract should remain small:

```json
{
  "eligible": true,
  "decision": "string",
  "alternatives": ["H1", "H2", "other/unknown"],
  "judgment_band": "likely",
  "evidence_confidence": "low",
  "linchpins": ["string"],
  "next_probe": {
    "action": "string",
    "can_flip": true,
    "lineage": "different-known-lineage",
    "source_reliability": "unknown|weak|mixed|strong",
    "claim_credibility": "unknown|weak|mixed|strong",
    "cost": "low|medium|high"
  },
  "stop_reason": null
}
```

The hook permits the call, requests one reframing pass, or stops optional escalation. It may not block mandatory safety/acceptance checks. It should fail open for ordinary reversible work and fail to the existing safety rule—not to an invented budget rule—for consequential operations. These failure semantics are **likely (55-80%)** to keep the mechanism from becoming a brittle guardrail, based on its advisory purpose and the asymmetric cost of suppressing mandatory verification; exact behavior remains **UNVERIFIED until fault-injection tests cover malformed state, model refusal, timeout, and hook absence**.

## 8. Bottom line

Intel tradecraft contributes a strong question to cost control: **“What is the cheapest credible, differently sourced observation whose possible result could change the decision?”** That question transfers. The ACH worksheet, military grading codes, and implied numerical precision do not.

The best next build is therefore a **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** shadow-mode diagnostic gate, followed by a randomized non-inferiority/cost experiment. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce redundant compute without degrading eligible-task quality, based on the diagnostic-collection structure and adaptive-compute evidence, but the recommendation remains **UNVERIFIED until that experiment demonstrates both savings and no unacceptable severe-error increase**. If it does not, stop: retain the vocabulary for post-hoc analysis and do not force the analogy into runtime policy.
