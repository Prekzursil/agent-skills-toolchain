# X086 — cross-domain borrow: mechanism design → cross-session memory

**Unit:** X086  
**Source field:** mechanism design (incentive compatibility, proper scoring rules, Goodhart effects)  
**Target:** what an agent should remember across sessions, when it should forget, and how it should recognize staleness  
**Research boundary:** ideation lens only. I did not inspect or characterize the local harness, corpus, repository, or audit findings. That restraint is deliberate because the task assigns local grounding to other units. Harness-specific statements are therefore marked **UNVERIFIED** inline.

## Executive judgment

The borrow is **partial, not wholesale**.

The specific mechanism worth transferring is a **precommitted strictly proper scoring rule over resolvable memory-validity forecasts**, supplemented by randomized resolution of memories the harness would otherwise never check. In its native field, a strictly proper scoring rule makes a forecaster's expected score uniquely highest when the reported probability equals the forecaster's actual probability distribution. Gneiting and Raftery give the formal definition and show that the quadratic/Brier score is strictly proper ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007, pp. 359–363](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)).

The full incentive-compatibility claim does **not** transfer automatically. A language model is not thereby an economic agent maximizing a persistent score; many memory outcomes never resolve; retrieval determines which outcomes become visible; and a remembered claim can change subsequent actions and therefore its own observed outcome. Research on performative prediction proves that, when a prediction influences the event, even strictly proper scores can induce reports that do not equal the forecaster's post-report beliefs ([Oesterheld et al. 2023, pp. 1564–1566](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a/oesterheld23a.pdf)). Research on selective labels likewise shows that a decision policy can determine which outcomes are observed at all ([Wei 2021, pp. 1–2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wei21a/wei21a.pdf)).

My transfer verdict is **very likely (80-90%)**, because (1) strict propriety is mathematically established for exogenous, eventually observed events; (2) the two most important violations in memory—performative outcomes and selective observation—are independently established failure modes; and (3) recent memory benchmarks explicitly find failures in state revision and forgetting rather than mere recall. The remaining uncertainty is empirical: whether a small ledger improves this owner's real task outcomes has not been tested.

The recommendation is therefore not “pay the model for confidence.” It is: **turn each volatile memory into a dated, falsifiable forecast; resolve a statistically meaningful sample; use proper scores to calibrate memory classes; and make retrieval decisions from expected error costs rather than one Goodhartable importance number.** The score governs the harness's calibration policy, not the model's status or reward.

## 1. What mechanism design has actually solved

### 1.1 The principal's problem

A principal wants information held privately by a forecaster. The forecaster believes that a binary event has probability `q`, but can report any `p`. Merely asking for “confidence” does not make `p = q`: a forecaster may exaggerate, hedge, or optimize whatever downstream treatment is attached to the report. Mechanism design changes the payoff rule so truthful revelation is optimal under stated assumptions.

For a realized binary outcome `y ∈ {0,1}`, take the negatively oriented Brier loss:

```text
L(p, y) = (p - y)^2
```

If the forecaster's belief is `q`, expected loss is:

```text
E[L] = q(1-p)^2 + (1-q)p^2
     = (p-q)^2 + q(1-q)
```

The second term does not depend on `p`; the first is uniquely minimized at `p=q`. Equivalently, `S = -L` is uniquely maximized by truthful reporting. This is not a metaphor. It is the incentive-compatibility property for a risk-neutral expected-score maximizer. Gneiting and Raftery define a proper score as one whose expected score is maximized by reporting the believed distribution and a strictly proper score as one where that maximizer is unique; they identify the quadratic/Brier score as strictly proper ([definition and equation (1)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf), pp. 359–361; [quadratic example](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf), p. 363).

### 1.2 What the theorem requires

The useful guarantee has a narrow contract:

1. **A definite event space.** “Still useful” is not enough. The event must be operationally resolvable, such as “the user's stated preference is unchanged when next directly queried” or “the referenced repository default still equals X at the first task after date H.”
2. **An eventual outcome.** Without `y`, there is no proper score. A proxy label is not automatically equivalent. Work on proper proxy scoring rules explicitly starts from this missing-resolution problem and needs assumptions about an appropriate proxy ([Witkowski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v31i1.10590)).
3. **Outcome exogeneity.** The report must not materially alter the distribution of `y`, or the standard proof elicits something other than the original belief. Oesterheld et al. show this failure formally for performative predictions ([Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a/oesterheld23a.pdf)).
4. **Expected-score optimization under the specified payoff.** Human risk preferences, side stakes, competition, and hedging can disturb reports. A forecasting competition that rewards only the winner, for example, can induce exaggerated probabilities even when the underlying score is proper ([Witkowski et al. 2023, *Management Science*](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4410)).
5. **Precommitment.** The reported probability and resolution rule must be frozen before the outcome. Retrospectively assigning confidence after seeing a contradiction is not elicitation; it is relabeling.

Thus proper scoring rules solve **truthful probabilistic belief elicitation under explicit conditions**. They do not solve truth discovery, causal attribution, preference aggregation, or the definition of a valuable memory.

### 1.3 Goodhart's law supplies the negative design

Goodhart's original observation was about statistical regularities collapsing when used for control. Manheim and Garrabrant sharpen “Goodhart effects” into four mechanisms: regressional (selecting noise in an imperfect proxy), extremal (leaving the regime where the relationship held), causal (intervention breaks the proxy-goal relation), and adversarial (other agents respond strategically) ([Manheim & Garrabrant 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Their taxonomy matters more here than the popular slogan.

Applied to memory:

- **Regressional:** selecting the highest LLM-rated “importance” memories also selects rating noise.
- **Extremal:** optimizing retrieval frequency can produce a small set of endlessly resurfaced memories, a regime unlike the sample on which relevance was judged.
- **Causal:** retrieving a memory changes the answer and the future conversation; later apparent “usefulness” is partly caused by having retrieved it.
- **Adversarial:** prompt injection or even ordinary user phrasing can manufacture claims shaped to the admission metric.

This yields a hard design rule: **no single scalar may simultaneously decide admission, truth, currentness, retrieval, and deletion.** That rule is a reasoned translation of the taxonomy, not a theorem about this harness. Its benefit here is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the documented distinct Goodhart mechanisms and on the separable semantics of those memory decisions; **UNVERIFIED — settle with an ablation comparing one-score ranking against the separated-state design on the same replay corpus.**

## 2. Does the analogy structurally transfer?

### 2.1 Correspondence table

| Mechanism-design object | Memory analogue | Transfer quality |
|---|---|---|
| Principal seeking a belief | Harness deciding whether to rely on a stored claim | Strong: both need uncertainty, not just a point assertion. |
| Forecaster's private probability `q` | Model's latent estimate that a claim remains valid through horizon `H` | Weak-to-moderate: an LLM can emit `p`, but “latent belief” may not be coherent or stable. |
| Report `p` | `valid_through` probability recorded at write time | Strong as a data representation; weak as truthful revelation. |
| Exogenous event `y` | Later primary-source check or explicit user correction | Strong only for memories with a defined resolver. |
| Payment/utility | Harness policy updated from historical calibration | Different: this trains or configures the institution, not necessarily the current model's incentives. |
| Proper score | Brier loss on resolved memory-validity forecasts | Strong mathematically once `p`, `y`, and exogeneity exist. |
| Repeated forecaster reputation | Calibration table by memory class/source/model/version | Moderate: enough samples are required and distributions can shift. |

### 2.2 The decisive disanalogy: no demonstrated utility channel

Mechanism design gets truthful reports by arranging consequences the agent prefers. A one-shot prompted LLM may not have a persistent utility over future Brier scores. Whether the subject harness has a real optimization channel connecting later memory scores to write-time reports is **UNVERIFIED — settle with two mechanically independent signals: (a) static trace from the memory-write output through any score/reward/config update, with a known-present wired control; and (b) a randomized intervention where only the future scoring consequence changes and reported probabilities measurably respond.** I therefore make no claim that such a channel is absent or present.

Without that channel, “proper” describes the loss function, not the model's behavior. The narrow transfer still works because the ledger can evaluate and calibrate a *system* even when it cannot incentivize the individual generation. For example, if claims labeled “user preference / 90% / 180 days” resolve true only 52% of the time, the harness can shorten horizons or require confirmation for that class. This is empirical calibration, not truthful revelation.

### 2.3 The second disanalogy: memory creates selective labels

A memory that is retrieved may be checked, contradicted, or acted upon. A memory suppressed or forgotten often produces no outcome label. This is structurally the same observation problem Wei describes: accepted loans reveal repayment; rejected loans do not ([Wei 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wei21a/wei21a.pdf)). Scoring only retrieved memories would make frequently retrieved classes look “well measured” and leave discarded classes unknowable. Worse, a bad memory that is never retrieved produces no recorded harm and could look harmless.

Randomized audit is therefore not optional if the scores are used for policy learning. A small, budgeted sample of non-retrieved and low-confidence memories must be resolved independently of the retrieval policy. The claim that such audits materially reduce selection bias in this application is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the exact selective-label correspondence, but the needed sampling rate and resolver cost are **UNVERIFIED — settle with an offline fully labeled benchmark where audit-sampled estimates are compared with known population calibration at several sampling rates.**

### 2.4 The third disanalogy: the forecast can be performative

Suppose memory says “the user prefers concise answers.” Retrieving it causes concise answers; the user's subsequent acceptance may then be treated as confirmation. The report changed the action that generated the “evidence.” Standard properness does not rescue that loop. Oesterheld et al. prove that performatively optimal reports under strictly proper scores generally need not be fixed points reflecting post-report beliefs ([Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a/oesterheld23a.pdf)).

Therefore only **independent resolution events** should receive a proper score: direct user correction/confirmation not solicited with a leading premise, authoritative source reread, test result, repository state check, or externally observed task outcome with a predeclared resolver. “The agent reused it without complaint” is not a valid truth label.

### 2.5 Verdict

The **incentive mechanism does not transfer as an incentive mechanism** unless a real utility channel and exogenous resolution exist. The **proper-scoring institution transfers as a calibration and audit mechanism** for the subset of memories that can be converted into falsifiable, time-bounded claims.

This qualified transfer is stronger than a forced analogy because it identifies the boundary: score resolved forecasts; do not score vague importance; do not interpret use as truth; do not treat unobserved outcomes as successes; do not claim elicited confidence is honest merely because the formula is proper.

## 3. Concrete harness translation

## Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Memory Futures Ledger** — **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build one append-only JSONL ledger and thin adapters on the existing memory write/read/correction bus. Do **not** build another orchestrator, daemon, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability service, or guardrail framework.

The `<~200 lines` estimate is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the deliberately narrow operations below (schema validation, append, scoring, calibration lookup, routing); **UNVERIFIED — settle by implementing a throwaway spike against the actual hook interfaces and counting non-test source lines.** If integration needs a resident scheduler or generalized retrieval engine, this proposal fails the BUILD-SMALL gate and should be rejected rather than silently promoted to BUILD-LARGE.

### 3.1 Write-time contract

Store an atomic claim, not a prose summary:

```json
{
  "memory_id": "m_...",
  "claim": "User currently prefers concise status updates",
  "claim_type": "user_preference",
  "scope": "status_updates",
  "observed_at": "2026-07-19T00:00:00Z",
  "source_ref": "session/event pointer, not copied prose",
  "evidence_kind": "explicit_user_statement",
  "validity_horizon": "2026-10-19T00:00:00Z",
  "p_valid_at_horizon": 0.70,
  "resolver": "later unprompted explicit statement or neutral confirmation",
  "supersedes": [],
  "state": "active",
  "decision_stakes": "low"
}
```

Three fields must remain separate:

- **Evidence:** how well the source supported the claim when written.
- **Currency forecast:** probability the claim remains valid at a named horizon.
- **Decision stakes:** harm if the harness acts on a false current claim.

Relevance is query-dependent and should be computed at read time. It is not a durable truth attribute. Access count is telemetry, not evidence. Passage of time lowers a currency forecast only through a class-specific calibrated hazard; it does not retroactively make the historical observation false.

The exact schema and which claim types deserve persistence are **UNVERIFIED — settle with blinded annotation of real candidate memories by two independent raters, measuring atomicity, resolver agreement, and later task value.** The proposal's expected benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, because recent benchmarks explicitly expose failures to revise changed state and to distinguish remembering from forgetting, but no owner-specific replay has yet tested this schema. STALE evaluates state resolution, resistance to stale premises, and policy adaptation, with its best evaluated model at 55.2% overall ([STALE, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527)); Memora argues that existing benchmarks overemphasize static retrieval and adds frequent knowledge updates and forgetting ([Memora, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20006)). These are recent preprints, not settled production evidence.

### 3.2 Admission: what to remember

Admission should be a typed gate, not “importance ≥ 0.8.” Persist only when the candidate is at least one of:

1. an explicit owner preference, constraint, identity fact, or standing authorization with provenance;
2. a task commitment or unresolved obligation with an external completion condition;
3. a reusable environment fact whose next rediscovery cost exceeds its verification cost;
4. a diagnosed failure lesson with a reproducible trigger and remediation;
5. a relationship among state variables needed to invalidate dependent claims later.

Reject or keep session-local:

- transient chain-of-thought or speculative rationales;
- copied source content that can be fetched reliably from its authority;
- vague evaluations without a resolver (“this library is good”);
- duplicates that add no evidence or scope;
- secrets or personal data outside an explicit retention policy;
- claims whose future correctness cannot be distinguished from non-use.

The admission categories are **likely (55-80%)** to reduce context pollution while retaining high-value state, based on the separation of resolvable claims from vague proxies; **UNVERIFIED — settle with a counterfactual replay measuring task success, correction rate, tokens, and re-discovery time with and without each category.**

### 3.3 Forecast: replace generic confidence with a validity curve

A single timeless `confidence: 0.9` conflates evidence and persistence. Ask for probability at one or more standard horizons appropriate to a type:

```text
P(valid now | evidence)
P(valid in 7 days)
P(valid in 30 days)
P(valid in 180 days)
```

Use class-level empirical calibration when enough resolved observations exist. Until then, use conservative priors and show them as priors, not learned facts. Stable historical observations (“the user said X on date D”) and volatile state claims (“the user currently prefers X”) must be different records: the former can remain true forever while the latter expires.

Multi-horizon forecasts are **likely (55-80%)** to distinguish durable facts from volatile state better than one timestamp, because validity is horizon-dependent by definition; **UNVERIFIED — settle with calibration error and decision-loss comparisons among fixed TTL, one-horizon, and multi-horizon variants on a temporally labeled replay.**

### 3.4 Resolve and score

When a predeclared resolver fires, append—not overwrite—a resolution:

```json
{
  "memory_id": "m_...",
  "resolved_at": "2026-08-01T00:00:00Z",
  "horizon": "2026-08-01T00:00:00Z",
  "outcome_valid": 0,
  "resolver_evidence": "new explicit owner statement pointer",
  "brier_loss": 0.49,
  "cause": "superseded",
  "independent_of_retrieval": true
}
```

Aggregate Brier loss and reliability diagrams by `claim_type × evidence_kind × horizon bucket × model/version`. Do not rank individual agents publicly or pay only the “winner”; competition can distort proper-score reports ([Witkowski et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4410)). Use the aggregates to adjust default priors, verification frequency, and the maximum horizon allowed for a class.

Sample a small fraction of non-retrieved memories for neutral resolution. Record the inclusion probability so estimates can be weighted. Do not silently label unresolved items as valid, invalid, or useless.

The ledger will produce useful calibration estimates only if enough independent outcomes accrue. Adequate sample size is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a power analysis after measuring base rates and intra-class correlation; until then report confidence intervals and merge no classes solely to improve headline scores.**

### 3.5 Read-time policy: when to trust, verify, quarantine, or omit

Use a small decision table rather than a universal decay score:

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Current calibrated `p_valid` high; low consequence if false | Retrieve with date and provenance. |
| Expected harm `(1-p_valid) × cost_wrong` exceeds verification cost | Verify from the resolver before use. |
| Contradiction or newer superseding claim | Quarantine old current-state assertion; retain as dated history. |
| Horizon passed; resolver available | Mark “stale-unverified” and refresh before consequential use. |
| Horizon passed; no resolver; low omission cost | Suppress from default retrieval, retain in audit history. |
| High stakes and unresolved | Ask the owner or authoritative source; never launder the memory into fact. |

This policy separates **forgetting from deletion**. “Forget” means remove from default action context; append-only historical provenance remains available for audit and temporal questions. Hard deletion remains a privacy/retention operation, not a prediction-score outcome.

Expected-loss routing is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform a fixed confidence threshold when error costs differ across tasks, because the same probability rationally warrants different actions at different stakes; **UNVERIFIED — settle with cost-weighted errors in a replay containing both low- and high-consequence decisions.**

### 3.6 How the harness notices staleness

Staleness is not “old.” It is evidence that a claim no longer safely represents current state. The ledger should emit a stale candidate through three independent routes:

1. **Clock route:** a declared horizon has passed without revalidation.
2. **Evidence route:** a newer atomic claim contradicts or supersedes the old one.
3. **Dependency route:** a changed parent state invalidates downstream assumptions even without textual negation.

Route 3 targets the “implicit conflict” identified by STALE: later evidence can invalidate an earlier memory without explicitly negating it, and systems may retrieve updated evidence yet fail to act on it ([STALE, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527)). For example, “owner moved from Windows to Linux” can invalidate remembered shell instructions and path assumptions even if no one says “the old path is false.”

Automatic dependency propagation is the riskiest part. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a small, typed `depends_on` relation yields net benefit without generating false invalidations, because STALE supplies evidence for the failure but not for this exact minimal implementation; **UNVERIFIED — settle with a blinded benchmark of implicit conflicts, measuring stale recall, false quarantine, and downstream action accuracy.** Start with explicit, shallow dependencies only; do not build a knowledge graph.

## 4. What this catches that ordinary memory metrics miss

These are target failure classes, not claims about the owner's harness:

1. **Confident but expired current-state claims.** A dated observation can remain historically true while its current-state projection becomes untrusted.
2. **Implicit invalidation.** A parent state change can quarantine dependent instructions even without lexical contradiction.
3. **Premise laundering.** A user's question may presuppose stale state; retrieval can surface the newer state and resist the premise. STALE calls this “Premise Resistance” ([STALE, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527)).
4. **False confirmation by reuse.** Mere retrieval or lack of complaint is excluded as a truth label.
5. **Immortal unverifiable summaries.** Admission requires a source, horizon, and resolver or explicitly labels the item historical/unresolvable.
6. **Calibration drift by memory class.** A 90% claim class resolving near 55% becomes measurable rather than an anecdotal correction.
7. **Selective-label blindness.** Random audits estimate outcomes for memories the retrieval policy suppresses.
8. **High-confidence/low-stakes confusion.** Truth probability is not used as a proxy for decision importance.
9. **Deletion of useful history.** Superseded current claims leave the active context but remain dated audit records.

Whether the present owner harness currently misses any particular class is **UNVERIFIED — settle with two mechanically independent signals for each alleged absence: (a) a detector validated on a seeded known-present stale-memory case, then run on recorded sessions; and (b) behavioral replay showing the harness acts on the stale premise despite newer evidence.** I make no harness-specific absence claim from this ideation unit.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: how this proposal can fail

### 5.1 The model games the score

If scores affect model selection, prompt status, or reward, the model may report probabilities near the base rate, choose easy-to-resolve memories, or avoid writing volatile but valuable claims. That is Goodhart's regressional/adversarial pressure. The safe response is not a more elaborate critic: keep admission coverage and calibration as separate metrics, score a randomized eligible set, and never reward only the top score.

Gaming is **unlikely (20-45%)** if the score is used only offline to calibrate class priors and the current model cannot influence inclusion or resolution; it becomes **likely (55-80%)** if score directly controls model reward or status. Evidence is the incentive distinction in proper-scoring theory and the documented distortion from winner-take-all forecasting competitions; **UNVERIFIED — settle with a red-team experiment varying score visibility and consequence while measuring admission rate, forecast extremity, and Brier decomposition.**

### 5.2 The resolver is not ground truth

Owner statements may be context-dependent; repository state may change between observation and check; automated tests can be flaky; two memories may define different scopes. A precise score against a bad resolver gives false certainty.

Resolver error causing material miscalibration is **likely (55-80%)** for loosely scoped natural-language memories, based on the need for a definite event space in scoring-rule theory; **UNVERIFIED — settle by dual independent resolution on a stratified sample and adjudicate disagreements without showing raters the forecast.** Store resolver type and disagreement rate.

### 5.3 The forecast changes the world

Retrieving a behavioral preference can cause the interaction later treated as confirmation. This is causal Goodhart and performative prediction. Exclude endogenous labels from proper-score claims, mark them separately, and retain the retrieval/exposure log for causal analysis. Do not pretend a correction factor restores strict propriety.

### 5.4 Random audits waste owner attention

Directly asking the owner to reconfirm trivial memories can be more harmful than stale memory. Audits should prefer machine-checkable authoritative sources, batch low-priority questions, and spend owner attention only where expected harm warrants it.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that unrestricted owner-facing audits would be annoying, based on the direct interaction cost; **UNVERIFIED — settle with a capped pilot measuring interruption count, response rate, and owner-rated value.** A zero-owner-interruption shadow phase should precede any prompts.

### 5.5 Distribution shift destroys calibration

Calibration by model/version, claim type, and horizon may not transfer after a model upgrade, workflow change, or new domain. Treat prior calibration as versioned evidence, not a permanent constant. Use wide intervals and back off hierarchically.

Material post-upgrade drift is **likely (55-80%)**, because the forecaster and candidate distribution change; **UNVERIFIED — settle with pre/post-upgrade reliability curves on the same frozen replay plus live shadow outcomes.**

### 5.6 Scoring encourages overproduction of testable trivia

Easily resolved memories may crowd out important but hard-to-resolve commitments. This is extremal Goodhart: the measurable subset becomes the target. Keep coverage/value audits distinct from accuracy scores; cap memory volume; randomly inspect rejected candidates; never equate “has a Brier score” with “worth remembering.”

This failure is **likely (55-80%)** if admission is optimized for score volume and **unlikely (20-45%)** if admission is independently sampled and capped; evidence comes from the Goodhart taxonomy, while the application-specific rates are **UNVERIFIED — settle with an incentive-ablation replay comparing scored admission against fixed eligibility sampling.**

## 6. Rejected cross-domain borrows and dead ends

### 6.1 Prediction market / token economy — rejected

A market needs multiple agents with differentiated information, budgets, settlement, and a reason prices aggregate information. Spawning model personas does not create independent information; correlated hallucinations can become consensus. A token economy would also risk a second orchestration runtime and adds allocation gaming. It fails the anti-overfit gate.

It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that an internal multi-agent prediction market would beat the proposed single ledger per unit of complexity here, based on missing independent private signals and the added runtime; **UNVERIFIED — only a controlled comparison with genuinely heterogeneous information sources could settle it.** No build is proposed.

### 6.2 VCG/auction for context-window slots — rejected

Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanisms elicit private valuations under quasi-linear utility and allocate scarce goods using payments/externalities. Memory snippets do not possess stable private valuations or money; their value is complementary and query-dependent. Assigning each memory a bid merely renames an LLM relevance score and invites Goodhart pressure. The structural assumptions do not transfer. No build is proposed.

### 6.3 Peer prediction / agreement rewards — rejected

When truth is unavailable, peer-prediction families reward reports using other reports under assumptions about signal structure. In an agent harness, model instances often share training, prompt, retrieved evidence, and failure modes. Agreement can reward correlated error. Proper proxy scoring itself requires an appropriate proxy and states weaker/conditional properties than ordinary resolved scoring ([Witkowski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v31i1.10590)). No build is proposed.

### 6.4 Universal exponential time decay — rejected

Time does not make “the owner said X on date D” false, while a volatile current-state claim can become false immediately after writing. A universal decay curve conflates historical truth, current validity, and retrieval relevance. Typed horizons and observed hazard calibration are narrower and falsifiable. No build is proposed.

### 6.5 Delete low-Brier or low-use memories — rejected

A Brier score evaluates forecasts, not the underlying claim's future value. Low access is selectively produced by retrieval policy. Deleting on either signal destroys the audit trail and makes later temporal questions impossible. Demotion/quarantine is the reversible control; deletion belongs to privacy and retention policy. No build is proposed.

### 6.6 Train a critic to police memory — rejected

A trained critic is explicitly excluded by the anti-overfit gate and would introduce its own calibration, drift, and reward-hacking surface. The ledger needs resolvers and sampling, not another opaque judge. No build is proposed.

Adopt the borrow only as a **small, reversible measurement institution**:

- represent volatile memories as dated forecasts with provenance, horizon, probability, resolver, scope, and stakes;
- resolve outcomes independently when possible;
- score with Brier loss and calibrate by class/horizon/version;
- audit a randomized sample outside the retrieval policy;
- distinguish historical truth, current validity, query relevance, and action stakes;
