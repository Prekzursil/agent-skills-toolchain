# X002 — Cross-domain borrow: clinical medicine → use-vs-mention guards

## Executive judgment

The clinical-medicine analogy transfers, but only in a constrained form. The useful borrow is **not** “have a diagnostic model decide whether dangerous text is dangerous.” It is medicine's separation of (1) a real workflow act, such as an order or administration, from (2) a statement *about* an act, followed by explicit assertion context—negation, temporality, experiencer, intent, and evidential source—before a contraindication rule is eligible to fire. HL7 FHIR makes the first separation concrete: `MedicationRequest`, `MedicationDispense`, `MedicationAdministration`, and `MedicationStatement` represent different workflow meanings; a statement is explicitly not an order, while an administration records actual consumption ([HL7 FHIR MedicationRequest, “Boundaries and Relationships”](https://hl7.org/fhir/medicationrequest.html#11.1.2); [HL7 FHIR MedicationStatement, “Boundaries and Relationships”](https://hl7.org/fhir/medicationstatement.html#11.4.2)). Clinical NLP's ConText mechanism makes the second separation concrete: a concept mention is assigned contextual properties such as affirmed/negated, recent/historical/hypothetical, and patient/other experiencer from scoped cues, rather than being treated as an affirmed current patient condition merely because the words occur ([Harkema et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2757457/)).

**Transfer judgment: very likely (80-90%)** that making *native action provenance* a prerequisite for enforcement, with assertion-context classification only as a fallback, will eliminate most of the stated class of document-description false fires. Evidence: the target defect is defined as payload-text matching across five detectors with 12+ false fires; in the source field, both the workflow standard and evaluated NLP mechanism explicitly distinguish action/event semantics from mere mentions. This remains **UNVERIFIED in this harness; a replay of the 12+ known false-fire fixtures plus matched true-use fixtures through old and proposed gates would settle it**.

**Important limit: likely (55-80%)** that a text-only ConText clone by itself would merely move the errors. Evidence: ConText's own authors report variation across document types, sparse evidence for non-patient experiencers, and that fully resolving historical versus recent conditions requires knowledge beyond surface clues ([Harkema et al., 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1532046409000744)). The right transfer is therefore “typed workflow event first; scoped language context second,” not “add a smarter regex.”

## Scope and evidence discipline

This is the requested ideation lens. I did not inspect or characterize the local harness, its detector implementations, or its repository. The only target-side premise used is the unit brief's measured statement: five guards match payload text rather than semantic role and have produced at least 12 false fires. Consequently, this memo does not claim which hooks expose which fields, what language the existing guards use, or how many lines a patch would take in this particular tree.

External evidence was selected for mechanically different purposes:

1. HL7 FHIR is a workflow/data-model standard showing how medicine distinguishes an order, a report, dispensing, and actual administration.
2. ConText is an evaluated clinical NLP mechanism showing how medicine qualifies the semantic status of a concept mention.
3. Clinical decision-support studies show the iatrogenic cost of nonspecific alerts and the value of contextual risk modifiers.
4. Evidence-based medicine's absolute-effect measures motivate a harness evaluation metric based on interventions and burdens, not an attractive relative percentage alone.

These are independent signals about the source field; none is offered as empirical proof of performance in this harness.

## What clinical medicine has actually solved

### 1. It does not equate a medication name with a medication act

Clinical systems model a medication across distinct stages and epistemic strengths. FHIR describes:

- `MedicationRequest`: an order for supply and instructions;
- `MedicationDispense`: provision of a supply;
- `MedicationAdministration`: actual consumption or administration;
- `MedicationStatement`: a report or belief that use occurred, possibly based on a patient, caregiver, clinician, or other record.

FHIR explicitly says a `MedicationStatement` is not part of the prescribe → dispense → administer sequence and is not used to request or order medication. It also notes that a statement is less specific than administration and may lack exact time, quantity, or product ([HL7 FHIR MedicationStatement](https://hl7.org/fhir/medicationstatement.html)). The standard even allows a `MedicationRequest` to report an external order for informational purposes that is “not authoritative and ... not expected to be acted upon,” illustrating that resource type alone is insufficient without status/intent/provenance ([HL7 FHIR MedicationRequest](https://hl7.org/fhir/medicationrequest.html)). `MedicationRequest.intent` is mandatory and distinguishes proposal, plan, order, and related forms; `doNotPerform` reverses the operational meaning ([HL7 FHIR R5 MedicationRequest](https://hl7.org/fhir/medicationrequest.html); [HL7 FHIR R4 definitions](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/medicationrequest-definitions.html)).

This is a direct answer to use-versus-mention: clinical informatics does not ask “does the payload contain *warfarin*?” It asks something closer to “is this an active order, for this patient, with this intent and status, at this workflow point?” The token is a candidate concept; the typed event is the enforcement substrate.

### 2. It represents assertion context instead of treating every mention as current truth

Clinical notes routinely contain phrases such as “no pneumonia,” “history of pneumonia,” “if pneumonia develops,” and “mother had pneumonia.” A raw keyword detector maps all four to the same condition. ConText instead starts with a detected clinical concept and uses pre/post triggers, pseudo-triggers, termination terms, and bounded scope to assign:

- negation: affirmed versus negated;
- temporality: recent, historical, or hypothetical;
- experiencer: patient versus someone else.

The mechanism is intentionally small and legible. Trigger terms change a default property only when the concept falls within the trigger's scope; pseudo-triggers prevent phrases that look like modifiers but are not; termination terms stop scope propagation. The original study evaluated 2,377 annotated development conditions and multiple report types. In its scope experiment, end-of-sentence scope with termination terms reached F1 0.98 for negation, 0.78 for historical status, and 0.96 for hypothetical status; test-set performance varied materially by report type ([Harkema et al., 2009, Tables 7 and 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2757457/)). Earlier emergency-department evaluation reported 97% recall and precision for negation, 67.4% recall/74.2% precision for historical status, 82.5% recall/94.3% precision for hypothetical status, and only eight non-patient-experiencer cases, making the last estimate unstable ([ACL BioNLP 2007 paper](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1011.pdf)).

The lesson is not that lexical context is perfect. It is that **mention detection and assertion classification are separate stages**, with explicit defaults, scopes, exceptions, and error measurement.

### 3. Contraindication decision support combines a candidate exposure with risk modifiers

Medication alerts are not most useful when they fire on drug-name co-occurrence. They become more specific when order parameters and patient characteristics modify risk. A proof-of-concept study of 15 drug-drug interactions identified 33 risk-reducing modifiers across 14 interactions; 67% were order parameters such as route and dose, with other modifiers including comorbidities and laboratory results. The authors also found poor agreement among drug value sets, warning that even the concept registry itself can be inconsistent ([Wang et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35136935/)). A 2024 interrupted time-series study replaced a broad fixed seven-day interaction window with interaction-specific screening intervals; alerts fell from 9.8% to 6.3%, avoiding an estimated 201 false-positive alerts per day without measured loss of sensitivity ([Seidling et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39347841/)).

The operational pattern is:

```text
candidate exposure
  AND eligible workflow state
  AND relevant subject
  AND temporally overlapping exposure/state
  AND no risk-reducing or cancelling modifier
  => alert at the decision point
```

That is much closer to a semantic eligibility predicate than a content filter.

### 4. Medicine treats bad alerts as an iatrogenic harm, not as free safety

An alert can cause harm indirectly: interruption, habituation, inappropriate cancellation, and reduced attention to later high-value warnings. A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of 16 studies estimated that 90% of drug-drug interaction alerts were overridden (95% CI 85-95%), with very high heterogeneity ([Felisberto et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38899788/)). In a before/after EHR conversion, switching from a highly tailored system to a general one multiplied interruptive alert burden by six; acceptance of the most severe alerts fell from 100% to 8.4%. Disabling the least severe alerts halved burden and increased acceptance of top-tier alerts ([McGreevey et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29766382/)). An expert panel separately identified 33 low-priority interaction classes that did not warrant interruptive alerts; at one institution they represented 36% of displayed interactions ([Phansalkar et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23011124/)).

Those results do not prove identical human behavior in an agent harness. They establish the source-field principle that alert specificity and interruption policy are safety properties, not cosmetic UX.

### 5. Differential diagnosis preserves alternatives until evidence changes their weight

The transferable part of differential diagnosis is modest: detection of a sign creates candidates, not a final diagnosis. A mention of a dangerous operation should create a candidate for classification, not immediately establish active agent intent. The analogy becomes misleading if carried further: clinical differentials reason about latent biological causes, while a harness often has direct structural evidence of the event that is about to execute. Where a tool-call event and argument schema exist, inferring action intent from prose is inferior to reading that native event.

### 6. NNT/NNH forces evaluation in absolute units

Evidence-based medicine reports absolute risk reduction and its inverse, number needed to treat (NNT), alongside harm. BMJ defines `NNT = 1 / absolute risk reduction` and `NNH = 1 / absolute risk increase` ([BMJ Best Practice, “How to calculate risk”](https://bestpractice.bmj.com/info/toolkit/learn-ebm/how-to-calculate-risk/)); the Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine likewise defines NNT as the inverse of ARR ([CEBM NNT guide](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/resources/ebm-tools/number-needed-to-treat-nnt)). The conceptual transfer is to report concrete counts—true hazardous actions stopped, benign actions interrupted, and owner overrides—not merely “false positives reduced by 80%.”

## Concrete translation to an agent harness

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: semantic-role eligibility gate before the five guards

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The premise that the existing hook bus exposes enough event metadata for all five guards is **UNVERIFIED; inspecting hook event schemas and replaying one known tool call, one user message, one tool result, and one document read through a diagnostic hook would settle it**. If adequate provenance is already exposed, the change can remain a small shared predicate plus fixtures. If not, only the guards attached to structured events should adopt it first; this memo does not justify a new daemon.

Create one shared `semantic_role(event)` normalizer used before content-specific rules. Its minimum output should be:

```text
channel:
  user_instruction | assistant_plan | tool_invocation | tool_result |
  file_content | fetched_content | policy_content | metadata | unknown

act_status:
  executing | requested | proposed | quoted | reported | negated |
  historical | hypothetical | unknown

actor:
  agent | owner | third_party | unknown

authority:
  executable | advisory | inert | unknown

provenance:
  native_event | structured_field | text_inference

target/effect:
  normalized operation and resolved object when the hook supplies them
```

Then require every enforcement rule to declare an eligibility tuple before its existing concept/target logic:

```text
rule = {
  eligible_channels: [tool_invocation],
  eligible_statuses: [executing],
  eligible_actors: [agent],
  minimum_authority: executable,
  unknown_policy: block | confirm | audit | ignore,
  effect_predicate: ...,
  severity: ...,
  response: ...
}
```

The decisive rule is: **no interruptive action solely because a dangerous string appears in an inert document, fetched page, tool output, quoted example, policy text, or historical report.** Such content can still be scanned by separate content-security rules whose protected outcome is data handling rather than action prevention. This separation matters: “the document contains a credential” and “the agent is about to transmit a credential” are different predicates, just as a medication statement and administration are different clinical events.

### Classification order

1. **Prefer native event type.** A tool invocation is an action candidate; a file-read result is a mention container. Do not ask an NLP classifier to rediscover information already encoded by the event bus.
2. **Read structured modifiers.** Tool name, action type, resolved target, dry-run flag, write/read mode, and approval state outrank free text.
3. **Apply assertion-context rules only inside ambiguous text-bearing fields.** Detect quotation/code-example boundaries, negation, hypothetical language, historical/reporting verbs, and third-party attribution. Use scoped triggers, pseudo-triggers, and termination boundaries in the ConText style.
4. **Retain an explicit `unknown`.** Do not silently coerce unknown context to affirmed execution.
5. **Choose unknown behavior by expected harm.** A potentially irreversible high-severity action can request confirmation; a low-severity unknown can log or continue. Unknown is not synonymous with safe or dangerous.

**very likely (80-90%)** this ordering is more robust than running semantic classification over the whole payload, because native event provenance is direct evidence of workflow role while prose classification is inferential; FHIR's explicit workflow distinctions and ConText's cross-document variability support that judgment. It is **UNVERIFIED; a blinded replay stratified by channel would settle it**.

### Scoped text fallback, not a universal “intent model”

For a field that truly mixes instruction and quotation, borrow ConText's mechanics narrowly:

- triggers for active intent: imperative/request syntax tied to the agent and current turn;
- negation/cancellation triggers: “do not,” “never execute,” “cancel”;
- hypothetical triggers: “if,” “suppose,” “could,” “example”;
- historical/reporting triggers: “previously ran,” “the log says,” “the document describes”;
- quotation/code-fence/document-region markers;
- pseudo-triggers for phrases that resemble action modifiers but do not change role;
- termination markers at sentence, field, message-part, XML/JSON boundary, and quote/fence end;
- actor/experiencer attribution: owner request, agent plan, quoted third party, document author.

Do not make this a generative-model call on every hook. A deterministic classifier over structured message parts and a small scope table is inspectable, cheap, and compatible with the anti-overfit gate. An optional model may label ambiguous replay cases offline, but should not become the enforcement authority without calibration data.

**likely (55-80%)** that a deterministic scoped classifier will handle the known mention patterns if they are ordinary quotation, negation, examples, or historical description. Evidence: ConText achieved strong negation/hypothetical performance using this mechanism, but historical and experiencer performance was weaker and domain-sensitive. This is **UNVERIFIED; annotate the 12+ false fires by modifier class and run leave-one-pattern-out tests to settle it**.

### Separate indication from contraindication

Clinical prescribing asks both “is there a reason to act?” and “is there a reason not to?” The harness equivalent is:

```text
indication: a native event says the agent is attempting an effect now
contraindication: the normalized effect violates a safety rule
modifier: role/status/provenance makes the apparent effect inert or uncertain
```

A keyword in a document supplies, at most, a candidate contraindicated concept. It does not supply the indication that enforcement is needed. Requiring both halves prevents the detector from treating its own policy documentation as an attempted violation.

### What this would catch or distinguish that payload matching misses

Subject to the event schema, the gate is designed to distinguish:

- an actual destructive shell invocation from a Markdown example containing the same command;
- a current outbound network/tool action from a fetched page describing that endpoint;
- an agent's proposed action from a historical transcript reporting that someone performed it;
- an affirmative request from “do not run X”;
- the current actor's intended operation from a third-party quotation;
- a file write target from file content that happens to name the same path;
- a real credential transmission event from a document that merely contains a credential-like string;
- an unresolved high-risk action from an inert mention, by preserving `unknown` and applying severity-specific behavior.

**very likely (80-90%)** the first six distinctions can be made exactly when native hook fields separate invocation arguments from surrounding content; the evidence is structural, not linguistic. This is **UNVERIFIED; schema inspection plus one fixture per row would settle it**. **roughly even (45-55%)** that credential transmission can always be resolved before the action, because values may be assembled dynamically and hooks may expose only partial arguments; **UNVERIFIED, and replaying templated, environment-derived, and encoded values through the outbound hook would settle it**.

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: contraindication registry with explicit context modifiers

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

If guards already read declarative rule data, extend each entry with eligibility and modifier fields rather than embedding one-off text exclusions. If they do not, this proposal merges into the preceding BUILD-SMALL normalizer; a second rule engine is not justified.

Each registry entry should name:

- protected effect (delete, overwrite, transmit, install, mutate remote state, etc.);
- eligible native events and argument fields;
- current-actor/current-time requirements;
- cancelling contexts;
- target-resolution requirement;
- severity and reversibility;
- behavior for unknown role;
- interruptive versus advisory response;
- fixture IDs proving true-positive and true-negative behavior;
- owner override semantics and audit reason.

This translates the medication-alert finding that order route, dose, time window, comorbidity, and labs modify the applicability of a nominal interaction. It also prevents a “contraindication registry” from degenerating into a larger bag of strings.

**likely (55-80%)** declarative modifiers will reduce rule drift across five detectors because shared fields make omissions visible in review. This is **UNVERIFIED; diff the five rule shapes, implement two representative entries, and measure duplicate logic and fixture coverage to settle it**.

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: staged intervention and absolute-effect scorecard

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Deploy by severity and evidence, not all-or-nothing:

1. replay only;
2. shadow classification with no behavioral change;
3. advisory output for uncertain/low-severity cases;
4. confirmation for high-severity unknowns;
5. hard block only for high-confidence, high-severity, active events.

For every rule and semantic-role class, report a 2×2 confusion matrix against reviewed fixtures and natural frequencies per 1,000 eligible events:

```text
true hazardous acts blocked
hazardous acts allowed
benign acts interrupted
benign acts allowed
owner overrides
unknown-role events
median and p95 added latency
```

Add two deliberately plain ratios:

- **number needed to interrupt (NNI)** = all interruptive firings / true hazardous acts correctly interrupted;
- **number harmed by interruption (NHI)** = benign interruptive firings / all interruptive firings.

These are harness analogues, not established clinical metrics, and must not be labeled NNT/NNH. Their purpose is the same communication discipline: expose absolute benefit and burden. Report them alongside severity because preventing one catastrophic action may justify more friction than preventing one reversible edit.

**very likely (80-90%)** this scorecard will reveal detectors whose impressive relative improvement hides poor base-rate performance; evidence is the mathematics of absolute versus relative risk and clinical alert studies with very high override rates. Whether it changes owner decisions is **UNVERIFIED; compare configuration decisions made from percentages alone versus the full natural-frequency table to settle it**.

## Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### Where the structure genuinely matches

1. **A surface token is not an event.** “Warfarin” in family history is not current patient exposure; a command in a policy document is not an agent invocation.
2. **Context is multidimensional.** Polarity alone is inadequate. Current versus historical, actor, authority, and workflow stage all change meaning.
3. **False alerts create downstream risk.** Clinical evidence shows alert burden and lower acceptance can move together. In a harness, false blocks consume attention and may encourage bypasses; the latter harness-side consequence is **likely (55-80%) but UNVERIFIED; measure override/bypass behavior before and after precision changes to settle it**.
4. **Timing matters.** A contraindication is useful at ordering/administration, not whenever a term appears. A guard is useful at the effectful hook, not while reading an inert document.
5. **Exceptions should be typed modifiers.** Dose/route/labs in medicine map to tool/event/target/reversibility in the harness.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **Clinical state is latent; tool intent is often observable.** A clinician infers disease from noisy signs. A harness may receive a typed `tool_invocation` immediately before execution. Therefore differential-diagnosis machinery is unnecessary when direct provenance exists.
2. **Clinical language is comparatively bounded.** Agent payloads contain arbitrary programming languages, nested quotations, encoded values, patches, generated code, logs, and prompt-injection content. ConText's token-window rules cannot reliably parse all of these.
3. **The “experiencer” mapping is richer.** Clinical ConText mostly distinguishes patient from other. Harnesses need owner, agent, tool, document author, quoted speaker, remote server, and possibly nested delegates. The 2007 evaluation had only eight non-patient-experiencer cases, so it is weak evidence for multi-actor attribution.
4. **Adversarial pressure differs.** Clinical prose is not usually written to evade a contraindication engine. Tool payloads or retrieved content may be adversarial. A lexical fallback must never downgrade a native effectful event merely because its text says “this is only an example.”
5. **False-negative costs are heterogeneous and sometimes catastrophic.** Suppressing an alert on a document mention is easy; suppressing it on an ambiguous executable payload may be unsafe. Unknown-role behavior must depend on severity and reversibility.
6. **FHIR is a representation standard, not proof that implementations are correct.** Its clean resource distinctions demonstrate a design mechanism, not universal clinical success.

These disanalogies change the design materially. Native provenance must dominate text. Textual assertion status can suppress a candidate only when the surrounding event is already non-authoritative or when structured evidence corroborates it. It must not be an attacker-controlled escape hatch on an effectful tool call.

## Rejected borrows and dead ends

### Rejected: full Bayesian differential diagnosis runtime

This would require priors, calibrated likelihoods, and a maintained model for each action class. The harness usually has a direct event signal, so the latent-disease assumption does not transfer. It would add complexity where a typed predicate suffices.

### Rejected: LLM judge on every guard firing

An LLM could understand some quotations better, but it introduces latency, nondeterminism, prompt-injection surface, and a second policy authority. No cited source establishes calibrated reliability for this harness. Offline adjudication of ambiguous fixtures may be useful; online veto authority is not justified.

### Rejected: allowlist of document phrases

Adding exemptions such as “example,” “documentation,” or code fences copies only ConText's trigger vocabulary without its scope, pseudo-trigger, actor, and workflow semantics. It is easy to evade and likely to produce both false suppression and continuing false fires.

### Rejected: global “ignore content inside code fences”

Commands meant for execution are frequently presented in code fences. Formatting is evidence of a quoted region, not proof of inertness. The enclosing channel and native event decide authority.

### Rejected: a second orchestration or policy runtime

The anti-overfit gate rules out another daemon/service/runtime absent exceptional evidence. Nothing in the clinical analogy supplies that evidence. Both proposed mechanisms ride the existing hook/skill/MCP bus: one shared predicate, declarative rule fields, and replay fixtures.

### Rejected: trained critic, vector database, knowledge graph, durable execution engine, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework

None is necessary to represent event role, assertion context, or absolute alert outcomes. These are previously rejected component classes and would expand the system without addressing the narrow structural defect.

## Minimum experiment that can falsify the borrow

The analogy should be rejected for this harness if the following experiment fails.

1. Build a gold replay set containing every known false fire, matched true active uses of the same dangerous concepts, and counterfactual variants that change only one dimension: channel, negation, temporality, actor, quotation, or authority.
2. Validate the detector with known-present controls before interpreting any zero count. For example, prove the replay collector captures one deliberately marked tool invocation and, independently, prove its event record contains the expected invocation channel/argument field. This satisfies the requirement that absence claims rest on two mechanically independent signals.
3. Run the current detector and record firings.
4. Add only the semantic-role eligibility predicate; keep the underlying safety rule fixed.
5. Measure per stratum: true positives, false positives, false negatives, unknowns, and latency.
6. Adversarially mutate examples: nested quote, prose plus real invocation, “do not” inside an executable argument, code fence around an instruction, retrieved prompt injection, dynamically composed command, and misleading self-description (“this is only documentation”).
7. Fail the proposal if any known high-severity active use is downgraded solely by attacker-controlled prose, or if native event provenance does not separate document content from invocation in the available hooks.

Suggested acceptance criteria are intentionally **UNVERIFIED** rather than presented as facts: zero regressions on the known high-severity true-use fixtures; all known document-description false fires suppressed or made non-interruptive; no semantic downgrading based solely on payload self-description; and p95 added latency small enough for interactive use. The exact latency threshold requires baseline hook timings; measuring those timings would settle it.

**very likely (80-90%)** this experiment will decisively tell whether the core borrow transfers, because it isolates role gating from detector content and uses paired counterfactuals. Evidence: the proposed mechanism's causal claim is specifically that role, not keyword identity, explains the false-fire difference. Generalization beyond represented patterns remains **UNVERIFIED; a subsequent shadow run over fresh sessions with blinded review would settle it**.

## Final recommendation

Adopt the clinical pattern, not the clinical machinery:

1. represent an effectful event separately from a statement about it;
2. make native workflow role a mandatory eligibility condition for interruptive guards;
3. attach explicit assertion modifiers—polarity, temporality, actor, authority, and provenance—to ambiguous mentions;
4. express contraindications as structured predicates over the event plus modifiers;
5. preserve `unknown` and escalate it according to severity/reversibility;
6. validate in replay/shadow mode using absolute intervention and harm counts.

**Overall recommendation confidence: very likely (80-90%)**, based on the strong structural match between FHIR's event/statement separation, ConText's assertion-status mechanism, and the target's stated use-versus-mention failure. The exact false-fire reduction and implementation size are **UNVERIFIED; the paired replay and hook-schema experiment above would settle both**.
