## Executive judgment

The borrow transfers, but only in a narrow and useful form. The specific legal mechanism worth borrowing is the **Rule 1006 evidentiary summary with an inspectable record**, strengthened by four adjacent controls: authentication/foundation, link-by-link treatment of derivative statements, contemporaneous completeness, and decision-specific burdens of proof. Federal Rule of Evidence 1006 lets a fact-finder use a summary, chart, or calculation as substantive evidence when admissible source material is too voluminous to examine conveniently, while requiring the underlying originals or duplicates to be available to the other side. The 2024 committee note calls this “alternative proof,” says the summary may be the only evidence the fact-finder examines, yet requires a fair opportunity to evaluate it and warns that an inaccurate or argumentative summary remains vulnerable under Rule 403 ([FRE 1006](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1006)). That is almost exactly the structural problem here: the decision-maker cannot read the whole record, so the compression must be usable as the decision surface without becoming unchallengeable.

My conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the direct structural match between FRE 1006’s “voluminous materials / inspectable originals / substantive summary” arrangement and the stated “thousands of findings / agent cannot read 12 MB” problem, that a claim-level evidentiary digest would improve the auditability of synthesis. The recommendation below therefore carries the UNVERIFIED tag.

This is not a recommendation to make an LLM act like a judge, to import courtroom exclusions literally, or to reduce judgment to legalistic admissible/inadmissible flags. ## The mechanism in its own field

### 1. Rule 1006: compression is evidence, provided the record remains contestable

Rule 1006 solves a practical bandwidth problem. A court may admit “a summary, chart, or calculation” to prove the content of admissible writings, recordings, or photographs that are too voluminous to examine conveniently. The summarized materials need not themselves be introduced, but originals or duplicates must be made available for examination or copying, and the court may order their production ([FRE 1006(a)–(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1006)).

Three details matter more than the generic idea of summarization:

1. **The summary is a first-class decision object.** The 2024 amendment makes explicit that it is substantive evidence, not merely a visual aid. This honestly recognizes the cognitive reality that the decision-maker may never inspect the underlying bulk.
2. **Compression does not destroy inspectability.** The opposing party gets reasonable access to the originals and can test whether the mapping is accurate.
3. **The summarizer does not obtain unlimited framing power.** The committee note says an inaccurate or argumentative summary can fail Rule 403 balancing because of prejudice or confusion ([FRE 1006 committee note, 2024 amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1006)). Rule 403 separately permits exclusion when probative value is substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice, confusion, misleading the fact-finder, delay, wasted time, or needless cumulative presentation ([FRE 403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_403)).

This is the central transferable mechanism: **permit aggressive compression, but require a reversible mapping to an available record and a defined way to contest distortion**.

### 2. Authentication and personal knowledge: require foundation before weight

Evidence law distinguishes “what this item purports to be” from “how persuasive it is.” Rule 901 requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item is what its proponent claims ([FRE 901(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). The committee note treats authentication as conditional relevance: identity or origin is a logical precondition, not proof that the item is true or otherwise admissible. Rule 602 likewise permits fact testimony only after enough evidence supports a finding that the witness has personal knowledge ([FRE 602](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_602)). Rule 104(b) expresses the general pattern: if relevance depends on a fact, evidence sufficient to support a finding of that fact must be introduced ([FRE 104(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)).

In its own field, this prevents a document, recording, or assertion from gaining weight before its identity and basis are established. Importantly, passing authentication does not prove truth. It only clears one gate.

### 3. Chain of custody: preserve identity across handling and transformation

NIST defines chain of custody as tracking evidence through collection, safeguarding, and analysis by recording each handler, the collection or transfer time, and the purpose of the transfer ([NIST CSRC glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/chain_of_custody)). NIST also defines the chain-of-custody record as chronological documentation of seizure, custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition ([NIST, Chain Of Custody Records](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/20081)).

In the legal/forensic setting, the chain does not magically establish the proposition for which evidence is offered. It establishes continuity and exposes where substitution, contamination, or unexplained handling could have occurred. The useful abstraction is a record of custody **and analysis**, not merely a source URL.

### 4. Hearsay within hearsay: reliability cannot be inherited through an opaque chain

Rule 801 defines hearsay as an out-of-court assertion offered for the truth of what it asserts ([FRE 801(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801)). Rule 805 provides that a compound derivative statement survives the hearsay rule only if **each part** of the combined statements conforms to an exception ([FRE 805](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805)). Rule 806 then permits attacks on the credibility of a declarant whose hearsay statement has been admitted, including through inconsistent statements ([FRE 806](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_806)).

The point in its own field is not “secondhand information is always false.” It is that reliability assurances must cover every assertion-producing link. “Report A says analyst B says log C proves X” contains several distinct opportunities for error. An exception covering the report format does not automatically validate the analyst’s interpretation or the log’s identity.

### 5. Completeness and adversarial challenge: a true excerpt can still mislead

Rule 106 allows an adverse party, when all or part of a statement is introduced, to require contemporaneous introduction of another part or related statement that fairness requires—even over a hearsay objection ([FRE 106](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)). Its committee note identifies the two motivating problems: misleading impressions created by taking material out of context and the inadequacy of repair after the distortion has already landed. The 2023 note emphasizes that completion is narrow: contradiction alone is insufficient; the additional material must actually correct a misimpression.

This is a subtle but important synthesis mechanism. A sentence can be accurately copied and correctly attributed yet still be misleading because an adjacent limitation, counterexample, denominator, time range, or scope qualifier was omitted.

### 6. Burden and standard of proof: thresholds encode error costs

Evidence law separates a burden of production from a burden of persuasion. Rule 301, for civil presumptions, can require the opposing party to produce rebuttal evidence without shifting the ultimate burden of persuasion ([FRE 301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_301)). The Supreme Court’s Addington opinion describes proof standards as both a requested degree of confidence and an allocation of error risk: ordinary civil preponderance roughly shares error risk, criminal proof beyond reasonable doubt places much more risk on the state, and clear-and-convincing proof is intermediate for some especially important interests ([Addington](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/441/418/)).

The field has therefore solved a problem often obscured in synthesis: “How sure are we?” cannot be answered sensibly without “Who must show what?” and “Which error is worse for this decision?” A reversible cleanup proposal and an irreversible destructive migration should not use the same evidence threshold.

### Compiler gates

**Proposal: foundation gate**  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Reject from the decision brief—but retain in the ledger—any material claim without an atomic proposition, claimant, basis type, decision relevance, and at least one resolvable source leaf or explicit `UNVERIFIED` experiment. A claim based on a computation must name inputs and transformation. It is **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on the inability of schema validation to establish semantic truth, to catch fabricated-but-well-formed evidence without an independent source check.

**Proposal: derivative-chain gate (“hearsay depth”)**

**Proposal: decision-specific burden profile**  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

For each decision, declare the proponent, the evidence it must produce, and asymmetric costs of false acceptance and false rejection. Use qualitative action policies rather than invented probabilities:

Do not convert legal verbal standards into fake numerical posterior probabilities.

**Proposal: digest coverage certificate**  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

The compiler must append a machine-generated certificate to the brief:

- artifacts discovered / parsed / rejected;
- claim records admitted / limited / contested / excluded / unverified;
- decisions with supporting and opposing evidence;
- unresolved contradictions and completion challenges;

- orphan claims not attached to a decision;

“Complete” may be displayed only after **two mechanically independent coverage signals** agree: (1) enumeration from the input manifest and parser accounting, with a known-present fixture proving discovery; and (2) reconciliation of producer-declared artifact/claim IDs against the ledger, with a separately injected known claim proving reconciliation. Re-running either counter is not a second signal.

This is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on independent set reconciliation, to catch silently skipped artifacts and claims that a fluent narrative would conceal. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, because both signals depend on producer declaration, to detect a finding that no producer ever recorded; an external sampling audit is still required for that question.

### The bounded decision brief

The agent-facing output should be ordered by decision, not by source unit:

1. decision and error-cost profile;
2. strongest supporting claims, with independence count;
3. strongest opposing or qualifying claims;
4. unresolved contradictions and exact settling experiments;
5. proposed action, reversibility, and rollback evidence;
6. coverage certificate;
7. pointers into the ledger and originals.

A hard byte/token budget should remove cumulative repetitions first, then lower-decision-value material, never provenance pointers, counterevidence, scope qualifiers, uncertainty labels, or coverage failures. Overflow becomes a linked annex generated by the same compiler. The ideal brief size is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a token-budget sweep (for example 8k, 16k, 32k, and 64k tokens) measuring decision accuracy, unsupported-claim rate, contradiction recall, and latency on the same cases**.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### Disanalogy 1: courts have parties with incentives and rights; artifact pipelines often do not

Rule 1006 works partly because an opponent can inspect the originals and attack the summary. Rule 106 likewise presupposes an adverse party motivated to identify a misimpression. A single family of cooperative agents may share prompts, sources, blind spots, and incentives. Merely storing originals does not cause anyone to challenge them.

The transfer therefore requires a **challenge affordance**, not necessarily another agent runtime. A later unit, owner, or ordinary synthesis pass can attach contradiction/completion records. The expectation that this will produce meaningful adversarial pressure is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because shared-model correlation may dominate; it is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is to compare same-model, different-prompt challenge; different-model challenge; and human spot-checking on a seeded distortion set**.

### Disanalogy 2: admissibility optimizes more than truth

Legal exclusions protect privileges, constitutional rights, jury functioning, procedural regularity, and fairness. Some probative evidence is excluded; some hearsay is admitted for necessity or policy. A harness seeking the best engineering decision generally should not discard a useful secondhand lead merely because it resembles hearsay.

Therefore, use `limited` and provenance depth, not a literal hearsay exclusion. A secondhand claim can trigger investigation or a reversible experiment even if it cannot justify an irreversible action. Importing the legal categories literally would be **very likely (80-90%)** to discard useful engineering evidence, based on the mismatch between courtroom admissibility policy and iterative technical diagnosis.

### Disanalogy 3: legal standards are deliberately normative, not calibrated probabilities

“Beyond reasonable doubt” is not a reusable numeric threshold for software decisions. Addington grounds standards in social allocation of error, and even notes that practical proof limits matter. Harness decisions range from cheap probes to destructive changes; their priors, observability, and error costs differ.

Hence the valid borrow is “declare burden and error asymmetry,” not “assign courtroom labels to confidence bands.” Numeric thresholds would be **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment would require outcome-calibrated historical decisions with known base rates and loss functions**, which this unit does not have.

### Disanalogy 4: a chain of custody proves continuity, not semantic correctness

A perfect hash and transformation log can show which bytes were handled. It cannot establish that a report interpreted those bytes correctly, that a benchmark measures the target, or that a source was truthful. A provenance ledger that displays green checks risks converting procedural integrity into unwarranted epistemic confidence.

The UI and schema must keep separate fields for identity/integrity, source basis, derivation validity, corroboration independence, and decision weight. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the distinct functions of Rules 901, 602, and 805, that collapsing them into one “verified” bit would mislead.

### Disanalogy 5: the legal record is bounded; frontier research is open-world

A trial closes its evidentiary record and reaches judgment. Harness research often faces unknown unknowns and can run another search or experiment. A digest can certify coverage of submitted artifacts, not coverage of reality or even of all relevant sources.

Therefore the certificate must say “all manifested inputs accounted for,” never “all relevant evidence considered.” Discovering missing research domains is outside this compiler. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the logical difference between a finite manifest and an open-world source universe, that no internal reconciliation can prove global completeness.

### Disanalogy 6: summary admissibility assumes underlying material is independently admissible

Rule 1006’s source set is already constrained to admissible materials. A research corpus may mix observation, opinion, speculation, duplicate claims, and generated text without prior gatekeeping. Treating every input as an equally valid “original” would amplify noise with formal provenance.

The proposed foundation statuses address this only partially. Whether the status rubric predicts actual correctness is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to label a stratified sample at source level for factual support and compare error rates among `admitted`, `limited`, `contested`, and `unverified` records**.

## Final answer to the cross-domain question

Law of evidence has already solved a close analogue of “no decision-maker can inspect all the material” through **substantive summaries of voluminous evidence whose source record remains available for examination and challenge**. Authentication, chain of custody, link-by-link derivative-source analysis, completeness, and variable burdens of proof supply the necessary safeguards. The concrete harness translation is a claim ledger and bounded decision brief with reversible provenance, independent-root accounting, adjacent countercontext, decision-specific error costs, and two-signal coverage certification.

The analogy does **not** justify courtroom admissibility rules, a legal expert system, a trained judge, or legal proof labels. Its transfer is procedural, not institutional: **compress aggressively; preserve the path back; expose every inferential link; surface correcting context at the point of use; declare who bears which risk; and never certify more completeness than the manifest can prove**.

Overall, it is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the unusually direct match to FRE 1006 plus the deterministic nature of the proposed integrity checks, that this is worth a controlled pilot. It remains **UNVERIFIED whether it improves final decision quality; the blinded bake-off specified above is the experiment that must settle that premise before broad adoption**.
