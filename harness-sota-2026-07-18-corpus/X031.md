# X031 — Cross-domain borrow: aviation safety → synthesis

**Unit:** X031  
**Source field:** aviation safety (checklists, crew resource management, non-punitive reporting/post-event learning, automation-surprise research)  
**Target problem:** collapse thousands of heterogeneous findings into an actionable decision without requiring one agent to read a multi-megabyte report pile  
**Research boundary:** external aviation literature only. I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. The target scale and observed harness failure modes are taken as pinned premises from the unit brief, not rediscovered here.

## Executive verdict

The aviation mechanism worth borrowing is **not “use a checklist.”** It is a compact decision-production system assembled from mechanisms that aviation keeps separate:

1. **ASRS-style loss-preserving intake:** retain the original narrative, add an expert synopsis and structured codes, permit multiple perspectives, and escalate urgent hazards; do not pretend voluntary observations measure prevalence.
2. **CICTT/ADREP-style common, multi-label taxonomy:** make unlike reports comparable without forcing each event into one mutually exclusive bucket.
3. **CAST-style staged conversion:** move from event sequences and standard problem statements to intervention strategies, then rank, package, implement, and monitor a small set of “Safety Enhancements.” Preserve the link from each enhancement back across the full event set rather than summarizing within source silos.
4. **CRM-style challenge and acknowledgment:** require a challenger to question the synthesis, require explicit resolution or recorded dissent, and make the decision and its safety bottom line unambiguous.
5. **Mode-awareness and recommendation-status instrumentation:** continuously expose what stage the synthesis is in, why it is there, what it will do next, and whether a recommendation is merely proposed, accepted, implemented, verified, rejected, or superseded.

My transfer verdict is **very likely (80-90%)** that this *epistemic shape* transfers: both domains must convert many imperfect observations into a small set of interventions while retaining provenance, surfacing weak signals, and preventing a polished summary from being mistaken for completed action. Evidence is the direct match between ASRS’s narrative-plus-codes processing, CICTT’s common taxonomy, CAST’s analysis/implementation/monitoring stages, and the target’s high-volume finding pile. This is not a claim that aviation’s domain categories or numeric risk equations transfer.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a literal port of aviation’s taxonomy, consensus structure, or fatality-based scoring would improve harness decisions. Evidence against literal transfer comes from aviation itself: ICAO warns that a too-narrow taxonomy or mismatched code scope can obscure common problems; CAST had to replace its normal fatality-based scoring for turbulence because injuries, not fatalities, carried the signal; and NASA says ASRS voluntary reports cannot establish prevalence. Those are structural warnings, not implementation footnotes.

The concrete recommendation is an **Aviation-style Synthesis Packet**, generated by small scripts and existing agent roles on the current skill/hook/artifact bus. It is a typed evidence graph rendered as ordinary Markdown/JSON, not a new orchestration service, knowledge graph, vector database, critic model, or observability product.

## 1. What aviation has actually solved

### 1.1 ASRS: preserve raw testimony, add analysis, and distinguish signal from prevalence

The NASA Aviation Safety Reporting System (ASRS) is a voluntary, confidential reporting channel. NASA de-identifies reports, and the FAA provides qualified incentives against punitive use. That institutional design aims to elicit reports about near misses and operational difficulties that formal accident data would miss ([NASA ASRS confidentiality and incentives](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/confidentiality.html)).

The synthesis-relevant mechanism begins after intake:

- ASRS says every report is read by **at least two analysts**. Analysts first screen for hazards requiring immediate alert, then classify the report and diagnose underlying causes; the original de-identified report and analyst observations both enter the database ([NASA ASRS report processing](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/report.html)).
- The processing description says analysts add a synopsis, analytic comments, codes for enabling/associated factors, and codes for recovery factors—why an occurrence remained an incident rather than becoming an accident. A second reviewer checks the package for completeness ([NASA, *The Acquisition and Use of Incident Data*](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/docs/rs/51_Acquisition_and_Use_of_Incident_Data.pdf), especially the processing discussion around PDF pp. 7–8).
- The database retains sanitized narratives as well as coded fields. NASA describes the narratives as useful for policy and human-factors research and the codes as useful for retrieval and analysis ([NASA ASRS database description](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/database.html)).
- When a report alleges a significant hazard, ASRS sends an Alert Bulletin or FYI notice to an authority able to investigate; ASRS itself does not claim operational authority ([NASA ASRS alerting mechanism](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/directline/dl7_spell.htm)).

This is a **loss-preserving compression** mechanism. The short synopsis and codes make a large corpus navigable, but they do not replace the narrative. Urgent single cases can escape the aggregate path. Recovery factors are recorded alongside failures, so synthesis can learn from successful defenses rather than only tally errors.

The limitation is equally important. NASA calls ASRS reports “soft data,” notes self-reporting bias and lack of independent corroboration, and explicitly says their presence cannot establish prevalence in the national system ([NASA, “About ASRS Data”](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/search/dbol/aboutdata.html)). Therefore aviation has not solved truth from testimony; it has solved how to make testimony usable without erasing its epistemic status.

**Transfer judgment:** It is **very likely (80-90%)** that “raw finding + typed synopsis + provenance + second review + urgent bypass” transfers to agent-harness synthesis. Evidence: a finding corpus has the same compression hazard—discarding the decisive qualifier while shortening text—and the ASRS design directly preserves both layers. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that merely copying ASRS’s double read with two prompts creates truly independent verification. Evidence: two LLM passes can share model, prompt, retrieved context, and correlated failure modes; mechanical independence requires a different signal, such as deterministic source validation or a human/sample audit.

### 1.2 CICTT/ADREP: a common language that allows multiple applicable categories

ICAO’s Accident/Incident Data Reporting system uses the ADREP taxonomy: defined entities, attributes, events, phases, explanatory factors, consequences, and other values. The joint Commercial Aviation Safety Team/ICAO Common Taxonomy Team (CICTT) develops common taxonomies and definitions so different reporting systems can communicate in a standard language ([ICAO taxonomy overview](https://www.icao.int/safety/AIG/taxonomy)).

The design is not strictly one-event/one-label. The ICAO entities-and-attributes specification states that multiple occurrence categories may be associated with one occurrence because prevention requires recording every pertinent element ([ICAO ECCAIRS/ADREP Entities and Attributes](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/airnavigation/AIG/ECCAIRS-Aviation-1.3.0.12-Entities-and-Attributes-en.id_.pdf), occurrence-category description around p. 52).

ICAO also publishes the key failure condition for this mechanism: a taxonomy that is too narrowly focused, or whose codes operate at mismatched levels of scope, may fail to reveal common problems. ICAO recommends pairing aggregate analysis with review of individual occurrences and dialogue with operational experts ([ICAO human-performance occurrence taxonomy guidance](https://www.icao.int/operational-safety/hp/casd)).

**Transfer judgment:** A shallow, multi-label harness taxonomy is **likely (55-80%)** to improve cross-report synthesis. Evidence: CICTT’s common language makes heterogeneous occurrence data comparable, and multi-label coding avoids throwing away secondary contributing factors. The lower confidence reflects a disanalogy: harness findings mix defects, design proposals, owner preferences, evidence gaps, and duplicated claims, whereas ADREP mostly describes occurrences inside one regulated operational domain.

### 1.3 CAST: turn a corpus into a few monitored Safety Enhancements

CAST’s process is the closest direct analogue to the target decision problem. Its published reports show a pipeline with distinct questions and products:

1. select high-leverage areas;
2. conduct Joint Safety Analysis Team (JSAT) analysis;
3. agree on problems and intervention strategies;
4. use Joint Safety Implementation Teams (JSITs) to evaluate and prioritize interventions;
5. integrate chosen Safety Enhancements into existing work;
6. execute, monitor progress, and adjust.

The process diagram and its “data analysis → set safety priorities → implement safety enhancements” stages appear in CAST’s turbulence report ([CAST, *Turbulence: A Data-Driven Safety Enhancement Plan*](https://www.cast-safety.org/pdf/jsit_turbulence.pdf), process discussion and Figure 1 around p. 7).

CAST does not merely count recommendations. It derives **standard problem statements (SPSs)** from event sequences, proposes **intervention strategies (ISs)** against those problems, estimates effectiveness and feasibility, and packages selected strategies as **Safety Enhancements (SEs)**. One Airplane State Awareness analysis split events across two teams, deliberately crossed a few events between teams to improve consistency, identified 161 problem statements and 274 intervention strategies, and reused prior standardized statements where applicable ([CAST, *Airplane State Awareness JSAT Final Report*](https://www.cast-safety.org/pdf/JSAT-ASA_FinalReport_June2014.pdf), dataset and analysis discussion around p. 3).

Its implementation phase scored feasibility across technical, financial, operational, schedule, regulatory, and social dimensions, multiplied feasibility by estimated effectiveness, then used team review and a “sanity check” to select a cutoff. It retained below-cutoff strategies for later consideration rather than deleting them ([CAST, *Airplane State Awareness JSIT Final Report*](https://www.cast-safety.org/pdf/JSIT_ASA_FinalReport_June2014.pdf), feasibility assessment around p. 2).

The especially useful move is **cross-silo coverage**. CAST’s JIMDAT process regrouped outputs into Safety Enhancements and estimated each enhancement’s effectiveness against a broad accident set, removing recommendations from the separate accident-category silos in which they were generated ([CAST, JSIT Loss-of-Control report](https://www.cast-safety.org/pdf/jsit_loss%20-control.pdf), discussion of Safety Enhancements and JIMDAT).

CAST also demonstrates adaptation rather than ritual compliance. For runway excursions, a combined analysis-and-implementation team reviewed 15 existing reports from 11 organizations instead of repeating event-sequence analysis that had already been done; it categorized, organized, and prioritized existing contributing factors and recommendations into SPSs and ISs ([CAST, *Runway Excursion JSAIT Final Report*](https://www.cast-safety.org/pdf/JSAIT_RE_FinalReport_Feb2015.pdf), process discussion around pp. 5–6).

**Transfer judgment:** CAST’s staged artifacts and cross-silo intervention matrix are **very likely (80-90%)** to transfer at the conceptual level. Evidence: the runway-excursion case is almost isomorphic to the target—many already-produced reports must be organized and prioritized without redoing all primary analysis. The numerical scoring formula is only **roughly even (45-55%)** to transfer usefully. Evidence against it is CAST’s own turbulence case: the normal fatality statistic was unsuitable because the event set contained many injuries but few fatalities, so the team developed additional scoring processes ([CAST turbulence report](https://www.cast-safety.org/pdf/jsit_turbulence.pdf), p. 7). Harness outcomes likewise have multiple non-commensurate loss functions.

### 1.4 CRM: synthesis as a crew decision, not a single-author summary

FAA Crew Resource Management guidance describes specific observable behaviors rather than a vague instruction to “collaborate”: crewmembers state information with appropriate persistence until resolution; the environment uses challenge and response; questions are open and nondefensive; members question others’ actions and automated-system status; decisions are clearly stated and acknowledged; and safety bottom lines are communicated. It also calls for crew self-critique of product, process, and people, including positive and negative performance ([FAA AC 120-51C, CRM behavioral markers](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-51C.pdf), Appendix 1 around PDF pp. 14–15 / printed appendix p. 2).

In checklists, “challenge-verification-response” creates mutual redundancy: one pilot calls the item, both verify the actual aircraft state, and the responding pilot announces the verified status. NASA’s checklist study contrasts this with a call-do-response list, where interruption can allow an error to pass unnoticed ([NASA/FAA, *Human Factors of Flight-Deck Checklists: The Normal Checklist*](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf), checklist methods around p. 19).

This distinction matters. A synthesis checklist that asks “did you include dissent?” invites box-ticking. A synthesis challenge asks “Recommendation H-04 claims it covers problem statements P-2, P-7, and P-19; show the evidence links, state the remaining risk, and have another role acknowledge or dispute them.” It verifies state, not ceremony.

**Transfer judgment:** Challenge-verification-response is **likely (55-80%)** to catch unsupported leaps and silent disagreement in high-stakes synthesis. Evidence: the FAA mechanism is specifically designed to make another crew member verify a claimed state and resolve disagreement before continuing. Confidence is not higher because an agent ensemble lacks cockpit crews’ genuinely distinct perceptions unless the harness supplies different evidence slices or deterministic checks.

### 1.5 Automation-surprise research: show synthesis mode and intent

Aviation automation research defines mode confusion as a discrepancy between the automation’s actual state and the pilot’s mental model. NASA describes automation surprise as unexpected action or failure to act, associated with lack of current and future mode awareness ([Sarter et al., NASA record, “Team play with a powerful and independent agent”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040112190)). NASA’s mode-awareness program used executable models and scenarios to find states where a crew input had no visible effect and to test pilot expectations against actual system behavior ([NASA Langley Mode Awareness project](https://shemesh.larc.nasa.gov/fm/fm-collins-mode.html)). A related NASA paper summarizes the operator’s recurring questions as: what is the automation doing now, why, and what will it do next ([Sherry & Feary, *Evaluating Flight Crew Operator Manual Documentation*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20090011901/downloads/20090011901.pdf)).

The target analogy is direct: a synthesis agent can silently switch from evidence collection to clustering, from clustering to ranking, or from tentative scoring to final prose. The owner then sees output but not the active mode, stop condition, excluded evidence, or next transition.

**Transfer judgment:** Explicit mode annunciation is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce “summary surprise” during long autonomous synthesis. Evidence: the synthesis pipeline has discrete, consequential modes, and NASA’s research locates surprise in mismatches between actual automation state and the operator’s mental model. Whether it improves final decision quality is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E4, the mode-annunciation interruption test described below**.

### 1.6 NTSB: “recommended” and “done” are different states

The NTSB recommendation ledger distinguishes, among others, open/awaiting response, open/acceptable response, open/unacceptable response, closed/acceptable action, closed/acceptable alternate action, and closed/unacceptable action. A planned action remains open; closure depends on the action achieved, and an alternate can be accepted if it meets the original objective ([NTSB safety-recommendation field and status definitions](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/field-descriptions.aspx); [NTSB recipient guidance](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/RecipientTipRecommendations.aspx)).

This prevents the classic collapse of “someone proposed a mitigation” into “the hazard is handled.” It also preserves the objective separately from the proposed implementation, allowing a better alternate without losing accountability.

**Transfer judgment:** A recommendation status machine is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch proposed-but-unimplemented harness changes and implemented-but-unverified changes. Evidence: the NTSB state definitions encode exactly those distinctions. The correct harness states need empirical calibration, but the distinction itself is not domain-specific.

## 2. The proposed harness translation

## Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: Aviation-style Synthesis Packet

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build one small “synthesis packet” generator that consumes finding cards produced by existing agents and emits ordinary Markdown plus JSON. It must not become a second runtime. The generator performs deterministic validation and aggregation; agents supply semantic classification and judgment.

The output packet has six layers, ordered by decision utility:

1. **Decision brief (one screen):** the decision question, top 3–7 proposed Safety Enhancements, expected benefit, confidence, cost/risk, owner decision needed, and explicit no-action option.
2. **Enhancement cards (one page each):** objective, mechanism, problem statements covered, evidence for and against, dependencies, adverse effects, residual risk, test/rollback, status, and accountable next action.
3. **Problem-statement register:** 10–30 normalized problems, each with severity, recurrence evidence, affected workflows, recoveries already observed, and links to every source finding.
4. **Coverage matrix:** rows are normalized problems/findings; columns are enhancements. Cells say `prevent`, `detect`, `recover`, `worsen`, `unknown`, or blank. This makes cross-silo leverage and uncovered high-severity problems visible.
5. **Dissent and unknowns:** rejected alternatives, unresolved contradictions, taxonomy `OTHER`, unverified premises, minority recommendations, and the experiment that would change the decision.
6. **Source manifest:** every input artifact, byte/hash or stable identifier, number of extracted cards, parse failures, duplicate groups, and drill-down links to original text.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this packet will let an owner decide without reading the entire multi-megabyte corpus while preserving enough drill-down to audit the decision. Evidence: CAST’s runway-excursion team synthesized 15 reports from 11 organizations into standardized problems and interventions, while ASRS retains narrative beneath codes and synopsis. The recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 (blind decision-reconstruction trial) must compare decisions made from the packet against decisions made by reviewers with full-source access**.

What it catches that a conventional executive summary often misses:

- the same causal problem appearing under several symptom/tool/source categories;
- a severe single finding hidden by frequency ranking;
- recommendations with no source coverage, and high-severity problems with no recommendation;
- one intervention that prevents one problem but worsens another;
- duplicates masquerading as independent corroboration;
- contradictions compressed into false consensus;
- a mitigation that exists only as prose, with no implementation or verification state;
- successful recovery mechanisms worth preserving;
- inputs that were never parsed or classified.

These catch claims are **likely (55-80%)**, based on the explicit coverage, dissent, recovery, and source-manifest fields; actual recall is **UNVERIFIED and must be measured by Experiment E2’s seeded-omission test**.

### Finding-card schema

Each source claim becomes a compact card, not a free-standing summary paragraph:

```yaml
finding_id: stable source-derived ID
source: artifact + anchor
source_kind: measured | observed | static-claim | external-research | proposal
claim: one falsifiable sentence
evidence_excerpt: minimal exact excerpt or structured measurement
epistemic_status: verified | supported | contested | unverified | invalidated
problem_labels: [multi-label, shallow]
sequence:
  threat_or_trigger: ...
  error_or_failure: ...
  undesired_state: ...
  consequence: ...
  recovery_or_barrier: ...
severity: local rubric value + rationale
frequency_basis: count | lower-bound | unknown
duplicates: [IDs]
supports: [enhancement IDs]
contradicts: [finding IDs]
review:
  semantic_reviewer: ...
  independent_signal: ...
```

The `frequency_basis` field imports ASRS’s most important caveat: a pile of reports is not automatically a denominator. `recovery_or_barrier` imports Threat and Error Management/ASRS attention to how errors are trapped before an unsafe outcome. Multi-label problem codes import CICTT’s non-exclusive categories. The raw source anchor prevents the card from becoming an untraceable paraphrase.

### Taxonomy design

Use no more than two shallow axes initially:

- **lifecycle:** discover, plan, execute, verify, synthesize, handoff, maintain;
- **failure mechanism:** availability, selection/routing, interface/schema, context/provenance, state/mode, human coordination, resource bound, security/authority, recovery/rollback, unknown.

Allow multiple labels, `OTHER`, and `UNKNOWN`. Never require one leaf category. Generate candidate new labels from the `OTHER` queue only after recurring examples exist. This is **likely (55-80%)** to be more robust than a deep fixed ontology, based on ICAO’s warning about overly narrow/mismatched taxonomies and CICTT’s multi-category design. The specific axes are **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E3 must measure inter-rater agreement, `OTHER` rate, and decision-relevant retrieval against at least two alternative taxonomies**.

### CAST-like stage gates, expressed as files rather than a service

```text
MODE 0 INTAKE        -> source-manifest.json + parse-failures.md
MODE 1 CODE          -> finding-cards.jsonl + OTHER/UNKNOWN queue
MODE 2 ANALYZE       -> problem-statements.md + event-sequence links
MODE 3 INTERVENE     -> intervention-candidates.md + coverage matrix
MODE 4 CHALLENGE     -> dissent.md + resolved-challenges.md
MODE 5 DECIDE        -> synthesis-packet.md + decision record
MODE 6 IMPLEMENT     -> recommendation-status.json
MODE 7 ASSURE        -> measured outcomes + residual-risk update
```

Every mode header answers:

- What is it doing now?
- Why is this mode active?
- What completion condition advances it?
- What inputs failed or remain outside scope?
- What will happen next?

The generator refuses to label MODE 5 complete if source-manifest counts do not reconcile, a high-severity problem has neither an intervention nor explicit risk acceptance, a recommendation lacks contrary evidence/residual-risk fields, or unresolved challenges are omitted from the brief. Those are completion checks, not claims that the substantive decision is correct.

## Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: challenge-verification-response synthesis gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Use existing agent roles for a tightly specified challenge, not an unconstrained second summary. The proposer emits the packet. The challenger receives:

- the decision brief and enhancement cards;
- a stratified sample of raw sources selected deterministically (all highest-severity cards, all contradictions, all `OTHER/UNKNOWN`, all parse failures, plus a seeded random sample from each source family);
- the coverage matrix with column headings hidden during the first pass, reducing anchoring on the proposed ranking.

For each enhancement the challenger must return one of:

- `ACKNOWLEDGED — evidence and residual-risk statement match sampled sources`;
- `CHALLENGE — specific source/logic mismatch`;
- `UNRESOLVED — evidence insufficient; decision owner must see dissent`.

The proposer may amend or rebut, but may not erase an unresolved item. A deterministic validator confirms that every top recommendation received a response and that every challenge is linked into the final packet.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this catches unsupported coverage claims better than asking a second agent to “review the report.” Evidence: CRM’s challenge-verification-response verifies actual state and requires resolution, while ASRS uses a second analyst completeness check. This is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E2 must seed unsupported recommendations, source contradictions, uncovered severe findings, and fabricated duplicate corroboration, then compare gated and ungated recall**.

Two agent outputs are not labeled mechanically independent. The independent signals are (1) source anchors/hash/count reconciliation and (2) the semantic challenger’s evidence-based judgment. Where feasible, the challenger uses a different model family or a human sample, but model diversity alone is not accepted as independence.

## Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: recommendation objective/status ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Represent every accepted recommendation with two separate fields: `safety_objective` and `proposed_mechanism`. Use these statuses:

```text
PROPOSED
ACCEPTED-PENDING
REJECTED-WITH-RATIONALE
IMPLEMENTED-UNVERIFIED
VERIFIED-EFFECTIVE
VERIFIED-INEFFECTIVE
ACCEPTABLE-ALTERNATE
SUPERSEDED
ROLLED-BACK
RISK-ACCEPTED
```

Only an outcome measurement can move `IMPLEMENTED-UNVERIFIED` to a verified state. An alternate mechanism may close the objective if its acceptance test matches the original objective. Superseding a mechanism does not silently erase whether its objective remains open.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that these state distinctions prevent “recommended,” “configured,” and “effective” from collapsing into one completion label, based directly on the NTSB open/closed and acceptable/unacceptable distinctions. Whether the proposed exact states fit existing workflows is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E5 must replay a sample of historical change lifecycles and measure ambiguous or impossible transitions**.

## Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: synthesis checklist only at transition boundaries

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Do not use a giant checklist as the synthesis engine. Use a short challenge-response checklist at MODE 4 → MODE 5 and MODE 6 → MODE 7:

1. Manifest reconciled? State expected and observed counts.
2. Highest-severity singletons reviewed? Name them.
3. `OTHER`, `UNKNOWN`, contradictions, and parse failures exposed? State counts.
4. Every selected enhancement linked to sources, contrary evidence, adverse effects, residual risk, test, and rollback?
5. Every high-severity problem covered or explicitly risk-accepted?
6. Challenger response recorded for every selected enhancement?
7. Decision owner can see what changed since the previous packet?
8. If interrupted, resume from the last verified item and revalidate any input that changed.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a short boundary checklist catches omission and interruption errors without replacing judgment. Evidence: aviation uses challenge-verification-response as backup verification, while NASA/ASRS documents interruption, wrong-checklist, omission, and checklist-confusion failure modes ([NASA ASRS, “Checklist Checkup”](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/callback/cb_410.html)). The exact checklist is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E4 must interrupt runs at each mode and test whether resume/revalidation prevents stale or skipped output**.

## 3. Prioritization without fake precision

Do not copy CAST’s `effectiveness × feasibility` number as a universal rank. Use a decision table with separately visible dimensions:

| Dimension | Required evidence | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Harm/severity | concrete failed outcome or credible failure path | ordinal, with rationale |
| Recurrence | denominator-backed rate, lower bound, or unknown | never infer prevalence from report count alone |
| Breadth | number/diversity of independent problem statements covered | show coverage matrix |
| Prevent/detect/recover | causal position of the intervention | separate fields |
| Evidence confidence | measured, observed, claimed, contested, unverified | never fold invisibly into score |
| Feasibility | technical, operational, maintenance, migration, owner friction | separate ordinal judgments |
| Reversibility | rollback cost and blast radius | explicit |
| Adverse effects | problems worsened/new failure modes | explicit |

The decision brief may sort by a declared policy—for example, eliminate catastrophic/high-confidence failures first, then maximize broad coverage among reversible actions—but it must show the component values. It is **likely (55-80%)** that visible dimensions will produce more auditable decisions than a single weighted score. Evidence: CAST itself keeps effectiveness and six feasibility components before combining them, and its turbulence adaptation shows that an apparently standard outcome metric can be wrong. The best owner-specific policy is **UNVERIFIED; a pairwise decision study using historical choices would settle it**.

Frequency claims use one of four syntaxes only:

- `rate: x/y opportunities` when a valid denominator exists;
- `observed lower bound: n` for voluntary/partial report corpora;
- `repeated across k mechanically independent sources` when source independence is established;
- `frequency unknown` otherwise.

This directly prevents report volume, duplicated audits, and repeated paraphrases from masquerading as prevalence.

## 4. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 4.1 Structural matches

| Aviation condition | Synthesis condition | Transfer strength |
|---|---|---|
| Many incident narratives, measurements, and formal investigations | many findings, telemetry summaries, audits, and owner corrections | **very likely (80-90%)**, based on matching heterogeneous evidence forms |
| Rare severe cases must survive aggregation | a low-frequency failure can invalidate an otherwise high-performing harness | **very likely (80-90%)**, based on asymmetric harm in both settings |
| Contributing factors cross accident categories | root harness mechanisms cross tools, agents, and workflows | **likely (55-80%)**, based on the value of CAST’s cross-silo JIMDAT analysis; target evidence remains to be tested |
| A recommendation is not implementation, and implementation is not effectiveness | a proposed rule/hook/skill is not a deployed or verified improvement | **very likely (80-90%)**, because this is a domain-independent state distinction |
| Automation state can diverge from operator mental model | an autonomous synthesis pipeline can change phase/criteria outside owner awareness | **likely (55-80%)**, based on shared mode-rich automation, but stakes and timing differ |

### 4.2 Disanalogies that prohibit a literal port

1. **Physical causality versus socio-technical preference.** Aviation events often support time-ordered event sequences with concrete aircraft states. Harness findings include preferences, architectural bets, usability concerns, and mutually exclusive goals. Forcing all findings into threat → error → undesired state would manufacture causality. Therefore sequence fields are optional, and non-causal claims retain their type.

2. **Exposure denominators.** Aviation can sometimes divide events by departures, flight hours, or operations. A report pile often cannot say how many opportunities existed. Harness session corpora may have tool-call denominators for some failures but not for recommendations, owner corrections, or latent misses. Thus the transfer keeps ASRS’s prevalence warning and rejects report counts as universal risk rates.

3. **Stable regulated ontology versus fast-changing tool ecology.** Aircraft phases and occurrence categories change slowly and have international governance. Agent tools, models, interfaces, and failure types change quickly. A deep fixed taxonomy would lag and suppress novelty. The harness taxonomy must be shallow, versioned, multi-label, and contain `OTHER/UNKNOWN`.

4. **Human diversity versus correlated agents.** CAST/CRM teams include pilots, engineers, regulators, labor, data experts, and maintainers with different incentives and evidence. Multiple instances of one model are correlated simulations of diversity. Agent challenge adds value only when roles receive different source slices or are paired with deterministic or human evidence.

5. **Consensus is not truth.** CAST uses joint government-industry consensus to make voluntary changes implementable. In harness design, consensus among agents can merely amplify a shared prior. The transfer uses explicit dissent and owner adjudication, not majority vote.

6. **Safety-only loss versus multi-objective ownership.** Aviation’s dominant loss is safety. A harness also trades capability, latency, cost, autonomy, security, and cognitive load. Fatality-like scalar scoring has no honest analogue. Component dimensions and owner policy remain visible.

7. **Confidentiality incentive mismatch.** ASRS’s non-punitive protections are essential because humans may fear discipline. Machine logs do not feel fear, and the target already has a large corpus. The transferable piece is preserving candid owner/operator narrative and separating learning from blame—not building an elaborate anonymous reporting institution.

8. **Operational tempo.** Cockpit checklists handle seconds-to-minutes decisions with trained crews and fixed procedures. Research synthesis may run for hours and can revisit sources. Checklists therefore belong at mode transitions and resume points, not as step-by-step control of all reasoning.

These disanalogies make a full aviation-process clone **almost certainly not (1-5%)** the right architecture. Evidence is the mismatch in causal observability, denominators, ontology stability, participant independence, and objective function. They do not defeat the narrower packet/coverage/status borrow because those mechanisms explicitly preserve uncertainty and remain domain-neutral.

### 4.3 Mechanisms considered and rejected as primary answers

- **A checklist alone:** rejected. Aviation’s own reports document interrupted, skipped, wrong, and confused checklists. A list enforces remembered transitions; it does not cluster evidence or choose interventions.
- **CRM-style majority/consensus:** rejected. CRM’s valuable unit is persistent challenge plus acknowledgment, not voting. Correlated agents make majority agreement weak evidence.
- **Blameless post-mortem template alone:** rejected. Non-punitive intake improves what enters the system; it does not solve cross-corpus synthesis, and the target already contains extensive operational history.
- **Aviation taxonomy copied verbatim:** rejected. Categories such as occurrence class and flight phase do not describe harness lifecycle and failure mechanisms.
- **CAST scalar score copied verbatim:** rejected. Aviation’s own turbulence project invalidated the normal fatality basis for its outcome distribution.
- **Full safety-management system:** rejected. It would introduce governance and runtime weight beyond the synthesis problem and violate the anti-overfit gate.
- **Vector database/knowledge graph for finding retrieval:** rejected by the brief’s anti-overfit gate. The required corpus is small enough after finding-card extraction for JSONL, deterministic indexes, and direct provenance links.

## 5. Experiments required before adoption

### E1 — Blind decision-reconstruction trial

Take two historical finding sets with known final owner decisions. Team A receives full sources; Team B receives only the generated packet, with drill-down allowed only when requested and logged. Compare selected interventions, rejected interventions, confidence calibration, time, source-open volume, and missed decisive evidence. Success requires Team B to reproduce all decision-critical considerations while reading materially fewer source bytes. This settles the **UNVERIFIED** premise that the packet compresses without decision loss.

### E2 — Seeded-omission and contradiction test

Create a controlled corpus containing:

- one severe singleton;
- three duplicated paraphrases presented as separate reports;
- one contradiction to the leading recommendation;
- one recommendation unsupported by any finding;
- one high-severity problem covered by no recommendation;
- one intervention that fixes one category and worsens another;
- one malformed/unparsed source.

Run ordinary synthesis versus packet + challenge gate. Score exact detection. This settles **UNVERIFIED** catch claims for the coverage matrix and challenge mechanism.

### E3 — Taxonomy calibration

Have at least two reviewers independently code a stratified finding sample with (a) the proposed shallow taxonomy, (b) a deeper defect taxonomy, and (c) no fixed taxonomy followed by clustering. Measure multi-label agreement, `OTHER/UNKNOWN`, time, retrieval precision/recall for decision questions, and novel-pattern discovery. Validate the detector by inserting known-present labels before claiming any category absent. This settles the **UNVERIFIED** taxonomy proposal.

### E4 — Mode-annunciation interruption test

Interrupt synthesis after each mode, mutate one input, and resume. Test whether the system exposes current state, revalidates affected downstream artifacts, and avoids falsely declaring completion. Include a control run without mode headers/checkpoint validation. This settles **UNVERIFIED** claims about mode awareness and the transition checklist.

### E5 — Recommendation-ledger replay

Replay historical recommendations that were implemented, abandoned, superseded, rolled back, or replaced by alternates. Two reviewers map the history independently to proposed states. Measure disagreement, impossible transitions, and false “done” classifications. Validate the lifecycle parser on known-present transitions before claiming a state absent. This settles the **UNVERIFIED** exact status machine.

### E6 — Anti-overfit ablation

Compare four variants: plain one-agent summary; packet only; packet + challenge; packet + challenge + any proposed extra machinery. Measure decision recall, false claims, time, token/tool cost, and maintenance surface. Adopt only the smallest variant whose improvement survives a second corpus. It is **likely (55-80%)** that packet + deterministic validation captures most available benefit, based on the deliberately small artifact design and the brief’s evidence that simple loops remain competitive; the result is **UNVERIFIED until this ablation**.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

The proposals pass the stated anti-overfit gate:

- They ride the existing artifact/skill/hook/agent bus.
- The only executable additions are small deterministic validators/renderers.
- Markdown and JSONL remain the durable formats.
- No second orchestration runtime is introduced.
- No vector database or knowledge graph is introduced.
- No durable-execution engine is introduced.
- No trained critic is introduced.
- No observability SaaS is introduced.
- No guardrail framework is introduced.

The ambition is in **decision structure**, not infrastructure. The radical part is replacing “one giant synthesis report” with a typed, challengeable packet and lifecycle ledger; the implementation remains intentionally small.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that BUILD-LARGE is unjustified for this unit’s problem. Evidence: the required mechanisms are schemas, source manifests, matrices, validators, and state transitions that can be represented in files and existing agent passes. A daemon would add failure and maintenance modes without supplying new evidence. If E1–E6 show file-based passes cannot maintain consistency at actual scale, that result—not analogy—would justify reconsideration.

## 7. What this borrow would catch, in one concrete walk-through

Suppose 2,000 findings mention schema errors, tool-selection mistakes, quoting failures, unavailable integrations, and owner corrections. A conventional summary may produce five topic sections and recommend five fixes.

The aviation-style path instead:

1. preserves 2,000 anchored cards and marks their evidence types;
2. records parse failures and duplicates before semantic ranking;
3. multi-labels a finding about a dead integration as both availability and selection/routing, rather than assigning it to only “MCP”;
4. turns repeated causal patterns into a standard problem such as “capability remains selectable after its availability state becomes false”;
5. distinguishes the threat (stale advertised capability), error (selection), undesired state (failed task/retry), consequence (delay or abandonment), and any recovery (fallback tool) where evidence supports that sequence;
6. proposes an intervention such as “availability-gated capability advertisement” and shows which problems it prevents, detects, or leaves untouched;
7. makes a challenger inspect severe singletons, contradictions, and `OTHER` items rather than reread an undifferentiated report;
8. emits a short decision card with adverse effects—e.g., transient detector errors could hide a live capability—and a rollback/test;
9. keeps the recommendation `IMPLEMENTED-UNVERIFIED` until measured behavior changes.

This walk-through is illustrative, not a claim about the local harness implementation. The likelihood that the mechanism exposes cross-category leverage is **likely (55-80%)**, based on CAST’s explicit cross-silo enhancement analysis. The exact candidate intervention and its benefit are **UNVERIFIED; a controlled availability-gating experiment would settle them**, so they are not recommendations of this unit.
