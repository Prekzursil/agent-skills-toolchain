## Executive judgment — UNVERIFIED transfer; settle with `CAPSULE-TRIAL-01`

The analogy transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form.

The useful clinical mechanism is a **layered decision capsule**: (1) a one-sentence problem representation, (2) a small differential of live action hypotheses, (3) an outcome-level Summary of Findings with explicit certainty downgrades, (4) absolute benefit and harm rather than relative rhetoric, and (5) a contraindication/interaction pass before the recommendation. Medicine does not solve information overload by asking a clinician to remember every observation at the point of action; it preserves the underlying record while presenting a small, structured, decision-facing view. Cochrane’s standard Summary of Findings format is unusually concrete: it identifies the population and comparison, limits the decision-facing outcomes to seven or fewer, includes desirable and undesirable outcomes, gives absolute and relative effects, states how many participants and studies contributed, and assigns certainty separately for each outcome ([Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)).

The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve the traceability and decision usefulness of large harness audits, because the source mechanism and target problem share three structural properties: many heterogeneous observations, a small number of mutually exclusive actions, and asymmetric costs of omitted harms; the evidence is the explicit Cochrane decision-table design plus the USPSTF’s use of an evidence chain, outcomes tables, and benefit-minus-harm judgment ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14); [USPSTF methods](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/about-uspstf/methods-and-processes/update-methods-estimating-certainty-and-magnitude-net-benefit)). It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that an unmodified medical scoring system such as GRADE or number-needed-to-treat would produce valid harness rankings, because harness evidence generally lacks randomized allocation, stable populations, comparable outcome definitions, and defensible baseline rates; that disanalogy is supported by Cochrane’s warnings that absolute effects and NNT depend on comparator risk, comparison, population, and time frame ([Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

Accordingly, the recommendation is to borrow the **information architecture and forcing questions**, not clinical labels, causal estimators, or an aura of medical authority.

## 1. What clinical medicine has actually solved

Clinical medicine has not solved “summarize everything perfectly.” It has developed several linked mechanisms that constrain what must be in working memory at each decision stage while leaving an auditable route back to the complete record.

### 1.1 Problem representation: compress the case before generating answers

In clinical reasoning, a problem representation sits between data acquisition and hypothesis generation. It converts case-specific details into a structured and actionable description using discriminating semantic qualifiers such as onset, site, severity, chronology, and context. The representation activates plausible diagnostic hypotheses; subsequent data collection is directed at comparing those hypotheses. The clinical-reasoning text hosted by NCBI states that better representations address relevant attributes and that the representation evolves into a more specific summary as hypothesis-driven inquiry adds information ([NCBI Bookshelf, “Problem Representation”](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK543761/)).

This mechanism compresses without pretending to be a complete record. “Middle-aged patient with chronic, progressive, symmetric small-joint disease” is useful precisely because it retains discriminators and drops low-value narrative detail. The clinician is not supposed to infer that omitted observations never occurred.

Illness scripts provide the comparison structure. A script contains enabling conditions, the underlying fault, expected consequences, course, and management; a new problem representation is compared with stored scripts to generate hypotheses ([NCBI Bookshelf, “Illness Script”](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK543763/)). The key transferable idea is not the clinician’s memory. It is the explicit separation among:

1. the compact representation of the current case;
2. candidate explanations/actions;
3. features expected if each candidate is true;
4. observations that discriminate among candidates.

### 1.2 Differential diagnosis: preserve alternatives long enough to resist premature closure

A differential diagnosis is not a bag of every possible disease. It is a prioritized set of plausible hypotheses, revised as evidence accumulates. Its safety value is protection against premature closure: accepting an initial explanation despite later contrary evidence. AHRQ’s diagnostic-error primer lists anchoring/premature closure, framing effects, availability bias, and blind obedience to tests or experts as recurrent diagnostic hazards ([AHRQ PSNet, “Diagnostic Errors”](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/diagnostic-errors)).

Diagnostic time-outs are a forcing function rather than another comprehensive analysis. AHRQ describes the prompt “what else does this look like?” and reports mixed evidence for cognitive checklists, stronger evidence for task-based checklists, and a small pediatric pilot in which more than half of cases selected for a diagnostic time-out did not confirm the initial diagnosis. The same source stresses that decision support fails when it is not integrated into workflow ([AHRQ PSNet, “Improving Diagnostic Safety and Quality”](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/perspective/improving-diagnostic-safety-and-quality)). This is important negative evidence: the borrow must be a stage on the existing path to a decision, not a document people may optionally remember to open.

### 1.3 Summary of Findings plus GRADE: compress by outcome, not by document

Cochrane’s Summary of Findings (SoF) table is the closest direct answer to the target problem. It does not summarize every paper sequentially. It pivots the evidence around a small set of outcomes that matter to the decision. A standard table contains:

- the population/setting;
- intervention and comparator;
- at most seven critical or important outcomes, including desirable and undesirable outcomes;
- comparator burden or risk;
- absolute and relative effect;
- number of participants and studies;
- certainty for each outcome;
- comments and explanatory footnotes.

Those fields and the seven-outcome ceiling are specified in the Cochrane Handbook ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). Cochrane also explicitly distinguishes the short SoF table from a more detailed evidence profile linked beneath it. That is the essential compression architecture: the decision view is bounded, while the detailed rationale is retained one level down.

GRADE prevents compression from laundering weak evidence into a strong conclusion. Certainty is assessed per outcome, not once for the whole report, using risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Downgrades are explained in footnotes ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). The USPSTF adds a useful chain concept: indirect evidence can support a recommendation only when the analytic framework has an adequately supported route from the target population through intermediate steps to the health outcome; gaps in that chain lower certainty ([USPSTF methods](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/about-uspstf/methods-and-processes/update-methods-estimating-certainty-and-magnitude-net-benefit)).

This is a much stronger borrow than “have an LLM summarize the findings.” It specifies the loss function for compression: preserve action, outcome, magnitude, certainty, contradiction, and provenance; permit most prose to disappear from the decision view.

### 1.4 Absolute effects and number needed to treat: force magnitude into decision units

Medicine distinguishes relative effect from absolute effect. A 25% relative reduction can be a one-percentage-point absolute reduction when risk changes from 4% to 3%. Cochrane therefore recommends showing baseline/comparator risk and absolute effect, often in natural frequencies such as “10 fewer per 1,000,” alongside relative measures ([Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

Number needed to treat (NNT) is the expected number of people who must receive one intervention rather than a comparator for one additional beneficial or harmful outcome over a stated time frame. It is comparative, not an intrinsic property of an intervention. Cochrane further warns that NNT depends on the comparator risk; different risk groups and follow-up periods require different absolute estimates ([Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

The transferable mechanism is therefore not the reciprocal formula by itself. It is the demand to state:

> Compared with what, for which population/context, over what exposure window, how many concrete successes or failures change, and with what uncertainty?

That question blocks persuasive but decision-poor claims such as “cuts failures by 40%” when the baseline is two failures per million calls, or “fixes 500 incidents” when those incidents are duplicated retries rather than independent opportunities.

### 1.5 Contraindications, interactions, and iatrogenics: screen the recommendation for harm it creates

Clinical prescribing treats “beneficial on average” as insufficient. FDA prescribing information has separately structured sections for indications, contraindications, warnings/precautions, adverse reactions, drug interactions, and use in specific populations. FDA defines a contraindication as a situation in which risk clearly outweighs any possible therapeutic benefit and the medicine must not be used ([FDA labeling FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fdas-labeling-resources-human-prescription-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-about-labeling-prescription-medicines)). FDA’s Structured Product Labeling gives these sections machine-identifiable codes, including codes for contraindications and drug interactions ([FDA SPL section headings](https://www.fda.gov/industry/structured-product-labeling-resources/section-headings-loinc)). FDALabel then makes the structured archive searchable across contraindications, warnings, adverse reactions, interactions, and populations ([FDA FDALabel](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/bioinformatics-tools/fdalabel-full-text-search-drug-product-labeling)).

This structure addresses iatrogenics: an intervention can cause a new problem, interact with another intervention, or become unsafe in a particular context. WHO’s medication-safety program concentrates on high-risk situations, polypharmacy, and transitions of care, precisely the settings where individually reasonable actions combine badly or lose critical context ([WHO, “Medication Without Harm” policy brief](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240062764); [WHO polypharmacy technical report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-UHC-SDS-2019.11)).

For synthesis, the transferable move is to make every recommendation pass an explicit “can this fix itself cause or amplify another known failure?” query against a small, maintained registry. It should not depend on the synthesizing agent remembering harms buried hundreds of findings away.

## 2. The concrete harness translation

## Proposal A — UNVERIFIED Decision Capsule compiler; settle with `CAPSULE-SPIKE-01` and `CAPSULE-TRIAL-01`

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a deterministic compiler that accepts normalized finding records and emits two linked artifacts:

1. `decision-capsule.md` — bounded decision view, intended to be read in full;
2. `evidence-profile.jsonl` — complete claim/evidence ledger, intended for targeted retrieval and audit.

This is not a second orchestration runtime, memory service, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It is a format validator, grouper, and renderer invoked at the existing synthesis boundary. It therefore passes the anti-overfit gate: it rides an existing bus and adds no durable service.

The proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to fit within a small implementation if input findings already have stable identifiers and source pointers, because grouping, validation, sorting, and Markdown rendering are straightforward transformations; the evidence is the deliberately bounded schema below, while the dependency on normalized identifiers is explicit. **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `CAPSULE-SPIKE-01`: implement against 500 representative finding records and count non-comment source lines plus records requiring manual normalization.** If stable identifiers do not exist, the normalization work is outside this proposal and could exceed the BUILD-SMALL bound.

### 2.1 Required input contract

Each finding must be one record rather than free-floating prose:

```yaml
finding_id: stable-id
claim: one falsifiable sentence
decision_area: discovery | execution | synthesis | safety | maintenance
action_supported: retain | enable | modify | replace | remove | investigate
outcome: reliability | task_success | latency | cost | safety | maintainability | capability
direction: benefit | harm | mixed | no_effect | unknown
magnitude:
  numerator: null          # changed events, when measured
  denominator: null        # eligible opportunities, when measured
  window: null             # sessions/calls/days/etc.
  comparator: null
evidence_kind: measured | observed-operation | static-analysis | external | inference
source_pointer: exact path:line, command output id, or URL
independence_group: detector-or-dataset-family
context: where the claim applies
contradicts: [finding-id]
limitations: [text]
```

No confidence number is invented by the compiler. It checks whether an upstream finding contains the required confidence language and evidence, but judgment remains visible and attributable.

### 2.2 Decision Capsule format

The generated capsule should have a hard semantic budget, not merely a token budget:

```markdown
# Decision Capsule

## Problem representation
One sentence: current failure pattern + affected context + owner objective + binding constraints.

## Live differential
| Candidate action | Why plausible | Expected observations | Disconfirming observations | Status |

## Recommendation
One primary action; alternatives only where the evidence does not discriminate.

## Summary of findings (maximum seven owner-critical outcomes)
| Outcome | Baseline/comparator | Expected absolute change | Harms | Evidence volume | Certainty | Downgrade reasons | Evidence IDs |

## Contraindications and interactions
| Candidate | Context/condition | Harm caused | Severity | Evidence IDs | Disposition |

## Evidence-chain gaps
| Recommendation link | Evidence present | Missing link | Consequence |

## Diagnostic time-out
- What else could explain the same evidence?
- Which finding most strongly contradicts the recommendation?
- What harm could the proposed fix create?
- What result would reverse this decision?

## Decision and verification
Action, owner, reversible first step, success metric, stop condition, rollback condition.
```

The seven-outcome limit is borrowed directly from Cochrane, but “outcome” here means an owner-valued result rather than a component or audit category. Reliability and latency are outcomes; “hooks” and “skills” are interventions or decision areas. This prevents a report with 140 topical sections from turning into a 140-row pseudo-summary.

The capsule is **likely (55-80%)** to be readable in roughly 2–6 KB for a single decision while retaining the decisive evidence, because seven outcome rows plus a small differential and contraindication table bound the surface area; the evidence is the Cochrane seven-outcome architecture, but the byte range is an inference rather than measured harness data. **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `CAPSULE-READ-01`: render 20 past audits, measure capsule size, and have blinded reviewers answer the decision, strongest contradiction, and rollback-condition questions using capsule-only versus full-report access.**

### 2.3 Preserve “unknown” and disagreement as first-class outputs

If an outcome has no evidence, its row remains and says `unknown`; it is not deleted. Cochrane Summary of Findings tables can include important outcomes even when effects are not estimable, and certainty explanations expose why ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)).

Contradictory findings must not be merged into a bland average. The compiler groups them beneath the same outcome and marks inconsistency. GRADE explicitly treats inconsistency as a certainty downgrade ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). For harness claims, disagreement can arise from different platforms, model versions, task mixes, or detectors; those contextual strata must remain visible.

The explicit contradiction row is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch “majority vote hides a platform-specific failure,” because the compiler can mechanically detect opposite `direction` values within an outcome/context group and require a disposition; the evidence is the input contract and GRADE’s inconsistency domain. It will not catch a contradiction that upstream extractors failed to encode.

## Proposal B — UNVERIFIED recommendation contraindication and interaction registry; settle with `CONTRA-REPLAY-01`

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a small, versioned data file consumed by the capsule compiler. This is a registry of proposal classes and contexts in which they are forbidden, require caution, or require an explicit override. It is not a registry of every historic finding.

```yaml
- rule_id: no-second-orchestrator
  proposal_match: [daemon, scheduler, orchestration-runtime]
  context_match: [agent-harness]
  class: contraindication
  severity: stop
  rationale: duplicates control plane and violates architecture constraint
  evidence: [policy-or-experiment-pointer]
  exception_requires: [owner-approval, comparative-evidence, migration-plan]

- rule_id: command-line-expansion
  proposal_match: [fan-out-args, enumerate-paths, plugin-scan]
  context_match: [windows-process-launch]
  class: interaction
  severity: redesign
  rationale: combined argument expansion may exceed platform process limits
  evidence: [measured-pointer]
  exception_requires: [bounded-argv-test]
```

The clinical analogy supplies the schema separation:

- **indication:** the problem this proposal is meant to solve;
- **contraindication:** a condition where it must not be used;
- **warning/precaution:** a risk requiring mitigation or monitoring;
- **interaction:** another component or proposal that changes its risk;
- **adverse effect:** a failure the proposal can itself produce;
- **specific population/context:** platforms, shells, models, repo shapes, or task classes where behavior differs.

The registry should contain only rules backed by a source pointer and a named scope. Stale rules should expire into review rather than silently disappear.

This pass is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch direct conflicts between a synthesized recommendation and a known architecture prohibition when both use registered proposal terms, because matching is deterministic and the registry is evaluated before rendering the recommendation; the evidence is analogous to FDA’s machine-sectioned contraindication and interaction labeling ([FDA SPL headings](https://www.fda.gov/industry/structured-product-labeling-resources/section-headings-loinc)). It is **roughly even (45-55%)** to catch novel iatrogenic interactions, because lexical or typed rules only detect anticipated combinations; that estimate is an inference from the open-world nature of software interactions. **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `CONTRA-REPLAY-01`: encode rules from 50 previously rejected proposals, replay 100 accepted/rejected proposals, and report recall, false-stop rate, and misses caused by vocabulary versus missing knowledge.**

## Proposal C — UNVERIFIED absolute-impact gate and conditional “number needed to adopt”; settle with `NNA-COVERAGE-01`

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Before a recommendation can use magnitude language such as “major,” “highest impact,” or “cuts failures,” require these fields:

```text
eligible opportunities
baseline failures / eligible opportunities
post-change failures / eligible opportunities (or an explicitly modeled range)
time/exposure window
comparator
uncertainty interval or explicit reason unavailable
counting unit and deduplication rule
```

Render natural frequencies first: “estimated 18 fewer failures per 1,000 eligible calls over 30 days,” not only “37% reduction.” Render cost and adverse outcomes in the same units where possible.

Only when comparable intervention and baseline data exist may the system calculate a software analogue of NNT, named **number needed to adopt for one additional successful task (NNA)**:

```text
NNA = ceil(1 / absolute increase in task-success probability)
```

The output must include comparator, context, time/exposure window, and uncertainty. If those are missing, print `NNA: NOT ESTIMABLE`; never substitute call count, error fingerprint count, or an LLM confidence score.

Example of valid phrasing:

> In Windows repository tasks using configuration A rather than B, an estimated 1 additional task completes without recovery for every 14 task runs (95% interval: …), over the evaluation suite and version stated.

Example of invalid phrasing:

> NNA = 14 for configuration A.

The gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch denominator-free impact claims because the required-field validator can reject them mechanically; the evidence is the schema above and Cochrane’s requirement that NNT be tied to a comparator, baseline risk, population/risk group, and time frame ([Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)). NNA itself is **unlikely (20-45%)** to be estimable for most historical audit recommendations, because comparable exposure denominators and controlled post-change outcome data are often not collected; this premise is not grounded in local data in this ideation unit. **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `NNA-COVERAGE-01`: sample 200 recommendations and count how many have comparator, eligible-opportunity denominator, stable outcome definition, exposure window, and uncertainty.** This low expected coverage is not a reason to drop the gate: `NOT ESTIMABLE` is useful evidence that magnitude claims must remain qualitative.

## Proposal D — UNVERIFIED differential plus diagnostic time-out at synthesis commit; settle with `TIMEOUT-AB-01`

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Make four fields mandatory in the synthesis output schema immediately before a final recommendation is accepted:

1. `leading_action`;
2. `best_alternative_action`;
3. `strongest_disconfirming_finding`;
4. `reversal_test` — the observation that would change the choice.

For replacement-versus-evolution decisions, the minimum differential is usually:

- retain/enable native capability;
- evolve the current mechanism;
- replace it with a simpler mechanism;
- remove it without replacement;
- gather discriminating evidence before acting.

This is not a demand to populate every option. Implausible options can be dismissed with one evidence pointer. The purpose is to force comparison against the strongest alternative, not to produce performative breadth.

The schema is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce premature closure in synthesis because it makes an alternative and disconfirming evidence impossible to omit silently; the evidence is AHRQ’s account of anchoring/premature closure and diagnostic forcing prompts ([AHRQ Diagnostic Errors](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/diagnostic-errors); [AHRQ Improving Diagnostic Safety](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/perspective/improving-diagnostic-safety-and-quality)). The effect on final decision accuracy is **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `TIMEOUT-AB-01`: randomize 60 historical synthesis tasks to ordinary output versus the four-field schema, blind expert raters to condition, and score omitted alternatives, unsupported certainty, changed decisions after full-evidence review, and time cost.** The recommendation inherits the uncertainty in its expected effect but not in its low implementation cost.

## 3. What this catches that ordinary report summarization misses

### 3.1 High-volume, low-importance findings crowding out decision outcomes

Document-by-document summaries reward frequency and prose volume. The outcome pivot asks whether the repeated observations change reliability, task success, cost, latency, safety, capability, or maintainability. Ten thousand observations about one subsystem can occupy one outcome cell; one catastrophic contraindication can occupy another.

The outcome pivot is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent raw finding count from being treated automatically as importance, because the renderer groups by owner-valued outcome and requires absolute magnitude separately from evidence volume; the evidence is the SoF separation of effect magnitude, participant/study count, and certainty ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). It cannot prevent a human or model from choosing the wrong critical outcomes; those must be set before synthesis and recorded.

### 3.2 A recommendation supported by many observations but missing a causal link

Example pattern: a tool often fails; a proposed wrapper catches a related exception; therefore the wrapper will improve completed tasks. The missing link may be whether recovery changes task completion rather than merely adding retries.

The evidence-chain row is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose an explicitly unfilled intermediate link when each recommendation is represented as `problem → mechanism → proximal outcome → owner outcome`, because validation can mark a link with no evidence ID; the evidence is the USPSTF analytic-chain method ([USPSTF methods](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/about-uspstf/methods-and-processes/update-methods-estimating-certainty-and-magnitude-net-benefit)). It will not prove causality from observational links; it makes the inferential gap visible.

### 3.3 Relative improvement rhetoric hiding trivial absolute value

“Halves failures” may mean 2 per million to 1 per million. Conversely, a modest relative change in a ubiquitous failure may dominate. Natural-frequency output reveals both.

The absolute-impact gate is **almost certain (90-99%)** to distinguish those two cases when valid baseline and eligible-opportunity counts are provided, because the arithmetic directly computes changed events per denominator; the evidence is Cochrane’s 4%-to-3% example and absolute-risk guidance ([Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)). Garbage denominators remain garbage; provenance and counting rules are mandatory.

### 3.4 A locally beneficial fix that worsens the whole harness

Examples include adding retries that amplify load, adding fan-out that crosses a platform limit, adding a second control plane to solve coordination, or enabling two individually sound components whose interaction is unsafe. A contraindication/interaction table puts such harms beside the benefit rather than in an appendix.

The registry is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch previously known and encoded harmful combinations, because each candidate is checked against machine-readable context and interaction rules; the evidence is FDA’s structured separation and retrieval of contraindications/interactions ([FDA FDALabel](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/bioinformatics-tools/fdalabel-full-text-search-drug-product-labeling)). It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to discover an entirely novel interaction by itself, because a registry is retrieval, not causal discovery. Novel-interaction discovery remains the job of experiments, incident review, and the time-out question.

### 3.5 Compression that silently converts “not measured” into “no problem”

An outcome row is retained even when evidence is unavailable. Detector validity is a property of the evidence profile; absence claims require control evidence and independent signals before entering the capsule as absence.

The fixed-row design is **almost certain (90-99%)** to make an explicitly selected but unmeasured outcome visible as `unknown`, because omission is a schema error; the evidence is the capsule contract and Cochrane’s practice of reporting important outcomes even when results are not estimable ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). It cannot reveal an outcome no one selected, so outcome selection must be versioned and owner-visible.

### 3.6 Contradictory evidence averaged into false confidence

Per-outcome certainty and downgrade reasons separate “lots of data” from “consistent data.” Context-specific contradictions are shown, not resolved by majority count.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to flag encoded inconsistencies because opposing directions in the same context are mechanically detectable and GRADE treats inconsistency as a distinct certainty domain; the evidence is [Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14). Whether the contradiction is real or caused by incomparable populations is a judgment; the capsule must show context and indirectness rather than auto-resolving it.

## 4. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 4.1 Structural matches — why the borrow is legitimate

| Clinical structure | Synthesis structure | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Long chart and many tests | Thousands of audit findings and traces | Strong match: both require loss-aware compression with drill-down provenance. |
| Small set of management choices | Retain/enable/evolve/replace/remove/investigate | Strong match: evidence must discriminate among actions, not merely describe state. |
| Benefits and adverse effects coexist | A fix can improve one metric and damage another | Strong match: harms need equal visibility and interaction checks. |
| Evidence differs in bias, precision, consistency, applicability | Findings differ by detector, platform, model, task, and evidence kind | Strong match at the level of forcing questions, not clinical rating rules. |
| Missing evidence can be decision-critical | A detector gap or missing denominator can reverse a recommendation | Strong match: unknown must remain visible. |
| Decision needs a short view plus full chart | Agent needs capsule plus exact evidence pointers | Strong match: bounded front layer, lossless backing ledger. |

These matches make the information architecture **very likely (80-90%)** to transfer usefully; the evidence is the field mechanisms documented above and the direct correspondence in the table. That is a reason to prototype, not proof of improved harness outcomes.

### 4.2 Structural mismatches — what must not be imported

#### No randomized-treatment assumption

Clinical effect estimates often rest on randomized trials, explicit comparators, participants, and follow-up. Audit findings are usually observational, correlated, and generated under changing software versions and task mixes. Therefore, GRADE’s starting levels and statistical pooling rules must not be copied.

Importing clinical effect estimators unchanged is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** defensible for ordinary harness findings, because the necessary trial design and stable event definitions are absent by construction in many audits; the evidence is Cochrane’s grounding of intervention synthesis in comparative studies and its cautions about baseline risk and pooling ([Cochrane Chapter 6](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-06); [Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)). “Absent in many audits” is a general methodological statement, not a claim about the uninspected local corpus.

#### No disease ontology or biological causal model

Diseases have partially stable natural histories, pathophysiology, and diagnostic reference standards. Software failures are designed-system behaviors that can change immediately with versions, flags, prompts, or environment. An “illness script library for every harness failure” would become a stale pseudo-ontology and resembles a previously rejected knowledge-graph memory direction.

Building such an ontology is **unlikely (20-45%)** to outperform direct evidence records and simple typed rules over time, because software mechanisms and vocabulary change faster than the clinical entities that motivate illness scripts; this is a reasoned disanalogy, not an empirical harness result. **UNVERIFIED — settling experiment would be `SCRIPT-DRIFT-01`: compare maintenance effort and retrieval accuracy of a 100-case script library against flat typed records over two release cycles.** No ontology is proposed.

#### Findings are not independent patients

Ten fingerprints may arise from one retry loop; ten agents may repeat the same upstream claim; several audits may share data and prompts. Counting them as “participants” fabricates precision. The `independence_group` field is therefore mandatory, and the capsule shows evidence volume without converting it to certainty automatically.

The risk of fabricated confidence from correlated findings is **very likely (80-90%)** in any multi-agent audit that reuses sources or detectors, because shared provenance creates mechanical dependence; this is a structural inference. **UNVERIFIED for this unit’s local target — settle with experiment `DEPENDENCE-01`: sample 300 findings, cluster by source dataset, detector, prompt lineage, and copied evidence pointer, then compare raw count with independent clusters.**

#### Contraindications are not timeless laws

FDA labels are regulated artifacts with defined update processes. Harness rules can become obsolete after a platform or model change. Every registry entry therefore needs scope, evidence, version/last-reviewed date, and a review trigger. A `stop` rule requires explicit evidence and an override path.

A non-expiring registry is **very likely (80-90%)** to accumulate stale constraints in a fast-changing harness, because the target’s components and platforms update while the rule persists; this is a lifecycle inference. **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `RULE-DECAY-01`: replay registry rules against two subsequent releases and measure rules whose premise or scope no longer holds.**

#### Clinical “certainty” terminology can create false authority

Calling audit evidence “high-quality medical evidence” would be category error. The harness should use the mandated confidence bands for claims and expose downgrade dimensions such as detector validity, dependence, indirectness, inconsistency, and imprecision. It may say “GRADE-inspired evidence profile,” never “GRADE-certified.”

False-authority risk is **likely (55-80%)** if clinical labels are copied without qualification, because users conventionally associate GRADE with a developed healthcare evidence methodology; the evidence is GRADE’s explicit healthcare scope and defined domains ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). The mitigation is terminological and structural separation.

#### A seven-outcome ceiling can hide the eighth catastrophe

Seven is a presentation constraint, not a law of cognition or harness safety. The full profile must run contraindication and severity checks before outcome selection. Any catastrophic harm is promoted into the capsule even if it displaces a lower-priority outcome; multiple materially different decisions get separate capsules, just as Cochrane uses separate tables for different major comparisons ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)).

The safety-promotion rule is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent a known, correctly classified catastrophic harm from being hidden by the seven-row limit, because severity is checked before truncation; the evidence is the proposed deterministic ordering. Classification errors remain possible. **UNVERIFIED — settle with experiment `EIGHTH-HARM-01`: seed known high-severity harms below the frequency cutoff in 100 synthetic and replay datasets and verify 100% promotion without unacceptable capsule expansion.**

### 4.3 Net transfer verdict

The clinical mechanism transfers at the level of **decision-facing representation, explicit alternatives, per-outcome evidence quality, absolute magnitude, harm/interaction screening, and drill-down provenance**. It does not transfer at the level of disease ontologies, treatment-effect inference, NNT without denominators, or regulated-label authority.

The net verdict is **very likely (80-90%)** that a thin Decision Capsule compiler plus configuration registry is worth a bounded prototype, based on the strong structural matches, authoritative evidence-synthesis precedent, and the absence of a new runtime. **UNVERIFIED — the decisive experiment is `CAPSULE-TRIAL-01`: on a stratified set of historical large syntheses, compare full-report-only against capsule-plus-on-demand-profile for decision agreement with an expert reference, missed contraindications, unsupported claims, evidence-pointer accuracy, review time, and reversals after full-record access.** Adoption should depend on non-inferior decision accuracy and contraindication recall, with materially lower review burden; compression ratio alone is not a success criterion.

## 5. Implementation and evaluation specification

### 5.1 Minimal pipeline

```text
normalized finding records
        |
        v
schema/provenance validation ----> rejected-record report (never silent drop)
        |
        v
deduplicate + independence grouping
        |
        v
group by decision, candidate action, owner outcome, and context
        |
        +----> contradictions/inconsistency flags
        +----> evidence-chain gaps
        +----> contraindication/interaction registry match
        +----> absolute-impact eligibility check
        |
        v
bounded Decision Capsule + complete evidence profile
```

The renderer should be deterministic. An agent may create the normalized claims and make judgments, but identical accepted records and configuration must produce identical capsule structure and included IDs. That makes omissions testable.

Determinism is **almost certain (90-99%)** to make renderer-level omission regressions reproducible, because sorting, validation, grouping, and templating can be pure functions; the evidence is the specified pipeline. It does not make upstream LLM extraction deterministic or correct.

### 5.2 Two-stage synthesis rather than one giant reader

Stage 1 agents emit finding records with exact provenance. Stage 2 reads grouped records and the compiler-generated evidence profile, not all source prose. The final decision agent receives the capsule first and requests underlying records only for contradictions, weak links, or contested outcomes.

This staged access is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce context consumed by the final decision agent without reducing evidence access, because the complete ledger remains addressable while the default view is bounded; the evidence is the SoF/evidence-profile layering in Cochrane ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)). **UNVERIFIED — settle with `CAPSULE-TRIAL-01`, recording tokens read from capsule and drill-down records as well as accuracy.** This is data shaping on the existing agent bus, not a new orchestrator.

### 5.3 Acceptance tests

The prototype is acceptable only if all of these hold:

1. **Provenance retention:** every substantive capsule cell links to at least one finding ID, and every finding ID resolves to a concrete source pointer.
2. **No silent loss:** invalid records appear in a rejected-record report with reasons.
3. **Contradiction visibility:** seeded opposing claims appear in the inconsistency view.
4. **Unknown preservation:** a critical outcome with no evidence renders as `unknown`.
5. **Harm promotion:** seeded stop-severity contraindications appear regardless of frequency.
6. **Magnitude honesty:** relative improvement without a denominator cannot render as absolute impact or NNA.
7. **Detector honesty:** absence claims cannot achieve the top confidence band without a known-present control and two mechanically independent signals encoded in evidence metadata.
8. **Reversal condition:** every recommendation names an observation that would change it.
9. **Bounded surface:** one capsule covers one major comparison; multiple decisions create multiple linked capsules rather than an expanding master table.
10. **No runtime creep:** implementation remains a compiler/validator/renderer on the existing bus.

These tests are design requirements, not claims that an implementation passes them.

### 5.4 Evaluation design

Use historical decisions for which later operational evidence or owner correction supplies a reference outcome. Stratify by decision type, evidence volume, platform specificity, and whether the eventual correction involved a hidden harm, missing alternative, denominator error, or detector failure.

Compare:

- full report only;
- generic LLM summary;
- Decision Capsule plus targeted profile retrieval.

Measure:

- agreement with the reference decision;
- recall of known contraindications and strongest disconfirming evidence;
- unsupported-claim rate;
- exact-source-pointer accuracy;
- calibration of mandated confidence bands;
- review tokens and wall time;
- decision reversals after reviewers are allowed to inspect the full record;
- false stops from stale or overbroad contraindications;
- percentage of findings rejected or left ungrouped.

The capsule should be adopted only if it is non-inferior on decision agreement and materially better on at least one of harm recall, unsupported claims, or review burden. This acceptance rule deliberately prevents a tiny summary from “winning” by compression while losing decisive minority evidence.

The evaluation is **very likely (80-90%)** to distinguish lossy compression from useful compression if the reference set contains corrected decisions and seeded hazards, because reversals, hazard recall, and source accuracy directly test what the capsule claims to preserve; the evidence is the evaluation design. The available sample size and quality of reference outcomes are **UNVERIFIED — settle by experiment `REFERENCE-AUDIT-01`: enumerate candidate historical decisions, validate outcome labels with two independent signals, and conduct a known-present control before estimating power.**

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 6.1 “Have a stronger model read all 12 MB”

Rejected as the primary mechanism. Even if a context window accepts the bytes, the result lacks a fixed contract for preserving harms, disagreements, denominators, and provenance. It also repeats full-read cost for each decision.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a sufficiently capable model can extract useful decisions from very large input, but **unlikely (20-45%)** that raw context alone provides the same mechanically testable omission guarantees as typed rows and a deterministic renderer; this is an architectural inference. **UNVERIFIED — settle within `CAPSULE-TRIAL-01` by including a full-context model arm.**

### 6.2 Vector database or knowledge graph memory

Rejected. Retrieval is not the core problem here: the target is deciding what must survive compression and how harms and uncertainty are forced into view. A vector store could retrieve semantically similar prose while missing a rare contraindication; a graph introduces ontology and lifecycle costs. Both violate the anti-overfit exclusions for this program unless independently justified.

The Decision Capsule is **very likely (80-90%)** to remain simpler operationally than a new vector/KG service because it consists of files plus a small renderer and has no serving, embedding, indexing, or ontology lifecycle; the evidence is the explicit component count. Comparative decision quality is **UNVERIFIED — no vector/KG experiment is recommended unless the file-backed prototype fails targeted retrieval.**

### 6.3 Trained critic or debate runtime

Rejected. The differential and diagnostic time-out are output fields on the existing synthesis path. They do not require another agent topology or a learned evaluator. A critic can still anchor on the same compressed premise; the schema instead requires disconfirming evidence and a reversal test.

The schema-only forcing function is **very likely (80-90%)** to have lower complexity than a trained critic or debate runtime, because it adds no model, training set, or scheduler; the evidence is the proposal definition. Its accuracy advantage is **UNVERIFIED and is not claimed.**

### 6.4 Copy GRADE scores literally

Rejected. Risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, and imprecision are excellent questions; clinical evidence-level starting points and upgrade/downgrade arithmetic are not validated for agent-harness findings. Use transparent reasons and the program’s mandated verbal confidence bands.

Literal GRADE scoring is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** warranted without a validation study mapping harness evidence kinds to calibrated outcomes, because GRADE was developed for bodies of healthcare evidence with defined study designs ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)).

### 6.5 Calculate “number needed to fix” from error fingerprints

Rejected. Fingerprints are not necessarily independent opportunities, and a recommendation is not an intervention outcome until comparable before/after success is measured. The valid conditional analogue is NNA, and it prints `NOT ESTIMABLE` by default.

Computing NNA directly from fingerprint counts is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** valid, because NNT is the reciprocal of an absolute outcome-risk difference between intervention and comparator, not the reciprocal of a defect count ([Cochrane Chapter 15](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

### 6.6 One universal capsule for the entire harness

Rejected. Cochrane uses separate SoF tables for different major comparisons or materially different populations. Similarly, “replace orchestration,” “repair tool discovery,” and “change synthesis format” can have different evidence and contraindications. A short index may link decision capsules, but must not merge them into one pseudo-decision.

Separate capsules are **very likely (80-90%)** to preserve context better than one universal table when comparisons materially differ, because baseline, harms, and evidence applicability are comparison-specific; the evidence is Cochrane’s separate-table guidance ([Cochrane Chapter 14](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-14)).

## 7. Recommended adoption sequence

1. Define the finding-record contract and a fixed list of owner-critical outcomes.
2. Implement the deterministic validator/renderer as a BUILD-SMALL spike.
3. Encode only a small seed contraindication registry from already evidenced prohibitions; do not attempt a comprehensive ontology.

6. Adopt only if decision accuracy is non-inferior and either harm recall, unsupported-claim rate, or review burden improves.
7. Keep NNA disabled except where the absolute-impact eligibility check passes.
8. Review contraindication rules on platform/model changes and record false stops.

The sequence is **very likely (80-90%)** to contain downside because it begins with file artifacts and replay evaluation rather than a runtime migration; the evidence is the reversible implementation boundary and explicit adoption gate. Whether the prototype meets the gate is **UNVERIFIED and is the purpose of `CAPSULE-TRIAL-01`.**

## 8. Gaps and boundaries

- I did not inspect the local repo or corpus, by explicit unit instruction. Therefore no statement in this artifact claims that normalized finding IDs, source pointers, decision outcomes, or an existing synthesis hook are present.
- I did not validate a local 12 MB artifact or measure its structure. The target size comes from the task statement; local applicability is tested by the proposed replay experiments.
- The literature supports the clinical mechanisms in their own field. It does not directly evaluate agent-harness synthesis. Every predicted transfer effect is therefore confidence-banded and paired with a settling experiment.
- AHRQ reports mixed evidence for cognitive checklists and workflow-integration problems for decision support ([AHRQ Improving Diagnostic Safety](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/perspective/improving-diagnostic-safety-and-quality)). This is why the time-out is mandatory at the synthesis boundary and why improved accuracy is not assumed.
- NNT/NNA is only appropriate for binary, well-defined outcomes with a comparator and baseline risk. It is not a universal priority score.
- The contraindication registry only retrieves known rules. It cannot replace experimentation or incident discovery.
- The seven-outcome presentation bound requires a pre-render safety promotion pass and separate capsules for separate comparisons.
