# X053 — Control theory → finite-context management

**Unit:** X053  
**Lens:** cross-domain research/ideation only  
**Source field:** control theory (observability, controllability, moving-horizon estimation, event-triggered control, actuator saturation/anti-windup, dead bands and hysteresis)  
**Target:** deciding what enters, remains in, and leaves a finite agent context over a long horizon  
**Local-grounding boundary:** Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims from the local harness. Paths below are external citations, not claims about the subject tree. The only file written is this artifact.

## Executive verdict

The strong analogy does **not** transfer. A transformer conversation is not a known finite-dimensional plant, prompt tokens are neither clean sensor outputs nor ordinary control inputs, and the harness cannot inspect the model's latent state. Therefore it is **very unlikely (5–20%)** that a Kalman observability rank test, a controllability Gramian, or a classical stability proof can provide a valid guarantee about an agent's context; the evidence is the missing prerequisites made explicit in the classical definitions below: a specified state, dynamics, inputs, and outputs. Applying the equations directly would be control-theory decoration, not engineering.

A weaker mechanism-level borrow **does** transfer. It is **likely (55–80%)** that three patterns can improve a finite-context harness: (1) anti-windup when the token actuator saturates, (2) event-triggered admission plus hysteretic eviction, and (3) moving-horizon compression with an explicit boundary state (“arrival information”) rather than an untyped prose summary. Evidence: all three mechanisms address structural problems that really are shared—bounded capacity, costly updates, and loss of older observations—without requiring the language model itself to be linear. This probability is not a performance claim. It is a design plausibility claim that remains **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the replay/ablation study specified under Evaluation**.

A fourth borrow—observability/controllability as *empirical context-influence tests*, not analytic rank tests—is **roughly even (45–55%)** to pay for itself. Evidence: paired removal and counterfactual-flip replays can reveal context that does not affect decisions or missing context that would; counterevidence is model stochasticity and the high cost of replays. This is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a shadow-mode sample of 200 long-horizon decision points with repeated seeds and task-level adjudication**.

The recommended shape is consequently a small **context governor on the existing hook/skill bus**, not another orchestrator or memory platform. It does not require a vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. What the source field actually solved

### 1.1 Observability and controllability are relational properties of a specified model

For the linear time-invariant system

```text
x_dot = A x + B u
y     = C x
```

controllability asks whether admissible input can move the state between states over a finite interval; observability asks whether the initial state can be uniquely inferred from input/output behavior over an interval. In the finite-dimensional LTI case, these reduce to rank conditions on the controllability matrix `[B AB … A^(n−1)B]` and the observability matrix `[C; CA; …; CA^(n−1)]`. Kalman introduced this state-space framing and the dual concepts in the foundational paper [“Mathematical Description of Linear Dynamical Systems”](https://doi.org/10.1137/0301010). The open textbook by Åström and Murray gives the operational definitions, rank tests, Gramians, and estimator/controller treatment in its linear-systems chapters: [*Feedback Systems*, second-edition draft](https://fbsbook.org/).

Two details matter for the translation:

1. Observability is not “having lots of logs.” It means the available measurements distinguish states that matter under the assumed dynamics.
2. Controllability is not “having lots of tools.” It means inputs have authority to move the modeled state in the required directions.

In control design, unobservable modes cannot be reconstructed by an observer, and uncontrollable modes cannot be moved by the controller. The concepts therefore force a useful two-sided question: *Does this information distinguish relevant task states?* and *Can anything in this context change the future action or outcome?*

### 1.2 Moving-horizon estimation preserves a boundary representation of older evidence

Moving-horizon estimation (MHE) estimates current state by optimizing over only a recent window of measurements and dynamics. It does not simply pretend that pre-window measurements never happened. Their effect is represented by an **arrival cost** or prior at the window boundary. The arrival cost is crucial: it summarizes information that “arrived” before the active horizon, while the recent raw observations remain available for constrained estimation. Rao, Rawlings, and Mayne analyze stability and moving-horizon approximations in [“Constrained state estimation for nonlinear discrete-time systems”](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2002.808470). Rawlings, Mayne, and Diehl cover MHE, full-information estimation, arrival costs, and horizon approximations in the open [*Model Predictive Control: Theory, Computation, and Design*](https://sites.engineering.ucsb.edu/~jbraw/mpc/MPC-book-2nd-edition-5th-printing.pdf).

The transferable insight is not “keep the last N turns.” It is: **retain a recent evidence window plus an explicit, uncertainty-bearing boundary state representing what older evidence established**. In the source field, exact sufficiency depends on model/noise assumptions; approximate arrival costs trade computation against fidelity.

### 1.3 Anti-windup handles demand that continues accumulating after actuation saturates

In a PID loop, an integrator can continue accumulating error while an actuator is pinned at a physical limit. The controller's internal requested actuation then moves far beyond what the plant can receive. When the reference changes or the system re-enters the feasible region, the accumulated integral causes delayed recovery or overshoot: windup. Åström and Rundqwist's classic treatment is [“Integrator Windup and How to Avoid It”](https://doi.org/10.23919/ACC.1989.4790464), also catalogued by [Lund University](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/integrator-windup-and-how-to-avoid-it/).

Two standard remedies are:

- **Clamping/conditional integration:** stop integrating in the direction that drives an already-saturated actuator farther into saturation.
- **Back-calculation/tracking:** feed the difference between requested and actual actuator output back into the integrator so its internal state tracks feasible actuation and “unwinds.”

MathWorks' worked PID example directly documents saturation, delayed recovery, clamping, tracking, and the back-calculation feedback loop: [Anti-Windup Control Using PID Controller Block](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simulink/slref/anti-windup-control-using-a-pid-controller.html). That example is product documentation rather than the foundational research source, but it makes the mechanism inspectable.

### 1.4 Event-triggered control and dead bands spend updates only when deviation warrants them

Time-triggered control updates periodically whether or not the state has materially changed. Event-triggered control instead updates when a state/error condition crosses a threshold chosen to retain a stability property. Tabuada provides a canonical construction and proves a positive lower bound on inter-execution times under its assumptions, avoiding infinitely fast triggering (“Zeno” behavior): [“Event-Triggered Real-Time Scheduling of Stabilizing Control Tasks”](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2007.904277); an [author-hosted manuscript](http://www.ee.ucla.edu/~tabuada/Papers/EventTriggered.pdf) is also available.

A dead band suppresses small changes around a reference. Hysteresis uses different thresholds depending on direction or state: for example, enter an “admit/refresh” state above a high threshold but do not leave it until falling below a lower threshold. MathWorks' [Relay block documentation](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simulink/slref/relay.html) exposes this standard form as distinct switch-on and switch-off points and explains that their separation affects switching frequency. This prevents chatter caused by noise near one threshold. These devices do not make updates free; they deliberately exchange sensitivity for fewer switches.

The important qualification is that event-triggered control's guarantees come from a known plant, feedback law, trigger function, and Lyapunov analysis. A heuristic “semantic novelty score > 0.7” inherits none of those guarantees.

## 2. Structural mapping—and the point where it breaks

| Control concept | Source-field object | Harness analogue | Validity of the analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant state `x` | Minimal dynamical condition needed to predict evolution | Task state: accepted constraints, unresolved hypotheses, commitments, artifacts, evidence provenance, tool/environment state | Useful bookkeeping analogy; not the model's latent state |
| Measurement `y` | Noisy/partial observation of state | User messages, tool outputs, file snippets, test results | Fair structural match, but language observations are open-world and adversarial |
| Control input `u` | Command applied through an actuator | Prompt instruction, tool call, edit, escalation, delegation | Partial match; the model and environment mediate authority |
| Actuator limit | Physical saturation | Maximum usable prompt/context tokens and attention dilution | Strong capacity analogy; “attention quality” is not a sharp physical limit |
| Observer | Model-based state estimator | Context compiler plus typed task ledger | Mechanism transfers; formal convergence generally does not |
| Arrival cost | Prior information from before an MHE window | Boundary ledger distilled from evicted history | Strong conceptual match; sufficiency is unprovable without task-state assumptions |
| Trigger/dead band | Update only when error warrants it | Admit or refresh context only on typed novelty/impact | Resource-allocation pattern transfers; stability theorem does not |
| Windup | Integrator accumulates impossible actuation | “Must remember” queue grows after context is full; summaries recursively accrete | Strong failure-shape analogy if the harness maintains such a queue |

### Disanalogies that rule out a literal transfer

1. **There is no agreed finite-dimensional state.** Future relevance can depend on a phrase that no current schema anticipated. In an LTI observer, `x` is defined before design; in an agent task, the sufficient statistic may only become apparent later.
2. **The dynamics are opaque and change with prompts/model versions.** The harness has no stable `A`, `B`, or `C`, and it cannot measure the transformer's internal state.
3. **Tokens are simultaneously measurements and control.** A tool result informs the model; an instruction also steers it; examples can do both. Classical separation between sensors and actuators is blurred.
4. **The objective is nonstationary and partly supplied by the user.** A later correction can legitimately redefine the target, making an old “error” newly relevant.
5. **Small events can be decisive.** Dead bands are hazardous when a one-line credential constraint, negation, exact path, or owner correction is low-volume but high-impact.
6. **Summarization errors are endogenous.** The estimator is another language-model operation with correlated blind spots, not an independent calibrated sensor.
7. **Stochastic replay is not a deterministic reachability experiment.** Removing one token can cause broad sample changes without showing that the token was causally useful.

For these reasons, any proposal below uses control theory as a design discipline and test vocabulary, not as borrowed proof.

## 3. Concrete proposals

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: saturation replay): anti-windup context admission

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a token-budget governor that treats the prompt compiler's **requested context** and the model's **actually admitted context** as separate signals.

Suggested behavior:

1. Maintain hard and soft token watermarks, measured on the exact serialized prompt rather than character count.
2. Below the soft watermark, admit typed records normally.
3. Above it, **conditionally clamp** low-priority accumulation: repeated progress narration, superseded plans, reproducible bulk output, and already-grounded duplicates go to an external append-only evidence file, while the context receives only a stable pointer plus typed digest.
4. Compute `saturation_error = requested_tokens − admitted_tokens`. Feed this back to the ledger compiler: a record is not marked “remembered” merely because it was requested; it is marked `externalized`, `summarized`, or `admitted` with byte/token counts.
5. When budget returns, do not automatically flood deferred records back in. Re-admit only records selected by the current task-state query.
6. Never clamp owner intent, active acceptance criteria, safety/permission boundaries, unresolved contradictions, or provenance needed to validate a current claim.

This is anti-windup rather than ordinary truncation because the internal memory state tracks the saturated output. The harness must know what was *not* admitted and avoid recursively believing that an ever-growing summary is all present.

It is **likely (55–80%)** to reduce delayed recovery after long tool-output bursts and recursive-summary growth, because those match the saturation-plus-continued-integration structure. This outcome is **UNVERIFIED; replay long sessions at fixed model/version, force the same tool-output burst with and without clamping/back-calculation, and compare token recovery time, owner-constraint recall, and task success**.

It would target a class a recency-only policy misses: “memory demand continued to accumulate after capacity was exhausted.” Whether the present harness actually has that class is **UNVERIFIED; inspect serialized prompts and memory-ledger status across real saturation events, then confirm both (a) requested/admitted divergence and (b) later behavior referencing content that was not admitted**.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: shadow admission): typed event triggers with asymmetric hysteresis

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Do not run a generic semantic-memory service. Add a small admission classifier on the existing event stream. Each candidate record receives a type and cheap, inspectable trigger signals:

- `owner_delta`: new/corrected goal, constraint, preference, or explicit rejection;
- `state_delta`: changed file/test/tool/environment state;
- `hypothesis_delta`: new evidence that opens, closes, or reverses a live hypothesis;
- `commitment_delta`: promise, pending action, approval boundary, or external side effect;
- `provenance_delta`: evidence needed for an active conclusion;
- `duplicate`: exact hash, normalized command/output fingerprint, or typed identity already present;
- `expiry`: superseded or no longer connected to a live task edge.

Use asymmetric thresholds:

- promotion requires one high-impact typed delta or multiple independent weak signals;
- once promoted, a record is protected until a lower “still relevant” threshold fails for two compilation cycles (dwell time);
- owner corrections and active constraints bypass the dead band;
- small numerical/text changes in paths, refs, permissions, negatives, and test outcomes are treated as high-impact regardless of length;
- exact duplicates update a count/timestamp instead of inserting another copy.

No embedding store is needed. Exact hashes, normalized fingerprints, schema fields, and a bounded model classification already riding the context-compiler call are sufficient for the experiment. A model-produced “importance score” alone is not sufficient because it is neither mechanically independent nor calibrated.

It is **likely (55–80%)** to reduce prompt churn from duplicates and oscillating promote/evict decisions; the evidence is the direct correspondence to chatter around a threshold. It is **roughly even (45–55%)** to improve end-task correctness, because rare low-amplitude facts may be exactly what later work needs. Both are **UNVERIFIED; run the policy in shadow mode over long sessions, compare its proposed set with actual later citations/corrections, and measure false-negative retention separately for negations, exact identifiers, and owner corrections**.

It would target two classes that token-count or recency policies miss: repeated records with no new state information, and low-volume records with high control authority. Whether those are current misses is **UNVERIFIED; settle by sampling evicted/admitted records and tracing later dependency edges plus owner corrections**.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: compaction A/B): moving-horizon boundary ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Replace the idea of a free-form rolling summary with two explicit layers:

```text
[boundary ledger / arrival information]
  objective + acceptance criteria
  live constraints and permission boundaries
  established facts, each with evidence pointer and confidence
  unresolved hypotheses and discriminating experiments
  commitments / pending external effects
  artifact map and current state
  rejected approaches with reason and re-open condition
  known omissions / externalized ranges

[recent raw horizon]
  last K task-relevant events, preserving exact language and tool evidence
```

At compaction, advance the boundary by folding only records leaving the recent horizon into the typed ledger. Preserve explicit uncertainty and contradictions; never synthesize agreement from conflicting evidence. Store stable pointers to raw evidence outside the prompt. Periodically rebuild the ledger from raw evidence rather than repeatedly summarizing summaries; this is a drift check, not a claim of exact reconstruction.

The MHE analogue is precise at the interface: recent measurements remain explicit, and pre-window information reaches the estimator through a boundary prior. The analogy stops at sufficiency: an MHE arrival cost is tied to a specified model and noise law, while this ledger is a fallible task schema.

It is **likely (55–80%)** to retain accepted constraints, unresolved branches, and provenance better than untyped prose compaction, because the schema makes those fields explicit and testable. It is **unlikely (20–45%)** to be a sufficient representation for arbitrary future user pivots, because unforeseen relevance violates the assumed task-state schema. Both statements are **UNVERIFIED; A/B replay compaction points, ask downstream agents to continue identical tasks, and score exact constraint recall, provenance validity, reopenable hypotheses, token cost, and final success**.

It targets “compaction looked fluent but silently dropped an unresolved obligation,” a failure a prose-summary quality check can miss. Whether the current harness exhibits it is **UNVERIFIED; settle with two independent signals at each suspected point: a raw-history-to-ledger field diff and a downstream continuation probe that requires the omitted field**.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED (settling experiment: 200-point causal sample): empirical context influence probes

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Use observability and controllability as questions for offline evaluation, not runtime matrices.

For a sampled decision point and context record `r`:

1. **Removal ablation:** replay the next decision without `r`, holding model/version/tools/temperature and visible environment fixed as far as the platform permits.
2. **Counterfactual flip:** replace `r` with a minimally changed contradictory or alternative value, such as a different exact path or acceptance constraint.
3. Repeat each arm across several seeds.
4. Score both action distribution and task-relevant outcome. Mere wording divergence is not control authority.

Interpretation:

- If removal and flip do not move task-relevant action across seeds, `r` is a candidate low-authority record, not automatically safe to evict.
- If flip reliably moves action in the expected dimension, `r` has empirical control authority.
- If two plausible task states still produce indistinguishable actions until failure, the context is empirically non-discriminating: add a measurement/tool check rather than more narrative.
- Require two mechanically independent signals before automatic policy changes: paired perturbation evidence and later dependency/ground-truth evidence. Multiple seeds of one perturbation are precision estimates, not a second signal.

This could catch “salient-looking but behaviorally inert” context and “two live hypotheses that the current evidence cannot distinguish.” It is **roughly even (45–55%)** that the probe produces stable enough rankings to tune admission, because the intervention is causal in the prompt but outcomes remain stochastic and interaction-heavy. This is **UNVERIFIED; the 200-point study must report confidence intervals, seed sensitivity, interaction tests for record pairs, and human/task-oracle agreement**.

Do not run this on every turn. Its cost is appropriate for policy evaluation and regression tests, not the live path.

## 4. Combined minimal architecture

The four proposals compose into one bounded loop:

```text
tool/user/harness event
        |
        v
typed delta detector ---- exact duplicate/fingerprint ----> external evidence log
        |
        v
hysteretic admission policy
        |
        v
boundary ledger + recent raw horizon
        |
        v
exact prompt-token measurement ---- saturation error ----+
        ^                                                 |
        +------------ clamp / externalize / status <------+

offline only: removal + flip replays tune and audit the policy
```

The runtime data model can remain small: an append-only event record, a bounded typed ledger, a recent raw window, and an admission decision log. The governor is not an autonomous planner. It never schedules work, owns durable execution, or becomes the source of truth for artifacts. Raw evidence remains in existing files/session logs; context holds pointers and current task state.

It is **very likely (80–90%)** that this design can ride an existing event/hook/skill bus in the abstract, because every operation is a transform on already-produced events and serialized prompts. Compatibility with this specific harness is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a read-only interface check showing both a pre-compaction/event interception point and access to the final serialized token count**. Until that check, no implementation-size claim beyond the isolated algorithms is warranted.

## 5. What not to borrow

### 5.1 Do not compute a fake observability Gramian over embeddings

An embedding similarity matrix is not an observability Gramian: it lacks defined task dynamics and an output map. It is **very unlikely (5–20%)** to support the claimed observability interpretation, based on the absent `A/C` model and state definition. It may still be a retrieval heuristic, but that is a different claim and would introduce the explicitly rejected vector-memory direction if made persistent.

### 5.2 Do not claim closed-loop stability for agent behavior

A dead-band heuristic does not inherit Tabuada's Lyapunov proof. The trigger, plant, feedback law, and bounded disturbance assumptions are absent. It is **almost certainly not (1–5%)** defensible to label such a harness “stable” in the control-theoretic sense without a formal task/environment model; the evidence is the mismatch with the theorem's prerequisites.

### 5.3 Do not optimize only token occupancy

Low occupancy is not task success. An aggressive controller can achieve a beautiful token setpoint by discarding decisive evidence. The correct evaluation includes downstream outcome, exact constraint/provenance recall, and recovery after saturation.

### 5.4 Do not turn the context governor into a second orchestration runtime

The governor decides representation and admission only. Planning, scheduling, retries, and durable state transitions stay on the existing bus. This boundary is the anti-overfit gate: the useful mechanisms are small feedback transforms, not a new agent operating system.

## 6. Evaluation design and falsification gates

### Dataset construction

Select long-horizon sessions with at least one actual compaction or high-context interval and a checkable downstream outcome. Stratify by coding, research, multi-tool, and owner-correction tasks. Do not select only sessions already known to fit the theory.

### Arms

1. Existing context behavior (control).
2. Typed boundary ledger only.
3. Boundary ledger + hysteretic admission.
4. Boundary ledger + admission + saturation anti-windup.

Run the empirical influence probe separately; do not bundle its replay cost into runtime performance.

### Metrics

- final task success under a task-specific oracle;
- exact owner-constraint and acceptance-criterion recall;
- provenance correctness (does a claim's pointer support it?);
- unresolved-hypothesis preservation;
- false eviction rate: evicted record later needed;
- false retention rate: retained record had no later dependency and no probe influence;
- serialized prompt tokens and compiler latency;
- number of promote/evict reversals per record (chatter);
- recovery turns/tokens after a forced saturation burst;
- contradiction preservation versus accidental resolution;
- owner corrections attributable to context loss.

### Required two-signal standard

Do not label a context record useless, a policy broken, or an omission fixed from one detector. Use:

- **Signal A, intervention:** removal/flip replay or controlled saturation replay;
- **Signal B, independent consequence:** later dependency trace, exact acceptance test, provenance oracle, or owner correction.

Before trusting either detector, demonstrate it on known-present controls: seed a required exact constraint that the dependency tracer must recover, and seed a redundant duplicate that the influence probe should rank below it. Repeating a seed is not a second signal.

### Falsification criteria

Reject or sharply narrow the borrow if any of the following occurs:

- typed compaction improves recall fields but reduces final success beyond the predeclared non-inferiority margin;
- the dead band disproportionately evicts short negations, exact identifiers, or owner corrections;
- saturation feedback reduces tokens but increases time-to-recover or re-fetch cost;
- influence rankings fail to replicate across seeds/model point releases;
- the implementation requires a second orchestration runtime or persistent semantic store;
- ledger rebuilds cannot detect intentionally seeded omissions.

The overall proposal is **likely (55–80%)** to survive as a narrower token-governance mechanism and **unlikely (20–45%)** to survive unchanged as a general theory of context relevance. Evidence for the first is the shared bounded-resource structure; evidence for the second is open-world future relevance and opaque model dynamics. Both remain **UNVERIFIED until the above stratified replay experiment is run**.

## 7. Anti-overfit gate

All recommended runtime mechanisms ride the existing hook/skill/MCP event bus and remain representation transforms. None proposes a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The design deliberately avoids a learned global relevance model: model upgrades can invalidate it, while typed deltas, exact fingerprints, saturation accounting, and replay tests remain inspectable.

The only potentially expanding element is the offline replay evaluator. It is bounded test infrastructure, not a live runtime. If the harness cannot replay deterministically enough for useful comparisons, Proposal D is dropped; Proposals A–C do not depend on keeping it.

## 8. Gaps and evidence limits

- **Current-harness prevalence is UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the local saturation/compaction audit described under each proposal.** This unit was ordered not to ground in the repository, so I make no claim that a particular hook, summary policy, or defect exists.
- **Token interception compatibility is UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a read-only demonstration that the harness exposes pre-compaction events and the exact final serialized token count.** If either is absent, the proposal must move to a native surface or be reduced to an explicit skill.
- Control-theory sources establish their own-field mechanisms, not agent benefits. Every predicted harness benefit above carries a confidence band and experiment.
- Classical MHE can reason about an arrival cost because a state transition and measurement model exist. The proposed boundary ledger has no equivalent guarantee; its schema must be treated as a falsifiable approximation.
- Event-triggered control literature often optimizes communication/computation under mathematical stability assumptions. Here, “novelty” and “impact” are task-semantic and only empirically testable.
- The first external search request was rate-limited. I therefore retrieved the cited DOI records, author manuscript, university publication record, open textbooks, and technical documentation directly. This limits literature breadth but does not alter the stated source-field definitions. No claim of an exhaustive control-theory review is made.
