# X100 — Cross-domain borrow: metrology → orchestration

**Unit:** X100  
**Source field:** metrology (calibration, traceability, measurement uncertainty, reference standards)  
**Target decision:** how many parallel agent workers to run, and when another worker stops adding useful evidence  
**Lens constraint honored:** this is external research and design ideation. I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness or repository.

## Executive finding

The borrow transfers **partially and conditionally**. The useful mechanism is not “ask several agents and average them.” It is metrology's combination of (1) an explicit uncertainty budget, (2) covariance terms for non-independent observations, (3) calibration against traceable references, and (4) check standards/control evidence that reveal drift. In metrology, repeated observations reduce only the repeatable component of uncertainty; correlated and systematic components remain in the combined budget. NIST explicitly limits its standard-error-of-the-mean example to **independent** observations and explicitly represents correlations by covariance or correlation coefficients ([NIST TN 1297 §3](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-3-type-evaluation-standard-uncertainty), [NIST TN 1297 §2.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-2-classification-components-uncertainty), [NIST TN 1297 Appendix A](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty)).

Translated narrowly, the harness would maintain a calibrated uncertainty budget for each **task class × worker recipe**, estimate how correlated the recipes' errors are, and launch another worker only when its expected reduction in *decision-relevant* uncertainty exceeds its cost. This is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent some wasteful fan-out and some false confidence from homogeneous consensus; the evidence is the exact covariance mathematics below plus the well-supported observation in the source field that check standards and long-term empirical models improve uncertainty evaluation, but effectiveness on agent tasks is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 (stratified replay with counterfactual fan-out) would settle it** ([NIST TN 1297 §4.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-4-type-b-evaluation-standard-uncertainty)).

This mechanism must **not** be used as a universal truth estimator. For open-ended software and research tasks, “the answer” is often not a stable scalar measurand; reference answers can be incomplete; workers can discover qualitatively different solution paths rather than make exchangeable repeated measurements. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a universal numeric uncertainty controller would produce misleading precision, because the metrology equations require a specified measurand, measurement model, and meaningful uncertainty components, while agent work frequently lacks all three; the cited metrology definition attaches uncertainty to values attributed to a stated measurand and to the information used ([JCGM VIM 2.26](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html)). The correct result is therefore a **calibrated redundancy controller with an abstain path**, not a general metrology-themed orchestrator.

## 1. The specific source-field mechanism

### 1.1 Measurement uncertainty is a budget, not observed disagreement

The International Vocabulary of Metrology defines measurement uncertainty as a non-negative parameter characterizing the dispersion of quantity values attributed to a measurand, based on the information used. Its notes say uncertainty generally contains multiple components, including components arising from systematic effects; some components are evaluated statistically (Type A), while others use experience or other information (Type B) ([JCGM VIM 2.26](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html)). Thus, five instruments agreeing is not by itself evidence of low total uncertainty: all five can share a calibration bias or environmental influence.

NIST operationalizes this as an **uncertainty budget**. A Type A component is evaluated statistically from observations; a Type B component is evaluated by other information and represented as a variance-like quantity. Correlations in either category are represented with covariance or correlation coefficients ([NIST TN 1297 §2.4–2.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-2-classification-components-uncertainty)). NIST's worked end-gauge budget separately lists calibration of the reference gauge, repeated observations, comparator effects, thermal expansion, room-temperature cycling, and temperature differences, then combines them into one standard uncertainty ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix D.2](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-d2-identification-uncertainty-components)). The important mechanism is the decomposition: observed scatter is one line item, not the whole epistemic state.

For a result \(y=f(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\), NIST gives the first-order propagation law

\[
u_c^2(y)=\sum_i c_i^2 u^2(x_i)+2\sum_{i<j}c_i c_j u(x_i,x_j),
\]

where \(c_i=\partial f/\partial x_i\) are sensitivity coefficients and \(u(x_i,x_j)\) are covariances ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix A, Eq. A-3](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty)). This explicitly prevents a correlated set of measurements from masquerading as independent evidence.

For the special case of \(n\) equally weighted observations with common variance \(\sigma^2\) and common pairwise correlation \(\rho\), direct substitution into that covariance law gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\,[1+(n-1)\rho],
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{n}{1+(n-1)\rho}.
\]

These two equations are an algebraic specialization of NIST's covariance formula, not a separate empirical claim ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix A, Eq. A-3](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty)). They expose the orchestration lesson sharply: if \(\rho=0\), four observations provide effective size four; if \(\rho=0.75\), four provide only \(4/3.25\approx1.23\). As \(n\) grows, correlated common-mode error creates a floor rather than vanishing.

### 1.2 The square-root benefit requires independence

NIST's Type A example estimates a quantity using the sample mean of \(n\) **independent observations obtained under the same conditions**, with standard uncertainty equal to the estimated standard deviation of that mean ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix A, Eq. A-4 and A-5](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty)). NIST also names ANOVA as a way to identify and quantify random effects in suitable measurement designs ([NIST TN 1297 §3](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-3-type-evaluation-standard-uncertainty)). Metrology therefore does not license the naïve rule “uncertainty falls as \(1/\sqrt n\)” unless the independence/design assumptions are earned.

This distinction maps to agent workers unusually well. Copies sharing the same base model, system prompt, retrieved sources, tool failures, and initial framing have multiple plausible common-mode error sources. Whether those sources induce stable, estimable error covariance is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the shared causal inputs and the covariance mechanism, but it is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E0 (paired reference-task runs across worker recipes, seeds, and shared-context ablations) would settle it**. Merely varying persona text must not be treated as independence without that calibration result.

### 1.3 Traceability makes calibration claims auditable

Metrological traceability is the property by which a result is related to a reference through a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, with each calibration contributing uncertainty; the VIM notes that traceability requires an established calibration hierarchy and that the reference and time must be specified ([JCGM VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). This is stronger than attaching a benchmark score to an agent. It records what reference was used, when, by what procedure, and with what uncertainty.

NIST's Type B guidance allows prior data, instrument behavior, manufacturer specifications, calibration reports, and reference-data uncertainty to inform the budget. It recommends long-term empirical models, check standards, and control charts where feasible, because those can indicate whether a measurement process remains under statistical control ([NIST TN 1297 §4.1 and §4.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-4-type-b-evaluation-standard-uncertainty)). NIST's proficiency-testing program likewise uses interlaboratory comparisons to assess laboratory performance and traceability ([NIST Laboratory Metrology PT Services](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/laboratory-metrology/pt-program)); the current NIST policy describes round-robin comparisons as part of ongoing measurement assurance ([NISTIR 7082e2026](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.7082e2026)).

The transferable mechanism is therefore a loop:

1. define the measurand and procedure;
2. calibrate each process against references;
3. decompose repeatable, systematic, and correlated components;
4. propagate those components into the reported result;
5. periodically run check standards to detect drift;
6. refuse traceability claims when the chain or operating regime is broken.

That loop, rather than the vocabulary of “confidence,” is the valuable borrow.

## 2. Concrete translation to an agent harness

### 2.1 Define the agent-side measurand narrowly

The harness should not define the measurand as “truth.” For each eligible task class, define it as a measurable downstream quantity, for example:

- probability that a patch passes a held-out deterministic test suite;
- probability that a cited factual claim survives source verification;
- probability that a defect classification matches independently adjudicated labels;
- expected loss of a routing decision (accept, ask another worker, escalate, or abstain).

This restriction is **very likely (80-90%)** necessary to avoid false precision, because metrological uncertainty is attached to a specified measurand and agent tasks without a scorable outcome supply no corresponding reference quantity ([JCGM VIM 2.26](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html)). Which task classes in this owner's workload meet the restriction is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E0's reference-task inventory and inter-rater audit would settle it**.

### 2.2 Proposal A — traceable calibration manifest — UNVERIFIED (Experiment E0)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a versioned manifest for every calibrated `task_class × worker_recipe` containing:

- task-class definition and explicit out-of-scope boundary;
- worker recipe identity: model/version, system/developer prompt hash, tool set, retrieval/source policy, temperature/seed policy, and isolation mode;
- reference-set identifier, provenance, creation date, adjudication method, and known limitations;
- evaluation procedure and loss function;
- sample counts, base error rate, calibration curve, and uncertainty interval;
- pairwise residual covariance/correlation with other recipes;
- drift-check schedule and last valid check;
- expiration conditions (model, prompt, tool, benchmark, or environment change);
- a `traceability_status` of `valid`, `stale`, or `not_established`.

The recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to make worker-count decisions reproducible across runs, because the VIM requires a documented calibration chain with time and reference specified; its harness benefit is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E0 would build two manifests independently and test whether they reproduce the same eligibility and uncertainty classification** ([JCGM VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). Configuration alone does not perform calibration; it makes calibration inputs and invalidation mechanically visible.

### 2.3 Proposal B — covariance-aware sequential fan-out controller — UNVERIFIED (Experiment E1)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Implement a small policy function on the existing orchestration bus. It consumes the task class, candidate worker recipes, current outputs, calibrated residual covariance matrix, estimated Type B floor, cost/budget, and consequence tier. It returns `launch(recipe)`, `stop_and_aggregate`, or `abstain/escalate`.

For eligible scalar or categorical decisions, use this sequence:

1. **Eligibility gate.** If no current calibration manifest matches the task and worker recipe, return `not_established`; do not emit a numeric uncertainty.
2. **Start small.** Run one strong recipe. For consequence tiers configured to require redundancy, run a second recipe selected for calibrated residual diversity, not merely a different label.
3. **Update the budget.** Combine (a) observed within-run disagreement, (b) historical recipe error, (c) pairwise residual covariance, and (d) non-reducible/reference uncertainty. Bootstrap or a discrete calibrated model may replace a Gaussian approximation; NIST explicitly permits documented equivalent numerical methods such as bootstrap for combined uncertainty ([NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)).
4. **Estimate marginal value.** For each unused recipe, estimate the expected change in calibrated downstream loss after observing it, using held-out calibration records.
5. **Stop by utility, not agreement.** Stop when every candidate's expected loss reduction is below its configured token/time opportunity cost, or when the uncertainty interval no longer crosses the action boundary. If the uncertainty floor still crosses the boundary but no candidate has value, abstain/escalate.
6. **Record provenance.** Save selected recipes, calibration IDs, covariance version, pre/post uncertainty, stopping reason, and realized outcome when it becomes available.

The uncertainty propagation in step 3 is metrological; the utility comparison in steps 4–5 is not. Metrology describes the quality of a measurement result but does not decide the owner's price of latency, tokens, or a wrong answer. The controller's stopping rule is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce unnecessary workers without worsening calibrated task loss, because it explicitly charges correlated workers less than independent ones and conditions stopping on a held-out loss model; this is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 would settle it by replaying tasks under worker caps 1…N and comparing policy-selected cost/loss with fixed-N and disagreement-only baselines**. No production enablement should precede that replay.

### 2.4 Proposal C — check-task drift probes — UNVERIFIED (Experiment E2)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Periodically inject a small, blinded set of reference tasks through each calibrated recipe. Plot residual error, calibration error, and cross-recipe correlation over time. Mark a manifest stale when a preregistered control limit is crossed or when a recipe-defining component changes. This directly borrows NIST's recommendation to use check standards and control charts to determine whether a measurement process remains under statistical control ([NIST TN 1297 §4.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-4-type-b-evaluation-standard-uncertainty)).

Drift probes are **likely (55-80%)** to catch performance or covariance changes after model/tool/prompt updates before the controller trusts obsolete fan-out estimates; the causal rationale is the versioned traceability chain plus check-standard practice, but detection power and cadence are **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E2 (synthetic recipe changes plus naturally occurring version transitions, scored for detection delay and false alarms) would settle them**. Check tasks must be held out from prompt optimization to reduce benchmark contamination.

### 2.5 Proposal D — calibrated diversity selection — UNVERIFIED (Experiment E0/E1)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Choose the next worker by expected incremental information conditional on workers already run. A worker with slightly lower standalone accuracy can be preferable if its residual errors are sufficiently less correlated. Conversely, multiple nominal “agents” with the same model, context, and retrieval should be treated as one family until calibration demonstrates otherwise.

This selection rule is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform choosing the next worker by standalone benchmark rank on eligible tasks, because the covariance term changes the combined variance and effective sample size exactly as shown in §1.1; the task-level ranking effect is **UNVERIFIED; Experiments E0 and E1 would settle it** ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix A, Eq. A-3](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty)). The manifest can express the ordering; Proposal B executes it.

## 3. What this would catch that common orchestration misses

The following are design capabilities, not claims about the present harness:

1. **Correlated consensus.** Five workers agree because they share a model prior, prompt framing, retrieval corpus, or tool failure. A vote count says “5–0”; a calibrated covariance budget may say “about 1.2 independent observations.” Detection is **likely (55-80%)** if shared-input correlation persists on reference tasks; that premise is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E0 would settle it**. The governing source-field mechanism is explicit covariance ([NIST TN 1297 §2.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-2-classification-components-uncertainty)).

2. **A systematic uncertainty floor.** More samples reduce observed scatter while reference ambiguity, evaluator error, or shared blind spots dominate. A budget keeps those Type B/reference components visible instead of letting apparent consensus drive uncertainty toward zero ([JCGM VIM 2.26 notes 1 and 3](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html), [NIST TN 1297 §4](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-4-type-b-evaluation-standard-uncertainty)). Catching a real floor is **likely (55-80%)** on stable calibrated task classes and **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 would settle it**.

3. **Marginal-worker collapse.** Worker four costs tokens but barely changes the calibrated action probability because it is redundant with workers one through three. The sequential policy exposes expected marginal loss reduction rather than relying on a fixed fan-out. Cost savings are **likely (55-80%)** but **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 would settle them**.

4. **Calibration-chain breakage.** A model alias, prompt, tool set, reference set, or environment changes, invalidating the old worker covariance and error rates. A versioned manifest plus check-task control marks the result stale. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** for declared/versioned changes because exact identity mismatch is mechanically testable; detection of silent provider-side changes is **roughly even (45-55%)** and **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E2 would settle it**. The analogy rests on traceability's explicit reference, time, and calibration chain ([JCGM VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)).

5. **Disagreement that deserves escalation rather than more copies.** If uncertainty remains decision-relevant but every available worker belongs to the same correlated family, the correct output is “no calibrated additional information,” not “spawn more.” This behavior is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce futile loops if covariance estimates are stable; stability is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E0 would settle it**.

6. **A weak but complementary worker.** Accuracy-only routing can exclude a worker that detects a distinct failure mode. Residual-covariance calibration can reveal positive ensemble value. This is **likely (55-80%)** where heterogeneous recipes genuinely produce different residuals; it is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 would settle it**.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 No stable measurand

A mass calibration has a specified quantity and unit. “Best architecture,” “good explanation,” and “correct research synthesis” are multi-objective judgments whose target can change as evidence arrives. Treating worker prose as repeated measurements of one latent scalar can be category error. This disanalogy is **very likely (80-90%)** to apply to a large share of open-ended agent work, based on the VIM requirement that uncertainty concerns values attributed to a measurand; the share in this owner's workload is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E0's task inventory would settle it** ([JCGM VIM 2.26](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html)).

**Consequence:** the controller must abstain outside declared task classes. It must never invent a universal “answer uncertainty.”

### 4.2 Workers are often explorers, not repeat instruments

Parallel agents may search different parts of a solution space, run different experiments, or create artifacts that later workers inspect. Their value can be option discovery, not variance reduction around a common estimate. The measurement-average analogy is **unlikely (20-45%)** to model these creative/decompositional workflows adequately, because the NIST repeated-observation formula assumes observations of the same input quantity under the same conditions ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix A, A.4](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty)).

**Consequence:** use ordinary dependency-aware planning for decomposable work. Apply the metrology controller only to redundant evidence/verification branches or to a clearly scored terminal decision.

### 4.3 Reference tasks are not primary standards

Most agent benchmarks are finite, contestable, and subject to leakage or evaluator error. Their “true” labels may be adjudicated conventions, not realizations of an invariant unit. Calling them traceable does not make them equivalent to SI standards. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that reference-set uncertainty and distribution shift will materially limit calibration, because NIST itself includes reference calibration and other systematic components in an uncertainty budget rather than treating the reference as exact ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix D.2](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-d2-identification-uncertainty-components)).

**Consequence:** record label/adjudicator uncertainty as a non-reducible component, maintain task-class scope, and expire calibration rather than claiming universal traceability.

### 4.4 Independence and stationarity can fail abruptly

Agent providers update models; search indexes change; tool availability changes; prompts and context introduce task-dependent coupling. A covariance matrix estimated last month may not describe today's rare task. It is **likely (55-80%)** that covariance is conditional on task class and recipe version, based on the number of shared causal inputs, but the size and stability are **UNVERIFIED; Experiments E0 and E2 would settle them**. Metrology responds to analogous process drift with check standards/control charts, but those only detect changes represented by the checks ([NIST TN 1297 §4.7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-4-type-b-evaluation-standard-uncertainty)).

**Consequence:** no global covariance constant; use hierarchical shrinkage or conservative bounds per task class, expose sample size, and fall back to `not_established` when checks do not cover the regime.

### 4.5 Output distributions need not be Gaussian or locally linear

Code either passes a test or does not; a single worker may discover a decisive counterexample; aggregation can be discontinuous. NIST's propagation formula is a first-order Taylor approximation, although NIST permits documented numerical alternatives such as bootstrap ([NIST TN 1297 Appendix A, Eq. A-3](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty), [NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)). It is **likely (55-80%)** that discrete calibration, bootstrap, or conformal-style empirical intervals will be safer than Gaussian propagation for many eligible agent tasks; this is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 would compare interval coverage and settle the choice**.

**Consequence:** borrow decomposition and covariance, not a mandatory normal model.

### 4.6 Uncertainty is not utility

Metrology tells us how to express uncertainty; it does not say whether another measurement is worth ten seconds or ten dollars. A low-impact formatting choice and a high-impact destructive action can have identical uncertainty but require different worker counts. This is not a flaw in metrology; it is a missing decision-theory layer.

**Consequence:** the stopping threshold must be configured from consequence and cost. A controller that stops solely at an arbitrary uncertainty number does not solve the orchestration problem.

## 5. Experiments that can falsify the proposal

### E0 — calibration and covariance feasibility

1. Define 3–5 task classes with independently scorable outcomes and at least one deliberately open-ended negative-control class.
2. Sample a held-out, time-split reference set; document provenance, adjudication uncertainty, and exclusions.
3. Run a crossed design over worker recipe, base model, prompt family, seed, tool set, retrieval corpus, and shared-versus-isolated context.
4. Measure residual error, calibration error, pairwise residual covariance, and covariance stability across folds and time.
5. Include known controls: duplicated identical outputs must register strong dependence; independently generated synthetic Bernoulli residuals must register near-zero dependence. These controls validate the detector before any absence claim.
6. Reject the transfer for a task class if covariance/error estimates are unstable, reference disagreement dominates, or two independent analysts cannot reproduce eligibility from the manifest.

Success would make recipe-conditioned covariance **likely (55-80%)** usable for offline policy testing; this remains **UNVERIFIED until E0 is run**. Failure is a valid conclusion: use fixed conservative fan-out or direct verification instead.

### E1 — counterfactual sequential-policy replay

Precompute outputs for worker caps 1…N so all policies see the same possible observations. Compare:

- fixed \(N\);
- stop on agreement;
- stop on raw disagreement threshold;
- accuracy-ranked next worker;
- covariance-aware expected-loss policy;
- oracle policy using realized outcomes (upper bound only).

Evaluate held-out outcome loss, token/time cost, abstention rate, worst-class loss, interval coverage, and number of workers. Require non-inferior outcome loss under a preregistered margin and lower cost; report bootstrap intervals. The proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to beat fixed-N on cost at matched loss, but this is **UNVERIFIED; E1 is the settling experiment**. If gains vanish after time-split evaluation, do not build the runtime policy.

### E2 — drift and invalidation test

Introduce controlled changes one at a time: model version, prompt, tool availability, retrieval source, and task distribution. Also observe any natural version transitions. Compare manifest identity checks and check-task control charts against known change points. Measure detection delay, false alarms, and missed changes, with a known-stable period as a negative control. Drift invalidation is **likely (55-80%)** useful but **UNVERIFIED; E2 is the settling experiment**.

### Production gate

Only after E0–E2 should the hook run in advisory shadow mode. Promotion requires mechanically checked traceability status, calibrated interval coverage, and cost/loss non-inferiority on a time-split holdout. Production benefit is **UNVERIFIED; a shadow A/B using realized downstream outcomes would settle it**.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

All proposals ride existing configuration and hook/skill/MCP-style orchestration buses. They require no second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The runtime addition is one small policy function plus calibration artifacts; offline experiment code can remain evaluation tooling. This satisfies the stated anti-overfit gate by construction.

Do **not** build a metrology daemon, a universal “epistemic twin,” or a new telemetry platform. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that such infrastructure would add more operational surface than this narrow decision requires, because the proposed online computation is a manifest lookup, a small covariance/empirical update, and a three-way policy return; that implementation-size assessment is **UNVERIFIED; a spike implementing Proposal B in under ~200 lines would settle it**. If the spike exceeds that bound because the existing bus lacks needed observations, stop and reassess rather than silently crossing into **BUILD-LARGE**.

## 7. Decision and priority

**Proceed only with E0, then E1; do not deploy yet.** The cross-domain borrow is structurally sound for calibrated redundant-verification tasks because agent workers and instruments share the specific problem of correlated observations plus common-mode uncertainty. It does **not** transfer to universal orchestration because many agent workers are explorers and many tasks lack a stable measurand or trustworthy reference.

If E0 finds stable task-conditioned residual covariance and E1 shows cost reduction at non-inferior loss, Proposal B is **likely (55-80%)** worth a small advisory implementation; that conclusion is **UNVERIFIED until E0/E1 settle it**. If either condition fails, stop: the honest artifact is the calibration manifest and evidence of non-transfer, not a forced controller.

The highest-value conceptual change is to replace “How many agents agree?” with three questions:

1. What exactly is being measured and against which reference?
2. How much independent evidence did these workers add after covariance and systematic floors?
3. Would the next calibrated worker change the decision enough to justify its cost?

Metrology answers the first two structurally. The harness owner must supply the third's consequence and cost function.

## 8. Gaps and evidence boundaries

- I did not inspect the local corpus, defects, audit findings, subject tree, or repository because this unit explicitly forbids duplicating local grounding. Accordingly, this report makes no claim that the current harness lacks or already has any proposed capability.
- I found no metrology standard that directly optimizes agent count, token cost, or sequential decision utility. The stopping rule is an explicit translation combining metrological uncertainty with an extra-metrological cost/loss layer; its performance is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 would settle it**.
- I did not claim an absent capability or successful implementation. All absence-like runtime outcomes (`not_established`, stale calibration) are proposed states, and E0/E2 include known-positive and known-negative controls so detector validity precedes any future absence claim.
- Exact worker residual distributions, reference-task availability, correlations, drift rates, and implementation size are unknown. Each recommendation that depends on them is marked **UNVERIFIED** inline with its settling experiment.
