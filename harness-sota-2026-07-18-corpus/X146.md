# X146 — Cross-domain borrow: cartography → orchestration

**Unit:** X146  
**Lens:** cartographic generalisation, scale-dependent detail, and deliberate omission  
**Target question:** how many parallel workers to run, and when another worker stops helping  
**Research boundary:** external ideation only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation.  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive finding

The useful cartographic borrow is **constraint-led generalisation**, not a literal conversion of map scale into worker count. In cartography, a smaller-scale map has a finite display budget. The cartographer does not merely delete objects uniformly: they select, omit, simplify, aggregate, collapse, exaggerate, symbolise, or displace representations while trying to preserve important characteristics and relationships. Ordnance Survey documents exactly those operations, including grouping nearby amenities into one point, enlarging important features that would otherwise disappear, and aggregating buildings ([OS, “Generalisation”](https://docs.os.uk/os-downloads/products/maps-and-imagery-portfolio/os-vectormap-district/os-vectormap-district-overview/generalisation)). USGS describes the governing purpose as reducing detail enough to remain legible at a target scale while highlighting features whose importance depends on purpose and scale ([USGS, “Generalization”](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)). The International Cartographic Association gives the more formal version: express competing generalisation goals as analytical constraints and find a compromise using optimisation ([ICA Commission on Multi-Scale Cartography, examples](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html)).

Translated to orchestration, the scarce “map sheet” is the coordinator’s integration bandwidth: time, tokens, attention, conflict-resolution capacity, and the number of independently useful evidence channels it can reconcile. Parallel workers are not automatically useful detail. Several workers may be redundant symbols for the same underlying feature; several tiny work packets may need aggregation into one assignment; a high-risk but small subproblem may deserve “exaggeration” into its own worker; and tasks touching the same mutable state may need “displacement” in time rather than concurrent execution.

My central judgment is **very likely (80-90%)** that this control pattern transfers usefully, because three mechanically different external signals line up: operational map-product documentation describes selection/aggregation under a finite representation budget; ICA describes constraint-based compromise rather than uniform thinning; and contemporary agent-scaling experiments report redundancy, coordination overhead, topology effects, and diminishing or negative returns from additional agents ([OS](https://docs.os.uk/os-downloads/products/maps-and-imagery-portfolio/os-vectormap-district/os-vectormap-district-overview/generalisation), [ICA](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html), [Google Research et al., 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). The bounded claim is the transfer of the **decision architecture**—budget, selection, aggregation, invariant preservation, conflict tests, and stop rule—not the transfer of cartography’s geometry or formulae.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that Töpfer and Pillewizer’s radical law should **not** be copied as a worker-count law. Their original principle relates how many occurrences of a feature can be shown at source and derived map scales; it is a quantity guide, not a method for choosing the individual objects ([Töpfer & Pillewizer, 1966](https://doi.org/10.1179/caj.1966.3.1.10)). A later cartographic implementation review makes the limitation explicit: the law gives a selection-algorithm parameter but does not say how selection should be performed ([Savino et al., research review, p. 21](https://kartographie.geo.tu-dresden.de/downloads/ica-gen/publications/savino.pdf)). Agent work has no defensible analogue of a map-scale denominator, and worker outputs are neither fixed-size symbols nor independent geographic objects. The evidence therefore supports borrowing the mechanism’s shape while rejecting its most tempting equation.

## 1. What cartography actually solved

### 1.1 The problem is constrained representation, not indiscriminate deletion

Scale reduction creates a mismatch: a detailed source contains more objects and geometry than the target display can legibly represent. USGS states that smaller-scale maps cover larger areas and therefore require reduction in information and detail; it also says important features vary with map purpose and scale ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)). Ordnance Survey similarly defines generalisation as reducing scale and complexity while maintaining important elements and characteristics ([OS product documentation](https://docs.os.uk/os-downloads/products/maps-and-imagery-portfolio/os-vectormap-district/os-vectormap-district-overview/generalisation)). These are authoritative operational descriptions from national mapping organisations, independent of one another.

That framing matters. A map is not improved by retaining the maximum number of features. It is improved by retaining enough of the right structure for a particular purpose while avoiding illegibility. “More source truth” and “better target representation” are different objectives.

The cartographic operators make this concrete:

| Cartographic operation | What it does in its own field | Orchestration analogue |
|---|---|---|
| Selection / omission | Keeps features appropriate to purpose and target scale; excludes others. | Spawn only assignments likely to add a distinct evidence channel or cover an unowned requirement. |
| Aggregation / amalgamation | Combines several nearby or same-type features into a meaningful larger representation. | Give one worker a coherent cluster of tightly coupled subtasks instead of spawning one worker per bullet. |
| Simplification | Removes geometric variation while retaining a recognisable characteristic form. | Ask a worker for decision-relevant claims, evidence, risks, and deltas rather than its whole reasoning transcript. |
| Collapse / symbolisation | Changes representation dimension, such as an area becoming a point symbol. | Replace a population of agents with a deterministic check, search query, test run, or one typed summary when that representation answers the coordinator’s need. |
| Exaggeration | Enlarges a small but important feature that would otherwise disappear. | Allocate a dedicated verifier to a small, high-impact uncertainty even when its apparent work volume is low. |
| Displacement | Moves a representation to avoid collision while retaining its identity. | Serialize workers that would collide on shared mutable files, external state, or a single scarce tool. |

The cartographic column is supported directly by Ordnance Survey’s operator descriptions ([OS product documentation](https://docs.os.uk/os-downloads/products/maps-and-imagery-portfolio/os-vectormap-district/os-vectormap-district-overview/generalisation)) and its broader explanation that unnecessary information should be weighed against clutter and confusion ([OS, “What is Cartography?”](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/what-is-cartography)). The orchestration column is a translation proposed by this unit, not a claim that cartographers use or validate agent systems.

### 1.2 A quantitative budget is useful, but it does not choose the content

Töpfer and Pillewizer’s “Principles of Selection” is the specific historic mechanism most directly concerned with **how much** to retain. Its basic radical-law form is conventionally written:

\[
n_f = n_a\sqrt{m_a/m_f}
\]

where \(n_a\) is the number of objects at the source scale, \(n_f\) the number at the derived scale, and \(m_a,m_f\) are the respective scale denominators. The original article abstract describes it as an equation relating the count of a feature at source and derived scales and notes correction constants for symbolic form and exaggeration ([Töpfer & Pillewizer, 1966](https://doi.org/10.1179/caj.1966.3.1.10)); an open later application prints the equation and describes it as a quantitative selection target ([Mirzayev & Gökgöz, 2022, pp. 130–131](https://www.scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2022/110648/110648.pdf)).

The important lesson is narrower than the formula: **capacity is an explicit target, distinct from salience**. Töpfer’s law can suggest how many symbols survive, but not which ones. A cartographic system still needs semantic, topological, and perceptual rules. That separation maps well to orchestration:

1. A capacity rule estimates how many concurrent results the coordinator can absorb.
2. A salience rule chooses the most complementary assignments.
3. Preservation constraints check that important task relationships survive.
4. A legibility/conflict test decides whether another representation helps or clutters.

The literal square-root relationship does not map. The abstract separation of capacity from selection does.

### 1.3 Preservation and legibility are competing constraint families

Cartographic generalisation is not allowed to make the output merely sparse. It must preserve important properties. ICA’s example of simultaneous graphic generalisation explicitly formulates requirements as analytical constraints and solves for a compromise among simplification, smoothing, exaggeration, and displacement ([ICA](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html)). A paper on cross-scale building representations distinguishes **legibility constraints** (such as minimum sizes and separations) from **preservation constraints** (topology, position, orientation, shape, pattern, and distribution), and notes that they pull in different directions ([Burghardt, 2009](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC2009/html/nonref/19_7.pdf)).

This is the strongest part of the borrow. An orchestrator likewise has at least two competing families:

- **Integration-legibility constraints:** each worker has a distinct charter; outputs fit a bounded schema; duplicate evidence is limited; merge conflicts are bounded; the coordinator can tell which conclusion came from which evidence.
- **Task-preservation constraints:** every acceptance criterion has an owner; dependencies are respected; disputed claims retain provenance; high-impact risks survive summarisation; no required end-to-end responsibility disappears between worker boundaries.

Maximising parallelism can improve coverage while violating integration legibility. Minimising workers can improve legibility while losing independent verification or leaving parallelizable work uncovered. Cartography’s answer is not a universal feature count; it is explicit constraints and a compromise appropriate to the target use.

### 1.4 Multi-representation is preferable to destroying the master

Cartography can derive multiple scale-appropriate representations from a more detailed model rather than treating one map as universally correct. The ICA’s commissioned book describes generalisation for on-demand mapping driven by user requirements and includes multi-scale outputs and continuous-generalisation data structures ([ICA, *Abstracting Geographic Information in a Data Rich World*](https://generalisation.icaci.org/book.html)). Operationally, USGS also frames research around representations tailored to different mapping and modelling needs ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)).

The orchestration analogue is to keep one canonical task/evidence model and derive different execution views:

- a one-worker overview for a small or sequential task;
- a clustered view for moderately decomposable work;
- a wider parallel view for independently verifiable regions;
- a compact integration view containing claims, evidence, conflicts, and decisions rather than every transcript.

This avoids confusing “more workers” with “more truth.” They are alternate representations of one task, chosen for the coordinator’s current scale and purpose.

## 2. The concrete borrow: a scale-dependent generalisation governor

### Recommendation A — UNVERIFIED: task-map policy

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Add a short orchestration policy that requires a task map before parallel spawning. This is a conceptual configuration proposal; whether the harness exposes the needed configuration point is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: inspect the supported orchestration/prompt configuration surface and run a no-write schema validation against the proposed fields**. Because that premise is unverified, this recommendation is marked UNVERIFIED in its heading.

The task map should contain only:

- `acceptance_regions`: independently checkable requirements or evidence regions;
- `dependencies`: edges that make work sequential;
- `collision_domains`: files, services, or state that cannot be safely edited in parallel;
- `salience`: impact if an item is wrong or omitted;
- `representation`: agent, deterministic tool/test, or coordinator-local work;
- `owner`: exactly one integrating owner per region, even if it has independent verifiers;
- `verification_need`: whether independence is valuable and what would count as an independent signal.

The policy should initially spawn only the **independent frontier**: regions with dependencies satisfied and no collision-domain conflict. It should aggregate tightly coupled regions into one assignment, collapse deterministic regions to tools/tests, and exaggerate high-salience uncertainty into a verifier assignment.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that merely requiring this map will reduce gratuitous fan-out, because it forces the coordinator to state decomposability and collision constraints before allocating workers; the evidence is indirect but aligned with the agent-scaling finding that sequential tasks degraded under all tested multi-agent variants while parallelizable tasks benefited from centralized coordination ([Google Research et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: randomise representative tasks between the existing spawn policy and the task-map policy at fixed model/token budgets, then compare unnecessary-worker rate, collision rate, completion quality, latency, and owner corrections.**

This recommendation rides the existing configuration/orchestration surface and introduces no new runtime, memory database, critic model, or observability service.

### Recommendation B — UNVERIFIED: a generalisation governor on the existing hook/skill bus

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build a small advisory gate around spawn batches and worker completion. The line-size classification is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: implement a spike using the narrow typed state below and count non-generated executable lines plus tests separately**. If the bus cannot expose spawn intent and completion summaries, the proposal must be reclassified or abandoned rather than silently becoming a daemon.

The governor stores ephemeral per-task state only:

```text
regions:       required acceptance/evidence regions
frontier:      regions ready without dependency or collision conflicts
covered:       region IDs supported by evidence
disputed:      region IDs with incompatible claims
channels:      worker charters plus compact claim/evidence fingerprints
budget:        remaining tokens, wall time, calls, and integration slots
```

Before a batch, it recommends a worker count bounded by:

```text
candidate_count = min(
  number of ready non-colliding regions,
  remaining integration slots,
  affordable workers under the fixed budget
)
```

It then ranks candidate assignments by salience-weighted expected **novel coverage**, not raw task count. No fabricated universal coefficients belong in the first version. The governor begins with one small batch, observes compact completion summaries, and expands only while a new batch is likely to contribute a distinguishable evidence channel or resolve a high-salience dispute.

After each batch, it applies cartography-like tests:

- **Selection test:** does the next assignment own a still-uncovered or disputed region?
- **Aggregation test:** are proposed assignments so dependent or overlapping that one coherent worker is a better representation?
- **Collapse test:** can a deterministic command, test, or query answer this region more directly?
- **Exaggeration test:** is a small region sufficiently high impact to justify an independent verifier?
- **Displacement test:** does shared mutable state require serialization?
- **Legibility test:** can the coordinator integrate another result without exceeding its result/token/conflict budget?
- **Preservation test:** would stopping now leave any acceptance region unowned, unverifiable, or severed from a required dependency?

The recommended stop condition is:

```text
stop spawning when every remaining candidate is at least one of:
  dependency-blocked;
  collision-blocked;
  already covered by a sufficiently independent channel;
  cheaper and stronger as a deterministic operation;
  below the calibrated marginal-value threshold;
  unaffordable under the integration budget.
```

“Sufficiently independent” and the marginal-value threshold are **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle them: on a labelled replay suite, sweep evidence-overlap, charter-overlap, and marginal-quality-per-cost thresholds, selecting values on a training split and reporting quality/cost and correction-rate curves on a held-out split**. Until calibrated, the safe implementation is advisory and records the reason; it must not hard-block a coordinator from covering a required region.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that distinct-channel count is more informative than raw worker count on homogeneous fan-out, because a 2026 scaling study reports sharply diminishing returns for homogeneous agents and associates continued gains with heterogeneous models, prompts, or tool access ([Li et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). It is only **likely (55-80%)** that a cheap text/evidence fingerprint can estimate distinct channels well enough for online gating, because the paper’s effective-channel proxy was evaluated in controlled benchmark settings rather than this owner’s workflow. **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: compare fingerprint-estimated novelty with blind human labels of whether each added worker changed the final supported conclusion, then measure precision/recall and calibration error.**

### Recommendation C — UNVERIFIED: preserve a multi-scale integration record

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Require each worker to return a small integration record rather than treating its full transcript as the representation consumed by the coordinator:

```yaml
charter: <region and non-goals>
claims:
  - id: <stable local ID>
    statement: <decision-relevant claim>
    evidence: <URL, path:line, or command/output reference>
    confidence: <required band where uncertain>
conflicts: [<claim IDs or state collisions>]
coverage: [<acceptance-region IDs>]
residuals: [<unresolved region IDs>]
artifact: <optional full-detail location>
```

This is the orchestration equivalent of a scale-appropriate cartographic representation: the detailed artifact remains available, but the coordinator sees a simplified, provenance-preserving view. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce integration overload without losing decision-relevant evidence, because cartography’s operational logic and the agent literature both identify finite representation/coordination budgets; however, the proposed schema itself is not externally validated. **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: compare blind final-answer quality and evidence traceability when coordinators receive full transcripts versus these records plus on-demand artifact access under the same context budget.**

The implementation-size and bus-compatibility premises are **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle them: build a throwaway serializer/parser spike against the supported completion payload, measure executable lines, schema failure rate, and context savings, and abandon the proposal if it requires a separate storage or orchestration service**.

## 3. What this would catch that count-only orchestration misses

This unit was explicitly instructed not to ground in the local repository, so it cannot honestly claim that the current owner harness lacks these checks. The following are **failure classes inherently invisible to a policy that looks only at raw worker count or available concurrency**. Whether the current harness already catches them is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: replay one labelled task for each class through the current orchestrator and inspect spawn decisions, completion metadata, and final integration independently**.

1. **Correlated redundancy.** Four workers can be one effective channel if they use the same model, prompt, sources, and reasoning route. Raw count says four; a generalisation governor sees repeated representation of one feature. This concern is supported by the reported diminishing returns of homogeneous agents ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)).

2. **False decomposition.** A task may contain many bullets but have a dependency width of one. Counting subtasks suggests fan-out; the task map shows a sequential chain. In the 2025 scaling study, all tested multi-agent variants degraded sequential reasoning by 39–70%, while centralized coordination helped parallelizable financial reasoning ([Google Research et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). The exact percentages are properties of those experiments, not universal constants.

3. **Integration collision.** Workers can be individually correct yet produce mutually incompatible edits, definitions, or assumptions. The displacement analogue catches shared collision domains before spawning rather than treating post-hoc merge pain as an execution accident.

4. **Tiny but decisive uncertainty.** Work-volume partitioning can omit a small premise whose failure invalidates everything. Exaggeration assigns it a dedicated verifier based on salience rather than size.

5. **Agent-shaped work that should collapse to a tool.** A deterministic query or test can be a more legible representation than another prose-producing worker. Counting only free worker slots never asks whether an agent is the appropriate symbol type.

6. **Coverage loss through simplification.** A coordinator can stop because many results arrived even though one acceptance criterion has no owner. Preservation constraints inspect region coverage, not activity volume.

7. **Unintegrable abundance.** Additional workers may improve local coverage while exceeding coordinator bandwidth, producing a worse final result. A legibility budget makes integration capacity a first-class constraint.

8. **Error propagation topology.** Independent workers are not automatically independent verification if their outputs feed one another unchecked. The agent-scaling study reports topology-dependent error amplification, with a much larger measured factor for independent than centralized configurations ([Google Research et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). The governor therefore records dependency and claim provenance rather than equating fan-out with safety.

9. **Premature uniform omission.** A hard cap may drop every “extra” assignment equally. Cartography instead keeps salient landmarks and aggregates low-level detail. The analogous governor preserves high-impact verification even while suppressing low-novelty workers.

10. **The wrong stop signal.** “All slots occupied,” “all proposed workers launched,” and “N workers completed” say nothing about marginal information gain. The proposed stop rule asks whether uncovered, disputed, affordable, non-colliding regions remain.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy fails

### 4.1 There is no agent equivalent of physical map scale

A scale denominator has a stable geometric meaning. “Task scale” does not. Token count, number of files, number of acceptance criteria, graph width, uncertainty, and risk are different variables; none is a universal denominator. Therefore a formula such as \(N_{workers}=N_{tasks}\sqrt{s_1/s_2}\) would be decorative mathematics.

This disanalogy is decisive against the literal radical-law transfer. I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that a universal cartography-derived worker exponent would fail across task classes, because agent studies already find task type, single-agent capability, tool intensity, topology, and diversity change the sign and size of scaling returns ([Google Research et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296), [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). **UNVERIFIED for this owner’s workload; experiment to settle it: fit candidate exponents on stratified historical tasks and test cross-task-family calibration on a held-out temporal split.**

### 4.2 Map objects pre-exist; useful agent decomposition is partly discovered

Cartographers usually start with identifiable features and relationships. An agent may discover that the initial problem partition is wrong only after research begins. Worker assignments can create new questions, invalidate dependencies, or change salience. The task map must therefore be revisable; a one-time static selection step would overfit the analogy.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a batchwise revise-and-expand policy will outperform a one-shot spawn count on heterogeneous research tasks, because information gained during early work can change the frontier, while the cited agent studies show performance depends on task structure and redundancy. **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: compare one-shot and batch-adaptive policies at identical total call/token caps on tasks with hidden dependency changes.**

### 4.3 Omitted map detail remains true; omitted reasoning may contain the answer

A small road left off a regional map still exists and may be available in the source database. An omitted hypothesis or failed approach may never be rediscovered. Omission in orchestration is epistemically riskier than omission in display. That is why the governor must omit **worker instantiations**, not erase source requirements or discovered evidence. It must preserve residuals and allow zoom-in/on-demand expansion.

### 4.4 Geographic proximity is meaningful; textual similarity may be deceptive

Nearby buildings are natural aggregation candidates. Two subtasks with similar words may require independent methods, while differently worded tasks may collide on the same file or premise. Semantic clustering is therefore only a proposal generator. Dependency, collision, evidence, and acceptance-region checks must make the final decision.

### 4.5 Cartographic quality is perceptual; orchestration quality is outcome-dependent

Minimum symbol sizes and visual coalescence can sometimes be measured without knowing an external truth. Whether another worker improves a novel software task may be observable only after tests, review, or deployment. USGS itself notes that generalisation research has often focused on representation and overlooked use in downstream applications ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)). The governor cannot rely solely on attractive process metrics such as low overlap or clean summaries; held-out outcome quality must calibrate it.

### 4.6 Workers act and mutate; map symbols do not

Parallel workers may edit files, call services, consume rate limits, or create irreversible external effects. This makes “displacement” more than a legibility concern: it is a correctness and safety constraint. The analogy understates the hazard. Collision-domain checks and authority boundaries must remain native orchestration rules, not be inferred from cartographic metaphors.

### 4.7 Deliberate distortion is acceptable in maps more often than in evidence

Cartography may displace or exaggerate features to retain legibility. An evidence harness must never move a citation, inflate confidence, or blur provenance. The transferable operation is allocation emphasis—give a high-risk item more verification—not distortion of the reported fact.

### 4.8 Verdict on structural transfer

The analogy **does transfer** at the level of a bounded control loop:

```text
detailed task model
    → choose a target integration budget and purpose
    → select / aggregate / collapse / emphasize / serialize work
    → preserve acceptance and provenance invariants
    → test coverage, conflict, and legibility
    → zoom in only where residual value justifies it
```

It **does not transfer** as a numeric law, geometric clustering method, or license to distort evidence. The bounded transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to be productive because independent cartographic and agent-scaling evidence supports every structural element except the online novelty estimator; that estimator remains the major empirical risk. The exact performance benefit is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: the controlled evaluation in Section 6**.

## 5. Anti-overfit gate

The proposal passes the specified anti-overfit gate by design:

- It rides the existing configuration and hook/skill/orchestration bus.
- It uses ephemeral per-task state, compact typed records, and deterministic metrics.
- It does not add a second orchestration runtime.
- It does not add a vector database or knowledge graph.
- It does not add a durable-execution engine.
- It does not add a trained critic.
- It does not add observability SaaS.
- It does not add a guardrail framework.
- It starts advisory, with a small configuration policy and at most a small bus component.

The strongest competing design would be a learned scheduler trained on historical tasks. I reject it for this unit: it is a trained routing/critic component, needs stable labels under changing models, risks encoding yesterday’s optimum, and violates the minimal-architecture bias. A fixed global worker cap is also rejected as the sole control because it cannot represent dependency width, collision, diversity, or integration budget. A separate distributed workflow engine is rejected because the problem is a spawn decision on an existing bus, not durable execution.

The small-build classification is **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: complete the implementation spikes named under Recommendations B and C**. If either exceeds the boundary because existing bus events are insufficient, the correct result is to keep only the CONFIG policy or stop, not expand into BUILD-LARGE.

## 6. Falsification and calibration plan

### 6.1 Hypotheses

H1: At fixed compute budget, task-map plus adaptive generalisation yields higher final quality per cost than a fixed-count policy — **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: the stratified, fixed-budget multi-arm evaluation in Sections 6.2–6.5**.

H2: Distinct evidence-channel count predicts marginal benefit better than raw worker count — **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: compare their held-out prediction errors for marginal quality gain in the evaluation in Sections 6.2–6.5**.

H3: Explicit collision and dependency constraints reduce merge/rework failures without harming coverage — **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: compare the full governor with the preservation-constraint ablation in Sections 6.3–6.5**.

H4: Compact integration records preserve traceability and final quality while reducing coordinator context consumption — **UNVERIFIED; experiment to settle it: randomise full transcripts versus compact records plus on-demand artifact access and score the outcomes in Section 6.4**.

All four are **UNVERIFIED; the experiment below settles them**. Before results, they must not be stated as harness facts.

### 6.2 Dataset and controls

Construct a temporally held-out task suite stratified by:

- sequential versus parallelizable dependency topology;
- tool-heavy versus reasoning-heavy execution;
- low versus high single-worker baseline success;
- homogeneous versus deliberately diverse useful methods;
- read-only research versus shared-state editing;
- small versus large acceptance-region count.

Use detector controls before reporting an absence: seed at least one known dependency, one known collision domain, one known duplicate worker charter, and one known uncovered acceptance region. The detector must find each known-present seed before its negative outputs count. For any claim that a failure class is absent or fixed, require two mechanically independent signals—for example, the structured event field plus an independent artifact/test result, not the same parser rerun.

### 6.3 Arms

At the same model family and total token/call ceiling, randomise tasks among:

1. one worker;
2. fixed 2 workers;
3. fixed 4 workers;
4. fixed 8 workers where capacity permits;
5. one-shot task-map count;
6. adaptive cartographic governor;
7. adaptive governor with novelty gating ablated;
8. adaptive governor with preservation constraints ablated.

The ablations are essential. Without them, an improvement could come from better prompting rather than the borrowed mechanism.

### 6.4 Outcomes

Primary outcomes:

- blind task-quality score against acceptance criteria;
- verified defect/correction rate;
- end-to-end completion rate;
- wall time and total model/tool cost;
- unsupported-claim rate;
- destructive or conflicting mutation rate for editing tasks.

Mechanism outcomes:

- marginal quality gain per added worker;
- novel supported claim/evidence contribution per worker;
- charter and evidence overlap;
- uncovered acceptance regions at integration time;
- coordinator context consumed per accepted claim;
- merge conflicts and reconciliation turns;
- number of workers collapsed to deterministic operations;
- stop-decision false positives (stopped while a useful affordable channel remained) and false negatives (spawned a worker with no useful marginal contribution).

### 6.5 Decision rule

Adopt the governor only if it improves the Pareto frontier of blind quality, correction rate, latency, and cost on the held-out split, not merely one aggregate score. Require no material regression on high-salience safety/correctness tasks. Report results by topology stratum; a policy that helps parallel research but harms sequential edits should route between policies rather than average the difference away.

I am **likely (55-80%)** to expect the best policy to be conditional rather than a single worker count, because current controlled studies report task- and topology-dependent signs of multi-agent benefit ([Google Research et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)) and diminishing returns conditioned on channel diversity ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794)). **UNVERIFIED; this multi-arm held-out experiment is the settling test.**

## 8. Dead ends and rejected translations

### Dead end 1: “Radical law for agents”

Rejected. There is no stable target/source scale ratio for cognitive work. Fitting an exponent would produce a workload-specific empirical scheduler, not a cartographic law. The cartographic source itself separates a count target from semantic selection.

### Dead end 2: spawn one worker per map feature / requirement

Rejected. Requirements can be coupled, sequential, or collide on state. Cartographic aggregation is the better lesson: representation units need not equal source-object units.

### Dead end 3: head/tail omission solely by task size

Jiang et al. propose retaining the “head” and eliminating or aggregating the “tail” of heavy-tailed geographic distributions ([Jiang, Liu & Jia, 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.1561)). This is interesting for prioritisation but unsafe as a direct orchestration rule: task-size distributions need not be heavy-tailed, and a tiny premise can have enormous causal impact. Salience must include impact and uncertainty, not frequency or apparent size alone.

### Dead end 4: visual similarity as task proximity

Rejected. Text embeddings or lexical overlap can miss shared mutable state and can collapse genuinely independent verification. Evidence and dependency relations matter more than surface similarity. A vector database is neither needed nor allowed by the anti-overfit gate.

### Dead end 5: maximise “map clarity” by suppressing disagreement

Rejected. Disagreement is clutter on a display but valuable evidence in research. Conflicts should be made legible and routed for resolution, not averaged away.

### Dead end 6: a new autonomous scheduling service

Rejected. The useful proposal is a small policy/gate on an existing bus. A daemon would create a second orchestration runtime and fail the anti-overfit gate without evidence that a small advisory mechanism is insufficient.

## 10. Bottom line for the neighbouring build units

Borrow **the generalisation loop**, not the square root:

1. Model the detailed task and its dependencies.
2. Treat coordinator integration capacity as the target representation budget.
3. Select distinct ready regions; aggregate coupled work; collapse deterministic work; exaggerate high-impact uncertainty; displace colliding mutations.
4. Preserve acceptance coverage, provenance, dependency, and independent-verification constraints.
5. Expand in batches and stop on calibrated marginal evidence value, not on a fixed worker count.
6. Keep the first version advisory and small; prove benefit on held-out tasks before enforcement.

This is ambitious in decision quality but deliberately minimal in machinery. It opens a new orchestration control surface—scale-dependent representation—without importing a second runtime or pretending that geographic scale is cognitive scale.
