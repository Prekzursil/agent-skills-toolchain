# X034 — Aviation-safety → observability when there is no baseline

**Unit:** X034  
**Lens:** cross-domain borrow from aviation safety  
**Target:** determine whether an agent-harness change helped when no trustworthy baseline exists  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external research and design only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness or repository.

## Executive result

The useful aviation borrow is **not a checklist, a post-mortem template, or an incident counter**. It is the coupled assurance pattern formed by:

1. **LOSA (Line Operations Safety Audit/Assessment):** a time-bounded, representative sample of *normal work*, observed through a stable Threat and Error Management (TEM) taxonomy, including threats, errors, recoveries, and undesired states;
2. **FOQA (Flight Operational Quality Assurance):** routine capture of operational parameters, validated event definitions, exposure denominators, distributions, and trends—not just catastrophes; and
3. **ASRS (Aviation Safety Reporting System):** protected, de-identified narratives that reveal context, surprises, workarounds, and near misses the recorder cannot explain.

This pattern transfers, but only with a hard qualification: **aviation has not solved causal inference in the complete absence of a baseline or counterfactual.** It solves the practical precursor: it creates a trustworthy baseline from normal operations, preserves definitions and denominators, and triangulates automatic traces with structured observation and narratives. The FAA explicitly describes LOSA as producing baseline and outcome data and says a follow-up LOSA shows whether organizational changes reduced threats, errors, or undesired states ([AC 120-90, pp. 3–4](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AC-120-90%2C%20LOSA.pdf)). FOQA likewise says that after a baseline is established, distributions can be monitored for trends ([AC 120-82, p. 9](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-82.pdf)). Neither source licenses “it looks better afterward, therefore the change helped.”

My recommendation is therefore a small **Harness Operational Assurance Loop**: declare the change and its predicted signals before activation; run its detectors in shadow to build a baseline; record every eligible session with versioned event definitions and exposure denominators; preserve a deterministic concurrent control or paired replay when feasible; add a sampled normal-operations audit; and solicit a short, protected surprise narrative. A static change card then reports effect, uncertainty, regressions, detector health, and evidence gaps.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this will prevent the most important observability error—declaring victory from incomparable before/after counts—because LOSA/FOQA explicitly require normal-operation baselines, validation, and trendable event definitions, while the proposed card refuses an effect claim without comparable exposure. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to attribute modest improvements to a particular harness change in live, heterogeneous agent work, because task mix, model drift, external-service drift, owner behavior, and stochastic generation remain confounders; the settling experiment is a pre-registered, session-level randomized or stepped rollout with enough eligible sessions to estimate the chosen primary metric.

## 1. The aviation mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 LOSA: observe ordinary operations, including successful recovery

Accidents are too rare and too selected to describe everyday operating risk. LOSA addresses that problem by sending trained observers into normal line operations and coding what crews actually encounter and do. Its unit of learning is not merely “accident/no accident.” The observer records:

- **threat prevalence** (weather, congestion, time pressure, dispatch problems, equipment issues);
- whether crews **manage threats effectively**;
- **error prevalence** and error management from diagnosis through response and outcome;
- **undesired aircraft states**, which are precursors rather than necessarily incidents; and
- organizational safeguards that succeed as well as those that fail.

The FAA’s TEM description says these observations quantify the working environment, threat management, errors, error management, systemic weaknesses, and strengths in safeguards ([AC 120-90, Appendix 1, pp. 19–20](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AC-120-90%2C%20LOSA.pdf)). The FAA’s history of LOSA emphasizes the unusual part: it captures responses to errors that never become incidents and therefore learns from effective recovery during normal flights, not only from bad outcomes ([FAA LOSA history](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/maintenance_hf/losa/history)).

LOSA is deliberately designed to measure *typical* rather than inspection-day performance:

- observations are peer-to-peer, voluntary, confidential, and non-punitive;
- the observer is a non-intervening data collector, except for immediate danger;
- crews can decline;
- observers are trained and standardized on the form and taxonomy;
- observations are sampled across fleets and operations;
- the form combines categorical codes with a context-rich narrative;
- local experts validate coded events against actual procedures; and
- a later LOSA repeats the measurement after an intervention.

These are not ornamental “safety culture” details. They are measurement controls. The FAA notes that people naturally “work to the rules” for auditors and explains why peer observers are intended to reduce that distortion ([FAA LOSA FAQ](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/maintenance_hf/losa/faq)). AC 120-90 recommends 50 or more observations per fleet as a rule of thumb, scripted practice and follow-up coaching for observers, a concentrated one-to-three-month observation window, secure data handling, and independent expert verification of coded events ([AC 120-90, pp. 7–10](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AC-120-90%2C%20LOSA.pdf)). It also recommends 10–20% external observers as a “control group” because insiders can become blind to systemic threats ([AC 120-90, p. 5](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AC-120-90%2C%20LOSA.pdf)).

Most directly for this unit, the FAA states that LOSA provides baseline and outcome measurement data for organizational interventions, and that a follow-up LOSA tests whether changes reduced threats, errors, or undesired states ([AC 120-90, pp. 3–4](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AC-120-90%2C%20LOSA.pdf)). Thus LOSA’s answer to “there is no baseline” is not statistical alchemy. It is: **measure representative normal work now, using a stable instrument, before claiming the next change helped.**

### 1.2 FOQA: a flight recorder becomes an assurance program only after validation and normalization

FOQA routinely collects operational flight data and combines it with other sources and operational experience. The FAA defines an event set as a set of events intended to measure normal operations for an aircraft type and operator, and defines event validation as deciding whether an apparent parameter exceedance is a genuine sample outside the established norm ([AC 120-82, pp. 2–3](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-82.pdf)). That distinction matters: a deviation detector firing does not itself prove an unsafe event.

FOQA uses two complementary views:

1. **Exceedance analysis:** pre-set thresholds and severity levels, trended per flight segment and stratified by phase, airport, runway, or aircraft type.
2. **Distribution analysis:** profiles using all flights, not only threshold crossings, to reveal shifts in means, tails, and variation.

The FAA describes both approaches, including the explicit denominator “per flight segment,” risk-tiered thresholds, full-flight distributions, and baseline-based trend monitoring ([AC 120-82, pp. 8–9](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-82.pdf)). It further requires procedures for manual and automated data-quality checks, invalid-data handling, event categories and severity, validating/refining/tracking event-definition changes, periodic trend review, corrective-action follow-up, retention, and revision control ([AC 120-82, pp. 27–29](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-82.pdf)).

In its own field, then, FOQA is not “log everything and draw a dashboard.” It is a controlled measurement contract:

`operational exposure → versioned parameters → validated event → normalized trend → reviewed action → follow-up`

The important observability lesson is that the detector and its definition are part of the measured system. If a threshold, parser, data source, or coverage rate changes, the series is not automatically comparable.

### 1.3 ASRS: protected narratives fill the recorder’s semantic blind spots

ASRS is voluntary, confidential, non-punitive, de-identified, and administered by NASA as a third party. FAA guidance explains that NASA’s independence and anonymity protections are intended to increase the information flow needed to evaluate the system; reports are analyzed and de-identified, time-critical information can be referred after de-identification, and reports generally cannot be used for enforcement ([NASA ASRS / AC 00-46F](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/immunity.html)).

The protection changes the measurement surface. People can report:

- what they expected the automation to do;
- what it actually appeared to do;
- uncertainty, workload, communication failures, and workarounds;
- near misses and recoveries; and
- ideas for corrective action.

But ASRS carries a decisive caveat. NASA says reports are not verified, reflect a reporter’s perception, and cannot establish prevalence ([About ASRS Data](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/search/dbol/aboutdata.html)). Voluntary reports are not a random sample; reporting propensity varies, so counts cannot be treated as an incident rate ([ASRS caveat on quantitative use](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/docs/rpsts/ctlr.pdf)). The proper use is qualitative explanation and hypothesis generation, paired with a measured denominator from FOQA/LOSA—not a chart of complaint counts labeled “quality.”

### 1.4 Automation surprise: instrument the gap between expected and actual mode

Aviation automation research adds a specific event type that ordinary error logs miss: **automation surprise**. NASA’s summary of A-320 research identifies lack of mode awareness—current and future automation status and behavior—as a major source of surprises, and interprets those surprises as a mismatch between human and machine properties and capabilities ([NASA Technical Reports Server, Sarter et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040112190)). A NASA report describes two common structures: an input’s effect depends on the active mode, or the operator has an incorrect mental model of the current mode ([NASA report NPS-OR-04-008, p. 30](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050061321/downloads/20050061321.pdf?attachment=true)).

The transferable measurement is not merely “automation failed.” It is the tuple:

`declared/visible mode + operator expectation + automation action + mode transition + recovery`

An agent harness can be technically correct while surprising the owner: a hook blocks in one context but not another, an agent delegates when the owner expected direct execution, a fallback silently changes providers, or a rule stops applying after compaction. Success/error counters miss this mismatch.

## 2. Structural transfer test

### 2.1 Why the analogy transfers

| Structural property | Aviation operation | Agent-harness operation | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rare terminal failures | Crashes are rare; precursor states are more frequent | Catastrophic loss/corruption is rare; retries, corrections, abandonment, and silent wrongness are more frequent | Strong transfer: measure precursor chains and recovery |
| Heterogeneous exposure | Routes, weather, airports, fleets, phases | Task types, repos, tool mix, model/provider, permissions, session length | Strong transfer: raw counts are incomparable without denominators and strata |
| Human adapts to automation | Crews develop workarounds and mental models | Owner adapts prompts, avoids broken tools, compensates for rules | Strong transfer: “fewer errors” can mean avoidance, not improvement |
| Automation has modes | Autoflight behavior depends on mode | hooks, rules, skills, MCP availability, subagent state, compaction, permission mode | Strong transfer: expected-versus-actual mode is observable in principle |
| Logs lack context | Parameters show what happened, not necessarily why | tool traces show calls/errors, not intent, surprise, or task success | Strong transfer: combine traces with narratives |
| Measurement can change behavior | Formal checks evoke “by the book” performance | eval prompts and known benchmarks evoke optimized behavior | Strong transfer: sample normal work and separate observation from evaluation |
| Safety is a system property | Threat management spans crew, procedures, aircraft, ATC | outcome spans owner, model, prompt, rules, tools, services, filesystem | Strong transfer: do not blame the last component in the chain |

The mapping is **very likely (80-90%)** to remain useful across newer models because it is about measurement under heterogeneous, adaptive operations rather than a specific model defect; evidence is the same structural dependence on exposure, operational modes, recovery, and mixed automatic/human evidence. The residual uncertainty is whether future agents make task outcomes sufficiently self-verifying to reduce the need for human narratives; the settling experiment is to compare adjudicated failure recall with and without the narrative channel on a future-model session sample.

### 2.2 Where the analogy breaks

The analogy is not a license to import aviation bureaucracy wholesale.

1. **Physical dynamics versus semantic work.** Aircraft parameters have stable units and certified envelopes. “Correct implementation,” “useful research,” and “owner intent satisfied” are semantic and task-dependent. A harness has no universal equivalent of airspeed or bank angle. Therefore most useful measures must be explicitly tied to task classes and owner outcomes.

2. **Scale and repetition.** Airlines produce many comparable flight segments. One owner may perform a small number of highly diverse tasks. LOSA’s published 50-observation-per-fleet heuristic cannot be copied as “50 sessions per harness change”; the relevant effective sample may be far smaller or larger depending on metric noise and task strata.

3. **Independence of units.** Flights are reasonably separable exposure units. Agent sessions reuse repositories, evolving external state, cached knowledge, and owner learning. A later session is not exchangeable with an earlier one.

4. **Hazard versus utility.** Aviation prioritizes reducing safety risk under strict operating constraints. A harness optimizes a vector: correctness, completion, time, cost, autonomy, reversibility, and owner experience. A change can reduce retry rate by refusing difficult work, which is “safer” but worse.

5. **Observer identity.** A peer pilot can understand latent context independently of the flight crew. An LLM “observer” may share the same blind spots as the acting model, and its judgment is correlated rather than independent.

6. **Reporting incentives.** ASRS’s legal and employment protections address consequences borne by human reporters. An agent has no analogous personal jeopardy. The relevant reporter is the owner, whose costs are interruption, friction, privacy exposure, and fear that candid correction data will be used to add more obstructive rules.

7. **Causal attribution.** LOSA and FOQA support trend measurement; they do not automatically distinguish a harness effect from a different task mix, model release, provider outage, or owner learning. A concurrent control, randomized rollout, reversal, or credible paired replay is still required for causal language.

Conclusion of the transfer test: **the assurance architecture transfers; aviation-specific thresholds, staffing, sample-size heuristics, and authority structures do not.** It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that copying checklist compliance or incident-report counts alone would fail the target problem, because neither supplies a comparable baseline or counterfactual and NASA expressly prohibits prevalence inference from ASRS counts. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the coupled LOSA–FOQA–ASRS pattern is a better transfer, because each channel covers a documented blind spot of the other two.

## 3. Concrete translation: Harness Operational Assurance Loop

### Design rule

No harness change may be labeled “helped” merely because a post-change metric is low or a handful of anecdotes are positive. A result may instead be labeled:

- **instrumented, baseline pending**;
- **directional signal only**;
- **associated improvement** (comparable baseline, residual confounding);
- **causal evidence** (pre-registered concurrent control, randomized/stepped rollout, or a defensible reversal); or
- **no conclusion** (coverage, detector, denominator, or comparability failure).

This language is the direct operational guard against manufacturing certainty from missing history.

### Proposal A — Change flight plan and invariant event contract

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Before enabling a change, create one small versioned `change-card.json` with:

```json
{
  "change_id": "...",
  "hypothesis": "...",
  "eligible_sessions": "explicit predicate",
  "activation": {"start": "...", "treatment_rule": "..."},
  "primary_metric": {"name": "...", "unit": "...", "denominator": "..."},
  "guardrails": [{"name": "...", "direction": "must_not_worsen"}],
  "event_contract_version": "...",
  "minimum_coverage": "...",
  "decision_rule": "...",
  "confounders": ["model", "task_class", "tool/provider", "session_length"],
  "rollback_trigger": "..."
}
```

The primary metric must measure opportunity, not just events. Examples: retry loops per failed-call opportunity; owner corrections per completed task; unmatched-quote failures per shell call containing shell metacharacters; premature “done” declarations per adjudicated completion claim. Completion rate, abandonment, latency, and owner intervention are guardrails so that suppressing work cannot masquerade as reliability.

Freeze the event contract across baseline and treatment. If parsing or taxonomy changes, increment the version and either backfill both periods using the same raw traces or break the series. This directly borrows FOQA’s event-definition validation, modification tracking, and revision control.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this card will catch denominator tricks and post-hoc metric selection because eligibility, outcome, guardrails, and decision rule exist before the treatment data. Evidence is FOQA’s explicit use of events per flight segment plus tracked event definitions. It is **likely (55-80%)** to remain lightweight enough for routine use if generated from a short template; the settling experiment is adoption across ten consecutive proposed changes, measuring completion time and omitted required fields.

### Proposal B — Shadow baseline recorder with a positive-control heartbeat

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Run the detector and aggregation logic *before* activating the intervention. A post-tool or session-end hook appends compact local JSONL records:

```text
session_id_hash, change_id, treatment, task_class, model_id,
event_contract_version, eligible_opportunities, events_by_severity,
recovered_events, completion_status, owner_interventions,
detector_coverage, positive_control_seen, timestamp
```

The hook records only derived events and hashed correlation identifiers by default; raw prompts, secrets, and file contents remain out of the assurance log. Each session injects or observes a harmless known-present sentinel event through a separate code path. Reporting is disabled if either (a) expected telemetry coverage is below the card’s floor or (b) the positive control is absent. A second mechanically independent signal—such as session-end reconciliation of tool-call totals against the post-tool stream—must agree before the report claims the recorder worked.

This is the harness equivalent of FOQA’s automatic and manual data-integrity checks, invalid-data handling, and event validation. “Zero detected errors” becomes distinguishable from “the detector saw nothing.”

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** to catch the specific class “silent detector outage presented as zero incidents,” because a missing known-present control and an independently reconciled call count explicitly invalidate the report. It is **likely (55-80%)** to build a useful baseline within a practical window for high-frequency tool events, but **unlikely (20-45%)** to do so quickly for rare semantic failures; evidence is the difference between routine telemetry opportunities and sparse owner-adjudicated outcomes. The settling experiment is a 30-session shadow run with seeded detector dropouts and pre-labeled semantic failures.

### Proposal C — Normal-operations sample (Harness-LOSA)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For a stratified sample of ordinary sessions—not benchmark sessions and not only failures—run a short end-of-session audit skill over the trace. Use a stable TEM-inspired form:

```text
CONTEXT: task class, novelty, external dependencies, time pressure
THREAT: condition increasing difficulty but not caused by the active agent action
ERROR: action/omission diverging from intent or procedure
MANAGEMENT: detected? by whom? recovery action? recovery cost?
UNDESIRED STATE: wrong artifact, blocked task, lost work, unsafe mutation, false completion
SAFEGUARD: which rule/tool/human intervention trapped the chain?
WORKAROUND: owner/agent behavior that avoided or masked the hazard
NARRATIVE: brief causal context and alternate explanations
```

Sampling must cover both success and failure, control and treatment, short and long sessions, and major task classes. The acting agent must not score itself as the sole judge. Where a human review is too expensive, two differently constructed signals should be retained: deterministic trace coding plus an independent blinded review of a small subset. Agreement and disagreement are reported, not averaged away.

This audit catches what error counters miss: a threat handled cleanly; an error recovered before a tool failure; a rule obeyed but the owner’s intent missed; success achieved through a costly workaround; a decrease in errors caused by avoiding a capability; and safeguards that prevented propagation.

It is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal compensating owner behavior and hidden recovery cost that automatic counters miss, because LOSA’s documented advantage is observing normal responses and workarounds. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that an LLM-based reviewer will code semantic threats reliably without periodic human calibration, because reviewer and actor may share failure modes. **UNVERIFIED — settle with a blinded study of at least two independent reviewers and owner adjudication on a stratified session sample, reporting per-category agreement and recall.**

### Proposal D — Preserve a counterfactual: concurrent canary first, replay second

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

When the intervention can be gated, assign eligible sessions deterministically to control or treatment using a salted hash of the session identifier. Stratify or block on declared task class and model. Keep assignment stable within a session. Roll out progressively only after the guardrails remain acceptable. For high-risk fixes, use a stepped rollout so all sessions eventually receive the treatment while the delayed cohorts provide temporary contemporaneous controls.

When live control is impossible, replay immutable historical task fixtures in isolated disposable worktrees against both configurations. Pair on the same task, inputs, model version, permissions, and external-service mocks; run repeated trials where generation is stochastic. Treat replay as mechanism evidence, not proof of live-owner benefit.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a concurrent canary will reduce bias from model and provider drift compared with a simple chronological before/after, because both variants experience the same calendar period. It is **likely (55-80%)** to be practical for rules, skills, and hook behavior that can be gated at session start; **UNVERIFIED — settle by implementing one reversible rule change under session-level assignment and checking for treatment leakage and sample balance.** It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that replay alone will estimate owner-facing benefit on open-ended work, because repositories, services, stochastic choices, and human adaptation are not faithfully replayed; evidence is the structural disanalogy between stable flight parameters and evolving semantic tasks.

Do not randomize a treatment when withholding it creates a material risk of destructive action or data loss. For such changes, use shadow detection, sandboxed replay, and staged rollout with immediate safety guards active for everyone.

### Proposal E — Protected “automation surprise” micro-report

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

At session end—or immediately after an owner correction—offer one optional four-field record:

1. What did you expect the harness/agent to do?
2. What did it do instead?
3. Which visible mode or rule did you believe was active?
4. What recovered the situation, and at what cost?

Store the narrative separately from quantitative event counts; de-identify paths, people, and secrets before aggregation. Never treat report volume as a rate. Link a report to the hashed session only long enough to join it with detector data, then apply a short retention rule. Reports are hypothesis generators and semantic explanations.

This catches silent mode confusion: an invisible fallback, an unexpected permission boundary, rules applied in one context but not another, agent handoff surprise, or a “successful” completion that violated the owner’s expected process.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that these reports will expose high-value semantic failure modes absent from traces, based on ASRS’s role in collecting contextual near-miss narratives and NASA’s automation-surprise findings. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that users will complete the prompt consistently if it appears after every session; **UNVERIFIED — settle with an A/B test of event-triggered versus always-on prompting, comparing response rate, reported burden, and unique actionable themes.**

### Proposal F — Assurance report, not dashboard theater

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Generate a local Markdown report from the JSONL and change card. No daemon and no observability SaaS. Each report includes:

- eligibility and exposure in control/baseline and treatment;
- detector coverage and both health signals;
- event-contract version and any definition changes;
- primary effect with an uncertainty interval, not only a point estimate;
- full distributions or quantiles for cost/latency, not only averages;
- severity and phase/task-class strata;
- recovery rate and recovery cost;
- completion, abandonment, owner intervention, and capability-use guardrails;
- qualitative surprise themes clearly separated from rates;
- missingness and known confounders;
- decision: expand, hold, revise, rollback, or no conclusion;
- a falsification note: “What observation would show this story is wrong?”

If no baseline or control exists for a change already shipped, the report must say **“effect unknown; establishing prospective baseline from this date.”** It may compare mechanism-level paired replays or external cohorts, but it must not retrofit a causal claim.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a forced “no conclusion” state will reduce false success declarations, because it makes detector health, exposure, and comparability prerequisites rather than footnotes. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that confidence intervals will be stable for low-volume task strata; the settling experiment is prospective power/simulation using observed event rates and within-stratum variance after the shadow period.

## 4. What this would catch that ordinary harness telemetry misses

| Currently easy-to-miss pattern | Aviation-derived signal | Harness evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Error count falls because the owner avoids the broken capability | LOSA workaround + FOQA exposure denominator | eligible opportunities and capability use fall while completion/corrections do not improve |
| Hook/detector stops firing, yielding a perfect zero | FOQA data-integrity validation | positive control absent and end-of-session reconciliation disagrees |
| Change helps one task class and harms another | FOQA stratification by phase/fleet/airport | treatment effect by task class, tool family, session length, model/provider |
| Agent makes errors but recovers cheaply before failure | LOSA threat/error management | error chain, detection source, recovery success, recovery cost |
| Agent “succeeds” only after owner rescue | LOSA crew/safeguard observation | owner intervention and correction linked to eventual completion |
| Automation behaves correctly but violates the owner’s mental model | automation-surprise tuple | expected mode versus visible mode, actual action, recovery narrative |
| New rule suppresses risky work rather than improving it | safety-margin plus utility guardrails | fewer errors alongside lower completion, more refusals, abandonment, or capability avoidance |
| Detector definition changes and creates an apparent trend | FOQA revision control | event-contract version break; series invalid unless backfilled |
| Voluntary reports increase after trust improves | ASRS reporting-bias caveat | narratives used qualitatively; rate comes only from recorder/audit denominator |
| Benchmark improvement fails to transfer to ordinary work | LOSA normal-operations sampling | representative live sample disagrees with fixture/replay results |
| Insiders normalize a systemic workaround | LOSA external-observer control | blinded independent review disagrees with routine coding |

## 5. Proposals rejected or sharply limited

### Rejected — “just add a pre-change checklist”

Checklists are valuable memory and configuration aids. FAA material describes the challenge-verification-response method as mutual verification of critical configuration ([NASA/FAA, *Human Factors of Flight-Deck Checklists*](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). But a checklist does not supply baseline observations, exposure, outcome data, or a counterfactual. It may improve compliance while leaving effect unknown. Use a short checklist to ensure the change card is complete, not as the measurement system.

### Rejected — “count post-mortems or correction reports”

Blameless review can improve causal narratives, but report volume is jointly determined by incidence, detection, willingness to report, and reporting burden. NASA explicitly warns that ASRS records do not establish prevalence. A rise after a change might mean worse behavior or better reporting culture. Narratives must remain a qualitative channel anchored to routine trace denominators.

### Rejected — “build a second orchestration/observability runtime”

A daemon, durable workflow engine, vector/KG memory, trained critic, guardrail framework, or observability SaaS is unnecessary for the proposed loop. Each accepted proposal rides settings, hooks, skills, and local append-only artifacts. Adding a second runtime would increase the system whose failures must themselves be observed and would violate the anti-overfit gate absent evidence of necessity.

### Limited — CRM as an observability mechanism

CRM addresses communication, workload, situation awareness, and cross-checking. FAA guidance includes speaking up about overload, communicating workload distribution, and anticipating automated-system distraction ([AC 120-51E](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac120-51e.pdf)). Those ideas support owner correction and independent review, but CRM does not itself solve the missing-baseline problem. The transferable fragment is an explicit challenge channel and shared mode awareness, not a new multi-agent committee.

### Limited — pure replay evaluation

Paired replay is useful for deterministic mechanisms and regression detection. It does not reproduce owner learning, changing repositories, live external services, or novel intent. It can justify “the mechanism fires under these fixtures,” not “the owner’s live work improved.”

## 6. Minimum viable protocol

The smallest defensible deployment is:

1. **Declare.** Write the change card: eligible opportunities, one primary metric, utility guardrails, event-contract version, minimum coverage, and rollback rule.
2. **Validate instrumentation.** Seed a harmless known-present event and reconcile the hook stream against an independent session summary. Do not proceed if either fails.
3. **Shadow.** Run detectors without intervention long enough to observe relevant exposure across the main task strata. Record missingness and coverage.
4. **Compare.** Prefer a concurrent session-level canary. If unsafe or impossible, use paired isolated replays and label the result mechanism-only.
5. **Sample normal work.** Audit a small stratified set of successes and failures with the TEM form, including recovery and workarounds.
6. **Capture surprise.** Solicit the optional four-field narrative only after corrections or detected mode transitions at first; tune burden empirically.
7. **Decide.** Generate the assurance report. Expand only if the primary metric improves, guardrails do not materially degrade, detector health is valid, and qualitative evidence does not reveal a masked failure.
8. **Repeat.** Preserve definitions and perform a follow-up operational sample. If the event contract changes, explicitly break or backfill the series.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that Proposals A, B, E, and F can be implemented in under roughly 200 lines each because they are a schema, append-only hook, prompt template, and report reducer respectively; **UNVERIFIED — settle by a line-counted spike using the actual hook payloads and supported session metadata.** Harness-LOSA reviewer logic may fit in a skill prompt plus deterministic extractor, but its calibration work is not captured by line count.

## 7. Decision rules for legacy changes with literally no baseline

For a change already deployed with no comparable pre-change data:

- Do **not** claim that it helped from current performance alone.
- Start prospective instrumentation and label the current state “baseline pending.”
- If the change is reversible and low risk, conduct a short randomized withdrawal or alternating canary, with safeguards and owner consent.
- If withdrawal is unsafe, compare treatment variants prospectively (current change versus refinement), not fictionally against the unknown past.
- Use immutable historical sessions only if the required raw inputs exist and the same event contract can be applied to both periods; otherwise historical comparison is **UNVERIFIED — settle by a coverage audit that demonstrates identical required fields and detector semantics in both periods.**
- Use paired replay for mechanism confirmation, with external state mocked and stochastic repetitions disclosed.
- Report current absolute performance and hazards even when effect is unknown. “We cannot attribute improvement” does not mean “we learned nothing.”

This is the most important negative result of the unit: **aviation offers a disciplined way to stop being baseline-less; it does not offer permission to reconstruct a causal baseline from anecdotes.**

All accepted proposals use the existing hook/skill/configuration bus and local files:

The design deliberately avoids continuous judgment by a second agent, exhaustive trace retention, and global scoring. It samples costly semantic review, keeps automatic telemetry narrow, and makes every measurement change explicit. This is compatible with the brief’s warning that elaborate scaffolding can overfit as models improve.

## 10. Gaps and experiments

1. **No local feasibility claim.** The unit explicitly prohibited local grounding. Hook payload availability, session identifiers, dynamic treatment gating, and trace access are therefore **UNVERIFIED — settle by a read-only interface audit followed by a disposable spike in the actual harness.**
2. **No universal sample size.** Aviation’s “50 observations per fleet” is contextual, not a harness constant. Required samples are **UNVERIFIED — settle with simulation/power analysis from the shadow-period rate, clustering, and variance of the predeclared metric.**
3. **No validated harness TEM taxonomy.** The proposed categories have face-valid structural mappings but coding reliability is **UNVERIFIED — settle with independent double-coding and owner adjudication, then revise definitions before treatment.**
4. **No privacy proof.** Hashing and derived-event logging reduce collection, but re-identification risk depends on retained dimensions and local threat model. Safety is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a field-level data-flow review and adversarial re-identification test before retaining narratives.**
5. **No claim that one-owner randomization is always ethical or powerful.** Eligibility may be rare and withholding a safety fix may be unacceptable. Feasibility is **UNVERIFIED — settle per change using risk classification, expected exposure, and minimum detectable effect.**
6. **No evidence that an LLM reviewer is mechanically independent.** A different prompt or instance may remain correlated with the acting model. Independence is **UNVERIFIED — settle by comparing deterministic trace labels, a different-model blinded review, and owner adjudication; do not call two same-model passes independent.**
