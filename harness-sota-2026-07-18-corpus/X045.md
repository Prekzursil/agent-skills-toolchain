# X045 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → agent tool design

**Unit:** X045  
**Lens:** nuclear-operations mechanisms applied to tool interfaces and error messages  
**Target defect:** 1,631 measured unmatched-quote EOF shell failures in the pinned corpus  
**Research boundary:** external ideation only. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. The only local write is this artifact.

## Executive determination

The analogy **partially transfers**, but only after two important transformations: make the checks machine-speed and grade their cost by consequence. Nuclear operations has solved a structurally similar problem: a competent operator can intend the right outcome yet select the wrong component, encode the wrong action, act under uncertainty, or fail to notice that the result differs from the expected plant response. Its useful answer is not one ritual but a system: STAR self-checking at the point of action; explicit pause/abort criteria; peer or independent checking selected according to consequence; and defence in depth so that failure of prevention does not imply failure of detection or mitigation.

The mapping to shell tooling is **very likely (80-90%)** to be valid at the structural level because both workflows have (1) an intended state transition, (2) a representation that can be mistaken or ambiguous, (3) an actuator, and (4) observable results. Evidence for the source structure is DOE's description of STAR as focusing on the correct component, intended action, expected outcome, and result verification, including computer data entry and calculations ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). Evidence for the target structure is the pinned measurement of 1,631 unmatched-quote EOF failures: the intended command did not survive encoding into shell text.

Literal transplantation—two people approving every command—does **not** transfer. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** to impose disproportionate latency and approval fatigue on a high-volume agent loop; the evidence is the sharp consequence and frequency disanalogy discussed below, plus DOE's own warning that peer checking can be over-used until both parties become complacent ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, p. 43](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). Two calls to the same model are also not meaningfully independent. The borrow that survives is therefore: **STAR as a tool transaction contract, independent native parsing as the routine second check, and human/independent-agent concurrence only at high-consequence boundaries**.

## What nuclear operations actually does

### 1. STAR self-checking is a closed-loop action, not a mnemonic alone

DOE defines self-checking as focusing attention on the appropriate component or activity, thinking about the intended action and expected outcome before acting, and verifying the result afterward. DOE says it is particularly useful for repetitive skill-based work and also for entering data, calculations, and computer-mediated document changes ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The operational sequence is:

1. **Stop:** pause before a critical activity and remove distractions.
2. **Think:** understand the action, verify the current condition, identify the expected output, compare against the controlling document, and consider a contingency.
3. **Act:** perform the planned action on the correct component under the applicable procedure.
4. **Review:** compare actual output with expected output and invoke the contingency if it differs.

Those steps and the associated at-risk practices—including rapid batches of manual actions, acting despite discrepancies, and failing to verify results—are explicit in the handbook ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). IAEA independently describes STAR as “stop, think, act, review” within a broader requirement to stop and review safety before work, seek information when necessary, and assume adverse outcomes under uncertainty ([IAEA INSAG-15, §3.3, p. 7](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1137_scr.pdf)).

The specific mechanism is **prospective expectation followed by retrospective comparison**. “Review” is not equivalent to observing an exit code; it asks whether the anticipated state change occurred and whether only that change occurred. This distinction is central to the tool design below.

### 2. “Pause when unsure” converts ambiguity into a stop condition

DOE instructs workers to pause for persistent uncertainty, procedure/condition mismatch, unexpected results, missing knowledge, or another person's concern; put the equipment into a safe condition; and obtain knowledgeable help. It explicitly lists missing abort criteria as an at-risk practice ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 16–17](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). IAEA similarly says conservative decision-making rejects an option unless safety is demonstrated, rather than proceeding until danger is proven, and calls for systematic decisions under unexpected conditions ([NRC safety-culture components, “Decision-Making”](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/enforcement/sc-componets011306)).

The mechanism is a **fail-closed uncertainty boundary**. It prevents production pressure or completion bias from turning an unresolved representation problem into a state-changing action.

### 3. Peer checking and independent verification are different controls

DOE is unusually precise here:

- Peer checking is contemporaneous. Performer and peer each self-check, agree on the correct action and component, observe execution, and stop an inconsistent action. DOE recommends it for critical, irreversible, or otherwise unwanted actions, but warns about inexperience, authority gradients, reliance on the other checker, and overuse ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 41–43](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
- Independent verification occurs after the action, separated in time and distance. The verifier establishes the as-found state without relying on the performer's observation or oral confirmation. DOE says this has a higher probability of catching an error because the verifier did not produce the state or document and is less cued by the performer ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 46–47](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The specific mechanism is **freedom from correlated cues**. A checker that merely repeats the performer's reasoning is redundant in headcount but not independent in failure mode.

### 4. Defence in depth assumes prevention can fail

IAEA describes five levels: prevent abnormal operation; detect and control failures; control design-basis accidents; prevent escalation and mitigate severe conditions; and mitigate off-site consequences. It also emphasizes the single-failure criterion and diversity against common-cause failure ([IAEA-TECDOC-1436, pp. 3–4](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/TE_1436_web.pdf)). The NRC's public explanation likewise identifies multiple physical barriers, safety systems, trained staff, testing, maintenance, and inspection rather than one perfect control ([NRC, Backgrounder on Nuclear Reactor Risk](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/fact-sheets/reactor-risk)).

The mechanism is **independent layers with different jobs**, not duplication of the same check. Prevention, detection, containment, recovery, and learning remain distinct.

## Adversarial transfer test

### Structural correspondences

| Nuclear operation | Shell/tool operation | Why the correspondence matters |
|---|---|---|
| Component identity | executable, working directory, file, host, repository, or external target | A syntactically valid action can hit the wrong target. |
| Procedure/action instruction | tool-call schema plus command or script | Intent must be encoded into an executable representation. |
| Operator manipulation | process spawn and system call | This is the state-changing boundary. |
| Expected indication | exit status, stdout/stderr, file/state query, API response | Execution is not the same as accomplishing the goal. |
| Unexpected plant response | parse error, wrong-shell behavior, partial mutation, false-success exit | The tool needs an abort or recovery path. |
| Independent verifier | native parser, policy AST check, postcondition probe, or selectively a human/independent agent | Different evidence can catch a failure the performer rationalized away. |

The correspondence is strongest for discrete commands that mutate named state and expose a verifiable postcondition. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve control of those calls because DOE applies the source tool precisely at status-altering and data-entry boundaries, while shell parsing exposes a machine-checkable pre-execution boundary. GNU Bash documents that `-n` reads commands without executing them specifically to check syntax ([GNU Bash manual, `set -n`](https://www.gnu.org/software/bash/manual/html_node/The-Set-Builtin.html)); PowerShell exposes `Parser.ParseInput`, returning tokens and parse errors without execution ([Microsoft PowerShell SDK](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.management.automation.language.parser.parseinput?view=powershellsdk-7.4.0)).

### Disanalogies that reject naive borrowing

1. **Consequence distribution differs.** Reactor manipulations are comparatively rare and can have large, irreversible physical consequences. Most shell reads are frequent, cheap, and reversible. Universal ceremony would consume the agent loop.
2. **The agent is not an accountable licensed operator.** A model can emit a plausible STAR narrative without changing the command. Natural-language self-attestation is therefore weak evidence.
3. **A second instance is not automatically independent.** Same model, context, prompt, and tool description create correlated failure. Nuclear IV explicitly removes performer cues; a second model call shown the proposed command may anchor on it.
4. **Shell state is incompletely observable.** Exit zero is only a process result. Some desired postconditions are expensive or impossible to specify, and external side effects may not be reversible.
5. **Native syntax validation has a hard ceiling.** It catches an unmatched quote, but not a syntactically valid command aimed at the wrong directory, an unintended expansion, or a valid destructive command.
6. **Defence layers can share a common cause.** A preflight using the same shell parser as execution is excellent for syntax, but not diverse against a parser defect or semantic misunderstanding. Diversity matters only where its additional cost is justified.

These disanalogies mean the analogy **does not support** a universal two-person rule, a mandatory prose checklist before every call, or claims that syntax checking makes commands safe. It supports a graded interface in which cheap mechanical checks are universal and expensive independent judgment is selective.

## Proposed target design

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: argument-vector-first execution (settle with Experiment E1)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add an existing-bus tool such as:

```json
{
  "tool": "exec_argv",
  "program": "git",
  "args": ["show", "origin/main:path with spaces/file.txt"],
  "cwd": "C:\\work\\repo",
  "timeout_ms": 30000
}
```

Make this the documented default for a single executable. Each argument is a separate schema value; the agent must not quote arguments for a shell. Reject shell operators (`|`, `&&`, redirects, substitutions) as apparent program/argument text with an error that says to use the checked-script interface if shell composition is truly intended.

This borrows “correct component, correct action” by separating **program identity** from **argument identity** instead of asking the model to encode both into one shell string. Python's standard-library documentation says an argument sequence is generally preferred because the library handles escaping/quoting, and that a shell is not implicitly invoked; it warns that explicit shell use transfers responsibility for metacharacter quoting to the application ([Python `subprocess` arguments and security](https://docs.python.org/3/library/subprocess.html#security-considerations)).

It is **very likely (80-90%)** to eliminate unmatched-quote failures for eligible single-process calls, because there is no shell grammar in which a quote can remain unmatched; the evidence is the documented argv behavior and the failure's measured parse signature. The proportion of the 1,631 failures eligible for argv execution is **UNVERIFIED**; Experiment E1 must replay/classify those historical commands and is the only basis for projecting an aggregate reduction.

What it catches or prevents that a string interface misses:

- spaces, quotes, and metacharacters in paths becoming syntax;
- accidental nested quoting around `bash -c`/`pwsh -Command` for simple calls;
- ambiguity over whether a token is data or shell syntax;
- a missing executable as a spawn error distinct from a shell's “command not found.”

Windows caveat: Windows ultimately supplies a command-line string to many programs and `.cmd`/`.bat` files can re-enter shell parsing. Python documents both Windows argument conversion and the batch-file exception ([Python `subprocess`, Windows and security notes](https://docs.python.org/3/library/subprocess.html#security-considerations)). Therefore the facade must label the actual launch mode in its result (`direct`, `cmd_batch`, `powershell_script`, `bash_script`); it must not promise quote-free semantics for batch shims.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: STAR-shaped checked-script transaction (settle with Experiments E1 and E2)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For genuine pipelines, redirection, loops, conditionals, and expansion, expose a separate interface:

```json
{
  "tool": "exec_script_checked",
  "dialect": "bash",
  "script": "rg --files -g '*.md' | sort",
  "cwd": "C:\\work\\repo",
  "expected": {
    "exit_codes": [0],
    "observation": "stdout is a sorted Markdown-file list"
  },
  "risk": "read_only",
  "timeout_ms": 30000
}
```

The transaction maps STAR mechanically:

- **Stop:** require an explicit dialect and resolve the executable/cwd before execution. Do not silently guess Bash versus PowerShell.
- **Think:** run the dialect's native no-execute parser; compute a token/AST summary; classify obvious mutation/risk features; record expected exit codes. Require a machine-testable postcondition or rollback description only for elevated-risk calls, not routine reads.
- **Act:** only after preflight succeeds, pass the script through stdin or a short temporary script file rather than nesting it inside another `-c`/`-Command` command string. Return the exact dialect and launch mode used.
- **Review:** return actual exit code and streams, compare declared expectations, and optionally execute a read-only postcondition probe. A mismatch is `EXECUTED_UNVERIFIED` or `POSTCONDITION_FAILED`, not generic “success.”

For Bash, `bash -n` is a documented non-executing syntax check ([GNU Bash manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/bash/manual/html_node/The-Set-Builtin.html)). For PowerShell, `Parser.ParseInput` provides tokens plus parse errors ([Microsoft PowerShell SDK](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.management.automation.language.parser.parseinput?view=powershellsdk-7.4.0)). It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that these native preflights detect the measured unmatched-quote syntax class before the intended script executes, because an unclosed quotation is a grammar error and both interfaces are the dialect parsers; Experiment E2 must verify this against a mutation corpus, including here-strings, escaped newlines, Unicode quotes, and nested invocations. It is **UNVERIFIED** whether sending scripts via stdin/temp files is compatible with every current PTY, environment, and Windows shim; Experiment E2 must test those launch modes.

This catches additional currently easy-to-confuse cases:

- declared dialect disagrees with syntax (`$env:X` sent to Bash, Bash substitution sent to PowerShell);
- a parse failure occurs before any partial action;
- exit zero conflicts with a declared state postcondition;
- a shell script was used where argv would be simpler and safer;
- a retry repeats an identical parse fingerprint without changing the representation.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: diagnostic errors as recovery procedures (settle with Experiment E3)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Replace opaque stderr forwarding with a stable, structured error envelope while preserving raw stderr:

```json
{
  "status": "REJECTED_PRE_EXECUTION",
  "code": "SHELL_PARSE_UNCLOSED_QUOTE",
  "dialect": "bash",
  "did_execute": false,
  "location": {"line": 1, "column": 34},
  "parser_message": "unexpected EOF while looking for matching quote",
  "recovery": [
    {"action": "USE_ARGV", "when": "no shell operators are required"},
    {"action": "EDIT_SCRIPT_AT_LOCATION", "when": "shell composition is required"}
  ],
  "retry_policy": "DO_NOT_RETRY_UNCHANGED",
  "fingerprint": "bash:unclosed-single-quote:line1"
}
```

The error must answer: **what phase failed, did anything execute, which dialect interpreted it, where is the defect, what evidence produced the diagnosis, and what materially different recovery is permitted?** It must not auto-insert a quote: that may yield syntactically valid but semantically different code. This is the tooling equivalent of putting the system in a known safe condition and consulting the controlling procedure before resuming.

It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce unchanged-command retry loops because the envelope explicitly forbids unchanged retry and offers a representation change; the evidence is the pinned retry/fingerprint context and DOE's use of abort criteria, but agent compliance with the envelope is **UNVERIFIED**. Experiment E3 must compare retry behavior using raw stderr versus the envelope across the same failing prompts.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: consequence-graded independent concurrence (settle with Experiment E4)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Do **not** add a second agent to routine quoting. Use three grades:

1. **Routine/read-only:** argv separation or one native parser is enough.
2. **Mutating but reversible/local:** native parser plus policy AST scan plus explicit postcondition/rollback field.
3. **Irreversible, broad, privilege-changing, secret-moving, or externally consequential:** require concurrence from a human approval surface or a verifier that receives the goal, resolved targets, risk summary, and postcondition—but not the performer's chain-of-thought. The performer cannot self-approve.

This is a selective translation of peer checking, not a claim that parser validation equals a two-person rule. It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch wrong-target or unacceptable-consequence actions that syntax validation misses, because a second evidence path examines resolved targets and consequences; the premise that an available verifier is sufficiently independent is **UNVERIFIED**, and Experiment E4 must compare same-model/same-context, context-isolated model, deterministic policy, and human review. If none produces materially uncorrelated detections, reject the agent-verifier portion and retain deterministic policy plus human approval.

## Defence-in-depth stack for the measured quoting class

| Layer | Control | Failure contained | Independence note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent | `exec_argv` default | Quote/metacharacter data never enters shell grammar | Removes the failure mechanism rather than detecting it. |
| Detect | explicit dialect + native no-execute parse | Unclosed quote, incomplete construct, many wrong-dialect forms | Independent of model reasoning, but shares parser implementation with execution. |
| Block | `did_execute=false` fail-closed gate | Syntax error cannot become partial execution | Same facade, so tests must cover bypass paths. |
| Limit | risk grading and target resolution | Valid but broad/destructive command | Policy logic is diverse from syntax parser. |
| Verify | postcondition read/probe | Exit zero with wrong or incomplete result | Must measure state independently of stdout where possible. |
| Recover | typed recovery action and no-unchanged-retry fingerprint | Repeated malformed retries | Agent behavior remains an empirical question. |
| Learn | aggregate code/fingerprint by dialect and launch mode | Recurring interface weakness remains invisible | Uses structured results, not the broken hooks detector identified in pinned inputs. |

This stack is **very likely (80-90%)** to be more robust than prompt-only “quote carefully” guidance for syntax failures because its first three layers are enforced at the execution boundary and do not rely on model recollection. Aggregate production benefit is **UNVERIFIED** until E1–E3 replay real failures and measure bypasses.

## Experiments and acceptance gates

### E1 — Historical replay/classification

Sample all 1,631 unmatched-quote fingerprints, de-duplicate by normalized command shape, and label each as direct-exec eligible or truly shell-dependent. Run original calls in a no-mutation fixture, then translate eligible calls to argv. Acceptance: zero quote-parser failures in argv-eligible cases; report coverage, new failure classes, latency, and Windows batch-shim exceptions. This settles the unverified eligible fraction and Proposal A's aggregate effect.

### E2 — Parser mutation matrix

Generate valid Bash and PowerShell fixtures, mutate quotes/here-docs/here-strings/escapes/substitutions at every token boundary, and compare preflight outcome with actual shell parse outcome in disposable environments. Include Git Bash, PowerShell 7, `.cmd` shims, paths with spaces, CRLF, Unicode punctuation, and nested shell calls. Acceptance: 100% detection of unclosed-quote mutations before execution, no execution during preflight, and an explicitly measured false-rejection rate. This settles Proposal B compatibility and quote detection.

### E3 — Error-message A/B test

Give the same agent prompts either raw shell stderr or the structured envelope. Measure unchanged retry count, time/calls to a valid representation, accidental semantic changes, and abandonment. Acceptance should be set from baseline after replay; a proposed initial gate of at least 50% fewer unchanged retries is **UNVERIFIED** and must not become a production promise until this experiment measures it.

### E4 — Independence and consequence test

Construct valid-but-wrong-target, unexpectedly broad, destructive, and secret-exfiltrating commands. Compare deterministic target/policy analysis, same-model reviewer, context-isolated reviewer, and human review. Measure unique detections, correlated misses, false blocks, and latency by risk tier. Acceptance: routine calls incur no human/second-model gate; elevated review must add unique detections beyond deterministic policy. If not, discard it.

### E5 — End-to-end shadow mode

Run the facade in non-blocking shadow mode first: record what it would reject, which path agents use, and whether postconditions are expressible. Then enforce only the high-precision unclosed-quote rule; expand after measured precision. It is **likely (55-80%)** that staged enforcement avoids disabling valid edge-case scripts while still capturing the measured class, based on the narrow determinism of quote parsing versus the greater ambiguity of semantic policy; the operational rate is **UNVERIFIED** until E5.

## Anti-overfit gate

All four proposals ride the existing tool/hook/skill/MCP bus and can be implemented as a thin facade plus schemas. They do not introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The critical innovation is a narrower interface and better transaction semantics, not a new agent architecture.

The design deliberately avoids forcing STAR prose into every call. Cheap constraints—argv separation, explicit dialect, native parsing, typed result—are mechanical. Postconditions and concurrence scale only with consequence. This preserves the minimal-harness principle while changing the error-producing boundary.

## Rejected alternatives and dead ends

1. **“Prompt the model to use STAR.”** Rejected: a fluent checklist is not mechanically tied to the command representation or observed result.
2. **“Ask a second identical agent to approve every command.”** Rejected: correlated cues violate the source mechanism's independence, and universal review invites complacency.
3. **“Auto-fix unmatched quotes.”** Rejected: many completions are syntactically possible; choosing one can change command semantics.
4. **“Treat exit code zero as Review.”** Rejected: it verifies process convention, not intended state.
5. **“Run a parser and call it defence in depth.”** Rejected: syntax prevention/detection is one layer and cannot detect a valid wrong-target command.
6. **“Replace shell with a large workflow runtime.”** Rejected by the anti-overfit gate; argv plus checked scripts covers the defect without a second orchestrator.
7. **“Forbid shell entirely.”** Rejected: pipes, redirection, expansion, and compound control flow are legitimate. The correct move is explicit escalation from argv to checked script.

## Gaps and limits

- The unit instruction prohibits grounding in the local repo, so I did not verify current tool schemas, hook capabilities, PTY semantics, approval surfaces, or implementation line counts. The BUILD-SMALL classifications describe deliberately bounded facade prototypes; production hardening size is **UNVERIFIED** and would be settled by implementing E1/E2 prototypes on the existing bus.
- The pinned corpus provides the count of unmatched-quote failures but not, in this unit, the individual commands or their direct-exec eligibility. Any total reduction estimate would therefore be fabricated; E1 is required.
- I found authoritative DOE/IAEA mechanisms and authoritative Bash/PowerShell parser interfaces. I did not find controlled evidence quantifying STAR's causal error reduction in agentic shell use. Transfer effectiveness remains experimental as tagged inline.
- Native parsing proves grammatical acceptability, not safety or correctness. No proposal claims otherwise.
- “Two-person rule” is often used broadly across security and operations contexts. The directly supported mechanism used here is DOE peer checking/concurrent verification/independent verification, with defined independence and risk selection; I do not claim that every nuclear manipulation legally requires two people.

## Bottom line

The nuclear borrow is worth keeping, but its correct form is **closed-loop, graded, and mechanically independent**: remove shell grammar where it is unnecessary; preflight the declared grammar where it is necessary; refuse to act on ambiguity; compare actual state with expected state; and reserve genuine concurrence for consequential actions. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a correct native parser gate catches unmatched-quote inputs before execution, based on the documented Bash and PowerShell parser behavior. It is **UNVERIFIED** how many of the measured 1,631 failures the complete design eliminates end to end; E1–E5 are the required settlement, and the design should be rejected or narrowed if those experiments do not demonstrate unique benefit at acceptable latency.
