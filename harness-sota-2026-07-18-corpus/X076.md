# X076 — Cross-domain borrow: law of evidence → finite-horizon agent context

## Executive judgment

The borrow transfers, but only at the level of **a staged admissibility and provenance discipline**, not as a literal court simulation and not as a complete context-eviction policy.

The strongest mechanism is the combination of (1) issue-relative relevance, (2) exclusion despite relevance when decision harm or cumulative cost dominates, (3) authentication and source-by-source treatment of derived assertions, (4) completeness when excerpts would mislead, and (5) reviewable summaries backed by accessible originals. Those mechanisms solve a recognizably similar problem: a decision maker cannot responsibly attend to every available item, and an item's mere availability or superficial relevance is not enough to justify putting it in the decision surface.

The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve the *inspectability* of context selection, because the legal mechanisms require the proponent to state what proposition an item supports, where it came from, and which transformations it passed through; the evidence is the explicit relational test in Federal Rule of Evidence (FRE) 401, the authentication foundation in FRE 901, and the originals-available safeguard in FRE 1006 ([FRE 401](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401), [FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901), [FRE 1006](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1006)). Whether it improves task success is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the replay experiment specified in “Evaluation,” comparing completion quality and provenance errors on long-horizon trajectories**. Until that test, the narrower conclusion is design plausibility, not demonstrated harness efficacy.

This is not an argument for a second orchestrator, knowledge graph, vector database, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The proposal is a small metadata envelope and deterministic admission pass on the existing context-compaction/hook bus. It passes the anti-overfit gate because the model still performs the work; the mechanism only makes context inclusion explicit, reviewable, and reversible.

## What evidence law actually solved

### 1. Relevance is a relationship to a proposition, not an intrinsic score

In its own field, FRE 401 says evidence is relevant only when it has some tendency to make a fact more or less probable **and** that fact matters to deciding the action. The Advisory Committee note makes the crucial structural point: relevance is not inherent in an item; it is a relationship between the item and a matter properly provable in the case. The rule deliberately sets a low admission threshold and separates admissibility from the sufficiency of the whole proof ([FRE 401, rule and notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401)).

That is more useful than generic “importance.” A tool result is not important in the abstract. It is useful because it bears on a live proposition such as “this test failure is caused by parser behavior,” “the user authorized this deletion,” or “the requested artifact exists at this path.” Context selection therefore needs an explicit set of **facts in issue** derived from the current goal, constraints, open decisions, and completion criteria. An item without a link to at least one live issue is background, a candidate for cold storage, or noise; it is not automatically active context.

The analogy does not imply that only disputed facts belong. FRE 401’s notes expressly recognize helpful background evidence even when it is not disputed. The harness equivalent is a small orientation layer—owner instruction, environment constraints, current plan, and vocabulary—that can remain admitted because it makes other material intelligible, not because it proves a contested claim.

### 2. Admissible does not mean worth scarce decision attention

In its own field, FRE 403 permits exclusion of relevant evidence when probative value is substantially outweighed by dangers including unfair prejudice, issue confusion, misleading the jury, delay, wasted time, or needless cumulative presentation. Its Advisory Committee note describes balancing value and need against likely harm ([FRE 403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_403)). This is not merely a relevance filter; it is a second-stage attention-allocation rule.

For an agent, the transferable categories are:

* **Confusion / misleading effect:** an obsolete plan, superseded user preference, failed hypothesis, or truncated output that appears current.
* **Waste / delay:** verbose logs whose useful fact has already been extracted.
* **Needless cumulation:** repeated retries, duplicated search results, or ten confirmations of the same low-risk fact.
* **Prejudicial salience (only an analogy):** an emotionally or rhetorically strong earlier hypothesis that can anchor later reasoning despite weak support.

The legal rule intentionally favors admission by requiring the danger to *substantially* outweigh probative value. That asymmetry is a useful warning against aggressive eviction: uncertainty about an item's value should not silently become deletion. In a harness, exclusion from the hot window should normally mean demotion to an addressable record, not destruction.

### 3. Foundation and conditional relevance

FRE 104(b) handles evidence whose relevance depends on another fact: enough foundation must exist to support finding that predicate fact, although the court may admit the item conditionally pending later proof ([FRE 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)). FRE 901 separately requires the proponent to produce enough support that an item is what the proponent claims; examples include knowledgeable testimony, distinctive characteristics, public-record provenance, and evidence that a process or system yields accurate results ([FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). FRE 602 similarly requires evidence sufficient to support personal knowledge for witness testimony ([FRE 602](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_602)).

Translated: a context item can be relevant only if its predicate survives. A claim such as “the build is green” depends on which revision ran, whether the command completed, which test set ran, and whether the output came from that command. A remembered owner preference depends on who stated it, its scope, and whether a later instruction superseded it. A summary depends on the records it summarizes and the transform that produced it.

This supports **conditional admission** rather than binary confidence theater: include the claim with an unresolved-foundation flag and its missing predicate, and prohibit it from satisfying completion criteria until the foundation arrives. The important transfer is not courtroom terminology; it is representing the dependency explicitly.

### 4. Hearsay within hearsay maps to transformation chains

In its own field, hearsay doctrine is concerned with an out-of-court statement offered for the truth of what it asserts; FRE 801 defines the category and its exclusions ([FRE 801](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801)). FRE 805 states that a nested statement survives the hearsay rule only if **each layer** independently conforms to an exception; the Advisory Committee example disaggregates a hospital entry from the wife's statement embedded in it ([FRE 805](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805)).

The exact legal exclusions and exceptions should not be copied. The transferable mechanism is **layer-wise source accounting**. Agent context routinely contains “the model said the tool said the webpage said X,” followed later by “we know X.” Each summarization, paraphrase, agent handoff, OCR step, extraction, and retrieval is a new transformation layer with a distinct failure mode. Treating only the outer summary as sourced launders uncertainty from inner layers.

A context record should therefore retain a compact derivation chain: original locator or event ID → extraction span or structured result → transform kind and actor → current assertion. Every link need not occupy the hot prompt; its pointer and integrity metadata do. An unsupported link lowers the assertion’s usable weight or triggers retrieval of the underlying material before a consequential action.

This is related to, but not identical with, forensic “chain of custody.” Authentication asks whether the item is what it purports to be; hearsay asks whether a statement may be used for its asserted truth. Conflating the two would be a design error: a perfectly authentic transcript can contain an unreliable assertion, and a substantively accurate fact can arrive with poor custody metadata.

### 5. The rule of completeness prevents excerpt-induced distortion

FRE 106 allows an adverse party to require contemporaneous introduction of another part or related statement when fairness requires it. The Advisory Committee explains that the rule targets misleading impressions created by taking statements out of context and recognizes that delayed repair can be inadequate ([FRE 106](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)).

This maps unusually well to compaction. A compressor that retains “owner asked to delete X” while dropping the later qualification “after backup and only in staging” has not merely omitted detail; it has reversed the operative meaning. Likewise, retaining a tool error without the subsequent successful retry, or a hypothesis without its falsification, creates a false state.

The transferable mechanism is a **completeness bundle**: selected spans carry links to qualifications, negations, superseding instructions, outcomes, and adjacent material necessary to preserve meaning. If any linked item is hot, the selector must either admit the minimal completing item or replace both with a faithful combined proposition. This is more precise than preserving arbitrary conversational adjacency.

### 6. Voluminous evidence may be summarized, provided originals remain examinable

FRE 1006 permits a summary, chart, or calculation to prove the content of voluminous admissible records that cannot conveniently be examined in court, while requiring the proponent to make originals or duplicates available to other parties. The 2024 committee note clarifies that the summary itself can be substantive evidence and that underlying records need not themselves all be admitted ([FRE 1006](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1006)). FRE 1002 separately states the original-writing rule when a party seeks to prove the content of a writing, recording, or photograph ([FRE 1002](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1002)).

The clean transfer is **summary in hot context, inspectable substrate out of band**. Compaction is legitimate when it does not sever the path back to raw observations. This also avoids the false choice between keeping every log token and trusting an irreversible synopsis.

### 7. Limited purpose is conceptually valuable but operationally dangerous

FRE 105 requires a court, on timely request, to restrict evidence to its proper scope and instruct the jury when it is admissible for one purpose but not another. Its notes also acknowledge that limiting instructions are not sufficient in all situations ([FRE 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_105)).

The context analogue is a scope tag: “use to choose a search lead, not to assert truth”; “historical preference, not current authorization”; “diagnostic hypothesis, not completion evidence.” This is conceptually excellent. But language models do not provide a reliable cognitive firewall between visible content and prohibited use. Scope labels are therefore advisory metadata, not a safety boundary. When misuse would be costly, the item should be withheld from the hot window and retrieved only inside a constrained subtask.

## Concrete harness translation

### Proposal A — issue ledger and evidence envelopes — UNVERIFIED (settle with the long-horizon replay experiment below)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a compact, append-only context manifest maintained at natural bus events (user turn, tool result, agent handoff, compaction). It is not a new memory service. Each admitted unit is an envelope with fields such as:

```yaml
id: event-or-claim-id
kind: instruction | observation | assertion | hypothesis | decision | outcome | summary
content_or_pointer: "..."
supports: [issue-id]
opposes: [issue-id]
foundation: [predicate-id]
source: {actor, tool, locator, observed_at}
derivation: [parent-id, transform-kind]
scope: [truth, lead-only, authorization, completion-proof]
status: live | disputed | superseded | falsified | resolved
completes_with: [qualification-or-outcome-id]
token_cost: integer
```

The issue ledger contains the current objective, non-negotiable constraints, open factual questions, open decisions, and completion claims. Admission is relational: `supports`, `opposes`, background necessity, or a live foundation dependency. The envelope is deliberately small; raw content stays in the existing transcript/tool record and is addressed by pointer.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a useful minimal envelope and selector can fit under roughly 200 lines if serialization, persistence, and event hooks already exist; the evidence is the intentionally shallow schema and deterministic rules above. This size claim is **UNVERIFIED — settle by implementing a throwaway parser/selector against the actual event schema and counting nonblank, noncomment lines**. If the existing bus lacks stable event IDs or retrievable raw records, this proposal no longer qualifies as BUILD-SMALL and must be rejected or reduced rather than smuggling in a service.

### Proposal B — two-stage context admission and reversible demotion — UNVERIFIED (settle with replay plus ablation)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Configure the existing context assembly/compaction policy, wherever such policy is exposed, to apply two explicit questions:

1. **401 gate:** Which live issue, foundation, or necessary background function does this item serve?
2. **403 gate:** Is its marginal decision value substantially outweighed by confusion, staleness/supersession risk, token cost, or needless cumulation?

Items failing the first gate do not enter hot context. Items passing the first but failing the second are demoted to the retrievable record with a one-line docket entry: ID, reason for demotion, and retrieval cue. Never silently destroy the only substrate for a live claim. Reserve hot space for: governing instructions; current issues; strongest support and counterevidence; unresolved foundations; operative decisions; recent outcomes; and compact retrieval pointers.

The assumption that the target harness exposes enough settings to express this policy is **UNVERIFIED — settle by inspecting the harness’s documented context/compaction configuration surface**. Accordingly, the CONFIG implementation route is **roughly even (45-55%)**, based only on the common existence of prompt/config-level compaction rules, not on local inspection (which this ideation unit was explicitly told not to perform). If unavailable, Proposal B should be folded into Proposal A’s existing-bus hook without changing the architecture tag of that combined implementation.

### Proposal C — completeness and derivation checks at compaction — UNVERIFIED (settle with seeded-distortion tests)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Before accepting a compacted summary:

* For each operative instruction, include or resolve linked qualifications and superseding instructions.
* For each hypothesis, include its status and decisive confirming or falsifying outcome.
* For each completion claim, require its stated foundation (command identity, exit status, revision/environment, and relevant result pointer).
* For each derived assertion, preserve the parent pointer and transform kind; recursively flag a missing parent.
* For each excerpt, either include its `completes_with` records or synthesize a combined proposition that preserves their effect.

This checker need not judge semantic truth. It checks graph-local invariants over explicit IDs. “Graph” here means a few ID lists in the manifest, not a knowledge graph or graph database.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch deliberately seeded missing-qualification and missing-parent cases when those links have already been annotated, because checking referenced IDs and statuses is deterministic. The evidence is the direct correspondence between the stored fields and invariants. Catching naturally occurring distortions without prior annotation is **UNVERIFIED — settle by blinding an annotator to paired original/compacted trajectories, then measuring detection recall on distortions the checker did not seed**; success there is only **roughly even (45-55%)**, because semantic link creation remains the difficult step.

### Proposal D — risk-indexed burden for action, not for mere context admission — UNVERIFIED (settle with counterfactual action trials)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Do not make “beyond reasonable doubt” a fake numeric confidence score. Legal standards of proof allocate error risk to decisions, not token slots. The Ninth Circuit’s model instruction describes clear-and-convincing evidence as higher than preponderance but lower than beyond reasonable doubt ([Ninth Circuit model civil jury instruction 1.7](https://www.ce9.uscourts.gov/jury-instructions/civil/chapter-1/1-7-burden-of-proof-clear-and-convincing-evidence/)).

Use a qualitative burden ladder only at the action boundary:

* reversible exploration: one authenticated, relevant lead may justify proceeding;
* user-facing factual assertion or durable edit: corroborated observation or direct primary source;
* destructive, irreversible, security-sensitive, or external side effect: direct authorization plus current target verification and outcome verification, with unresolved counterevidence surfaced.

This is not a claim that those tiers are legal standards. They are an error-cost policy inspired by the legal separation between burden allocation and evidentiary admission. It is **likely (55-80%)** that separating “may be considered” from “is enough to act” reduces premature completion or action, because FRE 401’s notes explicitly separate relevance from sufficiency and legal burdens exist precisely to govern when the accumulated record warrants a consequence. Harness benefit is **UNVERIFIED — settle by replaying ambiguous and high-impact tasks with counterfactual action scoring, measuring unsupported action rate and unnecessary abstention rate**.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### Courts have an adversary; an autonomous agent often does not

Evidence rules assume parties can object, expose missing foundation, supply completing text, and cross-examine sources. A single agent that generates both the summary and its admission rationale has correlated blind spots. Adding a permanent “opposing counsel” agent would violate the anti-overfit gate by adding orchestration and cost. The narrow substitute is deterministic invariant checking plus explicit counterevidence slots—not a second runtime. Whether the same model can reliably annotate evidence against its own favored hypothesis is **UNVERIFIED — settle with blinded human labels on annotation recall** and is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because self-review is correlated while structural fields still constrain omissions.

### A judge controls evidence before a human jury; tokens affect the model even when labeled

FRE 105 can rely, imperfectly, on a limiting instruction. An LLM may use visible material despite a “lead only” tag. Thus legal limited-purpose admission does **not** justify leaving prompt-injection text, secrets, or dangerously misleading claims in hot context. Physical exclusion and constrained retrieval remain necessary where misuse has high cost. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that labels are weaker than withholding as an information-flow boundary, because withholding removes the token while labeling leaves it in the model’s input; the exact behavioral gap is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the adversarial visible-label versus withheld-content test in Evaluation**.

### Trials converge on a fixed case; agent issues mutate

Legal relevance is anchored to pleadings and issues in an action. Agent tasks branch, user instructions revise the goal, and discovery creates new issues. A static issue ledger would over-evict serendipitously useful context. The ledger must therefore support issue creation, supersession, and background admission, and demotion must be reversible. This is a strong disanalogy and the main reason not to turn the 401 gate into hard deletion.

### Evidence rules optimize fairness and legitimacy, not token-normalized expected utility

FRE 403 offers qualitative factors, not a computable eviction score. Courts also have institutional values—jury protection, constitutional rights, procedure, and appellate review—that do not map cleanly to task completion. “Probative value per token” is a harness invention, not borrowed law. It may be a useful tie-breaker, but its efficacy is **UNVERIFIED — settle by ablation against relevance-only and recency baselines**, and superiority is **roughly even (45-55%)** absent data.

### Exclusion from trial and eviction from context are not the same operation

Excluded evidence can remain in the case file and in counsels’ possession. LLM context eviction can make information functionally unavailable unless a retrieval address survives. Therefore the safe mapping is hot-window admission versus docketed cold record, not admissible versus erased. If the existing harness cannot reliably retrieve by stable pointer, the analogy’s central safeguard fails and the design should stop rather than add a bespoke durable-memory service.

### Authentication is not truth, and provenance can become bureaucratic theater

FRE 901 establishes that an item is what it purports to be; it does not establish that every assertion inside it is true. A signed, hashed, perfectly preserved hallucination remains false. Metadata can also consume context and create false reassurance. Keep only the compact pointer and decisive foundation fields hot; retrieve the chain when challenged or before high-impact action.

### Legal burdens resist numerical transplantation

“Preponderance,” “clear and convincing,” and “beyond reasonable doubt” are decision standards allocated by substantive and procedural law. They are not calibrated probability bins for model self-confidence. Using them as numeric thresholds would import prestige without measurement. Proposal D borrows only asymmetric error-cost allocation and explicitly rejects faux quantification.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

1. **Copy all FRE categories into a rule engine.** Rejected. Privilege, character evidence, settlement exclusions, expert rules, and many hearsay exceptions serve legal policies without a structural context analogue. This would be cargo-cult complexity.
2. **A model-as-judge deciding every token.** Rejected. It adds latency, correlated error, and elaborate scaffolding. Deterministic metadata checks plus the primary model’s ordinary compaction are smaller.
3. **A second adversarial agent as opposing counsel.** Rejected under the anti-overfit gate: it is effectively extra orchestration and can double cost without independent ground truth.
4. **A vector database or knowledge graph for the evidence record.** Explicitly rejected as a previously disallowed component. Stable event IDs and small adjacency lists are sufficient for this proposal.
5. **Hash every token and build forensic custody infrastructure.** Rejected. Cryptographic integrity solves tampering, not relevance or truth, and would be disproportionate unless a separate threat model demonstrates tampering risk.
6. **Pure recency eviction.** Rejected as the proposed target baseline because it cannot represent older governing instructions, foundation dependencies, qualification bundles, or falsification outcomes.
7. **Never evict anything; rely on larger context windows.** Rejected as a general solution. Even if capacity grows, attention dilution, duplicated evidence, misleading superseded state, latency, and cost remain selection problems; the extent of those harms for a particular model is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the token-scale ablation below**, and persistence across frontier models is only **likely (55-80%)** based on the continued finite nature of context and inference budgets rather than a cited model-specific study.

## Evaluation: experiments that would convert the borrow from plausible to earned

### E1. Long-horizon replay benchmark

Sample completed long trajectories and create time-sliced replay points before consequential decisions. Compare:

* recency/legacy compaction baseline;
* issue-relative admission only;
* issue + 403-style balancing;
* full envelope with completeness and derivation checks.

Blind graders to condition. Measure task completion, governing-instruction retention, unsupported factual claims, use of falsified hypotheses, completion claims lacking foundation, token count, latency, and retrieval count. Stratify by task type and horizon. The proposal earns adoption only if the full condition improves error-adjusted task outcome without unacceptable latency; inspectability alone is insufficient.

### E2. Seeded evidentiary pathologies

Create controlled trajectories containing: qualified authorization, later supersession, failed then successful retries, nested agent/web summaries, duplicated logs, missing command exit status, and a recent irrelevant item competing with an old decisive constraint. Because ground truth is constructed, calculate detection precision and recall mechanically. Include negative controls where adjacent text is not a qualification and repeated outputs contain meaningful differences.

### E3. Purpose-label attack

Place an untrusted or lead-only assertion in three conditions: unlabeled hot context, labeled hot context, and physically withheld with a pointer. Ask downstream models to make factual and high-impact decisions. This settles whether limited-purpose labels have operational value and whether they are safe enough for any use class.

### E4. Summary auditability test

Randomly sample summary propositions and require reconstruction of their derivation to raw events. Score pointer validity, source identity, transform completeness, and whether completing/qualifying material is reachable. Corrupt links as positive controls so the detector’s ability to find a known defect is proven before any “no broken chains” claim.

### E5. Anti-overfit and token-scale ablation

Run the same tasks with no harness addition, schema-only prompts, deterministic checks, and any proposed semantic scoring. Repeat across at least two materially different model families/context sizes. Remove each metadata field in turn. If a field does not improve outcomes or enable a demonstrated invariant, delete it. This protects the 100-line-loop principle: the smallest condition that captures the measured gain wins.

### Acceptance criteria should be set before results

Exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED — settle by examining baseline variance and setting minimum detectable effects before running E1**. At minimum, pre-register: primary error metric, maximum token/latency regression, minimum number of trajectories, handling of retrieval failures, and which high-impact seeded failures are zero-tolerance. Do not tune thresholds after seeing conditions.

## Recommended decision

Proceed only to a disposable, existing-bus prototype of Proposals A and C, then evaluate against the current compactor. This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED — it inherits the E1–E5 experiments above**. It is **likely (55-80%)** to be worth the prototype cost because the strongest checks—missing foundation, broken derivation link, missing qualification, and needless exact duplication—are deterministic once the minimal annotations exist, while the prototype is explicitly bounded to BUILD-SMALL and does not introduce a rejected architecture.

Do **not** adopt legal vocabulary in user-facing operation, a universal evidence ontology, numeric “standards of proof,” or an autonomous judge service. If annotation coverage is poor, retrieval pointers are unstable, or replay shows no outcome gain, stop. The defensible residue would then be only two prompt-level principles: relevance is relative to a live issue, and summaries must preserve a path to their substrate.
