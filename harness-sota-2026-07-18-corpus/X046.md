# X046 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → observability without a baseline

## Unit boundary and verdict

This unit is an ideation lens. I did not inspect the local harness, corpus, audit findings, or subject tree, because the assignment explicitly says not to duplicate grounding work being performed by other units. The target premise—“measuring whether a harness change actually helped, when there is no baseline”—is taken from the unit brief, not rediscovered here.

**Verdict:** the analogy transfers, but only partially. Nuclear operations has not solved the statistical impossibility of recovering an unobserved historical counterfactual. It has solved a closely adjacent assurance problem: how to make a consequential change under uncertainty, establish an explicit acceptable state, verify that state through cognitively independent channels, monitor leading and lagging evidence, and escalate conservatively when the evidence is incomplete or discordant. The useful borrow is therefore a **graded, independently verified performance envelope**, assembled from established nuclear mechanisms rather than falsely presented as a single named nuclear doctrine.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this bounded borrow would prevent the strongest unsupported claim—“the change helped”—from being emitted when only post-change telemetry exists. Evidence: the proposed decision record mechanically requires a predeclared reference or explicitly labels the result “baseline unavailable / causal effect indeterminate”; nuclear guidance likewise compares current performance with established standards and uses independent confirmation rather than treating one indicator as proof ([IAEA performance-improvement course](https://www.iaea.org/events/evt2204526), [IAEA-TECDOC-1125](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1125_prn.pdf)). It is only **likely (55-80%)** that the borrow would correctly identify the direction of improvement on heterogeneous real owner work. Evidence: the NRC combines indicators with inspection findings because indicators alone are incomplete, while agent tasks are even less stationary and repeatable than plant processes ([NRC ROP framework](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description)).

The crucial disanalogy is non-negotiable: a reactor has engineered state variables, licensed limits, stable system boundaries, recurrent surveillance tests, and extraordinarily high consequence asymmetry. An agent harness operates on changing tasks with stochastic models, shifting software and web dependencies, preference-sensitive outcomes, and much lower per-action consequences. Consequently, importing mandatory dual control for every agent action would be ceremony, correlated model agreement could masquerade as independence, and “conservative” could become permanent stagnation. The transfer is justified only for harness changes and high-impact measurement decisions, not ordinary tool calls.

## The nuclear mechanism in its own field

### 1. STAR: local action-point self-checking

The U.S. Department of Energy’s *Human Performance Improvement Handbook, Volume 2* defines self-checking as a performer focusing on the correct component or activity, thinking through the intended action and expected result, acting, and then verifying the result. STAR is the mnemonic **Stop, Think, Act, Review**. DOE identifies uses including entering or recording data, calculations, reading critical indicators, editing procedures, and impending equipment-status changes ([DOE handbook landing page](https://www.energy.gov/ehss/articles/doe-hdbk-1028-2009), [DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2, pp. 18 onward](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)). The NRC’s human-factors coding definitions independently recognize inadequate self-checking as failure to check before, during, or after a task and give “Stop, Think, Act, Review” as the example ([NRC Human Factors Definition List, item 197](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)).

STAR is not retrospective analytics. Its function is to interrupt automatic action at the point where an error can be introduced and to make the expected response explicit before the performer observes the actual response. That ordering matters: expectation written after observing the outcome is vulnerable to hindsight rationalization.

### 2. Second-person checking, with genuine independence rather than “two people looked”

DOE distinguishes peer checking, concurrent verification, independent verification, and peer review. Independent verification confirms equipment condition or the accuracy of a document or calculation after the state-changing action. The verifier is separated from the performer in time and distance, does not rely on the performer’s oral confirmation or observed actions, and checks the condition directly. DOE says this is more likely to catch an error than peer or concurrent checking because the verifier was not involved in producing the state and is less cognitively influenced by the performer. DOE also warns of failure modes: a junior verifier may hesitate to challenge, either participant may assume the other will catch errors, and cues from the performer may replace personal confirmation ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2, checking and verification practices, pp. 40–49](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)).

This is the precise operational mechanism relevant to the task’s “two-person rule.” Its value is not the number two. Its value is **cognitive and evidentiary independence**: separate derivation, direct observation, and a recorded comparison. The NRC separately codes failures to identify errors through “independent verification (e.g., peer/second check)” as a human-performance problem ([NRC Human Factors Definition List, items 185 and 196](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). These are two mechanically different source signals—a DOE operating handbook and an NRC event-classification scheme—supporting the role of independent checking.

### 3. Defence in depth: do not let one measurement channel carry the safety claim

IAEA guidance describes operational safety as including conservative decision-making, operation within the safety-analysis envelope, and maintenance of defence in depth against unplanned events through equipment reliability and human performance ([IAEA-TECDOC-1125, §1.3](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1125_prn.pdf)). In the performance-measurement context, IAEA-TECDOC-1141 is unusually direct: safety is difficult to define and measure; focusing on one aspect can be misleading; indicator values are not direct measures of safety; and indicators are only one part of a larger set that includes probabilistic safety assessment, regulatory inspection, quality assurance, and self-assessment. The report says the integrated picture yields the best result and notes that its indicator framework was enhanced through pilot-plant studies ([IAEA-TECDOC-1141, Introduction](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1141_prn.pdf)).

The NRC Reactor Oversight Process (ROP) is a concrete implementation. Licensees submit performance indicators, NRC inspectors produce findings, indicators are compared with prescribed risk-informed thresholds, findings go through a significance determination process, and an action matrix scales the response. Baseline inspections deliberately cover areas not captured fully by indicators, verify the accuracy of licensee-reported indicators, and review whether utilities independently find and fix problems ([NRC ROP framework, “Overall Description,” “Inspection Programs,” and “Assessing Plant Performance”](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description)). Thus the metric producer, metric verifier, and gap-finding inspection are distinct roles.

### 4. Conservative decision-making: uncertainty changes the action, not the prose

The nuclear version is not “be pessimistic.” It preserves margins and stops or establishes controls when unfavorable or uncertain conditions appear. The NRC explicitly codes “failure to stop work/non-conservative decision-making” when personnel do not stop or establish appropriate controls under unfavorable or uncertain conditions ([NRC Human Factors Definition List, item 190](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). IAEA guidance connects conservative decision-making with staying inside the safety envelope and maintaining defence in depth ([IAEA-TECDOC-1125, §1.3](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1125_prn.pdf)).

The relevant lesson for observability is epistemic: when the evidence cannot distinguish improvement from workload drift, model variance, evaluator drift, or selective sampling, the decision state is “indeterminate,” not “improved with caveats.” An indeterminate result may permit a reversible pilot but not fleet-wide attribution.

### 5. Assessment of the assessment system

IAEA-TECDOC-1125 depicts four assessment levels: individual/work-group self-assessment, management/supervision self-assessment, internal assessment, and independent external assessment. It says participants need opportunities to calibrate their findings through independent confirmation outside the plant or utility, at a frequency responsive to the effectiveness and results of the self-assessment process ([IAEA-TECDOC-1125, assessment-process triangle and §4.1](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1125_prn.pdf)).

The NRC applies the recursion to its own oversight process. It piloted the revised ROP before implementation, conducts annual self-assessment using data analysis and trending, evaluates whether significant ROP changes were effective, tests implementation against pre-established goals and intended outcomes, performs periodic in-depth reviews of the baseline inspection program, and has commissioned independent evaluations by bodies including GAO, OIG, and ACRS ([NRC ROP Evaluations](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/program-evaluations)). This is “observability of observability”: the detector is itself periodically challenged rather than assumed valid because it emits data.

## Concrete translation: the Harness Change Assurance Card

The minimal transferable unit is a small, append-only **Harness Change Assurance Card (HCAC)** generated for any harness change that claims to affect quality, reliability, latency, cost, autonomy, or owner burden. It is a record plus a few hook checks, not a second orchestrator.

### Proposal A — Pre-action STAR claim contract — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Before enabling a measured harness change, a change hook requires four fields:

1. **Stop:** change identifier, exact scope, and reversibility/rollback condition.
2. **Think:** one primary intended effect, plausible harms, known confounders, and the expected observable result stated before results are collected.
3. **Act:** treatment window or paired-trial assignment, frozen task/sample identifiers, and the instrumentation version.
4. **Review:** actual result, missingness, discordant signals, and disposition: promote, continue pilot, roll back, or indeterminate.

The hook should refuse the semantic status `improved` if the Think fields or a valid prospective comparison are missing. It should still allow `instrumented-only` and `indeterminate`, so lack of history does not block learning.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this would catch post-hoc metric selection and success criteria invented after outcomes are visible. Evidence: STAR explicitly places expected-outcome reasoning before action and verification after action; the enforcement is a deterministic field-order and timestamp check, not a hoped-for behavioral norm ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)). It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce false improvement claims caused by ordinary outcome drift. Evidence: freezing the claim and sample closes one common degree of freedom, but it cannot control unrecorded environmental changes.

### Proposal B — Prospective reference envelope, not a fabricated historical baseline — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

When no baseline exists, do not synthesize one from memory. Start an envelope at change time using one of these evidence grades:

- **Grade 0 — no counterfactual:** post-change observations only. Permitted conclusion: “current behavior observed”; causal direction is indeterminate.
- **Grade 1 — invariant controls:** known-present controls, known-failing controls, telemetry completeness, parser correctness, and exact replay fixtures. Permitted conclusion: “measurement path worked on controls,” not “owner outcomes improved.”
- **Grade 2 — paired shadow comparison:** unchanged harness and candidate harness run on the same frozen task bundle, with random ordering and equivalent tool/network conditions where feasible. Permitted conclusion: candidate differed on this bundle.
- **Grade 3 — interleaved live pilot:** prospective random or alternating assignment across comparable live tasks, with predeclared stop conditions and owner-correction capture. Permitted conclusion: estimated treatment effect within the stated population and window.
- **Grade 4 — replicated evidence:** the Grade 2 or 3 effect repeats in a later window, model/version, or independently sampled task stratum. Permitted conclusion: evidence is less sensitive to one sample or environment.

The envelope stores several dimensions rather than a single score: task success/acceptance; owner corrections or reversals; tool-error and retry-loop rate; elapsed time; token/cost proxy; destructive/unsafe near misses; and telemetry completeness. It also records denominators and missingness. No weighted “harness score” is computed initially.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that Grade 0 correctly prevents causal attribution from post-only data. Evidence: without an untreated or pre-change comparator, the counterfactual outcome is unobserved by definition; the card makes that limitation an executable state. It is **likely (55-80%)** that Grades 2–4 would distinguish large, repeatable harness effects from noise on the sampled tasks. Evidence: paired assignment controls task composition more directly than before/after trending, and replication challenges window-specific effects; however, stochastic model behavior and external dependencies remain. The exact sensitivity is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with Experiment E1, a seeded A/A trial followed by injected-effect A/B trials**.

### Proposal C — Independent verification packet for consequential promotion — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For changes crossing a consequence threshold—for example broad command-policy changes, deletion/overwrite protections, evaluation logic, telemetry parsers, or global skill routing—the implementer produces the HCAC, and a verifier receives a blinded packet containing:

- the predeclared hypothesis and acceptance boundary;
- raw task/run identifiers and immutable result references;
- an independently executable aggregation command;
- control outcomes and missingness;
- labels `A` and `B`, without revealing which is candidate until the verifier records a result.

The verifier reruns the aggregation from raw evidence, checks at least one known-present and one known-failing control, records discrepancies, and only then unmasks A/B. “Independent” means a separate context and derivation; asking the same model in the same conversation to “double-check” does not qualify. A human is required only for owner-preference outcomes or irreversible/high-impact promotion; mechanical claims can be verified by a clean process with frozen code and inputs.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this catches aggregation bugs, treatment-label expectation effects, missing-denominator errors, and a detector that emits zero because it is disconnected. Evidence: DOE independent verification separates the verifier from the producer and requires direct checking; NRC baseline inspections explicitly verify licensee indicator accuracy and inspect areas indicators do not cover ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf), [NRC ROP framework](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description)). It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that a second model instance materially improves subjective task-quality judgment. Evidence: separation of context reduces direct anchoring, but instances may share model biases and training; genuine evaluator independence is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with Experiment E2, comparing same-model, different-model, and human adjudication disagreement on a blinded sample**.

### Proposal D — Defence-in-depth evidence matrix and discordance rule — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Configure promotion policy so no single telemetry family can establish “helped.” Require at least two mechanically independent signal families:

| Claim | Signal 1 | Independent signal 2 | Typical discordance interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reliability improved | tool-call error/retry rate | task completion without recovery or owner intervention | fewer logged errors but unchanged completion may mean errors were hidden or reclassified |
| Quality improved | blinded task acceptance | owner corrections/reverts on assigned live tasks | benchmark gain with more owner correction suggests benchmark overfit |
| Efficiency improved | wall-clock/tool-call count | accepted result per token/cost proxy | faster runs with lower acceptance are not efficiency gains |
| Detector is healthy | known-present positive control | independently sourced expected event count or known-failing negative control | zero on both is detector failure, not absence |
| Safety improved | blocked seeded unsafe action | audit of actual side effects / near misses | more blocks with unchanged side effects may be noisy policy, not protection |

The policy reports each dimension and discordance; it does not collapse them. Promotion requires no severe regression, adequate telemetry, and either Grade 3 evidence or Grade 2 plus explicit limited scope. A result that passes one signal and fails the other is `investigate`, not averaged into green.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this catches metric gaming and silent detector failure that a single dashboard line misses. Evidence: IAEA-TECDOC-1141 warns that one aspect can mislead and that indicators are not direct safety measures; NRC uses both licensee indicators and independent inspection findings and verifies the accuracy of indicator reports ([IAEA-TECDOC-1141](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1141_prn.pdf), [NRC ROP framework](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description)). It is **likely (55-80%)** that an initially unweighted matrix is more robust than a composite score. Evidence: it preserves trade-offs and reveals discordance, though it makes ranking harder; the best policy is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with Experiment E3, retrospective decision replay comparing matrix decisions with candidate weighted scores against blinded owner adjudication**.

### Proposal E — Conservative, reversible action matrix — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Map evidence to action before the trial:

| Evidence state | Allowed action |
|---|---|
| Grade 0 or failed controls | Observe/fix instrumentation; no improvement claim |
| Grade 1 | Limited canary solely to gather comparison data |
| Grade 2, mixed or small effect | Continue paired pilot; investigate discordance |
| Grade 2, large effect, no severe regression | Narrow reversible rollout with monitoring |
| Grade 3, acceptance met | Broader rollout with rollback trigger |
| Severe safety/owner-burden regression at any grade | Stop exposure and revert the candidate where rollback is within the already authorized change process |

This imports conservative decision-making as a graded response, not a universal veto. The card must distinguish “no evidence of harm” from “evidence of no material harm.” Lack of telemetry automatically lowers the evidence grade.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this reduces premature broad rollout while preserving experimentation. Evidence: NRC’s action matrix increases oversight as performance evidence worsens, and nuclear guidance calls for establishing controls under uncertainty ([NRC ROP framework](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description), [NRC Human Factors Definition List, item 190](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). The threshold values are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with Experiment E4, simulated historical rollouts measuring false promotions, false rollbacks, and time-to-detection over a preregistered set of injected regressions**.

### Proposal F — Periodic challenge of the observability system — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add a scheduled or release-triggered “detector surveillance” fixture that injects synthetic known events through the ordinary telemetry path: one success, one tool failure, one retry loop, one owner-correction-shaped event, and one deliberately malformed record. Verify end-to-end capture, classification, denominator accounting, schema-version handling, and alerting. Separately, quarterly or after material schema changes, have a fresh evaluator audit a random raw-to-report sample and the promotion decisions driven by it.

This is the closest translation of surveillance plus assessment-of-assessment. It does not merely test parser unit functions; it challenges the whole measurement path and proves the detector can see a known-present event before any absence claim is accepted.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that synthetic surveillance catches disconnected collectors, schema drift, dropped event classes, and denominator corruption before those defects support an absence claim. Evidence: NRC baseline inspections verify reported indicator accuracy, while its annual ROP self-assessment evaluates whether recent changes to the oversight system were effective and whether pre-established goals were met ([NRC ROP framework](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description), [NRC ROP Evaluations](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/program-evaluations)). It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to catch semantic misclassification of novel real-world events by itself. Evidence: seeded fixtures only cover anticipated classes; the independent raw-sample audit is needed for unknown failure modes.

## What this catches that a post-change dashboard misses

The combined mechanism catches distinct failure classes:

1. **No counterfactual dressed up as improvement.** Grade 0 cannot emit the `improved` state.
2. **Regression hidden by task-mix drift.** Paired or interleaved assignment compares candidate and control on contemporaneous, comparable work.
3. **Instrumentation failure reported as zero incidents.** Positive/negative controls and detector surveillance test the path before absence is inferred.
4. **Metric gaming or proxy inversion.** Task acceptance is checked against owner corrections; latency against accepted outcome; blocks against actual side effects.
5. **Aggregation and denominator defects.** A blinded verifier recomputes from immutable raw references and checks missingness.
6. **Expectation leakage.** A/B labels stay masked until the verifier records the result.
7. **Common-mode “two-agent” agreement.** The card records verifier type and disallows same-context self-review from counting as independent.
8. **A benchmark win that does not survive operations.** Grade 4 requires another window, stratum, or environment.
9. **Observability changes that quietly weaken observability.** The measurement system is challenged after schema/parser changes and periodically audited.
10. **Forced binary decisions under uncertainty.** `Indeterminate` is a first-class state with a prescribed next action.

Claims 1, 3, 5, 6, and 10 are **very likely (80-90%)** to be caught because they are enforced by explicit state-machine or recomputation checks, supported by the STAR/independent-verification/ROP mechanisms cited above. Claims 2, 4, 8, and 9 are **likely (55-80%)** to be caught because independent channels and replication expose many—but not all—confounders. Claim 7 is **roughly even (45-55%)** to be caught in practice because recording model/context lineage exposes obvious common mode, while shared latent model bias may remain invisible; settle this with Experiment E2.

## Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### Where structure genuinely matches

- **Consequential state change:** both plant work and harness changes can alter future behavior across many subsequent operations. The consequence magnitude differs radically, but pre-action expectation and post-action verification have the same temporal structure.
- **Latent failure:** a wrong valve state can remain hidden until demanded; a broken detector or evaluation rule can remain hidden until it supports a false decision. Independent direct checks are relevant to both.
- **Proxy measurement:** neither “safety” nor “harness quality” is directly observable as one number. The IAEA explicitly rejects reliance on a single safety-performance aspect ([IAEA-TECDOC-1141](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1141_prn.pdf)).
- **Common-cause risk:** redundant checks are not independent if they share the same information, procedure error, or cognitive cue. DOE’s separation requirements translate directly to evaluator blinding and independent recomputation.
- **Need to validate the evaluator:** the NRC’s ROP evaluates its own implementation and significant changes; a harness measurement path also needs seeded controls and audit.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that these structural matches are sufficient to justify a small record-and-hook mechanism. Evidence: every mapping concerns information ordering, independence, or detector validity rather than reactor-specific physics, and each can be enforced on the existing hook/skill bus.

### Where the analogy fails or becomes dangerous

1. **Stable plant envelope vs. open-ended task distribution.** Nuclear acceptance limits often derive from design and licensing analyses. There is no equivalent immutable “safe operating envelope” for writing, research, or aesthetic judgment. Harness thresholds must be empirical, scoped, and revisable.
2. **Physical independence vs. correlated foundation models.** Two model calls can be separated in time and context yet share architecture, training data, evaluator biases, and reward-model preferences. They are not equivalent to independently measuring a valve position.
3. **Repeatability vs. stochasticity and non-stationarity.** Identical prompts may yield different trajectories, while dependencies and model versions change. Paired trials estimate a local effect, not a timeless property.
4. **Consequence asymmetry.** Nuclear conservatism rationally prioritizes prevention of rare catastrophic releases. Excessive harness conservatism can impose large opportunity cost, tool latency, and friction. Risk grading is essential.
5. **Proceduralization can create new error.** DOE itself warns that participants may relax because they expect the other checker to catch mistakes. A second agent can produce automation complacency rather than assurance ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)).
6. **Indicators invite gaming.** A rigid threshold can redirect the harness toward what is counted. The defence is raw evidence, multiple dimensions, owner-correction signals, and periodic threshold review—not more metrics.
7. **No recovery of lost history.** None of STAR, independent verification, defence in depth, or oversight evaluation manufactures a pre-change baseline. The only honest remedies are prospective comparison, an explicit no-counterfactual grade, or rollback/replay where authorized and technically possible.

Because of these disanalogies, it is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** appropriate to require dual verification on every ordinary agent tool call. Evidence: DOE scopes independent verification to conditions/documents whose undetected error could cause adverse consequences, and universal checks would violate that risk grading ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)). It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a single universal benchmark threshold will remain decision-valid across harness capabilities. Evidence: IAEA uses a hierarchy of leading and lagging indicators and an integrated assessment rather than one direct safety number ([IAEA performance-improvement course](https://www.iaea.org/events/evt2204526), [IAEA-TECDOC-1141](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1141_prn.pdf)).

## Experiments that settle the UNVERIFIED premises

### E1 — A/A calibration and injected-effect sensitivity

Take a frozen, stratified bundle of representative tasks. Run unchanged harness A against unchanged harness A under the exact proposed pairing, randomization, and aggregation. Predeclare acceptable false-positive rate and missingness. Then introduce seeded changes with known directions: one deterministic telemetry drop, one latency increase, one forced retry, and one answer-quality degradation on a subset with objective tests. The system passes only if A/A remains indeterminate/no-change at the target rate and detects seeded effects at preregistered sensitivity. This tests the measurement mechanism before evaluating any real improvement.

### E2 — Independence audit

Create blinded A/B result packets with seeded aggregation defects and a separate set requiring subjective quality judgment. Randomly assign verification to (a) same model/fresh context, (b) different model family/fresh context, and (c) human reviewer. Measure defect detection, inter-rater agreement, correlated misses, cost, and latency. The experiment settles whether “another model call” deserves independent-verifier status and for which claim types.

### E3 — Matrix versus composite decision replay

Freeze historical or synthetic run evidence before showing decision outcomes. Have the evidence matrix and several candidate weighted composites recommend promote/pilot/reject. Then compare them with blinded owner adjudication and injected ground truth. Measure severe-regression promotions separately from ordinary ranking error; do not optimize one aggregate accuracy number.

### E4 — Conservative action-matrix tuning

Replay prospective rollout streams with seeded regressions of varying prevalence and magnitude. Compare candidate evidence thresholds on false promotion, false rollback, detection delay, verification cost, and owner burden. Choose thresholds by consequence class, not globally. Re-run after material model or workload shifts.

Each experiment uses two mechanically independent signals before an absence or success conclusion: an objective injected control plus an independently derived raw-to-report check. Each also contains a known-present control so detector silence can be distinguished from true zero.

## Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

The proposed system is deliberately small:

```text
existing change hook / skill command
        |
        v
append HCAC JSONL record + immutable run references
        |
        +--> paired assignment / control fixtures
        |
        +--> independent aggregation command
        |
        v
policy reads evidence grade + matrix --> pilot / promote / investigate / stop
```

No proposal requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. Storage can be JSONL plus existing run artifacts; execution rides existing hooks/skills; verification is an ordinary fresh process or explicitly assigned reviewer. This passes the anti-overfit gate. The smallest viable implementation is a schema, a pre/post change hook, a deterministic aggregator, five synthetic fixtures, and policy configuration. If that cannot fit approximately within several sub-200-line pieces, implementation should be simplified before a daemon is considered.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that no new daemon is needed for the described mechanism. Evidence: all required operations are event-triggered record validation, deterministic aggregation, and scheduled fixture execution, which fit existing hook/skill buses by construction. The exact line count is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with a throwaway implementation spike that counts nonblank, noncomment lines without installing or deploying it**.

## Rejected translations and dead ends

### Rejected: mandatory two-agent approval for all work

This confuses redundancy with independence, ignores DOE’s risk-based scoping, doubles cost, and may increase complacency. Use independent verification only at promotion gates and high-consequence changes.

### Rejected: one “safety culture” or “harness health” score

IAEA guidance says individual indicators are not direct safety measures and that a complete, integrated picture is needed. A composite would hide the exact discordance the mechanism is meant to surface.

### Rejected: infer the missing baseline from the post-change period

Seasonality, task mix, dependency state, and evaluator drift make the causal counterfactual unidentified. A post-only trend may describe current operation but cannot establish that the change helped.

### Rejected: copy reactor thresholds or color labels literally

The NRC’s thresholds are risk-informed within a mature regulatory and engineering context. Agent-harness thresholds need local calibration through E1–E4. Borrow the graded response structure, not its numeric cutoffs or institutional weight.

### Rejected: “independent” reviewer sees the implementer’s conclusion first

DOE’s mechanism depends on freedom of thought from the performer. A reviewer anchored by the proposed conclusion is peer confirmation, not independent verification. Mask treatment labels and conclusions until the recomputation is recorded.

### Rejected: permanent heavy evaluation infrastructure

The evidence does not justify a new daemon, durable workflow engine, evaluator service, or observability platform. Those components violate the stated anti-overfit constraint and introduce a second system whose own reliability would need measurement.

## Recommended adoption sequence

1. **CONFIG (settings/JSON only):** define evidence grades, the multidimensional evidence matrix, an `indeterminate` state, consequence classes, and the conservative action matrix.
2. **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** implement HCAC schema/timestamp validation and refuse `improved` without a valid comparison grade.
3. **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** implement deterministic paired assignment and raw-reference aggregation.
4. **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** implement known-present/known-failing detector-surveillance fixtures.
5. Run E1 before using the system to judge a real change. Run E2 before allowing model-only verification to satisfy the independent-verifier field. Tune CONFIG thresholds through E3/E4 only after raw results exist.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this order minimizes the chance of building an impressive dashboard atop an unvalidated detector. ## Gaps and limits

- This unit deliberately did not determine whether an equivalent change card, hook, evaluator, or telemetry fixture already exists locally. Any local-absence statement would require repository evidence and two validated detectors; that work belongs to grounding units.
- The DOE handbook is a DOE nuclear/high-hazard human-performance source, not a binding universal rule for all commercial nuclear plants. I therefore use it as the source-field mechanism, not as a claim of universal industry mandate.
