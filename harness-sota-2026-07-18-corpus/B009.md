# B009 — Groundedness engineering: uncited-claim-cost

**Unit:** B009  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** Design a mechanism under which an uncited claim has zero decision value, determine what breaks under enforcement, and specify how to measure catch rate, cost per claim, and false-positive rate.  
**Subject access:** Read-only bundle at `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle`; only this artifact was written.

## Executive decision

Do **not** add a global regex guard that blocks any final answer containing a sentence without a citation. The harness already supplies the counterexample: four text-matching detectors confused mention with use, including a result sentinel that told an agent to discard a successful `ok: true` result because a JSON field name contained `timedout` while its value was `false` (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-22,52-82`). The corpus's alleged owner-correction detector likewise produced 41 false positives in 41 smoke-sampled candidates (`corpus.json:10371-10375`). A fifth syntactic gate would repeat the known architectural fault.

Instead, introduce an **Evidence-Bounded Claim Compiler (EBCC)** only at explicit synthesis boundaries. Producers may say anything in scratch work, but downstream synthesis receives only typed claim records. A claim enters the authoritative findings/conclusions section only if its evidence handles resolve and its admission rule passes. An unadmitted claim receives weight zero: the compiler omits it from authoritative prose and records it in a rejection ledger with the exact reason. It may be exposed as a clearly labelled gap or hypothesis, never silently converted into fact.

This does not eliminate hallucination. Citations can support the wrong proposition, sources can be wrong, a model can omit a claim from its claim record, and an accepted inference can still be bad. The attainable property is narrower: **unsupported factual content at a controlled synthesis boundary is mechanically detectable and cheap to reject**. The authoritative section is compiled from admitted records rather than freely regenerated, so the final model cannot introduce new uncited factual sentences after the gate.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that this architecture will make *syntactically uncited* factual claims unable to influence controlled research/audit syntheses, because the authoritative renderer consumes only admitted records; the uncertainty is implementation bypasses and claim-type mistakes. **UNVERIFIED:** settle with the staged 300-claim prospective experiment in §11, including attempts to bypass the record path and a byte-level assertion that every rendered finding maps to an admitted claim ID.

**Confidence:** only **likely (55-80%)** that EBCC will catch a useful fraction of *false but cited* claims, because citation resolution does not establish entailment or source truth. The evidence is the separation of citation completeness, citation correctness, and source quality in ALCE, plus ALCE's reported incomplete support even for its best evaluated systems ([Gao et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)). **UNVERIFIED:** settle by human-labelling entailment and truth separately in the §11 corpus and reporting both, never a blended “grounded” score.

## 1. Non-negotiable semantics

### 1.1 “Worthless” means zero authority, not erased history

For this design:

`claim value = 0` means the claim cannot:

1. appear in the authoritative findings, factual background, conclusion, or completion receipt;
2. contribute to prioritisation, confidence, severity, or a downstream recommendation;
3. satisfy a Definition-of-Done item;
4. be recalled later as a fact; or
5. substantiate another claim.

The rejected text is retained in a bounded diagnostic record containing `claim_id`, rejection reason, producer/work-unit identity, and evidence handles. Retention matters: silent deletion without a receipt would make missing evidence and detector false positives indistinguishable. The existing agent-as-judge contract already uses the weaker version of this rule: an item lacking a ledger/hash citation must be labelled `UNBACKED` and remains advisory forever (`claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md:54-78,165-174`). EBCC applies zero weight at synthesis rather than merely printing a warning.

### 1.2 Grounded is not true

A claim is **grounded** when a resolvable evidence span supports it under the declared support test. It is **true** only if reality agrees. EBCC must never collapse these concepts:

- a source can itself be false or stale;
- a citation can resolve but not entail the claim;
- one half of a compound claim can be supported while the other is not;
- a test receipt can be genuine but irrelevant to the stated product behavior;
- two “independent” citations can copy the same upstream source;
- a negative finding can be an instrument failure.

FActScore operationalizes long-form evaluation as the percentage of atomic facts supported by a reliable source, explicitly because a response mixes supported and unsupported facts ([Min et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)). SAFE similarly decomposes long responses into individual facts and checks each using search, rather than declaring a whole answer true or false ([Wei et al., 2024, arXiv:2403.18802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). Those papers support atomic evaluation as a useful decomposition; neither proves that an automated verifier is safe enough to delete this owner's supported work.

### 1.3 The unit of enforcement is an atomic claim

The compiler admits or rejects atomic claim records, not paragraphs and not citation tokens. “The hook is registered, fired, and prevented the bug” is at least three claims. A single citation supporting registration cannot launder the firing and effectiveness clauses.

The required kinds are:

| Kind | Example | Admission burden |
|---|---|---|
| `workspace_fact` | “Stop invokes the shared dispatcher.” | Resolvable file span or current tool receipt; bind revision/hash. |
| `external_fact` | “Paper X reports metric Y.” | Resolvable URL/document span, source date, exact supporting excerpt hash. |
| `observation` | “Probe returned `ok:true`.” | Tool-result/event handle owned by this work unit/session. |
| `computed` | “846/3694 = 22.9%.” | Admitted inputs plus reproducible operation/receipt. |
| `absence` | “No registration exists.” | Known-positive control plus two mechanically independent negative signals. |
| `inference` | “This likely increases false drops.” | `depends_on` admitted claims, stated rule/reasoning, confidence band, contrary evidence. |
| `recommendation` | “Deploy in shadow first.” | Admitted premises, cost/risk statement, confidence band; it is advice, not fact. |
| `user_premise` | A pinned owner constraint. | Prompt/brief digest; label as owner-provided, never source-independent fact. |
| `hypothesis` | “The hook payload may lack fence context.” | No authority; may enter only a labelled GAPS/HYPOTHESES section. |
| `procedure` | “Run command X.” | No factual citation required unless it asserts expected output or safety. |

Headings, connective words, acknowledgements, and purely imperative steps are not factual claims. This avoids taxing normal conversation and avoids the use-vs-mention error. Any sentence that embeds a factual premise must be split so the premise is admitted separately.

## 2. Ground truth from this harness

### 2.1 Current gates warn or block after lexical classification; they do not compile evidence-bounded output

The active shared `ClaimWarningModule` runs on `Stop` and `TaskCompleted`, locates completion families through regular expressions, and consults the event ledger (`agents/harness/modules.py:2000-2090`). Its base path injects a warning or optionally blocks Stop when completion language lacks evidence (`modules.py:2111-2137`). Claude's live-bundle settings register the shared dispatcher for both `TaskCompleted` and `Stop` (`claude/settings.json:458-490`), and the module is in dispatcher order (`modules.py:420-431`). The native adapter can return allow, block, continuation, or additional context; it has no response-rewrite result that could delete individual sentences (`agents/harness/adapters.py:159-207`). Therefore global silent sentence deletion is not a native capability of this deployed hook surface.

The dispatcher is explicitly fail-open on internal errors (`agents/harness/dispatcher.py:530-556`). That is reasonable for lifecycle reliability but means it cannot establish the stronger invariant “every authoritative claim passed.” EBCC consequently belongs inside an explicit synthesis/renderer workflow, not as another Stop matcher.

### 2.2 Current “objective receipt” classification can launder irrelevant commands

The ledger labels commands by searching their command text for tokens (`agents/harness/ledger.py:303-310`), and it calls an exit-code-zero classified command a pass even with zero parsed passing tests (`ledger.py:354-371,383-403`). B009 ran this read-only controlled probe with bytecode writing disabled:

```text
known_positive       python -m pytest ... exit 0, "1 passed"
  -> Receipt(receipt_class='test', status='pass', passed=1, failed=0, ...)
search_false_positive rg -n pytest modules.py exit 0, source match
  -> Receipt(receipt_class='test', status='pass', passed=0, failed=0, ...)
failed_control       python -m pytest ... exit 1, "1 failed"
  -> Receipt(receipt_class='test', status='fail', passed=0, failed=1, ...)
```

This confirms both that the detector recognizes a known-present real test result and that an irrelevant search is also misclassified. Audit finding E14 reported the same reproduction; this unit independently reproduced it against the bundled code (`inputs/audit-findings/E14.md:21-26`). Thus evidence admission must validate **evidence type and relationship**, not merely “there is a receipt.”

Freshness is still useful: the ledger only accepts a passing typed receipt whose sequence follows the last mutation (`agents/harness/ledger.py:1065-1117`). EBCC should reuse that ordering after receipt classification is strengthened, not replace the ledger.

### 2.3 Strict fan-out schemas are already expensive

`StructuredOutput` failed 846 of 3,694 calls (22.9%); its largest fingerprint is 294 missing-required-field mismatches (`inputs/corpus.json:103-108,8964-8992`). A mandatory, fully populated claim schema at every producer would predictably expose the same failure mode. This is why EBCC uses a tolerant, append-only JSONL record with four required fields and optional typed extensions, then rejects individual malformed records without failing the producer's entire work unit.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that reusing the existing over-strict fan-out pattern would impose material retry/drop cost, based on the measured 22.9% failure rate and 294 identical schema mismatches. **UNVERIFIED:** the direct EBCC comparison requires an A/B run of strict structured output versus tolerant JSONL on the same 100 producer tasks, measuring record recovery, retries, tokens, and wall time.

### 2.4 Corpus limits prevent a historical effectiveness claim

The corpus covers 11,633 files and 10.62 GB with zero file failures, one bad line, and 32 oversized lines (`inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). However its entire `hooks` section is explicitly marked `DETECTOR_STATUS: BROKEN` and contains no usable fires/blocks data (`corpus.json:13260-13263`). Therefore this unit makes no claim about historical claim-guard fire rate, precision, or benefit. The 2,268 owner-correction candidates are also recall-biased and their 41/41 smoke sample was false positive (`corpus.json:10371-10375`); they cannot serve as labels without manual verification.

## 3. Primary mechanism — Evidence-Bounded Claim Compiler (UNVERIFIED: prospective 300-claim shadow-and-enforce experiment in §11 must establish catch rate, deletion false-positive rate, cost, and bypass resistance)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Build one bounded `claim-compile.py` renderer/resolver used by explicit synthesis skills/commands. It does not create a daemon, second orchestration runtime, database, trained critic, observability service, or guardrail framework.

### 3.1 Minimal record

One line per claim:

```json
{"v":1,"id":"B009-C17","kind":"workspace_fact","text":"Stop invokes the shared dispatcher.","evidence":[{"type":"file_span","uri":"bundle/claude/settings.json","start":484,"end":490,"sha256":"..."}],"observed_at":"2026-07-19T...Z","work_unit":"B009"}
```

Only `v`, `id`, `kind`, and `text` are parser-required. Missing evidence is a valid parsed record with admission `REJECT:no_evidence`; it does not trigger a producer retry. Optional fields include `depends_on`, `confidence`, `source_date`, `revision`, `contrary_evidence`, `positive_control`, `independent_signal`, `scope`, and `sensitivity`. Unknown fields are preserved. This deliberately avoids the measured required-field fan-out failure.

### 3.2 Evidence handles, not ornamental citations

Accepted handle types:

- `file_span`: canonical path, start/end lines, content digest, repository revision or observed timestamp;
- `tool_result`: provider, session/work-unit, event/receipt sequence, exact tool verb, normalized input digest, result digest, status and parsed semantic outcome;
- `url_span`: canonical URL, retrieved timestamp, title/author/date, supporting excerpt digest and local bounded excerpt;
- `prompt_span`: immutable brief digest and line/section for owner-pinned premises;
- `claim_ref`: an already admitted atomic claim ID;
- `calculation`: operation plus admitted input claim IDs and output receipt.

A bare URL, bare file hash, or ledger sequence is insufficient. A hash proves identity, not semantic support. The agent-as-judge currently allows a hash of inspected bytes as its sole citation (`claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md:54-67`); EBCC tightens this by requiring a span locator and typed relation.

### 3.3 Admission pipeline

1. **Parse.** Recover every valid JSON object independently. Malformed lines go to `REJECT:parse`, not a whole-output retry.
2. **Resolve.** Prove that the referenced object and span exist now or in the pinned revision. Recompute the digest. `REJECT:unresolvable` and `REJECT:stale` have zero value.
3. **Check ownership/freshness.** Tool evidence must belong to the same work unit/session or be explicitly imported with provenance. Verification claims require a receipt after the relevant mutation. This closes the audit-reported cross-session evidence leakage risk; the current receipt reader accepts empty `turn_id` as matching a requested turn (`agents/harness/ledger.py:1081-1117`; audit claim corroboration at `inputs/audit-findings/A11.md:32-35`).
4. **Check kind-specific burden.** An absence needs the known-positive control and two independent negative signals. A computation needs admitted inputs. A recommendation needs admitted premises. A completion claim needs an appropriate semantic receipt, not just a command token.
5. **Check support relation.** Deterministic checks handle exact quotations, direct tool outcomes, file-presence/registration facts, arithmetic, and claim dependencies. Ambiguous natural-language entailment is `INSUFFICIENT`, not accepted by guess.
6. **Detect contradiction.** If admitted evidence handles explicitly disagree, render both under `CONFLICT` and withhold a single factual conclusion. Do not choose by source count alone.
7. **Compile.** Render admitted `text` without model paraphrase, adding canonical citations. Group/order by metadata; headers are from a fixed vocabulary. Recommendations may render only after every `depends_on` claim is admitted.
8. **Receipt.** Emit counts and IDs for `accepted`, `conflict`, `hypothesis`, and every rejection reason. Assert mechanically that every authoritative bullet carries exactly one admitted claim ID.

The decisive feature is step 7: a model is not asked to write fluent prose and then police itself. The compiler renders only already-admitted text. Unsupported claim records can exist, but they cannot enter the authoritative channel.

### 3.4 Output channels

The artifact has four visibly distinct channels:

1. **AUTHORITATIVE FINDINGS** — admitted factual, observed, and computed claims only;
2. **INFERENCES / RECOMMENDATIONS** — admitted premises plus explicit confidence and reversal conditions;
3. **GAPS / UNVERIFIED HYPOTHESES** — zero-authority content, with the experiment that would promote it;
4. **REJECTION RECEIPT** — counts/reasons and a path to bounded diagnostics, not rejected prose copied into the main report.

This preserves useful uncertainty without allowing “uncited therefore invisible” to masquerade as completeness.

## 4. Producer contract (UNVERIFIED: the 100-task strict-schema-vs-tolerant-JSONL A/B in §11 must verify recovery and cost)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add an explicit claim-record instruction to research, audit, reviewer, and agent handoff templates: “Narrative is scratch; only emitted claim records are eligible for synthesis.” Do not apply it to ordinary interactive answers, source code, or creative writing.

The contract should say:

- split compound claims;
- cite the smallest supporting span actually inspected;
- label user-pinned material `user_premise`;
- never infer support from a filename, URL title, command name, exit code alone, or another agent's prose;
- emit unsupported but useful possibilities as `hypothesis` with a settling experiment;
- give negative/absence claims a positive control and two independent signals;
- attach confidence bands to inference/recommendation records;
- do not retry the entire handoff when one record lacks evidence.

Raw child-agent prose must not be prefixed into the synthesizer. This agrees with existing agent contracts that prohibit forwarding raw agent output and require observed evidence for success, e.g. `claude/agents/harness-optimizer.md:53-71` and `claude/agents/refactor-cleaner.md:92-110`.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that prompt/config alone will increase the fraction of atomic, attributable claims, based on the existing claim/handoff disciplines and the research benchmarks' use of atomic decomposition. It is not enforcement; a producer can omit or misclassify records. **UNVERIFIED:** settle with the §11 producer A/B and score atomicity, evidence completeness, and omitted claims against human annotation.

## 5. Downstream zero-weight rule (UNVERIFIED: adversarial fan-in test in §11 must prove raw prose cannot influence rankings or conclusions)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Fan-in prompts and ranking logic consume `accepted.jsonl`, never raw work-unit prose. A claim absent from `accepted.jsonl` has numerical weight 0 and cannot be cited through a sibling's summary. Every inference lists `depends_on`; if any dependency is rejected or later tainted, the inference is withheld.

This uses an existing concept rather than a new graph store. The audit found a `derived_from` taint reader but no production writer (`inputs/audit-findings/D30.md:55-58`). Claim IDs and `depends_on` fields can be kept in bounded JSONL and traversed in memory. No vector DB or knowledge graph is warranted.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that an explicit accepted-only input boundary prevents uncited upstream prose from directly influencing a deterministic compilation, because the data dependency is structural. The remaining uncertainty is accidental side channels such as raw prose still entering the prompt. **UNVERIFIED:** settle by planting unique canary claims only in rejected/raw prose and testing that none of their tokens or conclusions appear in compiled output across 100 fan-in runs.

## 6. Evidence resolver hardening (UNVERIFIED: typed receipt and locator validation suite in §11 must pass before any enforcement)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add kind-specific resolvers to `claim-compile.py`; reuse the event ledger for durable IDs and freshness, but do not trust the current receipt classifier as admission proof.

For command receipts, require:

- parsed executed verb/argv rather than a token anywhere in a search or document payload;
- a class-specific semantic success signal (e.g. test runner identity plus test summary/protocol), not exit 0 alone;
- same work-unit/session ownership;
- post-mutation freshness where applicable;
- exact failing-command rerun for “fixed” when a failure fingerprint exists;
- scope correspondence between evidence and claim (one unit test cannot support “the product works end to end”).

For file claims, require a line span and digest at the inspected revision. For external claims, retain a bounded supporting excerpt internally so link rot can be distinguished from fabricated citation. For sensitive/private sources, render a redacted handle while retaining internal provenance; never force secret text into a public citation.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that typed resolvers will reject the confirmed `rg ... pytest` false receipt and the documented `timedout:false` result-sentinel error, because both become impossible when the detector reads structural verb/result fields rather than arbitrary payload substrings. Evidence: the controlled receipt probe in §2.2 and live defect D1b (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:63-82`). **UNVERIFIED:** settle with regression fixtures for every 12+ known false fire, plus known-positive real tests, failures, JSON successes, prose mentions, negations, and a real executable marker.

## 7. Semantic support: shadow only, never immediate deletion

A syntactically valid citation can be irrelevant. Some semantic support check is necessary for cited claims, but an LLM/NLI judge is itself fallible and correlated with the generator. ALCE evaluates citation correctness and completeness separately and reported substantial incompleteness in evaluated systems ([Gao et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)). A newer preprint reports a coverage/correctness trade-off between generation-time and post-hoc citation approaches and identifies retrieval as a major driver ([Huang et al., 2025, arXiv:2509.21557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21557)); this is frontier evidence, not local validation and not by itself grounds for deployment.

### Semantic support scorer experiment (UNVERIFIED: human-labelled entailment trial in §11 must meet the deletion-precision gate)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Use an existing model call in evaluation/shadow mode to label each `(atomic claim, evidence span)` as `SUPPORTED`, `CONTRADICTED`, or `INSUFFICIENT`, and require a quoted rationale span. Do not build or train a critic. Do not let its verdict delete or admit claims in production until independently calibrated.

Until calibration:

- deterministic support checks may admit;
- semantic `CONTRADICTED` or `INSUFFICIENT` only routes to human/sample review;
- the scorer never converts an unresolvable citation into support;
- a same-model score is not an independent signal;
- the shadow log records would-have-dropped cases and later owner/human labels.

**Confidence:** **roughly even (45-55%)** that an off-the-shelf model-based support scorer can reach the extremely low false-drop rate this owner requires across code, RE, scientific, and general-web claims. Evidence for possible utility comes from automated atomic factuality work (FActScore and SAFE); evidence against promotion is this workspace's 12+ semantic-role false fires and the existing agent-as-judge policy that remains advisory (`claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md:15-41,101-130`). **UNVERIFIED:** only the cross-domain human-labelled prospective trial settles it.

## 8. What breaks under enforcement

### 8.1 Novel synthesis is penalised

Many valuable outputs are inferences that no source states verbatim. A naïve “citation or delete” rule removes causal synthesis, design judgment, and recommendations. EBCC therefore admits `inference` and `recommendation` records based on admitted premises, explicit reasoning, confidence, and reversal conditions. They remain visibly different from facts.

**Confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that treating all non-quoted synthesis as unsupported would remove useful content, because recommendations and derived calculations are not normally verbatim source statements; the residual uncertainty is task mix. Evidence: the owner's task explicitly requests design, alternatives, and forward mechanisms, not only extraction.

### 8.2 Recall falls before precision rises

Omitting unsupported claims can make a report deceptively sparse, especially when retrieval failed rather than when the proposition is false. Rejected claims must therefore produce a visible coverage receipt: “7 candidate claims omitted: 4 no evidence, 2 stale, 1 conflict.” For a requested exhaustive report, GAPS should name the missing experiment.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that hard admission reduces factual coverage at first, supported by ALCE's reported citation-completeness limitations and the 2025 preprint's coverage/correctness trade-off. **UNVERIFIED:** quantify retained human-valued claims and omission reasons in §11 rather than assuming the precision gain is worth it.

### 8.3 Compound-claim splitting can distort meaning

Automatic atomisation may lose qualifiers, anaphora, negation, or the relationship between clauses. This is precisely where a high-FP semantic detector becomes dangerous. Producers should create records; the compiler should not regex-split arbitrary final prose for enforcement. Automatic post-hoc atomisation remains evaluation-only.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that producer-side records preserve meaning better than regex sentence splitting, based on the confirmed use-vs-mention and negation failures (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-47`). **UNVERIFIED:** a blinded human comparison on 100 compound statements must measure lost qualifiers and false deletions.

### 8.4 Citations can launder falsehood

A plausible URL or ledger ID can create undeserved trust. Resolution catches fabricated references; span support catches irrelevance; neither guarantees source truth. High-stakes or absence/completion claims still require independent signals. Source independence must follow provenance, not domain count.

**Confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that citation presence alone cannot guarantee truth; this follows logically from false/stale sources and is consistent with ALCE's distinct correctness/quality dimensions. Hallucination remains possible by design.

### 8.5 Negative and absence claims become expensive

An absence claim needs a known-positive control plus two mechanically independent signals. That raises tool cost but prevents the exact seven wrong absence conclusions in the live defect record (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). EBCC should reject an under-probed absence as `INSUFFICIENT`, not assert the opposite.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that this raises cost per admitted absence claim while lowering false absence conclusions, because it mandates extra probes and directly addresses seven observed failures. **UNVERIFIED:** measure tool calls and adjudicated errors per absence claim in the §11 stratified set.

### 8.6 Temporal and revision drift invalidate once-good evidence

File lines move, branches diverge, web pages change, and service status expires. A citation without revision/time scope is not durable proof. Re-resolving all sources on every render could be expensive or impossible, so EBCC distinguishes immutable digests, revision-bound workspace facts, and TTL-bound operational facts. Expiry yields `STALE`, not “false.”

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that revision/time binding prevents some stale-evidence reuse; the harness already uses post-mutation sequence freshness (`ledger.py:1093-1117`). **UNVERIFIED:** mutation and link-change fixtures must test staleness detection and measure needless invalidations.

### 8.7 Private evidence conflicts with visible citations

Credentials, proprietary files, or private messages may substantiate a claim but cannot safely be printed. A public-facing renderer needs redacted internal handles and possibly a public downgrade (“verified against private source; not independently inspectable”). A missing public citation is not the same as missing internal evidence.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that redacted handles preserve auditability for the owner without leaking sensitive text, because hashes/locators can bind internal evidence; the usefulness to an external reader is necessarily lower. **UNVERIFIED:** red-team the renderer with seeded secrets and have an authorised reviewer resolve the redacted handle.

### 8.8 Deterministic compilation is less fluent

Rendering admitted claim text verbatim produces repetitive bullets and weaker narrative transitions. Allowing a model to paraphrase after admission reopens the unsupported-claim channel. The authoritative section should accept reduced elegance; an optional clearly non-authoritative executive narrative may be generated only if a second claim map proves every factual clause maps to admitted IDs.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that deterministic rendering reduces fluency but closes post-gate factual injection, by construction. **UNVERIFIED:** blinded readers in §11 should score usefulness/readability, while byte-level mapping measures introduced claims.

### 8.9 Host-global enforcement would wedge ordinary work

The current adapter can block or inject at Stop but cannot rewrite selected response spans (`agents/harness/adapters.py:159-207`). Global enforcement would require repeated stop/revision loops or a new interception runtime. The former risks token drains already acknowledged in the claim module's re-entrant Stop guard (`agents/harness/modules.py:2054-2067`); the latter violates the anti-overfit ban on a second orchestration runtime absent extraordinary evidence.

**Decision:** do not enforce globally. Restrict EBCC to explicit high-value synthesis workflows.

### 8.10 Strict schemas can lose whole work units

Making every field mandatory recreates the measured 22.9% StructuredOutput error channel (`corpus.json:8964-8992`). Tolerant per-line parsing may admit fewer records, but it preserves valid siblings and surfaces exact rejections without retry loops.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that tolerant per-record failure containment is more reliable than whole-envelope rejection in this environment, given the measured schema failures. **UNVERIFIED:** the §11 A/B must measure complete claim recovery, partial recovery, and retries.

## 9. Rejected alternatives

### 9.1 Global “sentence must contain `[n]`” Stop hook

Rejected. It tests punctuation, not support; it will match prose mentions and miss citation laundering. It cannot silently rewrite the answer on the current hook API. It repeats D1's confirmed use-vs-mention defect.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** if built, but not recommended because small size does not cure the discriminator error.

### 9.2 Ask the same model to self-cite and trust the result

Rejected as enforcement. It is useful producer guidance only. The model can fabricate a locator or cite an irrelevant span; same-model checking is correlated.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** if used as guidance, with zero grant authority.

### 9.3 Post-hoc semantic sentence deletion on every answer

Rejected pending calibration. It may catch uncited prose but its false-positive surface includes claim splitting, entailment, negation, mention/use, and scope. In this workspace, a false detector already instructed the agent to discard a successful result (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:63-76`).

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** for cross-provider transparent rewriting. It reintroduces a second runtime and fails the anti-overfit gate; no evidence here justifies it.

### 9.4 Vector database / knowledge graph provenance system

Rejected. Claim IDs, evidence handles, and dependencies fit bounded JSONL plus the existing ledger. Audit finding D30 indicates the missing piece is a production `derived_from` writer, not a graph database (`inputs/audit-findings/D30.md:55-58`).

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and explicitly reintroduces a previously rejected vector-DB/KG memory component. No local scale or query evidence warrants it.

### 9.5 Trained critic or agent-as-judge as gate

Rejected. The existing judge contract explicitly stays advisory, never grants done, and requires external calibration (`claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md:15-41,101-130`). A trained critic is also a prohibited previously rejected component.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and fails the anti-overfit gate.

### 9.6 Drop all unsupported text with no rejection receipt

Rejected. It would make detector failure look like report completeness and suppress the evidence needed to debug the gate—the same self-amplifying failure documented for prose guards (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:24-41`). Zero authority is required; invisible diagnostics are not.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** if implemented as a prompt, but it is operationally unsafe.

## 10. Cost model and success metrics

The unit of accounting is an **atomic candidate claim**, not a response and not a detector fire.

For each stage report:

```text
false-claim catch rate = false claims rejected / all false claims
unsupported catch rate = unsupported claims rejected / all unsupported claims
deletion false-positive rate = supported claims rejected / all supported claims
admission false-positive rate = unsupported claims admitted / all admitted claims
citation-resolution precision = truly resolvable handles / handles marked resolvable
entailment precision = truly supported pairs / pairs marked supported
cost per candidate claim = (wall ms, model tokens, tool calls, currency) / candidates
cost per caught false claim = total stage cost / false claims uniquely caught by stage
coverage retention = useful supported claims rendered / useful supported claims available
decision contamination = conclusions/recommendations depending on rejected claims / all conclusions
```

Do not report one “groundedness score.” A detector that rejects everything has perfect unsupported recall and catastrophic false positives. Report the confusion matrix, per-kind rates, confidence intervals, and cost distributions. Separate syntactically uncited, unresolvable, non-entailing, stale, contradicted, source-false, and compound-partial cases.

### Promotion gates

These are design thresholds, not measured results:

1. Deterministic compiler invariant: **0** authoritative bullets without an admitted claim ID in all tests.
2. Known-false-fire regression: **0** false deletions across every recorded 12+ use-vs-mention/negation/success fixture, while all known-positive controls still fire.
3. Hard-deletion precision: lower 95% confidence bound at least **99.5%** on supported-vs-rejected labels in each enforced claim kind. This intentionally makes promotion difficult because the brief states a high-FP detector is worse than none.
4. Unsupported recall is reported but has no threshold until deletion precision passes.
5. Semantic scorer remains shadow if any domain fails the precision gate or if fewer than 200 human-labelled supported cases exist in that domain.
6. No enforcement if median incremental cost exceeds the owner-selected budget; report the curve rather than inventing a budget.
7. Any confirmed supported-claim deletion auto-demotes the implicated semantic rule to shadow pending root-cause and regression fixture. Deterministic resolver defects similarly fail closed for the **artifact** (emit degraded receipt), not by silently publishing a partial report.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that these gates yield lower false-drop risk than immediate hard deployment, because they force prospective labels and include the exact local failure class. **UNVERIFIED:** the gates' practical attainability and opportunity cost are outputs of §11, not assumptions.

The adopted architecture passes the gate:

- It adds bounded JSONL records and an under-~200-line compiler/resolver, not a daemon.

The more radical global rewriting alternative is rejected because the host adapter cannot rewrite response spans, the new runtime would violate the anti-overfit constraint, and no measured false-claim recall/precision evidence justifies that complexity.

## 14. Frontier research synthesis

The research frontier supports four design choices, with important limits:

1. **Atomic claims:** FActScore decomposes long-form output because paragraph-level binary judgment hides mixtures of supported and unsupported facts ([Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)). This supports record granularity, not automatic deletion safety.
2. **Separate citation dimensions:** ALCE evaluates fluency, correctness, and citation quality and reported large citation-completeness gaps in its evaluated systems ([Gao et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)). This supports separate resolution, entailment, and quality metrics.
3. **Search-assisted verification:** SAFE searches and judges individual facts ([Wei et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). This supports a shadow evaluator and evaluation methodology; its authors' benchmark results do not establish precision in this owner's mixed code/RE/science corpus.
4. **Generation-time versus post-hoc trade-off:** a 2025 preprint reports coverage/correctness trade-offs and recommends retrieval-centric post-hoc citation for some high-stakes uses ([Huang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21557)). It is a preprint and locally **UNVERIFIED**; the §11 A/B between producer records and post-hoc extraction would settle relevance here.

No cited work promises hallucination elimination, and this design does not either.

## 16. Final recommendation

Adopt **zero-weight evidence admission at explicit synthesis boundaries**, not global citation policing. Build the smallest deterministic compiler that can resolve typed evidence handles, enforce kind-specific burdens, compile only admitted atomic claims, and emit a rejection receipt. Reuse the ledger after repairing receipt semantics and ownership. Keep semantic entailment and fluent post-hoc rewriting in shadow until they demonstrate extraordinarily high deletion precision on this owner's cross-domain corpus.

This makes an uncited claim worthless where authority is actually created—fan-in and synthesis—while preserving exploratory reasoning upstream. It catches unsupported claims cheaply by structure, makes misses and deletions auditable, and avoids trusting another uncalibrated text matcher. It does not make hallucination impossible; it makes one important class of unsupported output mechanically visible and unable to silently acquire decision weight.
