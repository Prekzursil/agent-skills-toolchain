# X169 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → cost-control

**Unit:** X169  
**Lens:** military doctrine (commander's intent, OODA, after-action review, red teaming) applied to spending compute and quota where it changes the answer  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** This is an ideation/research unit. Per the task, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, corpus, audit, or subject repository. All harness-specific feasibility claims are therefore explicitly marked **UNVERIFIED** and paired with an experiment.

## Bottom line

The borrow transfers, but only in a narrow and useful form. The transferable military mechanism is **intent-led, decision-triggered concentration of resources on a shifting main effort, followed by an after-action update**. It is not “make an agent more military,” and it is not merely “run OODA faster.”

Military doctrine separates four things that cost-control systems often blur:

1. **The outcome that must survive changing circumstances** — commander's intent.
2. **The place where scarce resources matter most now** — the main effort, which can shift.
3. **The information worth paying to obtain** — information requirements linked to actual decision points and triggers.
4. **The evidence that changes the next allocation rule** — assessment and after-action review.

Translated to an agent harness, this becomes a small control protocol: every task gets an outcome/evidence contract and a bounded reserve; execution begins at the cheapest credible posture; additional model calls, context, search, verification, or agents are released only against an explicit answer-changing decision; the “main effort” may move as uncertainty changes; and sampled completed runs compare each expensive tranche with whether it changed the accepted answer.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the direct structural match between doctrine's temporary resource prioritization ([FM 3-94, paras. 2-88–2-89](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A4FC8E8D-7A8C-48F9-91FA-33A2490EACDA-1398867642924/fm3_94.pdf)) and demonstrated per-query LLM routing/cascades ([RouteLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665); [FrugalGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)), that this control skeleton can outperform a uniform compute policy on a heterogeneous task mix. The exact savings and quality effect on this owner's workload are **UNVERIFIED; settle with a shadow replay experiment on a stratified sample of historical tasks, comparing accepted-answer quality, correction rate, latency, tokens, tool calls, and dollars against the current policy**.

The analogy must stop at the control skeleton. Combat has an adaptive opponent, lethal consequences, trained human subordinates, command authority, and physical resources with movement and readiness constraints. An agent task often has none of these. Importing tempo worship, hierarchy, jargon, or permanent red teams would be cargo cult. In particular, OODA does not by itself solve cost allocation: going around a loop faster can increase spend faster.

## 1. The specific military mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 Commander's intent: preserve purpose while decentralizing method

Army mission command addresses action under uncertainty when a superior cannot specify every future contingency. ADRP 6-0 defines mission command as authority and direction using mission orders to enable disciplined initiative within the commander's intent, and describes it as **centralized intent with dispersed execution** ([ADRP 6-0, paras. 1-5 and surrounding discussion](https://home.army.mil/wood/application/files/7715/5751/8336/ADRP_6_0_Mission_Command.pdf)). The same publication says a well-formed intent conveys the operation's **purpose, key tasks, and desired outcome**, while defining the boundaries within which subordinates can adapt when orders no longer fit ([ADRP 6-0, paras. 14–18](https://home.army.mil/wood/application/files/7715/5751/8336/ADRP_6_0_Mission_Command.pdf)). An Army operational account makes the resource connection explicit: mission orders focus on results rather than methods, provide priorities, and allocate resources ([“Applying mission command to overcome challenges,” U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/179942/applying_mission_command_to_overcome_challenges)).

In its own field, the mechanism avoids two symmetrical failures:

- A detailed order becomes obsolete when the situation changes, leaving subordinates compliant but ineffective.
- Unbounded autonomy fragments effort, with local actions no longer serving the larger end state.

Intent supplies a stable objective and limits; decentralized actors select the locally appropriate method. This is not a budget mechanism by itself. Its relevance is that one cannot evaluate whether additional resources “changed the answer” without first specifying what outcome and evidence count.

### 1.2 Main effort: concentrate resources temporarily, then shift

FM 3-94 states that commanders establish main and supporting efforts to create priorities for support and resources. The main effort is the subordinate mission currently most critical to overall success, receives priority, and commonly shifts during execution. The doctrine explicitly calls designation of a main effort a **temporary** prioritization of resource allocation, while noting that a commander may withhold resources for a later decisive operation or future mission ([FM 3-94, paras. 2-88–2-89](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A4FC8E8D-7A8C-48F9-91FA-33A2490EACDA-1398867642924/fm3_94.pdf)).

This is the core cost-control borrow. It differs from a uniform austerity rule and from “always use the best.” Resources are weighted toward the bottleneck whose resolution currently has the greatest effect on mission success, while a reserve prevents current work from consuming all future optionality.

Three details matter:

- **Criticality is time-dependent.** Research may be the main effort early; disambiguating a requirement, executing a test, or validating a citation may become the main effort later.
- **Supporting efforts remain bounded, not necessarily zeroed.** They receive enough to enable the main effort, not equal shares by default.
- **Reserve is deliberate.** Spending everything on the first plausible bottleneck makes later adaptation impossible.

### 1.3 Decision points and critical information requirements: pay for information only when it can drive a choice

Joint assessment doctrine links information and intelligence requirements to commander's intent, end states, objectives, and decision points; requires methods for detecting decision triggers; and calls for recommendations for plan adjustments, branches, and sequels ([JP 3-24, pp. VI-10–VI-11](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp3_24.pdf)). It warns that too many requirements make assessment burdensome and unmanageable, while poorly defined critical assumptions leave a force without enough information to understand effects and waste opportunities to improve resource allocation (same source, p. VI-10). During execution, collection occurs both periodically and responsively to decision triggers, and assessment asks whether assumptions remain valid, objectives are being met, or a decision point has been reached (same source, pp. VI-10–VI-11).

A sustainment example shows the operational mechanics: identify the decisions required, turn them into information requirements, define indicators, and place them in a decision-support matrix. The examples include when to resupply and thresholds based on quantity, cost, maintenance, transport, weather, or threat ([“Modification of the planning process for sustainers, part 2,” U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/101130/modification_of_the_planning_process_for_sustainers_part_2_the_military_decisionmaking_process)).

The important principle is not “collect more telemetry.” It is **decision relevance**:

> If no plausible result of a check changes the next action, the check is not a critical information requirement; it is overhead.

### 1.4 OODA: reassess after action, but do not confuse tempo with value

The OODA loop is a repeating observe–orient–decide–act sequence originally developed for fighter combat; its classic competitive claim is relative decision-cycle advantage ([U.S. Army, “An Easy and Effective Tool for Sense-Making”](https://www.lineofdeparture.army.mil/Journals/Military-Intelligence/Military-Intelligence-Archive/2026-January-June/An-Easy-and-Effective-Tool/)). In this unit, OODA contributes only a feedback cadence:

1. observe the task state and remaining budget;
2. orient against intent, evidence requirements, and current uncertainty;
3. decide whether the main effort or spend posture changes;
4. act once, then reassess.

The competitive “turn inside the enemy's loop” premise does not generally transfer. A compiler error, ambiguous request, or missing citation is not an intelligent adversary with its own OODA loop. Faster cycling is **roughly even (45-55%)** as a cost-control benefit: it may terminate waste earlier, or it may create extra calls and coordination overhead. Evidence is the loop's focus on repeated decisions, not any demonstrated cost result in agent harnesses; **UNVERIFIED—settle by A/B testing event-triggered reassessment against both fixed intervals and no explicit loop, measuring controller overhead and avoided downstream spend**.

### 1.5 After-action review: convert execution evidence into the next policy

The Army's current TC 7-0.1 provides processes for planning, preparing, conducting, and following up AARs; the Army describes AARs as a primary tool for training evaluation and for guiding participants' self-discovery about how to perform better ([TC 7-0.1, February 2025](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A6C09408-2436-47A4-93A3-6684A1B59042-1739993594606/TC7_0x1.pdf); [Army announcement](https://www.army.mil/article/283214/tmd_publishes_training_circular_to_augment_fm_and_adp_7_0)).

For cost-control, the AAR supplies the missing counterfactual discipline: not merely “how much did we spend?” but “which spend tranche changed a decision, artifact, or verification result?” AAR does not prove the counterfactual—one cannot observe the same run both with and without a call—but it generates hypotheses that replay can test.

### 1.6 Red teaming: challenge the allocation premise, selectively

Army design methodology describes red-team members as trained to broaden understanding, explore alternatives, challenge assumptions, consider other perspectives, identify vulnerabilities and opportunities, and identify areas for assessment. It lists multiple modes: review a planning product, facilitate the main team, or independently frame a portion and compare results ([ATP 5-0.1, paras. 2-26–2-27](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/9315A0BC-9300-4192-9340-788A2950A0CD-1436193112061/atp5_0x1.pdf)).

For cost-control, the valuable target is not every generated sentence. It is the expensive allocation decision: “Is this really the main uncertainty?”, “Would the proposed verification distinguish competing answers?”, and “Are we buying redundant confidence from correlated agents?” Permanent or automatic multi-agent red teaming would often cost more than it saves.

## 2. Concrete translation: Decision-Linked Compute Maneuver

I call the translated protocol **Decision-Linked Compute Maneuver (DLCM)**. The name is disposable; the mechanics are the proposal.

### 2.1 State carried per task

At task admission, represent the following small state object:

```yaml
intent:
  purpose: "why the owner wants this"
  end_state: "observable completion condition"
  key_evidence: ["tests", "primary citations", "owner acceptance criterion"]
  constraints: ["read-only", "deadline", "safety", "scope"]
budget:
  ceiling: "tokens/tool calls/dollars/time, whichever is enforceable"
  reserve: "held for late verification or surprise"
main_effort:
  current_uncertainty: "the one bottleneck most likely to change the answer"
  next_decision: "what choice resolving it enables"
  release_condition: "observable trigger for more compute"
supporting_efforts:
  - name: "context/search/formatting/etc."
    ceiling: "bounded allocation"
decision_log: []
```

The state must stay short. If maintaining it requires another long model call each step, the controller defeats itself.

### 2.2 Spend postures

Use three postures rather than a single model/effort setting:

| Posture | Use | Typical actions | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Probe** | Establish task shape cheaply | inspect metadata, retrieve targeted context, run a narrow test, ask one clarifying question when material | task is routine and evidenced, or an explicit uncertainty is identified |
| **Concentrate** | Resolve the current main uncertainty | stronger reasoning, broader primary-source search, one specialist, focused reproduction, alternative hypothesis | evidence resolves the decision, progress plateaus, or a different bottleneck becomes dominant |
| **Assure** | Protect the end state | tests, citation checking, destructive-action review, schema validation, selective independent review | acceptance evidence passes or the remaining gap is disclosed |

This is not a mandated cheap-model-first cascade. Some tasks have a high cost of early misorientation; beginning with the strongest capable model may be cheaper overall. The “cheapest credible posture” is selected against risk and expected rework, not unit token price.

### 2.3 Escalation must name an answer-changing decision

Before releasing an expensive tranche, record:

```text
DECISION: What choice is blocked?
SIGNAL: What observation would distinguish the choices?
ACTION: What is the least-cost credible way to obtain that signal?
STOP: What result stops this line of work?
MAX: What is the tranche ceiling?
```

Examples:

- “Run the full test suite” is unjustified if the next decision is which parser function is failing and a focused test isolates it.
- “Ask three agents” is unjustified if all see the same evidence and no disagreement would change the action.
- “Fetch ten more sources” is unjustified if two independent primary sources already settle the claim and additional sources only decorate it.
- “Use a stronger model” is justified when the cheap attempt fails a machine-checkable verifier or when unresolved ambiguity changes the implementation branch.
- “Spend on verification” is justified when a pass/fail result controls delivery, rollback, or escalation.

This echoes the doctrine warning that too many information requirements make assessment unmanageable and that information should connect to decision points ([JP 3-24, p. VI-10](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp3_24.pdf)).

### 2.4 Shift the main effort, do not merely add effort

When a trigger fires, the controller should normally **reallocate**, not only increase the total:

- If direct inspection reveals the defect, stop broad research and shift to reproduction/verification.
- If implementation passes focused tests, shift from generation to assurance.
- If primary evidence invalidates the plan, stop polishing it and shift to reframing.
- If two retries reproduce the same failure fingerprint, stop retrying and shift to diagnosis or a different mechanism.
- If the owner changes the acceptance criterion, retire now-irrelevant work rather than preserving sunk-cost momentum.

The main effort can be a verification step. Military “support” language must not seduce the harness into treating checking as secondary; the deciding uncertainty determines priority.

### 2.5 Preserve a reserve

Hold a small task reserve for late-discovered risk, final verification, or one controlled recovery. A specific reserve fraction is **UNVERIFIED; settle by replaying several candidate fractions (including zero and dynamically sized reserves) on cost/quality frontiers grouped by task class**. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on FM 3-94's explicit withholding of resources for decisive or future operations and the common late arrival of verification work, that some reserve policy will beat spending to the admission ceiling immediately; the band is broad because agent budgets are replenishable and more fungible than deployed military assets.

### 2.6 Close the loop with sampled compute AARs

For a stratified sample of completed runs—especially high-cost, owner-corrected, failed, or surprisingly cheap successful runs—ask:

1. What was the intended end state and evidence bar?
2. What did the controller predict would be the main uncertainty?
3. Which tranches changed a decision, artifact, or verifier result?
4. Which tranches repeated the same evidence or polished without changing acceptance?
5. Where did under-spending cause rework, correction, or escaped defects?
6. Which trigger or stop rule should change for the next comparable task?

Do not automatically turn every anecdote into a global rule. Promote a rule only after replay or a controlled shadow period shows a better cost/quality frontier across more than the originating case.

## 3. What this would catch that blunt cost controls miss

The proposed controller targets both overspending and false economy:

| Missed pattern | Decision-linked signal | Controller response |
|---|---|---|
| Frontier model or maximum reasoning for every task | no high-consequence or unresolved decision at admission | stay in Probe or use a cheaper capable route |
| Uniform low-cost model despite difficult/high-risk task | failed verifier, high consequence, ambiguity affecting branch | concentrate stronger compute on the specific uncertainty |
| Search/research accumulation | new source cannot change a cited claim or branch | stop collection |
| Multi-agent fan-out as a ritual | agents share evidence/prompt and no disagreement policy exists | do not fan out; request a deliberately independent alternative only at a decision point |
| Retry loops | repeated fingerprint with no new observation or changed action | shift main effort from retry to diagnosis/fallback |
| Context inflation | added material is not tied to intent, a current decision, or acceptance evidence | exclude or summarize it |
| Premature polishing | correctness/acceptance uncertainty remains dominant | move compute from prose refinement to assurance |
| Exhausting budget before tests | reserve threshold reached without acceptance evidence | stop supporting work and protect assurance tranche |
| Verification theater | test/check outcome does not control any action | remove the check or define its decision consequence |
| Cheap first pass that creates expensive rework | task class repeatedly escalates after cheap attempts | start that class at Concentrate in future |
| Sunk-cost continuation after premise failure | observation invalidates a critical assumption | abandon branch and reorient |
| Cost reporting without learning | ledger shows spend but not decisions changed | sample for compute AAR and replay |

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the explicit stop/shift semantics and decision-linked collection doctrine, that this catches waste from redundant search, undifferentiated fan-out, and repeated retries earlier than a task-level hard cap. **UNVERIFIED—settle by annotating 100 high-cost historical runs with the first point at which later calls stopped changing the delivered answer, then simulate DLCM triggers against that point**.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the protected Assure posture and reserve, that it also catches under-spending that a pure “cheapest model” router misses. **UNVERIFIED—settle by measuring owner corrections and verifier failures under equal total budget for cost-only routing versus DLCM**.

## 4. Proposals

Add a compact task-state schema containing purpose, observable end state, key evidence, constraints, budget ceiling, reserve, current main uncertainty, next decision, release condition, and supporting-effort ceilings. Make completion compare against end state and evidence, not a procedural checklist.

The feasibility premise that the current harness has a task-admission/config surface capable of carrying this state is **UNVERIFIED; settle by enumerating documented native task, hook, and run-metadata fields and proving a known test field survives from admission to completion**.

Expected effect is **very likely (80-90%)**, because the card makes allocation criteria explicit and requires little machinery; evidence is the doctrine role of purpose/key tasks/end state in enabling adaptive execution ([ADRP 6-0](https://home.army.mil/wood/application/files/7715/5751/8336/ADRP_6_0_Mission_Command.pdf)). Whether agents will reliably keep the card current is **UNVERIFIED; settle with a blinded audit comparing recorded state to actual next decisions on 50 runs**.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED hook interception and accounting surfaces: decision-linked tranche governor — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Implement a small pre-expensive-action hook or skill that requires the five fields `DECISION`, `SIGNAL`, `ACTION`, `STOP`, and `MAX`, checks remaining reserve, and appends the outcome to an existing run log. It should be advisory in shadow mode, then enforce only hard ceilings and repeated-identical-failure stop rules after evaluation. It must not call an LLM merely to decide whether to call an LLM; deterministic thresholds and the active agent's compact declaration are preferable.

The premise that existing hooks can intercept model/tool/subagent spend and expose cost or stable proxies is **UNVERIFIED; settle with two mechanically independent signals: (1) documentation/static enumeration of supported hook events that successfully detects a known-present event, and (2) an instrumented canary run demonstrating interception plus reconciliation against provider or session usage totals**.

Useful control with under 200 lines is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the small state transition and logging surface described here. This band is not higher because provider/model accounting, nested agents, cached tokens, and delayed billing may make a truly authoritative governor larger.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED configuration enforcement surface: selective allocation red team — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Do not create an always-on red-team agent. Add a rule that an independent challenge is eligible only when:

- the next allocation tranche is large relative to remaining budget;
- the task is high-consequence and the main assumption is weak;
- proposed fan-out lacks an explicit disagreement-resolution rule; or
- the plan depends on a single interpretation that would reverse the implementation branch.

The challenger receives the intent, decision, evidence, and proposed spend—but not the original chain-of-thought—and returns at most: strongest alternative hypothesis, cheapest discriminating test, and whether the tranche should be released. Use an existing agent only if already available; otherwise the active agent performs a short adversarial checklist.

Net savings are **roughly even (45-55%)**, because a challenger consumes the very resource it protects and model errors may be correlated. Evidence for its possible value is ATP 5-0.1's selective use of red teams to challenge assumptions and compare alternative framings; **UNVERIFIED—settle by triggering it in shadow mode on proposed high-cost tranches and measuring challenger cost, canceled spend, escaped-defect changes, and false cancellations**.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED replayable usage data: sampled compute AAR — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Generate a compact post-run record from already captured events: intent, total spend, tranche decisions, main-effort shifts, verifier outcomes, owner correction if later observed, and candidate wasted/underfunded tranches. Sample rather than reviewing every routine run. Export rule-change candidates for human acceptance or replay; do not self-modify global policy from one run.

The premise that run events, costs, and eventual corrections can be joined without a new datastore is **UNVERIFIED; settle by taking ten known runs, locating each field through a detector first validated on a known-present control, and reconciling totals through an independent provider/session field**.

An AAR sampler will improve allocation rules over time **likely (55-80%)**, based on the Army's use of AARs for performance improvement and on the availability of replayable decision logs in the proposed design. The exact benefit remains **UNVERIFIED; settle with a pre-registered sequence of policy changes where each candidate must improve a held-out cost/quality frontier before promotion**.

### Proposal E — UNVERIFIED availability of joined outcome/cost labels: benchmark the policy, not a single savings number — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Evaluate on a frontier, not “percent cheaper” alone:

- accepted-answer or task-success rate;
- owner correction/reopen rate;
- machine-verifier pass rate where available;
- total tokens, model dollars, tool calls, and wall time;
- 50th/90th/99th percentile spend;
- wasted tranche rate (no decision/artifact/verifier change);
- underfunding rate (later correction or rework attributable to denied escalation);
- controller overhead;
- calibration by task class and consequence.

RouteLLM demonstrates dynamic selection between stronger and weaker models and reports more than 2× cost reduction in some benchmark settings without quality loss ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)). FrugalGPT demonstrates cascades that stop when a response is reliable and reports 50–98% savings across its evaluated datasets, while also documenting unnecessary escalation when its scorer remains uncertain ([paper, especially sections 3–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)). Those are proof that adaptive allocation can work, not a savings forecast for this harness. Any projected transfer of those percentages would be **UNVERIFIED; settle only on the owner's task distribution with held-out replay and live shadow traffic**.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy fails

### 5.1 No active enemy in most tasks

Military decision cycles operate against actors that conceal, deceive, adapt, and exploit delay. Most harness problems are not adversarial in that sense. A stale API, unclear requirement, or failing test does not deliberately manipulate the controller. Therefore:

- competitive OODA speed is not a general objective;
- secrecy and deception concepts should not be imported;
- “outthinking the enemy” is a misleading cost metric.

The structural transfer is uncertainty under change, not adversarial competition.

### 5.2 Military resources and compute differ materially

Forces, fuel, ammunition, transport, and readiness are spatially constrained, slow to move, perishable, and sometimes irrecoverable. Tokens and API calls are relatively fungible, rapidly dispatchable, and replenishable, although quota and latency impose limits. Human harm dominates military risk; agent-harness cost usually does not. The reserve and main-effort concepts transfer as allocation abstractions, but military weighting ratios and risk tolerances do not.

### 5.3 “Subordinate initiative” assumes competence and shared understanding

Mission command relies on trained humans, trust, lawful authority, communication, and an ability to interpret purpose. An LLM can misread intent, invent state, or confidently claim completion. The mission card cannot grant reliability. It must be coupled to external evidence and hard constraints. Treating a model as a trusted junior commander would be a category error.

### 5.4 Intent can become a costly semantic tax

Military organizations invest heavily in shared doctrine and training. A harness-generated “intent” paragraph may simply restate the prompt or drift as context changes. If every tool call requires a rich intent reconstruction, overhead can exceed savings. This is why the proposed state is compact and reassessment is event-triggered.

### 5.5 Main-effort designation can starve necessary breadth

Some problems genuinely need parallel exploration because the uncertainty is multimodal or because independent evidence is the acceptance criterion. Overconcentration can lock onto the wrong bottleneck and create tunnel vision. A reserve, shift triggers, and selective alternative-hypothesis check are safeguards; they do not eliminate the risk.

### 5.6 Decision linkage is gameable

An agent can rationalize any desired call by inventing a “decision.” Deterministic checks can also reward easily measured actions while discouraging valuable exploration. The ledger is therefore not proof of value; sampled replay and outcome metrics are required.

### 5.7 AARs suffer hindsight and selection bias

Once an answer is known, useful exploration can look wasteful; failed paths may have been rational ex ante. Reviewing only expensive failures misses cheap lucky successes and under-spending. Stratified sampling and counterfactual replay reduce, but do not remove, these biases.

### 5.8 Red teams can become a permanent tax

Military red teams are trained humans with distinct perspectives. Two instances of the same model, prompt family, and evidence may produce correlated confidence rather than independence. A second agent is not automatically a mechanically independent signal. Red teaming transfers only as a selective challenge function tied to an expensive or consequential decision.

### 5.9 Verification has special value, but not every verifier is valid

Research at ICML 2025 argues that verifier-guided approaches are superior to verifier-free alternatives under fixed compute/data budgets in its formal setting ([Setlur et al., 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/setlur25a.html)). That supports spending compute where an outcome signal can steer search; it does not establish that arbitrary LLM self-critique is a verifier. A unit test, schema validator, source check, or reproducible command can be external evidence. A model saying “looks correct” is usually correlated generation, not independent verification.

## 6. Transfer verdict

### What transfers

- **Intent before allocation:** define purpose, end state, constraints, and acceptance evidence.
- **Temporary main effort:** spend disproportionately on the current answer-changing bottleneck.
- **Decision-linked collection:** buy evidence only when its possible outcomes alter an action.
- **Shift and reserve:** reallocate as uncertainty changes and protect late verification capacity.
- **OODA as feedback cadence:** act, observe consequences, and update; do not optimize raw loop speed.
- **AAR as policy learning:** inspect which tranches changed decisions and validate new rules by replay.
- **Selective red challenge:** test allocation assumptions at high-leverage decision points, not continuously.

### What does not transfer

- combat tempo as a universal objective;
- command hierarchy or military vocabulary as governance theater;
- military force ratios, risk tolerances, or lethal-stakes logic;
- trust in subordinates as trust in model self-report;
- a standing red-team organization;
- exhaustive intelligence collection;
- allocation by static task label alone.

The final judgment is **very likely (80-90%)** that the narrow transfer is structurally sound. Evidence comes from (a) military doctrine explicitly combining intent, temporary priority of resources, decision-triggered information, assessment, and adaptation; and (b) LLM routing/cascade research independently showing that heterogeneous per-query allocation can improve the cost/quality trade-off. It is only **likely (55-80%)** that this exact DLCM implementation would improve this owner's harness, because the task distribution, hook surfaces, accounting fidelity, and quality labels were deliberately outside this unit's scope and are **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the staged shadow replay and live canary described below**.

## 7. Minimal experiment sequence

1. **Offline annotation.** Stratify historical runs by task class, spend, failure/correction, and success. Mark intent, acceptance evidence, first decisive uncertainty, each expensive tranche, and whether it changed a decision/artifact/verifier.
2. **Policy replay.** Simulate Probe/Concentrate/Assure, several reserve rules, repeated-fingerprint stops, and decision-linked escalation. Compare to fixed-model, hard-cap, and simple cheap-first baselines.
3. **Shadow governor.** Emit would-allow/would-deny/would-shift records without changing execution. Reconcile spend through a mechanically independent usage source.
4. **Selective live canary.** Enforce only repeated-identical-failure stops and hard ceilings first. Randomize eligible low-risk tasks; retain rollback and compare quality, corrections, spend, tail latency, and overhead.
5. **Add assurance protection.** Test reserve and high-risk escalation against cost-only routing.
6. **Test selective red challenge last.** It has the weakest expected net-cost case and should earn its place by canceling more waste or preventing more consequential errors than it costs.

Success is not “the controller saved tokens.” Success is a strictly better or owner-preferred point on the quality–cost–latency frontier, with no material increase in high-consequence escapes.

## 8. Anti-overfit gate

All proposals ride the presumed existing settings/hook/skill/MCP bus; that presumption is marked **UNVERIFIED** above and must be tested before implementation. No proposal requires or recommends a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The state object is per-task and compact; the decision log uses existing run events if available; red challenge is conditional; AAR is sampled.

The most ambitious justified change is not a new platform. It is replacing uniform or task-level-only budgeting with **decision-level allocation and explicit shift/stop semantics**. If existing buses cannot expose authoritative usage or intercept expensive actions, escalating to a new daemon would be **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and is not recommended by this unit. It would violate the minimal-architecture bias unless a separate experiment proves that advisory/configuration approaches cannot enforce the required ceiling and that the recovered spend or avoided failures exceed service complexity.

## 9. Gaps and evidence boundaries

- I did not inspect the local harness or repository, as explicitly required by this ideation lens. Exact event names, configuration locations, model prices, native budget controls, and metadata schemas are not conclusions here.
- I did not treat the pinned corpus's broken hooks detector as evidence for or against hook availability.
- RouteLLM and FrugalGPT evaluate particular benchmarks, model sets, prices, and quality measures. Their savings establish possibility, not transfer magnitude.
- Military doctrine is prescriptive and institution-specific; citation of doctrine establishes what the field's mechanism is, not causal proof that every military use succeeds.
- Counterfactual “answer changed” labels are intrinsically imperfect without replay. The experiment must preserve unsuccessful exploratory paths as potentially rational ex ante.
- Cost must include controller calls, extra tokens in the mission card, red-team calls, and review labor; excluding them would make the comparison invalid.
