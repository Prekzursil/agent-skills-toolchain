# X047 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → compute cost-control

## Unit scope and method

This is an ideation-lens analysis, not a local-harness audit. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect the repository, subject trees, corpus, audit findings, or known-defects file. I used the pinned ground truth only as context and make no claim that a particular cost-control mechanism is presently absent from the owner's harness. Any such current-state claim would require two mechanically independent local signals and belongs to another unit.

The source-field question is narrower than “what safety practices sound useful?” The useful nuclear mechanism is a **risk-informed graded approach**, with selectively applied human-performance tools: self-checking, independent verification/two-person control, conservative stop-work, and defence in depth. The target translation is likewise narrow: **grade compute rigor according to the expected ability of the next expenditure to change a consequential answer, then put bounded checks around the small number of high-consequence or high-cost transitions**. Copying nuclear ceremony uniformly would defeat the target.

## Bottom line

**Verdict: the core allocation mechanism transfers; the nuclear objective function does not.** I am **very likely (80-90%)** confident this is a productive structural borrow because authoritative nuclear guidance explicitly allocates rigor and resources in proportion to hazard or safety significance, while authoritative NRC guidance explicitly says risk-informed practice focuses attention and resources on the most important activities. That is the same allocation shape as spending more inference, retrieval, verification, and tool quota only where it can affect an important answer. Evidence: DOE G 226.1-2A states that oversight rigor and resources are commensurate with hazard magnitude ([DOE, §2, p. 3](https://www.energy.gov/documents/federal-line-management-oversight-department-energy-nuclear-facilities)); DOE-STD-1027-92 says grading is intended to use competing resources efficiently and produce maximum benefit ([DOE-STD-1027-92, §4](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2018/02/f48/DOE%201992_Hazard%20Categorization.pdf)); and the NRC says risk-informed, performance-based regulation focuses attention on the most important activities and defines measurable parameters ([NRC, “Risk and Performance Concepts”](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/risk-informed/concept)).

The confidence is not “almost certain” because estimating nuclear hazard and estimating whether another model/tool call will change an answer are materially different inference problems. Nuclear operations often have engineered boundaries, analyzed accident sequences, stable equipment identities, and observable plant states. Agent work has open-ended objectives, ambiguous correctness, endogenous uncertainty, and model outputs correlated with their own reviewers. Those differences make a literal import unsound.

The transferable unit is therefore:

> classify consequence and uncertainty → require an explicit answer-changing hypothesis → authorize one bounded increment of compute → review the observed result → escalate, stop, or reclassify.

This is not “always use two agents,” “always research more,” or “default to the safest/most expensive model.”

## What nuclear operations has already solved

### 1. Graded rigor: spend attention according to significance

In its own field, the graded approach prevents both under-control of high hazards and indiscriminate application of maximum ceremony to low hazards. DOE’s federal nuclear-facility oversight guide states that the level of rigor and resources is commensurate with hazard magnitude and that more oversight rigor and resources go to high-hazard facilities than low-hazard facilities ([DOE G 226.1-2A, §2](https://www.energy.gov/documents/federal-line-management-oversight-department-energy-nuclear-facilities)). DOE-STD-1027-92 is unusually direct about the resource objective: it proportions analysis, evaluation, and documentation to potential hazards; less complex facilities can achieve acceptable understanding with less sophisticated techniques; and competing resources should thereby produce maximum benefit ([DOE-STD-1027-92, §4](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2018/02/f48/DOE%201992_Hazard%20Categorization.pdf)).

The NRC’s risk framework supplies the decision variables. It defines risk through what can go wrong, how likely it is, and how serious the consequences are ([NRC, “Risk Assessment in Regulation”](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/risk-informed)). Its combined risk-informed/performance-based approach adds engineering judgment, defence in depth, safety margins, performance history, measurable parameters, and outcome focus rather than relying on probability alone ([NRC, “Risk and Performance Concepts”](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/risk-informed/concept)). The Reactor Oversight Process then operationalizes grading with safety-significance thresholds and a response matrix: findings and indicators are assessed, then response intensity can increase to supplemental inspection or enforcement ([NRC, “ROP Framework”](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/oversight/rop-description)).

This is the most important borrow for cost-control. Nuclear practice does not merely add checks; it supplies a rule for **where checks deserve resources**.

### 2. STAR self-checking: force a prediction before an action

DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Volume 2 describes self-checking as focusing attention on the correct component or activity, considering the intended action and expected outcome before acting, and verifying the result afterward. Its mnemonic is Stop, Think, Act, Review (STAR). The handbook emphasizes use before important actions, during uncertainty or unexpected conditions, and when assumptions or missing information appear ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Vol. 2, “Self-Checking,” pp. 17-19](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)). The NRC’s human-factors taxonomy independently recognizes failure to stop under uncertain conditions, inadequate independent verification, and inadequate STAR self-checking as distinct human-performance problem types ([NRC, “Definition List for Human Factors,” items 190, 196, 197](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). These are mechanically different signals—a DOE operating handbook and an NRC event-coding taxonomy—that the pattern is operational, not merely a slogan.

The cost-control insight is not the four words. It is the **pre-action prediction plus post-action comparison**. Without an expected outcome, the harness cannot tell whether an expensive probe provided decision-relevant information or merely produced more text.

### 3. Independent verification and the two-person rule: selective independence

Two related nuclear mechanisms must not be conflated:

* In safety operations, independent verification confirms an equipment condition, document, or calculation after separation from the person/action that created it. DOE says independence improves error-catching because the verifier was not involved in producing the state or document and does not rely on the performer’s oral confirmation ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Vol. 2, “Checking and Verification Practices,” pp. 41-46](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)). NRC Information Notice 84-51 ties independent verification to consequential safety-system state changes, explains that even strong operators err, and states that a second check is warranted where risks and consequences are extreme ([NRC IN 84-51](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/gen-comm/info-notices/1984/in84051)).
* In nuclear security, the IAEA defines the two-person rule as requiring at least two authorized and knowledgeable people to be present to verify that activities are authorized and to detect unauthorized access or action ([IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 13, definition on p. 54](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/publications/documents/infcircs/1975/pub1481_web.pdf)). This addresses insider threat and authorization, not merely accidental error.

The distinction matters in an agent harness. A second sample from the same prompt and model is not automatically independent verification; it can share framing, retrieval, and model failure modes. Nor does a reviewer solve authorization unless it has an enforceable veto before expenditure or side effect.

### 4. Defence in depth: assume an individual control can fail

IAEA design requirements define defence in depth as consecutive and independent levels of protection, with later levels available if an earlier level fails. They describe five levels spanning prevention of deviations, detection/control, engineered safety features, accident management, and off-site response ([IAEA SSR-2/1 Rev. 1, paras. 2.12-2.13](https://nucleus.iaea.org/sites/committees/Set%20of%20valid%20safety%20standards%20202409/safety%20standards_%20valid%20set/SSR-2.1%20%28Rev.%201%29%20Safety%20of%20Nuclear%20Power%20Plants%20Design.pdf)). The NRC’s fire-protection example makes the structure concrete: prevent fires; rapidly detect, control, and extinguish fires that occur; and preserve safe shutdown even if suppression fails ([NRC, “Defense-In-Depth”](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/fire-protection/defense-in-depth)).

For cost-control, the key is not “many budget rules.” It is controls with different failure modes at prevention, detection, control, and recovery stages.

### 5. Conservative decision-making: uncertainty triggers a safe state

IAEA operational-safety guidance describes conservative decision-making as avoiding risk, placing the system in a safe configuration when uncertain, using verified procedures, asking when in doubt, challenging peers and superiors, and seeking peer verification ([IAEA Nuclear Energy Series No. NP-T-5.4, §9.6.1](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1338_web.pdf)). NRC coding similarly defines non-conservative decision-making as failure to stop or establish controls under unfavorable or uncertain work conditions ([NRC human-factors definition 190](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)).

This transfers only after redefining the safe state. In a reactor, stopping and maintaining a safe configuration protects against harm. In cost-control, “conservative” cannot mean “buy more confidence whenever uncertain”; that produces runaway spend. The cost-safe state is **do not authorize the next expensive increment until a bounded, answer-changing test is named**. For high-consequence tasks, the safe state may instead be to stop and request owner authority, not return a low-confidence answer.

## Concrete harness design

## Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: Risk-informed compute permits — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

The recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a randomized replay experiment comparing task outcomes and spend under this gate versus the current policy on a stratified sample of historical tasks**. Implement a small pre-tool/pre-delegation hook that issues a short-lived “compute permit” for the next bounded increment. It does not orchestrate work. It validates a compact decision record and either allows, caps, or pauses a costly transition.

The record should contain:

1. **Decision at stake:** what conclusion, action, or artifact field could change?
2. **Proposed observation:** what will this model/tool/retrieval/verifier produce?
3. **Answer-changing outcomes:** name at least one plausible result that would change the decision; “more confidence” alone is insufficient.
4. **Consequence class:** low (reversible/advisory), medium (material rework or owner time), high (external side effect, destructive/irreversible action, security/financial/legal impact, or an expressly critical deliverable).
5. **Uncertainty class:** known evidence agrees; evidence incomplete; evidence conflicts; or framing/requirements unresolved.
6. **Incremental cost cap:** calls, tokens, wall time, or quota for this one probe, plus cumulative session spend.
7. **Abort/review criterion:** what observed result ends the probe, triggers a different probe, or escalates to the owner?

Use three grades, deliberately coarse to avoid a false-precision scoring model:

| Grade | Trigger | Permitted treatment |
|---|---|---|
| G0 routine | Low consequence, reversible, clear requirements, cheap probe | Default model/tool; no second checker; ordinary global limits |
| G1 uncertain | A named result could materially change the answer, or evidence conflicts | One bounded retrieval/test/model increment, followed by STAR review |
| G2 consequential | High consequence plus unresolved material uncertainty, or a large spend jump | Independent verification or owner authorization before the transition; tighter audit record and a hard cap |

This maps the nuclear risk triplet concretely: **what can go wrong** becomes the wrong decision/action; **likelihood** becomes an uncertainty bucket informed by evidence conflict and prior failures; **consequence** remains impact if wrong. A fourth agent-specific variable—**marginal answer-change potential**—is essential because this is a resource-allocation problem, not solely a hazard-control problem.

The gate should deny a spend increment when no plausible observation is named that would change the current answer. It should not deny cheap exploration early in poorly framed work; exploration can be G0 or a small G1 probe. It should also never treat sunk spend as a reason to continue.

I am **likely (55-80%)** confident that this gate would reduce low-value repeated retrieval, repeated model sampling, and escalating to a larger model without an explicit discriminating question. Evidence for the mechanism is the DOE graded-resource principle and the NRC’s use of significance thresholds and response matrices; the transfer-specific effect remains UNVERIFIED until the randomized replay experiment above measures cost, correctness, and owner acceptance.

### What it could catch that the current harness may miss — UNVERIFIED

Current-state absence is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by validating two detectors against a known-present cost gate, then inspect both hook/config definitions and independent execution traces for each case below**. The proposed permit can catch:

* A research loop that changes query wording but cannot state what finding would change the draft conclusion.
* A verifier spawned after the answer is already supported, solely because “important tasks need review.”
* A jump to a premium/large-context model when the actual blocker is missing owner intent rather than inference quality.
* Parallel fan-out whose branches answer the same question with correlated methods rather than test distinct hypotheses.
* Continuing after two successive probes return no decision-changing evidence.
* Spending tokens to summarize material already within usable context rather than directly consuming it.
* A cheap but destructive action incorrectly graded by dollar/token cost alone; consequence classification moves it to G2 despite low compute cost.

I am **likely (55-80%)** confident these are detectable from explicit permit records because each pattern leaves a mismatch between the proposed observation, the answer-changing condition, and the actual next action. Evidence is the record schema above and the pre-action/post-action structure of DOE STAR. Whether these patterns occur at meaningful frequency in this owner’s harness is UNVERIFIED by the two-detector trace experiment just specified.

## Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: STAR at spend transitions, not every call — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an A/B replay measuring tokens, task success, and latency when STAR is invoked only at grade changes/large increments versus on every call and versus no STAR gate**.

Translate STAR as follows:

* **Stop:** pause before a large model switch, fan-out, long-context ingestion, repeated failing tool, or cumulative-budget threshold.
* **Think:** state the current decision, the uncertainty, the expected result, and what result changes the answer.
* **Act:** authorize exactly one capped probe, not an open-ended research phase.
* **Review:** compare expected with actual result, record actual spend, and choose stop/answer/reframe/escalate. Two non-changing probes in the same uncertainty class trip a circuit breaker unless new evidence explains why another probe is different.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** confident that applying STAR to every cheap tool call would be counterproductive because fixed ceremony consumes a larger fraction of small actions and directly violates the graded-resource principle. Evidence: DOE explicitly grades rigor/resources by hazard, while its STAR guidance emphasizes important steps, uncertainty, and unexpected conditions rather than indiscriminate maximal process ([DOE G 226.1-2A](https://www.energy.gov/documents/federal-line-management-oversight-department-energy-nuclear-facilities); [DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 17-19](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf)).

## Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: Consequence-triggered independent verification — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a blinded comparison of single-pass answers, same-model reviews, and independently framed reviews on high-consequence tasks, including total compute and correlated-error rate**.

Configure independent verification only for G2 decisions. The verifier receives the decision criteria, primary evidence, and artifact to inspect, but not the performer’s chain-of-thought or persuasive rationale. It must independently identify the relevant requirement/evidence and return one of: confirm; specific contradiction; insufficient evidence; wrong grade. For external or irreversible actions, make the verifier’s approval an enforceable precondition rather than advisory prose.

This is not automatically a second full agent. A deterministic test, schema validator, direct source check, permission boundary, or owner confirmation is preferable when it supplies a more independent signal at lower cost. If a model verifier is needed, diversity should come from different evidence access or a different framing, not merely a second stochastic sample.

I am **likely (55-80%)** confident selective verification will outperform universal two-agent review on cost-adjusted quality because nuclear guidance reserves independent verification for states/documents whose undetected error can cause adverse consequences, and explicitly links second checks to extreme risk/consequence ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 45-46](https://www.jlab.org/sites/default/files/Perf-Assur/DOE%20HPI%20Handbook%20Tools%20for%20Individuals%20Work%20Teams%20and%20Management_Volume%202.pdf); [NRC IN 84-51](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/gen-comm/info-notices/1984/in84051)). The agent-domain performance claim remains UNVERIFIED pending the blinded comparison because correlated model errors may erase much of the benefit.

## Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: Four-layer cost defence — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with fault-injection replays in which each layer is independently disabled, confirming that the remaining layers catch runaway retries, fan-out, oversized context, and unproductive verification**.

Use distinct layers:

1. **Prevent:** cheap default route, per-action maximum, coarse G0/G1/G2 classification.
2. **Detect:** cumulative-spend counters, repeated-semantic-question detection, marginal-answer-change review, and a visible budget delta after each G1/G2 probe.
3. **Control:** deny or require authority for a grade jump, quota jump, or a third probe in the same unresolved uncertainty class.
4. **Recover:** return the best-supported partial result with named uncertainty, preserve evidence already acquired, and offer the owner a priced next probe rather than silently continuing.

These layers are only defence in depth if their failure modes differ. Four prompts all asking the model to “be cost conscious” are one correlated control, not four layers. A hard quota, a stateful cumulative counter, a semantic decision record, and external owner authority are more independent.

I am **likely (55-80%)** confident layered controls will catch more runaway-spend paths than a single budget prompt because the NRC/IAEA defence-in-depth mechanism explicitly assumes an earlier barrier can fail and requires later protection ([IAEA SSR-2/1 Rev. 1, paras. 2.12-2.13](https://nucleus.iaea.org/sites/committees/Set%20of%20valid%20safety%20standards%20202409/safety%20standards_%20valid%20set/SSR-2.1%20%28Rev.%201%29%20Safety%20of%20Nuclear%20Power%20Plants%20Design.pdf); [NRC fire defence-in-depth](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/fire-protection/defense-in-depth)). The specific coverage and independence are UNVERIFIED until the disable-one-layer fault-injection test.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### What is genuinely isomorphic

1. **Scarce oversight must be concentrated.** Nuclear oversight cannot apply infinite analysis and inspection everywhere; agent harnesses cannot spend unlimited inference and retrieval everywhere. DOE and NRC explicitly describe significance-based resource focus.
2. **Consequences are asymmetric.** Many steps are reversible and cheap; a smaller set can create large downstream harm or rework. Selective friction is rational in both domains.
3. **Operators/agents are fallible and controls are fallible.** STAR, independent verification, and defence in depth address different stages of error formation and detection.
4. **Unexpected conditions invalidate the original plan.** Both domains benefit from stop/reclassify behavior instead of blindly completing the planned sequence.
5. **Outcomes matter more than ritual.** NRC performance-based regulation defines desired measurable results without prescribing one universal technique ([NRC, “Risk and Performance Concepts”](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/risk-informed/concept)). A harness should similarly judge whether spend changed evidence, decision, or artifact quality—not whether a fixed number of agents ran.

### Where the analogy fails

1. **Safety significance is not answer value.** Nuclear practice aims to keep risk within a safety envelope; it does not optimize token/dollar spend per answer. An agent harness must add marginal answer-change potential, which is not supplied by nuclear doctrine.
2. **The “safe state” differs.** Reactor conservatism usually favors stopping or retaining a known-safe configuration. For an agent, stopping can itself cause harm if it returns an unsupported answer or abandons an urgent task. The safe response depends on consequence: answer with bounded uncertainty, request authority, or spend one more discriminating probe.
3. **Hazards are more modelable in nuclear operations.** Equipment, barriers, modes, and accident sequences are engineered and repeatedly analyzed. Open-ended knowledge work lacks stable failure probabilities and often lacks an observable ground truth. Numerical expected-value scoring would create false precision.
4. **Independence is harder.** Two humans separated in time and task context can still anchor each other; two instances of one foundation model can share much more of the same training, prompt framing, and tool evidence. “Two agents” is not the two-person rule unless authority and information independence are designed.
5. **Redundancy consumes the resource being protected.** A two-person check increases labor in nuclear work but protects safety. A second model directly consumes compute, so universal replication is self-defeating for cost-control.
6. **Conservatism can invert the objective.** “When uncertain, investigate more” is an intuitively conservative agent policy and a cost-control failure. The transferred conservative rule must protect the budget boundary, not maximize confidence.
7. **Security and epistemic verification differ.** The IAEA two-person rule is partly an insider-threat authorization mechanism. Most answer-quality failures are not malicious unauthorized actions. Its direct translation is appropriate for external side effects or quota overrides, not routine factual synthesis.
8. **Defence layers can be correlated theatre.** Multiple model-authored prompts can fail together. Independence must be demonstrated by fault injection, not inferred from the number of named layers.

Because of these disanalogies, I am **almost certainly not (1-5%)** confident that wholesale “nuclear-style operations” with universal two-person review, formal checklists on every action, and conservative maximum verification would improve compute cost-control. Evidence: the nuclear sources themselves prescribe grading by significance, and the IAEA documents an actual conflict where deploying two people in sensitive areas can increase radiation exposure, requiring circumstance-dependent management rather than blind use of the rule ([IAEA TECDOC-1801, Appendix II-6](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/TE1801web.pdf)). That source-field counterexample is especially important: even nuclear practice recognizes that one protection can worsen another objective.

## Anti-overfit gate

All four proposals ride existing settings or pre/post hook buses. They do not require a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. Proposal A is intentionally a small permit validator and state record, not a scheduler or planner. Proposal B is a transition checklist, Proposal C is routing configuration, and Proposal D composes existing hard limits/counters/authority gates.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** confident that the minimal implementation can remain below roughly 200 lines for the permit validator if the harness already exposes pre-tool/pre-delegation hooks and cumulative usage counters; evidence is the deliberately coarse record and three-grade state machine above. **UNVERIFIED; settle it by implementing a throwaway prototype against the documented hook payload and counting non-test source lines.** If cumulative token/quota data are not present in hook payloads, that premise fails; extending the existing event payload is still preferable to building a daemon.

No BUILD-LARGE proposal is justified. A daemon would add a second locus of state and failure for a policy that only needs transition validation and counters. No trained cost critic is justified: it would cost inference to decide whether to spend inference and would share model failure modes. No vector memory is justified: the required state is a few current decision records and counters, not semantic long-term recall.

## Measurement plan and kill criteria

The proposal should survive only if it changes owner-relevant outcomes. Run stratified historical replays across routine, research-heavy, tool-error, high-consequence, and ambiguous-requirement tasks. Randomize current policy versus graded permits. Hold model versions and external data snapshots constant where possible.

Measure:

* total tokens, tool calls, premium-model calls, wall time, and quota;
* correctness against available tests or source-backed adjudication;
* owner acceptance/correction rate;
* proportion of probes whose result actually changes a claim, action, evidence set, or explicit confidence band;
* false denials: a blocked probe that would have corrected a material error;
* false escalations: extra reviews that neither change nor substantively strengthen the answer;
* repeated probes in the same uncertainty class;
* G2 events that bypass independent authorization;
* overhead on G0 tasks.

Pre-register kill criteria rather than rationalizing after the test. Reject or redesign the mechanism if it reduces spend by truncating correct work, if G0 overhead is material, if agents game the form with generic “could change the answer” language, if reviewer correlation yields little error reduction, or if owners override the gate frequently for legitimate exploration. I am **likely (55-80%)** confident gaming will occur without machine-checkable linkage between the permit’s named outcome and the post-action review, based on the general weakness of self-reported intent and the DOE emphasis on checking actual expected results; **UNVERIFIED; settle it by scoring permit specificity and outcome linkage blind during the replay**.

The strongest success criterion is not lower aggregate spend alone. It is a better frontier: equal or higher accepted-task quality at lower spend, or materially higher quality for the same spend, with no regression on high-consequence tasks.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Universal two-agent rule — rejected

It directly doubles a large part of the protected resource, provides weak independence when both agents share model and context, and contradicts graded application. Reserve enforced dual authorization for high-consequence external actions or major budget overrides.

### Fixed per-task token cap — retained only as a backstop

A cap is useful recovery/control, but it cannot distinguish a cheap task being overworked from a difficult task where one more probe changes the answer. It is one defence layer, not the allocation policy.

### Numeric expected-value-of-information optimizer — rejected for the first version

Expected value of information is the right conceptual question, but the harness normally lacks calibrated probabilities for “next call changes the answer” and comparable utilities for different task outcomes. Coarse consequence/uncertainty grades plus an explicit falsifiable outcome are less precise but more auditable. I am **very likely (80-90%)** confident precise numerical scores would be false precision in an initial deployment because no calibration dataset or stable utility scale is established in this unit; evidence is the open-ended target and the absence of any such pinned measurement. This is not a claim that no calibration exists locally; that current-state premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by inspecting a validated registry of cost-policy calibration artifacts and execution traces with two independent detectors**.

### “Always use the cheapest model first” — rejected

This optimizes call price, not total task cost. A weak first pass can generate retries, bad tool choices, or rework. Grade by consequence and discriminating information need, then compare route-level total cost empirically.

### Prompt-only “be cost conscious” — rejected

It lacks an enforceable transition boundary, a cumulative counter, and an actual-versus-expected review. It is also correlated with the agent whose spend it is meant to control.

## Gaps

* No local current-state assessment was performed by design. Therefore this unit does not claim these mechanisms are absent, novel relative to the installed harness, or compatible with a specific hook schema.
* The nuclear sources establish graded allocation and human-performance mechanisms; they do not establish efficacy for LLM inference economics. Every proposed agent-domain performance effect is tagged UNVERIFIED with a settling experiment inline.
* Dollar prices and quota semantics vary by provider and change over time. The design uses provider-neutral units and should consume the harness’s actual metering fields.
* The appropriate boundary for “high consequence” needs owner policy. The examples above are design candidates, not a verified taxonomy.
* Same-model reviewer correlation is not quantified here. The blinded comparison in Proposal C is required before paying for systematic model verification.

## Final recommendation — UNVERIFIED

Adopt the **shape**, not the theatre, of nuclear operations: a graded approach allocates rigor; STAR forces an answer-changing prediction and post-action review; independent verification is reserved for consequential states; defence in depth supplies differently failing backstops; conservative decision-making means stop at the spend boundary when uncertainty cannot be converted into a bounded test.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** confident that this is the only defensible transfer among the named nuclear mechanisms because it preserves their shared structural principle—consequence-triggered, independently checked control—without importing maximum ceremony. Evidence is the convergence of DOE graded-resource guidance, NRC risk-informed thresholds, DOE STAR/verification practices, and IAEA/NRC defence-in-depth sources cited above. Whether it improves this owner’s harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the randomized, stratified replay and fault-injection experiments specified above before enabling enforcement**.
