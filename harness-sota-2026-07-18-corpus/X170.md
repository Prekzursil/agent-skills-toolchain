# X170 — cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → trust calibration

**Unit:** X170  
**Lens:** military doctrine (commander’s intent, mission command, OODA, after-action review, red teaming)  
**Target:** deciding how much to rely on a particular agent for a particular kind of task  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external ideation only. I did not inspect or characterize the local harness. Where I say “target blind spot,” I mean a failure class the proposed mechanism is designed to expose, not a claim that a detector proved the current harness lacks a feature.

## Executive finding

The borrow transfers, but only as a three-part control pattern:

1. **Mission command:** delegate latitude according to demonstrated competence, with purpose and boundaries stated in advance.
2. **After-action review (AAR):** compare intended performance with observed performance against task standards, and feed the lesson into the next task.
3. **Independent red-team sampling:** challenge a small, risk-weighted sample of apparently successful work so the learning loop receives evidence about silent failures, not just visible errors.

This pattern maps well because both fields must allocate authority to semi-autonomous performers under incomplete information, and because competence is conditional on the assigned task. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the explicit task-conditioned definition of mutual trust in ADP 6-0 and the task/standards structure in TC 7-0.1, that the correct translation is a per-agent × per-task evidence record rather than a global “trusted agent” score ([ADP 6-0, paras. 1-27 and 1-30, pp. 1-7](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/1FE33715-CFD1-4614-A489-B3E0480C3F80-1428688882108/adp6_0.pdf); [TC 7-0.1, paras. 3-2 through 3-4, pp. 13-14](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A6C09408-2436-47A4-93A3-6684A1B59042-1739993594606/TC7_0x1.pdf)).

The OODA loop does **not** transfer as a trust estimator. It transfers only as a reminder that observations must update the next decision. Combat-relative speed and deliberate disruption of an opponent’s decision cycle have no stable analogue in ordinary agent delegation. Using “faster loops” as the goal would likely reward premature agent action rather than calibrated reliance. That negative conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, because MCDP 6 makes relative tempo against an antagonist the central payoff of OODA, whereas harness trust calibration concerns prediction of correctness and control of authority, not defeating a decision-making enemy ([MCDP 6, pp. 63-65](https://www.marines.mil/portals/1/Publications/MCDP%206%20Command%20and%20Control.pdf)).

The concrete design is an **earned-latitude ledger** implemented on the existing task/hook/event bus, not a second orchestrator. Each task begins with a compact mission contract; verified outcomes update only the relevant agent-task cell; the cell controls verification/approval latitude; and sampled red-team checks search for false confidence. The smallest useful pilot is **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. Its benefit in this owner’s harness is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the shadow-mode experiment in “Evaluation and kill criteria.”** It is **likely (55-80%)** to improve routing and review allocation, based on the military mechanisms’ structural fit plus empirical evidence that raw verbalized model uncertainty can be unreliable; the wide band reflects the lack of a harness-specific trial ([Tanneru, Agarwal & Lakkaraju, AISTATS 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/harsha-tanneru24a.html)).

## 1. What military doctrine has actually solved

Military doctrine has not solved probabilistic model calibration in the statistical sense. It has solved an adjacent operational problem: **how a commander varies subordinate freedom of action under uncertainty without requiring detailed control of every action, then develops justified trust from task performance.** This distinction matters. The useful object is not “confidence language”; it is *earned authority bounded by intent and updated through observed performance*.

### 1.1 Mission command: competence precedes trust; trust changes latitude

ADP 6-0 defines mission command as an approach that empowers subordinate decision making and decentralized execution appropriate to the situation. Its seven principles are competence, mutual trust, shared understanding, commander’s intent, mission orders, disciplined initiative, and risk acceptance. More specifically:

- Competence is achieved through training, education, assignment experience, and professional development; commanders continually assess subordinate and organizational competence (ADP 6-0, para. 1-27).
- Mutual trust is shared confidence that commanders, subordinates, and partners can be relied upon and are competent **in performing their assigned tasks** (para. 1-30).
- Commanders delegate greater authority to subordinates who have demonstrated tactical and technical competence and whose judgment they trust (para. 1-31).
- Commanders should focus guidance on the problem rather than prescribing the method once expected competence has been demonstrated (para. 1-32).

Source: [Department of the Army, ADP 6-0, *Mission Command: Command and Control of Army Forces*, 2019, pp. 1-7 to 1-8](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/1FE33715-CFD1-4614-A489-B3E0480C3F80-1428688882108/adp6_0.pdf).

The mechanism in its own field is therefore not blind faith and not a personality rating. It is a relationship among **assigned task, demonstrated competence, shared context, delegated discretion, and accepted risk**. Trust is earned over repeated common experience and affects the degree of control.

Marine doctrine sharpens the role of intent. MCDP 1 says a mission has a task and a reason; intent explains why and remains useful when changed circumstances make the original task obsolete. Intent lets subordinates depart from the plan while remaining aligned with higher aims ([MCDP 1, “Commander’s Intent,” pp. 89-90 in the printed text / PDF pp. 97-98](https://www.marines.mil/portals/1/publications/mcdp%201%20warfighting.pdf)). In its own field, commander’s intent is thus a compact invariant for decentralized adaptation, not a detailed procedure.

**Translation insight:** an agent’s trust record should be conditional on what it was asked to do and under what conditions, while the task contract should specify the stable objective against which an adaptive trajectory is judged. This is **very likely (80-90%)**, because “assigned tasks,” continuously assessed competence, and intent-guided departure from plan are explicit in two independent service publications cited above.

### 1.2 AAR: reconstruct observed performance against intent and standards

TC 7-0.1 describes an AAR as a candid, professional discussion focused on performance measured against Army standards. Its agenda covers objectives and commander’s intent (“what was supposed to happen”), a summary of events (“what happened”), key issues, and sustaining or improving performance. It emphasizes that no participant has all the information, different observations are encouraged, and the purpose is learning rather than simply grading success or failure ([Department of the Army, TC 7-0.1, *After Action Reviews*, 2025, paras. 3-2 through 3-4, pp. 13-14](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A6C09408-2436-47A4-93A3-6684A1B59042-1739993594606/TC7_0x1.pdf)).

An older field manual states the same four-part skeleton particularly clearly: what was supposed to happen; what happened; what was right or wrong; and what should be done differently next time. It also says recommendations are applied to the next mission and the next debrief examines the result of that change ([FM 3-04.113, paras. 2-158 through 2-161, pp. 2-38 to 2-39](https://www.moore.army.mil/infantry/doctrinesupplement/atp3-21.8/PDFs/fm3_04x113.pdf)). This second publication is an independent doctrinal signal for the feedback structure.

In its own field, the AAR converts an event into evidence usable for training and future execution. Two details are critical for calibration:

1. The comparator is stated intent and task standards, not the performer’s own retrospective confidence.
2. Multiple perspectives help reconstruct what actually occurred before causal conclusions are drawn.

**Translation insight:** trust should update from externally checkable task outcomes and trace evidence, not from the agent’s prose about how well it did. This is **almost certain (90-99%)**, supported independently by AAR doctrine and an AISTATS 2024 study finding verbalized uncertainty was not a reliable estimate of explanation confidence ([Tanneru et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/harsha-tanneru24a.html)).

### 1.3 Red teams: independently challenge assumptions and apparently sound plans

Joint Publication 2-0 defines command red teams as trained experts providing an **independent** capability for critical review, exploration of plans and operations, alternative-perspective analysis, assumption validation, and wargaming. It distinguishes them from red cells that emulate a threat ([JP 2-0, *Joint Intelligence*, p. I-28, PDF p. 45](https://www.benning.army.mil/infantry/doctrinesupplement/atp3-21.8/PDFs/jp2_0.pdf)). TRADOC Regulation 71-20 separately assigns a Devil’s Advocate Red Team to conduct independent critical reviews, identify weaknesses and gaps, challenge premises, and identify alternative courses of action ([TR 71-20, para. 2-4h, p. 15 / PDF p. 14](https://adminpubs.tradoc.army.mil/regulations/TR71-20.pdf)). These are two mechanically independent documentary signals for the importance of independent challenge.

Red teaming also has a known failure mode in its own field: naming a group “red team” does not make it independent. A peer-reviewed Army case study reports that a red team assembled from existing staff sections was hampered by poor integration and conflicting parent-group identities; it warns that such teams can become sounding boards for the same planning sections they are meant to challenge ([Matherly, “The Group Psychology of Red Teaming,” *Journal of Military Learning*, Oct. 2020, pp. 30-49](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Portals/7/journal-of-military-learning/Archives/October-2020/OCT-20-JML-Book.pdf)).

**Translation insight:** a verifier using the same prompt, context, model family, and evidence path is not a mechanically independent check. A sampled challenge must change at least one failure-generating condition—e.g., use executable tests, retrieve primary evidence independently, or use a different model/tool route. This is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on JP 2-0’s explicit independence requirement plus the Army case study’s observed failure when the challenger retained parent-team allegiances.

### 1.4 OODA: continuous updating is useful; competitive tempo is not

MCDP 6 presents Observe–Orient–Decide–Act as a continuous command-and-control cycle. Observation collects information; orientation forms estimates, assumptions, analyses, and judgments; decision chooses; action changes the situation; feedback begins the next cycle. It notes that multiple loops operate simultaneously ([MCDP 6, pp. 63-64](https://www.marines.mil/portals/1/Publications/MCDP%206%20Command%20and%20Control.pdf)).

The document then makes relative speed against an antagonist the strategic payoff, while cautioning that the aim is meaningful rather than merely rapid action (pp. 65-66). The first part transfers: trust estimates must update when outcomes arrive. The second part usually does not: routine agent work has no opponent whose loop must be disrupted, and a faster confidence update has little value if the outcome label is weak.

**Decision:** do not brand the proposed system “OODA,” do not optimize it for cycle speed, and do not treat the four words as a calibration algorithm. Retain only the feedback invariant: new verified observations revise future delegation. This negative recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)**, for the direct disanalogy above. No implementation tag is attached because this is a rejection, not a proposal.

## 2. Structural transfer test

### 2.1 What is isomorphic enough to borrow

| Military structure | Agent-harness structure | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| A subordinate has uneven competence across assigned tasks. | An agent/model/tool route has uneven accuracy across coding, research, planning, UI inspection, shell work, and other task families. | **Likely (55-80%)**; model specialization and task variance are established broadly, but the owner-specific effect size is **UNVERIFIED — settle by stratifying historical verified outcomes by agent and task taxonomy.** |
| Detailed control is costly and can suppress local adaptation. | Mandatory review and prescriptive step-by-step prompts consume owner time/tokens and can prevent useful tool-driven adaptation. | **Likely (55-80%)**; the cost relation is structurally direct, but net benefit here is **UNVERIFIED — settle with shadow-mode review-hour and correction-rate comparison.** |
| Intent supplies a stable end state when the plan becomes stale. | A compact success contract remains checkable even when an agent changes its execution path after tool feedback. | **Very likely (80-90%)**, supported by MCDP 1’s explicit purpose for commander’s intent and by the observable nature of many harness outcomes (tests, file state, citations, user acceptance). |
| Competence is assessed from training and experience, then changes delegated authority. | Verified task outcomes can update the amount of autonomy or verification required for the next similar task. | **Very likely (80-90%)**, directly mirroring ADP 6-0 paras. 1-27 to 1-32; operational gain remains **UNVERIFIED — settle by the proposed pilot.** |
| AAR compares intent, event, standards, and improvements. | A completion event can compare the contract, trace, verifier evidence, correction, and next routing rule. | **Very likely (80-90%)**, supported by two Army publications above. |
| Red teams challenge accepted assumptions from another perspective. | Risk-weighted independent audits can estimate silent-error rates among outputs that appeared successful. | **Likely (55-80%)**; the sampling logic is sound, but useful detection yield in this harness is **UNVERIFIED — settle by auditing a random sample of accepted outputs.** |

### 2.2 Disanalogies that constrain the borrow

1. **Agents do not bear responsibility or possess loyalty, morale, character, or mutual social trust.** ADP 6-0’s trust construct includes human relationships and Army values. Harness “trust” must be an operational estimate of task reliability, never an anthropomorphic moral judgment. This disanalogy is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the categorical difference between software execution and accountable human command.

2. **Military success can be contested, delayed, political, and hard to label; many harness outcomes are more mechanically checkable, but not all are.** Coding tests may yield strong evidence; architecture quality and research completeness may remain partially observed. Therefore one score cannot mix heterogeneous label quality. This is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by the contrast between executable artifacts and judgment-heavy work; exact proportions are **UNVERIFIED — settle by labeling a sample of owner tasks for outcome observability.**

3. **A military commander has lawful authority and accepts consequences; an automated router must not manufacture authority.** The owner remains the authority source. The ledger may reduce or add verification only inside predeclared boundaries. This is **almost certain (90-99%)**, a direct governance distinction rather than an empirical prediction.

4. **Battlefield adversaries deliberately deceive. Most harness errors are not intentional.** Red-team methods transfer for challenging assumptions and correlated failure, not because the primary agent is an enemy. This is **almost certain (90-99%)**, absent a threat-model-specific prompt-injection or compromised-tool case.

5. **Military organizations invest heavily in common doctrine, repeated training, and stable roles. Agent versions, prompts, context, tools, and model providers drift rapidly.** Trust evidence must be versioned and decay or reset when material components change. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that unversioned historical performance would overstate current relevance, based on this nonstationarity; the right decay rate is **UNVERIFIED — settle by backtesting several half-lives across model/config changes.**

6. **OODA’s relative-tempo advantage is not calibration.** Faster completion can be negatively related to verification depth. This is **likely (55-80%)** in tool-using agent work; the direction and magnitude are **UNVERIFIED — settle by correlating latency/tool depth with verified success within task strata.**

The analogy therefore passes only if implemented as bounded empirical delegation, not as military vocabulary pasted onto agent orchestration.

## 3. Proposed harness mechanism

## UNVERIFIED — earned-latitude ledger; settle with the shadow-mode experiment in §5

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The proposal is a tiny event-driven layer, not a runtime. It consumes task-start, task-end, verifier, owner-correction, and material-version-change events already carried by the harness bus. It emits advisory routing/verification policy. During the pilot it must not block or autonomously approve anything.

### 3.1 Mission contract at task start

Before delegation, record a compact contract:

```yaml
task_family: code.change              # hierarchical, owner-readable taxonomy
risk_class: reversible-local          # e.g. read-only / reversible-local / external / destructive
purpose: "fix the failing parser without changing accepted syntax"
key_tasks: [reproduce, patch, run_targeted_tests]
end_state_checks: [targeted_tests_pass, no_unrelated_diff]
evidence_required: [test_output, diff]
agent_route: codex:model+prompt+toolset-version
```

This is commander’s intent translated into machine-checkable purpose, key tasks, desired end state, boundaries, and evidence. It does not prescribe every step. Contract fields should remain few; otherwise the mechanism degenerates into over-specified planning.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that an explicit contract will reduce false “done” judgments and make heterogeneous outcomes more comparable, because AAR doctrine requires “what was supposed to happen” before reconstructing what happened. The owner-specific gain is **UNVERIFIED — settle by comparing completion-claim reversals on matched tasks with and without contracts.**

### 3.2 Per-agent × per-task competence cell

Maintain cells keyed by:

`agent route × task family × risk class × material configuration epoch`

Each cell stores only compact sufficient evidence:

- verified successes, verified failures, and indeterminate outcomes;
- label source and label strength (executable check, independent source check, owner correction/acceptance, same-agent assertion);
- false-positive severity;
- sample count and recency;
- calibration buckets if the agent supplies a confidence band;
- audit selection status, so sampled cases are distinguishable from error-triggered cases.

Do **not** collapse this to a leaderboard. A route may earn latitude for `code.change/parser` while remaining unproven for `research/legal` or `ui.visual`. Parent-family evidence may initialize a prior but must be visibly weaker than direct evidence.

A minimal estimate can use a Beta-Binomial posterior for objectively labeled binary outcomes, with a conservative lower credible bound controlling latitude. Partial/judgment outcomes should remain a separate ordinal evidence stream rather than being coerced into binary labels. Exact priors, hierarchy, and decay are **UNVERIFIED — settle by preregistered backtesting on held-out chronological outcomes.** It is **likely (55-80%)** that conservative task-stratified estimates will outperform a global pass rate for predicting failure, because the doctrinal definition is assigned-task-specific and emerging calibration work reports task dependence; however, the recent task-dependence paper located during research is too new to treat as settled ([“Confidence is Not Universal,” 2026 preprint](https://openreview.net/pdf/c445c67db1257b6d8cf2ba64bf7d44d903b8c447.pdf)).

### 3.3 Latitude policy, not a confidence badge

Map the competence cell and risk class to one of four actions:

| Evidence state | Low/reversible risk | High/external/destructive risk |
|---|---|---|
| insufficient or stale | run with mandatory verification | human approval plus independent verification |
| mixed / lower bound below threshold | run with targeted verification | route to stronger proven path; human approval |
| strong, recent, task-matched evidence | allow normal autonomy; sample audits | retain explicit approval boundary; reduce redundant review only where authorized |
| drift signal or severe failure | temporarily narrow latitude | quarantine that route for the task cell pending re-evaluation |

This maps trust to a control consequence. It avoids the dangerous interface pattern “Agent A: 93% trusted,” which hides task scope, sample size, evidence quality, and risk. No cell may override a hard approval/safety boundary.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this policy will concentrate verification where expected value is highest, based on mission command’s demonstrated-competence/greater-authority relationship. Net owner-time savings are **UNVERIFIED — settle by recording counterfactual review decisions and estimating avoided review versus caught failures in shadow mode.**

### 3.4 Completion AAR as structured event, not prose ceremony

At task end, generate an automatic five-field record:

1. **Intent:** contract end-state checks.
2. **Observed:** immutable references to test results, tool receipts, diffs, citations, or owner feedback.
3. **Delta:** which checks passed, failed, or remain unobserved.
4. **Cause class:** only when supported; otherwise `unknown`.
5. **Next change:** verification/routing adjustment for the same task cell.

The agent may propose the record, but trusted evidence sources populate outcome fields. “Agent says success” is trace metadata, not a success label. Owner correction is strong negative evidence for the relevant claim but must be attributed narrowly: a style correction should not mark tool execution as failed, and a later preference change should not rewrite a previously correct factual result.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that narrow attribution prevents some trust-poisoning from ambiguous corrections, based on the AAR separation of event reconstruction, standards, and causal/improvement discussion. The exact attribution classifier is **UNVERIFIED — settle with double-coded owner-correction samples and agreement measurement.** Start with explicit rules/manual labels; do not introduce a learned critic.

### 3.5 Independent, risk-weighted red-team sampling

Audit a small random sample of outputs that would otherwise be accepted, oversampling high-impact task/risk cells while preserving known inclusion probabilities. Each audit uses a mechanically different signal:

- code: clean-room test execution, property test, or diff invariant rather than another prose review;
- research: independently retrieve the cited primary source and verify claim support;
- visual work: rendered artifact inspection plus structural checks;
- filesystem/config: postcondition query through a different tool/data field;
- planning: counterexample search by a distinct model/prompt with access to the same intent but not the first agent’s rationale.

Sampling accepted work is essential because a ledger trained only on user corrections and visible failures suffers selection bias: apparently successful silent errors never enter the denominator. The statistical claim is **almost certain (90-99%)** as a property of outcome-dependent observation. The yield and cost in this harness are **UNVERIFIED — settle by a 100-case stratified audit with recorded inclusion probabilities.**

For prose-only challenges, using a different agent name while retaining the same base model and evidence path does not qualify as independent. The audit record must state which failure-generating condition changed. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this rule will catch more correlated errors than same-context self-critique, based on JP 2-0’s independent-capability requirement and the red-team case study’s sounding-board failure.

### 3.6 Optional self-confidence: retain as a feature, never as authority

If an agent supplies a confidence band, store it to measure calibration within its task cell, but never let it directly grant autonomy. TACL 2022 found conversational agents poorly linguistically calibrated, while AISTATS 2024 found verbalized uncertainty unreliable for explanation confidence ([Mielke et al., 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.50/); [Tanneru et al., 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/harsha-tanneru24a.html)). EMNLP 2023 also found that explicitly elicited verbal confidence can be better calibrated than conditional token probabilities on several QA datasets, so the signal is not useless; it is merely task- and method-dependent ([Tian et al., “Just Ask for Calibration,” 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/)).

A 2026 preprint proposes trajectory-level calibration features and reports improvements across eight benchmarks, including out-of-domain GAIA ([“Agentic Confidence Calibration”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15778)). Those results are **UNVERIFIED — settle by independent reproduction on this owner’s trajectories**, and the paper’s trained calibrator conflicts with this program’s anti-overfit gate. Do not build it now. It is **likely (55-80%)** that simple trace features such as repeated tool errors, unsupported completion, verifier disagreement, and context/config drift add predictive information beyond verbal confidence; the evidence is the preprint plus older verbal-uncertainty failures, with uncertainty widened because no owner-specific reproduction exists.

## 4. Target blind spots and concrete catches

These are predicted catches, not assertions of locally proven absence.

1. **Cross-task halo:** an agent that performs well on routine code edits is trusted for evidence synthesis. A task-cell ledger with insufficient research evidence keeps research verification high. Catch likelihood is **very likely (80-90%)** when the taxonomy correctly separates the tasks; taxonomy quality is **UNVERIFIED — settle by inter-rater labeling of a task sample.**

2. **Stale reputation after change:** a model, prompt, tool permission, or MCP route changes but an old success rate remains displayed. Configuration epochs and decay prevent old evidence from silently granting latitude. Catch likelihood is **very likely (80-90%)** for changes represented in the route fingerprint; completeness of fingerprinting is **UNVERIFIED — settle by controlled changes to each material component and confirming epoch rotation.**

3. **Confident but unsupported completion:** the agent reports success without the contract’s evidence. The completion AAR records the check as unobserved, so no positive update occurs and the task cannot gain latitude from assertion alone. Catch likelihood is **almost certain (90-99%)** for end states expressible as required evidence fields; judgment-only end states remain outside that band.

4. **Silent accepted error:** no user correction arrives, so error-triggered telemetry calls the task successful. Random independent auditing estimates the hidden failure rate and adds negative labels. Catching any individual error is only **unlikely (20-45%)** at a small audit rate; estimating a cell’s silent-error prevalence becomes **likely (55-80%)** after adequate stratified samples. Required sample size is **UNVERIFIED — settle with power analysis after the pilot’s base-rate estimate.**

5. **Verifier monoculture:** producer and reviewer share the same source, prompt assumptions, or model failure. The audit independence field rejects the second opinion as non-independent unless another signal changed. It is **likely (55-80%)** to expose some correlated-failure risk; effect size is **UNVERIFIED — settle by seeding known errors and comparing same-route versus independent-route detection.**

6. **Overreaction to one failure:** a severe but narrow error globally demotes an otherwise capable agent. Narrow cell updates plus severity preserve unrelated competence while tightening the affected route. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the data structure prevents global demotion mechanically; whether narrowness causes underreaction to systemic failures is **UNVERIFIED — settle with seeded cross-family regressions.**

7. **Success-rate gaming through easy work:** a route accumulates many trivial wins and appears trusted for difficult cases. Task hierarchy, conditions, risk class, and sample support prevent raw volume from dominating a mismatched cell. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce this bias; difficulty stratification is **UNVERIFIED — settle by comparing predictive log loss with and without a difficulty feature.**

8. **Correction misattribution:** a preference/style correction marks factual research or tool execution wrong. Field-level AAR attribution updates only the affected criterion. It is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve cleaner labels; attribution reliability is **UNVERIFIED — settle by two humans independently coding a correction set and adjudicating disagreements.**

## 5. Evaluation and kill criteria

## UNVERIFIED — shadow-mode pilot; this experiment settles the recommendation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Run shadow mode first. The ledger observes and predicts but cannot change routing, permissions, approval, or completion status.

### Phase A — retrospective feasibility

1. Select chronological tasks with an independently observable outcome or owner correction.
2. Define a small taxonomy before looking at agent results; double-label a sample and report agreement.
3. Fingerprint agent route and material configuration.
4. Backtest three baselines on a held-out later period:
   - global route success rate;
   - per-task-family empirical rate;
   - proposed conservative cell with evidence strength and recency.
5. Report Brier score/log loss, calibration plots, selective-risk curves, coverage, and results by risk/task family. Do not report accuracy alone.

The proposed cell is **likely (55-80%)** to beat the global baseline on calibration or selective risk, because task conditioning follows both doctrine and modern calibration evidence. This is precisely the premise under test, not a conclusion.

### Phase B — prospective shadowing

For 4-6 weeks or until predeclared sample sufficiency:

- predict `normal`, `verify`, or `escalate` before the outcome;
- preserve the policy that actually ran;
- independently audit a randomized, risk-weighted accepted sample;
- record human review minutes, verifier cost, correction severity, rollback, and time-to-detection;
- freeze thresholds until the evaluation checkpoint.

### Pass criteria

Adopt advisory use only if all are met:

1. lower held-out Brier score or log loss than the global baseline, with uncertainty intervals;
2. at a fixed review budget, higher detection of consequential failures, or at fixed miss rate, fewer review minutes;
3. no risk/task slice with materially worse consequential false-accept rate;
4. audit-adjusted estimates, not correction-only labels;
5. decisions display task scope, evidence count, recency, and reason—never just a scalar trust score.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that all five strict criteria will pass on the first iteration. Evidence: the structural mapping is strong, but taxonomy noise, sparse cells, delayed labels, and nonstationary agent routes can erase theoretical gains.

### Kill or simplify criteria

Stop or reduce the mechanism to plain mandatory evidence gates if any of these occurs:

- most task cells remain too sparse to outperform a global/risk-only baseline;
- outcome labels are dominated by same-agent assertions or ambiguous preferences;
- audits cost more owner time than the review they can safely displace;
- the policy’s consequential false accepts are worse in any high-risk slice;
- maintainers begin adding a daemon, vector store, learned critic, or second scheduler to keep it alive;
- agents adapt behavior to the score without improving verified outcomes.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the first useful version, if any, will be much simpler than a full probabilistic hierarchy: a task/risk matrix plus evidence counts and mandatory checks. This expectation is based on the anti-overfit gate and the fact that decision usefulness, not score sophistication, is the objective.

## 6. Anti-overfit and architecture gate

The proposal passes the gate only in the narrow form above:

- **Existing bus:** consumes existing lifecycle and verification events and emits advice.
- **No second orchestration runtime:** routing remains with the current harness.
- **No vector DB or knowledge graph:** the ledger is a compact table/event file with bounded records.
- **No durable-execution engine:** no workflow recovery or daemon is introduced.
- **No trained critic/calibrator:** despite the 2026 HTC preprint, use transparent counts and held-out evaluation first.
- **No observability SaaS or guardrail framework:** local summaries and existing hooks are sufficient.
- **Complexity ceiling:** if the pilot cannot fit in roughly 200 lines plus a small schema/report, reject it or split only after measured value.

The proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to fit the BUILD-SMALL ceiling if lifecycle events already expose task identity, route identity, outcomes, and corrections. That premise is **UNVERIFIED — settle with a read-only event-schema spike before implementation.** If any two of those inputs require a new persistent service, the current proposal fails its own architecture gate.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 7.1 Global Elo or leaderboard

Rejected because it collapses task, risk, evidence strength, nonstationarity, and sample size. It recreates the precise category error the military doctrine avoids by defining competence around assigned tasks. A global score may remain a display aggregate but must never control latitude.

### 7.2 Ask every agent for a probability and trust the number

Rejected as the primary control. Published results are mixed across elicitation methods and tasks: verbalized confidence can improve over token probabilities on some QA benchmarks, while verbalized uncertainty is unreliable for generated explanations and conversational models can be poorly calibrated ([Tian et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/); [Tanneru et al. 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/harsha-tanneru24a.html); [Mielke et al. 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.50/)). Self-confidence is diagnostic input, not earned authority.

### 7.3 Review every output with another agent

Rejected because it spends continuously, can reproduce correlated errors, and can create a second-agent theater of independence. Risk-weighted sampling plus mechanically different evidence offers a falsifiable estimate of silent failure without doubling every trajectory. It is **likely (55-80%)** to be more cost-effective, but this is **UNVERIFIED — settle by comparing caught consequential failures per review-minute in the pilot.**

### 7.4 Full military staff process or “mission command framework” everywhere

Rejected. Rank, command authority, adversarial tempo, morale, and human judgment do not transfer. The useful mechanisms can be represented in a tiny contract, ledger, and audit sampler. Importing the institution would be analogy theater.

### 7.5 Learned trajectory calibrator now

Rejected under the anti-overfit gate. The 2026 HTC results are promising but are a recent preprint and require training a calibrator. That would reintroduce a trained critic-like component before simple task-stratified evidence has been tested. Reconsider only if the transparent pilot passes yet leaves large, stable, reproducible residual error that trace features predict on held-out owner data.

### 7.6 OODA-branded speed optimization

Rejected. MCDP 6’s competitive relative-tempo objective does not match trust calibration. The feedback loop is retained; “operate inside the other agent’s loop” is not.

## 8. Final recommendation

## UNVERIFIED — pilot the selective borrow, not the military metaphor

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a shadow-mode earned-latitude ledger with five rules:

1. Trust is **agent × task × conditions × version**, never a personal/global trait.
2. Every task states purpose, end-state checks, risk, and required evidence before execution.
3. Only independently evidenced outcomes increase latitude; self-reports do not.
4. Latitude controls verification burden within owner-authorized boundaries; it never bypasses hard safety/approval rules.
5. Random, risk-weighted independent audits measure silent failures and continuously recalibrate the cells.

This selective borrow is **likely (55-80%)** to improve trust calibration and review allocation, based on a strong structural match to mission command and AAR, two independent military sources supporting red-team independence, and empirical warnings against relying on verbalized uncertainty alone. It remains **UNVERIFIED — the chronological backtest plus prospective shadow pilot in §5 is the experiment that settles it.** If it cannot outperform a global/risk-only baseline without exceeding the small existing-bus architecture, discard it. That failure would mean the analogy does not earn implementation, even though it remains conceptually attractive.
