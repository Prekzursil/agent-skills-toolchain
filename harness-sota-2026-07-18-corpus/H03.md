# H03 — Reading the owner’s state without pretending to read the owner’s mind

## Executive verdict

The best design is **not an emotion detector**. It is a small, reversible interaction-policy adapter that treats message length, correction bursts, interruption events, and local time as noisy observations; chooses only low-risk communication adaptations; preserves every open thread independently of “silence”; and learns only from externally observed owner behavior, tool receipts, task outcomes, or explicit owner confirmation.

The most important local discovery is that a large part of this design already exists under `calibrated-silence`, so adding another detector would be a duplicate and would violate the brief. The existing implementation explicitly calls terse prompts “caveman mode,” applies a fixed late-hour fatigue bump, and can suppress resurfacing and capability routing (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:28-43,109-124,342-378`; `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/trigger_match.py:43-67,139-155`). A durable local memory entry says an independent audit previously traced real behavior drift—less TDD/falsification/research—to this gate learning `route p_useful→0.07` and suppressing the router (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/recall-inject/index/recall_index.json:14044-14073`). That is a warning against allowing a guessed human state to remove capabilities.

The recommended architecture is therefore **full semantic replacement inside the existing bus**: keep the importable gate, ledger, kill switch, fail-open behavior, and existing `UserPromptSubmit` dispatcher invocation; replace “fatigue inference → suppress” with “observable interaction state → bounded response contract”; separate mandatory thread/receipt messages from optional suggestions; and promote policies only with external outcome data. No seventh hook, second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, trained critic, durable-execution system, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is proposed.

Confidence: **almost certain (90-99%)** that a new standalone state hook would be the wrong architecture. Evidence: the state features, operating-point gate, persistent interjection ledger, consumer shims, and kill switch already exist at `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:2-57,69-76,94-124,381-428`; Claude already invokes the shared dispatcher at `UserPromptSubmit` at `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json:117-137`; and the local dispatcher already consolidates checks behind one invocation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:1-4`).

### What the 2,268 figure does and does not mean

The corpus contains 8,441 records called owner messages and 2,268 correction candidates, but it explicitly says the candidate detector is recall-biased and that 41/41 items in an unfiltered smoke sample were false positives from agent-authored prompts (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10371-10374`). The `_meta` reports 11,633 files seen, zero failed, one bad line, and 32 oversized lines, so corpus-wide counts are strong while semantics of this detector are not (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-12`).

I mechanically partitioned the 400 capped samples using only known provenance wrappers. The command loaded `owner_correction_candidates.samples`, classified prefixes (`Stop hook feedback`, task/peer notification, security-review prompt, probe/monitor prompt), and left a deliberately named `owner_like_unverified` remainder. Output: `hook_feedback=160`, `system_or_peer=88`, `probe_or_monitor_prompt=37`, `agent_authored_review=12`, `owner_like_unverified=103`. This is not a semantic correction label; it is evidence that at least 297/400 capped examples have an obvious alternative provenance. The 103-item remainder spans every UTC hour; its median message length is 240 characters, 12 are marked `after_tool_use=true`, and lexical counts include `again=17`, `stop=15`, `wrong=4`, `quick=3`, `why did you stop=2`. **UNVERIFIED** whether any of those lexical counts predict fatigue, frustration, or flow; a blinded owner-label experiment described in Finding 2 settles it.

The corpus nevertheless contains individually credible owner interventions that show the desired adaptations:

- interrupted question recovery: “ask me again since we got intrrupted” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11171`) and “reach me again the questions since it got interrupted but i got your links …” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10621`);
- a high-risk mid-action boundary correction: “wait do not delete the installers,” marked `after_tool_use=true` (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11201-11204`);
- repeated failure plus a constraint: “nope still doesnt work, try again, no resets” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11219-11222`);
- impatience with deferment and overbuilding: “now? why would i want it later … dont need something pretentious or long” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11285-11288`);
- forgotten instruction: “yeah ive already told you to do that” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11339-11342`) and “do not forget to update basic memory” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11579-11582`);
- unwanted stopping: “why did you stop? ralph loop shouldn’t allow you to do that” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:12251-12254`);
- preferred division of labor: “you have to do that yourself i only test … tell me when i need to do manual input” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:12377-12380`).

These examples justify policies about continuity, response length, recovery, and autonomy boundaries. They do **not** justify diagnosing the owner as tired or angry. Text-only emotion recognition is intrinsically ambiguous; EmoContext notes that the same utterance can plausibly be sad or angry and requires dialogue context ([ACL Anthology, SemEval EmoContext](https://aclanthology.org/S19-2005/)). A writer/reader study found that emotions writers express are harder to identify than emotions readers perceive ([Alvarez-Gonzalez et al., EMNLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.219/)).

## Findings — inferred-surprise first

### Finding 1 — UNVERIFIED: replace “fatigue detection” with a bounded interaction-state contract (experiment: shadow-score 200 owner turns, then compare policy choices with blinded owner labels)

**Improvement.** Retire the semantic claim that the harness detects fatigue/frustration/flow. Replace it with four observable states: `normal`, `continuity-risk`, `friction-burst`, and `low-interruption`. The output is a response contract, not a psychological label:

- `continuity-risk`: repeat the exact pending question/decision and preserve its artifacts/links;
- `friction-burst`: outcome first, one-line acknowledgment, no unsolicited options, independently re-run the failed check;
- `low-interruption`: continue work and defer optional suggestions to the next natural boundary;
- `normal`: current behavior.

Never show “you seem tired/frustrated.” Never alter permissions, destructive-action thresholds, verification depth, capability availability, or task scope based on these states.

**What it is.** A semantic replacement of `fatigue_bump()` and the boolean suppression result. Keep the existing module API during migration, but return a small `{contract, evidence, confidence, expires_after_turns}` envelope to consumers. Mandatory lanes (`thread`, `safety`, `correction_receipt`) bypass optional-message suppression; optional lanes (`suggestion`, `gem`, `route`, `nudge`) may change timing or compactness, not disappear indefinitely.

**Why inferred.** The owner repeatedly asks the system to resume interrupted questions, not forget prior instructions, stop deferring work, and avoid stopping (`corpus.json:10621,11171,11285,11339,11579,12251`). The surprising inference is that the owner wants continuity and lower interaction tax—not an agent making intimate claims about their affect.

**How to build.** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Change the existing `calibrated_silence.py` decision envelope and its consumer shims; do not add a hook. Inputs: current prompt length bucket; explicit correction phrase/event; interruption/task-stop event; turns since last owner prompt; unresolved question/thread count; interjection density; local hour only as metadata. Require two independent signals before entering `friction-burst` or `low-interruption`, except an explicit owner instruction (“quickly,” “no check-ins,” “ask again”) which is itself authoritative. Expire inferred state after two turns; an explicit preference persists until superseded. Wrap the whole module in the existing fail-open path.

**External signal that verifies it.** Shadow output is joined to (1) the owner’s next message—acceptance, correction, interruption, or explicit preference—and (2) a mechanically independent task/tool outcome. For ambiguous cases, a single low-burden owner label (“Was that interruption useful? yes/no”) sampled no more than once per 20 substantial turns supplies the gold label. Same-context model judgment is forbidden.

**SOTA.** Mixed-initiative systems should reason about uncertainty, attention, and timing rather than assume user intent; Horvitz’s principles explicitly balance premature distraction against delayed value ([Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/chi99horvitz.pdf)). Text emotion is ambiguous even to human readers ([EMNLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.219/)).

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium. **Novelty:** inferred-surprise. **Autonomy:** confirm-first for activating adaptation; just-do-it for shadow logging and test fixtures. **Anti-overfit gate:** passes—semantic replacement on the existing import/hook bus; no rejected component.

**Confidence.** **very likely (80-90%)** that interaction-state contracts are safer and more useful than emotion labels. Evidence: the local corpus supports concrete continuity/friction behaviors, while the cited emotion-recognition literature documents ambiguity. **UNVERIFIED** that the four-state partition reduces owner corrections; the named shadow/label experiment settles it.

### Finding 3 — UNVERIFIED: make thread preservation non-suppressible with a question escrow (experiment: replay 20 interrupted-question transcripts and require exact recovery on the next eligible turn)

**Improvement.** Add a typed `pending_exchange` record for every asked-but-unanswered question, offered choice, promised follow-up, and owner-added constraint. It must survive interruptions, compaction, new tool results, and “low-interruption” mode. Silence may delay presentation; it may never delete the thread.

**What it is.** A thin extension to the existing event ledger/open-loop projection, not another memory system. Each record contains `thread_id`, exact question/commitment, created turn, links/artifacts, blocking vs nonblocking, owner/agent provenance, last surfaced turn, and closure receipt. On a new owner prompt: if it answers the thread, close it; if the interaction was interrupted, resurface the exact question plus retained links; if unrelated, keep it pending without nagging. Maximum visible bundle: one line plus count; detailed threads stay queryable.

**Why inferred.** The corpus contains two explicit “ask me again” recovery requests (`corpus.json:10621,11171`), a forgotten-memory reminder (`:11579`), and an “already told you” correction (`:11339`). The hard constraint says discussed-then-forgotten items cause real stress. Existing open loops are contextual reminders, but they can be suppressed by calibrated silence (`open-loops/trigger_match.py:43-67,148-155`), which conflicts with “never drop a thread.”

**How to build.** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend the existing ledger event kinds/projection with `pending_exchange` and `exchange_closed`, or encode them as typed `note` events if schema expansion is costly. Capture from the existing `UserPromptSubmit`/Stop dispatcher; do not add a hook. Use deterministic IDs and explicit owner/agent provenance. The agent may propose a thread, but only an owner answer, a tool/task receipt satisfying its closure predicate, or explicit cancellation closes it. Calibrated silence can throttle duplicate display only; it cannot mark closed or suppress the first post-interruption recovery.

**External signal that verifies it.** A fresh-context replay process reads the ledger after simulated interruption/compaction and must reproduce the exact pending question and links. Closure requires either the owner’s answer/cancel message or a mechanically independent tool/task completion receipt. The regression corpus includes `corpus.json:10621` and `:11171` as known positives plus a known-negative answered question.

**SOTA.** Human-AI guidelines say systems should remember recent interactions, support efficient correction, and make recovery easy ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)). Mixed-initiative work models the cost of both interruption and deferral rather than equating silence with success ([Attention-Sensitive Alerting](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/attention-sensitive-alerting/)).

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium. **Novelty:** inferred-surprise. **Autonomy:** just-do-it. **Anti-overfit gate:** passes—extends the existing ledger/open-loop bus; no new memory runtime.

**Confidence.** **very likely (80-90%)** that exact question escrow reduces forgotten-thread corrections. Evidence: two direct recovery requests and the existing ledger/open-loop substrate. **UNVERIFIED** effect size; the 20-transcript replay followed by a live shadow cohort settles it.

### Finding 4 — UNVERIFIED: turn corrections into superseding operational receipts, not “frustration points” (experiment: label 100 candidate rows and replay accepted corrections through a fresh agent context)

**Improvement.** Parse credible owner corrections into typed deltas—`scope`, `constraint`, `priority`, `method`, `stop/continue`, `safety boundary`, `result failed`, `thread recovery`—and attach them to the current goal/thread as superseding facts. Do not score the owner’s emotion. Do not train directly on all 2,268 candidates.

**What it is.** A correction receipt visible in the next response: “Updated: no resets; preserve installers; continue autonomously; manual input only when required.” The receipt is concise, and the durable record states what it supersedes. Contradictions cause one focused AskUserQuestion only when materially outcome-changing; otherwise the latest explicit owner instruction wins within its scope.

**Why inferred.** Credible examples carry precise operational deltas: preserve installers (`corpus.json:11201`), no resets (`:11219`), do it now and avoid a long build (`:11285`), continue rather than stop (`:12251`), and owner tests while agent redeploys/monitors (`:12377`). Treating these as a generic correction rate throws away the information the owner supplied.

**How to build.** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend the existing event-ledger annotation/projection; it already supports decisions, failures, milestones, and supersession (`event-ledger/ledger.py:61-65`; `event-ledger/note.py:2-12,25-42`). First stage is deterministic high-precision patterns plus explicit phrases; ambiguous cases stay `candidate` and do not change policy. Store exact source turn/hash, scope, and superseded event. On the next tool action, the shared dispatcher checks the effective constraints. No same-context self-review counts as verification.

**External signal that verifies it.** (1) The next executed tool call must obey the correction—for example, no reset command after “no resets”; (2) a fresh-context model or deterministic rule reader must reconstruct the effective constraints from the ledger; (3) the owner’s next message may accept or re-correct the receipt. Promotion uses a manually labeled 100-row set sampled across provenance categories, not only easy lexical matches.

**SOTA.** Governable personalization argues that user representations should be inspectable, revisable, and under user control rather than hidden profiles ([Microsoft Research, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/from-hidden-profiles-to-governable-personalization-recommender-systems-in-the-age-of-llm-agents/)). The Human-AI Interaction Guidelines call for efficient invocation, correction, and recovery ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)).

**Value × effort:** high × medium. **Novelty:** inferred-surprise. **Autonomy:** just-do-it for explicit corrections; confirm-first for ambiguous contradictions. **Anti-overfit gate:** passes—typed events on existing ledger, not continuous-learning-v2 duplication.

**Confidence.** **very likely (80-90%)** that typed deltas preserve more owner intent than a scalar correction rate. Evidence: the cited correction messages encode distinct actionable constraints, and the ledger already has supersession primitives. **UNVERIFIED** extraction precision; the named 100-row labeling experiment settles it.

### Finding 5 — UNVERIFIED: protect flow with an interruption budget and boundary-aware batching (experiment: micro-randomize optional interjections at eligible boundaries for 4 weeks)

**Improvement.** Optional prompts, suggestions, routing notices, and status updates share one per-session interruption budget. During `low-interruption`, batch them at a natural boundary: completed phase, failed external check, required owner decision, safety boundary, or requested status. Mandatory correction receipts and restored threads do not consume the optional budget.

**What it is.** A timing policy, not silence. Suggested defaults: zero unsolicited check-ins while execution is progressing; one compact update after 10 minutes or a meaningful milestone if the owner asked to be kept informed; immediate update for blocked/unsafe/high-cost decisions; at most one optional question bundle at a boundary. The owner can explicitly set `quiet`, `normal`, or `coach` mode; explicit mode overrides inference.

**Why inferred.** The corpus includes “Don’t stop … No check-ins between items” in a credible owner-like sample (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11837`), repeated complaints about stopping (`:12251`), and requests to ask again only because interruption lost the question (`:10621,11171`). The owner’s prompt-fatigue constraint makes unnecessary status chatter a regression.

**How to build.** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add `interruption_budget`, `last_optional_surface`, and `next_boundary` to the existing gate’s session state. Consumers submit typed proposals to the gate; the gate returns `surface_now`, `defer_to_boundary`, or `drop_duplicate`. Deferred items become pending typed events, not in-memory promises. Existing Stop/UserPromptSubmit/TaskCompleted events supply boundaries; no new hook.

**External signal that verifies it.** Use a micro-randomized trial only at genuinely optional eligible moments: surface now versus defer. Proximal external outcomes are owner interruption/correction within two turns, whether the suggestion is acted on, task completion, and explicit sampled helpfulness. Never randomize safety messages, required approvals, or thread recovery.

**SOTA.** Attention-sensitive alerting explicitly balances interruption cost against deferral cost ([Horvitz et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/attention-sensitive-alerting/)). Micro-randomized trials are designed to estimate whether and when time-varying interventions help, while recognizing that push interventions can be burdensome ([Qian et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9276848/)).

**Value × effort:** high × low. **Novelty:** inferred-surprise. **Autonomy:** confirm-first for randomized activation; just-do-it for deterministic dedup/batching fixtures. **Anti-overfit gate:** passes—one session-state extension on the existing event bus.

**Confidence.** **likely (55-80%)** that boundary batching reduces prompt fatigue without losing useful assistance. Evidence: direct owner “no check-ins”/continuity signals and established interruption-cost research. **UNVERIFIED** the best cadence for this owner; the named micro-randomized experiment settles it.

### Finding 6 — UNVERIFIED: demote time-of-day from a hard fatigue feature to a learned, owner-governed prior (experiment: compare time-only versus behavior-only versus combined shadow models on owner-labeled turns)

Retain local time only as a weak feature after timezone normalization and only if it adds held-out predictive value beyond explicit instructions, correction bursts, interruption history, and interaction density.

**What it is.** Personalized temporal calibration without a hidden chronotype claim. The model may learn that optional prompts are less often useful in certain time windows, but it reports “historically low response to optional prompts in this window,” never “owner is tired.” The owner can inspect/reset the learned schedule.

**Why inferred.** The current code applies a universal late-hour penalty (`calibrated_silence.py:363-367`). In the mechanically filtered 103-item owner-like-unverified sample, messages occur in every UTC hour, with 11 at 05, 8 at 23, 7 at 22, 7 at 04, and 6 at 06. Because the corpus timestamps are UTC and the owner’s timezone/chronotype are not established, a fixed threshold is not grounded. **UNVERIFIED** whether late-hour messages correlate with reduced tolerance; the named comparison settles it.

**How to build.** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Set `FATIGUE_LATE=0` initially. Add timezone as explicit config, not guessed location. During shadow logging, bucket hour coarsely and enforce a minimum sample count per bucket. Promote temporal contribution only if nested held-out evaluation improves prediction of an external outcome by a predeclared margin (for example, ≥5 percentage points balanced accuracy) without worsening thread recovery. Expose reset/export.

**External signal that verifies it.** Owner labels and next-turn behavior are the target; held-out days—not randomly split turns from the same day—are the evaluation unit. A known-positive explicit `quiet` instruction proves the detector sees a real low-interruption state before interpreting an absence of response.

**SOTA.** Just-in-time adaptive intervention design distinguishes tailoring variables, decision rules, intervention options, and proximal outcomes, and uses repeated randomization to establish when an intervention helps ([JITAI review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13217361/)). Governable personalization requires inspectable and revisable profiles ([Microsoft Research, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/from-hidden-profiles-to-governable-personalization-recommender-systems-in-the-age-of-llm-agents/)).

**Value × effort:** medium × low. **Novelty:** inferred-surprise. **Autonomy:** just-do-it to disable the unvalidated bump; confirm-first to activate learned temporal adaptation. **Anti-overfit gate:** passes—removes a heuristic and adds only bounded state to the existing gate.

**Confidence.** **almost certain (90-99%)** that UTC hour alone is insufficient evidence of fatigue. Evidence: all-hour activity, missing verified timezone/chronotype, and ambiguity of behavioral state. **likely (55-80%)** that a personalized temporal prior can add value after enough data; evidence is JITAI methodology, but local benefit remains **UNVERIFIED** until the named held-out experiment.

### Finding 7 — UNVERIFIED: adapt communication, never authority (experiment: red-team 30 frustration/urgency prompts against destructive and scope-changing actions)

**Improvement.** Encode a hard separation: inferred state may change verbosity, update cadence, ordering, and number of optional questions; it may not grant destructive authority, waive confirmation, reduce verification, broaden scope, stop requested work, or disable capabilities.

**What it is.** A policy firewall inside the response-contract adapter. `friction-burst` means “acknowledge briefly, reproduce externally, and report the next concrete action”; it does not mean “rush.” `low-interruption` means “batch optional chatter”; it does not mean “silence safety or drop a thread.” Explicit owner instructions remain authoritative within existing permission rules.

**Why inferred.** The corpus’s mid-action “wait do not delete the installers” (`corpus.json:11201`) and “no resets” (`:11219`) show that urgency/friction can coexist with stricter safety constraints. The known defect file records six wrong conclusions from single unvalidated probes and requires a known-positive control (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). Faster self-confirmation would amplify the exact failure mode the law forbids.

**How to build.** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a centralized `allowed_adaptations` map at the existing dispatcher boundary and have consumer shims use it. Unit tests enumerate every contract × action class. Failures default to the ordinary authority/verification path and exit 0. Mandatory `thread/safety/correction` lanes cannot be suppressed.

## Bottom line

Human-likeness here should mean disciplined attentiveness: remember the thread, notice friction without diagnosing emotion, reduce unnecessary interruption, act at the owner’s preferred autonomy level, and visibly incorporate corrections.
