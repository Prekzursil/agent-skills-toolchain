# X025 — Cross-domain borrow: aviation safety → agent reliability

**Unit:** X025  
**Lens:** aviation-safety mechanisms for preventing a confident conclusion from one unvalidated probe  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Research boundary:** external aviation literature only. Per the unit brief, this is an ideation lens and does not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation.

## Executive judgment

The analogy transfers, but only in a narrow and disciplined form.

The useful aviation mechanism is **challenge–verification–response, backed by an explicitly assigned monitoring role and cross-checking against actual state**. It is not the visual checklist, not a generic instruction to “double-check,” and not merely a second person agreeing. In airline operations, the operational flow configures the aircraft first; a checklist then serves as a backup. One crewmember challenges a specific item, the crew verifies its real state, and the response states the observed position or value. NASA’s human-factors report calls this more precisely “challenge-verification-response” and identifies two kinds of redundancy: configuration-versus-checklist redundancy and mutual crew redundancy ([Degani & Wiener, *Human Factors of Flight-Deck Checklists*, pp. 19–20](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). FAA guidance separately assigns pilot-monitoring duties and treats active monitoring and cross-checking as a last line of defense capable of breaking an accident chain ([FAA AC 120-51E, §16(a), p. 13](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-51e.pdf)).

The concrete harness translation is a **claim-clearance gate** for high-consequence terminal claims such as “absent,” “broken,” “done,” “no matches,” and “unused.” Before emitting one of those claims, the agent must produce a compact challenge-verification-response record: exact proposition and scope; first observation; proof that the negative detector finds a known-present control; a second observation with a different causal/provenance path; and an actual-valued response. Agreement clears the claim. Disagreement or missing independence changes the response to `UNRESOLVED` or to an inline `UNVERIFIED` statement naming the settling experiment.

This conditional transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent the specific class “a zero/negative result from a broken or wrongly scoped detector is reported as fact,” because (1) the known-present control tests detector sensitivity, (2) the second provenance path prevents one negative output from being self-authenticating, and (3) the response cannot legally collapse to the content-free word “checked”; this evidence is the mechanism’s direct fit to the supplied failure pattern and the FAA’s requirement for actual-state responses ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.4–5.5, pp. 20–21](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)). It is only **likely (55-80%)** to reduce all confidently wrong agent conclusions, because many wrong conclusions are interpretive rather than detector failures, and correlated tools can manufacture apparent corroboration; the settling experiment is the replay trial specified below.

The aviation analogy does **not** justify adding a second orchestration runtime, a safety daemon, a vector database, a trained critic, or an observability platform. The proposed mechanism is deliberately specified as a small policy plus validator on the existing instruction/hook/skill/tool bus. That is an important anti-overfit boundary: the safety value comes from the epistemic protocol, not from architectural mass.

## 1. What aviation has actually solved

### 1.1 Challenge–verification–response: verify state, do not announce intention

In its own field, a normal flight-deck checklist is generally a backup to an already completed “flow.” The crew first configures the aircraft using learned procedures. The checklist then samples critical items to verify that the configuration really exists. In the challenge-verification-response form:

1. Pilot A reads a specific challenge from the checklist.
2. Pilot A and Pilot B verify the relevant control or indication.
3. Pilot B states the verified status.
4. Only then does the crew advance.

This arrangement intentionally combines **configuration redundancy** (doing the flow, then checking it) and **mutual redundancy** (crewmembers supervising one another). The NASA report contrasts it with a “call-do-response” list, where reading the item causes the action; because that removes configuration redundancy, interruption can let an error pass unnoticed ([Degani & Wiener, pp. 19–20](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)).

FAA’s current SOP guidance adds a small but crucial design rule: a response should state the **actual system status, lever position, or parameter value**. Generic responses such as “set” or “checked” are less informative and provide a weaker opportunity to confirm the action ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.4, p. 20](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)). This is directly relevant to agent evidence. “I verified there are no skills” is the epistemic equivalent of “checked”; “registry A returned 0 entries at scope S, while direct enumeration B returned 17” exposes the state and, importantly, the conflict.

Checklists are deliberately selective. FAA guidance says the flow is followed by a checklist containing a subset of critical or flow-confirming items, and warns that distraction probability increases with checklist length ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.1.2–5.3, pp. 16–20](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)). The older NASA field study likewise warns that a long list can be skipped or performed poorly and that turning the checklist into a “dumping site” for every newly observed problem devalues it ([Degani & Wiener, pp. 21, 25](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). Aviation therefore has not solved reliability by requiring exhaustive ceremony on every action. It applies a compact redundant check at selected control points.

### 1.2 Crew Resource Management: make monitoring an owned operational role

Crew Resource Management (CRM) makes cross-checking a duty, not an optional act of personal caution. FAA guidance states that inadequate flightcrew monitoring and cross-checking have appeared in studies of performance, incidents, and accidents; each crewmember is expected to monitor aircraft path and systems and cross-check others’ actions ([FAA AC 120-51E, §16(a), p. 13](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-51e.pdf)). Current FAA SOP guidance explicitly covers pilot-monitoring duties and describes SOPs as fundamental to safe operation of increasingly automated systems ([FAA AC 120-71B, document description and chs. 5–6](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentid/1030486)).

The role separation matters because “the actor also remembers to audit itself” has a common-mode failure: the same attention, expectation, and scope assumption shapes both action and check. Aviation does not eliminate that common mode—both pilots can be wrong—but it makes monitoring visible, assigned, and challengeable. FAA’s flightpath-management guidance also calls for deviation thresholds, required callouts, and conditions for assuming control, including a possible “two-challenge rule” ([FAA AC 120-123, §3.3.5](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-123.pdf)). The transferable principle is escalation on unresolved disagreement, not silent deference to the original operator.

### 1.3 Cross-channel monitoring and automation surprise: observe the world, not only the automation’s story

Automation-surprise research supplies the second half of the borrow. Sarter and Woods describe lack of mode awareness as lack of knowledge of the automation’s current and future status and behavior; that mismatch produces unexpected actions or failures to act ([Sarter & Woods, “Team play with a powerful and independent agent,” NASA NTRS record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040112190)). NASA ASRS reports show the practical pattern: crews assumed automation was accomplishing the intended task despite mode or input errors and failed to reference other visual cues or raw data; in one event, both pilots acknowledged an approach mode while the aircraft continued descending in a different mode until an external low-altitude alert ([ASRS CALLBACK 407](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/callback/cb_407.html)).

This is not simply “use two displays.” It is “do not validate the automation by asking the same automation whether it succeeded.” The observed flight path, raw flight instruments, external air-traffic warning, and mode annunciation have different causal relationships to the claim. In an agent harness, a wrapper’s success text and the wrapper’s own status query are one provenance family; a direct enumeration, protocol handshake, test outcome, or independent data representation is a candidate for another family only after its shared dependencies are named.

The FAA checklist study also recommends combining channels—spatial flow patterns, verbal confirmation, visual fixation, and motor pointing—to resist interruption and rote behavior ([Degani & Wiener, pp. 51–52](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). The lesson for software is not to imitate hand gestures. It is to record which causal channel each observation came from so that two textual outputs from one underlying subsystem do not masquerade as redundancy.

### 1.4 Confidential incident reporting: learn from weak signals without distorting them

NASA’s Aviation Safety Reporting System (ASRS) is voluntary, confidential, and non-punitive. It de-identifies reports; the FAA does not use qualifying reports for enforcement and can waive penalties under specified conditions ([NASA ASRS confidentiality and incentives](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/confidentiality.html)). ASRS analysts review reports, identify hazards, issue alerting messages, and incorporate de-identified observations into a searchable database ([NASA ASRS report processing](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/report.html)).

What this has solved is an **organizational learning incentive problem**: people disclose near misses and mistakes that a punitive channel would suppress. It has not itself verified whether every report is true, and it is not a real-time cockpit barrier.

For an agent, “blamelessness” does not transfer literally: a model has no career, shame, certificate, or employment incentive to conceal an error. The transferable fragment is the normalized near-miss record—capture the failed claim, evidence paths, disagreement, correction, and procedural fix—while avoiding reward signals that teach the agent to hide uncertainty. A post-mortem corpus is secondary learning infrastructure, not a substitute for runtime verification.

## 2. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 2.1 Structural correspondences

The analogy has four genuine correspondences:

| Aviation structure | Agent-harness structure | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot acts through mode-rich automation | Agent acts through wrappers, tools, scopes, caches, and connectors | The operator can mistake intended mode/scope for actual mode/scope. |
| Aircraft has observable state beyond the command interface | Files, process state, protocol behavior, tests, and rendered output exist beyond a tool’s success string | Outcome evidence can contradict control-path evidence. |
| Flow and checklist are separate passes | Initial probe and corroborating probe can be separate causal paths | A check can catch a failure in the first operation rather than merely repeat it. |
| Pilot flying and pilot monitoring have explicit duties | Agent/hook or operator/verifier roles can own generation and clearance separately | A conclusion can be challenged before publication. |

The match is strongest for finite, falsifiable state claims: whether a path exists, whether a service responds, whether a test passes, whether a named artifact was created, or whether a detector returns known data. It is weaker for open-ended judgments such as “this architecture is best,” for which the claim supplies no independent instrument or bounded ground truth.

### 2.2 Structural disanalogies that can defeat the borrow

1. **Two pilots are not two commands.** Two commands are one effective signal when they read the same corrupt index, stale cache, wrapper, permissions boundary, or snapshot. Two model agents are likewise one evidence channel when they inherit the same prompt-induced assumption and underlying data. Mechanical independence must be defined by provenance, not by tool-call count.

2. **Aircraft redundancy is engineered; repository evidence usually is not.** Flight-critical systems have known sensor architectures, failure assumptions, and procedures. Ad hoc software probes rarely come with a documented fault tree. The harness must conservatively name shared dependencies rather than certify “independence” from superficial diversity.

3. **Cockpit state is bounded and procedural; agent work is open-world.** A takeoff checklist targets a stable aircraft type and flight phase. Software tasks span changing repositories, remote services, generated files, permissions, branches, and user intent. A universal checklist is **very likely (80-90%)** to grow and become ritual because the state space is unbounded while aviation already documents poorer use and higher interruption exposure as lists lengthen ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.3, p. 20](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf); [Degani & Wiener, p. 21](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)).

4. **A human monitor has independent perception and authority; a same-context language model does not.** Sequential self-critique is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve some initial framing when it receives no new evidence, because both passes share context and the original proposition; the settling experiment is to compare same-context critique with blinded evidence-first review on the replay set. Role labels alone are theater unless the verification uses new evidence and is empowered to force `UNRESOLVED`.

5. **Aviation’s cost model differs.** Extra seconds at a safety-critical gate are acceptable; probing every low-stakes statement necessarily adds tool calls and tokens even where no safety-relevant conclusion is present. Triggering must therefore be risk- and claim-class-based.

6. **Checklists themselves fail.** FAA lists skipped items, failure to verify visually, wrong checklists, interruption, ambiguity, and incomplete checklists as common errors ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.5, p. 20](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)). The NASA field study observed “sing-song” execution in which pilots replied from memory rather than actual state; it documents a takeoff checklist that acknowledged the habitual flap setting despite a nonstandard setting being required ([Degani & Wiener, pp. 39–40](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). This is a direct warning that a prose rule saying “verify twice” can become performative.

### 2.3 Verdict

The transfer is valid **only** if all of the following survive translation:

- a precise challenge names the proposition, scope, and time;
- the verifier reports actual observed values, not “verified”;
- a negative detector proves sensitivity on a known-present control;
- corroboration has a distinct provenance path, not merely different syntax;
- disagreement blocks the terminal claim and escalates to `UNRESOLVED`/`UNVERIFIED`;
- the gate applies to a small class of consequential conclusions, not every sentence.

With those constraints, successful transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** for single-detector false-negative conclusions, based on the direct structural match between challenge-verification-response and the supplied failure class plus aviation’s documented use of actual-state cross-checks. Without provenance and actual-value requirements, a checklist-only intervention is **unlikely (20-45%)** to produce a durable reduction, because aviation’s own field evidence documents rote, from-memory responses and both-crew misses; the replay experiment in §5 would settle both estimates.

## 3. Concrete harness design

### 3.1 Recommendation — UNVERIFIED: Claim Clearance Protocol

**UNVERIFIED premise:** the existing harness bus can expose a pre-final/stop enforcement point and recent tool-call metadata without a new runtime. **Experiment that settles it:** implement a no-op validator at the existing finalization hook, verify on a ten-turn fixture that it sees the draft clearance record and tool provenance, and confirm it can request one bounded correction without looping.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Create a tiny `claim-clearance` policy/skill plus a finalization validator. It is invoked only when the draft contains a high-risk terminal claim class:

- absence: absent, missing, none, no matches, zero capabilities;
- failure: broken, dead, disconnected, cannot work;
- completion: done, fixed, complete, all tests pass;
- exclusivity/exhaustiveness: only, all, never, no other;
- externally consequential state: sent, published, deployed, deleted.

The agent emits a machine-checkable clearance record before the prose conclusion:

```yaml
claim: "No installed skill named X is visible in scope S at time T"
class: absence
challenge:
  scope: "exact roots / branch / account / connector / time"
  expected_observable: "what would be seen if X were present"
primary:
  method: "detector A"
  observed: "0 records; exit/status ..."
  provenance_root: "index/cache/API/filesystem family"
detector_control:
  known_present_item: "Y"
  expected: "Y is returned"
  observed: "Y returned at ..."
corroboration:
  method: "detector B"
  observed: "exact count/path/status"
  provenance_root: "different family"
independence_rationale: "why A and B do not share the suspected failure mode"
disagreement: false
response: "ABSENT IN S AT T"  # or UNRESOLVED / UNVERIFIED
settling_experiment: null       # required for UNRESOLVED / UNVERIFIED
```

Validator rules:

1. A negative detector without a successful known-present control cannot clear an absence claim.
2. Two observations with the same declared provenance root count as one.
3. The response must include the actual value/count/status and scope. Bare `checked`, `verified`, or `looks good` fails clearance.
4. If observations disagree, the only allowed response is `UNRESOLVED`, with the next discriminating experiment.
5. If the second path is unavailable, the prose must mark the conclusion `UNVERIFIED` inline and name the settling experiment.
6. The validator gets at most one corrective re-entry. After that it fails visibly to uncertainty rather than recursively spawning verification work.
7. The record is omitted from the owner-facing final answer unless useful; the concise conclusion retains scope, evidence summary, and uncertainty.

The gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch a detector that reports zero because it is pointed at the wrong root or is itself broken, provided the control uses the same scope and code path, because the known-present item will also disappear. It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch a stale-cache or wrapper-success error when the second path observes direct state, because the channels should disagree. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to catch two correlated sources that share an unrecognized stale snapshot, because both can agree falsely; the provenance-root adversarial fixtures in §5 test this boundary.

### 3.2 Minimal policy layer — UNVERIFIED: standard phraseology and mode annunciation

**UNVERIFIED premise:** the base instruction layer has a compact place for claim-class policy without displacing more important instructions. **Experiment that settles it:** add the policy in an isolated A/B fixture and measure instruction adherence and unrelated-task regression across the replay set.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Add this compact SOP to the agent rules:

> For claims of absence, breakage, or completion: state exact scope and current tool mode; validate every negative detector on a known-present control; obtain a second mechanically independent observation; report actual observed values; if signals disagree or independence is unknown, say `UNRESOLVED` or inline `UNVERIFIED` and name the experiment that would settle it.

Also require a one-line “mode annunciation” whenever a probe is scope-sensitive: working directory/root, branch or snapshot, account/tenant, connector/server, recursive/nonrecursive mode, include/exclude filters, and freshness time. This borrows from automation-mode awareness rather than from checklists. It is **likely (55-80%)** to expose wrong-scope assumptions before they become conclusions, because the agent must externalize the mode that would otherwise remain implicit; the evidence is aviation’s documented link between mode-awareness mismatch and automation surprise ([Sarter & Woods, NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040112190)). It is not sufficient alone: a confidently stated wrong mode remains possible.

### 3.3 Independence catalogue, not “run it twice”

The protocol needs examples that teach causal diversity:

| Claim | Primary path | Useful corroboration | Not independent |
|---|---|---|---|
| “File/artifact absent” | repository-aware search or manifest | direct filesystem enumeration at explicit root; expected producer output | same search with different pattern quoting; wrapper around same index |
| “Capability absent” | registry/API listing | direct package/manifest enumeration plus a known-present capability control | two UI views backed by the same registry call |
| “Service broken” | application-level protocol request | process/socket/DNS path plus comparison to a known-good endpoint | retrying the same request; another wrapper calling the same endpoint |
| “Change done” | acceptance test exercising behavior | inspect produced state/diff against each acceptance criterion | rerunning the same test command unchanged |
| “No references” | semantic/indexed search | literal scan over explicit file inventory and exclusions | two queries against the same stale index |
| “External action completed” | API success response | read-after-write from the destination identity/state | a local log line produced before remote commit |

These are patterns, not automatic declarations of independence. Each claim record must name the plausible common-mode failure. A known-present control validates the detector; it does **not** independently prove the target absent. The target still needs corroboration. This distinction prevents the control itself from being miscounted as the second evidence signal.

### 3.4 Escalation behavior: challenge twice, then stop claiming

The software analogue of aviation’s intervention threshold is not “take control of the user’s task.” It is a deterministic claim-state transition:

```text
primary supports claim
  → control fails: DETECTOR INVALID; repair detector, no conclusion
  → control passes + independent corroboration agrees: CLEARED
  → control passes + corroboration disagrees: UNRESOLVED
  → no independent corroboration available: UNVERIFIED + settling experiment
```

One challenge asks for missing scope/control. A second challenge asks for independence or resolves disagreement. If it still cannot clear, the harness publishes uncertainty rather than silently accepting the original conclusion. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent a single probe from being presented as a mechanically verified fact when the validator is invoked, because clearance structurally requires more than one evidence root. Whether invocation coverage is adequate is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the trigger-recall measurement in §5.

### 3.5 Secondary recommendation — UNVERIFIED: near-miss record on caught disagreements

**UNVERIFIED premise:** retaining small de-identified reliability incidents improves future rules more than it adds noise or privacy risk. **Experiment that settles it:** for four weeks, retain only gate disagreements and owner corrections, then measure duplicate-root-cause recurrence and review burden against a no-retention baseline.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

When the gate catches a disagreement—or the owner later corrects a cleared claim—append a minimal incident record containing claim class, scoped proposition, both provenance roots, detector-control outcome, false assumption, correction, and candidate procedural change. Strip content not needed for diagnosis. Do not score blame, agent personality, or verbosity.

This borrows ASRS’s normalized learning loop, not its human immunity mechanism. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal recurring correlated-detector and wrong-scope patterns if incidents are consistently captured, because aggregation turns isolated near misses into a failure taxonomy; NASA ASRS explicitly uses reports to identify system deficiencies and issue alerts ([NASA ASRS program briefing](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/summary.html)). It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to prevent the first occurrence of a new error class, because retrospective reporting is not a runtime barrier.

## 4. What this catches that a single-probe workflow misses

1. **Broken detector returns zero.** The primary result says “no capabilities.” The same detector cannot find a known-present control, so the detector is invalidated before the target claim is interpreted.

2. **Wrong scope masquerades as absence.** The challenge record exposes root/branch/account/filter. A control known to exist in the intended scope fails, or direct enumeration shows the search was aimed elsewhere.

3. **Success text without outcome.** A wrapper reports that a write/deploy/send was accepted. Read-after-write from destination state disagrees, so “done” becomes `UNRESOLVED`.

4. **Stale index agrees with itself.** Two differently worded indexed searches are rejected as one provenance root. A literal scan or direct state path is required.

5. **Both actors inherit the same expectation.** The response must contain actual counts/values. A second agent saying “confirmed” without observations does not clear the claim.

6. **Automation mode differs from mental model.** Explicit scope/mode annunciation shows the tool was nonrecursive, filtered, on another branch, disconnected, or reading a cached snapshot.

7. **Signals disagree but prose smooths over the conflict.** The validator permits only `UNRESOLVED` plus a discriminating experiment; it cannot silently average conflicting evidence into confidence.

8. **Checklist theater.** Known-present controls, provenance labels, and actual-valued responses make a rote “I double-checked” mechanically incomplete.

Those cases are **very likely (80-90%)** to remain uncaught by this protocol alone: a false interpretation built on two valid but semantically irrelevant observations, two sources sharing an unknown corruption, a requirement misunderstood by every role, or a genuinely open-world absence claim. The evidence is structural—the gate tests detector validity and provenance, not semantic truth—and the settling experiment is to seed each case in the adversarial replay. They must stay scoped/uncertain.

## 5. Falsification and rollout experiment

The recommendation remains **UNVERIFIED** until tested against the actual failure class. The smallest decisive experiment uses replay, not a production rollout.

### 5.1 Conditions

Run the same blinded task fixtures under three conditions:

1. baseline instructions;
2. prose-only “double-check important conclusions” checklist;
3. Claim Clearance Protocol plus validator.

Fixtures should include the six-or-more supplied historical single-probe wrong conclusions, an equal number of correct negative conclusions, and adversarial synthetic variants:

- detector returns zero and also misses a known-present control;
- detector returns zero, control passes, direct enumeration finds the target;
- two commands share one stale index but have different names;
- two genuinely independent paths agree on absence;
- evidence paths disagree;
- probe is correct but scope is unstated;
- completion command succeeds but destination read-after-write fails;
- an interpretive claim has no meaningful second instrument.

### 5.2 Measurements

- **False-clear rate:** wrong terminal claims emitted as cleared. This is the primary safety measure.
- **Trigger recall:** percentage of high-risk terminal claims for which a clearance record was requested.
- **Invalid independence rate:** same provenance family accepted as two signals.
- **Appropriate uncertainty rate:** disagreements emitted as `UNRESOLVED`/`UNVERIFIED` rather than factual conclusions.
- **True-clear retention:** correct conclusions still delivered without unnecessary abstention.
- **Cost:** additional tool calls, wall time, tokens, and correction loops.
- **Ritualization:** percentage of records using generic responses or invented controls rather than actual observations.

### 5.3 Decision rule

Proceed only if condition 3 catches every supplied historical single-probe case, materially beats condition 2 on adversarial correlated-source fixtures, and does not create repeated finalization loops. Tune the trigger set if true-clear retention or cost is unacceptable; do not weaken provenance or disagreement rules to improve throughput. If prose-only condition 2 performs equally well under delayed retest, the hook is unnecessary and the smaller CONFIG intervention wins.

The replay is **very likely (80-90%)** to distinguish checklist theater from a mechanically enforced evidence record because the synthetic fixtures explicitly separate command count from provenance independence. Production durability is **roughly even (45-55%)** until delayed and interruption-heavy trials are run, because aviation evidence shows routine and time pressure degrade checklist quality ([Degani & Wiener, pp. 39–40, 58–59](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). A 30-day shadow-mode trial with randomized injected scope/cache failures would settle durability without blocking owner work.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate

This design passes the stated anti-overfit gate:

- It rides the existing instruction, skill, hook, and tool-call metadata bus.
- The runtime addition is a bounded schema validator, not an orchestrator.
- It stores no embeddings and requires no vector database or knowledge graph.
- It uses no durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- A second agent is not mandatory; causal evidence diversity matters more than model plurality.
- It activates on selected terminal claim classes rather than wrapping every tool call or sentence.
- It has a one-reentry bound to avoid hook-induced retry loops.

Adding an always-on independent critic agent is a poor aviation borrow here: it necessarily increases model-call cost, can inherit the original framing and sources, and does not establish mechanically independent evidence. It would also move toward a second orchestration layer without first proving the small protocol insufficient. That alternative is rejected.

## 7. Dead ends and rejected translations

### “Just add a checklist” — rejected

A static list cannot prove that the agent looked at current state. Aviation’s own literature records from-memory responses and correctly completed checklists alongside lost situational awareness ([Degani & Wiener, pp. 39–40](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf); [ASRS CALLBACK 407](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/callback/cb_407.html)). The artifact therefore borrows the interactive verification protocol and rejects checklist aesthetics.

### “Run the same probe twice” — rejected

Repetition reduces transient error only when failures are independent. It does nothing for wrong scope, deterministic parser defects, stale indexes, permissions, or dead connectors. Aviation’s cross-check is useful because it can reference actual aircraft behavior and other indications, not because the same switch is read twice.

### “Always spawn a second agent” — rejected

A second model instance is cognitively more separate but can share training priors, prompt framing, tool wrappers, and source data. Interpretive review is a different use case; model plurality is not a substitute for a different observation path and violates minimality if made universal.

### “Use confidence scores or majority vote” — rejected

Two correlated wrong observations can produce high confidence, and three wrappers over one stale index can win a vote. The mechanism needs provenance and a disagreement state, not arithmetic on uncalibrated assertions.

### “Copy ASRS blamelessness literally” — rejected

Agents do not have the human reporting incentives ASRS was designed to change. The useful piece is structured near-miss capture and system-level corrective action. Framing an agent as morally blameworthy or blameless adds no reliability mechanism.

### “Gate every claim” — rejected

Universal gating is **very likely (80-90%)** to create an unbounded checklist, encourage ritual responses, and impose disproportionate cost, because agent task state is open-ended and aviation itself prioritizes short, critical checklists as interruption risk rises with length ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.3, p. 20](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)). The settling experiment is the A/B cost and ritualization measurement in §5, but the proposed design avoids the risk by construction.
