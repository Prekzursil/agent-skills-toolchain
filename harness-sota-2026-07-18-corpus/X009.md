## Executive verdict

The useful borrow is not “make the agent think like a doctor.” It is a **prescribing-safety stack**:

1. replace free-text ordering with a structured order whenever possible;
2. check a short, evidence-calibrated registry of context-dependent contraindications at the point of order;
3. when an order fails, return a differential diagnosis with discriminating findings rather than a single opaque label;
4. treat failures induced by the tool or its advice as iatrogenic adverse events; and
5. admit the intervention to broad use only on absolute benefit and harm measurements analogous to NNT and NNH.

**Transfer verdict: very likely (80-90%) for the narrow shell-quoting problem**, because the supplied corpus identifies a recurrent, mechanically classifiable adverse event (1,631 unmatched-quote EOF failures), both domains mediate a consequential action through an order-entry interface, and both can place a context-sensitive check immediately before action. ## 1. What medicine actually solved

### 1.1 Differential diagnosis: maintain alternatives, then seek discriminating evidence

In clinical medicine, a differential diagnosis is not merely a list of things that might be wrong. It is an iterative procedure. The National Academies’ diagnostic-process model describes repeated information gathering, integration, interpretation, hypothesis generation, and probability updating. A working diagnosis may remain a list; clinicians test what findings would be expected under each candidate, gather additional history/tests, and narrow or revise the list. The uncertainty is meant to be communicated rather than hidden ([National Academies, *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care*, chapter 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/), especially the process description and clinical-reasoning discussion).

AHRQ’s review of diagnostic checklists supplies an important refinement: a process prompt such as “what else can it be?” can trigger deliberate reconsideration, but content-specific checklists have shown effects that are often small or limited to subgroups. In other words, “always consider alternatives” is not by itself a strong intervention; the candidates and tests need to fit the case ([AHRQ, “Content-Specific Versus Process-Focused Checklists”](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)).

The transferable mechanism is therefore:

- keep multiple hypotheses only when evidence does not yet distinguish them;
- attach each hypothesis to expected and contradictory findings;
- run the cheapest/highest-information discriminating test;
- update, do not merely repeat the first action; and
- expose uncertainty.

A generic tool error that presents only a terminal string such as “unexpected EOF” leaves the agent to guess whether the cause is an unmatched quote, a wrong shell dialect, a wrapper truncation, or a child-program failure. The proposed translation is more discriminating than that error shape; this unit does not claim every current tool uses it.

### 1.2 Contraindications: context predicates, not a global blacklist

In medicine, a contraindication is not synonymous with “this drug has risk.” FDA guidance says a contraindication is warranted where, in a clinical situation, the likelihood and severity of an adverse reaction make risk outweigh benefit and the causal relationship is well established; anticipated reactions in a specific clinical situation can also qualify. It further says contraindications should be ordered by relative clinical significance, considering severity and likelihood. Boxed warnings are reserved for especially serious risk/benefit cases or serious reactions preventable by patient selection, monitoring, avoiding concomitant therapy, or avoiding use in a specific situation ([FDA, *Warnings and Precautions, Contraindications, and Boxed Warning Sections of Labeling*](https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Warnings-and-Precautions--Contraindications--and-Boxed-Warning-Sections-of-Labeling-for-Human-Prescription-Drug-and-Biological-Products-%E2%80%94-Content-and-Format.pdf), pp. 9, 12–13).

Computerized clinical decision support operationalizes this at order entry. The US eCQI Resource Center describes CDS as more than pop-up alerts: it includes order sets, templates, diagnostic support, and contextually relevant information. Its “Five Rights” are the right information, person, format, channel, and time in workflow ([CMS/ONC eCQI Resource Center, “Clinical Decision Support”](https://ecqi.healthit.gov/cds/about)).

The transferable mechanism is:

- represent an action and relevant context in fields a machine can inspect;
- match a small rule whose antecedent is explicit;
- grade responses by expected severity and confidence;
- deliver the response before the action, in an actionable format; and
- preserve an override for uncertain/non-catastrophic cases while recording the reason.

Crucially, this is not permission to create a giant regex warning catalog. Medicine’s own experience argues against that.

### 1.3 Iatrogenics and reconciliation: inspect whether the intervention caused the new problem

An iatrogenic condition is caused by care rather than merely co-occurring after it. The operational lesson is to keep the intervention in the causal differential. WHO describes medication errors as emerging at prescribing, transcribing, dispensing, administration, and monitoring stages, driven by weak systems and human factors—not only individual ignorance ([WHO, “Medication Without Harm”](https://www.who.int/initiatives/medication-without-harm)).

Medication reconciliation is a concrete defense at transitions: build the best possible medication history and verify it against another reliable source; compare it with current orders; identify and resolve discrepancies; document changes; and communicate the current list and reasons for change ([WHO, *Medication Safety in Transitions of Care*](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/325453/WHO-UHC-SDS-2019.9-eng.pdf), §1.2). The second-source requirement is particularly relevant: it prevents the new order from silently inheriting a bad or incomplete representation of prior state.

The transferable mechanism is provenance-aware reconciliation:

- retain the exact requested operation, the normalized operation actually executed, parser findings, and tool-generated remediation;
- compare request with execution rather than assuming the wrapper transmitted it faithfully;
- if a retry copies or transforms the tool’s prior advice, mark that exposure;
- after another failure, test “the adapter/remediation caused or preserved the defect” alongside “the agent made a new mistake.”

That is designed to catch a class ordinary exit-code logging cannot identify by itself: the harness rather than the agent transformed or recommended the command that produced the next adverse event. Whether such events occur in this harness is **UNVERIFIED: settle with request/normalized/spawn reconciliation plus causal replay**.

### 1.4 NNT/NNH: require absolute benefit and harm, not an impressive relative percentage

Number needed to treat (NNT) is the inverse of absolute risk reduction: `NNT = 1 / (control event rate − experimental event rate)`. Oxford’s Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine shows why baseline risk matters: an identical 71% relative reduction yields NNTs of 2, 15, or 147 at different baseline event rates ([Oxford CEBM, “Number Needed to Treat”](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/resources/ebm-tools/number-needed-to-treat-nnt)). Number needed to harm (NNH) analogously expresses the additional harmful-event rate under an intervention; larger NNH means rarer incremental harm ([BMJ, “What is number needed to harm?”](https://www.bmj.com/content/347/bmj.f4869)).

The transferable mechanism is an admission criterion for tool-design changes:

- report absolute quote-failure risk per eligible invocation in control and treatment;
- compute the number of eligible invocations that must receive the new interface to prevent one quote failure;
- separately compute the number exposed per additional false block, needless retry, or material latency event;
- stratify by baseline risk (direct executable call, simple shell, multiline shell, shell-with-nesting) rather than pooling everything; and
- include uncertainty intervals and the observation window.

No valid NNT-like value can be calculated from “1,631 failures” alone because the eligible-call denominator, exposure allocation, and counterfactual rate are absent. Claiming an effect size from that count would be false precision.

## 2. The concrete harness translation

## Proposal A — argv-first “order entry,” with explicit shell escape hatch — UNVERIFIED (settle with shadow replay plus canary A/B)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a narrow execution schema on the current tool bus:

```json
{
  "executable": "git",
  "argv": ["show", "origin/main:path with spaces/file.md"],
  "cwd": "C:/repo",
  "stdin": null,
  "shell": "none"
}
```

Only operations that genuinely require pipes, redirection, expansion, compound statements, or shell built-ins use:

```json
{
  "shell": "bash",
  "script": "printf '%s\\n' \"$value\" | tool --flag",
  "cwd": "C:/repo"
}
```

`shell` is an enum such as `none | bash | pwsh`, never inferred from punctuation. The schema rejects the ambiguous combination of `shell: none` with `script`, and rejects `shell != none` without `script`. Literal arguments remain separate values. Python’s standard library explicitly recommends a sequence of arguments because the library then handles escaping/quoting, including spaces in filenames ([Python `subprocess`](https://docs.python.org/3.13/library/subprocess.html)). That documentation is implementation evidence for the mechanism, not evidence that this particular harness already exposes it.

**What it catches/prevents that the present failure string misses:**

- In every eligible no-shell invocation, unmatched shell quotes cease to be representable: a quote is data inside one argv element rather than syntax. The proportion of calls eligible for this path is **UNVERIFIED: settle by classifying a representative execution-call sample**.
- The interface identifies shell dialect before parsing, preventing an error handler from proposing Bash quoting to PowerShell or the converse.
- Validation failures point to a field (`argv[3]`, `script`, `shell`) instead of reporting only an execution-layer EOF.
- The recorded structured request can be compared with the actual spawn request, revealing wrapper-introduced transformation.

It is **likely (55-80%) that this fits in roughly 200 lines for the adapter itself**, based on the small schema and delegation to native spawn/parser APIs; packaging, tests, and telemetry may exceed that line count. The experiment above must settle both implementation size and efficacy. If the existing bus cannot add a tool schema without a daemon, this proposal must be re-scoped to a pre-tool hook; it must not grow into BUILD-LARGE merely to preserve the metaphor.

## Proposal B — parser-backed contraindication registry — UNVERIFIED (settle with labeled replay of failures and successful complex commands)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For `shell != none`, call the selected shell’s parser before execution. Bash documents `-n`/`noexec` as reading commands without executing and using that mode to check syntax ([GNU Bash manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/bash/manual/html_node/The-Set-Builtin.html)). PowerShell’s parser API returns an AST, tokens, and parse errors for an input string ([Microsoft `Parser.ParseInput`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.management.automation.language.parser.parseinput)). Do not implement a home-grown quote parser.

The registry should initially contain only predicates backed by deterministic parser evidence:

| Predicate | Evidence | Response | Override |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selected parser reports incomplete string/unterminated quote | native parse error plus source span/token | hard stop; show exact span and one minimally changed candidate | explicit “execute anyway” only if the runtime meaningfully permits incomplete input |
| Parser is clean, but requested shell is unavailable | executable resolution result plus declared `shell` | hard stop; report missing runtime | change shell or environment |
| Parser is clean and spawn begins | exit status/stderr belongs to child program | no quoting warning; pass to runtime differential | not applicable |
| Adapter’s normalized request differs from submitted request | stored request/normalized-request diff | hard stop on lossy transformation | none until adapter fixed |

This is deliberately a **contraindication registry**, not a style linter. A suspicious-looking but parse-valid command is not contraindicated merely because it contains nesting or many quotes. Severity follows evidence and consequence, paralleling FDA’s requirement that contraindications reflect established causal relation and an unfavorable risk/benefit situation rather than any conceivable risk ([FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Warnings-and-Precautions--Contraindications--and-Boxed-Warning-Sections-of-Labeling-for-Human-Prescription-Drug-and-Biological-Products-%E2%80%94-Content-and-Format.pdf)).

**What it catches that is currently missed:** syntax failure before side effects; the responsible source span; dialect-specific parse evidence; and request-to-spawn corruption. It also suppresses a common false diagnosis: if the native parser reports clean syntax, “unmatched quote” is removed from the leading differential rather than reflexively suggested.

## Proposal C — evidence-bearing error differential, not generic remediation — UNVERIFIED (settle with blinded top-1/top-3 diagnosis scoring on held-out errors)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Return a stable envelope such as:

```json
{
  "phase": "parse",
  "executed": false,
  "primary": {
    "code": "SHELL_UNTERMINATED_STRING",
    "confidence": "parser-confirmed",
    "span": {"start": 41, "end": 58},
    "evidence": "native bash parser: unexpected EOF while looking for matching quote"
  },
  "differential": [
    {
      "code": "WRAPPER_TRANSFORMATION",
      "for": ["submitted and normalized payload differ"],
      "against": [],
      "next_test": "compare submitted, normalized, and spawned payload bytes"
    }
  ],
  "repair": {
    "kind": "minimal_patch",
    "requires_confirmation": true
  }
}
```

Candidate families should be phase-based and mutually distinguishable:

1. **schema/order error** — malformed structured request;
2. **parse error** — native parser finding and location;
3. **spawn/transport error** — child never started;
4. **child runtime error** — parser clean, child started, nonzero exit;
5. **adapter-iatrogenic error** — submitted and executed representations differ materially.

The response must not fabricate ranked probabilities. “Parser-confirmed,” “supported,” and “unresolved” are safer evidence states until calibrated frequencies exist. A differential with only one evidence-supported item is acceptable; diagnostic theater is not. This follows the National Academies mechanism of hypotheses updated by findings, without pretending a deterministic parser is a patient ([National Academies diagnostic process](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)).

**What it catches that generic stderr misses:** whether execution occurred; whether the failure belongs to schema, parser, spawn, child, or adapter; what evidence supports each diagnosis; and the next discriminating probe. It reduces blind retries by making a repeated unchanged command visibly unsupported once the same finding recurs. The amount of retry reduction is **UNVERIFIED: settle in the held-out replay and canary A/B described below**.

## Proposal D — iatrogenic provenance and reconciliation — UNVERIFIED (settle by mutation tests and causal replay)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Require the existing tool event record to retain, or attach hashes plus secure references for:

- submitted structured request;
- normalized request;
- exact shell/parser selected and version;
- parser result;
- exact spawn representation;
- tool-produced repair/remediation ID; and
- retry parent ID.

On a retry, reconcile the prior request, prior remediation, new request, and actual spawn request. Classify an event as `adapter-associated` when a representation changes before spawn, and as `remediation-associated` when the retry incorporates a tool-proposed patch. Do not claim causation merely from temporal order; confirm with a replay in which removing/reversing the transformation removes the failure. WHO’s medication-safety model supports treating system stages as possible sources of harm, while medication reconciliation supports explicit comparison and resolution of discrepancies ([WHO Medication Without Harm](https://www.who.int/initiatives/medication-without-harm), [WHO transitions-of-care report](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/325453/WHO-UHC-SDS-2019.9-eng.pdf)).

It is **likely (55-80%) that the provenance addition remains BUILD-SMALL**, because it adds a bounded record plus comparisons on an existing event bus; this premise is **UNVERIFIED: settle by inspecting the bus schema and prototyping the record in a repository-grounding/build unit**. If it requires a new service, reject or redesign it rather than silently crossing into BUILD-LARGE.

**What it catches that current outcome-only accounting misses:** tool-induced quoting changes, a bad remediation copied into a retry, and repeated execution of a transformed payload. It also makes it possible to distinguish agent error from interface-induced error in the evaluation.

## Proposal E — NNT/NNH admission gate — UNVERIFIED (settle with preregistered shadow replay and live canary)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Define the unit of exposure as an **eligible execution invocation**, not an agent session and not merely an alert. Pre-register:

- `QF`: native-parser-confirmed unmatched-quote failure;
- `FPB`: a parse-valid command blocked or diverted by the intervention;
- `NR`: needless retry attributable to the intervention;
- `LAT`: latency above a chosen material threshold attributable to validation;
- strata: `shell:none`, simple Bash, complex Bash, simple PowerShell, complex PowerShell;
- control and treatment windows/allocation; and
- how repeated attempts from one intent are clustered.

Then compute:

```text
ARR_QF = risk(QF | control) - risk(QF | treatment)
NNE_QF = 1 / ARR_QF

ARI_FPB = risk(FPB | treatment) - risk(FPB | control)
NNH_FPB = 1 / ARI_FPB
```

`NNE_QF` is “number needed to expose to prevent one quote failure.” Report raw counts, absolute rates, interval estimates, and strata alongside it. If `ARR_QF <= 0`, report no demonstrated benefit; do not emit an infinite-looking success metric. Apply the same scheme separately to needless retry and latency harms.

The rollout rule should compare benefit with harm in owner-valued units, not assert that any positive NNE is worthwhile. One candidate rule is broad rollout only if the upper confidence bound still shows meaningful absolute reduction and no material FPB increase, but its suitability is **roughly even (45-55%)** because statistical confidence does not encode the owner’s relative cost of failures, friction, and delay. The thresholds are **UNVERIFIED: settle by owner-specified cost weights before the canary**.

## 3. Example: how the new interface handles an unmatched quote

Suppose an agent submits a Bash script whose final double quote is missing.

**Current generic path:** shell returns an EOF/matching-quote message; the wrapper may label the whole call “Bash failed”; the agent changes quoting based on intuition and may repeat the defect.

**Proposed path:**

1. The tool knows this is Bash because `shell: "bash"` was declared.
2. Bash’s native no-exec parse returns a syntax error without executing the submitted user command; the adapter retains any line/token location the native parser supplies ([GNU Bash manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/bash/manual/html_node/The-Set-Builtin.html)).
3. The contraindication rule blocks this specific script because causality is parser-confirmed.
4. The error envelope reports `phase=parse`, `executed=false`, the best native location available, and a minimal proposed closure explicitly labeled as a proposed repair rather than parser fact.
5. The retry references the remediation. Reconciliation verifies that only the intended span changed.
6. If it still fails, the differential asks whether the wrapper transformed the repaired string; it compares submitted, normalized, and spawned representations instead of prescribing more escaping.
7. The evaluation counts one exposed invocation, whether a quote failure was prevented, any false block, and retry/latency cost.

For a simple command that does not need shell semantics, the better result occurs earlier: the agent submits executable plus argv, so there is no shell quote grammar to fail.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy transfers and where it breaks

### Structural matches

1. **Order entry mediates action.** A prescription and a tool call both convert intent into an executable order. Structured fields can eliminate ambiguity before execution. This is a strong match for interface design, not for diagnosis in general.

2. **Context changes safety.** A medication can be safe generally but contraindicated for a patient state; shell syntax can be valid generally but invalid under a selected dialect or wrapper representation. Explicit context predicates transfer well.

3. **The intervention can cause harm.** Drugs and clinical systems can cause iatrogenic harm; tool wrappers and repairs can introduce quoting or transformation defects. Provenance and reconciliation transfer well.

4. **Benefit depends on baseline risk.** NNT changes with baseline event rate; a quoting intervention will have different yield for argv-compatible calls and deeply nested shell scripts. Absolute, stratified evaluation transfers directly as a statistical discipline.

### Disanalogies and required constraints

1. **Shell parsing is deterministic; diagnosis is not.** A native parser result should dominate a heuristic differential. Copying medical “clinical judgment” into a parser problem would make the system less precise. Therefore the differential is phase/evidence-based and may contain one item.

2. **The agent and the tool are software components, not independent clinician and patient.** Their errors can share a common model or prompt source. “Second opinion” is not mechanically independent if another LLM rereads the same stderr. Therefore the second signal must be a native parser, spawn telemetry, or request/spawn byte comparison—not another model’s prose.

3. **Attention and cadence differ.** Automated agents do not have the same attentional physiology as clinicians, but repeated warnings still consume tokens and latency and can train the policy trace toward routine override. An interruptive warning on every complex command risks becoming a machine analogue of alert fatigue; that risk is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the cited high clinical override rates and the direct per-alert token/latency cost, though agent override behavior remains **UNVERIFIED: settle in the live canary**.

4. **Clinical contraindications encode population evidence; a local shell registry can be exact.** This argues for fewer deterministic rules, not a broad learned risk catalog. Rules with weak evidence belong in silent telemetry until validated.

5. **NNT is interpretable only with a defined intervention, population, comparator, and time horizon.** “Failures fell after launch” is not enough; model upgrades, task mix, and retries confound the result. Randomized canary assignment or matched replay is needed.

6. **Consequences differ.** A shell syntax failure is usually recoverable, while a medical adverse event can be irreversible. Hard stops therefore need a much higher precision threshold than the analogy’s emotive force might suggest; owner autonomy and task progress matter.

### The strongest negative evidence: clinical alert fatigue

Medication CDS demonstrates how this design can fail. In a large outpatient study of 2,004,069 orders and 157,483 alerts, clinicians overrode about half of alerts; among reviewed overrides, appropriateness varied dramatically by category—92% appropriate for patient-allergy alerts but only 12% for drug–drug and renal-suggestion alerts. The authors argue for category-specific refinement rather than indiscriminate alerting ([Nanji et al., JAMIA 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3994856/)). Another randomized primary-care study notes prior override ranges of 49–96% and found both triggered and on-demand decision support underused, with clinical relevance a central limitation ([Tamblyn et al., JAMIA 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2442270/)).

This negative evidence is central, not a footnote. It means a warning-heavy port is **unlikely (20-45%) to provide net benefit**, based on high medical alert override rates and the additional token/latency burden in an agent loop. The narrow argv-first plus parser-confirmed design is **very likely (80-90%) to be more faithful to the useful medical mechanism than a universal checklist**, because it changes the order set and reserves interruption for deterministic contraindications; evidence is the CDS Five Rights framework, the override studies, and native parser availability. Whether it improves this harness remains UNVERIFIED and requires the experiments below.

## 5. Evaluation protocol that would settle the recommendation

### Stage 0 — detector validation

Before measuring absence or improvement, validate two mechanically independent detectors:

1. **Known-present positive control:** a fixture with a deliberately unterminated single and double quote for each shell. The native parser must find it.
2. **Independent confirmation:** shell parse result plus the historical error fingerprint/exit phase. For request transformation, use request-versus-spawn byte/hash mismatch plus a replay showing that the untransformed request succeeds.

Also include known-valid hard cases: nested quotes, spaces, JSON literals, empty arguments, Unicode, PowerShell here-strings, and Bash heredocs. A detector that cannot find the positive controls or preserve the negative controls is invalid; do not use its zero count as evidence of absence.

### Stage 1 — frozen shadow replay

Replay a labeled, deduplicated sample containing:

- confirmed unmatched-quote failures;
- other parse failures;
- spawn failures;
- child-program failures with quote-like stderr;
- successful simple commands; and
- successful quote-heavy commands.

The intervention receives the original request but cannot execute or block. Score:

- quote-failure sensitivity and specificity;
- phase-classification accuracy;
- top-1 and top-3 differential accuracy;
- source-location accuracy at the granularity the native parser supports;
- fraction eligible for argv conversion;
- false-block counterfactuals; and
- whether proposed repairs pass the native parser and preserve an independently specified intent oracle.

Use a held-out set for final scoring. Do not tune rules on all 1,631 events and then report resubstitution performance.

### Stage 2 — mutation tests for iatrogenics

Generate controlled transformations: remove a closing quote, alter one escape, change declared dialect, truncate the payload, reorder argv, join two argv elements, and mutate remediation output. Verify that:

- native syntax defects are caught before execution;
- argv corruption is caught by reconciliation;
- a clean parser prevents mislabeling child failure as quote failure; and
- remediation provenance survives retry.

### Stage 3 — randomized live canary

Randomize eligible invocations, clustered by agent task/session to avoid cross-arm contamination, to current interface or new adapter. Start with non-destructive commands and a small exposure share. Pre-register QF, FPB, NR, LAT, cancellation criteria, and subgroup analyses. Report NNE/NNH-like measures from absolute differences with intervals. A time-only before/after comparison is insufficient because model and task mix can change.

### Stage 4 — decision rule

Roll out by stratum, not globally. `shell:none` may graduate independently of Bash/Powershell parsing. Remove or demote any rule whose false-block/override burden dominates its prevented failures. Preserve the shell escape hatch for operations requiring real shell semantics.

The canary is **likely (55-80%) to give a decision-relevant result if exposure is large enough**, based on the measured recurrence of quote errors and randomized allocation; the necessary sample size is UNVERIFIED and must be computed from the eligible-call baseline rate and the minimum absolute reduction the owner values.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Teach the model a quoting checklist”

Rejected as the primary intervention. A process checklist does not remove the affordance that permits the error, adds tokens to every call, and lacks a mechanically independent signal. AHRQ reports that checklist effects can be small or subgroup-limited ([AHRQ](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/dxchecklists-3.html)). A short skill may remain educational documentation, but it does not count as the architecture.

### “Add a giant regex contraindication database”

Rejected. It would reproduce low-specificity CDS alerting, be dialect-fragile, and likely create alert fatigue. Native parsers already encode the grammar. Only deterministic context predicates belong in the initial registry.

### “Ask a second model to diagnose every error”

Rejected. It is not mechanically independent, adds cost/latency, and risks a trained-critic/second-runtime pattern explicitly barred by the anti-overfit gate. Native parser output, phase telemetry, and representation diffs are better discriminators.

### “Automatically repair and execute”

Rejected for the first version. A syntactically valid repair can change semantics. Parser success proves grammar, not intent. Return a minimal diff and require the normal agent decision path; evaluate repair semantics on replay before considering limited automation.

### “Replace shell access entirely”

Rejected. Pipes, redirects, expansions, shell built-ins, and compound programs are legitimate. The design makes argv the default order set and shell an explicit escape hatch, preserving capability.

### “Build a durable clinical-style safety service”

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. It would be BUILD-LARGE and would introduce another runtime for a problem native parsers and the existing bus can address. There is no evidence here that such complexity earns its cost.
