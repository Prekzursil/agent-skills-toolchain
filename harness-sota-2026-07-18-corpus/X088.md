# X088 — Cross-domain borrow: mechanism design → orchestration

**Unit:** X088  
**Source field:** mechanism design (incentive compatibility, proper scoring rules, Goodhart/Campbell failure modes)  
**Target decision:** how many parallel agent workers to run, and when another worker stops being worth its cost  
**Lens constraint:** external research and design only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness.  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive result

There is a useful but deliberately narrow transfer: **Hanson's market scoring rule (MSR), combined with marginal-contribution accounting, can be borrowed as an empirical accounting rule for parallel workers. It cannot be borrowed as a literal incentive-compatible market.** In its native field, an MSR pays a forecaster only for the improvement from the previous public probability report to the forecaster's new report, measured after the outcome resolves. In the harness translation, each additional worker (or batch) is charged its token/latency cost and credited only for the out-of-sample improvement it adds over the workers already available. Historical marginal credits then train a stopping rule: launch another batch only while its predicted incremental task value exceeds its incremental cost.

My confidence that the **accounting principle** transfers is **very likely (80-90%)**, because (a) MSRs explicitly measure change from a prior information state rather than raw performance, as Hanson defines mathematically, and (b) parallel-worker orchestration has the same operational need to distinguish novel information from redundant repetitions. The evidence is Hanson's definition of payment as the difference between consecutive proper scores and current agent-scaling evidence that gains, overhead, redundancy, and error amplification vary by task and topology ([Hanson 2003, pp. 110-112](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/pubfiles/infomkts/litreview/Hanson%20-%20Combinatorial%20Information%20Market%20Design%2003.pdf); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

The claim that this accounting principle will choose better fan-out on this owner's real workload is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that would settle it is the “Parallel Marginal Value Trial” (PMVT) specified below**. I assess success as **likely (55-80%)**, supported by the mechanism's fit to marginal attribution and by published demonstrations that agent-count returns depend on task difficulty, tool use, topology, and baseline capability, but weakened by the absence of immediate objective outcomes for many real tasks ([Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

The literal mechanism-design theorem does **not** transfer. LLM calls are not expected-utility-maximizing economic agents with private values, monetary transfers, and enforceable liabilities. Thus neither strict propriety nor VCG supplies a theorem that an LLM will honestly report confidence or cost. This is an assumption-level disanalogy, not an implementation nuisance. Any design that asks workers to “bid” for work and then treats those bids as truthful would be mechanism-design theater.

## 1. The actual orchestration problem

“How many workers?” is not primarily a concurrency-limit question. It is a **marginal value of information under cost and correlation** question:

1. A first worker may solve the task.
2. A second worker can contribute an independent route, find a defect, or merely repeat the first worker.
3. Additional workers consume tokens, wall-clock concurrency, tool quotas, synthesis attention, and sometimes create more opportunities for propagation of a shared error.
4. The benefit of worker `k+1` depends on what workers `1..k` already produced, not only on that worker's standalone quality.
5. The relevant stopping point is where expected incremental owner value no longer exceeds expected incremental cost—not where agreement becomes numerically high, output count reaches a fixed constant, or the context window fills.

Published agent evidence makes the non-universality concrete. Li et al. report that sampling and voting can improve performance as instantiated-agent count rises, with larger relative gains on harder tasks; they also report proportional token growth and a code-task degradation attributed to noise from other agents ([Li et al. 2024, §§5-6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120)). A later controlled study across 180 configurations reports tool/coordination trade-offs, diminishing or negative returns after capability saturation, topology-dependent error amplification, and large degradation on sequential-reasoning tasks ([Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). These papers do not establish a universal production law—their tasks, models, and evaluation settings differ—but they directly reject “always one” and “always more” as evidence-based universal policies.

The statement that the owner's workload exhibits the same response surfaces is **UNVERIFIED; PMVT would settle it**. It is **likely (55-80%)**, because the cited studies span multiple tasks, models, tools, and topologies, but production tasks can differ substantially from benchmarks.

## 2. What mechanism design solved in its own field

### 2.1 Proper scoring rules: elicit a probability, not a confident slogan

A scoring rule assigns a numerical score `S(p, y)` to a probabilistic forecast `p` after outcome `y` is observed. A rule is strictly proper when a risk-neutral forecaster uniquely maximizes expected score by reporting its actual belief distribution. Gneiting and Raftery give this definition and characterize proper rules over general probability spaces; examples include logarithmic and quadratic scores ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)).

For a binary event `Y ∈ {0,1}`, a bounded quadratic/Brier reward can be written:

```text
S_Brier(p, y) = -(p - y)^2
```

If the forecaster's belief that `Y=1` is `q`, expected squared loss is uniquely minimized at `p=q`. The important mechanism-design move is not “measure confidence.” It is **choose a payment/evaluation rule under which honest probability reporting is the optimum**, given the theorem's assumptions.

This solves a field-native information-elicitation problem: a principal needs beliefs held privately by forecasters, while forecasters may otherwise shade reports toward dramatic, safe, or reputationally convenient numbers. Proper scores align the report with the forecaster's belief when outcomes resolve and the agent maximizes expected score.

### 2.2 Market scoring rules: pay for the update, not for being present

Hanson's market scoring rule turns a proper score into a sequential information-aggregation mechanism. The system holds a current probability distribution `r_(t-1)`. A participant may replace it with `r_t`. When state `i` resolves, that participant receives:

```text
ΔS_i = S_i(r_t) - S_i(r_(t-1))
```

Hanson's paper states this difference payment directly and explains that, because the participant cannot alter the previous report, maximizing expected difference is equivalent to maximizing the expected proper score of the new report. Under the assumptions, a participant whose beliefs differ from the current distribution expects to profit by correcting it ([Hanson 2003, pp. 110-111](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/pubfiles/infomkts/litreview/Hanson%20-%20Combinatorial%20Information%20Market%20Design%2003.pdf)).

This mechanism has three properties relevant to the borrow:

- **Baseline-relative credit.** A forecaster gets no credit merely because its final forecast is good; credit is the improvement over the immediately prior forecast.
- **Redundancy is naturally cheap.** If the current forecast already incorporates the information, repeating it produces little or no update and therefore little or no expected reward.
- **Sequential aggregation with bounded sponsor exposure.** Hanson explains that the patron ultimately pays the last user and that, for a logarithmic rule, worst-case expected loss relates to the initial distribution's entropy ([Hanson 2003, pp. 111-112](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/pubfiles/infomkts/litreview/Hanson%20-%20Combinatorial%20Information%20Market%20Design%2003.pdf)). This is a property of the economic mechanism; it does not mean LLM inference cost becomes independent of worker count.

The specific mechanism being borrowed is therefore **the market scoring rule's difference payment**, not the loose idea of “make agents vote,” and not a generic auction.

### 2.3 Peer prediction: important nearby work, but not the right borrow

Peer prediction addresses settings in which the principal lacks ground truth. Miller, Resnick, and Zeckhauser score a rater's report against the implied posterior over another rater's report; under their model, honest reporting is a Nash equilibrium ([Miller, Resnick & Zeckhauser 2005](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0379)).

That resembles agents reviewing one another, but it is a dangerous primary mechanism here. Correlated LLM workers can share training-induced mistakes, prompt-induced framing, or copied context. Agreement may then reward a shared falsehood. The claim that peer prediction would improve this harness is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, because the target problem is choosing marginal fan-out, not merely eliciting ratings, and because peer agreement is not independent task truth; the evidence is the peer-prediction mechanism's reliance on reports predicting peer reports rather than external correctness, together with multi-agent evidence of shared-misconception and error-propagation failure modes ([Miller et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0379); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

Peer agreement may be retained as a diagnostic feature, never as the resolving outcome.

### 2.4 VCG: conceptually illuminating, structurally rejected

The Vickrey-Clarke-Groves family selects an efficient outcome from reported private values and uses transfers tied to each participant's externality on others. In the standard private-value, quasi-linear setting, truthful revelation is a dominant strategy. The literature also explicitly notes that this efficient-implementation result does not generally extend to interdependent-value environments ([McLean & Postlewaite 2015 working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID2207941_code327792.pdf?abstractid=2207941)).

There is an appealing slogan-level mapping: charge each worker its externality and keep only positive marginal contributors. But the structural assumptions fail:

- A worker has no private economic valuation for being spawned.
- It does not receive or care about a transferable payment.
- Its “cost” is borne by the owner and infrastructure, not privately known to the worker.
- Values are interdependent: one worker's usefulness depends on other workers' outputs and the synthesizer.
- The system cannot generally compute the counterfactual owner value with versus without a worker at decision time.

Therefore VCG is **not** the proposed mechanism. My confidence in this rejection is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on direct mismatch with the private-value, quasi-linear, strategic-agent assumptions and the cited limitation under interdependent values. The residual 1-10% covers future agent runtimes that may explicitly optimize persistent utilities with enforceable budgets; that is not assumed here.

## 3. The narrow structural mapping that survives

| Mechanism-design object | Harness translation | What transfers | What does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resolved state `y` | Externally determined task outcome: tests pass, factual claims verify, patch accepted without correction, or rubric adjudication | Ex-post evaluation | A subjective judge is not ground truth |
| Current forecast `p_k` | Estimated probability that the current best artifact will meet the predeclared outcome | A compact state for expected success | It cannot represent every quality dimension |
| New report `p_(k+1)` | Updated success probability after adding one worker's evidence/artifact | Incremental information update | The worker's self-reported confidence is not theorem-backed |
| Difference score `S(p_(k+1),y)-S(p_k,y)` | Realized marginal predictive contribution of that worker/batch | Baseline-relative credit; redundancy receives little credit | It is not causal if worker order or synthesis changes |
| Forecaster payment | Internal credit used to update a dispatcher policy | Learning signal | LLMs do not become truthful because of virtual points |
| Market liquidity / participation | Decision to buy another sample of work | Continue only while expected value exceeds cost | There is no literal market or autonomous bidder |
| Sponsor's budget | Token, dollar, latency, tool-quota, and synthesis budget | Explicit resource constraint | MSR's bounded financial loss does not bound inference spend |

The deep commonality is that value is **conditional on the information already present**. That commonality is sufficient for an accounting borrow. The absence of strategic utility is fatal only to the incentive-compatibility theorem, not to computing score differences after outcomes resolve.

My confidence in that distinction is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the algebra: score differences can be computed regardless of who generated the forecasts, while truthful elicitation specifically requires the forecaster to maximize expected score. The remaining uncertainty is whether a single probability state is expressive enough for heterogeneous production work; PMVT tests that empirically.

## 4. Recommended mechanism — UNVERIFIED; PMVT is the settling experiment

### Marginal-Information Fan-out Controller (MIFC)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement as a thin policy and append-only event ledger around the existing worker-spawn and completion events. It is not a second orchestrator, daemon, durable-execution engine, vector database, critic model, guardrail framework, or observability service.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; run PMVT before enabling adaptive decisions**. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce wasted fan-out without harming outcome quality, because it prices marginal rather than standalone contribution and uses randomized evidence rather than worker rhetoric. The evidence supporting that band is the MSR difference-payment result and the observed task-dependent scaling/overhead in agent-system studies ([Hanson 2003](https://healy.econ.ohio-state.edu/pubfiles/infomkts/litreview/Hanson%20-%20Combinatorial%20Information%20Market%20Design%2003.pdf); [Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

#### 4.1 Predeclare a resolvable contract

Before spawning, map the task to one or more outcome contracts. Examples:

```text
Y_test      = 1 if the requested test suite passes after independent verification
Y_accept    = 1 if the artifact is accepted without material correction in the evaluation window
Y_claims    = fraction of sampled factual claims verified by primary sources
Y_rubric    = normalized score from a blinded, versioned rubric
```

Use an owner-value function declared before seeing worker outputs:

```text
U = V_test*Y_test + V_accept*Y_accept + V_claims*Y_claims + V_rubric*Y_rubric
    - λ_tok*tokens - λ_time*critical_path_seconds - λ_tool*scarce_tool_calls
```

For a first pilot, prefer bounded Brier scores on binary outcomes over logarithmic scores. A log score becomes unbounded at probabilities near zero and one, making one overconfident miss dominate a small production sample. The claim that Brier is operationally safer for this pilot is **very likely (80-90%)**, because bounded loss limits outlier leverage while retaining strict propriety for binary probability forecasts; the evidence for strict propriety and the available rule families is [Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf). “Safer” here means statistically less sensitive in a small pilot, not proven better final policy performance.

Tasks with no outcome that can be resolved independently of the workers are **not eligible for automatic MIFC decisions**. They stay on a conservative fixed policy while the system logs shadow predictions. This is a feature, not a missing fallback: optimizing an unresolvable proxy is precisely where Goodhart pressure is strongest.

#### 4.2 Run workers in small independent batches

Start with one worker for tasks with known serial structure and two workers for plausibly parallelizable tasks. Subsequent exploration uses small batches, for example fan-out levels `{1,2,4,8}`, not an unbounded swarm.

To buy information rather than duplicated prose, vary at least one source of epistemic diversity that can plausibly affect errors: model family, tool access, decomposition, or intentionally independent context. Cosmetic personas do not count as independence. The claim that such diversity will reduce error correlation on this workload is **UNVERIFIED; PMVT's within-task error-overlap analysis settles it**. It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because diversity can create complementary paths, but shared pretraining, shared tools, and shared task framing can preserve strong correlation.

Workers must not see sibling outputs during the initial production round. This gives a usable counterfactual set and reduces copying. A later synthesizer may see all outputs. The claim that blind initial work will improve marginal attribution is **very likely (80-90%)**, because it mechanically prevents direct cross-copying and allows leave-one-out evaluation; it does not eliminate latent correlation.

#### 4.3 Maintain two probabilities around each marginal addition

For each task and candidate batch, record:

- `p_before`: dispatcher/evaluator probability that the current best artifact satisfies the outcome contract;
- `p_after`: the same probability after revealing the new worker or batch;
- resource cost of the batch;
- final externally resolved outcome `y`;
- task and topology features available before the spawn decision.

Do **not** let the new worker authoritatively set `p_after`. Its confidence may be logged as a feature, but the operational probability comes from a calibrated dispatcher/evaluator fitted on held-out resolved tasks. Proper scoring rules only incentivize honest reports when the reporting entity optimizes the payment; that is not guaranteed for an inference call.

After resolution, compute:

```text
credit(batch) = S_Brier(p_after, y) - S_Brier(p_before, y)
net(batch)    = owner_value_scale * credit(batch) - measured_resource_cost(batch)
```

For continuous rubric outcomes, either pre-bin thresholds into binary contracts or use a proper score designed for the elicited functional. Do not silently apply a binary Brier score to an arbitrary scalar.

#### 4.4 Correct the parallel-order problem

Hanson's MSR is sequential; truly parallel workers have no natural “previous reporter.” Giving credit according to completion order would reward network speed and arbitrarily allocate shared contributions.

For each resolved task, approximate permutation-averaged marginal contribution:

1. Randomly sample several permutations of the completed workers.
2. For each permutation, reveal outputs to the fixed synthesizer/evaluator one at a time.
3. Recompute the score change at each reveal.
4. Average each worker's change across permutations.

This is Shapley-style accounting, not a new claim that the full Shapley mechanism has been implemented. It estimates whether a worker contributes across multiple plausible baselines and separates unique contribution from duplicated contribution better than finish-order credit.

The claim that a small permutation sample will give stable enough attribution is **UNVERIFIED; PMVT's bootstrap stability analysis settles it**. It is **likely (55-80%)** for small fan-outs such as 2-8, because exact enumeration is possible at the low end and Monte Carlo error can be measured, but nonlinear synthesis may cause high interaction variance.

Batch-level credit should remain the decision target even if worker-level attribution is noisy. The live decision is “buy another batch?”, so a stable batch effect is more important than a perfect leaderboard among individual workers.

#### 4.5 Stop on predicted net marginal value, with an exploration floor

Fit a small calibrated model or stratified lookup table:

```text
E[ΔU_(k→k+b) | task features, k, diversity, current evidence]
```

Then apply:

```text
spawn next batch if lower_confidence_bound(E[ΔU]) > 0
stop if upper_confidence_bound(E[ΔU]) < 0
otherwise follow a bounded exploration schedule
```

This three-way rule prevents noisy point estimates from pretending certainty. The exploration schedule is required because a purely greedy stopper stops collecting evidence about higher fan-outs and can lock in its initial bias.

The exact confidence method and exploration rate are **UNVERIFIED; PMVT should compare bootstrap intervals and a simple Bayesian beta-binomial/normal model**. A simple stratified lookup or regularized regression is **likely (55-80%)** to be adequate for a first deployment, because the initial decision space is small; evidence against needless complexity is the moderate cross-validated predictive fit reported even by a much broader 180-configuration scaling study ([Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

Do not optimize raw worker count, agreement rate, confidence, number of findings, or output length. Those are diagnostic features. The target is resolved owner utility net of cost.

#### 4.6 Pseudocode

```text
contract = classify_resolvable_outcomes(task)
if contract is unresolved:
    run_fixed_conservative_policy()
    log_shadow_features()
    return

k = initial_fanout(task)
outputs = run_blind_parallel_batch(k)

while budget_allows:
    p_before = calibrated_success_probability(task, outputs)
    estimate, interval = predict_marginal_net_value(task, outputs, next_batch_size)

    if interval.upper < 0:
        break
    if interval.lower <= 0 <= interval.upper and not exploration_selected(task):
        break

    new_outputs = run_blind_parallel_batch(next_batch_size)
    outputs += new_outputs

artifact = synthesize(outputs)
y = resolve_contract_later(artifact)
append_ledger(task, outputs, artifact, y, measured_costs)
update_policy_only_on_scheduled_retrain()
```

Scheduled retraining, rather than online self-modification after every task, keeps policy changes auditable and reduces feedback instability.

## 5. What this would catch that ordinary fan-out heuristics miss

These are predicted detection capabilities, not claims about the current harness.

### 5.1 The redundant expert

Two strong workers may each look excellent in standalone scoring while producing the same answer and the same mistakes. Standalone quality would keep both; permutation-averaged marginal scoring gives the second little credit when its information is already present.

MIFC is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect exact or near-exact redundancy in resolvable tasks, because score differences collapse when revealing an additional output does not change the calibrated success estimate. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to detect semantic redundancy expressed in different words, because that depends on evaluator quality; PMVT's leave-one-out stability analysis tests it.

### 5.2 The rare complementary specialist

A worker with mediocre average performance may uniquely catch a class of defect that high-average workers share. Winner-take-all leaderboards discard it; conditional marginal credit preserves it when its contribution changes outcomes specifically after the default workers.

MIFC is **likely (55-80%)** to identify such conditional specialists if there are enough repeated task strata and externally resolved outcomes. The evidence is the conditional nature of MSR difference payments; the uncertainty comes from sparse rare-event data. PMVT settles this through task-stratified interaction effects.

### 5.3 Premature consensus

High agreement can mean easy truth or shared error. A fixed “stop at consensus” rule cannot distinguish them. MIFC asks whether additional, genuinely diverse evidence historically changes externally resolved success, and agreement is never the resolving label.

The controller is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform agreement-only stopping where shared errors recur, because its target is external resolution rather than peer consensus; the claim remains **UNVERIFIED until PMVT's shared-wrong subset comparison**.

### 5.4 Negative marginal workers

Another output can make synthesis worse by introducing a persuasive error, increasing conflict, or consuming the synthesizer's attention. Count-based rules assume monotonic benefit. Difference scores allow negative realized contribution, and the stopping model can learn task/topology regions where more workers are harmful.

Detection of negative average batch effects is **very likely (80-90%)** on sufficiently repeated resolvable strata, supported by published reports of degradation from noisy multi-agent synthesis and sequential-task coordination ([Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). Per-task prediction is only **roughly even (45-55%)** initially because rare tasks and evaluator noise limit generalization; PMVT measures this separately.

### 5.5 Tool-heavy and latency-sensitive regimes

An agent may improve answer probability yet have negative owner value after expensive tool use or critical-path delay. Accuracy-only fan-out misses this. Net marginal utility makes those costs first-class.

The controller is **very likely (80-90%)** to identify retrospectively negative-cost batches if costs and outcomes are recorded correctly; this follows arithmetically from the predeclared utility. Whether it predicts them prospectively is **UNVERIFIED; PMVT's held-out policy evaluation settles it**, with **likely (55-80%)** success because tool/coordination trade-offs appear in controlled multi-agent research ([Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

### 5.6 False economies from an over-aggressive stopper

A simple average-marginal rule can stop early on precisely the rare hard tasks for which later diversity helps. The explicit uncertainty interval and randomized exploration arm reveal this failure; a deterministic cost cutoff cannot.

The exploration design is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect systematic under-exploration in the trial, because randomized higher-fan-out assignments provide counterfactual outcome data. It will not reveal benefits beyond the maximum tested fan-out.

## 6. Goodhart and Campbell analysis

Campbell's 1976 paper states the general corruption problem for quantitative social indicators used for decision-making; the original paper is available in a later authorized reprint ([Campbell 1976/2011](https://doi.org/10.56645/jmde.v7i15.297)). Here the pressure is straightforward: if workers or the dispatcher optimize “number of independent findings,” “agreement,” judge score, or self-confidence, outputs can become shaped to the proxy rather than owner value.

Proper scoring rules solve **one narrow Goodhart channel**: under their assumptions, they make truthful probability reporting optimal rather than rewarding confidence inflation. They do not solve:

- misspecified outcome contracts;
- a judge that prefers polish to correctness;
- workers that learn to exploit the evaluator;
- selective task assignment or selective outcome resolution;
- distribution shift after the policy changes;
- correlated failure among worker, synthesizer, and evaluator;
- owner value that is multi-dimensional or delayed beyond the measurement window.

Therefore the mechanism needs the following anti-Goodhart constraints:

1. **Predeclare contracts and cost weights.** Do not change the target after seeing outputs.
2. **Resolve with evidence outside the worker cohort.** Tests, source verification, owner correction, or blinded adjudication outrank peer agreement.
3. **Keep diagnostic features out of the primary target.** Diversity, disagreement, output length, and self-confidence may predict value but are not value.
4. **Maintain randomized audit traffic.** Continue sending a small fraction of eligible tasks to non-greedy fan-outs so the controller's counterfactual assumptions remain testable.
5. **Version and rotate rubrics, with holdouts.** If judge-based outcomes are necessary, reserve hidden audit items and periodically compare judge labels with a second independent outcome channel.
6. **Require two mechanically independent signals before declaring a policy region harmful or solved.** For example, a negative randomized outcome effect plus a negative leave-one-out/permutation marginal score. Two reruns of the same judge are not independent.
7. **Cap autonomy.** The controller may choose only among approved fan-outs and budgets; it may not invent new workers, tools, or spending classes.

These controls are **likely (55-80%)** to reduce proxy gaming relative to optimizing a single visible metric, supported by Campbell's indicator-corruption argument and by the use of external, randomized outcomes. They are **almost certainly not (1-5%)** sufficient to eliminate Goodhart failure, because every resolvable contract remains an incomplete representation of owner value. The experiment for the residual risk is PMVT plus periodic adversarial evaluator-red-team tasks.

## 7. Adversarial transfer audit

### Assumption A: strategic agents maximize expected payment

**Does not transfer.** An LLM inference call follows learned conditional generation; no persistent utility or enforceable balance is implied. Virtual points do not create incentive compatibility.

**Design consequence:** never claim proper scoring makes worker confidence truthful. Use scores to evaluate and calibrate a dispatcher from resolved outcomes.

### Assumption B: reports reflect private information

**Partially transfers.** Different samples can contain different reasoning paths or retrieved evidence, but their errors may be strongly correlated through shared training, prompt, tools, or context.

The claim that production workers carry enough conditionally independent information to justify parallelism is **UNVERIFIED; PMVT's error-overlap and marginal-gain curves settle it**. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** absent workload-specific evidence.

### Assumption C: the outcome resolves objectively

**Transfers only for a subset of tasks.** Tests and externally checkable claims can resolve. Design quality, strategic advice, and long-horizon maintenance may not.

The claim that enough workload is eligible to justify MIFC is **UNVERIFIED; the “Contract Coverage Census” within PMVT settles it by dual manual labeling of a random task sample**. It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because no local corpus was inspected under this ideation-only brief.

### Assumption D: order is meaningful

**Does not naturally transfer to parallel workers.** MSR assigns credit against the prior report, while simultaneous workers have arbitrary completion order.

**Design consequence:** use randomized permutation/leave-one-out batch attribution and treat worker-level credit as analysis, not a live payment.

### Assumption E: a probability forecast is sufficient state

**Only partially transfers.** A binary success probability compresses quality, safety, maintainability, latency, and reversibility. Multi-contract utility reduces but does not erase this loss.

The claim that the proposed contract vector preserves decision-relevant value is **UNVERIFIED; PMVT's owner-review disagreement analysis settles it**. It is **likely (55-80%)** for narrow verifiable tasks and **unlikely (20-45%)** for open-ended research/design tasks, based on outcome observability rather than a local measurement.

### Assumption F: score improvements are causal worker contributions

**Does not automatically transfer.** An evaluator's probability change can be driven by presentation, order, or synthesis interactions rather than genuine task improvement.

**Design consequence:** randomize reveal order, blind the evaluator to worker identity and fan-out, compare against actual resolved outcomes, and privilege batch-level randomized effects over narrative attribution.

### Assumption G: payment units and resource costs share a scale

**Does not transfer without an owner choice.** A Brier-point improvement is not inherently worth a dollar, token, or second.

**Design consequence:** the owner must set or approve shadow prices and outcome values. Sensitivity analysis must show how the stopping policy changes across plausible weights.

## 8. Parallel Marginal Value Trial (PMVT)

PMVT is the adoption gate, not post-deployment decoration.

### 8.1 Questions

1. Does marginal value become zero or negative at different worker counts for different task strata?
2. Does MIFC predict that crossover better than fixed fan-out and consensus stopping?
3. Are the outcome contracts sufficiently resolvable and aligned with owner review?
4. Does diversity reduce error overlap, or merely create stylistic variation?
5. Are permutation-averaged marginal credits stable enough to learn from?

### 8.2 Design

- Take a prospective, representative sample of eligible tasks. Do not select only benchmark-like tasks.
- Before execution, have two independent labelers classify contract eligibility and task stratum. Validate the detector on known eligible and known ineligible controls before making absence claims.
- Randomize eligible tasks within strata to fan-outs `1, 2, 4, 8`, subject to a hard total budget. Keep worker tools and synthesis policy fixed within each comparison.
- Run initial workers blind to sibling outputs. Preserve raw artifacts.
- Blind the outcome resolver to fan-out, worker identity, and the controller's prediction.
- Record token cost, monetary cost where available, critical-path latency, tool calls, failures, and outcome contracts.
- For multi-worker tasks, compute exact subset marginals for `k<=4`; for larger `k`, compare Monte Carlo permutation estimates against a larger reference sample on a subset.
- Pre-register primary utility, non-inferiority margin, task strata, stopping-policy candidates, and exclusion rules.
- Train only on an early partition; freeze the policy; evaluate on a later temporal holdout.

### 8.3 Comparators

1. Fixed fan-out `1`.
2. Fixed fan-out `2` or the current approved default, without presuming what the local default is.
3. Maximum tested fan-out.
4. Stop on raw agreement.
5. MIFC shadow policy.
6. Oracle retrospective choice, reported only as an upper bound and never as a deployable comparator.

### 8.4 Primary success criterion

Adopt only if the frozen MIFC policy:

- improves mean net owner utility over the best fixed comparator on the temporal holdout;
- is non-inferior on raw outcome quality under a predeclared margin;
- does not worsen the lower-tail failure rate or critical task strata;
- retains calibration under fan-out-induced distribution shift;
- shows the same directional benefit in two mechanically independent signals: randomized intent-to-treat outcome effect and resolved permutation/leave-one-out marginal accounting.

A suitable numerical margin cannot be responsibly chosen without owner value weights and baseline variance. Any number inserted here would be fabricated precision.

### 8.5 Kill criteria

Stop the proposal rather than forcing the analogy if any of these occur:

- Fewer than an owner-approved fraction of tasks have independently resolvable contracts.
- Contract outcomes disagree materially with blinded owner review.
- Worker-count effects are too sparse or unstable to generalize across the temporal holdout.
- Higher fan-out improves the proxy but not owner-reviewed utility.
- Reveal-order variance is comparable to or larger than the estimated worker contribution.
- The MIFC policy does not beat the best fixed fan-out after all costs.
- The only improvement requires a new orchestration runtime, learned critic, durable service, or other rejected heavy component.

The probability that at least one kill criterion triggers is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because the most serious unknown is outcome-contract coverage for real heterogeneous tasks; the evidence is the structural audit above, not local workload inspection. That uncertainty is why shadow evaluation precedes enforcement.

### 8.6 Minimum sample and analysis

Use simulation-based power analysis from pilot variance rather than asserting a universal task count. The analysis unit is the task, not the worker output. Cluster repeated tasks by project or task family to avoid pseudo-replication. Report:

- outcome and net-utility curves by randomized fan-out;
- marginal gain from `1→2`, `2→4`, and `4→8` with uncertainty intervals;
- calibration plots and Brier decomposition by task stratum;
- pairwise error overlap and unique-error-catch rate;
- critical-path latency, not only summed worker time;
- permutation-credit variance and rank stability;
- sensitivity to owner-value and resource-price weights;
- worst-decile outcomes and catastrophic misses;
- fraction of workload ineligible because no independent resolution exists.

The trial is **very likely (80-90%)** to determine whether fixed fan-out has measurable diminishing returns on common resolvable strata, because it randomizes count directly. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to yield a deployable general controller, because rare task strata and delayed outcomes may remain data-poor.

## 9. Implementation envelope and anti-overfit gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** The minimal implementation consists of:

- a pre-spawn policy function selecting among a small approved fan-out set;
- completion hooks that append task features, batch membership, costs, and outcome identifiers to JSONL;
- an outcome-resolution append step;
- an offline scheduled script that computes score differences and refreshes a small lookup/regression artifact;
- a shadow/enforce switch and hard budget caps.

The line estimate is **UNVERIFIED; a 50-task dry-run implementation spike would settle it**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that the control path fits under approximately 200 lines if it calls existing spawn, hook, evaluator, and logging facilities; outcome adapters may add separate small modules. If it requires a service, database, queue, or second scheduler, reject the BUILD-SMALL claim and reassess rather than hiding the expansion.

This proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate by design:

- It rides the existing worker-spawn and hook bus.
- It adds no second orchestration runtime.
- It adds no vector database or knowledge graph.
- It adds no durable-execution engine.
- It trains no critic model.
- It requires no observability SaaS.
- It adds no guardrail framework.
- It can be disabled to recover the fixed policy immediately.

The controller's statistical model should remain intentionally small. A lookup table by coarse task stratum and fan-out, or regularized logistic/linear regression, is preferable until held-out evidence proves it inadequate. Replacing a fixed constant with a sprawling learned orchestrator would violate both the evidence and the anti-overfit constraint.

## 10. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Literal auction among workers — rejected

Ask each worker to bid tokens or confidence for the right to act, then select the lowest-cost/highest-value bid.

Rejected with **almost certain (90-99%)** confidence because model calls lack enforceable utility over virtual payments, so incentive compatibility has no foundation. It also lets unverifiable self-confidence directly control resource allocation.

### VCG worker allocation — rejected

Compute each worker's externality and charge it accordingly.

Rejected with **almost certain (90-99%)** confidence because costs are principal-borne, values are interdependent, and counterfactual welfare is unavailable live. VCG is useful as a reminder to measure externalities, not as an implementable theorem here.

### Peer-prediction stopping — rejected as primary

Stop when workers predict one another or reach rewarded agreement.

Rejected with **very likely (80-90%)** confidence because it can reward correlated shared errors and targets peer reports rather than task truth. It may remain a secondary disagreement feature.

### Majority vote with fixed `N` — useful comparator, not controller

This is minimal and has published support on some benchmark families ([Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120)). It remains a strong baseline. It does not price tools, latency, task topology, or marginal redundancy, and published results also include noise-induced degradation and proportional token cost.

### Stop when all agents agree — rejected

Agreement is cheap to measure but confounds easy truth with shared misconception. Rejected with **likely (55-80%)** confidence as a general policy, based on correlated-error and topology evidence; it may be adequate on narrow independently calibrated task strata.

### Train a large orchestration policy or critic — rejected

Rejected because it violates the anti-overfit gate, increases evaluation circularity, and is unsupported before the small randomized trial establishes predictable signal.

### Full prediction market among persistent agents — rejected

A real prediction market requires persistent identities, budgets, settlement, strategic utility, and protections against collusion/manipulation. Adding those merely to decide fan-out would be a BUILD-LARGE second system with no proportional justification.

## 11. Decision

Proceed only with a **shadow-mode, BUILD-SMALL marginal-information ledger and randomized PMVT**. Do not describe it as incentive-compatible. Do not use worker bids as truthful. Do not automatically govern tasks lacking externally resolvable outcomes.

If PMVT shows stable positive net utility and owner-aligned contracts, enabling the bounded stopping rule is **likely (55-80%)** to be worthwhile, supported by direct workload evidence that the trial would then provide. If contract coverage is low, reveal-order effects dominate, or the best fixed fan-out wins, the correct result is to stop: mechanism design has supplied a diagnostic accounting lens, not a production controller.

The most durable borrow is one sentence:

> **Do not reward another worker for being good; buy it only when it is expected to improve the information state enough to pay for itself, and settle that belief against an outcome the worker cohort does not control.**
