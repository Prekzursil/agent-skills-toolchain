# X141 — Cartography → reliability: prevent conclusions from outrunning probe resolution

## Unit boundary and result

This is an ideation-lens result. I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, repository, settings, or subject tree. The only target facts used are those supplied in the unit brief: agents have made at least six wrong conclusions from single unvalidated probes, and the required policy is two mechanically independent signals plus a known-present detector control before declaring something absent, broken, or done.

**Result: the analogy transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form.** The useful cartographic mechanism is **scale-bounded generalisation backed by explicit completeness and lineage metadata**. A cartographer does not treat a small-scale representation as a failed large-scale map. The representation has a declared purpose and scale; selection, omission, simplification, aggregation, and exaggeration are transformations; source scale and lineage bound what can legitimately be inferred from the output. The corresponding harness rule is: **a conclusion may not be more detailed, broader, or more categorical than its probe receipts support**.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this mechanism is structurally transferable to *claim admissibility*, because the source field and target share the same information-loss shape: a lossy observation is transformed into a compact representation, and a consumer is tempted to infer unrepresented detail. The evidence is the explicit cartographic treatment of selection/omission and source scale described below, paired with the target failure pattern supplied in the brief. This is not a claim that the proposed harness implementation is already effective.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that a literal “map of evidence,” geographic UI, or numeric map-scale metaphor would be the wrong transfer, because agent evidence scopes are heterogeneous sets and partial orders (paths, branches, processes, schemas, time windows, permissions), not a single spatial continuum with a universal representative fraction. The mechanism transfers; the surface metaphor does not.

## The specific mechanism in cartography

### 1. Generalisation is controlled information loss, not accidental incompleteness

USGS defines generalisation as reducing information and detail to fit a map and selecting the proper amount of content and its display. The agency explicitly ties the chosen content to the map scale ([USGS, “Generalization”](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/science-topics/generalization)).

Ordnance Survey's current OS Open Map – Local product guide makes the operators concrete. Its smaller-scale geometry is derived from large-scale data, and its generalisation processes include:

- **selection/omission**: features present at larger scales are not selected at smaller scales;
- **simplification**: points are filtered while retaining a feature's shape;
- **exaggeration**: an important but too-small feature is enlarged rather than omitted;
- **aggregation**: several small features become one larger feature;
- **symbolisation/collapse**: detailed features can become points.

Source: [Ordnance Survey, *OS Open Map – Local Product Guide*, “Generalisation,” p. 11](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/os-open-map-local-product-guide.pdf).

This matters because “not depicted” has several distinct meanings. The feature might not exist; it might be outside the map's feature specification; it might have been intentionally omitted at this scale; it might have been aggregated into another symbol; or it might be unknown. The map alone cannot collapse those meanings safely.

A particularly sharp real-world warning appears in the joint Ordnance Survey/Land Registry guidance. It says that OS topographic maps show only physical features within the survey-scale specification, and that absence of a dividing feature from OS mapping does not mean information is missing or that a title plan is wrong ([GOV.UK, joint Ordnance Survey and Land Registry statement, §§2, lines corresponding to paragraphs 120–129](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/joint-statement-ordnance-survey-and-land-registry/joint-statement-ordnance-survey-and-land-registry)). This is the cartographic version of “zero results is not proof of absence.”

### 2. Source scale limits downstream inference

USGS describes the traditional warning against enlarging a small-scale source for large-scale work. Progressive generalisation omits detail as scale decreases; enlarging the rendering does not restore the source detail or confer finer positional authority. USGS states that the problem is implying accuracy that could only have come from original compilation at the larger scale ([USGS, Collins, “Mining Information from Published Geologic Maps,” especially “Blowing Up Maps”](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1997/of97-269/collins.html)).

This separates two concepts that interfaces often blur:

1. **display precision** — how large, polished, or numerically exact the representation looks; and
2. **source resolution/accuracy** — the detail and truth the observations can support.

Zooming a map changes the first, not the second. Likewise, asking an agent to give a categorical summary, adding prose, or formatting a count to several digits does not expand what a partial probe observed.

### 3. Quality is attached to sources and transformations, not asserted globally

USGS says modern US Topo maps combine varied sources, so a single simple accuracy statement for the whole map is not possible; accuracy information is instead supplied for individual sources in attached metadata ([USGS, “How accurate are US Topo maps?”](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-accurate-are-us-topo-maps-and-why-dont-they-have-accuracy-statement)). This avoids laundering heterogeneous source quality into one global confidence label.

The Federal Geographic Data Committee's metadata model operationalises the idea. Its data-quality record separates:

- attribute accuracy and the test that yielded it;
- logical consistency and the tests used;
- a **completeness report containing omissions, selection criteria, generalisation, definitions, and derivation rules**;
- positional accuracy and its test;
- lineage, including sources, source scale, source contribution, process steps, parameters, and tolerances.

Source: [FGDC, Content Standard for Digital Geospatial Metadata, §2 Data Quality Information](https://www.fgdc.gov/metadata/csdgm/02.html).

FGDC guidance further says that a logical-consistency report must name tests and results; a valid-values test can detect gross blunders but does not establish every aspect of logical consistency ([FGDC/SDTS logical consistency guidance](https://www.fgdc.gov/csdgmgraphical/dataq/logconfq.htm)). Its metadata teaching guide defines completeness reporting as identifying normally expected omitted data and the reason for exclusion, and warns that recorded precision does not establish accuracy or truth ([FGDC, *Ten Most Common Metadata Errors*, pp. 1–2](https://www.fgdc.gov/metadata/documents/top10metadataerrors.pdf)).

ISO 19157 independently supports the broader quality-reporting pattern: the standard defines components, evaluation procedures, measures, and reporting principles so producers can describe conformance and users can judge fitness for their application; it explicitly does not prescribe one universal acceptable quality threshold ([ISO 19157:2013 abstract and status](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)).

### Mechanism summary in its own field

The mechanism is a contract among four things:

| Cartographic component | What it prevents |
|---|---|
| Declared target scale and purpose | Treating every representation as suitable for every question |
| Explicit generalisation operators | Mistaking omission, aggregation, displacement, or simplification for literal ground truth |
| Source-scale and per-source quality metadata | Inferring finer or more uniform accuracy than the sources supply |
| Completeness, test, and lineage records | Treating an unexplained blank or a polished output as proof |

The central invariant is not “include everything.” It is **preserve fitness for the declared purpose while exposing the boundary beyond which the representation stops being authoritative**.

## Translation to an agent harness

### Structural mapping

| Cartography | Agent probing |
|---|---|
| Reality / surveyed landscape | Actual workspace, process state, service state, or external system |
| Survey/source dataset | Raw tool observations and command output |
| Feature specification | What the detector is capable of seeing and classifying |
| Geographic extent | Roots, repositories, hosts, branches, users, processes, and time window inspected |
| Source scale / resolution | File vs directory vs workspace; record vs page; process vs service; exact object vs sampled class |
| Generalisation operator | Filtering, truncation, sampling, grouping, counting, parsing, deduplication, summarisation |
| Omitted feature | Excluded path/type, unreturned page, ignored stderr, inaccessible object, stale state, unsupported entity |
| Map layer | One evidence source/tool family |
| Map product | Agent conclusion |
| “Do not enlarge a small-scale map” | Do not promote a partial probe into a universal claim |

The target needs a **claim-scale type check**: the scope and resolution of a conclusion must be contained within the combined scope and resolution of its validated evidence.

Examples:

- A search of one root can support “not found under this root with this pattern”; it cannot support “does not exist.”
- A parser that returns zero objects can support an absence claim only after a known-present control demonstrates that this parser invocation can detect one.
- A count grouped by tool name can support a tool-level rate; it cannot support uniform reliability for every operation or environment hidden inside that group.
- A successful write followed by reading the intended path can support “the expected bytes are present there”; an exit code alone cannot support “the artifact is done.”
- Two repetitions of the same command share detector, scope, parser, and permission failure modes. They are two samples but only one signal family.

## Recommendation — UNVERIFIED: evidence-scale receipts and a claim-admissibility gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

UNVERIFIED — the premise that a compact receipt plus gate materially reduces wrong categorical conclusions without excessive friction must be settled by the replay experiment specified under “Experiment,” not by this analogy.

Add a small receipt normaliser at the existing tool-result/hook boundary. It does not run tools, orchestrate agents, or store semantic memory. It records the epistemic “map collar” for probes that may feed absence, brokenness, or completion claims.

Minimum receipt:

```json
{
  "probe_id": "...",
  "signal_family": "filesystem-enumeration|content-search|parser|runtime-query|readback|...",
  "observed_at": "...",
  "target": {"kind": "...", "identity": "..."},
  "extent": {"included": ["..."], "excluded_or_unknown": ["..."]},
  "resolution": "object|collection|sample|aggregate",
  "selection": {"filters": ["..."], "pagination_complete": true},
  "transformations": ["truncate|parse|deduplicate|aggregate|none"],
  "exit_state": "success|partial|error|unknown",
  "known_present_control": {
    "identity": "...",
    "expected": "present",
    "observed": "present|absent|not-run"
  },
  "raw_evidence_ref": "..."
}
```

Then gate only three high-risk claim predicates: **absent**, **broken**, and **done**.

Admission rules:

1. **Detector validity:** at least one relevant probe must find a known-present control through the same detector path. The control validates the instrument; it is not evidence about the target.
2. **Mechanical independence:** at least two target signals must differ in a material failure axis, such as API field vs filesystem state, runtime query vs static configuration, content search vs enumerator, or write result vs byte-for-byte readback. Merely changing syntax or rerunning the same probe does not qualify.
3. **Extent containment:** a universal claim is rejected when any material extent is excluded or unknown. The harness offers the bounded wording: “not observed within `<extent>` using `<selection>` at `<time>`.”
4. **Resolution containment:** an aggregate cannot establish every member; a sample cannot establish a population; a summary cannot restore truncated raw output.
5. **Error/empty separation:** tool error, parse failure, pagination uncertainty, permissions uncertainty, and empty successful observation are distinct states.
6. **Lineage retention:** the final claim links to both raw receipts and identifies transformations. Heterogeneous evidence keeps per-source quality; confidence is not averaged into one undocumented global number.

The enforcement need not parse free-form epistemology. The agent can emit a tiny structured claim intent before using one of the three categorical predicates; the gate returns `admit`, `bound-the-wording`, or `seek-independent-signal`, with the unmet field. Ordinary exploration and positive observations remain unaffected.

I judge it **likely (55-80%)** that this gate would catch a meaningful share of the supplied single-probe failures, because its exact admission conditions target the documented failure class: detector control, two independent target signals, and bounded scope. This is still UNVERIFIED; replaying the measured correction candidates and live shadow-mode use would settle the rate.

I judge it **very likely (80-90%)** that a gate limited to the three categorical claim types will produce less friction than gating every assertion, because it runs only at the high-risk transition from observation to absence/broken/done and does not alter ordinary tool use. Evidence: the supplied brief identifies those declarations as requiring the special two-signal standard. This remains a forecast, to be measured by gate-trigger frequency and human override rate in the experiment.

### What it catches that a generic “double-check your work” rule misses

The mechanism catches **scale mismatch**, not merely lack of diligence:

1. **Wrong-root false absence.** The receipt exposes that the searched extent is smaller than the claimed universe. A second identical search cannot repair the mismatch.
2. **Broken detector reporting zero.** The known-present control fails, so zero target results are inadmissible even if repeated.
3. **Pagination or truncation masquerading as completeness.** `pagination_complete=false` or a truncation transformation forces bounded wording.
4. **Parser collapse.** Raw output contains candidates while a parser yields zero. Raw-source and parser signals disagree; the result is “detector uncertain,” not “absent.”
5. **Stale representation.** A static index and a live runtime query carry different observation times and signal families. The gate can prevent the older, coarser representation from overriding current state without reconciliation.
6. **Aggregate-to-member inference.** A global count of zero derived after filtering cannot prove that every subsystem is absent unless the selection and extent cover each subsystem.
7. **Exit-code completion.** A successful producer signal and independent consumer/readback signal are required before “done.”
8. **Shared-mode pseudo-independence.** `rg` twice, or the same API through two wrappers, retains the same family tag and fails the independence rule.
9. **Unexplained omission.** Ignored directories, unsupported entity classes, inaccessible pages, and filtered records must appear as exclusions/unknowns, so a blank cannot silently become a negative fact.

The distinction between a control and a corroborating signal is essential. A known-present control answers “can this detector see anything of the expected kind here?” The second independent signal answers “is the target conclusion supported through a different failure path?” Neither substitutes for the other.

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: bounded-language fallback

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

UNVERIFIED — whether the model will consistently obey a machine-readable fallback template without code enforcement must be settled by the same replay experiment, comparing template-only and gated conditions.

Configure a mandatory fallback when evidence is insufficient:

> I did not observe **X** within **extent E**, using **detector D** with **filters F**, at **time T**. This does not establish absence outside E or below D's resolution. Missing before a categorical conclusion: **known-present control / independent signal / complete pagination / readback**.

This is cartographic scale disclosure translated into language. It preserves useful partial results instead of forcing either a confident false conclusion or a content-free refusal.

I judge template-only enforcement **unlikely (20-45%)** to be sufficient on its own, because the target defect is confident inference after an unvalidated probe, while FGDC itself warns that tools and metadata can contain “garbage in” and emphasizes human review ([FGDC, *Ten Most Common Metadata Errors*, p. 1](https://www.fgdc.gov/metadata/documents/top10metadataerrors.pdf)). The settling experiment is the template-only replay arm below.

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: scale ladder for drill-down, not a second runtime

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

UNVERIFIED — whether three evidence levels are understandable and sufficient across the owner's workloads must be settled by receipt annotation during replay and shadow-mode use.

Expose three linked levels in existing logs/artifacts:

- **L0 — source:** raw tool result, error stream, query parameters, timestamp.
- **L1 — representation:** parsed/filtered/aggregated result with explicit transformations and omissions.
- **L2 — claim:** bounded conclusion plus links to qualifying receipts.

“Drill down” means following links from L2 to L1 to L0; it does not mean running another orchestrator or creating a visual map. A consumer can see whether the conclusion is derived from raw observation or from a coarse summary and can inspect the omitted regions.

I judge this **likely (55-80%)** to shorten diagnosis of disputed conclusions, because the FGDC quality model explicitly couples quality reports to tests, sources, process steps, parameters, and tolerances, while USGS places heterogeneous accuracy at the individual-source level. This is UNVERIFIED for agent workflows; the experiment should measure time-to-identify the invalid inference.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### Disanalogy 1: cartographic omission is often deliberate; probe omission is often unknown

A map producer usually has a product specification and knows that it selected or aggregated features. An agent may not know that a command skipped hidden files, an API stopped paginating, permissions hid objects, a parser rejected a variant, or a result was truncated. Therefore a self-authored `excluded_or_unknown: []` field cannot be trusted merely because it exists.

Consequence: the transfer fails if implemented as prose metadata alone. Controls and independent signals are non-negotiable because they can reveal unknown omissions. The metadata records the boundary; it does not prove the boundary is correct.

### Disanalogy 2: cartographic scale has a strong ordered metric; evidence scope usually does not

Representative fractions let cartographers order many spatial products from larger to smaller scale. Harness evidence has several independent dimensions: scope, time, entity type, parser coverage, permissions, and resolution. One probe can be broader in paths but older in time; another can be current but cover one process.

Consequence: do not invent an “evidence scale score.” Use set containment plus typed dimensions. Two receipts may be incomparable. A scalar confidence value would erase the exact limitation the mechanism is meant to preserve.

### Disanalogy 3: preserving topology is not preserving truth

Cartographic generalisation can preserve relationships and legibility while moving, aggregating, or exaggerating features. That is acceptable for the map's stated use. Agent conclusions often require literal state, exact bytes, or current service availability. A semantically tidy summary can still be factually wrong.

Consequence: generalisation principles govern what the summary may claim; they do not validate the source observation. Exact tasks still require exact readback or authoritative runtime data.

### Disanalogy 4: map purpose is established before production; agent questions evolve

Maps are commonly designed for a declared scale and purpose. Agent investigations change direction as evidence arrives. A receipt schema frozen around the first question may omit dimensions needed for a later claim.

Consequence: claim checking occurs at conclusion time against retained raw lineage, not only at probe time. New claim scope can require a new probe.

### Disanalogy 5: independent signals can disagree without either being wrong

Different tools may observe different times, namespaces, consistency levels, or definitions. A file can exist while a service has not loaded it; a configuration can declare a plugin while runtime discovery excludes it.

Consequence: “two signals” is not majority vote. Their target identity, observation time, and semantics must align. Disagreement yields a scoped state description or further investigation, not automatic rejection of one source.

### Disanalogy 6: the mechanism is asymmetric

Scale/omission controls are strongest against false universal negatives and overbroad completion claims. They do not reliably detect a false positive where a probe directly returns the wrong object, an API lies, or two nominally independent sources share an upstream dependency.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that this proposal cannot serve as a general truth-verification framework, because the mechanism constrains inference from coverage and transformations but provides no ground-truth oracle. The evidence is the disanalogy above and FGDC's explicit distinction among completeness, attribute accuracy, logical consistency, and positional accuracy: completeness metadata is not accuracy proof ([FGDC §2](https://www.fgdc.gov/metadata/csdgm/02.html)).

## Anti-overfit gate

The recommendations pass the stated anti-overfit gate:

- They ride the existing tool-result, hook, log, and claim buses.
- They add a small receipt normaliser/gate and configuration template, not a second orchestration runtime.
- They introduce no vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- Receipts can be ephemeral or attached to existing artifacts; no new semantic memory service is required.
- Only three categorical claim types are gated. The rest of agent work remains model-led.

The design deliberately avoids a general “world model.” Its narrow invariant is mechanically checkable: the claim's extent and resolution must not exceed its receipts, and special negative/completion predicates need a validated detector plus independent corroboration.

## Experiment to settle every UNVERIFIED premise

### Experiment: correction-candidate replay plus live shadow mode

1. Build a labeled sample containing the supplied six-or-more known single-probe false conclusions, an equal or larger set of valid absence/broken/done conclusions, and hard cases involving pagination, permissions, stale state, parsers, and shared upstream dependencies.
2. For each case, preserve the original tool transcript and label:
   - claim predicate;
   - target identity and intended extent;
   - actual probe extent/resolution;
   - known-present control outcome;
   - signal-family independence;
   - final correctness, adjudicated from the independently established ground truth.
3. Compare four arms on the same cases:
   - baseline instructions;
   - bounded-language CONFIG template only;
   - receipt gate only;
   - template plus receipt gate.
4. Measure:
   - false categorical conclusion recall (bad claims blocked or bounded);
   - false-block rate on correct conclusions;
   - extra tool calls and latency;
   - rate of fake independence (same upstream failure family mislabeled as two);
   - rate of incorrect “no omissions” metadata;
   - human override rate;
   - time to diagnose why a claim was disputed.
5. Stress the detector with injected faults: wrong root, hidden path, denied permissions, truncated output, incomplete page token, parser schema drift, stale cache, and two wrappers sharing one backend.
6. Run shadow mode on live work before enforcement. Emit decisions without blocking and adjudicate a stratified sample of `admit`, `bound`, and `seek` outcomes.

Pre-register acceptance thresholds based on the owner's tolerance; do not infer them from ISO 19157, which explicitly does not define universal minimum acceptable quality. A reasonable threshold is therefore not asserted here.

Evidence interpretation:

- If template-only performs comparably to the code gate with acceptable overrides, choose **CONFIG** and omit the build.
- If the receipt gate catches known faults but falsely blocks many correct claims, narrow claim predicates or improve signal-family definitions rather than adding an orchestration layer.
- If agents routinely misstate receipt coverage and injected unknown omissions pass, reject the mechanism as operationally non-transferable unless receipts can be derived from tool adapters rather than model self-report.
- If two independently derived signals still share hidden upstream dependencies often enough to defeat the gate, add explicit upstream provenance fields; do not add more votes.

I judge it **roughly even (45-55%)** that the first receipt taxonomy will need domain-specific refinements after replay, because the disanalogies show that evidence scope is multidimensional while the proposed enum is intentionally small. The replay annotations and misclassification matrix are the experiment that settles which refinements are necessary.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Always run every probe twice”

Rejected. Repetition improves sampling against transient faults but does not change the detector's blind spots, extent, parser, permissions, or upstream dependency. It fails the supplied mechanical-independence requirement and misses the cartographic lesson: two copies of the same small-scale map do not create large-scale detail.

### “Require two agents to agree”

Rejected. Agents reading the same result are not mechanically independent signals. This adds a critic/orchestration pattern without repairing source resolution and violates the minimal-architecture intent.

### “Assign one confidence percentage to every answer”

Rejected. A scalar obscures whether uncertainty comes from omitted extent, stale time, parser coverage, source accuracy, or disagreement. Cartographic quality practice separates these dimensions and may attach quality to individual sources rather than declaring one global accuracy.

### “Store all evidence in a knowledge graph or vector database”

Rejected. Storage volume is not the defect. A large memory can faithfully preserve a bad inference. This reintroduces components prohibited by the anti-overfit gate.

### “Never omit; dump all raw output into context”

Rejected. Cartography's valid insight is that omission and generalisation are necessary for usable representations. The reliability requirement is explicit, traceable omission and a ban on conclusions that exceed it, not elimination of summarisation. Raw evidence remains linked for drill-down.

### “Build a visual evidence map”

Rejected. Spatial presentation adds metaphorical machinery but no detector validation. The transferable unit is the scale/lineage contract, which is better expressed as typed receipts and claim containment.

## Final verdict

The cross-domain borrow survives adversarial scrutiny **only as an inference-boundary mechanism**:

> Treat every probe result as a scale- and purpose-limited representation. Record its extent, resolution, selection, omissions/unknowns, transformations, and lineage. Do not let the conclusion “zoom in” or “zoom out” beyond that evidence. For absent, broken, and done, require both a known-present detector control and a second mechanically independent target signal.

This does not make probes true. It prevents a common and measured route from partial observation to unjustified certainty. The build is intentionally small, rides existing buses, and has an explicit rejection condition if model-authored coverage metadata proves unreliable under injected omissions.
