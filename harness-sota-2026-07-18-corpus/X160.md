# X160 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → reliable agent conclusions

## Executive judgment

**The analogy transfers, but only narrowly.** The useful military mechanism is not “use OODA” and not “add a red-team agent.” It is a composite epistemic discipline already present in military doctrine:

1. state the intended result and decision boundary (commander’s intent);
2. keep observation distinct from orientation and return through feedback (OODA plus the information hierarchy);
3. expose the assumptions that make the current conclusion possible, construct a rival hypothesis, and predeclare observable indicators for both (Key Assumptions Check plus Indicators or Signposts of Change);
4. reserve genuinely separate Team A/Team B challenge for consequential or disputed judgments; and
5. compare intended versus actual results immediately afterward and carry the resulting lesson into the next operation (AAR and before-action use of prior AARs).

The direct harness translation is an **epistemic release gate**: an agent may not upgrade a single probe into a high-impact conclusion such as “absent,” “broken,” or “done.” It must instead produce a compact claim packet containing a positive-control result, two mechanically independent signals, the strongest rival explanation, and a calibrated confidence band. Contradiction means “unresolved,” not permission to select the preferred reading.

This transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to address the stated failure mode at the level of process design, because the military mechanisms explicitly target hidden premises, premature convergence, unexamined alternatives, and the difference between raw observations and evaluated knowledge; the cited doctrine says exactly those things. Whether a hook-plus-skill implementation reduces false conclusions in this particular harness without unacceptable latency is **UNVERIFIED; almost certain (90-99%) that an A/B replay of the measured correction cases, followed by a prospective shadow run, would settle it**, because those experiments directly measure false-release rate, catch rate, cost, and delay. This unit does not claim deployed effectiveness.

The correct borrow is deliberately small. It rides the existing rule/skill/hook bus, uses ordinary tool calls and a tiny claim record, and adds no second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It therefore passes the stated anti-overfit gate.

## What the source field has actually solved

### 1. Commander’s intent: specify purpose, key tasks, end state, and boundaries

In its own field, commander’s intent is a concise description of what the operation is for and what outcome must exist, so subordinates can adapt when orders no longer fit. Army Doctrine Publication 6-0 says a well-crafted intent conveys the operation’s **purpose, key tasks, and desired outcome**; it also says commanders cannot guide every contingency and therefore communicate boundaries for disciplined initiative. Mission orders emphasize the result to attain rather than prescribing how to attain it ([ADP 6-0, *Mission Command*, 17 May 2012, paras. 14-19, PDF pp. 13-14](https://www.moore.army.mil/mssp/security%20topics/global%20and%20regional%20security/content/pdf/adp6_0_new.pdf)).

The mechanism is not inspirational wording. It aligns decentralized action around an outcome while making constraints legible. The subordinate may vary the means, but cannot silently redefine the end state. Dialogue is part of the mechanism: ADP 6-0 directs commanders to collaborate with subordinates to ensure the intent is understood (same source, para. 15).

**Translation.** Give investigative tasks an epistemic intent, not merely an action instruction:

> Purpose: prevent an unvalidated detector failure from becoming an owner-facing conclusion.  
> Key tasks: validate the detector on a known-present control; obtain two mechanically independent signals; test the strongest rival explanation; expose disagreement.  
> End state: the claim is supported, refuted, or explicitly unresolved with traceable evidence.  
> Boundary: one probe may generate an observation or hypothesis, never “absent,” “broken,” or “done.”

This is **likely (55-80%)** to catch goal substitution such as “the command returned no rows, therefore the capability is absent,” because the release boundary makes a trustworthy inventory conclusion—not command completion—the end state. It also preserves context-sensitive tool choice instead of prescribing one detector everywhere. The range is not higher because an agent can understand the intent yet still comply cosmetically.

Commander’s intent alone is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to stop the stated error class, because it supplies purpose and bounds but no independent evidence requirement; ADP 6-0 is about decentralized execution, not epistemic corroboration. The transfer only becomes useful when paired with the mechanisms below.

### 2. OODA: observations are inputs, orientation contains assumptions, action requires feedback

In its own field, the Marine Corps’ MCDP 6 describes OODA as observation, orientation, decision, and action. Observation gathers information; orientation applies estimates, assumptions, analyses, and judgments to construct a mental picture; action includes monitoring results through feedback, beginning another cycle ([MCDP 6, *Command and Control*, pp. 2-3 to 2-4, PDF pp. 71-72](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCDP%206.pdf?ver=2019-07-18-093633-990)). This distinction matters because a sensor return is not a conclusion: it becomes meaningful only through fallible orientation.

MCDP 6 makes the point even more concretely in its information hierarchy. Raw data are unprocessed signals; processed data are understandable but not yet evaluated; knowledge is data analyzed and evaluated for **reliability, relevance, and importance**, integrated and interpreted into a situation picture ([MCDP 6, pp. 2-6 to 2-8, PDF pp. 74-76](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCDP%206.pdf?ver=2019-07-18-093633-990)). The target harness failure is structurally similar to labeling processed data “knowledge” before evaluating detector reliability.

**Translation.** Each consequential claim should show the stage transition:

```text
OBSERVE:  detector A returned zero matches
ORIENT:   hypotheses = {truly absent, detector blind, scope wrong, permissions/filtering}
DECIDE:   run positive control + detector B with a different failure lineage
ACT:      report supported / refuted / unresolved; retain evidence for feedback
FEEDBACK: did later evidence or owner correction overturn the released claim?
```

Making that transition explicit is **likely (55-80%)** to catch the silent leap from “zero matches” to “absent,” from “command failed” to “system broken,” and from “test exited zero” to “task done,” because each leap must expose its orientation assumptions before decision. The uncertainty reflects the possibility of a fluent but superficial stage recital.

The speed slogan is a dangerous part of the analogy. MCDP 6 emphasizes relative tempo, but also explicitly says that speed does not eliminate patience and that the aim is meaningful action, not merely rapid action ([MCDP 6, p. 2-5, PDF p. 73](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCDP%206.pdf?ver=2019-07-18-093633-990)). A harness that optimizes “loop speed” can amplify premature convergence. OODA is therefore a useful state model, **likely (55-80%)** to improve agent reasoning when it makes orientation and feedback explicit, but **unlikely (20-45%)** to improve reliability when implemented merely as a four-label prompt; the evidence is that the doctrine’s substantive controls lie in evaluation and feedback, not the acronym.

### 3. Red teaming: expose premises, preserve alternatives, predeclare discriminating observations

This is the strongest transfer.

In its own field, the U.S. Army TRADOC G-2/UFMCS *Red Team Handbook* defines red teaming as structured techniques used to ask better questions, challenge explicit and implicit assumptions, expose missed information, and develop unrealized alternatives ([*Red Team Handbook*, printed pp. 3-4, PDF pp. 15-17](https://home.army.mil/wood/6115/8222/0759/RedTeamHB.pdf)). Three specific tools matter:

**Key Assumptions Check.** The analyst writes the current analytic line, delineates every stated and unstated premise required for it, challenges whether each must be true and remains valid, states confidence and its basis, identifies undermining circumstances, asks whether it may have been true before but false now, and assesses how the conclusion changes if it is wrong ([*Red Team Handbook*, printed pp. 163-165, PDF pp. 176-178](https://home.army.mil/wood/6115/8222/0759/RedTeamHB.pdf)). This is almost an exact diagnosis of single-probe overclaiming: “my detector is valid over this scope” is normally an unstated premise.

**Indicators or Signposts of Change.** Analysts identify competing hypotheses or scenarios and create separate lists of expected activities, statements, or events. Each indicator must be unique, valid, observable, and collectable. The handbook says the technique is used to prevent premature convergence and recommends separate positive and negative indicator lists where an analyst must argue that something is unlikely ([*Red Team Handbook*, printed pp. 160-163, PDF pp. 173-176](https://home.army.mil/wood/6115/8222/0759/RedTeamHB.pdf)). This maps directly to declaring what evidence would appear if a capability were present and what evidence would appear if it were truly absent.

**Team A/Team B Analysis.** For an important issue with competing views, separate teams develop the best argument for each, standardize the logic, assumptions and evidence, rebut in parallel, and submit to an independent jury; the handbook says alternative views force searches for information that can confirm or disconfirm hypotheses ([*Red Team Handbook*, printed pp. 199-200, PDF pp. 212-213](https://home.army.mil/wood/6115/8222/0759/RedTeamHB.pdf)). The important element is not role-play language. It is separation before convergence and an independent verdict.

**Translation.** Convert each high-impact status claim into a miniature competing-hypothesis test:

```yaml
claim: "Capability X is absent"
release_class: absence
current_analytic_line: "Detector A returned zero results in scope S"
key_assumptions:
  - "Detector A enumerates this capability type"
  - "Scope S includes all relevant locations/states"
  - "A filter, permission boundary, parser error, or stale index did not erase results"
rival_hypothesis: "X is present; detector A is blind or mis-scoped"
positive_control:
  known_present_item: Y
  detector_result: found | not_found
signal_a: {mechanism: ..., result: ..., provenance: ...}
signal_b: {mechanism: ..., result: ..., provenance: ...}
independence_argument: "different data field + different parser/path/API"
disconfirming_check: ...
resolution: supported | refuted | unresolved
confidence: one mandatory band + evidence
```

The **positive control** is a harness-specific sharpening, not a claim that Army doctrine mandates software detector controls. The **two mechanically independent signals** are likewise a harness translation of competing hypotheses, separate teams, distinct indicators, and independent verdict—not a verbatim military rule. This distinction is important for intellectual honesty.

This would catch:

- an inventory command returning zero because its parser or glob failed;
- two commands that appear different but consume the same stale index;
- a filesystem probe that misses configuration available only through an API or origin revision;
- a “broken” conclusion based on one transient connection refusal;
- a “done” conclusion based solely on process exit status while the required artifact or behavior is absent;
- a current-state claim derived from stale cached material;
- correlated agreement, such as the same command rerun or the same model restating its first answer, falsely counted as corroboration.

The mapping is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch single-detector blindness before release, because a failed positive control directly invalidates the detector and a genuinely independent second signal removes many shared failure modes. The remaining 10-20% uncertainty reflects common-mode failures—both probes can share a wrong scope, false premise, stale upstream source, or misleading specification—and is supported by the handbook’s own caution that indicators remain subject to cognitive bias, faulty analogy, and underdeveloped theory (printed p. 163, PDF pp. 175-176, same source).

### 4. After-action review: close the learning loop, but do not pretend it prevents the first error

In its own field, the Army’s learning-organization guidance describes an AAR as an immediate internal discussion of what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, strengths to sustain, and weaknesses to improve. Its stated objective is improved individual and collective performance through immediate feedback. It also describes a “before action report” that brings prior AARs and relevant products into planning for a similar future mission ([Center for Army Lessons Learned, *Commander’s Guide for Driving Change: The Learning Organization Framework*, pp. 2-3](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2023/01/31/99194b3d/commanders-guide-for-driving-change-the-learning-organization-framework-jan-19-public.pdf)).

**Translation.** When a conclusion is overturned by later evidence or an owner correction, automatically record a tiny epistemic AAR:

```text
intended: trustworthy claim under the release rule
actual: claim overturned / detector invalid / scope incomplete
why: named assumption and failure lineage
sustain: useful probe or control
change: new positive control, independence rule, or claim-class example
next-use cue: inject this lesson when the same claim class/tool fingerprint recurs
```

This catches recurrence, not the original event. AAR is **very likely (80-90%)** to be useful as a learning format because it explicitly compares intended and actual performance and routes lessons forward; it is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to prevent a first-time false conclusion by itself because it is retrospective. The before-action step is what turns retrospective learning into prospective protection.

## Where the analogy transfers—and where it breaks

### Structural correspondences

| Military structure | Harness structure | Transfer quality |
|---|---|---|
| Sensors produce observations that require orientation | Tools produce outputs that require interpretation | Strong: both can fail, be incomplete, or be mis-scoped before a decision. |
| Hidden assumptions can collapse an analytic line | Detector validity and scope are often unstated premises | Strong: Key Assumptions Check directly externalizes them. |
| Competing hypotheses need discriminating indicators | “Absent” competes with “detector blind/mis-scoped” | Strong: positive/negative observable evidence can distinguish them. |
| Separate teams and independent verdict reduce premature convergence | Mechanically distinct probes reduce correlated tool failure | Partial: tool independence is mechanical; human/team independence is social and cognitive. |
| Commander’s intent permits adaptation within bounds | The agent chooses probes within an evidence-release rule | Strong: outcome and boundary transfer without prescribing every command. |
| AAR compares intended and actual execution | Correction analysis compares released and later-known truth | Strong retrospectively; weak for first occurrence. |
| Adversary actively deceives and reacts | Most software state is not strategic and does not react | Weak/disanalogous: adversarial tempo and deception should not be imported by default. |

### The decisive disanalogies

1. **A language model is not an independent red team against itself.** A second prompt in the same context shares the first answer’s framing, evidence selection, and model priors. Treating “critic says yes” as an independent signal would reproduce the defect under ceremonial language. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that same-context self-critique is correlated with the original reasoning; the evidence is mechanical identity of model, context, and inputs. The exact error correlation is **UNVERIFIED; replay the known false conclusions with blinded fresh-context critique versus same-context critique to settle it**.

2. **Military red teaming is expensive and selective.** Team A/Team B is expressly for important issues where the community will commit significant time and resources (Handbook, printed p. 199). Running multiple agents and a jury on every filesystem fact would violate proportionality and the anti-overfit gate. This borrow therefore uses two cheap, different probes by default and escalates to a separate challenger only for consequential contradiction.

3. **Tempo is not the target objective.** In combat, relative decision tempo matters. In an owner-facing investigation, a fast false conclusion can be worse than a slower correct one. The useful OODA transfer is feedback and separation of observation from orientation, not “get inside the enemy’s loop.”

4. **There may be no adversary.** Missing files, stale caches, parser defects, and scope mistakes need no intelligent opponent. “Red team” should be understood as alternative-hypothesis analysis, not as a permanent adversarial persona or arms race.

5. **Two signals are not automatically independent.** `find` and `grep` over the same generated list share the upstream list; two API wrappers may share the same service; two agents may quote the same cached page. Independence must be argued by failure lineage: different source, parser, scope path, or measurement principle. This is the most important engineering refinement beyond the metaphor.

6. **Doctrine is a design source, not efficacy evidence for agent harnesses.** The official documents establish how mechanisms work in their own field. They do not establish effect size in autonomous coding agents. All implementation benefits below remain experimental.

## Concrete harness design

### Recommendation 1 — UNVERIFIED: Claim Release Contract

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add a short, high-priority rule defining which language requires corroboration and what qualifies. Do not demand a ceremony for every factual statement. Trigger only on high-impact epistemic claim classes:

- absence/nonexistence: “no,” “none,” “missing,” “not installed,” “never fired”;
- malfunction: “broken,” “dead,” “unavailable,” “unsupported”;
- completion: “done,” “fixed,” “verified,” “all tests pass”;
- exhaustive/superlative: “only,” “all,” “root cause,” “cannot”;
- time-sensitive state where stale data is plausible.

The contract:

1. A single probe is an observation, not a conclusion.
2. Before an absence/broken/done conclusion, prove the detector on a known-present control.
3. Require two mechanically independent signals. Rerunning or rephrasing the same probe does not count.
4. State the strongest alternative explanation and run one check that could favor it.
5. If signals conflict, report `UNRESOLVED` and the next discriminating experiment.
6. Attach exactly one mandated confidence band and its evidence to the released claim.

Expected benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, because a rule alone can be skipped or satisfied cosmetically even though its logic directly targets the defect. This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; replay the measured correction candidates with the rule enabled and score compliance plus false-release rate to settle it**.

### Recommendation 2 — UNVERIFIED: Epistemic Release Hook

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a small stop/pre-response hook that checks for a compact machine-readable claim marker whenever the final response uses a high-impact claim. Claude Code natively supports `Stop` hooks and command-based hook handlers, including a blocking decision that sends a reason back to the agent; its documentation also warns about recursive stop-hook loops ([Claude Code, “Hooks reference”](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)). The hook should validate fields, not decide truth:

```text
EPISTEMIC-CLAIM
class: absence|broken|done|exhaustive|current
positive_control: <evidence ref>
signal_a: <evidence ref + mechanism>
signal_b: <evidence ref + mechanism>
independence: <different failure lineage>
rival: <alternative hypothesis>
resolution: supported|refuted|unresolved
confidence: <allowed band + evidence>
```

If required fields are missing, return a focused correction instructing the agent to either gather evidence or weaken the wording to an observation/hypothesis. Cap correction loops at one; after that, require explicit `UNRESOLVED` rather than creating the retry pathology this harness already measures elsewhere.

This hook is **likely (55-80%)** to improve compliance over prompt-only policy because structural fields are mechanically checkable. Semantic truthfulness and genuine independence remain **UNVERIFIED; mutate claim packets with fake controls, shared-upstream probes, and contradictory signals, then measure whether the hook plus model rejects them**. A regex-only hook is **unlikely (20-45%)** to identify every natural-language claim reliably, because claim classification and evidence semantics are not purely syntactic; evaluate precision/recall on held-out transcripts before blocking mode.

Rollout should be shadow → warn → block for the three clearest classes (absence, broken, done). That sequence is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal false positives before they disrupt work, because shadow mode logs decisions without changing agent behavior; the optimal threshold is **UNVERIFIED; the prospective shadow experiment settles it**.

### Recommendation 3 — UNVERIFIED: Probe Independence Matrix

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Supply a small declarative table used by the skill/rule to reject pseudo-corroboration. Independence is a property of failure lineage, not command spelling.

Examples:

| Signal A | Signal B | Count as independent? | Reason |
|---|---|---:|---|
| same command rerun | same command rerun | No | identical detector and source |
| `grep` generated manifest | parser of same generated manifest | Usually no | same upstream artifact |
| filesystem enumeration | application/API enumeration | Often yes | different interface and parser; may still share backing state |
| config parse | observed runtime behavior | Often yes | declared state versus behavioral measurement |
| process exit code | required output artifact/content check | Yes for “done” | execution status versus end-state observation |
| cached web result | direct current primary source | Often yes | different freshness/source path |
| same model self-critique | same model original answer | No | shared model, context, and framing |

The table must allow the agent to explain a novel pairing rather than becoming a universal ontology. A small matrix is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent obvious correlated duplicates; exhaustive classification is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to remain correct as tools change, because shared dependencies are contextual. The recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; inject paired-probe examples with known shared and distinct failure modes and score classification accuracy**.

### Recommendation 4 — UNVERIFIED: Escalation ladder, not permanent red-team runtime

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it).** Use existing fresh-context/subagent capability only when one of these conditions holds: signals contradict; the claim is consequential and difficult to reverse; the detector lacks a valid positive control; or the conclusion is an asserted root cause/exhaustive absence. Claude Code’s official documentation states that each subagent starts with a fresh, isolated context window and does not see the parent conversation history unless the delegation supplies it ([Claude Code, “Create custom subagents,” section “What loads at startup”](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)). Give the challenger the claim, raw evidence, and rival hypothesis, but not the first agent’s narrative. Ask it to identify shared failure lineages and propose one discriminating probe. The primary agent still owns the final judgment.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to add value on escalated cases because it introduces some framing separation and targets the exact disagreement, while the Army Team A/Team B mechanism supports separation and independent judgment. It remains **UNVERIFIED; compare blinded fresh-context challenge, same-context self-critique, and no challenge on known false conclusions**. It is not a second orchestration runtime and must not become one.

### Recommendation 5 — UNVERIFIED: Micro-AAR and before-action cue

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** On owner correction or later contradiction, capture only:

- claim class and original wording;
- failed assumption;
- detector/tool fingerprint;
- why the second signal was absent or correlated;
- one future discriminating check.

At the start of a later matching claim class/tool fingerprint, inject the one relevant lesson as a before-action cue. Store it in existing lightweight harness state or a flat append-only artifact; do not introduce vector memory or a knowledge graph.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce exact and near-exact recurrence because the cue is keyed to the detector and claim class, consistent with the Army’s before-action use of prior AARs. Generalization beyond close recurrence is **UNVERIFIED; conduct a temporal evaluation in which earlier corrections generate cues and later corrections form the test set**.

## Release-state machine

The smallest useful control can be expressed without a new runtime:

```text
single probe
    |
    v
OBSERVATION ONLY
    |
    +-- positive control fails -----------------> DETECTOR INVALID -> UNRESOLVED
    |
    +-- positive control passes
            |
            v
      independent probe + rival check
            |
            +-- signals agree -----------------> SUPPORTED/REFUTED + confidence
            |
            +-- signals conflict --------------> UNRESOLVED + discriminating probe
            |
            +-- high consequence/root cause ---> fresh-context challenge
```

The critical invariant is that “unresolved” is a successful reliability outcome, not a task failure. It prevents uncertainty from being laundered into confidence.

## What this design catches that a normal retry misses

1. **Broken detector:** zero-result probe fails to find the known-present control; the claim never leaves observation state.
2. **Wrong scope:** the control passes locally but an API or alternate scope reveals items elsewhere; the signals conflict.
3. **Shared upstream defect:** two surface commands read the same corrupt index; the independence argument fails even though outputs agree.
4. **Transient failure mislabeled as permanent:** a connection error is paired with service configuration/status or a time-separated behavioral check; “dead” is withheld.
5. **False completion:** exit code succeeds but the output artifact, behavioral assertion, or requested end state fails.
6. **Stale truth:** cached observation conflicts with a current primary source or runtime behavior.
7. **Premature root cause:** the agent must name a rival causal explanation and obtain a discriminating signal; mere correlation cannot be released as “the root cause.”
8. **Ceremonial self-review:** a same-context “critic” is rejected as non-independent.

No mechanism here guarantees truth. Two probes can share a hidden dependency, a known-present control can be unrepresentative, and an agent can fabricate or mischaracterize evidence. The defensible claim is narrower: the gate makes single-probe conclusions structurally noncompliant and makes the provenance of corroboration inspectable.

## Evaluation plan and falsification criteria

### Phase 1: offline replay

Build a labeled set from the measured wrong-conclusion/correction cases plus matched correct conclusions. For each case, freeze the evidence available at the original decision point. Run baseline and proposed contract/hook conditions. Score:

- false-release rate: wrong high-impact conclusions released as supported;
- safe-deferral rate: wrong conclusions changed to unresolved;
- true-release retention: correct conclusions still released;
- positive-control catch rate;
- pseudo-independence rejection rate;
- added tool calls, tokens, and wall time;
- correction-loop count.

The design earns promotion only if false release falls materially without turning most correct low-risk claims into unresolved. “Materially” must be set before the experiment; a reasonable starting decision rule is **UNVERIFIED** and should be chosen by the owner based on the cost ratio of false confidence to delay.

### Phase 2: detector mutation tests

Seed controlled failures:

- detector always returns zero;
- scope excludes one known location;
- two different commands share a poisoned intermediate file;
- stale cache disagrees with live state;
- exit zero without required output;
- transient network refusal;
- positive control is intentionally unrepresentative.

The gate should reject or defer each affected conclusion. This phase tests mechanism, not model eloquence.

### Phase 3: prospective shadow mode

Log candidate claim packets and hook decisions without blocking. Human-review a stratified sample of releases and warnings. Estimate precision/recall by claim class and overhead. Only then enable warnings, followed by blocking for absence/broken/done if the measured tradeoff is acceptable.

### Falsification conditions

Reject or redesign this borrow if any of the following occurs:

- most “independent” probes share unmodelled dependencies, so the matrix provides false reassurance;
- agents satisfy fields with rhetorical restatements rather than evidence and mutation tests do not detect it;
- false-release reduction is roughly even with baseline (45-55%) while latency or tool errors rise materially;
- the hook creates repeated correction loops;
- owner corrections show that wrong scope/intent, not detector validation, dominates the target failures;
- a simpler fixed instruction (“never infer absence from one probe”) performs equally well in held-out replay.

That last comparison is essential to the anti-overfit gate. The composite design is justified only if controls, rival hypotheses, and independence checks add measured value beyond the one-line rule.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Add OODA to the system prompt”

Rejected as a standalone mechanism. It names stages but does not enforce detector validation, independent evidence, or rival hypotheses. It may even privilege speed. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** if used as explanatory vocabulary, but not counted as a proposal because it lacks sufficient control value.

### “Run a red-team agent on every answer”

Rejected. Same-model critics are correlated, universal challenge is costly, and a permanent multi-agent review layer approaches a second orchestration runtime. It violates proportionality and risks overfitting. A fresh-context challenger is reserved for escalation only.

### “Require two tool calls”

Rejected. Two calls can share the same command, data source, parser, cache, or wrong scope. The requirement must be two mechanically independent signals plus an explicit failure-lineage argument.

### “Store every claim in a vector database/knowledge graph”

Rejected explicitly under the anti-overfit gate. The target mechanism needs only an inline claim packet and a small keyed correction record. There is no demonstrated need for semantic retrieval infrastructure.

### “AAR everything”

Rejected. AAR has value after surprise, correction, contradiction, or sampled high-impact work. Reviewing every trivial result adds ceremony and does not prevent first occurrence.

### “Block whenever confidence is below a threshold”

Rejected. Model confidence is not detector validity. The Army handbook’s assumption-sensitivity technique quantifies confidence, but confidence without discriminating observations can be confidently wrong. Evidence structure must lead; confidence communicates residual uncertainty.

## Final synthesis

Military doctrine has not solved software-agent truth verification, and it does not supply a magical “two probes” rule. What it has solved is how to keep distributed actors aligned under uncertainty, how to distinguish observation from evaluated knowledge, how to expose hidden premises and competing explanations before convergence, how to employ independent challenge proportionally, and how to turn surprises into future preparation.

The transfer is valid because the target failure has the same local structure: a noisy or blind observation is oriented through hidden assumptions and released as a decision without feedback or challenge. The transfer stops being valid when combat competition, human organizational independence, or adversarial intent is projected onto ordinary tool failures.

Therefore the ambitious but minimal design is:

1. a CONFIG claim-release contract;
2. a BUILD-SMALL structural hook with one-loop cap;
3. a BUILD-SMALL independence matrix;
4. NATIVE fresh-context escalation only for contradiction/high consequence; and
5. a BUILD-SMALL micro-AAR/before-action cue for recurrence.

This package is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform a prompt-only warning, based on the combination of mechanically checkable release fields, positive controls, independent failure lineages, and feedback. That comparative claim is **UNVERIFIED; only the baseline-versus-contract-versus-hook replay and prospective shadow evaluation described above can settle it**. Until then, the correct status is a well-grounded design hypothesis, not a completed reliability improvement.
