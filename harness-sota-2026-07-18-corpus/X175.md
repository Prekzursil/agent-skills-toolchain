# X175 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → finite-horizon context

**Unit:** X175  
**Lens:** experimental-design mechanisms (preregistration, power analysis, blinding, negative controls) applied to long-horizon context inclusion and eviction  
**Scope discipline:** This is an ideation unit. I did not inspect or characterize the local harness, corpus, audit findings, or subject tree. The owner explicitly assigned local grounding to other units. Consequently, “would catch” below means a failure class the proposed mechanism is designed to expose; it is **not** a claim that the current harness lacks every named check.

## Executive judgment

The analogy **partly transfers**, and the useful part is narrower than “treat context like an experiment.” The transferable structure is **decision hygiene under selection pressure**:

1. specify what success and failure mean before seeing the answer;
2. allocate scarce capacity against a declared minimum consequential effect rather than filling all available capacity;
3. hide outcome- or hypothesis-revealing information from the person/model making a discretionary selection;
4. include inputs that are known *not* to have the claimed causal effect but share the same bias channels, so unexplained sensitivity becomes observable; and
5. log deviations rather than pretending an adaptive process followed a fixed plan.

This transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve the *auditability* of context policy because preregistration and controls create machine-checkable predictions about retention, eviction, and answer invariance; the evidence is the source-field separation of prespecified from post hoc analysis and the explicit use of negative controls to detect suspected and unsuspected bias ([Nosek et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/); [Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). It is only **likely (55-80%)** to improve task success, because context selection is adaptive, observations are correlated, and “relevance” changes as an agent acts; the evidence for caution is the documented non-uniform use of long context and the greater complexity of multi-hop long-context evaluation ([Liu et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/); [Hsieh et al., RULER, 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=kIoBbc76Sy)).

The classical statistical pieces do **not** transfer literally. Tokens are not independent experimental units; there is no stable analytic variance term for an arbitrary task; context can change the policy that generates later context; and a “control” passage can alter an LLM merely by consuming tokens or shifting positions. Therefore this unit recommends a **replay-calibrated Context Trial Protocol**, not clinical-trial mathematics pasted onto prompts.

## 1. What experimental design has actually solved

### 1.1 Preregistration and Registered Reports: bind the decision rule before outcomes are known

In its own field, preregistration creates a time-stamped record of hypotheses, methods, outcomes, exclusions, stopping rules, and analyses before data are observed. Its central epistemic function is not to ban exploration. It makes the distinction between confirmatory tests and analyses invented after observing the data visible. Nosek et al. describe preregistration as a means to distinguish prediction from postdiction and to reduce hindsight bias in interpreting flexible analyses ([Nosek et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/)).

Registered Reports strengthen this temporal separation: the research question and method are reviewed before results exist; publication is accepted in principle based on question and rigor rather than whether the result is exciting; Stage 2 checks adherence and interpretation. The original Cortex initiative is identified by Chambers ([Chambers, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347556/)), and a practical account explains the two-stage, results-agnostic decision structure ([Nosek and Lakens et al., “Ten simple rules,” 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9612468/)). CONSORT similarly emphasizes access to the protocol and reporting deviations, because trial reports can diverge from what was planned ([CONSORT 2010 explanation](https://www.bmj.com/content/340/bmj.c869)).

The solved problem is **researcher degrees of freedom after outcome observation**: if the analyst can redefine the outcome, exclusion set, or analysis after seeing which choice produces a favorable story, nominal evidence no longer means what it appears to mean. Preregistration does not guarantee a sound study and does not make deviations wrong. It exposes the timing and identity of deviations so readers can interpret them correctly.

#### Translation to context

An agent has an analogous degree-of-freedom problem. Once it forms a tentative answer, plan, diagnosis, or patch, it can preferentially preserve evidence supporting that commitment, summarize away contradictory evidence, and retroactively call the surviving material “relevant.” Context management then becomes an invisible form of outcome-conditioned selection.

The concrete transfer is a **Context Retention Protocol** declared at task start and versioned at phase changes:

- primary task outcome and explicit failure conditions;
- classes that must remain verbatim (owner constraints, destructive-action boundaries, exact identifiers, unresolved errors, contradictory evidence, acceptance criteria);
- classes eligible for lossy summary and classes eligible for eviction;
- per-class minimum evidence requirements and expiry conditions;
- a stopping/replanning rule;
- the answer-independent scoring rubric used for discretionary eviction;
- planned checkpoint probes; and
- a deviation channel recording what rule changed, when, why, and whether the change occurred after seeing a result.

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED: versioned Context Retention Protocol. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** The premise that outcome-conditioned context selection is frequent enough in the owner’s workload to justify the extra protocol is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E1 below: label 200 context-compaction events for whether a tentative conclusion preceded asymmetric evidence loss, then compare against a frozen-policy arm**. The protocol is **likely (55-80%)** to catch retrospective relabeling of inconvenient evidence because it creates a before/after policy diff; this confidence rests on preregistration’s temporal-separation mechanism, not on a local frequency measurement.

What it catches that a relevance-only selector misses:

- a contradiction removed only after the agent committed to a diagnosis;
- an acceptance criterion silently narrowed after a test failed;
- a failed approach summarized as “resolved” without its unresolved condition;
- repeated extensions of a task’s stopping rule;
- exploratory context-policy changes presented as if they were the original policy.

Important adaptation: protocols must be **phase-local**, not frozen for the whole task. A long agent run legitimately changes from reconnaissance to implementation to verification. Each phase transition opens a new preregistration entry while preserving the previous entry and deviation log. This adaptation is **very likely (80-90%)** necessary because the target process is sequential and intervention-generating, unlike a fixed one-shot analysis; the evidence is the causal disanalogy itself—agent actions change the state that future context describes.

### 1.2 Power analysis: design capacity around the smallest effect worth detecting

In its own field, power is the probability that a statistical test rejects a false null hypothesis under a specified alternative. Sample-size planning connects the type-I error threshold, desired power, outcome variability, design, and minimum effect of interest. An underpowered experiment can miss a meaningful effect; indiscriminately increasing sample size also spends resources and can make trivial effects detectable. ICH E9 treats sample size as a design choice tied to the trial objective, primary variable, test, alternative, type-I error, and power ([ICH E9 guideline](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E9_Guideline.pdf)); a worked tutorial states the effect-size/variance/sample-size relationship and the role of a chosen power level ([Anderson et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425741/)).

The key mechanism is not “more samples are better.” It is **prospective sensitivity calibration**: name the smallest consequence the design must reliably detect, then provision resources and choose a design capable of detecting it.

#### Translation to context

For context, the analogue of an effect is not token count. It is a **minimum consequential dependency**: the smallest/oldest/least-salient fact whose correct use can change the action, answer, safety decision, or verification verdict. The analogue of power is the empirical probability that the full harness preserves *and uses* such a dependency at a given token budget, position, age, distractor load, and dependency count.

An analytic sample-size formula should not be used. Instead, construct held-out task replays with known consequential dependencies and measure a **retention-power curve**:

`P(correctly uses dependency | token budget, age, position, distractors, hops, policy)`

Select the smallest context budget/policy meeting a preregistered floor for the minimum consequential dependency, with separate strata for exact constraints, contradictions, file identifiers, error evidence, and ordinary background. RULER is relevant methodological precedent because it varies sequence length and task complexity rather than treating advertised maximum length as effective capacity ([Hsieh et al., 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=kIoBbc76Sy)). “Lost in the Middle” demonstrates that information use depends on position, so a budget calibration that ignores placement is misspecified ([Liu et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)).

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED: replay-based retention-power calibration. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** The premise that a stable enough replay distribution can represent future owner tasks is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E2 below: fit thresholds on one chronological slice and test calibration and task-loss on a later untouched slice, stratified by task family**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that stratified replay curves will find token budgets at which “fits in the window” materially overstates “is usable”; the evidence is position-sensitive degradation in Liu et al. and RULER’s finding that real context capability requires more than a single needle task. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that one global budget threshold will remain calibrated across task families; the evidence is that task complexity, number of dependencies, and placement are experimental factors in long-context benchmarks.

What it catches:

- a policy that passes simple single-fact recall but fails multi-fact aggregation;
- sufficient total tokens but insufficient protected capacity for exact constraints;
- middle-position loss hidden by beginning/end-only probes;
- compression that preserves topic similarity while erasing action-changing qualifiers;
- a “leaner” context whose savings come from unacceptable false negatives.

### 1.3 Blinding and allocation concealment: remove information that enables biased discretion

In randomized trials, allocation concealment prevents people enrolling participants from predicting or manipulating the next assignment. Blinding after assignment hides treatment identity from participants, providers, data collectors, or outcome assessors to reduce differences in treatment and assessment. These are distinct mechanisms. CONSORT requires reporting who was blinded and how; it also calls for the actual allocation-concealment mechanism rather than a vague assurance ([CONSORT 2025 explanation](https://www.bmj.com/content/389/bmj-2024-081124)). Empirical meta-epidemiology has found larger bias particularly for subjective outcomes when design protections are inadequate or unclear ([Savović et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22989478/)); an updated within-trial comparison found non-blinded assessors exaggerated subjective effects on average, while also documenting heterogeneity and limitations ([Moustgaard et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40258524/)).

The solved problem is not generic secrecy. It is **selective information partitioning at the moment discretion is exercised**.

#### Translation to context

A context-eviction judge needs the task, candidate content, dependency links, and safety class. It does *not* need the agent’s drafted conclusion, confidence rhetoric, or whether a candidate supports the favored hypothesis. Give the judge a masked candidate bundle:

- semantic content and exact quoted constraints;
- task/phase objective;
- dependency references;
- source reliability class where provenance is substantively necessary;
- but no drafted answer, “supports/refutes my plan” label, or downstream preferred action.

Run safety and owner-instruction retention as deterministic protected classes outside this blind judge. For discretionary items, reveal masked fields only after scoring, then log disagreements. This is closer to blinded outcome assessment than to double blinding.

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED: answer-blind discretionary eviction scoring. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Whether masking the tentative answer reduces motivated eviction without removing essential causal context is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E3 below: paired, randomized replay using masked versus unmasked scoring, with human-labeled contradiction retention and downstream task success**. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce confirmation-conditioned eviction when the candidate’s relevance can be judged from the task specification; evidence comes from the source-field mechanism and empirical sensitivity of subjective assessment to blinding. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to help when relevance depends on the exact planned action, because hiding that action removes legitimate information as well as biasing information; this confidence follows from the structural requirement that context value is policy-dependent.

What it catches:

- contradictory evidence down-ranked because it threatens a drafted answer;
- evidence supporting the current plan receiving an unexplained score boost;
- provenance prestige or speaker role dominating content where it is not relevant;
- phase recency being mistaken for importance.

It does **not** justify masking owner authority, safety provenance, chronology needed for state freshness, or dependency edges. Those are part of the estimand, not nuisances.

### 1.4 Negative controls: make hidden bias produce an observable signal

In laboratory science, a negative control is designed not to produce the effect of interest while sharing relevant parts of the experimental procedure. If it produces the effect, contamination, measurement error, confounding, or an analytic flaw becomes plausible. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen formalized negative-control exposures and outcomes for epidemiology: a negative-control exposure should not cause the outcome but should share confounding structure; a negative-control outcome should not be caused by the exposure but should share causes or measurement processes with the real outcome ([Lipsitch et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). Later work shows how prespecified controls can expose selection and measurement bias, while emphasizing that validity depends on the control sharing the bias pathway and lacking the causal pathway of interest ([Arnold et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4969055/)).

The important mechanism is a **structural null**, not merely random junk. A bad negative control that does not share the suspected bias channel can remain null even when the study is biased; one that actually affects the outcome yields a false alarm.

#### Translation to context

Use two paired control types at checkpoints:

1. **Negative-control context item (exposure analogue).** Insert a plausible, task-shaped passage known not to alter the correct action, but matched to the real evidence on length, lexical overlap, source format, age, and salience. It should be evicted or have no answer effect. If it survives preferentially or changes the answer, the selector/model is responding to a nuisance feature rather than causal relevance.
2. **Negative-control probe (outcome analogue).** Ask an invariant question whose answer depends on protected context but not on the candidate item under test, and compare with/without that item. A change indicates cross-talk, positional displacement, instruction leakage, or a broken invariance assumption.

Also retain **positive controls**—known consequential items and probes that must change the correct output—because a negative-control detector that never fires may simply be insensitive. This directly implements the owner’s requirement that an absence detector first demonstrate detection of a known-present condition. The positive control is not a negative-control concept, but it is essential instrumentation.

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED: matched context controls and checkpoint canaries. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** The premise that controls can be generated which are matched on nuisance features yet genuinely null is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E4 below: independent human adjudication of causal nullness plus randomized insertion tests across held-out tasks**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that naive “random irrelevant text” controls would miss important selector biases because they do not share lexical, structural, or provenance features; the evidence is the formal requirement that negative controls share the bias-generating structure. It is **likely (55-80%)** that carefully matched controls will expose lexical-overlap selection, salience bias, and position-induced cross-talk; the evidence is the negative-control mechanism plus documented position sensitivity in long-context models. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that online synthetic controls will be reliably null without human curation; the evidence is the target-domain difficulty that any natural-language passage may prime generation or consume positional capacity.

What it catches:

- a selector retaining “sounds relevant” passages because of shared vocabulary;
- a summary changing an unrelated protected fact;
- a candidate affecting the answer solely by shifting another item into a weak position;
- an injected instruction-like passage overriding policy despite being substantively irrelevant;
- a silent detector that passes everything, revealed by failure on the positive control.

## 2. The proposed combined mechanism: Context Trial Protocol

The four mechanisms fit together as one small protocol rather than four services:

1. **Stage 0 — context estimand.** State the task phase, target decision, minimum consequential dependency classes, protected loss limits, and evaluation probes.
2. **Stage 1 — precommit.** Hash/version the retention rubric, eviction order, compaction trigger, and stopping/replanning rule before generating a candidate answer.
3. **Capacity calibration.** Choose a policy/budget from held-out retention-power curves, not from advertised context length or “fill until full.”
4. **Blinded selection.** Apply deterministic protected-class rules; score only discretionary candidates with tentative conclusions and support/refute labels masked.
5. **Controls.** At sampled checkpoints, run a matched negative-control item, an invariant probe, and a positive-control probe.
6. **Stage 2 — deviation and outcome audit.** Reveal masked fields, record deviations, and score the final run against preregistered primary probes. Exploratory policy changes remain allowed but labeled.

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED: Context Trial Protocol as a hook-bus module. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** A feasible minimal form is a versioned JSON/JSONL protocol record plus a pre-compaction checker and an offline replay scorer; exact line count and hook availability are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with Experiment E0: implement a throwaway adapter against the actual supported hook events and count executable non-test lines without modifying production configuration**. It is **likely (55-80%)** that the online portion can remain under roughly 200 lines if replay generation and human labeling stay outside the runtime; evidence is the deliberately narrow responsibilities—schema validation, masking, probe dispatch, and append-only deviation logging. If the actual bus cannot expose pre-compaction candidates or checkpoint callbacks, that implementation estimate falls to **unlikely (20-45%)**; the settling evidence is E0.

### Minimal record shape

```json
{
  "phase": "implementation",
  "primary_outcome": "passes declared acceptance tests without violating owner constraints",

```

## 3. What transfers, what fails, and why

| Source-field assumption | Context analogue | Transfer verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Plans made before outcomes reduce outcome-conditioned flexibility | Retention rubric made before a drafted answer exposes conclusion-conditioned eviction | **Strong structural transfer.** Very likely (80-90%) useful for auditability, based on shared temporal ordering and discretionary selection. |
| Sample size can be calculated from a specified model, variance, alpha, power, and effect | Context budget can be chosen from token count alone | **Does not transfer.** Almost certainly not (1-5%) valid as a literal analytic formula because tokens are dependent, heterogeneous, positioned, and policy-changing. |
| Prospective sensitivity can be empirically calibrated | Held-out replays can estimate use of consequential dependencies under controlled loads | **Partial transfer.** Likely (55-80%) useful within task strata; evidence is long-context performance varying with length, position, and complexity. |
| Treatment identity is irrelevant to unbiased outcome assessment | Drafted conclusion/support label is irrelevant to judging every item’s value | **Partial transfer only.** Some fields are biasing; planned action can also be causally necessary to relevance. |
| A negative control shares bias channels but has no causal route to outcome | A matched irrelevant passage can share selector features yet have no legitimate answer effect | **Fragile but testable transfer.** Roughly even (45-55%) for automatically generated natural-language controls; nullness is hard to guarantee. |
| Experimental units are exchangeable or dependencies are modeled | Context fragments are separable samples | **Fails.** Context has ordering, cross-reference, compression, and interaction effects. |
| Study outcome is evaluated after a mostly fixed intervention | Agent action changes the environment and the meaning of later evidence | **Fails without phase-local re-registration.** The target is an adaptive closed loop. |

### The decisive disanalogy

In an experiment, observing a patient normally does not rewrite earlier patients. In an agent context, summarizing or evicting one item can alter the interpretation, position, and effective accessibility of every other item; the model’s next action then changes the external state. Thus there is interference between “units,” endogenous data collection, and a moving estimand. Any design pretending otherwise is cargo cult.

The borrow survives only because its most valuable mechanisms—precommitment, masked discretion, calibration, and controls—do not require independent units. They require a known decision boundary, a before/after distinction, and falsifiable invariances. Whether those invariances can be constructed is the central empirical question.

## 4. Preregistered experiments that would settle the UNVERIFIED premises

These experiments are part of the recommendation, not optional future polish.

### E0 — bus feasibility and size spike

- **Primary question:** Can a supported existing hook/skill/MCP event observe candidate context immediately before compaction and return a keep/summarize/evict decision?
- **Positive control:** demonstrate the detector sees a known candidate and that a forced “keep” changes the emitted context.
- **Second independent signal:** compare the hook event log with the next model request’s serialized context, not a second reading of the same log.
- **Success:** both signals agree for at least 95% of 100 synthetic events; no new daemon; executable online adapter remains below roughly 200 non-test lines.
- **Decision:** failure means stop or redesign on the existing bus; it does not justify a second orchestration runtime.

### E1 — precommitment against conclusion-conditioned eviction

- Sample 200 historical or synthetic compaction events with a tentative conclusion, at least one supporting item, one contradiction, and one neutral dependency.
- Randomize frozen phase-local protocol versus ordinary adaptive selection; blind adjudicators to arm.
- Primary outcome: contradiction-retention recall at equal token budget.
- Secondary outcomes: downstream correctness, protocol deviations, token cost, and neutral-item false retention.
- Negative control: a contradiction-shaped passage adjudicated irrelevant to the decision.
- Positive control: an exact owner constraint that must always survive.
- Predeclare exclusions and conduct the primary analysis regardless of direction.

### E2 — retention-power curves and temporal generalization

- Build chronological train/calibration/test slices; never tune on the last slice.
- Factor budget, absolute position, item age, distractor similarity, dependency hops, and task family.
- Primary outcome: probability of correctly *using*, not merely quoting, the minimum consequential dependency.
- Prespecify an acceptable protected-class false-negative ceiling and ordinary-class cost curve.
- Compare a global threshold with task-stratified thresholds.
- Report the full curve and confidence intervals, including null or adverse regions.

### E3 — answer-blind eviction

- Paired replay of the same candidate set with a drafted answer visible versus masked.
- Human reference labels created without seeing model arm or model score.
- Primary outcome: retention of valid refuting evidence.
- Co-primary harm: loss of action-dependent evidence.
- Negative control: candidates whose value is adjudicated independent of the drafted action.
- Positive control: candidates whose relevance genuinely changes with the planned action.
- A win requires better refutation retention without breaching the co-primary harm limit.

### E4 — validity of matched controls

- Construct controls matched on length, lexical overlap, format, provenance class, and position.
- Two independent adjudicators establish whether each control has a legitimate causal path to the correct answer; disagreements are excluded before model execution.
- Randomly insert/remove controls at several context positions.
- Primary outcome: false answer-change rate for negative controls.
- Detector-sensitivity outcome: true answer-change rate for positive controls.
- Reject any control generator that looks “clean” only because it also misses positive controls.

Across E1-E4, the recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)** to yield interpretable evidence only if hypotheses, primary outcomes, exclusions, and analysis are frozen before the held-out runs; the evidence is precisely the preregistration mechanism being borrowed. This is a statement about interpretability, not guaranteed improvement.

## 5. Anti-overfit gate and alternatives rejected

The proposal rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus and uses append-only protocol records plus offline replays. It does not require or recommend a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED: sample only some online checkpoints. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Run full matched controls offline and a low-rate sample online; reserve deterministic positive controls for critical compactions. This is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve most diagnostic value with less latency than testing every turn, based on the controls being instrumentation rather than task work. The appropriate sampling rate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by E4 plus a latency/cost curve before production selection**.

Rejected alternatives:

1. **“Keep everything; long windows solved context.”** Rejected because effective use is not identical to nominal capacity; position and task complexity matter ([Liu et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/); [Hsieh et al., 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=kIoBbc76Sy)).
2. **Literal clinical-trial power formula over tokens.** Rejected because no defensible independent-unit or variance model exists for arbitrary context.
3. **A second model as an always-on trained critic.** Rejected by the anti-overfit gate and because correlated model errors would not constitute independent evidence.
4. **Random lorem ipsum as a negative control.** Rejected because it does not share the suspected selection-bias channel and can create an easy, misleading pass.
5. **Blind every metadata field.** Rejected because authority, chronology, and planned action can be legitimate determinants of context value.
6. **Immutable task-long preregistration.** Rejected because agent tasks are adaptive; honest, versioned phase changes are better than covert deviation or brittle adherence.
7. **A large context-governance daemon.** Rejected because the mechanisms can be tested on the existing bus first. A BUILD-LARGE implementation has no justification unless E0 proves the existing bus incapable and a separate future unit establishes benefits exceeding runtime complexity.

## 7. Gaps and boundaries

- No local harness claim was verified in this unit. In particular, I do not claim that the current harness has no preregistration analogue, no context canaries, or no masked judge. Establishing absence would require two mechanically independent local signals and a known-present control; that belongs to a grounding unit.
- The causal nullness of natural-language negative controls is the largest conceptual risk. Until E4, all recommendations depending on synthetic controls remain explicitly UNVERIFIED.
- Replay validity over a changing model and changing workload is limited. Temporal holdouts and periodic recalibration reduce but do not eliminate distribution shift.
- Human adjudication is not automatically ground truth. Blinding adjudicators to treatment arm and measuring inter-rater disagreement makes subjectivity visible; it does not erase it.
- Statistical “power” is used only as an analogy for empirical sensitivity curves. The artifact rejects claims that a token budget has classical statistical power absent a specified replay population, outcome, and experimental design.

## Bottom line

The borrow is worth testing, but only in disciplined form. Pre-registration maps well to versioned, phase-local context policy; blinding maps narrowly to hiding drafted conclusions from discretionary eviction; negative controls map to matched null passages and invariant probes; power analysis maps only to held-out empirical retention curves. The analogy fails wherever it treats tokens as independent subjects, assumes fixed outcomes, or presumes a natural-language control is causally inert.

The best resulting harness mechanism is a small **Context Trial Protocol** on the existing bus, backed by replay experiments and positive-control-validated detectors. Its auditability benefit is **very likely (80-90%)**, grounded in the source mechanisms; its task-success benefit is **likely (55-80%) but UNVERIFIED**, to be settled by E1-E4 and a prospective shadow-mode evaluation. That uncertainty is a reason to preregister the test, not a reason to force the analogy or build a new runtime.
