# X010 — Cross-domain borrow: clinical medicine → harness observability without a baseline

**Unit:** X010  
**Source field:** clinical medicine (controlled treatment trials, differential diagnosis, iatrogenics, contraindication registries, NNT/NNH)  
**Target problem:** deciding whether a harness change helped when no trustworthy historical baseline exists  
**Research date:** 2026-07-18  
**Scope:** external research and cross-domain design only. Per the unit brief, this document does not inspect or make claims about the local harness.

## Executive finding

The useful clinical borrow is a **prospective, concurrent, active-comparator “harness change trial,” with an N-of-1 crossover variant for reversible changes**. Medicine does not solve a missing baseline by manufacturing one from anecdotes. It creates a counterfactual prospectively: eligible new patients are assigned concurrently to the new treatment or the current treatment, outcomes and harms are defined before looking at results, and the effect is expressed as both a relative contrast and an absolute difference. In harness terms, eligible new tasks are assigned to candidate or current configuration at the same time; the task—not a tool call—is the unit of outcome; failures and owner burden are harms; and results include “tasks needed to treat” and “tasks needed to harm.”

This transfer is **very likely (80-90%) to improve the validity of decisions about reversible, task-local harness changes**, because random concurrent assignment directly addresses time trends, task-mix shifts, and selective rollout—the same causal structures for which clinical trials use concurrent randomized controls. The evidence is the ICH E10 account of control groups and the FDA recommendation that primary comparisons use concurrently randomized eligible subjects, plus the target-trial literature’s explicit protocol structure ([ICH E10](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E10_Guideline.pdf), [FDA master-protocol recap](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/guidances-drugs/guidance-recap-podcast-master-protocols-drug-and-biological-product-development), [Hernán and Robins target-trial framework](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832051/)). It is **unlikely (20-45%) to identify the effect of irreversible, global, or strongly learning-mediated changes by simple per-task crossover**, because carryover and interference violate the N-of-1 assumptions; the CENT guidance explicitly treats carryover and period effects as design threats ([CENT explanation](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h1793)). Those changes need a cluster or phased design, or an honest “not identified” result.

The proposal deliberately does **not** introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector/KG memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It is a thin experiment protocol and assignment/aggregation layer riding existing session/task hooks. This passes the anti-overfit gate.

## The actual problem, stated causally

“Did errors go down after the change?” is not the causal question. It compares two time periods in which task difficulty, model version, operator behavior, repository state, network health, and measurement behavior may all have changed. A historical “before” series is therefore not automatically a control.

The causal question is:

> For tasks eligible at the same point in time, what would the distribution of owner-relevant outcomes have been under the candidate harness versus the current harness?

When no baseline exists, a concurrent control can create the missing comparison from the next eligible tasks. This does not recover the past. It estimates a prospective effect in the population actually enrolled.

The distinction matters because medicine separates:

1. **Treatment/exposure:** candidate harness versus current harness.
2. **Patient/unit:** a whole task or session, not each correlated tool call.
3. **Indication/eligibility:** the class of work for which the change is intended.
4. **Outcome:** owner-relevant task result, fixed before assignment.
5. **Adverse event:** a regression or burden caused or plausibly worsened by the change.
6. **Estimand:** the precise contrast—usually assignment to candidate versus current configuration.
7. **Time zero:** the instant eligibility is established and assignment occurs.

The target-trial framework requires eligibility, treatment strategies, assignment, follow-up, outcome, causal contrast, and analysis to be specified as a protocol. It also warns that misaligning eligibility, treatment assignment, and start of follow-up produces bias ([target-trial overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10400102/), [trial-emulation methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11220224/)). That maps unusually well: harness telemetry often has plentiful post hoc events but no declared task boundary, eligible population, or counterfactual.

## Mechanism 1: concurrent randomized active-comparator, new-user trials

### How it works in clinical medicine

A randomized controlled trial compares an intervention with placebo, no treatment, or active standard care in concurrently enrolled people. Random assignment makes treatment selection independent of both measured and unmeasured baseline prognostic factors in expectation. A concurrent control experiences the same calendar time and study procedures.

An **active comparator** is especially important when treatment is selected for an indication. In observational pharmacoepidemiology, comparing a drug’s users with non-users creates “confounding by indication”: people who receive treatment differ systematically from people who do not. The active-comparator new-user design compares initiators of two treatments used for the same indication and starts follow-up at initiation. This seeks to emulate a head-to-head randomized trial and captures early risks that prevalent-user designs can miss ([Lund, Richardson, and Stürmer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26954351/), [Yoshida et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25800216/)).

Clinical guidance is blunt about the alternative. ICH E10 identifies studies without a concurrent randomized control as having major interpretive problems, and FDA master-protocol guidance recommends that a primary comparison use concurrently randomized subjects who were eligible to receive the intervention, excluding nonconcurrent controls ([ICH E10](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E10_Guideline.pdf), [FDA master-protocol recap](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/guidances-drugs/guidance-recap-podcast-master-protocols-drug-and-biological-product-development)).

“New user” means follow-up begins when treatment begins, not after survivors have already tolerated it. This avoids losing early harms and mixing initiation effects with stable-use effects ([Yoshida et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25800216/)).

### Active-comparator translation — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Treat **the current harness configuration as standard care**, not “nothing.” Enroll only new tasks that satisfy a predeclared indication. At task time zero, assign candidate or current configuration. Do not select candidate tasks because they look easy, because the owner is watching, or after seeing partial task progress.

Examples of indications:

- “A task whose prompt requests editing at least one repository file and for which a test command is available.”
- “A task that triggers web research and has at least one externally verifiable factual answer.”
- “A Windows shell task containing one or more paths with spaces.”

Bad indications are retrospective, outcome-dependent categories such as “tasks on which the candidate would have helped” or “sessions that produced at least one error.” Those condition enrollment on post-assignment facts.

Assignment should be deterministic from a recorded experiment identifier and task/session identifier, but unpredictable to the model before the task is admitted. Block or stratify by a small number of pre-treatment factors with strong outcome relevance—task family, platform, and perhaps coarse size. Do not add dozens of covariates; sparse strata create imbalance rather than cure it.

The primary analysis is intention-to-treat: analyze tasks by assigned harness even when a fallback causes the actual path to differ. A separate per-protocol analysis can be exploratory. Medicine uses the intention-to-treat contrast to preserve the protection of randomization; target-trial reporting likewise distinguishes assignment effects from adherence effects ([TARGET statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/390/bmj-2025-087179.full.pdf)).

This design is **very likely (80-90%) to catch false improvement caused by changing task mix or calendar-time conditions**, because both arms are enrolled under the same eligibility rule and period; that is the direct role of concurrent controls in the cited clinical guidance. It is **likely (55-80%) to catch selective exposure of easy tasks to the candidate**, because assignment is fixed at time zero; the residual uncertainty is whether task identity can be declared before meaningful work begins.

### What it catches that ordinary telemetry misses

- **Regression to the mean:** a change installed after a bad week can look helpful even if the bad week was transient.
- **Task-mix drift:** later sessions may contain easier work.
- **Model or service drift:** both arms encounter the same external period.
- **Survivorship/prevalent-user bias:** early failures count from task initiation.
- **Selective rollout:** candidate assignment is not chosen after difficulty becomes apparent.
- **Denominator loss:** every admitted task is recorded, including aborts, rather than counting only tool calls emitted by completed tasks.

### Limits of the transfer

Randomization does not fix bad outcomes, missing outcomes, noncompliance, or interference. The latest target-trial review explicitly says the framework resolves design errors, not data limitations ([target-trial limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11936718/)). If candidate behavior changes shared caches, repositories, user expectations, or model learning for later control tasks, one task’s treatment affects another task’s outcome. That violates the no-interference assumption.

**Proposal — UNVERIFIED cluster assignment — CONFIG (settings/JSON only):** For changes with shared-state contamination, session- or workspace-level cluster assignment is **likely (55-80%) to reduce interference**, based on the structural separation rather than direct harness evidence. **UNVERIFIED: run an A/A contamination experiment** in which two nominally identical configurations receive different labels and measure whether outcomes correlate across adjacent tasks, shared workspaces, or assignment order. Until that test, a task-level causal estimate for stateful changes remains UNVERIFIED.

## Mechanism 2: randomized N-of-1 crossover trials

### How it works in clinical medicine

An N-of-1 trial is a randomized multiple-crossover comparison of treatments in one patient. The same patient receives A and B in multiple periods, with order randomized and often blinded. The patient acts as their own control, removing stable between-person differences. The design is suited to chronic, sufficiently stable conditions, reversible treatments with relatively rapid onset and offset, and outcomes that can be measured repeatedly.

CENT’s N-of-1 reporting guidance requires treatment sequence, period, washout, outcome, stopping rules, and statistical handling of carryover, period effects, and within-person correlation. It emphasizes that an earlier treatment can influence a later period and that washout is used to reduce carryover ([CENT explanation](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h1793), [CENT statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5058423/)).

### UNVERIFIED concrete N-of-1 translation — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Use repeated A/B blocks for a single recurring task class or owner workflow:

```text
block 1: candidate, current
block 2: current, candidate
block 3: current, candidate
block 4: candidate, current
```

The sequence is randomized within pairs. Each period is a new eligible task from the same declared class. The “patient” is the stable owner/workflow/repository context; the periods are tasks. Pairing controls stable idiosyncrasies better than pooling unrelated repositories and owners.

There is no literal washout for many software changes. A clean worktree, fresh process, cleared task-local cache, fixed model version, and no cross-period memory are possible operational analogues. If these cannot reverse the exposure, do not call it an N-of-1 trial.

N-of-1 is **likely (55-80%) to be more sample-efficient than unpaired comparison for highly repetitive, reversible workflows**, because within-context pairing removes stable between-context variance, which is the reason the clinical design exists. **UNVERIFIED: settle the harness-specific gain with a replayable A/A crossover experiment** and compare the variance of paired versus unpaired task differences. The recommendation does not depend on the gain: the A/A test is the admission gate.

### Hard stop: where N-of-1 does not transfer

Do not use crossover for:

- persistent instruction or memory changes;
- skill installation that changes later discovery or owner behavior;
- repository migrations;
- security policy changes whose exposure cannot ethically be reversed;
- changes that teach the owner a new workflow;
- rare, nonrepeatable tasks;
- changes with long or unknown carryover;
- tasks whose difficulty trend is monotonic across periods.

For those cases, the analogy fails. The “patient” is not stable, the treatment is not reversible, or repeated outcomes are not exchangeable. A parallel cluster trial or a prospective phased rollout may be possible, but a simple A/B/A/B sequence would produce false precision.

## Mechanism 3: negative controls as detector controls

### How it works in clinical medicine

A negative-control outcome is one the exposure should not plausibly cause but that shares sources of confounding or measurement error with the real outcome. A negative-control exposure similarly should not cause the outcome but shares bias structure. If the negative control shows an association, it is evidence that uncontrolled confounding, selection, or measurement bias may also contaminate the target association. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen proposed negative controls specifically to detect these otherwise hidden biases ([negative-controls paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). Later work formalized their use for selection and measurement bias ([Arnold et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4969055/)).

Negative controls are not magic falsifiers. A poor negative control can genuinely be affected by the exposure or fail to share the relevant bias. The original paper explicitly warns that the causal and bias assumptions governing control selection matter ([negative-controls paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).

### Negative-control translation — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Every harness trial should include at least two mechanically independent controls:

1. **Assignment control (A/A):** route tasks through two labels whose effective configuration is byte-identical. The analysis must find no systematic performance difference beyond expected sampling variation.
2. **Known-present event control:** inject or select a benign, unmistakable event that the outcome detector must count, while also verifying it through an independent event source. This tests observability, not treatment efficacy.

Optional negative-control outcomes should be change-specific. For example, a shell-quoting change should not alter image-generation latency on tasks that never invoke a shell. But this is valid only if shell policy cannot change planning or tool selection globally.

A negative-control association is **very likely (80-90%) to reveal at least one of assignment leakage, detector asymmetry, or shared upstream drift when the selected control genuinely shares that bias path**, based on the cited negative-control causal structure. A null negative control does not prove absence of bias.

This directly operationalizes the required two-signal rule: efficacy is not accepted from a single metric path. The result needs the task outcome plus an independent exposure/assignment record; absence detectors must first find a known-present control.

## Mechanism 4: iatrogenics, harms surveillance, and contraindication registries

### How it works in clinical medicine

Clinical medicine treats an intervention as capable of causing harm even when it improves its target outcome. “Adverse event” collection is therefore a separate stream from efficacy. CONSORT Harms 2022 asks trials to report how harms were collected, their timing and attribution, monitoring and stopping rules, and absolute risk by arm, event type, grade, and seriousness ([CONSORT Harms 2022](https://www.bmj.com/content/381/bmj-2022-073725)).

Post-market spontaneous-report systems serve another purpose: they collect suspected harms that controlled trials may be too small or narrow to detect. But a report is a **signal**, not an incidence estimate or proof of causality. FDA states that FAERS data have limitations and cannot be used to calculate adverse-event incidence because the necessary denominator and reporting completeness are absent ([FDA FAERS limitations](https://www.fda.gov/media/123624/download)). FDA Sentinel supplements reports with analyses over defined populations and, in some questions, self-controlled risk intervals ([FDA Sentinel assessment](https://www.fda.gov/files/about%20fda/published/Final-Assessment-of-Sentinel-%28PDF---2.3MB%29.pdf)).

A contraindication is a condition under which a treatment should not be used because risk clearly outweighs possible benefit. FDA guidance reserves a true contraindication for known hazards or sufficiently supported anticipated adverse reactions, not wholly theoretical possibilities ([FDA contraindications guidance](https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Warnings-and-Precautions--Contraindications--and-Boxed-Warning-Sections-of-Labeling-for-Human-Prescription-Drug-and-Biological-Products-%E2%80%94-Content-and-Format.pdf)). The transferable part is not the medical vocabulary; it is the **structured link between a candidate intervention, a context, a suspected harm, evidence status, and an action**. A theoretical harness concern belongs on a watchlist, not in the hard-contraindication tier.

### UNVERIFIED harms-register translation — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Maintain a small, reviewable **Harness Harms and Contraindications Register**. Each entry contains:

- change identifier and version;
- context predicate (platform, tool, task class, repository property);
- adverse event definition and severity;
- evidence status: theoretical, one signal, two independent signals, controlled estimate;
- denominator and exposure window, if known;
- required action: warn, exclude from trial, auto-fallback, or hard stop;
- dechallenge/rechallenge evidence, if ethically and operationally safe;
- expiry/review date and owner;
- links to the raw cases and the trial that could confirm or refute causality.

The registry should never convert a raw event count into a rate without exposed-task denominators. It should never label correlation as causality. A single report may justify a safety stop for catastrophic harm, while routine regressions should trigger investigation rather than permanent prohibition.

The register is **likely (55-80%) to catch context-specific regressions that a global average masks**, because it makes effect modification and harms explicit rather than pooling every task. The exact benefit is **UNVERIFIED: run a prospective six-week audit** comparing regressions discovered through the register with regressions found through ordinary aggregate metrics, adjudicated blind to discovery channel.

### Where the pharmacovigilance analogy fails

A raw harness error fingerprint database resembles spontaneous adverse-event reports, not a controlled trial. It is useful for signal generation but cannot answer “did the change help?” without a denominator and comparator. Medicine itself does not permit that inference from FAERS. Therefore:

- event counts alone are not rates;
- more reports after rollout can mean more exposure or better detection;
- fewer reports can mean detector failure;
- temporal proximity does not establish causality;
- duplicate fingerprints are not independent patients/tasks;
- a contraindication registry is not an efficacy estimator.

This is a decisive disanalogy. Borrowing only the registry, without the trial, would create organized anecdotes rather than observability.

## Mechanism 5: number needed to treat and number needed to harm

### How it works in clinical medicine

For a binary outcome, number needed to treat is the reciprocal of the absolute risk reduction. If outcome risk under control is \(p_C\) and under treatment is \(p_T\), then for an undesirable outcome:

\[
NNT = 1 / (p_C - p_T)
\]

For an added harm:

\[
NNH = 1 / (p_T - p_C)
\]

NNT means the expected number of patients treated with the new intervention rather than the comparator for one additional patient to benefit. At zero absolute difference NNT is infinite ([BMJ confidence intervals for NNT](https://www.bmj.com/content/317/7168/1309)). NNT is baseline-risk dependent: the same relative effect yields different absolute benefit in low- and high-risk populations ([BMJ EBM glossary](https://bestpractice.bmj.com/info/us/evidence/ebm-toolbox/a-glossary-of-ebm-terms/)).

### UNVERIFIED TNT/TNH translation — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Report **tasks needed to treat (TNT)** and **tasks needed to harm (TNH)** alongside relative metrics:

- TNT to prevent one owner correction;
- TNT to prevent one task-level tool failure;
- TNT to produce one additional independently verified successful task;
- TNH to cause one additional rollback, false block, or unrecoverable task failure.

Example: if owner-correction incidence is 20% under current and 12% under candidate, the absolute reduction is 8 percentage points and TNT is 12.5 eligible tasks. This example is arithmetic, not an expected real effect. Report the risk difference and interval first; reciprocal intervals become awkward or cross infinity when the effect is uncertain.

TNT is **likely (55-80%) to improve prioritization compared with relative error-rate reduction alone**, because it exposes absolute owner benefit and dependence on task prevalence, the same interpretive advantage of clinical NNT. **UNVERIFIED: settle this with a blinded decision study** in which maintainers rank candidate changes from relative-only reports versus absolute-risk-plus-TNT/TNH reports; score consistency with a predeclared utility function.

TNT cannot be computed from a “no baseline” event stream. The prospective concurrent control supplies \(p_C\). This is why NNT is an output of the controlled design, not a substitute for it.

## The proposed mechanism: Harness Change Trial Protocol

### Proposal A — prospective trial dossier — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Before exposure begins, create an immutable or append-only experiment dossier with:

```yaml
experiment_id: stable identifier
hypothesis: one causal sentence
indication: pre-assignment eligibility predicate
exclusions: safety and carryover exclusions
unit: task | session | workspace
candidate: content-addressed configuration identity
comparator: content-addressed current configuration identity
assignment: seed, ratio, blocks, strata
time_zero: exact admission event
primary_outcome: one owner-relevant endpoint
secondary_outcomes: explicitly exploratory or multiplicity-controlled
harms: definitions, severity, and collection window
negative_controls: A/A and known-present detector control
estimand: intention-to-treat by default
missingness: rules for aborts, lost telemetry, and fallbacks
stopping: sample/information target and safety boundaries
analysis: absolute difference, interval, TNT/TNH where defined
decision: ship, reject, extend, or indeterminate thresholds
```

This dossier is **very likely (80-90%) to prevent post hoc outcome switching when enforcement rejects undeclared fields from confirmatory analysis**, because the primary outcome and analysis are fixed before results. FDA warns that many endpoints inflate false conclusions without multiplicity control, and CONSORT discourages multiple primary outcomes ([FDA multiple-endpoints guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/multiple-endpoints-clinical-trials), [CONSORT 2010 explanation](https://www.bmj.com/content/340/bmj.c869)). Merely writing a dossier without enforcement is not enough.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED thin assignment and trial-record hook — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build one small hook or script that:

1. evaluates only pre-assignment eligibility fields;
2. computes and records assignment from experiment ID, task ID, and seed;
3. records candidate/comparator content identity;
4. emits an enrollment event before treatment-specific execution;
5. emits one terminal task outcome plus harm events;
6. records fallback and protocol deviations without changing assigned arm;
7. appends records rather than operating a daemon;
8. aggregates by task and experiment, not by raw tool-call count.

The line-size fit is **UNVERIFIED and roughly even (45-55%)** because no local hook API was inspected by design. **Experiment to settle:** spike the smallest supported hook against a five-task A/A trial; count non-generated executable lines and reject this design if assignment, append-only events, and aggregation cannot stay below roughly 200 lines. If it exceeds that threshold, prefer a simpler manual protocol over a new service.

The hook must fail closed for evidence, not for work: if assignment or outcome recording fails, the task may continue under the safe current configuration, but the trial record becomes “telemetry failure / not analyzable,” never a fabricated control success.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED endpoint hierarchy and harms — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Use one primary task-level endpoint. Suggested default:

> Independently verified task success without owner correction or emergency fallback within the declared follow-up window.

This default is **UNVERIFIED and likely (55-80%) to align better with owner value than per-tool success**, because a tool can succeed while the task fails and repeated retries create correlated pseudo-samples. **Experiment to settle:** have the owner blind-rate a stratified sample of task outcomes, compare agreement and predictive value for owner correction between the composite endpoint and tool-level error rate.

Secondary outcomes can include elapsed time, token/tool cost, number of retries, owner corrections, rollback, and serious failure. Treat all undeclared slices as hypothesis-generating. Harms remain reportable regardless of whether the primary outcome succeeds.

### Proposal D — negative-control admission gate — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

No candidate-versus-current inference is accepted until:

1. an A/A run shows no arm-specific instrumentation or routing difference large enough to matter;
2. the outcome detector finds a known-present success and a known-present failure;
3. assignment is recoverable from two mechanically independent signals—for example, admission record and content identity observed at execution;
4. missing task outcomes are enumerated by arm.

Passing does not prove the detector perfect. Failing invalidates the efficacy inference.

### Proposal E — harms/contraindication record — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Store structured context-specific harms beside, but not mixed into, efficacy estimates. A two-signal confirmed harm can become a temporary exclusion predicate for the next trial; a catastrophic single case can trigger an immediate safety stop. Every exclusion must have a review/expiry condition so folklore does not become permanent configuration.

### Proposal F — UNVERIFIED absolute effect report with TNT/TNH — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

The same small aggregation script should report:

- enrolled, assigned, exposed, completed, missing, and analyzed tasks by arm;
- primary-event incidence by arm;
- absolute risk difference and uncertainty interval;
- relative risk as secondary context;
- TNT/TNH only when the absolute difference’s direction is interpretable;
- harm incidence by arm and severity;
- strata and sequence plots for drift/carryover diagnosis;
- protocol deviations and detector-control results;
- an explicit result: ship, reject, extend, or indeterminate.

Do not continuously peek and stop at the first favorable fluctuation. If interim looks are necessary, prespecify their number and a sequential boundary. Repeated interim testing consumes error probability; clinical group-sequential methods use alpha-spending or related boundaries to preserve error control ([group-sequential monitoring](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11986074/), [exact sequential safety testing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8441767/)).

### Proposal G — no always-on observability platform — **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** — REJECT

Do not build a second orchestrator, streaming metrics service, experiment database, or dashboard for this mechanism. A dossier plus append-only trial events and a small analysis script are sufficient to test the causal design. A new platform would violate the anti-overfit gate and add a second thing whose observability must itself be established. Reconsider only after multiple completed trials demonstrate that manual file-based enrollment or analysis is the limiting failure mode, with two independent signals.

## Example trial

Suppose the candidate is a safer quoting policy for shell invocations.

**Protocol:**

- Indication: new tasks requiring at least one shell command containing a path with spaces or user-provided literal text.
- Exclusions: tasks already partially executed; tasks requiring secrets; global environment migrations.
- Unit: session, if quoting policy can influence planning across tool calls.
- Candidate: new quoting policy.
- Active comparator: current quoting policy.
- Time zero: eligibility determination before the first shell plan.
- Primary outcome: task completes without a shell parse/quoting failure and without owner correction.
- Harms: command semantics changed, unintended file target, additional owner correction, task abort.
- Negative-control outcome: a known-present non-shell event recorded by the same terminal outcome collector; it should not differ by arm.
- Assignment: randomized 1:1 in blocks within shell family.
- Analysis: intention-to-treat absolute difference, interval, TNT; TNH for changed-semantics harm.
- Stop: prespecified information target or immediate serious safety event.

This trial is **very likely (80-90%) to distinguish easier candidate-arm commands from a real policy effect when enrollment, randomization, and outcome collection pass their controls**, because the concurrent randomized assignment balances command difficulty in expectation. It is **very likely (80-90%) to expose a candidate that reduces parse failures by refusing more commands**, because refusal/owner correction is explicitly included in the task-level endpoint and harms stream; both claims depend on the cited concurrent-control logic rather than observed harness results.

## Differential diagnosis: a useful secondary borrow, not the estimator

Clinical differential diagnosis maintains multiple plausible causes, seeks discriminating evidence, updates probabilities, and avoids anchoring on the first explanation. For harness observability, an unexpected arm difference should trigger a compact causal differential:

1. true candidate effect;
2. assignment imbalance;
3. exposure nonadherence or fallback;
4. detector asymmetry;
5. missing outcomes by arm;
6. carryover/interference;
7. time or sequence effect;
8. multiplicity/chance;
9. outcome gaming or substitution (for example, fewer errors because fewer actions were attempted).

### Proposal H — UNVERIFIED result-adjudication differential — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Require the trial report to mark each of the nine alternatives as checked, contradicted, supported, or not testable, with the evidence field used. This is **likely (55-80%) to reduce premature causal closure**, because it forces explicit competing explanations, but the numeric benefit is **UNVERIFIED: settle with a blinded retrospective adjudication** of completed A/A and seeded-fault trials, comparing false causal conclusions with and without the checklist.

The differential is not itself evidence and must not become an elaborate critic agent. It is a fixed report section.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

| Clinical structural assumption | Harness analogue | Does it transfer? | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concurrent patients face broadly the same secular conditions | Candidate and current tasks run during the same calendar interval | Mostly | Strong reason to prefer concurrent assignment over before/after comparison |
| Randomization precedes treatment and outcome | Assignment occurs before treatment-specific planning/execution | Yes, if task admission exists | If assignment occurs after a first failure, discard causal claim |
| One patient’s treatment does not affect another’s outcome | One task’s configuration does not mutate shared state used by another | Often no | Cluster by session/workspace or do not estimate task-level effect |
| Treatment is well-defined | Candidate and comparator have content-addressed identities | Potentially | “Latest” or mutable config is not an analyzable treatment |
| Outcome ascertainment is symmetric | Same terminal evaluator and window for both arms | Potentially | Arm-specific detectors invalidate comparison |
| N-of-1 condition is stable across periods | Repeated task class has comparable difficulty over sequence | Sometimes | Randomize order, block, inspect period effects; reject if monotonic drift dominates |
| N-of-1 treatment is reversible with short washout | Config can be fully reset between tasks | Frequently false | Do not use crossover for memory, migrations, learning, or persistent caches |
| Patients are independent sampling units | Tool calls are independent | No | Use task/session as unit; cluster repeated calls |
| Blinding reduces expectancy effects | Model/owner does not know arm | Partial | Content often reveals treatment; prefer objective endpoints and blinded adjudication where possible |
| A treatment has both benefits and harms | Harness change can fix one failure while creating another | Yes | Separate efficacy and harms; use task-level composite and TNH |
| Spontaneous reports lack a denominator | Error fingerprints lack exposed-task denominators | Yes | Use them for signals, never effect rates |
| NNT depends on baseline risk and population | TNT depends on eligible-task failure risk | Yes | Never transport one TNT across task classes without re-estimation |

### Principal disanalogy: tasks are adaptive and treatment-aware

Patients generally do not rewrite the drug mid-dose. An agent can inspect instructions, infer its assigned policy, alter its plan, retry, delegate, or compensate. The treatment therefore changes the data-generating process and the number of observations. That is not fatal—the same is true of adherence and co-interventions in medicine—but it makes raw tool calls especially invalid as independent outcomes.

An intention-to-treat, task-level endpoint is **very likely (80-90%) to be less biased than per-call error percentages for comparing adaptive harness policies**, because assignment remains fixed while the number and type of calls are post-treatment mediators. The evidence is the target-trial distinction between assignment and adherence effects and the new-user paper’s warning against adjusting for post-treatment mediators ([TARGET statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/390/bmj-2025-087179.full.pdf), [Yoshida et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25800216/)).

### Principal ethical/operational disanalogy: withholding a known fix

Clinical trials require equipoise and safety monitoring. Likewise, randomizing tasks to a current configuration is inappropriate once a candidate has overwhelming evidence of preventing catastrophic irreversible harm, or when the current arm is already known unsafe for the indication. In that case, use deterministic rollout with prospective harm surveillance; do not pretend the causal effect is measured. For ordinary quality improvements with genuine uncertainty, an active current comparator is appropriate.

### External validity

A trial estimates the effect only in its eligibility population and operating period. A quoting-policy result on Windows paths should not be generalized to web research or image generation. NNT/TNT changes with baseline risk. This limitation transfers exactly.

## Decision rules: when to use which design

### Parallel concurrent randomized assignment — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

- the change is task-local or can be isolated by session/workspace;
- both configurations are permissible under genuine uncertainty;
- tasks recur often enough to enroll prospectively;
- the primary outcome can be measured symmetrically;
- interference is absent or controlled by cluster assignment.

### N-of-1 randomized crossover admission — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

- the workflow is repetitive within a stable context;
- exposure is fully reversible;
- onset and offset are short relative to each task period;
- carryover can be tested in A/A and sequence diagnostics;
- enough repeated periods exist.

### Prospective harms-only admission — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

- the change is irreversible or globally contaminating;
- there is no ethical or operational current-comparator arm;
- tasks are unique and no valid concurrent comparator exists.

In this last case, report association and signal strength, not causal improvement. A confident “effect not identified” is the correct result.

### Stop rather than force the analogy when

- treatment identity is mutable or unknown;
- task eligibility is declared after observing progress;
- owner-relevant outcomes cannot be measured for both arms;
- assignment changes the detector itself without an independent measurement path;
- shared-state interference cannot be bounded;
- missing outcomes are differential and unrecoverable;
- the only denominator is tool calls generated after treatment;
- sample size is too small to distinguish meaningful benefit from harm.

## Success criteria for this borrow

The borrow succeeds only if a harness trial can produce all of the following:

- a pre-exposure protocol;
- a concurrent current-configuration arm;
- recorded time-zero assignment;
- two independent exposure/detector signals;
- task-level outcomes for all enrolled units, including aborts;
- symmetric harms ascertainment;
- absolute effects and uncertainty, not only relative percentages;
- explicit missingness and protocol deviations;
- a negative-control result;
- a bounded claim tied to the eligible population;
- an “indeterminate” outcome when evidence is insufficient.

It fails if it merely adds dashboards, counts more events, compares this month with last month, or labels a fingerprint registry “causal observability.”

## Bottom line

Clinical medicine’s most valuable solution to “no baseline” is to **stop treating history as a counterfactual and prospectively create a concurrent one**. The harness analogue is a small, protocol-driven active-comparator trial at task/session level, strengthened by N-of-1 crossover only where reversibility and stability hold, negative controls that validate the detector, and a separate harms/contraindication stream. NNT becomes TNT only after a control risk exists.

This is **very likely (80-90%) to yield more causally defensible change decisions than before/after telemetry for narrow reversible harness changes**, supported by the clinical control-group, target-trial, active-comparator, negative-control, and harms literature cited above. It is **unlikely (20-45%) to rescue evaluation of global irreversible changes without isolation or a valid comparator**, because the core independence and reversibility assumptions fail. In those cases the correct output is not a forced metric but “causal benefit unverified.”
