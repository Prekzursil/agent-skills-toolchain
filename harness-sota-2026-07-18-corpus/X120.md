# X120 — Cross-domain borrow: forensic science → agent-claim verification

## Executive result

The forensic-science borrow transfers, but only as a **three-part quality system**, not as the familiar slogan “get a second opinion.” The transferable mechanisms are:

1. **Blind verification with staged information disclosure:** a second examiner reaches and records a conclusion without seeing the first examiner’s conclusion or irrelevant contextual information; only then are results compared and disagreements reconciled.
2. **Blind proficiency testing:** realistic, known-ground-truth cases enter the ordinary workflow without the worker knowing which cases are tests. This measures the whole system under operational conditions rather than merely testing a worker who knows they are on examination.
3. **Positive and negative controls for contamination:** known-present and known-absent material travels through the same process as questioned material. The controls show whether the process can detect what it is supposed to detect and whether signal has leaked into places it should not be.

The concrete harness translation is a small **verification broker** on the existing hook/tool/agent bus. It withholds the author’s answer from the verifier, commits both answers before reveal, periodically injects hidden known-answer tasks, and uses paired positive/negative “context controls” to detect answer leakage and rubber-stamping. It records claim-class-specific error and detection rates. It does **not** declare a claim true merely because two agents agree.

**Overall transfer judgment — very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: blind forensic verification directly addresses anchoring on another examiner’s conclusion; a field study found substantially more disagreement under blind than nonblind review; operational blind proficiency programs explicitly test the path from evidence receipt through report release; and forensic negative controls are designed to reveal contamination. The transfer is not “almost certain” because agent instances may share training, tools, prompts, retrieval sources, and deterministic failure modes to a much greater extent than separately trained human examiners. The settling experiment is the preregistered shadow trial in §8: compare current review, nonblind review, blind same-model review, and blind diverse-path review on concealed seeded claims and real claims later resolved by external outcomes.

This proposal passes the anti-overfit gate. It rides existing agent, hook, filesystem/artifact, and tool buses. It does not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. Scope and evidence discipline

This is deliberately an external ideation lens. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness. “Current” or “currently” below means a generic uncalibrated author/reviewer workflow unless explicitly tied to the pinned measured ground truth in the unit brief; it does not mean a static finding about the subject repository.

The central target is narrower than general output quality: **how can a harness obtain evidence that a claim is true without trusting the claim’s author and without requiring a person to read every case?** Three distinctions control the answer:

- **Corroboration is not truth.** Independent agreement is evidence only to the extent that the error processes are sufficiently independent and calibrated.
- **Verification is not validation.** A verifier can check that evidence supports a claim under a procedure; proficiency testing estimates how that procedure performs on cases whose truth is known.
- **Detection is not prevention.** Controls can reveal contamination or leakage without proving every neighboring sample or claim is clean. The US National Institute of Justice explicitly warns that sporadic contamination means a negative control does not prove all samples in a batch are contamination-free ([NIJ, “Quality Control Testing of Reagents and Consumables”](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/dna-amplification/contamination/prevention/quality-control-testing-reagents-and-consumables)).

## 2. Mechanism one in its own field: blind verification and information sequencing

### 2.1 What forensic science does

NIST’s forensic lexicon defines verification as comparison by a second, independent examiner so the first examiner’s findings can be corroborated or corrected when the two disagree. It distinguishes open from blind verification and states that blind verification is more robust ([NIST forensic lexicon, “Verification”](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/41106)). In a blind verification, the second examiner is denied the first examiner’s notes, results, and conclusion until the second examiner has made an independent assessment. A NIST standards review describes the underlying reason: bias is difficult to notice and can occur outside awareness, so access to the first examiner’s conclusion undermines independence ([NIST STRP final report on forensic physical-fit examination](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2023/04/14/STRP%20Final%20Report%202022-S-0015%20Standard%20Guide%20for%20Forensic%20Physical%20Fit%20Examination_Final.pdf)).

The protocol is more specific than “ask another expert”:

1. A case manager separates task-relevant material from potentially biasing context.
2. The verifier receives the questioned and comparison material needed to perform the examination, but not the first result.
3. The verifier performs the relevant analysis and records a conclusion before exposure to the first result.
4. Only after that commitment are the conclusions compared.
5. A defined discrepancy process handles differences; the first result is not silently treated as the answer key.

The Costa Rican Department of Forensic Sciences’ pilot combined a case manager, blind verification, and Linear Sequential Unmasking-Expanded (LSU-E); its verifier was shielded from the original opinion and information unnecessary to verification ([Quigley-McBride et al., 2024, *Forensic Science International: Synergy*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11720873/)). LSU-E generalizes the context-control idea: information is ranked by biasing power, objectivity, and relevance, then disclosed in an order that lets the examiner document an initial assessment before seeing more biasing material ([Dror & Kukucka, 2021, LSU-E](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsisyn.2021.100161)). This is not information deprivation for its own sake; it is **information architecture**: expose necessary information at the point it becomes necessary and preserve pre-exposure judgments.

There is a useful empirical warning against open “verification.” In a field study of bullet and cartridge-case peer review, the odds of disagreement were about five times higher when the verifier was blind to the first conclusion than when the verifier knew it; status differences amplified the effect ([Kukucka et al., 2020, *Science & Justice*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scijus.2020.06.001)). Disagreement is not synonymous with correction because casework truth in that study was unknown. It is nevertheless a mechanically relevant signal that nonblind review suppresses independent judgments.

### 2.2 What “double blind” does and does not mean here

Forensic literature uses several kinds of blindness that are often blurred together:

- In **blind verification**, the verifier does not know the first conclusion.
- In **blind proficiency testing**, the analyst does not know the case is a test.
- In a stronger allocation-blind design, the test provider or administrator does not know which examiner receives which packet, reducing differential treatment. An FBI review describes proficiency designs in which the provider does not know who has which packet and participants cannot learn answers from the provider or peers ([FBI archive, “Is a Match Really a Match?”](https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/about-us/lab/forensic-science-communications/fsc/july2009/review/2009_07_review01.htm)).

A literal clinical-trial-style double blind is not always possible or even desirable in forensic workflows: someone must retain ground truth, route the case, and reconcile discrepancies. The transferable property is therefore **role-separated knowledge**, not the label “double blind.” The custodian knows the truth but cannot influence the examiner; the examiner sees the evidence but not the expected answer; the scorer sees committed outputs and the key only after submission.

### 2.3 Concrete harness translation

#### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: Blind commit/reveal verification broker — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

For every selected atomic claim, create a `VerificationEnvelope` containing:

```text
claim_id
question_or_requirement
claim_class                 # file fact, command result, web fact, inference, prediction…
evidence_snapshot_refs      # immutable content hashes or captured tool outputs
allowed_tools
freshness_deadline
author_commit_hash          # hash of author result + nonce
verifier_result             # claim, status, evidence refs, reproduction transcript
verifier_commit_hash
reveal_and_discrepancy_record
```

The broker sends the verifier the original question, relevant immutable evidence, and allowed tools—but not the author’s prose, cited conclusion, confidence, scratchpad, or pass/fail expectation. The verifier must produce one of `SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, `INSUFFICIENT`, or `STALE`, cite the exact evidence span or tool result, and commit it. The broker then reveals both sides and applies deterministic comparison where possible. A disagreement routes to a fresh adjudication path; agreement is logged as corroboration, not a truth certificate.

The author’s exact answer must not be recoverable through filenames, prompt text, previous messages, shared scratch files, tool caches, or result ordering. The broker should give the verifier a clean context and opaque identifiers. **It is likely (55-80%) that prompt-level withholding alone would leak conclusions through shared artifacts or tool state.** Evidence: forensic context management treats notes and seemingly incidental case context as biasing channels, while agent tools routinely expose shared state by design. This agent-specific leakage rate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the “context canary” experiment in §8.2, which plants unique answer tokens in each possible channel and measures recovery by the verifier.**

What it catches:

- A reviewer that merely paraphrases or accepts the author’s answer.
- Confirmation search in which the second agent searches only for support.
- Fabricated citations, nonexistent files, or commands whose captured outputs do not support the claim.
- Claims that were true in an earlier snapshot but are stale at verification time.
- Disagreement hidden by a prose reviewer’s tendency to be agreeable.
- Cases where the available evidence is genuinely insufficient, if `INSUFFICIENT` is treated as a valid result rather than a failure to comply.

What it does not catch:

- The same misconception independently produced by both agents.
- A shared bad source, corrupted evidence snapshot, compromised tool, or incorrect ground-truth key.
- Claims whose truth is normative or underspecified rather than empirical.
- Errors outside the selected atomic claims.
- Sophisticated collusion if the two processes have an undeclared communication channel.

**Expected value — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: blind human verification changes review behavior in the cited field study and NIST calls it more robust than open verification. The lower bound reflects correlated model errors and the lack of a direct agent trial. **UNVERIFIED; the recommendation is conditional on Proposal D’s trial showing a materially higher seeded-error detection rate than nonblind review at an acceptable cost.**

## 3. Mechanism two in its own field: blind proficiency testing

### 3.1 What forensic science does

A proficiency test gives an examiner or laboratory material with a known or consensus-grounded answer and scores the returned result. NIJ distinguishes internal, external, and blind tests; in a blind test, analysts do not know they are being tested ([NIJ, “Proficiency Testing”](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/principles-forensic-dna-officers-court/05-assuring-quality-dna-testing/introduction-quality-assurance/proficiency-testing)).

The crucial distinction is **declared versus embedded** testing. Declared proficiency samples may be unusually clean, may cause workers to slow down or depart from ordinary procedure, and may test only the analytical endpoint. Harris County’s blind program introduced mock cases as ordinary casework specifically to test “the whole system, from evidence receipt to report release” and to remove anticipation that one is being evaluated ([Pierce & Cook, 2020, *Journal of Forensic Sciences*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31922611/)). A national DNA feasibility project similarly manufactured biological evidence, submitted simulated cases through law-enforcement channels, and used replicate evidence at reference laboratories ([Peterson et al., 2003, *Journal of Forensic Sciences*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12570196/); [full NIJ final report](https://www.ojp.gov/redirect-legacy/ncjrs/pdffiles1/nij/grants/192282.pdf)).

Realism matters. A study of latent-print proficiency tests found low observed error, high objective print quality, and examiner reports of ease; items most like real casework were harder and lower quality ([Kelley et al., 2020, *Science & Justice*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32111284/)). Thus a proficiency score estimates operational accuracy only to the extent the case distribution resembles operations. Hidden tests that are detectable as tests cease to be blind; tests that are too clean produce reassuring but poorly transported rates.

Proficiency tests answer a question ordinary review cannot: **when truth is independently known, how often does this whole system produce, detect, and repair error?** They also create an instrument for comparing verification protocols without assuming any protocol works.

### 3.2 Concrete harness translation

#### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: Hidden known-answer “ghost cases” — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Maintain a versioned bank of realistic cases with independently constructed keys. Inject a small random sample through the same router, prompts, tools, permissions, time limits, artifact paths, verification rules, and report path as owner work. Neither author nor verifier is told which cases are tests. The scorer unlocks the key only after both commitments.

The bank must cover distinct claim classes, because one pooled “accuracy” number is misleading:

- Exact local-state facts: a symbol exists, a test passed, a file changed.
- Tool-transcript facts: a command exited zero, output contains a value, a schema validates.
- Web/source facts: a URL supports a precisely scoped proposition and meets freshness requirements.
- Cross-source synthesis: multiple sources jointly entail a conclusion.
- Negative claims: an item is absent or a detector did not fire.
- Temporal claims: a fact held at time A and may differ at time B.
- Causal or diagnostic claims with decoys that support correlation but not cause.
- Calibrated abstention: the packet intentionally lacks enough evidence, so the correct answer is `INSUFFICIENT`.

Each case should include difficulty and provenance, expected evidence, permissible alternative answers, a maximum age, and a reason the key is trustworthy. Some cases should contain realistic tool errors, irrelevant context, stale documents, seductive but unsupported text, and evidence split across sources. Retire or rotate cases after exposure. Keep test generation separate from the models being evaluated; otherwise the same model may encode its own artifacts into both question and key.

Record at least:

```text
author false-support rate
author false-refutation rate
author correct-abstention rate
verifier seeded-error detection rate (sensitivity)
verifier correct-claim acceptance rate (specificity)
disagreement rate
repair rate after discrepancy
undetected shared-error rate
cost and latency per caught error
rates stratified by claim class, model family, tool path, and difficulty
```

What it catches that ordinary task success misses:

- A verifier that always agrees, always objects, or produces plausible prose without checking.
- A router/hook/tool path that is bypassed on real workflow-shaped cases.
- Accuracy collapse on messy cases despite good declared-test performance.
- Shared systematic errors, because the external key—not agreement—scores the pair.
- Bad abstention behavior and overclaiming under missing evidence.
- Drift after a model, prompt, tool, or routing change.

**Expected value — very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: blind testing is explicitly used to assess operational end-to-end performance, and the cited deployed program demonstrated feasibility in a forensic laboratory. The agent-specific effect size and sustainable injection rate are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with the staged pilot in §8.1 and require confidence intervals rather than a single pass percentage.**

## 4. Mechanism three in its own field: controls and contamination accounting

### 4.1 What forensic science does

Forensic DNA workflows put known samples through analytical steps alongside questioned samples:

- A **positive control** contains the target and demonstrates that the assay and detection path can produce the expected signal.
- A **negative control/reagent blank** lacks the target and can reveal contamination introduced by reagents, consumables, environment, or handling.
- Controls are interpreted with the batch, not as decorative checks after the fact.

NIJ states that negative controls and reagent blanks provide a way to detect reagent contamination and sometimes sporadic contamination; it also makes the essential limitation explicit: a clean negative control does not prove every sample in that batch is clean, and a contaminated control does not prove every sample is affected ([NIJ, “Quality Control Testing of Reagents and Consumables”](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/dna-amplification/contamination/prevention/quality-control-testing-reagents-and-consumables)). An early multi-laboratory forensic PCR trial found blank controls important for monitoring contamination and reported that procedural changes and stricter sterile technique corrected a contamination problem at one site ([Walsh et al., OJP abstract, “Report of the Blind Trial…”](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/report-blind-trial-cetus-amplitype-hla-dqalpha-forensic)).

The deep transferable idea is not DNA-specific sterility. It is that **the process must carry known-present and known-absent comparators through the same path as the unknown**, so a convincing result can be distinguished from a broken detector or introduced signal.

### 4.2 Concrete harness translation

#### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: Context-contamination controls — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

For sampled verification runs, create matched controls:

1. **Positive detector control:** supply a known-supported claim with resolvable evidence. If the verifier cannot support it, the verification path is not demonstrably functioning.
2. **Negative detector control:** supply a plausible claim for which the evidence packet contains no support, or contains decisive refutation. If the verifier supports it, the path is hallucinating, rubber-stamping, or contaminated.
3. **Answer-leakage blank:** give the same evidence and task shape but omit the author result everywhere. Seed the hidden author answer with a unique canary token. Any appearance of that token in the verifier’s prompts, tool arguments, scratch artifacts, or output is direct leakage evidence.
4. **Counterbalanced context pair:** hold evidence constant while varying an irrelevant contextual cue or a concealed author conclusion. A properly blinded verifier’s result distribution should not move materially.
5. **Tool-path blank:** run a harmless known-absent lookup through the same retrieval/tool path. Unexpected returned signal indicates cache, namespace, or state contamination.

The controls should be randomly interleaved, opaque to the worker, and scored automatically. A failed positive control invalidates a “nothing found” conclusion from that run: the detector did not prove it could find a known-present item. A failed negative/leakage control quarantines the result and captures the route for diagnosis. One clean control never certifies the entire batch.

What it catches:

- Conclusions leaked via prompts, shared memory, filenames, cached tool results, or scratch files.
- A broken search/retrieval detector that returns no results for everything.
- A verifier biased by an author’s status, stated confidence, or desired conclusion.
- Cross-task state pollution and namespace collisions.
- “Verification” implemented as a formatting pass rather than evidence examination.

**Expected value — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: the positive/negative-control logic is mature in forensic analytical work, and unique canary recovery gives a mechanically direct leakage signal. The analogy is weaker for semantic contamination, where the leaked information may alter reasoning without leaving a recoverable token. That residual sensitivity is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with paired randomized context variants and estimate the effect on verdicts, evidence selection, and confidence.**

## 5. Integrated design: a calibrated claim-verification lane

#### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: Combine A–C as a risk-sampled verification lane — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

The mechanisms reinforce one another:

```text
author receives task + evidence
        |
        v
author emits atomic claims + evidence refs ----> commit hash
        |
        v
risk sampler / hidden-test injector
        |
        +--> optional positive, negative, or leakage control
        |
        v
clean-context verifier receives task/evidence, not author answer
        |
        v
verifier reproduces/checks claim ----> commit hash
        |
        v
broker reveals + deterministic compare
        |
        +--> agreement: corroborated, confidence bounded by proficiency calibration
        +--> disagreement: fresh adjudication or direct machine oracle
        +--> control failure: quarantine result/path
        +--> proficiency case: score against sealed key
```

Use deterministic oracles before agent judgment: parse files, compare hashes, execute tests in a controlled environment, validate schemas, query an authoritative API, or check cited spans. The blind verifier is reserved for entailment, synthesis, and ambiguous evidence. Selection should be risk-based plus random sampling: verify all irreversible/high-impact claims; randomly sample mundane ones so authors cannot predict the audit boundary; increase sampling for claim classes whose hidden-test performance degrades.

The broker’s output language must remain epistemically honest:

- `MACHINE_CONFIRMED`: a specified deterministic predicate held on a captured snapshot.
- `INDEPENDENTLY_CORROBORATED`: a blinded path agreed and cited evidence, with the applicable proficiency interval.
- `DISPUTED`: committed analyses disagree.
- `INSUFFICIENT`: available evidence does not decide the claim.
- `CONTROL_INVALID`: a positive, negative, or leakage control failed.
- `STALE`: the evidence exceeds the claim’s freshness budget.

Never emit a context-free `VERIFIED=true`. That collapses different evidentiary strengths and invites the exact overclaim the system exists to prevent.

**Expected end-to-end value — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: each component has a distinct function—independence, calibration, or contamination detection—and all can be implemented on existing buses. It is not rated higher because their joint agent implementation, leakage resistance, and economics are **UNVERIFIED; the §8 trial is the experiment that settles whether the integrated lane beats simpler deterministic checks plus random human audit.**

## 6. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 6.1 Structural correspondences that do transfer

1. **Both domains convert ambiguous material into consequential propositions.** A forensic examiner interprets traces; an agent interprets files, tool output, sources, and requirements. In both, irrelevant context and a prior conclusion can steer feature search and interpretation.
2. **Both have inspectable process artifacts.** Evidence items, notes, instrument results, and reports correspond to immutable inputs, tool transcripts, cited spans, and claim records. This makes staged disclosure and commit/reveal technically possible.
3. **Both can construct some known-answer cases.** Manufactured forensic samples correspond to seeded repositories, frozen web snapshots, controlled command outputs, and deliberately supported/refuted claims.
4. **Both have an ordinary-work/test gap.** A worker or model can behave differently when it recognizes evaluation. Embedded blind cases are designed specifically for this gap.
5. **Both face contamination.** Physical carryover has a close procedural analogue in answer leakage through context, cache, shared state, or artifacts. Known-absent controls can reveal both.

### 6.2 Disanalogies that can make the borrow fail

1. **Agent errors can be far more correlated.** Two human examiners have different histories and moment-to-moment perception. Two instances of one model may have identical weights, system prompts, retrieval ranking, and tool wrappers. Temperature or a different random seed is not meaningful independence. Agreement may only replay the same learned misconception.

2. **Digital evidence is copyable but the environment is not necessarily stable.** A hash-stable file is easier to replicate than a physical trace; a web page, API, build cache, dependency resolver, or live database may change between author and verifier. Blindness without snapshot equivalence confounds different evidence with different judgment.

3. **A model can recognize synthetic tests.** Benchmark-like phrasing, clean fixture names, repeated schemas, or improbable task distributions can disclose ghost cases. Forensic programs also struggle to make mock evidence indistinguishable, but language models may exploit subtler generator fingerprints at scale.

4. **The verifier may inherit the author’s evidence selection.** If it sees only sources chosen by the author, it can independently interpret a biased packet yet miss withheld contrary evidence. True independence sometimes requires independent retrieval, not merely independent reading.

5. **Many agent claims have no cheap stable ground truth.** “This is the best architecture,” causal diagnoses, future outcomes, and normative tradeoffs cannot be keyed like a DNA mixture. Proficiency calibration transfers only within a defensible claim class and task distribution.

6. **Contamination is partly semantic and unobservable.** A leaked answer need not reproduce a canary token; it may change attention or search strategy. Controls estimate leakage but cannot certify its absence.

7. **Adjudication can reintroduce anchoring.** Once two answers are revealed, a third agent given the full transcript may defer to the majority or the more confident style. The adjudicator must either receive a fresh blind packet or rely on an external oracle.

8. **Operational incentives differ.** A lab proficiency program can attach professional accountability to individuals. Stateless or frequently upgraded agents do not learn from a score unless the harness changes routing, prompts, tools, or selection policy in response.

### 6.3 Verdict on the analogy

The analogy survives if and only if the harness treats it as **experimental quality control**, not as ceremony. Blindness makes a second result less anchored; hidden ground-truth cases measure whether that result detects errors; controls test whether the measurement path is working. Remove the proficiency layer and “two agents agree” becomes an uncalibrated social proof. Remove blindness and the second agent becomes a likely echo. Remove controls and a zero-finding report may mean either “clean” or “detector broken.”

**This conditional verdict is very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: the forensic sources distinguish these functions, the field study shows open versus blind review behaves differently, and NIJ explicitly limits what clean controls imply. The remaining uncertainty is whether diverse agent paths achieve enough practical independence; **UNVERIFIED, settled by measuring joint failures and conditional error correlation in §8 rather than by assuming model labels imply independence.**

## 7. Independence requirements and economical routing

Blindness and independence are orthogonal. A verifier can be blind yet use the same flawed method. For higher-risk claims, vary at least one causal error source:

- Different model family or provider, when permitted.
- Different retrieval engine/source set.
- Direct execution versus prose inspection.
- Bottom-up file search versus author-supplied evidence list.
- Primary-source lookup versus secondary-source synthesis.
- Static analysis versus runtime test.

Do not demand maximal diversity for every claim. First use the cheapest deterministic oracle. Then use a blind same-path verifier for moderate risk and a blind diverse-path verifier for high risk or historically correlated claim classes. Measure **conditional joint error** on proficiency cases: `P(verifier wrong | author wrong, claim class)`. Marginal accuracy alone does not reveal whether verification adds value.

**It is likely (55-80%) that method diversity will matter more than sampling diversity within one model.** Evidence: the disanalogy is shared weights and tools, while independent execution or primary-source retrieval changes the error-generating process. This ranking is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by factorially varying model family, prompt, evidence packet, and tool path on the same sealed cases and comparing conditional joint errors.**

## 8. Experiments that settle the UNVERIFIED premises

### 8.1 Preregistered shadow trial

#### Proposal E — UNVERIFIED: Four-arm verification trial — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Before changing production acceptance decisions, replay or inject a stratified case set across four arms:

1. Current/no second review.
2. Nonblind verifier sees author answer and evidence.
3. Blind verifier, same model/tool path.
4. Blind verifier, diverse model or independent tool/evidence path.

Use concealed seeded errors and correct claims in every claim class. Include `INSUFFICIENT` cases. Lock the scoring key before runs. Randomize case-to-arm assignment, counterbalance presentation order, and prevent cross-arm artifact access. Primary endpoints:

- Seeded-error detection sensitivity.
- Correct-claim specificity.
- Undetected joint error.
- Correct abstention.
- Repair after disagreement.
- Cost and latency per additional caught error.

Report binomial intervals and claim-class strata; do not aggregate away rare high-impact false support. Use real claims whose truth later becomes mechanically observable as an external-validity set. Do not tune on the final test set.

Adopt blind verification only if it improves seeded-error detection over nonblind review without an unacceptable increase in false objections, and adopt diverse-path routing only where its incremental caught-error value justifies cost.

**The trial is very likely (80-90%) to discriminate rubber-stamp review from useful review** because the key is concealed and includes both false and true claims. It is only **likely (55-80%) to predict long-run owner-work performance** because synthetic/selected cases can differ from future tasks. Evidence for this limitation is the latent-print study showing declared proficiency materials can be easier and cleaner than casework. The external-validity set is the settlement mechanism.

### 8.2 Leakage and test-detectability trial

Plant distinct random canaries in:

- author-only prompt,
- author output,
- scratch artifact,
- filename/path,
- tool cache/result,
- broker metadata,
- hidden key.

Give the verifier no legitimate need for any token. Search both verifier transcript and tool arguments for exact recovery, then use paired answer/context variants to measure semantic influence without token recovery. Separately ask an adversarial classifier to distinguish ghost cases from ordinary cases using only the worker-visible packet; if it beats chance on a held-out set, repair the generator and task distribution.

**Exact-token canaries are almost certain (90-99%) to detect direct textual leakage when the leaked token appears in recorded channels.** Evidence: exact matching against a high-entropy token is mechanically decisive; the remaining 1-10% covers logging gaps, normalization, and unrecorded channels. **They are unlikely (20-45%) to detect all semantic leakage** because an answer can influence reasoning without token reproduction; paired randomized variants settle that residual effect.

### 8.3 Proficiency-bank validation

Have independent case constructors and key validators work under role separation. For deterministic cases, regenerate the key from a script and content-hashed snapshot. For semantic cases, require two blinded key validators and preserve disagreement as ambiguity rather than forcing consensus. Compare ghost-case feature distributions with sampled ordinary work: prompt length, file count, tool types, failure frequency, ambiguity, evidence quality, and task source.

**It is very likely (80-90%) that an unvalidated synthetic bank will overestimate production reliability.** Evidence: the cited latent-print study found proficiency material easier, higher quality, and less case-like; benchmark generator artifacts add an agent-specific detection risk. The exact degree is **UNVERIFIED; the held-out ghost-case distinguishability test and later-observable real-claim set settle it.**

## 9. Rejected or bounded alternatives

### “Ask three agents and take the majority” — rejected

Majority voting hides correlation and provides no known truth. Three instances can amplify one shared error. It also reveals nothing about whether the detector worked on negative claims. It is inferior to one independent method plus calibration unless proficiency data demonstrates low conditional joint error.

### “Show the reviewer the answer so it can critique efficiently” — bounded to post-commit discrepancy resolution

This is useful after an independent commitment, not before. Pre-exposure makes the first conclusion an anchor and converts search into confirmation. The field study’s higher disagreement under blind review is direct evidence that exposure changes results.

### “Verify every sentence” — rejected

This is expensive and creates shallow checkbox review. Atomize externally consequential claims, use deterministic oracles first, sample routine claims unpredictably, and calibrate sampling by measured error class.

### “A clean control proves the run is clean” — rejected

NIJ explicitly warns that sporadic contamination defeats that inference. A control constrains one failure hypothesis at one point in a path; it does not certify all neighboring outputs.

### “Build a durable verification service with its own memory and workflow runtime” — rejected by the anti-overfit gate

The required operations are routing, context withholding, hashing, artifact capture, sampling, and scoring. They ride the existing bus. A second orchestration runtime or durable-execution engine adds failure surface without evidence that it improves error detection.

### “Use human review as the answer key” — bounded, not eliminated

The goal is to avoid a human reading everything, not to pretend humans are never needed. Humans should adjudicate a sampled set, high-impact disputes without deterministic oracles, and proficiency-key ambiguity. Their decisions should themselves be blinded where practicable. This concentrates attention where machines lack ground truth.

1. Define atomic claim classes and deterministic oracles.
2. Create a small sealed bank with positive, negative, stale, refuted, and insufficient-evidence cases.
3. Run the four-arm shadow trial; do not affect owner decisions yet.
4. Measure leakage and ghost-case detectability.
5. Enable blind commit/reveal only for claim classes with demonstrated incremental detection.
6. Add risk-based plus random sampling, with intervals visible in reports.
7. Revalidate after model, prompt, router, or tool changes; rotate exposed cases.

Stop or redesign when any of these holds:

- Blind and nonblind arms have indistinguishable seeded-error detection at adequately powered sample sizes.
- `P(verifier wrong | author wrong)` remains too high to justify cost.
- Ghost cases are reliably distinguishable from ordinary work.
- Leakage controls fail and no clean isolation boundary is available.
- False objections cause more owner harm than caught errors avert.
- Claimed calibration is transported to a task class not represented in the bank.

**This staged adoption is very likely (80-90%) to prevent ceremonial rollout of a useless verifier.** Evidence: it requires prospective comparative performance and explicit stop conditions before production reliance. It is not almost certain because flawed keys or an unrepresentative bank can still validate the wrong thing; independent key construction and the later-observable real-claim set are the countermeasures.

## 11. Direct answer to “what would this catch that we currently miss?”

Relative to a generic workflow that asks an author to self-check or shows a reviewer the answer, the borrow is designed to expose:

- **Echoed mistakes:** disagreement becomes observable before social anchoring suppresses it.
- **Broken detectors disguised as clean results:** positive controls prove the path can find a known-present target.
- **Hallucinating or contaminated detectors:** negative controls and blanks show support appearing where none exists.
- **Context leakage:** canary and counterbalanced controls expose answer transfer into the verifier.
- **System failures outside the final reasoning step:** blind ghost cases traverse routing, tools, evidence capture, verification, and reporting.
- **Shared author/verifier failure:** hidden ground truth scores agreement that is nevertheless wrong.
- **Distribution-specific weakness:** stratified rates reveal, for example, that local file claims may be reliable while negative or temporal claims are not.
- **Evaluation theater:** workers cannot selectively improve only declared test behavior if hidden cases are genuinely indistinguishable.
- **Stale truth:** freshness budgets and snapshot identities prevent yesterday’s evidence from silently certifying today’s claim.
- **Unwarranted certainty:** `INSUFFICIENT` cases measure whether agents abstain when the evidence cannot decide.

**The harness will likely (55-80%) catch more of these failure classes than nonblind prose review, conditional on clean isolation and a representative proficiency bank.** Evidence: the mechanisms separately target anchoring, detector failure, contamination, and operational calibration. This is **UNVERIFIED as an integrated agent system; the four-arm, leakage, and external-validity experiments above are the named settlement tests.**

## 12. Gaps and evidentiary limits

- I found direct forensic evidence that blind and nonblind review yield different disagreement behavior, but that study lacked known ground truth; it does not by itself prove blind review improves accuracy. The proficiency trial must measure accuracy directly.
- The operational blind-testing papers establish feasibility in particular laboratories, not universal cost-effectiveness for agent harnesses.
- I found no cited direct experiment in this unit measuring same-model versus cross-model blinded verification on realistic agent claims. Any recommendation depending on model diversity is marked UNVERIFIED above.
- Forensic “double blind” terminology is not standardized across all subfields. I therefore translated role-separated knowledge rather than claiming a universal double-blind laboratory protocol.
- Controls sample failure modes; they never prove global absence of contamination. This limit is part of the design, not a trailing caveat.
- This unit intentionally did not ground proposals in the local repository, settings, or existing hooks. BUILD-SMALL line estimates describe conceptual scope, not a verified fit to a particular codebase.
