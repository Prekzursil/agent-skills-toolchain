# X173 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → agent-claim verification

**Unit:** X173  
**Lens:** experimental design (preregistration, power analysis, blinding, and negative controls) applied to verification  
**Scope:** ideation only. Per the unit brief, this analysis uses external methodological sources and does not inspect or make claims about the local harness repository.

## Executive judgment

The borrow transfers, but only in a bounded form. The transferable object is not “run an experiment on every answer.” It is a **prospectively committed, independently measured claim protocol**: define the claim and pass/fail rule before looking at the favorable evidence; prevent the assessor from seeing information that can bias judgment; force the measurement path to demonstrate specificity and sensitivity with controls; and, when exhaustive checking is impossible, choose a random sample large enough to give a declared probability of detecting a declared defect rate.

My transfer judgment is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the structural match between (a) outcome switching/selective analysis in research and (b) an agent choosing a convenient test or interpretation after seeing its own output; the empirical observation that blinded outcome assessment reduces judgment bias; and the direct applicability of canary controls to tool-based detectors. The remaining uncertainty is material: an agent is adaptive and potentially adversarial, software defects are often clustered rather than independently distributed, and many software claims are universal propositions for which sampling cannot constitute proof. The settling experiment is the preregistered seeded-defect evaluation in “Evaluation plan” below.

The central recommendation is therefore a small verification layer on the existing hook/skill/tool bus, not another orchestration runtime. It should produce a machine-readable **claim contract**, seal it before evidence collection, collect evidence independently of the producing agent, run positive and negative controls through the same detector path, and return `VERIFIED`, `FALSIFIED`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, or `NOT-VERIFIABLE` with a verification scope. A model-written verdict alone is never sufficient evidence.

This design passes the anti-overfit gate: every component below is either configuration or a small hook/skill, and none requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. What experimental design has already solved

### 1.1 Prospective registration: prevent the result from silently redefining the question

**Mechanism in its own field.** Preregistration records hypotheses, methods, analyses, and decision criteria before data collection or analysis. OSF defines it as a time-stamped, read-only study plan posted before collection or analysis; a registration is frozen and cannot be edited or deleted, though withdrawal leaves metadata behind ([OSF registration documentation](https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations)). The important causal feature is temporal ordering: the analyst cannot present a post-result choice as if it were the original confirmatory plan. OSF explicitly describes predefined plans as reducing undisclosed modification and selective reporting ([OSF benefits and concerns](https://help.osf.io/article/603-benefits-of-registration)).

Registered Reports strengthen this mechanism with two-stage review. Methods and proposed analyses are reviewed before data are known; Stage 2 checks adherence, while publication does not depend on the direction or significance of results ([Scientific Reports registered-report policy](https://www.nature.com/srep/journal-policies/registered-reports)). This changes the incentive from “find an attractive result” to “execute an adequate test.” It does not prohibit exploration; it makes post hoc analysis visibly exploratory.

This is a response to a measured problem, not merely methodological taste. In a cohort comparison of trial protocols with published articles, 40 of 76 trials changed a protocol-defined primary outcome in some way, and 21 of 63 introduced a new primary outcome in the publication ([JAMA protocol/publication comparison](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/198809)). A systematic review later reported a median 41% discrepancy rate between prospectively registered and published outcomes across six studies that preserved the relevant registration time point ([systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4650202/)).

**What the mechanism actually warrants.** Registration makes deviations observable. It does not make the registered hypothesis sensible, the measurement valid, the analysis correct, or the data genuine. Vague registration can preserve almost all researcher degrees of freedom. Registered Reports add ex-ante review, but they also add cost and are explicitly not a universal solution ([Chambers and Tzavella review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01193-7)).

### 1.2 Blinded outcome assessment: separate production from judgment

**Mechanism in its own field.** An outcome assessor is denied treatment allocation so knowledge of the preferred or experimental condition cannot influence a subjective endpoint. The aim is to prevent systematic differences in measurement between groups, not merely to conceal identities. Blinding the final assessor is insufficient if the evidence packet was prepared by an unblinded person; biased source summaries can transmit allocation information or omit inconvenient facts ([open-label trial design analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247637/)).

The effect is empirically visible. A 2025 meta-epidemiological analysis of 66 trials with blinded and non-blinded assessments found that non-blinded assessors exaggerated odds-ratio effect estimates by 29% on average for the pooled binary/scale analysis ([PubMed abstract and DOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40258524/)). An earlier paired-assessment review found an average 36% exaggeration for subjective binary outcomes, even though blinded and non-blinded assessors had median agreement of 78%; only a small fraction of classifications had to move to bias the result ([BMJ/PubMed review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22371859/)). This matters for agent verification because high headline agreement between a producer and a reviewer does not demonstrate an unbiased acceptance boundary.

**What the mechanism actually warrants.** Blinding primarily addresses judgment-dependent outcomes. It adds little to an exact hash comparison, a parser exit status, or a deterministic test oracle. It can also fail if filenames, prose style, stack traces, or solution structure reveal the producer or expected answer. Blinding must therefore be tested, not asserted.

### 1.3 Negative controls: make hidden bias produce an observable contradiction

**Mechanism in its own field.** A negative-control exposure or outcome shares the suspected bias path with the primary exposure/outcome but is known not to have the causal relationship under study. An association in the negative control is evidence of uncontrolled confounding, selection bias, information bias, or another invalidating path. The canonical epidemiology paper describes negative controls as a way to detect confounding and bias that would otherwise create a spurious causal inference ([Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)); the FDA uses the same definition and emphasizes that the control must share the potential source of bias while lacking the causal relation of interest ([FDA regulatory-science project](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/advancing-regulatory-science/evaluating-utility-negative-controls-drug-safety-and-effectiveness-studies-using-real-world-data)).

A **positive control** is the complementary calibration: a known-present effect/item should be detected. Although the requested source lens names negative controls, verification needs both. A negative control tests specificity (“does the detector hallucinate a success?”); a positive control tests sensitivity and detector connectivity (“can this exact path find something known to be there?”). Neither control proves the focal result. They show whether the measurement path behaves coherently on nearby cases.

**What the mechanism actually warrants.** A failed negative control diagnoses that the focal inference is unsafe; it does not uniquely identify the bias or quantify the correct answer. A passing negative control is only useful if it genuinely shares the focal bias path. A decorative canary checked by different code provides false reassurance.

### 1.4 Prospective power analysis: quantify what “we sampled enough” means

**Mechanism in its own field.** Power is the probability that a specified design will reject the null under a specified alternative. Prospective sample-size calculation fixes the Type I error, desired power, effect size worth detecting, outcome distribution, and analysis design before observing results. NIH’s calculators require the Type I error, desired power, outcome assumptions, and design/analysis plan ([NIH Research Methods Resources](https://researchmethodsresources.nih.gov/Calculators)); CONSORT requires authors to report how sample size was determined and to identify the quantities used in a formal power calculation ([CONSORT explanation](https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/340/bmj.c869.full.pdf)).

Power analysis does not rescue a biased measurement, a wrong model, a post hoc endpoint, or a non-random sample. “80% power” is conditional on the stated effect and assumptions; it is not an 80% probability that a reported conclusion is true.

For verification sampling, a particularly transparent special case is useful. If each sampled unit is independently defective with prevalence at least `p`, then the chance of seeing zero defects in `n` random draws is `(1-p)^n`. To have probability `1-β` of detecting at least one defect, choose:

`n >= ceil(log(β) / log(1-p))`.

Thus 59 random units give at least 95% probability of detecting defects when the defect prevalence is at least 5%, under the independence/random-sampling assumptions; 299 units are needed for a 1% defect prevalence at the same power. These numbers are not valid when defects cluster, selection is non-random, the producer can predict the sample, or the enumerated population excludes the failure region.

## 2. The concrete translation: a prospective claim-verification protocol

### 2.1 Proposal — UNVERIFIED: sealed claim contracts

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Before a producing agent sees verification results, require a compact claim contract for every completion claim that matters. The contract is append-only or content-addressed and contains:

1. `claim_id` and the exact proposition, avoiding “looks good” or “should work.”
2. `claim_class`: exact state, absence/universal, behavioral, quantitative/rate, external/freshness, or judgment.
3. The population or scope: files, tests, routes, platforms, time window, or records covered.
4. The operational endpoint and pass/fail/inconclusive rule.
5. Admissible evidence sources and prohibited self-attestation.
6. The independent evidence collector and deterministic oracle, if available.
7. Required positive and negative controls.
8. Exhaustive versus sampled verification; for sampling, the sampling frame, randomization point, minimum defect rate of interest, target power, and sample size.
9. Known confounders, expected failure modes, and planned handling of missing evidence.
10. A hash of the task specification, artifact/tree state, and contract, plus a timestamp.
11. Any amendments, retained as a visible chain rather than overwriting the original.

The harness, not the producer, should seal the contract and later compare executed checks with planned checks. Deviations are not automatically failures: the verifier labels their evidence `POST-HOC`, records why the plan changed, and prevents it from silently replacing the prospective result.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this would expose some claim drift, based on the direct structural equivalence to registered-versus-published outcome discrepancies and on the fact that the contract makes test substitution mechanically diffable. This effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by randomly assigning seeded coding tasks to baseline versus sealed-contract verification and measuring undisclosed endpoint substitution from event logs**. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to reduce false acceptance overall, because a sealed bad oracle remains bad; this effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the same trial’s seeded-defect false-acceptance endpoint**.

**What it catches that ordinary post-hoc review misses:** replacing the requested full suite with a convenient subset; claiming “fixed” after selecting only a favorable reproduction; changing a latency threshold after seeing measurements; quietly narrowing “all files” to files already inspected; treating a newly invented check as if it were the original acceptance criterion; and omitting a failed planned check from the final narrative.

### 2.2 Proposal — UNVERIFIED: independently collected, outcome-blinded evidence packets

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Split roles:

- The **producer** creates or changes the artifact and submits the claim contract.
- The **collector** is a harness hook that runs the committed commands or reads the committed surfaces directly. It emits raw output, exit status, timestamps, hashes, environment identity, and completeness metadata. It does not accept a producer-written summary as evidence.
- A **deterministic adjudicator** evaluates exact predicates whenever possible.
- A **blinded model adjudicator** is used only for irreducibly semantic predicates. It receives the task, rubric, and collector packet, but not the producer’s “done” statement, confidence, chain of thought, preferred conclusion, identity, or cherry-picked explanation. Candidate labels and ordering are randomized for comparative judgments.
- An **unblinding audit** asks the adjudicator, before outcome scoring, to guess candidate identity or expected claim direction and state the cues used. Accuracy materially above chance flags compromised blinding.

The collector must acquire the information independently as well as the adjudicator being blind. This follows the experimental-design warning that a blinded committee cannot repair evidence summaries already biased by an unblinded preparer.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this separation would reduce acceptance caused by persuasive completion narratives on subjective claims, based on paired blinded/non-blinded outcome-assessment evidence and the direct removal of producer-selected summaries. The effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a randomized crossover in which the same masked artifacts are adjudicated with and without producer narratives, using seeded ground truth and false-acceptance rate as the primary endpoint**. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to materially improve exact machine-checkable claims, because their oracle already dominates subjective judgment; this prediction is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by preregistered interaction analysis by claim class in that crossover**.

**What it catches:** a persuasive explanation masking a failing exit code; a producer omitting stderr or environment differences; evaluator anchoring on “all tests pass”; preference for a familiar agent/style; and semantic grading that changes when the proposed answer is visible.

### 2.3 Proposal — UNVERIFIED: paired negative and positive controls on every critical detector

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Every detector used to support a critical claim receives controls through the same invocation, permissions, parser, environment, and decision path:

- **Positive control:** a known-present item or known-failing fixture that the detector must find. For an “absence” scanner, plant or select a harmless known match in the enumerated scope. For a test runner, include a fixture known to fail when enabled. For a citation checker, include a reachable source containing a known token.
- **Negative control:** a matched item that lacks the target property but shares nuisance features. A scanner sees a similar filename without the prohibited content; a test parser sees a successful command containing the word “failed” only in inert text; a citation checker sees a page that is reachable but does not support the proposition.
- **Focal measurement:** only runs or becomes admissible after the controls establish that this exact measurement path can detect the known-present target and reject the known-absent target.
- **Failure rule:** failed control makes the focal result `INCONCLUSIVE`, never “pass.” Control output is retained beside focal output.

For absence claims, add a second mechanically independent signal. Examples are enumeration by filesystem metadata plus content search; an API inventory plus a separately generated runtime trace; or an AST query plus execution of a known counterexample. Re-running the same command or asking another model to read the same summary is not independence. Both signals must have known-present controls before an absence verdict is admissible.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that same-path controls would catch disconnected tools, parser polarity errors, empty search universes, and permission-induced zero results, because each failure makes a known-present control disappear or a known-absent control appear. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by fault injection at the transport, permission, enumeration, parser, and decision layers and compare detection rates with controls enabled versus disabled**. It is only **likely (55-80%)** that the initial hand-authored control library will share all relevant bias paths, because poorly matched controls are a known limitation of negative-control reasoning; this premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it through mutation testing that injects faults outside the initial controls and measures escape rate**.

**What it catches:** “zero matches” caused by an empty or truncated corpus; a dead connector interpreted as no remote results; a parser that treats skipped tests as passed; a verifier that merely checks command completion; stale cached evidence; citation reachability mistaken for entailment; and two nominal verifiers that depend on the same broken substrate.

### 2.4 Proposal — UNVERIFIED: powered random audit only for genuinely statistical claims

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Use sampling only when exhaustive checking is impossible or disproportionate and the claim is explicitly statistical. The contract must freeze the complete sampling frame before the harness draws a sample using randomness unavailable to the producer. It must declare the minimum defect prevalence worth detecting, desired detection probability, clustering assumptions, strata, missing-unit rule, and stopping rule. The verdict must say what was tested:

> `No sampled failures; n=59 random units from frame N; design had ≥95% probability to detect at least one defect if independently distributed defect prevalence was ≥5%. This does not verify zero defects.`

Stratify by failure surface—platform, tool, file type, route, dependency boundary, or risk tier—when defects are plausibly heterogeneous. If units share a generator or component, treat them as clusters or use a conservative effective sample size. Never apply the simple binomial formula while calling correlated outputs independent.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that hidden post-commit random sampling would detect broadly distributed defects more efficiently than human spot-checking, based on probability sampling and the explicit binomial detection calculation. The harness effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by seeding defects under uniform, clustered, boundary-only, and adversarial placements, then measuring empirical detection against the nominal power curve**. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to provide defensible assurance for rare, boundary-localized, or adversarially placed failures without stratification, because those placements violate the sampling model; this prediction is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the same placement experiment**.

**What it catches:** a nontrivial rate of malformed generated files, broken links, incorrect migrated records, or inconsistent repeated transformations without requiring a human to inspect the whole population. **What it does not catch:** a single strategically hidden backdoor, a defect outside the enumerated frame, or a universal “none exist” claim.

### 2.5 Proposal — UNVERIFIED: two-stage verification for high-risk tasks, not every task

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Borrow the Registered Report’s Stage 1/Stage 2 division selectively:

- **Stage 1, before implementation or before final evidence:** for high-risk or expensive tasks, validate that the claim is testable, the oracle is relevant, controls are matched, and the sample has adequate power. This is a protocol review, not a solution review.
- **Stage 2, after production:** execute the sealed protocol, expose deviations, inspect control behavior, and adjudicate the result independent of whether it is favorable.
- **Routing:** invoke Stage 1 only for security, destructive migration, release, broad absence, expensive batch transformation, benchmark, and external factual claims above a configurable impact threshold. Low-risk exact checks use a predefined contract template with no separate review turn.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that selective Stage 1 review would find inadequate verification designs before expensive work is complete, based on Registered Reports’ ex-ante method review and the mechanical opportunity to repair an oracle before results exist. This benefit is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by randomizing high-risk benchmark tasks to ex-ante protocol review versus post-hoc verification and measuring oracle defects found, false acceptance, latency, and token cost**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that applying full two-stage review to every trivial task would add unjustified friction, based on the fixed extra review step and the availability of deterministic low-cost oracles; the exact cost is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring latency and token overhead by risk tier in the same evaluation**.

## 3. UNVERIFIED proposal: claim classes and the correct strength of verdict

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

| Claim class | Appropriate design | Admissible conclusion | Common overclaim blocked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact current state: “file hash is X,” “parser exits 0” | Independent direct read/run; deterministic oracle; provenance | Verified for the identified artifact and environment at time `t` | “The feature works everywhere” |
| Absence/universal: “no references remain” | Complete, validated enumeration; two mechanically independent detectors; known-present controls | Verified only over the enumerated scope | “Nothing exists anywhere” from one zero-result search |
| Behavioral: “bug is fixed” | Sealed reproducer, negative precondition where feasible, post-change test, regression test, environment identity | Reproducer no longer fails and regression test passes in stated environments | “Root cause eliminated in all conditions” |
| Quantitative/rate: “<1% malformed” | Prospective random/stratified sample, declared estimator and interval, adequate power/precision | Estimated rate with interval and population definition | “All outputs are valid” |
| External/freshness: “service currently supports X” | Primary source/API, timestamp, independent corroboration where material, stale-cache negative control | Supported as of time `t` by named sources | Timeless truth from stale documentation |
| Semantic/judgment: “answer satisfies the brief” | Blinded rubric adjudication, randomized order, raw evidence access, disagreement retained | Assessor agreement/disagreement under named rubric | Objective truth from one model’s preference |

The harness should reserve `VERIFIED` for the proposition actually operationalized. A broader prose claim than the measured endpoint is `NOT-VERIFIABLE` until narrowed. Missing or contradictory evidence is `INCONCLUSIVE`, not failure and not success. A failed predicate is `FALSIFIED` within its stated scope.

## 4. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 4.1 Where the structure genuinely transfers

1. **Researcher degrees of freedom ↔ verifier degrees of freedom.** Both systems can choose endpoints, subsets, exclusions, and interpretations after seeing results. A sealed protocol exposes that temporal leakage.
2. **Observer expectancy ↔ completion anchoring.** A reviewer who sees the producing agent’s confident conclusion can move borderline judgments. Blinded assessment directly targets that path.
3. **Hidden confounding ↔ broken measurement substrate.** A zero-result detector can reflect absence, disconnection, lack of permission, bad enumeration, or parser failure. Same-path controls make several of those alternatives observable.
4. **Finite sample sensitivity ↔ partial artifact inspection.** When a claim concerns an error rate in a large repeated population, power and randomization precisely characterize what sampling can and cannot detect.
5. **Publication bias ↔ selective evidence reporting.** If only successful checks appear in the final response, the consumer sees a biased sample. A sealed list plus append-only results retains failures and deviations.

These are mechanistic rather than metaphorical correspondences: in each pair, the borrowed intervention blocks the same information path or selection freedom.

### 4.2 Where the analogy breaks

1. **Agents are adaptive optimizers, not passive treatments.** If the producer knows the control fixtures or sampling seed, it can overfit or place defects elsewhere. The design needs post-commit randomness and rotating controls. This converts a static scientific protocol into an adversarial audit protocol.
2. **Software often has exact oracles.** Statistical inference is inferior to exhaustive deterministic checks when the state is enumerable. Power language must not decorate a claim that could have been proven directly.
3. **Many claims are universal.** Failure to observe a counterexample is not proof of absence unless enumeration is complete. Sampling can bound a prevalence under assumptions, not establish zero defects.
4. **Error distributions are clustered.** Generated artifacts share templates, dependencies, and platforms. Independent Bernoulli assumptions are often false; naive power calculations can substantially overstate detection probability.
5. **The harness is part of the trusted computing base.** Experimental design does not solve a compromised collector, falsified logs, or a malicious environment. Independent provenance and controls reduce required trust but do not eliminate it.
6. **Blinding can remove diagnostically necessary context.** It is **likely (55-80%)** that some maintainability or semantic judgments require design intent, based on those predicates depending on intended use rather than artifact state alone. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by comparing blinded and staged-unblinded adjudication against an independently authored intent rubric on semantic benchmark tasks**. The remedy is staged unblinding: score the predefined endpoint first, then reveal rationale for diagnosis, without retroactively changing the primary verdict.
7. **Preregistration can ossify a mistaken test.** Agents often discover legitimate new information during work. Amendments must be allowed, time-stamped, and reported as deviations. “Plan, not prison” is a core limitation acknowledged by OSF ([OSF benefits and concerns](https://help.osf.io/article/603-benefits-of-registration)).
8. **Controls can be gamed or poorly matched.** A canary that uses a different backend proves little. Control construction is itself a design problem and requires fault-injection validation.

The analogy therefore does **not** support mandatory clinical-trial ceremony, p-values for deterministic tests, or claims that an independent model is inherently trustworthy. It supports prospective commitment, information separation, calibrated measurement, and quantified sampling limits.

## 5. UNVERIFIED proposal: minimal end-to-end mechanism

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The smallest useful flow is:

1. **Classify claim.** Exact, absence, behavioral, quantitative, external, or judgment.
2. **Instantiate contract.** Choose a short versioned template for that class; fill scope, endpoint, evidence, controls, and verdict rule.
3. **Seal.** Harness records contract hash plus artifact/task state. Producer cannot silently overwrite it.
4. **Commit artifact.** “Commit” here means freeze a content hash for verification, not perform a Git commit.
5. **Draw hidden sample if needed.** Harness samples after freeze; no sampling for exact enumerable predicates.
6. **Run controls and focal collection.** Same execution path; raw provenance retained. Controls failing yields `INCONCLUSIVE`.
7. **Adjudicate deterministically first.** A model only handles residual semantics and is blinded to producer advocacy.
8. **Compare plan to execution.** Emit all omitted, added, changed, failed, and post-hoc checks.
9. **Scope verdict.** State artifact/environment/time/population; never widen beyond evidence.
10. **Optionally unblind for diagnosis.** Explanation can guide repair but cannot rewrite the prospective primary result.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the implementation footprint is one claim-contract schema, one sealing/result hook, class-specific verification templates, and a small control-fixture directory, based on the ten-step flow requiring only content addressing, tool execution, and result classification. That footprint estimate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by implementing a spike for three claim classes and counting non-generated lines and integration points**. If the spike exceeds roughly 200 lines because the existing bus lacks content hashing or raw tool provenance, the proposal must be reclassified rather than smuggling in a `BUILD-LARGE` service.

## 6. UNVERIFIED proposal: evaluation plan that decides whether to keep the borrow

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The harness should not adopt the mechanism on analogy alone. Run a preregistered, blinded, powered evaluation of the verification design itself.

### 6.1 Population and task construction

Construct a benchmark across the six claim classes above. Include clean tasks and seeded defects with machine-known ground truth. Seed at least these defect families: wrong scope, omitted failed check, disconnected detector, empty enumeration, parser polarity inversion, stale evidence, environment mismatch, clustered generation defect, single boundary defect, unsupported citation, persuasive-but-false producer narrative, and compromised blinding cue.

Ground-truth constructors must not adjudicate outcomes. Producers and adjudicators must not see defect labels. Artifact labels/order are randomized. Sampling seeds are generated only after artifacts and contracts are sealed.

### 6.2 Arms

- **Control:** ordinary producer completion plus post-hoc reviewer.
- **Contract only:** sealed claim contract and plan/execution diff.
- **Contract + controls:** add same-path positive/negative controls and independent evidence collector.
- **Full bounded design:** add blinded semantic adjudication and powered hidden sampling where applicable.

An ablation design is preferable to only baseline-versus-bundle because it identifies which component earns its cost.

### 6.3 Preregistered endpoints

Primary endpoint: false-acceptance rate—fraction of materially false seeded claims labeled verified/done.

Secondary endpoints: false-rejection rate on clean tasks; `INCONCLUSIVE` rate; defect-family detection; undisclosed endpoint substitutions; control failures; adjudicator blinding-guess accuracy; verification latency; tokens; tool calls; human minutes required for unresolved cases; and verdict calibration by claim class.

Predefine a minimum practically important reduction in false acceptance and calculate sample size from baseline rate estimates obtained in a pilot that is excluded from the confirmatory set. Correct or hierarchically model repeated judgments on the same task. Report intervals and all arms, including null or adverse results.

### 6.4 Negative and positive controls for the evaluation itself

- Positive control: obvious seeded false claims that every competent verifier is expected to reject.
- Negative control: clean matched tasks whose focal predicate is exactly satisfied.
- Blinding control: ask adjudicators to predict arm/producer identity before scoring.
- Detector-path control: include a known-present and known-absent fixture in each collection backend.
- Randomization check: confirm assignment balance using the allocation record and an independent artifact count.

### 6.5 Decision rule

Adopt only components that reduce false acceptance by the preregistered practically important amount without crossing preregistered cost or false-rejection limits. Retain heterogeneous results: enable a component for claim classes where it clears the rule and disable it where it does not. Publish deviations and exploratory findings separately from the primary comparison.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** whether the complete bundle will beat the best ablated subset on total utility, because controls and contracts target distinct failures but full two-stage/model adjudication adds latency and its marginal value on exact claims may be near zero. This is **UNVERIFIED; the factorial/ablation evaluation above settles it**. This uncertainty is a reason to test components, not a reason to adopt the maximal bundle.

## 7. UNVERIFIED proposal: failure policy and anti-gaming details

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

1. **No self-attestation as evidence.** “I ran tests” is a claim to verify, not a signal.
2. **Raw evidence precedes summaries.** Store command, exact arguments, exit status, stdout/stderr digest, environment identity, time, and artifact hash.
3. **Controls share the path.** Same backend, permissions, enumeration, parser, and decision code.
4. **Hidden post-commit randomness.** The producing agent cannot know selected units while choosing where defects might remain.
5. **Rotating canaries.** Static public fixtures invite memorization. Keep fixture-generation rules testable while varying instances.
6. **Stage unblinding.** Preserve a primary blinded score; use revealed rationale only for secondary diagnosis.
7. **Refuse scope inflation.** A passing Linux test is not cross-platform verification; a sampled rate is not universal correctness.
8. **Expose deviations.** Allow added checks, label them post hoc, and do not let them erase a failed registered endpoint.
9. **Treat missingness as data.** Unreadable units, timeouts, and unsupported formats remain in the denominator or receive a preregistered conservative rule.
10. **Audit the auditor.** Periodic seeded faults estimate detector sensitivity; matched clean items estimate false-positive behavior.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Ask a second agent to critique the first”

Rejected as the core mechanism. It does not separate evidence acquisition from advocacy, does not freeze the endpoint, and can reproduce the same model priors or tool failure. It remains useful as a blinded adjudicator for semantic residue after deterministic evidence collection.

### Majority vote among several agents

Rejected as proof. Correlated errors violate the intuition that votes are independent, and unanimous judgments can all rest on the same missing or poisoned evidence. Voting characterizes disagreement; it does not validate a claim.

### Sample everything at a fixed percentage

Rejected. A fixed percentage has no stable meaning across population sizes or minimum defect rates, ignores clustering, and encourages universal conclusions from partial inspection. Power/precision must be tied to an explicit estimand.

### Preregister every micro-action

Rejected. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to impose disproportionate ceremony and inhibit legitimate adaptation, based on applying a fixed sealing/review step to every micro-action rather than only externally meaningful claims. This effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring latency, token cost, amendment rate, and false acceptance under claim-level versus micro-action registration on matched tasks**. Register only the externally meaningful proposition, oracle, scope, controls, and decision rule.

### BUILD-LARGE verification service

Rejected under the anti-overfit gate. A new daemon/runtime would create another failure and trust surface without evidence that the existing hook/skill/tool bus cannot seal small contracts and collect provenance. A `BUILD-LARGE` service would be reconsidered only after a small spike demonstrates a hard bus limitation and a controlled evaluation shows material benefit exceeding operational cost.

### Learned critic or trained reward model

Rejected under the anti-overfit gate. It replaces an inspectable verification protocol with another opaque claimant, requires training and drift management, and still needs ground-truth controls. No evidence in this lens justifies the added system.
