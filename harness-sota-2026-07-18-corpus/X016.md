# X016 — Cross-domain borrow: intelligence tradecraft → agent memory

**Unit:** X016  
**Lens:** IDEATION only; no local-repository or harness-subject grounding was performed, by instruction.  
**Research date:** 2026-07-18  
**Question:** What should an agent remember across sessions, when should it forget, and how should it notice that a memory has gone stale?

## Executive judgment

The analogy transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form. Intelligence tradecraft has not solved storage, retrieval, or forgetting as computer-science problems. It has solved a more useful upstream problem: how to keep a consequential judgment attached to its provenance, uncertainty, assumptions, alternatives, and observable conditions that would make it worth revisiting. That structure maps well to durable agent memory because both domains reuse claims after the evidence-producing episode has ended, under partial context, and risk turning a qualified past judgment into an unqualified present fact.

The best borrow is therefore **not** “build an intelligence-analysis system” and not “run full ACH over every memory.” It is a compact **claim dossier** on the existing memory bus:

1. admit only reusable, consequential claims;
2. preserve observation, source, interpretation, and confidence separately;
3. attach explicit invalidators (“signposts”) and a scope/volatility class;
4. quarantine contradictions instead of silently overwriting either side;
5. revalidate against authoritative state at use time when that state is cheaply available;
6. demote or forget memories based on invalidation, loss of utility, or supersession—not merely elapsed time.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch a class of failures that plain text memory misses: stale paths and commands, superseded owner preferences, conclusions whose caveats have been dropped, circular “corroboration” copied across summaries, and conflicts that last-write-wins storage hides. Evidence for the mechanism is the direct structural match described below plus doctrine that explicitly requires attention to source age/currency, contrary information, source quality, assumptions, and indicators of change (ODNI ICD 203, pp. 3–5; UK JDP 2-00, pp. 67–70; CIA Tradecraft Primer, pp. 8–18). The harness-specific effect size is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the shadow-mode replay experiment in §9**.

The strongest negative judgment is equally important: full ACH as a default memory algorithm does **not** transfer. A 2024 experiment found that the conventional ACH matrix orientation did not reduce confirmation bias or improve sensitivity to evidence credibility, while a transposed layout did reduce confirmation bias in that task (Dhami et al., 2024). Agent memories are also often nonexclusive, compositional, and directly checkable; ACH assumes a bounded analytic question, a plausible hypothesis set, and evidence evaluated against alternatives. Full-matrix ACH would add ceremony, token cost, and false precision. Retain only its contradiction ledger, diagnostic-evidence idea, sensitivity question, and future indicators for high-impact disputes.

## 1. What intelligence tradecraft solved in its own field

### 1.1 Separate the reliability of the source from the credibility of this report

The NATO Intelligence Grading System, historically called the Admiralty Code, expresses two judgments independently. UK Joint Doctrine Publication 2-00 (JDP 2-00), 4th edition, describes source reliability from A (“completely reliable”) through F (“reliability cannot be judged”) and information credibility from 1 (“confirmed by other sources”) through 6 (“truth cannot be judged”). It explicitly allows combinations such as B5 and E1: a usually reliable source can make an improbable report, while an unreliable source can report something independently confirmed. An unknown grade does not make an item useless; it means the analyst must evaluate it in context. JDP 2-00 also says both judgments should be reviewed when later intelligence or deeper expertise changes the context (paras. 3.39–3.40, pp. 67–69).

Source: UK Ministry of Defence, [Joint Doctrine Publication 2-00, *Understanding and Intelligence Support to Joint Operations*, 4th ed.](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653a4b0780884d0013f71bb0/JDP_2_00_Ed_4_web.pdf), especially pp. 67–70.

The mechanism matters because “who said it?” and “is this particular claim true?” are different variables. A trusted analyst can be wrong about a newly encountered domain; a noisy log or low-status source can still contain a directly verifiable fact. JDP 2-00 grounds source reliability in factors such as access, expertise, motivation, technical capability, and reporting history, while report credibility can be assessed internally for factual/logical defects and externally against other information. It further requires continuing review of source evaluations.

ODNI’s analytic standard makes the temporal link explicit: source descriptions can include “age and continued currency of information,” along with completeness, deception, access, validation, motivation, bias, and expertise (ICD 203 §D.6.e(1), pp. 3–4).

Source: Office of the Director of National Intelligence, [ICD 203, *Analytic Standards*](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf), especially pp. 3–5.

### 1.2 Distinguish fact, estimate, and uncertainty; do not let prose erase the difference

Sherman Kent’s “Words of Estimative Probability” distinguishes an observed/knowable fact from an evidence-based but uncertain judgment and from a forecast about an unknowable future choice. His central operational concern is that probability words must convey stable meanings to producer and consumer; otherwise the consumer reads more or less certainty than the analyst intended. Kent proposed a limited vocabulary tied to odds ranges rather than a profusion of ambiguous synonyms.

Source: Sherman Kent, [“Words of Estimative Probability,” *Studies in Intelligence* 8(4)](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf), especially pp. 1–10.

Modern ICD 203 adds an essential distinction that must not be collapsed into a single confidence number: likelihood is the assessed probability of an event or development, while analytic confidence concerns the logic and evidentiary base supporting the judgment. It calls for the causes of uncertainty—such as currency, amount and type of information, knowledge gaps, and issue characteristics—to be explained (§D.6.e(2), pp. 4–5). In other words, a low-probability event can be assessed with high confidence, and a likely event can rest on weak evidence.

This is directly relevant to memory: “the owner prefers X” is not the same data type as “the owner said X once,” and neither is the same as “I infer X from repeated choices.” A flat natural-language note destroys that distinction.

### 1.3 Externalize key assumptions, especially for long-standing judgments built on aging information

The CIA’s Key Assumptions Check asks the analyst to write down the current analytic line, articulate stated and unstated premises necessary for it to hold, challenge why each must be true, and identify conditions or information that would make it fail. The CIA primer explicitly asks whether an assumption could have been true in the past but be less true now. Its Quality of Information Check warns that judgments can become anchored to weak information while old caveats are forgotten, and calls for periodic review of the information base (pp. 8–10).

Source: US Central Intelligence Agency, [*A Tradecraft Primer: Structured Analytic Techniques for Improving Intelligence Analysis*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf), especially pp. 8–18.

A concise CIA explanation says analysts use Key Assumptions Checks when a judgment is long-standing or rests on a dwindling or aging information pool. That is almost exactly the stale-memory condition, stated in intelligence terms.

Source: CIA, [“Ask Molly: SATs Advice”](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-sats-advice/), Key Assumptions Check and Signposts and Indicators sections.

### 1.4 Specify indicators/signposts before the change becomes convenient to explain away

Indicators or signposts are observable events or trends expected under a scenario. Analysts create lists for competing scenarios, review them periodically, and note which are changing. The CIA primer recommends both positive and negative indicator lists, because absence can matter as well as presence. It presents signposts as an objective baseline that makes reasoning inspectable and warns when an unanticipated change is occurring (pp. 11–12). JDP 2-00 likewise connects ranges of possible outcomes to indicators and warning for continued monitoring (para. 3.41, pp. 69–70).

This solves a subtle temporal problem: “review this later” is not a detector. “Reopen this judgment if the package lock hash changes, the owner contradicts it, or the referenced file disappears” is a detector. The trigger is chosen while the rationale is still available, rather than after the old belief has become habitual.

### 1.5 ACH: compare alternatives, seek diagnostic contradictions, preserve weaker hypotheses, and monitor what would change the result

Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) asks analysts to enumerate reasonable alternatives, list significant evidence, evaluate each evidence item against every hypothesis, focus on inconsistency rather than support, test sensitivity to critical evidence, ask what expected evidence is absent, retain weaker hypotheses for monitoring, and define indicators consistent or inconsistent with each alternative. Evidence consistent with all hypotheses is recognized as nondiagnostic and should not decide among them (CIA Tradecraft Primer, pp. 13–15 in the document’s printed pagination; PDF pp. 17–18).

In its own field this is less a truth machine than an anti-closure and auditability device. It makes the inferential path and conflicting evidence visible to another analyst. Heuer himself cautions that ACH will not always yield the right answer; its value is forcing plausible alternatives through a common body of evidence.

Source: Richards J. Heuer Jr., [*Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf), especially the ACH discussion; and the CIA [Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf), pp. 13–15 (printed pagination).

The empirical caveat is material. Dhami et al. randomly assigned 161 participants to alternative information layouts. The conventional ACH-style layout (hypotheses in columns, evidence in rows) did not reduce confirmation bias relative to prose and did not improve sensitivity to evidence credibility; hypotheses-in-rows did reduce confirmation bias in that experiment. A second study found most of 62 Dutch military analysts in the sample were not confirmation-biased and were sensitive to evidence credibility. Therefore claims that “ACH debiases” are too broad.

Source: Mandeep K. Dhami et al., [“Effects of task structure and confirmation bias in alternative hypotheses evaluation,” *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications* 9, 37 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-024-00560-y).

## 2. Structural transfer test

### 2.1 What genuinely matches

| Intelligence-analysis structure | Cross-session agent-memory structure | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Reports arrive from sources with different access, expertise, incentives, and histories. | Memories originate from owner statements, repository state, tool output, model inference, summaries, and external sources with different authority. | Strong match. Preserve provenance and source class. |
| A report can remain unchanged while its credibility changes with later evidence. | A stored sentence can remain unchanged while the world, repo, installed tools, or owner preference changes. | Strong match. Grade/revalidate claims, not blobs. |
| Consumers often see the finished judgment without the original collection context. | A later session receives a compact memory without the transcript/tool context that produced it. | Strong match. Preserve evidence pointer, caveat, and derivation. |
| Long-standing analytic lines become anchors; contrary evidence is discounted. | Retrieved memories prime the next model and can become self-reinforcing instructions. | Strong match. Surface contradictions and invalidators before prose retrieval. |
| Analysts need warning indicators for an evolving situation. | The harness needs machine-checkable triggers that signal a claim’s referent changed. | Strong match where observables exist. |
| Deception and circular reporting make apparent corroboration misleading. | Multiple summaries or agents may repeat one original claim, creating false independence; prompt content may also be adversarial. | Strong match for lineage, weaker match for human-source motive. |

The central structural invariant is **judgment reuse under lost context**. That is why the borrow transfers. The underlying subject matter need not be geopolitical; what matters is that a later decision consumes a compressed claim whose evidence, scope, or currency may no longer be visible.

### 2.2 What does not match

1. **Software state is frequently inspectable.** Intelligence analysts often cannot query the target directly. An agent can often re-read a file, inspect a version, run a test, or ask the owner. Treating directly checkable state as an estimative problem would be perverse. The correct translation is “verify before use,” not “assign a more elaborate confidence grade.”

2. **Memories are not usually mutually exclusive hypotheses.** “The project uses pnpm,” “tests require a database,” and “the owner prefers concise reports” can all be true. Full ACH matrices impose an artificial contest. Use competing hypotheses only for actual disputes: e.g., whether a recurring failure is environmental, quoting-related, or a product defect.

3. **Source reliability is domain- and time-specific.** A tool’s successful history for filesystem facts says little about its web freshness; an owner is authoritative about preferences but can mistype a path. A single global A–F reputation score would be a category error.

4. **Human-intelligence motives do not map cleanly to tools.** “Motivation” matters for people and adversarial sources; deterministic command output instead needs identity, version, scope, exit status, and reproducibility. Copying the labels without adapting the variables would produce theater.

5. **Forgetting has a resource/utility dimension intelligence grading does not solve.** Even perfectly true facts can be irrelevant, sensitive, redundant, or cheaper to recompute than store. Intelligence tradecraft supplies epistemic hygiene, not a complete retention policy.

6. **Probability bands do not imply calibration.** A model writing “very likely” does not become calibrated by vocabulary. Kent improves communication consistency, not forecast accuracy. Calibration requires scored outcomes across repeated predictions.

7. **The evidence base for full ACH is contested.** The 2024 experiment above directly cautions against assuming that a conventional matrix layout debiases its user. This disanalogy is sufficient reason not to make ACH the default storage/retrieval runtime.

Conclusion of the adversarial test: the **metadata discipline and revisability mechanisms transfer; the institutional labels, exhaustive matrices, and implied analytic authority do not**.

## 3. Concrete design: the Memory Intelligence Card

### 3.1 Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 shadow replay — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Create an append-friendly, human-readable “Memory Intelligence Card” (MIC) format for durable claims. It can be JSONL, YAML, or Markdown with a strict small header; storage technology is not the innovation. One atomic claim is one card.

Minimum fields:

```yaml
id: mem-20260718-001
claim: "Integration tests require Docker."
kind: observed | owner-stated | inferred | forecast | procedure
scope: repo/project-a
source:
  class: owner | authoritative-file | tool-observation | external-primary | external-secondary | model-inference
  locator: "path-or-session-event-pointer"
  observed_at: "2026-07-18T12:00:00Z"
  lineage_root: "sha256-of-origin-event"
source_reliability: direct-authority | strong-history | mixed | poor | unknown
claim_credibility: confirmed-independent | probably-true | possible | doubtful | contradicted | unknown
confidence_band: "very likely (80-90%)"
confidence_basis: "README plus failing test bootstrap; same repo revision"
assumptions:
  - "README and bootstrap describe the current default test path"
invalidators:
  - type: file_hash_changed
    target: "README.md"
  - type: file_hash_changed
    target: "tests/bootstrap.*"
  - type: owner_contradiction
supersedes: []
status: active | disputed | stale | superseded | retracted | archived
last_validated_at: "2026-07-18T12:00:00Z"
use_count: 0
last_used_at: null
```

Do **not** literally use A–F/1–6 as the user-facing representation unless experiments show users interpret them correctly. Plain labels communicate the adapted meaning and avoid pretending that a tool observation is HUMINT. The two axes remain separate, which is the mechanism worth borrowing.

This card schema is **very likely (80-90%)** to preserve caveats better than an untyped prose bullet because source, claim type, observation time, and invalidators cannot be silently merged into the claim text. Evidence: the schema directly instantiates ICD 203’s fact/assumption/source-quality/currency distinctions and JDP 2-00’s two-axis grading. Its usability and completion rate are **UNVERIFIED; settle with the shadow-mode replay experiment in §9**.

### 3.2 Admission rule: remember decisions and reusable constraints, not transcripts

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 admission-policy comparison — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

A candidate becomes durable only if all of these are answered:

- **Future decision:** What later choice could this change?
- **Scope:** Where is it valid—owner-global, machine, repo, branch, task, or time window?
- **Provenance:** What event or artifact supports it?
- **Volatility:** Is it immutable/history, slow-changing policy, or live state?
- **Invalidator:** What observable change would make it doubtful?
- **Recompute cost:** Is re-reading the authority cheaper and safer than remembering the result?

Store:

- explicit owner decisions and durable preferences, including scope and date;
- architectural decisions and the reason/alternatives rejected;
- non-obvious environment constraints that caused real failures;
- validated procedures whose rediscovery cost is material;
- unresolved contradictions and high-impact unknowns;
- outcome feedback that can update source reliability or procedural quality.

Do not store as durable claims:

- secrets or sensitive values merely because they appeared;
- transient process state, current branch, temporary ports, or live service status when cheaply observable;
- raw assistant speculation;
- duplicative summaries that cannot identify their lineage root;
- facts whose authoritative source is cheap to read and whose freshness matters more than lookup latency;
- “user likes X” inferred from a single behavior when no explicit preference was expressed.

This gate is **likely (55-80%)** to improve the signal-to-noise ratio of durable memory because it selects for future decision value and forces cheap live state back to its authority. Evidence: intelligence doctrine emphasizes relevance to requirements and source currency, while the disanalogy shows that agent-accessible state should be re-read. The threshold values are **UNVERIFIED; settle by comparing downstream task success and retrieval precision across admission policies in §9**.

### 3.3 Retrieval rule: validate before influence

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 latency/benefit measurement — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Before injecting a card into a session:

1. Match scope exactly; global memories do not automatically override repository-local instructions.
2. Evaluate cheap invalidators (file hash, package version, existence, branch/revision, timestamp, owner contradiction event).
3. If the claim is directly checkable, read the authority and update status before use.
4. Present status and provenance with the claim; never inject only the sentence.
5. Rank active/confirmed cards above possible or aging cards, but surface disputed cards ahead of both when the current task touches the dispute.
6. If validation is unavailable, inject the claim as a qualified lead, not as truth.

Example retrieval:

> **Possible; last validated 41 days ago.** Integration tests required Docker at repo revision `abc…`. `tests/bootstrap.*` has changed since validation, so re-check before acting.

This pre-use gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent stale memory from silently controlling action when invalidators are machine-checkable. Evidence: indicators/signposts are explicitly designed to warn of change, and software exposes stronger observables than intelligence targets. The latency/benefit tradeoff is **UNVERIFIED; settle by measuring validation calls, prevented bad actions, and added wall time in §9**.

## 4. Staleness detection: make change observable

Age is a weak proxy. A decade-old birth date can be valid; a five-minute-old branch name can be stale. Each card should select invalidators from a small vocabulary:

| Memory kind | Preferred signposts / invalidators | Default action |
|---|---|---|
| File/repo fact | content hash, path disappearance, revision change, generated-source marker | Re-read authority; supersede old observation. |
| Tool/API procedure | executable version, help/schema hash, repeated failure fingerprint, deprecation notice | Mark disputed after first incompatible result; revalidate procedure. |
| Owner preference | explicit contrary statement, scoped exception, nonuse over many relevant decisions | Preserve history; ask only when conflict affects outcome. |
| External fact | source update timestamp, scheduled review by volatility class, independent authoritative contradiction | Re-fetch when used; avoid background crawling unless justified. |
| Architectural decision | decision-record supersession, dependency removal, goal change | Preserve as history; remove from active guidance. |
| Failure workaround | upstream version fixed, targeted regression passes, workaround itself fails | Retire from active memory but retain outcome record. |
| Inference about behavior | contradictory owner action, weak lineage, no confirming observations | Demote quickly; never promote by self-repetition. |

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 replay of invalidator policies — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Use event-driven signposts first and time-to-review only as a fallback:

- **hard invalidator:** authoritative contradiction, deleted target, changed owner instruction, proven false outcome → `stale`, `superseded`, or `retracted` immediately;
- **soft invalidator:** dependent file/version changed, expected result missing, repeated near-miss → `disputed`, requiring validation before use;
- **review clock:** only for claims without observable events, with intervals assigned by volatility class;
- **negative signpost:** expected evidence fails to appear by a specified checkpoint, which is recorded just like positive evidence.

Event-driven signposts are **very likely (80-90%)** to outperform uniform TTLs for heterogeneous memory because they target causal dependencies instead of elapsed time. Evidence: CIA signposts are scenario-specific observables reviewed over time; software supplies precise dependency events. The best review intervals for eventless claims are **UNVERIFIED; settle by replaying memory uses and optimizing for stale-use misses versus needless reviews in §9**.

## 5. Contradiction handling: a small ACH-shaped ledger, not full ACH

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 labeled contradiction test — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

When a new candidate is semantically incompatible with an active card in the same scope, do not overwrite. Create a dispute record:

```yaml
question: "Which package manager is authoritative for repo/project-a?"
alternatives:
  - id: H1
    claim: "pnpm"
  - id: H2
    claim: "npm"
evidence:
  - origin: "packageManager field"
    lineage_root: "..."
    H1: consistent
    H2: inconsistent
  - origin: "old README"
    lineage_root: "..."
    H1: inconsistent
    H2: consistent
diagnostic_next_check: "inspect lockfiles and current CI config"
resolution_rule: "current executable configuration outranks prose documentation"
status: unresolved
```

Keep four ACH-derived behaviors:

- evaluate the same evidence against every real alternative;
- count independent lineage roots, not mentions;
- prioritize evidence inconsistent with a hypothesis over generic supporting evidence;
- identify the cheapest diagnostic observation that would separate alternatives.

Drop these full-ACH behaviors:

- exhaustive matrices for ordinary memory;
- treating all evidence as equally weighted;
- requiring mutually exclusive hypotheses where claims can coexist;
- converting inconsistency counts into pseudo-probabilities;
- assuming a matrix itself removes bias.

This ledger is **likely (55-80%)** to catch silent last-write-wins corruption and repeated-summary false corroboration. Evidence: ACH’s diagnosticity and inconsistency concepts directly address nondiscriminating evidence, while lineage roots address the agent-specific circular-reporting analogue. Effectiveness of automated semantic contradiction detection is **UNVERIFIED; settle with a labeled contradiction corpus and precision/recall thresholds in §9**.

## 6. When to forget

Intelligence tradecraft suggests revisability, not deletion. Agent memory needs a separate utility policy. “Forget” should have three different meanings:

1. **Remove from active retrieval** when a claim is stale, superseded, out of scope, or cheaper to recompute.
2. **Retain as compressed history** when the reason for a decision, correction, or failed workaround prevents recurrence.
3. **Delete irreversibly** when retention is unsafe, unauthorized, legally/policy constrained, or has no future value and no audit need.

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 retention-policy comparison — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Apply these rules in order:

- **Safety/privacy first:** do not retain secrets or sensitive personal material without an explicit durable need and authority.
- **Authoritative supersession:** replace active guidance but link the old card through `supersedes`; preserve decision history where useful.
- **Invalidation:** retract false claims; do not merely lower their rank.
- **Cheap recomputation:** archive observations of live state once their task ends.
- **No demonstrated utility:** archive never-used low-impact claims after a review window.
- **Repeated utility:** retain cards that repeatedly prevent errors, but continue validating their dependencies.
- **Unresolved conflict:** retain both sides and the dispute; forgetting one side creates premature closure.

Deletion based on confidence alone is wrong. A low-confidence, high-impact warning may be exactly what future work needs; an almost-certain but irrelevant fact may be worthless. Retention should be a function of **decision value × validation cost × harm if wrong × privacy cost**, with confidence and volatility as inputs rather than the sole score.

This separation of active retrieval, archival history, and deletion is **very likely (80-90%)** to be safer than a single decay-to-delete score because epistemic staleness, retrieval utility, and retention risk are independent dimensions. Evidence: JDP 2-00 says unknown grading does not equal uselessness; ACH retains weaker hypotheses for monitoring; the memory-specific privacy and recomputation dimensions are genuine disanalogies. Exact archival thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; settle through the policy comparison in §9**.

## 7. Confidence language in the memory layer

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending §9 compression and calibration tests — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Use the owner-mandated Sherman-Kent-style bands only for uncertain judgments, always with an evidence basis. Keep two fields:

- `likelihood`: the probability that the claim/event is true or will occur;
- `analytic_confidence`: high/moderate/low confidence in that likelihood assessment, justified by evidence quality, independence, completeness, and currency.

Do not force a probability onto observations that can be checked. Record the observation, the measurement method, and its validity scope. Do not raise confidence because the same claim appears in several derivative summaries. Do not equate `almost certain` with permission to skip verification where verification is cheap and the consequence is material.

This split is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce ambiguity in memory consumption because it follows ICD 203’s explicit separation between likelihood and confidence and Kent’s fact/estimate distinction. Whether language models will reliably preserve the split across summarization is **UNVERIFIED; settle with round-trip compression tests in §9**.

## 8. What this catches that ordinary memory misses

The proposed system is designed to catch the following failure classes:

1. **Stale authority:** a remembered command still looks fluent after the package version or configuration changed. A version/hash signpost marks it disputed before execution.
2. **Caveat evaporation:** “possibly caused by X in one environment” becomes “X is the cause.” Typed claim kind, evidence basis, and confidence travel with the claim.
3. **Scope leakage:** a repo-local preference is applied globally. Explicit scope blocks retrieval outside its authority.
4. **Circular corroboration:** three agent summaries repeat one observation and are counted as three sources. `lineage_root` collapses derivatives to one evidentiary origin.
5. **Silent overwrite:** a new contradictory note replaces the old note without explaining the conflict. The dispute ledger preserves both and asks for a diagnostic check.
6. **Source halo:** a usually accurate source makes a claim outside its access or expertise. Source reliability stays separate from claim credibility and domain.
7. **Truth without utility:** accurate transient state clutters retrieval. Admission requires a future decision and favors re-reading cheap authority.
8. **Useful minority warning lost to decay:** a low-confidence but high-impact contradiction is deleted. Conflict status and impact keep it visible until resolution.
9. **Workaround immortality:** a workaround persists after the underlying bug is fixed. A regression-pass or version-change invalidator retires it.
10. **Self-confirming memory:** model-generated inference is retrieved, restated, and later treated as independent confirmation. Lineage and `kind: inferred` prevent promotion by repetition.

These catch claims are **likely (55-80%)** at the mechanism level because each has an explicit field or transition that exposes the failure before or during retrieval. Actual catch rate in this owner’s workload is **UNVERIFIED; settle with §9’s historical replay and shadow deployment**.

## 9. Experiments required before rollout

### 9.1 Shadow-mode historical replay

### Recommendation — UNVERIFIED pending the experiment specified in this section — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Run the extractor and card lifecycle in shadow mode over a stratified sample of completed sessions. It must not inject memories into live work during the first phase.

Label at least these outcomes:

- candidate deserved durable retention / did not;
- claim was factual, owner-stated, inferred, procedural, or forecast;
- later evidence confirmed, superseded, contradicted, or left unresolved;

- retrieval would have helped, distracted, or caused harm.

Compare:

- plain summary memory;
- simple timestamp/TTL memory;
- MIC with provenance only;
- MIC with provenance + invalidators + dispute ledger.

Primary metrics: stale-claim use rate, harmful retrieval rate, useful retrieval precision, contradiction-detection precision/recall, time-to-invalidation, caveat survival after two compression cycles, and token/latency overhead. Stratify by owner statement, repository fact, external fact, procedure, and inference; an aggregate score can hide category failure.

Success gate proposed for pilot: no increase in harmful retrieval, at least a 30% relative reduction in stale-claim use, contradiction precision high enough that review load remains bounded, and p95 validation latency acceptable to the owner. These numerical thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; settle by establishing the baseline distribution in this replay before fixing final gates**.

### 9.2 Known-present detector controls

Every staleness or contradiction detector must be tested against a planted known-present case before absence is accepted:

- known changed file hash and known unchanged hash;
- known direct contradiction and known compatible pair;
- two derivative summaries with one lineage root and two genuinely independent origins;
- known expired version-scoped procedure and known valid immutable historical record.

Use a second mechanically independent signal before claiming a memory is stale or resolved. Examples: file hash change **plus** current config value; owner contradiction event **plus** explicit confirmation; version change **plus** regression result. Two runs of the same embedding classifier do not count.

This control is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent false “nothing changed” and false “conflict resolved” conclusions because it validates detector sensitivity and requires a distinct confirmation channel. Evidence is the logical independence of the checks and the program’s mandatory honesty instrumentation; harness-specific false-positive reduction is **UNVERIFIED; settle by seeded-detector trials during replay**.

### 9.3 Calibration and compression tests

For probabilistic memories with observable outcomes, score Brier loss by claim kind and confidence band. Test whether compression preserves:

- observation versus inference;
- scope;
- source pointer;
- invalidator;
- uncertainty cause;
- contradiction status.

If a summarizer repeatedly drops these fields, the card must remain structured and the prose should be regenerated from it—not the reverse. This design choice is **very likely (80-90%)** to preserve mandatory metadata better than repeated free-text summarization because fields are validated independently; exact robustness is **UNVERIFIED; settle through adversarial two- and five-generation round trips**.

## 10. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

All recommendations above ride the existing hook/skill/MCP/config bus. None requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The minimal architecture is:

```text
session outcome / owner correction / observed state
                    |
             admission filter
                    |
        append atomic claim card
                    |
      invalidator events update status
                    |
task query -> scope match -> cheap validation -> qualified injection
                    |
              outcome feedback
```

No daemon is justified. A small append/update helper plus retrieval hook is enough to test the hypothesis. Semantic contradiction detection may use the already-running model at memory-write time; it must not become a separately trained critic. Retrieval can begin with explicit scope, tags, and lexical matching. Embeddings are optional only if an existing native facility already supplies them; introducing a vector database would violate the anti-overfit gate and is not proposed.

A minimal bus-riding implementation is **very likely (80-90%)** to be sufficient for a valid experiment because the essential variables are metadata capture, event matching, state transitions, and qualified rendering—not novel orchestration. Evidence: each can be represented in an append-only card and evaluated at existing lifecycle boundaries. Whether it remains under ~200 lines in this harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle with a disposable prototype and line-count/functionality review before adoption**.

## 11. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 11.1 Full ACH for every memory — reject

It mismatches compositional claims, adds quadratic evidence/hypothesis work, and has weak/negative empirical support for the conventional matrix as a debiasing device in the cited 2024 study. Retain only the small dispute ledger for consequential genuine alternatives.

### 11.2 Global “trust score” per source — reject

Reliability varies by domain, access, version, and time. It also invites source halo effects. Track history by source × domain × method, and keep the report-level credibility judgment separate.

### 11.3 Universal TTL decay — reject

Age and staleness are not identical. TTL is a fallback review trigger for eventless claims, not a truth-decay function.

### 11.4 Last-write-wins correction — reject

It destroys the existence and rationale of the conflict, makes accidental overwrites indistinguishable from authoritative supersession, and eliminates the evidence needed to learn which source failed.

### 11.5 Never delete; keep everything — reject

It ignores privacy, retrieval pollution, cheap recomputation, and the risk that irrelevant memories prime future action. Archive decision history selectively; delete unsafe or valueless material under policy.

### 11.6 Vector database / knowledge graph memory — reject under the anti-overfit gate

Neither solves provenance, source independence, invalidation, or epistemic status by itself. Both add a second data architecture before the lifecycle semantics have been validated. The proposed experiment can run over a flat structured log and existing retrieval mechanisms.

### 11.7 Confidence bands alone — reject

They standardize expression but do not establish calibration, evidence independence, or freshness. Every band needs an evidence basis, and observable state should usually be rechecked rather than estimated.

## 12. Final recommendation

Proceed with a shadow-mode Memory Intelligence Card experiment, not a platform replacement.

- **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** atomic claim cards; provenance lineage; explicit invalidators; qualified retrieval; small contradiction ledger.
- **CONFIG (settings/JSON only):** admission, scope, retention, privacy, volatility, and confidence-language policies.
- **NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it):** use existing lifecycle hooks/skills for capture and retrieval if available; this unit intentionally did not inspect the local harness, so availability is **UNVERIFIED; settle by the implementation unit’s capability inventory and two-signal known-present control**.

Do not introduce **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)**. This unit presents no evidence-based justification for one.

Overall, the narrow borrow is **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally sound: intelligence tradecraft directly addresses qualified judgment reuse, source/claim separation, aging information, contrary evidence, and advance indicators of change, while the design explicitly excludes the parts that do not transfer. Its net benefit in this harness is **UNVERIFIED; the historical replay, seeded detectors, calibration checks, and shadow deployment above are the experiments that settle it**.
