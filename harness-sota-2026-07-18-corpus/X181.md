# X181 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → cost control

**Unit:** X181  
**Lens:** experimental-design mechanisms for spending agent compute and quota only where it can change the answer  
**Scope discipline:** external research and design only. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, corpus, audit, or subject tree. The only file written is this artifact.

## Executive judgment

The borrow **partly transfers**. The strongest transferable mechanism is not “run a power calculation on every agent task.” It is a **pre-specified, group-sequential compute protocol with blinded adjudication and randomized sentinel continuations**:

1. define the decision and smallest decision-relevant improvement before expensive work begins;
2. expose compute in stages rather than uniformly;
3. at fixed checkpoints, stop for sufficient evidence, stop for futility, or continue under rules written before seeing the branch result;
4. compare candidate outputs without model, agent, price, token-count, or ordering cues when the outcome is subjective;
5. continue a small randomized sample of apparently finished/futile tasks so the harness can measure the counterfactual it otherwise suppresses: how often more compute really would have changed the decision beneficially;
6. use positive and negative controls to distinguish a genuinely informative absence or improvement from a broken detector or evaluator sensitivity to irrelevant context.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to be a structurally valid transfer because adaptive trials solve the same abstract allocation problem—consume a costly resource sequentially, learn from interim evidence, and alter later allocation without allowing the observed result to silently rewrite the decision rule—and the source literature explicitly defines adaptive designs as changes made from accumulating results according to pre-specified rules, including early stopping, dropping arms, and shifting allocation ([Pallmann et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5830330/)). The remaining uncertainty is whether the harness has reliable task-level outcomes and enough repeated task strata to calibrate the rules; that is an empirical property of the target, not established by this ideation unit.

Literal clinical-trial power analysis is **unlikely (20-45%)** to transfer usefully to an individual open-ended agent task. Evidence: conventional power needs a specified estimand/effect size, sampling model, and error target, while agent tasks are often one-off, non-stationary, adaptively decomposed, and judged through noisy proxies. Lakens’s review makes the relevant source-field point: sample-size justification depends on the inferential goal, the smallest effect of interest, expected effects, desired accuracy, or explicit resource constraints; it is not synonymous with invoking a generic power threshold ([Lakens, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267)). The valid translation is therefore **decision-value calibration from repeated harness data**, not a decorative “80% powered” label.

The proposed architecture passes the anti-overfit gate. Every mechanism below uses settings, an existing hook/skill bus, append-only event records, and an offline report. It does **not** add a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The minimal architecture is intentional: fixed stage gates plus sparse randomized measurement are easier to delete or retune as model capability changes than a learned orchestration layer.

## 1. What experimental design solved in its own field

### 1.1 Pre-registration separates a prediction from an explanation invented after seeing the result

In its own field, preregistration records the research question and analysis plan before outcomes are observed. Nosek et al. describe the core failure as mistaking postdiction for prediction and the remedy as defining questions and analysis before outcomes, while still allowing exploratory work if it is labeled as such ([Nosek et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)). The mechanism is not paperwork for its own sake. It creates a time-stamped boundary between:

- what evidence was declared decision-relevant in advance;
- what threshold would trigger the conclusion;
- what analysis was confirmatory;
- what was discovered after looking and must be reported as exploratory.

That boundary prevents the researcher from repeatedly changing outcomes, subgroups, exclusions, or analyses until the observed data support a preferred story. Preregistration does not make a poor design good and does not prohibit deviations; it makes deviations visible.

### 1.2 Power/sample-size justification allocates observations against an inferential target

Power analysis is one member of a broader family of sample-size justifications. In its own field, the researcher specifies an effect worth detecting, acceptable error rates, an assumed data-generating model, and then selects a sample size with an adequate probability of detecting that effect. Lakens explicitly distinguishes six justifications: near-census collection, resource constraints, a-priori power, desired accuracy, heuristics, and acknowledging no justification. He emphasizes the smallest effect size of interest and how much the collected data informs inference about effects of that size ([Lakens, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267)).

The cost-control lesson is deeper than “calculate N”: **spending has no coherent optimum until the decision-relevant effect and tolerated errors are named**. If a tiny stylistic improvement cannot change acceptance, collecting enough evidence to detect it is waste.

### 1.3 Group-sequential/adaptive designs spend sample budget in stages

Adaptive designs permit planned modifications in response to accumulating data. Pallmann et al. list early stopping for success or lack of efficacy, sample-size re-estimation, dropping inferior arms in multi-arm multi-stage trials, population enrichment, and shifting allocation toward more promising or informative arms. Crucially, the modifications follow pre-specified rules; the paper also recommends pre-trial simulation of operating characteristics and separation between an unblinded monitoring committee and a blinded steering committee ([Pallmann et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5830330/)).

This solves two opposed wastes:

- a fixed large trial continues consuming participants and money after the conclusion is already adequately supported;
- a fixed small trial cannot recover when nuisance assumptions were wrong and more observations are genuinely needed.

It also creates new risks. Repeated looks can distort error rates; adaptations can leak interim information and change behavior; dropping an arm can bias naive estimates; and overly aggressive futility boundaries can discard a real but delayed effect. That is why the adaptation rule, information times, decision boundaries, analysis, and simulations are designed together rather than improvised after each look.

### 1.4 Blinding protects subjective decisions from identity and expectation cues

Blinding and allocation concealment separate assessment from knowledge that can change assessment. A meta-epidemiological analysis of 1,346 trials found exaggerated effects for subjective outcomes when allocation concealment was inadequate/unclear (ratio of odds ratios 0.69, 95% CI 0.59–0.82) or blinding was absent (0.75, 0.61–0.93); it found little evidence of such bias for objective outcomes ([Wood et al., 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2267990/)).

The source-field implication is appropriately narrow: blinding matters most when a human or judgmental process can be influenced by knowing the treatment, allocation, or hypothesis. It adds little when the endpoint is mechanically objective and correctly measured.

### 1.5 Negative controls reveal errors the primary analysis cannot diagnose by itself

A negative control is selected so the hypothesized causal mechanism cannot produce the target effect, while relevant confounding/error pathways are shared as closely as possible. If an association appears where the mechanism says none can exist, the primary result may be contaminated. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen describe negative-control outcomes and exposures, the required comparability assumptions, and the important limitation: a failed negative control is a sensitive but blunt warning and does not identify or quantify the bias without further assumptions ([Lipsitch et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).

The paper is unusually useful for guarding the analogy: a control chosen under a false causal assumption can itself mislead, and practical controls are only approximately comparable. A control is a detector check, not an oracle.

## 2. Structural mapping to agent compute allocation

| Experimental-design object | Harness analogue | What is preserved | What is not preserved |
|---|---|---|---|
| estimand / primary outcome | task-level decision: accept, edit, escalate, choose branch, or ask owner | spending is tied to a decision | many tasks lack a single scalar ground truth |
| smallest effect size of interest | smallest decision-relevant improvement (SDRI) | ignores changes too small to alter the decision | quality dimensions may be incomparable or lexicographic |
| participant/sample | an additional evidence acquisition: retrieval, test, rollout, model call, branch, or verification pass | each unit consumes budget and may reduce uncertainty | calls are correlated and adaptive, not IID people |
| treatment arm | candidate plan, tool route, model tier, or solution branch | alternatives compete for further allocation | branches can share evidence and mutate during execution |
| interim analysis | checkpoint after a bounded stage | permits stop/continue/reallocate | checkpoint evaluation may itself cost tokens and be gameable |
| efficacy boundary | evidence already sufficient to take the decision | avoids polishing past decision sufficiency | “sufficient” depends on asymmetric consequences |
| futility boundary | remaining affordable compute is unlikely to change the decision beneficially | stops doomed search/retry loops | delayed breakthroughs make futility harder than in many trials |
| blinded endpoint committee | comparator stripped of model/agent/cost/order metadata | reduces prestige, anchoring, and sunk-cost cues | content style can reveal model identity; complete blinding is impossible |
| negative control | irrelevant-context invariance check or known-null branch | tests whether evaluator/detector reacts spuriously | exact shared-error comparability is rarely knowable |
| positive control | known-present fact/error injected or selected before an absence claim | validates detector sensitivity | passing one control does not validate all task conditions |
| follow-up after nominal stopping | randomized sentinel continuation | estimates missed beneficial flips after early stop | spends budget deliberately on some cases that appeared done |

The shared structure is **sequential resource allocation under uncertain marginal information**. The most important preservation is temporal: rules are committed before the interim result that would tempt the system to reinterpret them.

## 3. Where the analogy fails

### 3.1 A task is usually not a population sample

Clinical power statements are repeated-sampling statements under a model. An agent’s next 20,000 tokens on one repository task are not 20,000 independent observations. They may all elaborate one mistaken premise. Tool calls share context, failures are clustered, and a model’s actions alter the environment from which later evidence is collected. Treating token count as sample size is therefore **almost certainly not (1-5%)** defensible; the evidence is the mismatch between the sample-size assumptions described by Lakens and the correlated, path-dependent target process.

### 3.2 The outcome arrives late or is partially unobserved

Many answers are only validated after tests, owner review, production behavior, or downstream use. If the harness stops early, it never observes the counterfactual result of continuing. Training a stopping rule only from completed trajectories creates selective labels: apparently easy cases are overrepresented among cheap stops, while evidence about false stops is absent by construction. This is why the randomized sentinel is central rather than optional measurement polish.

### 3.3 “Changed the answer” can be bad

More compute can flip a decision from correct to incorrect, add unsupported complexity, or merely change prose. The target cannot be raw answer difference. It must be a **beneficial decision flip** according to a task-specific external outcome: tests, factual verification, rubric adjudication, owner correction, or a later accepted/rejected action. Any design optimizing “difference after more tokens” would reward churn.

### 3.4 Error costs are asymmetric and task-specific

Stopping before a low-risk draft is perfect is cheap; stopping before a destructive migration is verified can be catastrophic. A uniform confidence threshold recreates the target problem. The protocol needs a risk class and verification floor. No amount of apparent success should let a high-impact task spend its reserved independent-verification budget on more generation instead.

### 3.5 Blinding is partial

Model-specific diction, formatting, and error signatures may reveal branch identity. Tests and compilers are objective enough that blinding adds little. Blinding is useful for subjective comparison, not a universal wrapper around all evaluation. A claim that anonymization removes evaluator bias would be **very unlikely (5-20%)**; Wood et al. support the direction of bias in subjective clinical outcomes, not complete transfer to model outputs.

### 3.6 Negative-control comparability is fragile

An “irrelevant” context item can legitimately change an answer by clarifying scope or revealing a security constraint. A null branch may not share the same failure pathways as the primary branch. Following Lipsitch et al., a failed harness control is evidence to inspect, not proof that the main answer is wrong; a passing control rules out only the pathways it actually exercises.

### 3.7 Adaptive allocation can starve exploration

If early scores favor familiar tools or common task types, reallocation can permanently underinvest in rare routes whose payoff appears late. This is a direct analogue of premature arm dropping, amplified by model non-stationarity. A fixed exploration/sentinel fraction and periodic reset are necessary. A learned contextual bandit would address some allocation questions but would introduce a trained decision component and greater monitoring burden; I reject it here under the anti-overfit gate.

## 4. Concrete harness design

### Proposal A — Compute protocol card

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Before the first costly stage, create a small machine-readable card:

```json
{
  "decision": "choose_patch",
  "risk_class": "high",
  "smallest_decision_relevant_improvement": "changes failing test, verified root cause, or safety finding",
  "objective_checks": ["targeted_test", "static_check"],
  "subjective_rubric": ["correctness", "scope", "maintainability"],
  "stages": [
    {"name": "triage", "budget": 1, "unit": "model_call"},
    {"name": "evidence", "budget": 6, "unit": "tool_call"},
    {"name": "candidate", "budget": 1, "unit": "model_call"},
    {"name": "independent_verify", "reserve_fraction": 0.20}
  ],
  "continue_if": ["decision unresolved", "high-impact claim has one signal", "test discriminates live hypotheses"],
  "stop_success_if": ["objective checks pass", "required claims have independent support"],
  "stop_futility_if": ["remaining routes repeat a disproven mechanism", "budget ceiling reached"],
  "override": ["new safety risk", "owner changes scope"],
  "exploratory_deviations_must_be_labeled": true
}
```

The numeric values above are **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles them is a retrospective replay over cost/outcome traces followed by a prospective randomized rollout by task-risk stratum**. They are schema examples, not recommended defaults.

The card catches a currently generic class of miss: expensive retrieval, branching, or polishing continues without naming what new result could change the action. It also makes “we need more research” falsifiable: the agent must name a live decision and a discriminating observation.

This mechanism is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce obviously non-decision-relevant spend with low implementation risk because it is a direct preregistration translation and requires no model training; evidence is Nosek et al.’s separation of planned from post-hoc analysis plus the card’s ability to expose deviations. Its effect size in this harness is UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the staged A/B rollout defined in Section 6.

### Proposal B — Stage-gate hook with success, futility, and verification reserve

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

At configured events—after initial triage, after a retrieval batch, after a failing test changes state, before spawning another branch, and before finalization—the hook requests one of four structured decisions:

- `STOP_SUCCESS`: enough evidence exists for the declared decision;
- `CONTINUE`: a named next observation can cross a declared boundary;
- `STOP_FUTILITY`: affordable remaining routes are not expected to resolve the decision;
- `OVERRIDE`: an allowed exception occurred, recorded with reason.

The hook maintains only counters and an append-only event record. It does not orchestrate tools. It warns or blocks only at explicit budget boundaries and always preserves a verification reserve for high-risk work. A branch cannot justify continuation with “might help”; it supplies `(live hypothesis, next observation, possible decision flip, maximum stage cost)`.

This catches repeated retries with no changed hypothesis, uniform deep search across low- and high-value branches, polishing after all acceptance checks pass, and spending the last quota on generation before verification.

The proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to improve compute allocation because group-sequential designs demonstrate that pre-specified early success/futility and arm dropping can reduce resource use while retaining planned operating characteristics ([Pallmann et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5830330/)). The magnitude and false-stop rate are UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is shadow-mode logging, then randomized sentinel continuation with blinded outcome review.

### Proposal C — Blinded branch adjudication only for subjective comparisons

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

When two or more candidate outputs survive objective checks, a wrapper:

1. removes model name, agent name, token count, price, latency, branch order, and self-reported confidence;
2. randomizes candidate order;
3. normalizes purely cosmetic wrappers while retaining substantive content;
4. asks the evaluator to score the pre-registered rubric and cite evidence locations;
5. reveals cost only after the quality judgment, then chooses the cheapest candidate within the predeclared equivalence margin.

The equivalence margin is the harness form of the SDRI: if the expensive branch is not better by enough to change acceptance, its extra compute did not buy a decision-relevant improvement.

This catches prestige bias (“the premium model must be better”), sunk-cost bias (“the longer branch deserves to win”), positional bias, and confident self-description masquerading as quality. Objective test results remain primary and are not blinded away.

The direction of benefit is **likely (55-80%)** for subjective branch comparison because Wood et al. found materially larger bias from lack of blinding for subjective than objective clinical outcomes. Transfer magnitude is UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is a crossover evaluation in which the same candidate pairs are judged with identity/cost metadata visible versus hidden and order randomized.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED randomized sentinel continuation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The central measurement problem is censored counterfactuals: after the harness stops, it cannot know whether one more bounded stage would have corrected or materially improved the decision. For a small randomized fraction of eligible `STOP_SUCCESS` and `STOP_FUTILITY` events, silently authorize exactly one additional bounded stage, preserve both pre- and post-continuation candidates, and send the pair to blinded adjudication. Log:

- stop reason and checkpoint features;
- extra cost;
- whether the decision changed;
- whether the change was beneficial, harmful, or immaterial;
- task/risk stratum;
- which evidence caused the change.

Random selection matters. If only suspicious stops are continued, the estimated false-stop rate is selection-biased. The sentinel sample becomes an experimental holdout that estimates marginal value at the boundary actually used by the harness.

The appropriate sampling fraction is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is a precision calculation after a pilot estimates the base rate and variance of beneficial flips in each task/risk stratum**. Do not canonize “5%” or any other convenient number. Use Lakens’s logic: first define the smallest rate of missed beneficial flips worth acting on and desired interval precision, then justify the sample against that goal and quota constraint.

This mechanism is **very likely (80-90%)** to produce less biased calibration data than logging only naturally continued tasks, because randomization breaks the selection rule that otherwise determines which counterfactuals become observable. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that the collected data will be dense enough for fine-grained per-task policies; evidence against confidence is the likely heterogeneity and non-stationarity of agent tasks. Start with coarse strata and report wide intervals.

This catches the failure ordinary cost dashboards miss: savings that look excellent only because the harness never measures the tasks it stopped too soon.

### Proposal E — Positive and negative control suite for allocation decisions

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** for control definitions, with execution through the existing evaluator/test bus.

Use three narrowly scoped controls:

1. **Detector positive control before absence claims.** Run a known-present item through the same detector and path. If it is not found, the absence result is invalid and the next spend goes to detector repair/alternate measurement, not broader search.
2. **Evaluator negative control for irrelevant metadata.** Judge the same candidate twice under randomized, semantically inert model/cost/order labels. A preference shift indicates cue sensitivity. This is a periodic calibration task, not an addition to every production task.
3. **Mechanism-negative evidence item.** For task classes with a defensible causal model, include an evidence item that shares the parsing/retrieval path but cannot bear on the declared decision. If it changes the decision, inspect contamination or prompt sensitivity.

The third control is **UNVERIFIED and must not be enabled for a task class until a domain reviewer can state why the item is causally irrelevant; the settling experiment is a review plus adversarial examples showing whether the item can legitimately alter scope or risk**. This warning inherits directly from Lipsitch et al.: imperfect controls can mislead, and their failure diagnoses neither the type nor magnitude of bias.

The positive control is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent wasted escalation after detector failure because it establishes sensitivity on the exercised path before interpreting a null; evidence is the control logic itself and the negative-control literature’s use of expected-null/non-null behavior to expose spurious inference. The evaluator negative control is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal metadata/position sensitivity when subjective adjudication is used; the exact incidence is UNVERIFIED and is settled by the randomized duplicate-judgment calibration.

### Proposal F — Offline cost-to-decision report, not a learned router

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Aggregate stage events and sentinel outcomes by coarse, predeclared strata (for example: objective coding task, open-web synthesis, destructive operation, subjective drafting). Report:

- median and tail cost per stage;
- stop-success, stop-futility, and override counts;
- beneficial, harmful, and immaterial decision-flip rates after sentinel continuation;
- verification-reserve consumption;
- false-stop interval estimates;
- branch win rate after blinding, with and without cost metadata;
- calibration age/model version.

Do not optimize a single “tokens saved” metric. The primary cost-control quantity is:

`net decision value = expected loss avoided by beneficial flips − expected loss introduced by harmful flips − incremental compute cost`.

Dollarizing all loss is unnecessary; a lexicographic rule can require safety/correctness constraints first, then minimize compute among candidates meeting them. The report recommends boundary adjustments for human/config review; it does not automatically train or deploy a router.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to remain robust longer than a learned policy because it exposes calibration drift and keeps allocation rules inspectable; evidence is the deliberate reduction in moving parts, not comparative harness data. That comparative advantage is UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a time-split backtest across model/version changes comparing fixed reviewed strata against any later learned baseline.

## 5. What the combined design catches that ordinary budgets miss

1. **Non-discriminating work.** A next action consumes quota but cannot cross any success, futility, or verification boundary.
2. **Retry loops without hypothesis change.** The same mechanism is retried because no pre-specified continuation record is required.
3. **Premature apparent savings.** Early stopping reduces observed cost while its missed-correction rate stays unmeasured; sentinel continuation reveals it.
4. **Uniform verification.** Low-risk prose and high-impact destructive work receive the same scrutiny; risk-class reserves prevent this.
5. **Premium-model halo.** A costly branch wins subjective evaluation because its identity or length is visible; blinding tests this.
6. **Sunk-cost continuation.** A long branch receives more budget because much has already been spent, not because the next observation has decision value.
7. **Broken-null escalation.** A detector says “absent,” causing more search, when the detector cannot find a known-present control.
8. **Proxy churn.** More compute changes wording and is counted as useful despite no acceptance/test/decision change.
9. **Late-effect starvation.** An aggressive futility rule kills routes with delayed payoff; sentinel false-stop rates expose this.
10. **Calibration drift.** A boundary learned under an older model/task mix remains in force after marginal returns change; versioned reports expose stale evidence.

## 6. Pre-registered validation plan

This recommendation should itself be evaluated like an experiment rather than installed on narrative plausibility.

### Phase 0 — Define outcomes before looking

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Pre-register:

- eligible task strata and exclusions;
- risk classes;
- primary outcome: compute cost per accepted decision subject to a non-inferiority bound on correctness/safety;
- secondary outcomes: owner corrections, objective test failures, time-to-decision, beneficial/harmful sentinel flips, overrides, and evaluator disagreement;
- smallest practically important cost reduction;
- largest acceptable degradation in correctness/safety;
- analysis unit (task, not tool call or token);
- handling of clustered/repeated owner tasks;
- model/version freeze or stratification;
- stopping and rollback rules;
- all exploratory analyses labeled as such.

The exact non-inferiority and cost margins are **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles them cannot infer owner preferences from trace data alone and therefore requires an explicit owner risk/cost decision before rollout**.

### Phase 1 — Shadow mode

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Generate protocol cards and stage decisions without enforcing them. Measure card completeness, how often proposed gates disagree with actual continuation, and whether objective outcomes are available. Include known-positive detector cases and ensure the measurement path finds them before interpreting any zero count.

Promotion criterion: two mechanically independent signals for outcome capture—e.g., objective test/verification events plus later owner accept/correction—on the relevant task stratum. Re-running the same parser is not independence.

### Phase 2 — Randomized controlled rollout

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** once the small hook exists.

Randomize eligible tasks within coarse risk/task strata to:

- control: current spending behavior with measurement only;
- treatment: stage gates, verification reserve, and blinded comparison.

Within treatment, randomize eligible nominal stops into sentinel continuation versus actual stop. Keep the treatment allocation concealed from the final subjective adjudicator. Analyze at task level and report intervals, not only point estimates.

The design is **very likely (80-90%)** to identify a gross cost/quality tradeoff if task outcomes are reliably captured and the effect is not tiny; evidence is random allocation and direct cost measurement. Whether those prerequisites hold is UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is Phase 1’s dual-signal outcome-capture audit and a pilot variance estimate.

### Phase 3 — Negative controls and falsification

Run these predeclared falsification checks:

- cost/identity labels randomized on identical outputs should not alter an objective test outcome;
- stage gating should not “save” compute on deliberately high-risk cases by consuming the verification reserve;
- a known-present detector item must be found before any absence-rate analysis;
- placebo checkpoint labels inserted without changing evidence should not change the continuation decision;
- results should be directionally stable when immaterial prose changes are excluded from “decision flip.”

A failed control invalidates only the claim it tests. It does not automatically prove the entire system harmful.

### Phase 4 — Decision rule

Adopt for a task stratum only if:

1. correctness/safety meets the predeclared non-inferiority constraint;
2. compute or quota improves by the predeclared practically important amount;
3. harmful sentinel flips remain below the predeclared bound;
4. both outcome-capture signals function;
5. no negative control indicates an unresolved measurement artifact.

Otherwise keep shadow mode, adjust one declared mechanism, and run a new versioned experiment. Do not retroactively move margins and call the original trial successful.

## 8. Rejected alternatives and dead ends

### Literal per-task power calculations

Rejected. There is normally no defensible IID sample, stable effect distribution, or scalar estimand. Calling tokens “sample size” would produce false precision. Use power/precision only for the repeated validation experiment and sentinel-rate estimation, where tasks are the analysis units and clustering is handled.

### Spend until the model reports high confidence

Rejected. Self-reported confidence is not an independent outcome and can rise with repeated exposure to the same mistaken premise. The continuation rule must point to external or independently acquired evidence.

### Always use multiple agents and take a vote

Rejected. Uniform replication is exactly the cost pattern this unit is meant to avoid; correlated agents can repeat the same error, and majority vote can erase a rare correct branch. Replication is a stage authorized when disagreement can change a high-value decision, and candidate adjudication should be blinded.

### Fully learned cost router / contextual bandit

Rejected at this stage under the anti-overfit gate. It would add a trained decision component, demand continual off-policy evaluation, and amplify selective-label problems before counterfactual continuation data exists. A future unit may reconsider it only after sentinel data demonstrates stable, repeated strata and a material advantage over inspectable rules.

### Bayesian optimization over every tool/model/branch choice

Rejected for the same reason. The action space changes as tools and models change, outcomes are delayed, and the optimization layer risks becoming a second orchestrator. Coarse stage gates capture the main experimental-design benefit with fewer assumptions.

### A global hard token ceiling

Rejected as the primary mechanism. It controls invoices but cannot distinguish waste from a necessary safety verification. Retain ceilings as backstops, then allocate within them using decision relevance and risk reserves.

### Negative controls on every task

Rejected. Controls themselves cost quota, and a poorly chosen “irrelevant” item can change scope legitimately. Use them to validate detectors and periodically calibrate subjective evaluators, or for task classes with an explicit causal rationale.

### A single composite quality-per-dollar score

Rejected. It permits cheap but unsafe outputs to compensate numerically for correctness loss. Apply safety/correctness constraints first; compare cost only among admissible outputs.

## Bottom line

The transferable insight is: **do not allocate compute by habit or evenly; predeclare what could change the decision, buy evidence in bounded stages, hide irrelevant identity/cost cues during subjective evaluation, reserve verification for asymmetric risk, and deliberately sample the counterfactual after stopping.** The last element is the most important expansion: without randomized sentinel continuation, a cost controller can appear efficient precisely because it censors evidence of its premature stops.

This is not a case for a new orchestration platform. It is a case for a small experimental protocol riding the current bus, with its own effectiveness established by a pre-registered randomized rollout rather than assumed from analogy.
