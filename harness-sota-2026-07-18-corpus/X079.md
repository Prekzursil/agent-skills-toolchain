# X079 — law of evidence → failure recovery

## Executive finding

The useful borrow is not “make an audit log.” It is a **recovery admissibility doctrine**: after a worker, session, or machine dies, the replacement worker must distinguish (1) records that may be admitted as a basis for action, (2) admitted records whose credibility is weak, and (3) live facts that must be proved again because a historical record cannot establish present state. The specific source mechanisms are authentication/chain of custody, contemporaneous regularly kept records, the rule of completeness, and preference for the original with a disciplined exception when it is unavailable.

The transfer is **likely (55–80%) to reduce unsafe or wasteful resumptions**, because both a court and a replacement worker must reason from records produced by actors who may no longer be available, and the Federal Rules explicitly separate identity/authentication, trustworthiness, completeness, and original-versus-secondary proof. This is an architectural analogy, not yet an implementation result. **UNVERIFIED: settle it with the crash-injection experiment specified under “Evaluation,” comparing blind-summary recovery with the proposed admission gate.**

The analogy does **not** transfer as a claim that a valid chain proves truth. Authentication establishes that an item is what its proponent says it is; the Federal Rules’ own note says authentication alone does not assure admissibility, and a court decision can tolerate imperfect custody while leaving gaps to evidentiary weight. A hash-perfect record can faithfully preserve a hallucination, a failed request, or a once-true state that later changed. Therefore the proposed mechanism admits historical claims only; it requires an independent live probe before repeating, skipping, or declaring completion of a consequential side effect.

This unit deliberately does not inspect or make claims about the local harness. The task defines this as an ideation lens and assigns repo grounding to other units. “What we currently miss” below consequently means failure classes that a summary-only or unauthenticated-checkpoint recovery path cannot distinguish, not a claim that a particular local file lacks a feature.

## Scope and source discipline

This analysis uses United States federal evidence law as a bounded source field, not “law” in every jurisdiction. The Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts says the Federal Rules of Evidence govern admission or exclusion in most U.S. federal-court proceedings and were last amended in 2024 ([U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Evidence](https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure/federal-rules-evidence)). Cornell’s Legal Information Institute supplies accessible rule text and committee notes; for chain-of-custody language I also use an authenticated Government Publishing Office reproduction of Rule 901 and a federal district-court opinion hosted by GovInfo.

No claim here is that software recovery is litigation or that legal admissibility is an engineering correctness criterion. The question is narrower: which epistemic controls developed for deciding whether unavailable actors’ records may support a later decision have the same shape as post-crash recovery?

## What the source field actually solved

### 1. Authentication and chain of custody answer “is this the item claimed?”

Federal Rule of Evidence 901(a) requires a proponent to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item is what the proponent claims. The listed methods are illustrative, not exhaustive. They include distinctive contents and internal patterns, and evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result ([FRE 901(a), (b)(4), and (b)(9)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). The official advisory note explicitly describes accounting for custody of narcotics from seizure through laboratory analysis as an example of authentication ([GovInfo authenticated Rule 901 reproduction, pp. 446–447](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title28/pdf/USCODE-2023-title28-app-federalru-dup2-rule901.pdf)).

In its own field, a chain of custody links an offered object to its claimed origin and tracks material handoffs or opportunities for alteration. It is a foundation for identity and condition, not a metaphysical proof that every associated assertion is true. The same Rule 901 note warns that satisfying authentication does not itself assure admission because other barriers, such as hearsay, may remain ([FRE 901 advisory note](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). A federal district court, applying Seventh Circuit authority, likewise stated that a perfect chain is not prerequisite, that reasonable precautions can suffice, and that gaps ordinarily affect weight rather than admissibility ([United States v. Snowden, GovInfo-hosted opinion, pp. 11–12](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ilsd-4_19-cr-40081/pdf/USCOURTS-ilsd-4_19-cr-40081-2.pdf)).

The important mechanism is thus a two-stage decision:

1. **Admission/foundation:** is there enough provenance to treat this as the claimed item or record?
2. **Weight:** once admitted, how strongly does it support the proposition for which it is offered?

That distinction prevents two symmetrical mistakes: discarding every imperfect record, and treating every well-identified record as conclusive.

### 2. The business-records exception answers “when may an unavailable maker’s routine record be trusted?”

Hearsay doctrine generally distrusts an out-of-court assertion offered for its truth because the original declarant is not being tested in court. Rule 803(6) nevertheless permits records of regularly conducted activity when the record was made at or near the time by, or from information transmitted by, someone with knowledge; keeping it was part of a regularly conducted activity; making it was a regular practice; a custodian or certification establishes those conditions; and the opponent does not show untrustworthy sources or preparation circumstances ([FRE 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

The mechanism does not say “all logs are reliable.” It uses a bundle of reliability proxies: temporal proximity, knowledgeable source, routine production rather than after-the-fact reconstruction, an accountable recordkeeping process, and a residual trustworthiness challenge. The advisory discussion is especially relevant to agent systems: if an outside informant is not acting within the reliable routine, accurately copying that informant into a business record does not repair the broken evidentiary link ([FRE 803 advisory notes on source information](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

Rule 803(7) adds a carefully bounded negative inference: absence from a regularly kept record can support that a matter did not occur only when matters of that kind were regularly recorded and circumstances do not undermine trustworthiness ([FRE 803(7)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)). This is a useful corrective to the common but invalid inference “there is no log entry, therefore it did not happen.”

### 3. The rule of completeness answers “is a true fragment misleading without its context?”

Rule 106 lets an adverse party require, at the same time, another part or related statement that fairness requires considering with the offered fragment. The committee note identifies two reasons: fragments can create a misleading impression when taken out of context, and later repair can be inadequate ([FRE 106 and advisory note](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)). After the 2023 amendment, necessary completion can overcome a hearsay objection; the rule is concerned with correcting a distortion when the initially selected fragment misleads ([FRE 106, 2023 committee note](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)).

In its own field this is not “include the whole file.” It is relational: include the parts needed to understand the offered proposition fairly. A statement such as “I owned the weapon” may mislead if separated from the contemporaneous continuation “but sold it months earlier,” the rule’s committee-note example. The key idea is that selection boundaries themselves carry epistemic risk.

### 4. Original-record preference answers “what if only a reconstruction survives?”

Rule 1002 requires an original writing, recording, or photograph when proving its contents ([FRE 1002](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1002)). It is a rule of preference scoped to proof of recorded content, not a universal demand that every event be proved from a document; the advisory note distinguishes proving an event through a witness from proving the content of its written record.

Rule 1004 then allows other evidence of content when originals are lost or destroyed without the proponent’s bad faith, cannot be obtained, are controlled by a notified opposing party who does not produce them, or are not closely related to a controlling issue ([FRE 1004](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1004)). In its own field, this avoids the two extremes of pretending a recollection is equal to a surviving original and making all proof impossible after innocent loss.

For recovery, the important idea is not the legal label “best evidence”—a potentially misleading shorthand—but explicit provenance degradation: a primary tool receipt is different from an agent’s later summary of that receipt, and loss of the primary should be recorded rather than silently flattened.

The core transfer is **very likely (80–90%) conceptually sound** because the cited legal rules independently address identity, source reliability, contextual completeness, and degradation from primary to secondary material—four separate questions that also exist after a crash. The evidence is the direct structural mapping above and the rules’ explicit separation of those questions. The claim that this improves a real harness remains **UNVERIFIED: settle it with the controlled crash corpus and mutation tests under “Evaluation.”**

The whole analogy is **almost certainly not (1–5%) sufficient as a complete recovery protocol**. The evidence is the live-world and action-causes-side-effects disanalogies: a court can weigh a historical receipt, while a recovery worker must also determine whether the receipt’s represented state still holds and whether retrying is safe. No chain-of-custody doctrine supplies idempotency, concurrency control, compensation, or a current-state oracle.

## UNVERIFIED — proposal: a recovery evidence envelope and admission gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a small hook-bus component that emits an append-only, per-run JSONL **recovery evidence envelope** around existing tool calls and artifacts. It is not a second orchestrator, scheduler, database, or daemon. On restart, a skill or hook reads the envelope, applies deterministic admission rules, and gives the replacement worker an evidence table plus the safest next action. The `<~200 lines` size and availability of adequate pre/post hook events are **UNVERIFIED: settle them with a spike that captures one read, one file write, one MCP call, one subagent handoff, and one killed process on the actual hook API; if the complete implementation exceeds 200 nonblank source lines or needs a resident process, retag and reconsider rather than smuggling in BUILD-LARGE.**

### Minimal record vocabulary

Each record should contain only fields needed to authenticate, contextualize, and assess a recovery claim:

- `run_id`, `attempt_id`, `worker_id`, monotonic `seq`, wall-clock timestamp, and `record_type`;
- `parent_hash` and a hash of the canonical current record, forming a per-attempt hash chain;
- for an intended operation: tool identity, canonicalized argument hash, redacted argument pointer, impact class (`read`, `reversible-write`, `irreversible/external`), and an idempotency key when the target supports one;
- for a result: matching invocation ID, tool-native status/exit code, raw stdout/stderr or content-addressed pointer, and result hash;
- for an artifact: absolute or workspace-relative identity as appropriate, before hash when available, after hash, and whether the bytes were independently read back;
- for a handoff: parent/child attempt IDs, exact delegated objective hash, and returned-result hash;
- for a checkpoint claim: proposition (`file X now hashes to H`, `message M accepted by service S`, `tests T passed`), evidence references, provenance grade, and disposition;
- for a gap or degradation: expected sequence or primary evidence that is missing, why it is missing if known, and which claims become inadmissible as a result.

Secrets should not be duplicated into the envelope; canonical hashes and protected pointers preserve linkage while leaving secret handling to the existing substrate. Whether hashes alone give enough debugging context without leaking sensitive arguments is **roughly even (45–55%)**, because the result depends on current redaction and storage facilities, which this ideation unit intentionally did not inspect. **UNVERIFIED: settle it by replaying 100 representative tool calls through the spike and having an operator score recoverability and secret exposure.**

### Provenance grades: do not flatten narration and receipts

Use a small, explicit grade set:

- **P0 — live observation:** a post-restart probe of the authoritative target now;
- **P1 — primary contemporaneous record:** tool-native response or artifact bytes captured automatically at the time, linked to the invocation;
- **P2 — authenticated derivative:** deterministic parse, hash, or summary whose source P1 record is still present;
- **P3 — reconstructed assertion:** worker summary, transcript recollection, or imported claim whose primary source is unavailable;
- **PX — excluded:** broken chain, call/result mismatch, missing required context, corruption, or a source/method that indicates untrustworthiness.

This is a provenance ordering, not a claim that P1 is true. A tool may return success before durable completion; an API receipt may only mean “accepted”; and a worker can pass a false input to a perfectly logged tool. That is why consequential present-state propositions require P0.

### Admission rules

1. **Authenticate before use.** Verify run/attempt identity, sequence continuity, record hashes, parent hashes, call/result linkage, and artifact hashes. A broken chain does not erase all later context, but dependent completion claims become `PX` until independently re-established.
2. **Apply the completeness neighborhood.** A result cannot be offered alone. Include its invocation, stderr/status, artifact or external receipt, later reversal/compensation records, and any record that changes the proposition’s meaning. Do not dump the entire transcript; select the necessary relational neighborhood, mirroring Rule 106 rather than equating completeness with volume.
3. **Challenge source trustworthiness.** Automatically captured tool output may be P1. Free-form worker claims remain P3 unless anchored to P1/P0. A wrapper’s assertion about an upstream service does not become P1 evidence of the upstream outcome merely because the wrapper logged it faithfully; this is the software counterpart of the unreliable outside informant embedded in a regular business record.
4. **Prefer the primary, disclose degradation.** If P1 survives, recover from it. If only P3 survives, it may guide cheap discovery but cannot justify skipping or repeating a consequential mutation. Record why the primary is unavailable.
5. **Permit negative inference only from a validated complete recorder.** “No success record” means “not proven,” not “did not happen,” unless the operation class is one for which the recorder is known to emit a record on every outcome and an independent authoritative query confirms absence. This borrows Rule 803(7)’s condition that the missing matter be of a kind regularly recorded.
6. **Separate admission from weight.** Imperfect but intelligible evidence can inform planning; it need not authorize action. For read-only work, P2/P3 may be enough to resume or cheaply repeat. For reversible local writes, require P1 plus a fresh target check. For irreversible or external effects, require P0 authoritative reconciliation and use an idempotency key when available; otherwise pause for owner review.
7. **Never infer present truth from custody alone.** Before declaring a task done, skipping a step, or retrying a non-idempotent effect, probe the authoritative current state using a mechanically independent signal from the historical recorder.

The graded gate is **likely (55–80%) to preserve useful partial work without converting every log gap into a hard stop**, because the legal source itself distinguishes admissibility from weight and permits secondary evidence after explained loss. **UNVERIFIED: settle it by measuring recovered useful steps and unsafe actions in the crash-injection matrix.**

### Recovery output shown to the replacement worker

The reader should generate a compact table, not a prose “resume summary”:

| Claim | Grade | Evidence | Missing/context | Permitted use | Required next probe |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| “Patch bytes H were written to path X” | P1 | invocation/result/artifact hashes | no later target read | planning only | read X and compare H |
| “External request K was accepted” | P1 | service receipt linked to call | final processing unknown | do not repeat blindly | query service by K |
| “All tests passed” | P3 | worker summary only | raw test output lost | discovery hint | rerun scoped tests |
| “Child task finished” | PX | completion event without returned-result hash | payload missing | none | inspect child artifact/result store |

This format forces each proposed next action to reveal its evidentiary basis. It also applies the owner’s two-signal discipline naturally: a historical capture and a fresh target probe are mechanically independent signals for consequential completion.

## What it would catch that a summary/checkpoint path misses

These are predicted detection capabilities, not claims about the uninspected local harness.

1. **Success narration after failed or partial execution.** A worker says “written” or “sent,” but no matched result/receipt and no postcondition exist. The gate excludes the completion claim. Detection is **very likely (80–90%)** when the relevant pre/post hooks execute, because the rule is a direct structural check for missing linked evidence. **UNVERIFIED: kill the worker between invocation, response, and postcondition in three separate trials.**
2. **Duplicate non-idempotent retry after an ambiguous timeout.** An invocation exists but response is absent. The system labels the outcome unknown rather than failed and requires authoritative lookup by idempotency key before retry. Prevention is **likely (55–80%)**, because it depends on the target exposing lookup or idempotency. **UNVERIFIED: test one API with native idempotency and one without, killing the connection after server acceptance.**
3. **Mismatched tool result or stale checkpoint.** Invocation/result IDs, sequence, and parent hashes reveal that a result belongs to another attempt or that an older checkpoint omitted later activity. Detection is **very likely (80–90%)** for accidental substitution or truncation, because deterministic linkage and hashes directly cover those faults. **UNVERIFIED: mutation-test swapped results, deleted middle records, and replayed prefixes.**
4. **Misleading fragment.** A successful command line is followed by stderr, rollback, compensation, or a later failing verification that a summary omitted. The completeness neighborhood brings the related record into the recovery decision. Detection is **likely (55–80%)**, because “related” records can be linked mechanically for known record types but semantic context may exceed those links. **UNVERIFIED: seed paired success/rollback and success/later-verification-failure traces and measure whether the reader presents both.**
5. **Faithfully logged hearsay.** A wrapper reports that an upstream action completed, but only the wrapper’s own acceptance is evidenced. Source grading stops the wrapper receipt from being promoted to proof of the upstream state. Detection is **likely (55–80%)** for integrations whose response schema distinguishes acceptance from completion. **UNVERIFIED: contract-test each consequential integration’s status semantics.**
6. **Lost subworker payload masked by a completion flag.** A child “done” event without the returned-result or artifact hash is excluded. Detection is **very likely (80–90%)** if handoffs use the envelope fields. **UNVERIFIED: kill parent and child at each handoff boundary and check that no payload-less completion is admitted.**
7. **Invalid inference from silence.** Missing “write complete” is treated as unknown unless the recorder’s completeness has been validated and the target independently lacks the effect. This would **very likely (80–90%)** prevent the specific logical error “no record means no action,” by construction. **UNVERIFIED: drop success records after real effects and ensure recovery reconciles rather than repeats.**
8. **Post-crash drift.** A once-valid file hash, branch head, ticket state, or remote object changed after the checkpoint. The required P0 probe catches the divergence before action. Detection is **likely (55–80%)**, limited by whether the authoritative system offers a sufficiently precise query. **UNVERIFIED: mutate each target after the last P1 record and before restart.**

The mechanism will **almost certainly not (1–5%) detect a semantically wrong plan merely because it was perfectly recorded**. Hash chains prove linkage and alteration evidence, not goal correctness. It will also **very unlikely (5–20%) resolve an ambiguous external side effect when the target offers neither idempotency nor queryable receipts**, because no evidentiary transformation can manufacture a missing authoritative fact. In that case the correct recovery result is explicit uncertainty and owner review, not autonomous guessing.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy can mislead

### “Chain of custody” can become security theater

A plain hash chain detects many accidental edits only if a trusted head or surviving anchor makes replay/truncation visible. If an attacker or faulty process can rewrite the entire file and its head, internal hashes authenticate nothing beyond self-consistency. This owner’s target problem names worker/session/machine death, not a hostile administrator, so a local append-only record plus externally stored checkpoint head may be proportionate. Protection against a machine-level adversary is **unlikely (20–45%) to be worth expanding this unit into signatures, remote transparency logs, or a service**, because that would change the threat model and risk BUILD-LARGE. **UNVERIFIED: settle the actual threat requirement with a threat-model review; do not infer it from the legal metaphor.**

### Courts can tolerate gaps; automation sometimes cannot

The Snowden opinion says chain gaps often affect weight rather than admissibility. Blindly copying that tolerance would be dangerous: a jury weighing an exhibit is not itself retrying a payment. The proposed gate therefore borrows the admission/weight distinction but sets engineering permissions by impact. Gappy evidence may guide a read-only investigation; it cannot authorize an irreversible retry.

### Routine production is only a proxy

Rule 803(6)’s regular-practice conditions address a human institutional reliability problem. Automated capture is more repeatable, but systematic bugs can make every record consistently wrong. “Machine-generated” is not synonymous with “true.” The Rule 901(b)(9) analogue—describe the process and show that it produces an accurate result—therefore maps to recorder control tests and mutation tests, not to trusting a logger because it exists.

### Completeness has no natural software adversary

Rule 106 assumes an adverse party motivated to expose a misleading selection. Recovery usually has one worker reading its predecessor. The transfer works only if the reader itself performs a deterministic completeness challenge: for every offered claim, seek linked invocation, outcome, stderr, artifact, compensation, and later contradiction. Semantic omissions outside those links remain possible. A trained critic or second orchestration runtime would violate the anti-overfit gate and is not proposed.

### Historical proof cannot replace live reconciliation

Legal evidence primarily reconstructs an event for adjudication. Recovery uses history to choose a future action in a concurrently changing environment. This is the decisive disanalogy and the reason P0 exists. A receipt that was authentic at 10:00 does not prove the object still exists at 10:05. Where no authoritative live probe exists, the system must expose uncertainty.

### Do not paste legal burdens of proof onto confidence scores

The Supreme Court has described preponderance, clear-and-convincing evidence, and beyond-reasonable-doubt as a continuum that allocates the risk of error according to the interests at stake; in *Addington v. Texas*, the Court selected an intermediate standard for involuntary civil commitment because an ordinary civil standard allocated too much error risk to the individual while the criminal standard could create an unreasonable barrier under diagnostic uncertainty ([*Addington v. Texas*, 441 U.S. 418, 423–433](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/441/418)). The transferable principle is asymmetric error cost, which supports stricter recovery permissions as side effects become less reversible.

The legal verbal standards themselves are **unlikely (20–45%) to improve a harness if mapped directly to model confidence labels**, because they are institutional instructions to factfinders, not calibrated numeric probabilities or transactional guarantees. **UNVERIFIED: a direct mapping could only be justified by a calibration experiment relating declared confidence, evidence grade, and actual recovery correctness.** Accordingly, this design uses evidence provenance plus action impact, not labels such as “beyond reasonable doubt.”

## Anti-overfit gate and rejected expansions

The proposal passes the gate at the design level: one append-only JSONL file, content-addressed raw records or pointers, a hook writer, and a restart reader ride the existing hook/skill/MCP bus. It does not propose a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Explicitly rejected alternatives:

- **Full event-sourced workflow engine — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification).** Rejected here. It would provide stronger transactional semantics but reintroduces the specifically disfavored durable-execution/second-runtime class. The law-of-evidence borrow only needs provenance and an admission gate; it does not justify owning scheduling and execution.
- **Remote notarization/transparency service — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification).** Rejected absent an adversarial machine threat model. It adds an external availability dependency to crash recovery.
- **Vector/KG recovery memory — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification).** Rejected. Semantic retrieval does not establish authenticity, completeness, or current state, and it is a previously rejected component class.
- **Second-model evidentiary critic — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification).** Rejected. It adds probabilistic narration where deterministic linkage checks are available and risks correlated model errors.
- **Transcript-only checkpoint template — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Rejected as the sole mechanism. A template may improve handoff prose but cannot independently capture tool-native results or distinguish primary from reconstructed evidence.
- **Legal-burden confidence ladder — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Rejected. Renaming model confidence “preponderance,” “clear and convincing,” or “beyond reasonable doubt” imports legal rhetoric without calibration; only the underlying idea of allocating error risk by consequence is retained.

The small design is **likely (55–80%) to remain useful as models improve**, because it constrains evidence provenance at tool boundaries rather than encoding a large model-specific planning workflow. This is forward-looking and **UNVERIFIED: settle it by running the same crash matrix across at least two materially different model generations or model families and comparing effect sizes.**

## Evaluation: the experiment that can falsify the recommendation

### Baselines

Compare three conditions on identical tasks:

1. transcript or prose checkpoint only;
2. ordinary unlinked tool log plus checkpoint;
3. recovery evidence envelope plus admission gate.

### Crash matrix

Inject termination at every boundary for representative operations:

- before invocation record;
- after intent but before tool dispatch;
- after external/local effect but before tool response;
- after response but before postcondition;
- during artifact recording;
- after child completion flag but before payload transfer;
- after checkpoint but before a later compensation or verification record;
- after machine-local record write but before any external anchor, if an anchor is used.

Cover read-only retrieval, reversible local write, test execution, Git operation, MCP/API call with idempotency, API call without idempotency, and parent/child handoff. Mutate logs by deleting a middle record, swapping results, replaying an old prefix, changing an artifact, and inserting a truthful but misleading fragment without its later reversal.

### Outcome measures

- unsafe duplicate irreversible effects;
- false “done” declarations;
- false “not done” declarations;
- correctly preserved useful completed steps;
- unnecessary repeated work;
- recoveries halted for owner review;
- recovery latency and bytes written per tool call;
- secret leakage in the evidence envelope;
- detector precision/recall on each seeded mutation.

Before claiming any detector works, validate it on a known-present control and use two mechanically independent signals. Example: for “external effect exists,” pair the historical service receipt with a fresh authoritative query; for “artifact complete,” pair the captured after-hash with a post-restart read and hash. Re-running the same parser is not an independent signal.

### Decision rule

Adopt the envelope only if, across the crash matrix, it reduces the combined count of unsafe duplicates and false completion declarations without materially increasing owner escalations or blocking recovery of valid read-only work. The exact acceptable tradeoff is owner-dependent and **roughly even (45–55%) without measured costs**. **UNVERIFIED: settle the threshold before the experiment—for example, zero seeded irreversible duplicates, at least 95% detection of false completion mutations, and no more than a predeclared percentage increase in manual escalations—so the result cannot be rationalized after the fact.**

If the envelope cannot be implemented on existing hooks in the BUILD-SMALL bound, or if tool-boundary records omit the critical period after effect but before response, stop and return the result to architecture review. Do not silently expand into a durable workflow runtime. A negative result would mean the borrow identifies a useful evaluation doctrine but not a viable harness component.

## Bottom line

Law of evidence has not solved crash recovery. It has solved a narrower, transferable epistemic problem: how to avoid treating every surviving assertion alike when the original actor is unavailable. Authentication/chain of custody asks whether the record is the claimed record; the regularly conducted records exception asks why an unavailable maker’s record deserves trust; completeness guards against a true but misleading fragment; and original-record preference makes provenance degradation explicit.

Translated conservatively, those mechanisms yield a small recovery admission gate: automatically capture linked contemporaneous tool evidence, grade primary versus derivative versus reconstructed claims, bring necessary context with each claim, treat silence as unknown unless recording completeness is established, and reconcile consequential claims against authoritative live state. The design’s expected benefit is **likely (55–80%)**, based on the strong structural match and direct coverage of enumerated crash faults, but it remains **UNVERIFIED: only the specified crash-injection, mutation, and live-reconciliation experiment can establish benefit on this harness.**

The analogy must stop there. It does not supply truth, idempotency, concurrency control, or current state. Any design branded “chain of custody” that omits the independent live probe is not a recovery mechanism; it is a better-preserved story.
