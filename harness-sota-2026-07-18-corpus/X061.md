# X061 — Cross-domain borrow: epidemiology → agent reliability

**Unit:** X061  
**Source field:** epidemiology, especially public-health surveillance and diagnostic-test reasoning  
**Target failure:** an agent confidently reports absence, breakage, or completion from one unvalidated probe  
**Scope:** ideation lens only. Per the unit instruction, this analysis does not inspect or make claims about the local subject repository. The six-plus observed failures and the mandatory two-signal rule are treated as pinned inputs, not re-measured here.

## Executive judgment

The borrow transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form.

The specific epidemiological mechanism is **tiered surveillance case classification backed by a sensitive screen, serial confirmation, positive/negative run controls, and predictive-value reasoning conditioned on prevalence**. Epidemiology does not treat every initial signal as a final diagnosis. It defines what counts as a suspected, probable, or confirmed case; deliberately trades sensitivity against specificity according to the operational stage; checks whether the detecting procedure was capable of producing a valid result; and interprets a positive result in light of the event's base rate.

The concrete harness translation is an **evidence case definition for consequential agent claims**:

1. A single probe creates a *suspected finding*, never a confirmed conclusion.
2. A probe whose known-present/known-absent controls fail creates an *invalid finding*, not negative evidence.
3. A consequential claim becomes *confirmed* only when two signals cross different mechanical failure domains and both probes' controls pass.
4. The threshold changes with the decision cost: broad/sensitive discovery is acceptable during search, but final claims of “absent,” “broken,” or “done” require specific confirmation.
5. The harness records the reference class (the base rate and prior evidence) so that a rare claim produced by a noisy detector is visibly weak even when the detector looks impressive in isolation.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent the exact class of single-unvalidated-probe overclaim in the pinned observations, because the proposed state machine makes a lone probe and a failed detector control structurally incapable of yielding `confirmed`; the remaining uncertainty is whether agents and hooks will consistently route all consequential prose claims through the gate. The settling experiment is a blinded replay of the six-plus known incidents plus matched valid conclusions, measuring false confirmations, false blocks, and bypass rate.

This is **unlikely (20-45%)** to solve general agent hallucination or faulty reasoning, because its protection applies only where a claim can be operationalized as a detectable event and checked through genuinely distinct evidence channels. The settling experiment is a stratified replay across detector failures, inference errors, semantic misunderstandings, and creative tasks, reporting effect by stratum rather than one aggregate score.

## 1. What epidemiology has already solved

### 1.1 Surveillance uses operational case definitions, not intuition

WHO defines a surveillance case definition as diagnostic criteria used to decide whether an individual counts as a case; criteria can combine clinical, laboratory, time, place, and person information ([WHO IHR Benchmark glossary](https://ihrbenchmark.who.int/document/glossary)). CDC likewise explains that standardized case definitions allow cases to be classified and counted consistently across jurisdictions ([CDC, What is Case Surveillance?](https://www.cdc.gov/nndss/what-is-case-surveillance/index.html)).

The important mechanism is operationalization. “Looks like the disease” is replaced by a published predicate over specified evidence. Surveillance definitions are also explicitly distinct from clinical diagnosis and individual care. CDC's tularemia definition, for example, says surveillance definitions exist for consistent classification and counting and are not intended to make an individual clinical diagnosis ([CDC, 2026 Tularemia Case Definition](https://ndc.services.cdc.gov/case-definitions/tularemia/)). This separation prevents an observation collected for one purpose from silently acquiring more authority than it has.

Case definitions can carry certainty levels. WHO describes case classification as gradations such as possible, probable, and confirmed, particularly useful when early reporting matters but definitive tests are difficult ([WHO, *Surveillance*](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/329403/9789289054454-eng.pdf?sequence=1)). CDC's outbreak teaching similarly says investigators often create confirmed, probable, and possible/suspect categories to represent uncertainty ([CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 6 §2](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)).

This is not merely vocabulary. Classification prevents urgency from forcing false certainty. A suspected case can trigger collection and triage without being counted as laboratory-confirmed.

### 1.2 The definition changes with the phase and decision

Early outbreak investigation favors a broad, sensitive case definition so investigators do not miss the signal. Later analytic work tightens the definition because false-positive cases can distort causal analysis ([CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 6 §2](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)). WHO's outbreak guidance makes the trade explicit: broad definitions are used so outbreaks are not missed; false-positive signals are expected and sent to verification and investigation ([WHO, *Outbreak surveillance and response*](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/70812/WHO_HSE_GAR_DCE_2012_1_eng.pdf%3Bsequence%3D1)). WHO also recommends refining a case definition as evidence develops and logging and communicating changes ([WHO, Stages of an outbreak investigation](https://www.who.int/emergencies/outbreak-toolkit/standardized-data-collection-tools/investigating-outbreak-of-unknown-disease)).

Thus, epidemiology separates **signal generation** from **claim confirmation**. High sensitivity is valuable at the first stage; high specificity becomes more important before expensive or consequential action.

### 1.3 Sensitivity and specificity are not enough; predictive value depends on the base rate

CDC defines:

- sensitivity as the fraction of true cases or outbreaks the system detects;
- specificity as the fraction of noncases it correctly excludes; and
- predictive value positive (PVP) as the fraction of reported signals that are actually cases or outbreaks ([CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 5 Appendix A](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/appendixa.html)).

CDC's surveillance-system evaluation guideline states that PVP reflects the sensitivity and specificity of the case definition **and the prevalence of the event in the population under surveillance**. It also explains the operational harm of low PVP: false reports consume investigation resources and can create pseudo-outbreaks ([CDC, Updated Guidelines for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/preview/MMWRhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

That base-rate dependence is the core correction to “the probe is 95% accurate, therefore its result is 95% trustworthy.” Those are different conditional probabilities.

Consider a deliberately hypothetical agent detector:

- Only 1% of inspected capabilities are truly absent.
- The detector has 95% sensitivity for absence.
- It has 95% specificity, so it falsely calls 5% of present capabilities absent.

Among 10,000 checks, approximately 95 of 100 true absences are detected, but 495 of 9,900 present items are also called absent. The positive predictive value of an “absent” result is therefore:

`95 / (95 + 495) = 16.1%`.

The numerical example is not an estimate of this harness. It demonstrates the algebraic consequence of a rare target event. A detector can have apparently strong sensitivity and specificity while most of its positive “absence” findings are wrong.

### 1.4 Serial confirmation raises specificity—only under the right dependence structure

Classical screening distinguishes tests in series from tests in parallel. Requiring serial tests to agree increases specificity but decreases sensitivity; treating either parallel test as sufficient increases sensitivity but decreases specificity ([CDC Stacks, *Evaluation of Screening for Disease*](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/73522/cdc_73522_DS1.pdf)). This is why an inclusive screen can be followed by a more specific confirmation test.

In the hypothetical example above, if two tests each have 95% sensitivity and 95% specificity **and their errors are conditionally independent**, requiring both to report absence yields:

- joint sensitivity: `0.95 × 0.95 = 90.25%`;
- joint false-positive rate: `0.05 × 0.05 = 0.25%`;
- PVP at 1% prevalence: `(0.01 × 0.9025) / ((0.01 × 0.9025) + (0.99 × 0.0025)) = 78.5%`.

The improvement disappears when errors are fully correlated. Two commands that call the same parser, read the same stale index, inherit the same permission failure, or merely reformat the same API response are one test repeated, not two independent confirmations. Under perfect error correlation, the illustrative PVP remains 16.1%, not 78.5%.

CDC's outbreak-surveillance framework says sensitivity measurement requires an alternative high-quality data source to find outbreaks missed by the system, and discusses two independent sources for capture-recapture assessment ([CDC, Framework for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems for Early Detection of Outbreaks](https://www.cdc.gov/mmWR/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5305a1.htm)). The relevant lesson is not that every pair is mathematically independent; it is that an allegedly confirmatory source must add information rather than reproduce the first detector's blind spot.

### 1.5 Controls establish whether a negative or positive result is interpretable

Laboratory quality control adds a second, distinct idea: validate the *run*, not only the specimen result. WHO recommends positive and negative controls and notes that controls should be processed like samples; controls can be selected to exercise extraction and other process stages ([WHO, *Laboratory Quality Management System Handbook*](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/44665/9789241548274_eng.pdf?sequence=1)).

CDC's *Candida auris* PCR procedure is especially concrete: positive and negative extraction controls are processed with specimens; if expected control results do not occur, specimen results are invalidated and testing starts again from the relevant stage ([CDC, Real-Time PCR Based Identification of *Candida auris*](https://www.cdc.gov/candida-auris/hcp/laboratories/real-time-pcr-identification.html)). The scientific move is crucial: a failed positive control does not mean all samples are negative. It means the run cannot support a conclusion.

For an agent detector, “find a known-present capability before accepting zero detected capabilities” is the direct analogue of a positive run control. A detector that misses the control has not produced evidence of absence; it has produced evidence that the detector or its execution context is invalid.

## 2. Concrete translation to an agent harness

### Proposal 1 — Evidence case definitions for consequential claims

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add a compact policy/rule to the existing agent instruction bus. It defines an evidence state machine for the claim classes `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, and `DONE`.

The rule should be operational rather than exhortative:

| State | Harness case definition | Permitted language/action |
|---|---|---|
| `SIGNAL` | One observation indicates the claim, but its detector has not been controlled or independently confirmed. | “Initial signal” only; continue investigation. |
| `SUSPECTED` | One valid probe supports the claim and its relevant controls pass. | Report inline as suspected; no definitive “is absent/broken/done.” |
| `PROBABLE` | Two valid signals support the claim but share a plausible fault domain, or one strong signal plus indirect corroboration. | Report as probable with the shared dependency named. |
| `CONFIRMED` | Two valid, mechanically independent signals support the same claim; each relevant detector control passes; scope and time are explicit. | Definitive conclusion allowed. |
| `INVALID` | A positive/negative control fails, required output is malformed, permissions are uncertain, or the probe did not exercise the intended path. | Do not use the result for the target claim; diagnose detector/run. |
| `DISCORDANT` | Valid signals disagree. | Preserve both results, do not average/vote, investigate the disagreement. |

“Mechanically independent” should be defined by failure domains, not command count. Two signals qualify only when they differ in at least the mechanism relevant to the suspected fault. Examples:

| First signal | Qualifying second signal | Non-qualifying repetition |
|---|---|---|
| Filesystem enumeration through one shell/tool | Direct read of an authoritative manifest or enumeration through a different runtime/API | Same tool with a different glob pattern |
| Runtime invocation fails | Static registration/config evidence plus a separately exercised known-good sibling through the same runtime | Re-running the same invocation |
| File content check | Parser/loader behavior that consumes the file, or a second parser implementation | Another text search over the same bytes |
| Task implementation observed | Targeted behavior test plus diff/requirements trace | Agent restates that it edited the file |
| Network tool says “not connected” | Server/process/transport inspection from a separate control plane | Same client call with a longer timeout |

This policy is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve the epistemic wording of final reports because the allowed language is directly tied to evidence states rather than left to model discretion. The evidence is the epidemiological utility of standardized classifications and the direct structural match to the pinned failure mode. The settling experiment is a randomized replay comparing current instructions against the case-definition rule on known and adversarial probe failures, scored for unsupported definitive claims.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that CONFIG alone will be followed reliably enough to produce a material reduction, because modern agents usually follow explicit structured rules but can omit bookkeeping under long contexts. This implementation premise is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is 200 blinded tasks at three context lengths, measuring state-label compliance and unsupported-conclusion rate**.

### Proposal 2 — Controlled-probe protocol

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** For any detector used to support `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, or `DONE`, require an in-run control appropriate to the claim:

- **Positive control:** prove the detector finds a known-present item of the same relevant type and through the same path. Example: before trusting “zero skills found,” demonstrate that the detector finds a fixture or canonical installed skill.
- **Negative control:** where false positives matter, prove the detector rejects a known-absent or deliberately nonmatching item.
- **Process control:** exercise the stages whose failure could masquerade as the target finding—path resolution, permissions, parsing, transport, and output decoding.
- **Invalidation rule:** if a control fails, mark the target result `INVALID`; do not reinterpret it as evidence for or against the target claim.

The positive control must be near the relevant decision boundary. A control proving that a shell can list `/tmp` does not validate a detector that must enumerate plugin metadata through a Windows command-line expansion path. WHO's laboratory handbook makes the analogous point by recommending weak-positive controls near a cutoff and controls capable of detecting extraction-stage errors ([WHO, *Laboratory Quality Management System Handbook*](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/44665/9789241548274_eng.pdf?sequence=1)).

This protocol is **almost certain (90-99%)** to catch a detector that returns zero because its own execution path is broken *when an appropriate known-present control traverses that same path*, because the control will also fail and force `INVALID`. The evidence is deductive from the invalidation rule and the CDC run-control pattern. It is not 100% because a control may fail to exercise the precise branch that broke. The settling experiment injects faults independently at discovery, permissions, parser, transport, and decoding stages, then measures how often the selected control invalidates the run.

Selecting controls that genuinely exercise all relevant stages is **likely (55-80%)** to be tractable for high-frequency detector families but not for every ad hoc probe. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to sample the 50 most frequent consequential probe patterns, design one fixture/control per family, and report stage coverage and maintenance cost**.

### Proposal 3 — Lightweight evidence ledger and pre-final gate — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a small, local, append-only evidence ledger plus a pre-final checker on the existing bus. Do not add an orchestration runtime, database, daemon, or external service.

Each consequential claim records:

```text
claim_id: stable local identifier
claim_type: ABSENT | BROKEN | DONE
scope: exact object and boundary
time: observation time or revision
prior: common | uncommon | rare | unknown, with basis
signal_1: command/tool + result + detector fault domain
control_1: positive/negative/process control + result
signal_2: command/tool + result + detector fault domain
control_2: result
dependence_check: shared parser/index/credentials/runtime/source?
classification: SIGNAL | SUSPECTED | PROBABLE | CONFIRMED | INVALID | DISCORDANT
```

The checker rejects or rewrites a definitive claim when:

- fewer than two valid signals exist;
- both signals share the fault domain relevant to the conclusion;
- a required control is missing or failed;
- scope/time differ between signals;
- the prose says `absent`, `broken`, or `done` while classification is below `CONFIRMED`.

The hook/skill interception point is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a disposable prototype that attempts to inspect a structured pre-final evidence block on the existing bus, with pass/fail criteria of zero silent bypasses across 30 scripted finalizations**. Because this premise is unverified, the proposal heading carries the required tag.

If reliable pre-final interception is unavailable, the fallback remains the CONFIG protocol plus a required evidence block in the agent's final response. The checker can run as an explicitly invoked skill rather than pretending an unenforceable hook exists.

The BUILD-SMALL gate is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce accidental policy bypass beyond instructions alone, because it converts an epistemic norm into a schema check. The evidence is structural, not an implementation benchmark. The settling experiment is an A/B replay measuring unsupported definitive claims and nuisance blocks with CONFIG alone versus CONFIG plus the checker.

### Proposal 4 — Base-rate-aware escalation, not fake precision

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add a qualitative prior field and an escalation rule; do not ask the model to invent numeric probabilities.

- `common`: repeated prior evidence or known unstable component;
- `uncommon`: most comparable checks succeed;
- `rare`: the conclusion contradicts inventory, established state, or a known-present fixture;
- `unknown`: no defensible reference class.

For `rare` or `unknown` claims, require the second signal to be stronger and more direct—for example, authoritative registration data or actual behavior—rather than another weak heuristic. For `common` claims, still require two mechanically independent signals before definitive reporting; the prior adjusts investigation priority, not the honesty threshold.

This qualitative scheme is **likely (55-80%)** to capture the useful direction of base-rate reasoning without inducing spurious numerical confidence, because it forces the reference class to be stated while avoiding fabricated calibration. The settling experiment is to have independent reviewers label priors for a historical sample, measure inter-rater agreement, and test whether the labels predict confirmation rates.

Its reliability as an automated agent-generated field is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the same historical sample with priors hidden, comparing agent labels to reviewer consensus and measuring whether incorrect priors change final decisions**.

### Proposal 5 — Periodic surveillance-system evaluation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Evaluate the claim gate itself on a versioned fixture suite. Track at least:

- sensitivity: fraction of truly absent/broken/incomplete cases eventually identified;
- specificity: fraction of present/working/complete cases correctly excluded;
- predictive value positive: fraction of confirmed harness findings that are true;
- invalid-run rate: fraction of probes rejected because controls failed;
- confirmation latency and tool cost;
- bypass rate: fraction of consequential definitive claims emitted without ledger entries;
- performance by detector family and failure domain, not just aggregate performance.

CDC recommends assessing surveillance sensitivity and PVP using external data and sometimes more than one measurement across fields, sources, combinations, conditions, or years ([CDC, Updated Guidelines for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/preview/MMWRhtml/rr5013a1.htm)). The translation matters because a gate can look successful by blocking nearly everything. Sensitivity, specificity, PVP, cost, and timeliness must be viewed together.

This evaluator is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose a trivial overblocking gate in the fixture suite, because overblocking yields high apparent caution but low sensitivity or unacceptable latency. The remaining uncertainty is fixture representativeness. The settling experiment is prospective scoring on unseen live incidents after thresholds are frozen.

The fixture suite's ability to predict live performance is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a pre-registered four-week shadow deployment comparing fixture metrics with adjudicated live outcomes**.

## 3. What the mechanism catches that the current failure pattern misses

The proposed mechanism targets the epistemic transition from observation to conclusion. It would catch:

1. **Detector-collapse masquerading as absence.** A capability enumerator returns zero because discovery, command invocation, parsing, or permissions failed. The known-present control also fails, so the run becomes `INVALID` rather than “capability absent.”

2. **Two syntactic probes with one mechanical cause.** An agent runs two globs through the same failing expansion path. The dependence check identifies the shared runtime/argument-construction fault domain; the result remains `PROBABLE` or `INVALID`, not `CONFIRMED`.

3. **Stale-index confirmation loops.** Two tools read the same stale inventory. Source provenance reveals that both signals derive from one index; direct filesystem, manifest, or runtime behavior is required.

4. **Permission denial interpreted as nonexistence.** A read/list call cannot access the target. A process control or alternate authority surface distinguishes “not observable from here” from “absent.”

5. **Malformed output interpreted as an empty set.** Output decoding control fails or raw output contradicts the parsed structure, invalidating the conclusion.

6. **Completion inferred from mutation.** A successful write or clean diff is one signal; a targeted behavior test and requirements trace supply distinct confirmation. “Edited” no longer entails “done.”

7. **Rare false alarms from a decent detector.** The prior field makes the low-base-rate problem visible and triggers stronger confirmation rather than false confidence based on detector accuracy alone.

8. **Contradictory evidence hidden by majority vote.** `DISCORDANT` preserves conflict and sends the agent to diagnose shared scope, freshness, or semantics instead of counting votes.

These catches are **very likely (80-90%)** for incidents whose causal structure is a broken detector, shared fault domain, or unjustified completion inference, because each maps directly to a state transition forbidden by the case definition. The settling experiment is fault-injection replay with cause labels blinded from the agent.

They are **unlikely (20-45%)** for wrong conclusions caused by a shared false premise that contaminates every evidence source, because all probes may consistently support the wrong operational definition. The settling experiment is an adversarial suite where manifest, filesystem, and runtime agree but the task's semantic requirement differs.

## 4. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 4.1 Why the structure transfers

The analogy has four genuine structural correspondences:

| Epidemiology | Agent reliability |
|---|---|
| Latent disease/outbreak state is not directly observed | Latent harness state (“exists,” “works,” “complete”) is not always directly observed |
| Tests and reporting systems have false positives/negatives | Tools, parsers, permissions, indexes, and model interpretations fail in both directions |
| Case definition determines what evidence justifies classification | Claim definition determines what evidence justifies definitive prose |
| Event prevalence changes the trustworthiness of a positive signal | Base rate of true absence/breakage changes the trustworthiness of an “absent/broken” detector output |
| Broad screen is followed by investigation/confirmation | Cheap search probe is followed by controlled, independent verification |
| Failed controls invalidate a test run | Failed known-present/process controls invalidate a probe result |

The mapping is not metaphorical decoration: both are inference under imperfect observation with asymmetric error costs.

### 4.2 Where it does not transfer cleanly

**First, biological specimens are more stable than many software states.** A harness can change between two probes. Disagreement may be temporal rather than diagnostic. Therefore every evidence record needs time/revision and the same scope. Two signals separated by a mutation do not confirm the same latent state.

**Second, diagnostic tests often have measured operating characteristics; agent probes usually do not.** Assigning 95% sensitivity to a shell command without benchmark data would be false precision. The harness should use qualitative priors until repeated adjudicated outcomes support calibration.

**Third, “independence” is harder in software.** Different commands can share libraries, environment variables, credentials, caches, or source data. The harness must reason about fault-domain diversity, not tool-name diversity. Conditional independence should never be assumed merely because two tools have different names.

**Fourth, the target labels are semantic.** Disease case definitions can rely on measurable clinical/laboratory criteria. “Done” depends on the owner's requirement, including omissions that no runtime probe can reveal. Completion therefore needs requirements traceability as one signal; test success alone is insufficient.

**Fifth, a gold standard may be absent.** Epidemiological surveillance can use laboratory confirmation, medical records, registries, or expert adjudication, though none is perfect. For some harness claims there is no authoritative oracle. In that case the correct output is `PROBABLE` or an explicitly bounded operational claim, not manufactured confirmation.

**Sixth, repeated tests can cause the state to change.** A diagnostic test generally observes a specimen; an agent “probe” may initialize, cache, repair, authenticate, or mutate the target. Read-only probes should be preferred for confirmation, and probe side effects must be logged.

**Seventh, serial confirmation sacrifices sensitivity and time.** Requiring two signals for every low-stakes statement would create friction and may cause agents to abandon valid findings. The gate should apply to consequential claims of absence, breakage, and completion, while allowing `SIGNAL`/`SUSPECTED` states during exploration.

### 4.3 Conditions under which the borrow should be rejected

Do not apply the mechanism when:

- the claim is subjective or generative and lacks an operational reference state;
- both available observations necessarily share the same unknown fault domain;
- observing changes the state so strongly that two comparable snapshots cannot be obtained;
- the verification cost exceeds the consequence of being wrong and tentative wording is acceptable;
- the base label cannot be adjudicated even after the fact, preventing meaningful evaluation.

Under those conditions, the epidemiology analogy does not justify “two probes.” The agent should state the bounded observation and uncertainty instead.

The judgment that the narrow mechanism transfers is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the matching latent-state, noisy-observation, base-rate, and asymmetric-cost structure. The residual uncertainty comes from semantic target definitions and correlated software failure modes. The settling experiment is a cause-stratified replay that reports benefit separately for detector failure, correlated-source failure, temporal mutation, and semantic misunderstanding.

## 5. Anti-overfit gate and rejected expansions

All proposals ride the existing instruction/hook/skill/MCP bus. None requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The minimal design is intentional:

- a short case-definition policy;
- a small structured evidence record;
- an optional checker below roughly 200 lines;
- a versioned fixture suite and simple counts.

No probabilistic graphical model, learned verifier, agent jury, or epidemiology service is proposed. Those components would add correlated complexity before the basic case-definition discipline is validated.

### Rejected: “ask two agents and take the majority”

This is rejected because models can share training priors, prompts, tools, and source data. It does not establish mechanical independence and can amplify a shared misconception.

### Rejected: numeric Bayesian scoring without measurements

This is rejected because likelihood ratios and priors would be invented. The hypothetical calculation above explains the base-rate effect; it is not a license to manufacture operational probabilities.

### Rejected: confirm every statement

This is rejected because serial confirmation reduces sensitivity and timeliness. Epidemiology itself changes definitions by phase and purpose. The target is consequential state claims, not every observation in working notes.

### Rejected: central evidence database or new reliability daemon

This is rejected by the anti-overfit gate. A per-task local ledger and fixture evaluator can test the mechanism without a new runtime or durable infrastructure.

## 6. Minimum viable experiment

### Phase A — Retrospective blinded replay

Create a labeled set containing:

- the six-plus measured wrong conclusions from single unvalidated probes;
- matched true absence/breakage/completion cases;
- correlated-pair traps where two probes share one fault domain;
- control failures at discovery, permission, parsing, transport, and decoding stages;
- temporal changes between probes;
- semantic “done” failures where tests pass but a requirement is omitted.

Run current policy versus Proposal 1 + Proposal 2. Freeze labels before evaluation. Primary metrics:

- unsupported definitive-claim rate;
- true-confirmation rate;
- invalid-run detection rate;
- time/tool calls to resolution;
- fraction left appropriately `PROBABLE` or `DISCORDANT`.

Acceptance threshold is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment should pre-register a target before replay, suggested starting target: catch at least 5 of the 6 known detector failures with no more than a 10 percentage-point loss in correct definitive conclusions**. The suggested numbers are design targets, not evidence-based performance claims.

### Phase B — Fault-domain ablation

Compare:

1. one probe;
2. the same probe repeated;
3. a different command over the same source;
4. a distinct source through the same runtime;
5. a distinct source and runtime with passing controls.

This tests the central claim that failure-domain diversity, not probe count, creates the gain.

### Phase C — Shadow deployment

Run the ledger without blocking outputs for four weeks. Human-adjudicate all consequential claims, including claims the checker did not recognize. This measures bypass and reveals vocabulary beyond the literal words “absent,” “broken,” and “done.” Only then enable blocking for confirmed high-value patterns.

Shadow mode is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal unrecognized phrasing and excessive friction before enforcement, because it observes real outputs without changing decisions. The settling experiment is the shadow run itself, with a predeclared review sample and bypass taxonomy.

## 7. Decision and concise design specification

**Adopt the narrow borrow.** Do not adopt a generic “epidemiology-inspired” framework.

The essential specification is:

```text
DISCOVER broadly.
CLASSIFY one valid signal as SUSPECTED, not confirmed.
CONTROL the detector with known-present / known-absent / process checks.
INVALIDATE the run when a relevant control fails.
CONFIRM consequential ABSENT, BROKEN, or DONE claims with two signals
  that cross the fault domain implicated by the claim.
RECORD scope, time/revision, provenance, shared dependencies, and qualitative prior.
PRESERVE discordance; do not vote it away.
MEASURE sensitivity, specificity, predictive value, cost, latency, and bypass.
```

This is the smallest form that retains the epidemiological mechanism. Removing case states recreates binary overconfidence. Removing controls lets detector failure masquerade as absence. Removing failure-domain analysis turns “two signals” into ceremonial repetition. Removing base-rate awareness encourages false confidence in rare-event detectors. Adding a new runtime would violate the anti-overfit gate before the mechanism has earned that complexity.
