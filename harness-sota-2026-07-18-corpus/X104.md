# X104 — Cross-domain borrow: metrology → cost-control

**Unit:** X104  
**Source field:** metrology (calibration, traceability, measurement uncertainty, reference standards)  
**Target problem:** spending agent compute and quota where it changes the answer, rather than uniformly  
**Research mode:** external-source ideation only; the local harness/repository was deliberately not inspected, as required by the unit brief  
**Conclusion:** **limited transfer, worth testing.** Borrow metrology's *operating discipline*—a declared target, a calibrated reference chain, a component-wise uncertainty budget, explicit decision risk, and periodic check standards—not its claim to SI-like numerical traceability. The strongest concrete translation is a small, hook-bus “compute metering loop” that buys the next verification action only when calibration data predict that it has enough expected decision value.

## Executive judgment

The mechanism to borrow is not generic “calibration.” It is the joined system of:

1. **target measurement uncertainty**: decide how much uncertainty the use case can tolerate;
2. **an uncertainty budget**: name and quantify the material contributors rather than applying effort uniformly;
3. **metrological traceability**: relate results through a documented, unbroken calibration chain, with each link contributing uncertainty;
4. **decision rules and guard bands**: translate uncertainty into explicit false-accept/false-reject risk rather than pretending a point estimate settles a decision; and
5. **measurement assurance with check standards**: continually test whether the calibration remains valid and adjust the checking interval from observed stability.

This package maps structurally to cost control because both domains must choose how much costly evidence to acquire before making a decision. It does **not** map literally: an agent answer usually has no stable physical measurand, no SI unit, no universally accepted reference value, and no justified law for propagating heterogeneous “uncertainties.” Therefore the harness should use empirical, task-stratified error probabilities and observed marginal gains—not root-sum-of-squares arithmetic over invented confidence numbers.

The limited-transfer judgment is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on two independent bodies of evidence: (a) the primary metrology standards make fitness-for-purpose and economic decision risk explicit, while warning that traceability alone does not establish adequate uncertainty or freedom from mistakes; and (b) LLM calibration research finds both that long-form correctness is graded rather than binary and that larger models are not necessarily better calibrated ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html); [JCGM 106:2012, especially §§8–9](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_106_2012_E.pdf); [Huang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.785/)). It remains **UNVERIFIED for this owner's workload**; the settling experiment is the preregistered shadow replay in “Validation plan” below.

## 1. What metrology has actually solved

### 1.1 Start from a target, not from maximum attainable precision

The International Vocabulary of Metrology (VIM) defines **target measurement uncertainty** as an upper bound selected from the intended use of a measurement result ([VIM 2.34](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.34.html)). This is important: the objective is not “make every measurement as precise as possible.” The objective is “make uncertainty adequate for the decision.” The same vocabulary defines an **uncertainty budget** as a statement of the uncertainty, its components, and their calculation and combination; it can include the measurement model, estimates, uncertainties, distributions, sensitivity coefficients, degrees of freedom, and correlations ([VIM 2.33](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.33.html)).

The Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM) operationalizes that discipline: specify a measurement model, evaluate uncertainty contributions for input quantities, account for correlations, and combine them into uncertainty associated with the output. It treats statistically evaluated (“Type A”) and otherwise evaluated (“Type B”) components consistently, and it requires users of imported values to account for the uncertainty of those values rather than silently treating them as exact ([JCGM 100:2008](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf), §§4–5 and F.2.3).

**How this controls cost in its own field.** Once a laboratory knows the target uncertainty and the contribution of each input, it can spend on the dominant contributors. Improving a component already negligible relative to the combined budget has little value. The VIM explicitly says that, for a branched traceability structure, the effort devoted to each input should be commensurate with its relative contribution to the result ([VIM 2.41, note 4](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). That is the exact cost-control principle this unit seeks.

### 1.2 Traceability is a documented chain with inherited uncertainty

VIM defines **metrological traceability** as the property by which a measurement result can be related to a reference through a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, with every calibration contributing to measurement uncertainty. It requires an established calibration hierarchy ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). This is stronger than merely logging provenance: a link is useful only insofar as its procedure, reference, competence, timing, and uncertainty are known.

Two warnings in the same definition prevent an overenthusiastic transfer:

- traceability does not itself ensure that uncertainty is adequate for the intended purpose; and
- traceability does not ensure the absence of mistakes.

Those warnings are central here. A beautifully logged chain of model calls can still produce a wrong answer, and a benchmark score can be irrelevant to the live task.

### 1.3 Decision rules expose the cost of being wrong near a boundary

JCGM 106 applies measurement uncertainty to conformity decisions. Instead of comparing a measured point estimate naively with a tolerance boundary, a laboratory defines an **acceptance interval** and a **decision rule**. A guard band moves the acceptance boundary to control the probability of a false decision. The guide distinguishes consumer risk (accepting a nonconforming item) from producer risk (rejecting a conforming one). Reducing one generally increases the other, so selecting the operating point is an economic or policy decision, not a fact supplied by the instrument ([JCGM 106:2012](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_106_2012_E.pdf), §§8–9; the worked ball-bearing example in §9.5.4 explicitly requires economic analysis).

**How this controls cost in its own field.** Measurement effort should be purchased where uncertainty can change the conformity decision or materially alter expected loss. A result far from a boundary may need no extra precision. A result near a high-consequence boundary can justify a better method, a repeat measurement, or referral. Guard bands make the trade explicit rather than spending the same amount everywhere.

### 1.4 Check standards detect drift; calibration intervals are evidence-driven

NIST's measurement-assurance practice uses stable check standards as recurring test items. Their observed values control long-term bias and variability; NIST recommends a check standard at each critical level and a persistent record containing the standard, design, instrument, repeatability, operator, environment, and out-of-control flag ([NIST Engineering Statistics Handbook, “Control of artifact calibration”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc35.htm)). Periodic check-standard observations can empirically estimate calibration uncertainty, provided they adequately sample the measurement process ([NIST, “Uncertainty for linear calibration using check standards”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc3672.htm)).

NIST does not prescribe one universal recalibration interval. It says intervals depend on accuracy requirements, stability, environmental factors, and other conditions; it recommends cross-comparisons and control charts, using observed time behavior to set and refine the interval ([NIST, “Recommended Calibration Interval”](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/recommended-calibration-interval)).

**How this controls cost in its own field.** Stable instruments earn longer intervals; unstable instruments or demanding uses receive more frequent checks. This is conditional spending based on demonstrated drift, not a uniform schedule.

## 2. The concrete translation: a traceable compute-metering loop

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: Traceable Compute Meter (BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus))

The recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because no workload-specific calibration has been run. The settling experiment is the shadow replay described later. The implementation should be one small hook plus a versioned tabular calibration artifact; it must not become a second orchestrator.

### 2.1 Define the “measurand” narrowly: residual decision loss

Do not ask the model for a metaphysical probability that “the answer is correct.” For each task class, define an observable outcome and loss:

- code change: tests, hidden regression findings, owner acceptance/correction, severity-weighted defects;
- factual research: citation entailment, source authority/recency, later correction, omitted required claims;
- operational command: postcondition checks and severity of unintended effects;
- design/review: rubric dimensions plus owner correction or downstream defect;
- low-stakes drafting: owner acceptance/edit distance, with a much lower false-accept cost.

The harness's target is then a **maximum tolerated residual expected loss** for the task stratum, not a universal confidence score. A destructive production action and a reversible local edit must have different targets even when the model emits identical confidence language.

This definition is **likely (55-80%)** to be more decision-useful than raw verbalized confidence because post-alignment models have been observed to be overconfident, while long-form answers require graded correctness rather than a binary label ([Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.173/); [Huang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.785/)). It is **UNVERIFIED on agentic coding and operations tasks**; settle it by comparing Brier score, calibration slope, and risk–coverage curves for outcome-based versus verbalized-confidence predictors on held-out owner sessions.

### 2.2 Build an empirical “uncertainty budget,” not a decorative score

At each decision point, record a small set of observable contributors available from the existing bus:

| Contributor | Cheap observable | Candidate next action | What new evidence can reduce it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Requirement ambiguity | unresolved alternatives; missing required input; owner correction pattern | clarify or branch analysis | owner answer or explicit assumption test |
| Evidence traceability | unsupported factual claims; stale/secondary sources; retrieval failure | retrieve primary source | authoritative evidence and entailment check |
| Execution verification | changed paths without relevant test/postcondition | run focused test/read-back | independent observed postcondition |
| Tool-chain integrity | tool errors, truncated output, retry loop, partial result | repair/reroute tool or validate through another mechanism | successful independent probe |
| Solution instability | materially different independent samples/approaches | targeted second solve or discriminating test | agreement on checked facts or experiment |
| Distribution distance | task features outside calibration strata; new model/tool version | conservative route or human review | new labeled examples/check-standard pass |
| Consequence | irreversibility, blast radius, external side effect | stronger verification/approval | consequence is not reducible; it changes the target |

These are *budget rows*, not probabilities. Offline, fit empirical mappings from the vector of rows plus task stratum and action history to residual loss. Use held-out reliability diagrams and risk–coverage curves. Online, expose a conservative interval around predicted residual loss and the calibration stratum/sample count. Do not sum the rows as if they were independent physical variances.

The budget is **likely (55-80%)** to identify where an extra action has value, because it separates reducible epistemic gaps (missing evidence/test) from consequence (which changes tolerance) and from infrastructure faults (which invalidate evidence). The evidence is structural—the GUM's component model and the VIM's “effort commensurate with contribution” rule—rather than a direct agent-harness trial ([JCGM 100:2008](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf); [VIM 2.41 note 4](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). It is therefore **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with feature ablations on held-out replays and retain a row only if removing it materially worsens calibration or compute-normalized loss.

### 2.3 Allocate compute by marginal value of the next action

At a checkpoint, enumerate only actions already supported by the harness bus—for example: stop; focused retrieval; one targeted test; independent read-back; a second model sample; specialist review; ask the owner. For each action `a`, estimate from matched historical strata:

`net_value(a) = expected reduction in decision loss from a − priced incremental compute/tool/time cost(a)`

The policy is:

1. stop when the conservative residual-loss bound is below the task's target;
2. otherwise choose the available action with highest positive estimated net value;
3. stop or defer when no action has positive net value;
4. require approval/deferral when consequence policy says residual risk cannot be accepted, even if further model compute has negative value;
5. cap repeated actions: the same evidence channel cannot be purchased again unless the latest result changed the state used by the policy.

This makes extra compute conditional on expected answer change, not on a generic “hard task” label. A cheap focused test may outrank a costly second full solve. Conversely, repeated sampling should receive near-zero value when errors are strongly correlated.

The policy is **likely (55-80%)** to dominate uniform budgets on a heterogeneous workload if action gains are predictable by stratum; that conditional premise is the entire reason the proposal remains **UNVERIFIED**. The settling experiment is an off-policy replay comparing loss at equal cost and cost at equal loss, with confidence intervals, against fixed-budget and simple consequence-tier baselines. Evidence that adaptive selective answering can optimize correctness under a constrained API budget exists for long-form generation, but it is not evidence for this owner's tool-using workload ([Huang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.785/)).

### 2.4 Attach a “calibration certificate” to each consequential result

For consequential outputs, emit a compact machine-readable record:

```json
{
  "policy_version": "...",
  "model_tool_versions": ["..."],
  "task_stratum": "...",
  "target_loss": "...",
  "calibration_set_id": "...",
  "calibration_n": 0,
  "predicted_residual_loss_interval": [0.0, 1.0],
  "budget_rows": ["..."],
  "actions_bought": ["..."],
  "independent_postconditions": ["..."],
  "out_of_distribution": false,
  "stop_reason": "target_met|no_positive_value|cap|defer"
}
```

“Traceable” here means traceable to a versioned empirical reference set and scoring procedure, **not metrologically traceable to SI**. The certificate allows a later correction to identify which policy, reference set, and evidence chain supported the stop decision.

This record is **very likely (80-90%)** to make routing decisions auditable, because all necessary links are explicit and immutable identifiers can be checked mechanically; its ability to improve answer quality is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because logging alone does not prevent mistakes. The evidence for the distinction is VIM's explicit warning that traceability does not ensure adequate uncertainty or absence of mistakes ([VIM 2.41 notes 5 and 7](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). The quality effect is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it by measuring whether certificate-driven incident reviews produce policy changes that reduce recurrence versus reviews without certificates.

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: Reference tasks as check standards (CONFIG (settings/JSON only))

Maintain a small, versioned panel of stable “check tasks” at critical operating levels: low/high consequence, code/research/operations, tool-heavy/tool-light, familiar/novel, short/long context. Each check task must have an independently adjudicated outcome and an explicit scoring rule. Run the panel after model, prompt, tool, or policy changes and periodically at an interval learned from observed drift. Plot calibration error, residual loss, action selection, and tokens/cost; an out-of-control signal disables adaptive stopping and falls back to conservative fixed policy.

This is the closest direct mapping of NIST check standards: stable reference items at critical levels, a persistent history, and evidence-driven checking intervals ([NIST artifact-calibration control](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc35.htm); [NIST recommended calibration intervals](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/recommended-calibration-interval)). It is **likely (55-80%)** to detect regressions caused by model/tool/prompt changes that ordinary aggregate cost dashboards miss. It is **UNVERIFIED** because agent tasks may be too nonstationary or leak into model context; settle it by injecting known policy regressions and checking detection power and false-alarm rate on a sequestered panel.

## Proposal — UNVERIFIED: Guard-banded stop/defer rules (CONFIG (settings/JSON only))

For decisions with a hard accept/reject boundary, define two thresholds rather than one:

- **accept/act region:** conservative residual-loss bound is below the allowed target;
- **reject/defer region:** the bound is above the target or the task is out of calibration;
- **guard band:** ambiguous middle region buys the highest-value independent check or asks the owner.

The width depends on asymmetric loss. A false-safe destructive action deserves a wider guard band than a false defer on a reversible draft. Do not encode one global “confidence ≥ 0.8” rule.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce high-consequence false accepts if the residual-loss intervals are calibrated, while increasing unnecessary deferrals—the exact producer/consumer trade documented in JCGM 106 ([JCGM 106:2012](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_106_2012_E.pdf), §§8–9). It is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; settle it with decision-curve analysis using owner-specified relative losses and report both false-accept and false-defer rates rather than only average accuracy.

## 3. What this would catch that uniform cost controls miss

These are predicted detection classes, not claims that the current harness lacks them. Whether each is currently missed is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is a two-signal audit consisting of (1) configuration/static policy inspection with a known-present control and (2) behavioral replay showing the actual routing response.

1. **Confident but ungrounded stopping.** Verbal confidence can be high while evidence traceability or test coverage remains poor. The budget routes on observable evidence gaps, not confidence prose. Detection is **likely (55-80%)**, supported by empirical reports of post-alignment overconfidence ([Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.173/)).

2. **Uniform overspending on answers far from a decision boundary.** If a cheap pass is already well inside a calibrated accept region, the meter stops. Savings are **likely (55-80%)** on heterogeneous workloads but **UNVERIFIED**; settle with equal-loss cost comparison on held-out replays.

3. **Uniform underspending on near-boundary, high-loss actions.** Guard bands buy a targeted check when the decision could flip. Risk reduction is **likely (55-80%)** if intervals are calibrated; settle with false-accept rates stratified by consequence.

4. **The wrong kind of extra compute.** A second prose answer may add little when the missing evidence is a test or primary source. Per-action marginal gain can favor the discriminating action. This is **likely (55-80%)** because different actions expose different failure modes, but **UNVERIFIED**; settle by estimating conditional gains for test, retrieval, resampling, and review separately.

5. **Correlated retry loops masquerading as independent evidence.** The policy devalues repeat actions from the same channel unless state changes and records dependence rather than treating repetitions as independent votes. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** mechanically once action identity and state deltas are recorded; evidence is the GUM requirement to account for correlations rather than blindly combine components ([JCGM 100:2008](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf), §5.2).

6. **Calibration drift after a model, prompt, or tool change.** Check-task control charts can trip even when average spend looks normal. Detection is **likely (55-80%)** for regressions represented by the panel, based on NIST check-standard practice; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** for novel failure modes outside the panel. The evidence for both bounds is NIST's requirement that standards cover critical levels and adequately sample the process ([NIST control](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc35.htm); [NIST uncertainty via check standards](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc3672.htm)).

7. **False precision from out-of-distribution routing.** Low sample count, changed versions, or distant task features cause fallback/defer rather than a precise-looking score. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** for declared version/stratum changes and **unlikely (20-45%)** for latent semantic shifts not captured by features; settle with controlled version changes plus adversarially novel tasks.

8. **Diminishing returns.** The meter sees when another action historically does not reduce residual loss enough to cover its cost. Detection is **likely (55-80%)** where matched history is dense and **very unlikely (5-20%)** in sparse novel strata; settle by plotting incremental gain by action ordinal and stratum.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 No stable physical measurand

A mass artifact has a quantity of a defined kind and can be compared through a calibration hierarchy. “Answer quality” is multidimensional, context-dependent, and often changes when requirements are clarified. Long-form answers can be partially correct, which directly breaks binary correctness assumptions ([Huang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.785/)).

**Consequence:** use task-specific observable loss vectors and only aggregate them under an explicit owner utility function. Do not label the resulting score “measurement uncertainty” without qualification.

### 4.2 No universal reference standard or SI chain

Owner corrections, tests, primary sources, and adjudicated rubrics are references, but none is a universal realization of “correctness.” Tests can be incomplete, sources can conflict, and owner preferences can change.

**Consequence:** call this *empirical decision calibration*, not metrological traceability. The reference-set ID and adjudication procedure must be visible. Any claim of SI-equivalent traceability would be **almost certainly not (1-5%)** defensible, because VIM traceability requires a reference and unbroken calibration hierarchy of quantities of the relevant kind ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)).

### 4.3 Uncertainty contributors are dependent and interventions change the object

Retrieval changes the model's context; testing changes the subsequent reasoning; two model samples share training and prompt biases. These are not passive repeated measurements of an unchanged artifact. Root-sum-of-squares propagation would manufacture precision.

**Consequence:** estimate whole-policy outcomes empirically, retain interaction features where supported, and use conservative bounds. A mathematically GUM-like propagation model is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be justified without a causal model and dependence estimates; this band is supported by the GUM's own requirement to model correlations and by the obvious shared model/context of repeated agent actions ([JCGM 100:2008](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf), §§5.1–5.2).

### 4.4 The model's self-report is not a calibrated instrument reading

Post-alignment overconfidence has been observed, and calibration behavior varies by elicitation method and task ([Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.173/); [Tian et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/)).

**Consequence:** verbal confidence may be one feature, never the reference. Prefer externally observable signals and held-out outcome calibration.

### 4.5 Drift is faster and more discontinuous than instrument drift

Hosted model aliases, system prompts, tool APIs, and available sources can change abruptly. A calibration interval learned from gradual drift may fail after a step change.

**Consequence:** version/config changes trigger immediate check tasks; time-based intervals cover only undeclared drift. Declared-change detection is **very likely (80-90%)** if identifiers are available; detection of silent provider changes is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because behavioral checks may notice them but a small panel may not. The latter is **UNVERIFIED**; settle by blind A/B substitution of behaviorally similar model snapshots.

### 4.6 Goodhart pressure and benchmark leakage

If the router is optimized on a small check panel, it can learn panel-specific shortcuts while live performance worsens. A stable check artifact in metrology does not adapt strategically; a model can memorize or infer benchmark patterns.

**Consequence:** sequester tasks, rotate only with adjudicated replacements, use live shadow samples, and measure calibration by stratum. A fixed public panel alone is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to remain sufficient over long periods; evidence is structural (models can train on text benchmarks), and the claim is **UNVERIFIED for this deployment**; settle with contamination probes and performance comparison on newly authored blind tasks.

### 4.7 Cost and latency are themselves contextual

Token price, cache behavior, tool latency, quota scarcity, and owner waiting cost vary. A fixed token budget is not a stable common unit of cost.

**Consequence:** price actions in a vector (`tokens`, monetary cost, wall time, scarce quota, human interruption) and apply an explicit current policy weight. Any scalarization is a policy choice, not a measurement fact.

## 5. Validation plan: the experiment that may kill the proposal

### Phase A — preregister and label

1. Sample completed tasks by task type, consequence, tool intensity, and novelty; split chronologically to preserve a future-like test set.
2. Define outcome rubrics before fitting. Use independent executable postconditions where possible and blinded adjudication otherwise.
3. Record every candidate action's incremental cost and the state before/after it.
4. Establish two baseline policies: fixed low/high budget and a simple consequence-tier rule. The latter is important: a complex calibrator must beat a cheap policy, not merely “always spend more.”

### Phase B — shadow replay

Fit only on the earlier split. In shadow mode, let the meter recommend actions without controlling execution. Evaluate:

- cost at matched severity-weighted loss;
- severity-weighted loss at matched cost;
- calibration intercept/slope and Brier score by stratum;
- risk–coverage and decision curves;
- false-accept and false-defer rates by consequence;
- action-level marginal gain and confidence intervals;
- out-of-distribution fallback frequency;
- sensitivity to model/tool/version changes.

The proposal passes only if it improves both of the first two comparisons' relevant operating region or provides a predeclared safety gain worth its extra cost. It fails if calibration collapses on the chronological holdout, if the simple consequence-tier baseline is indistinguishable, or if the extra bookkeeping costs more than the compute saved.

### Phase C — injected-fault detector validation

Prove each detector with a known-present control before claiming absence or coverage. Inject at least:

- an unsupported high-confidence answer;
- a tool result marked truncated/failed;
- a repeat loop with identical state;
- a changed model/policy version;
- a test-free consequential code change;
- a deliberately out-of-stratum task;
- a case where retrieval helps but resampling does not, and the converse.

Require two mechanically independent success signals for consequential detection claims: (1) the meter emits the expected budget-row/out-of-control signal and (2) the actual route changes to the predeclared action/fallback. A repeated log query is not a second signal.

### Phase D — bounded canary

Only after shadow success, enable routing for reversible low-consequence tasks with a hard spend cap and conservative fallback. Expand by consequence stratum only after enough fresh labels show calibration stability.

The full validation sequence is **likely (55-80%)** to distinguish useful adaptive allocation from an overfit router because it combines chronological holdout, simple baselines, injected faults, shadow evaluation, and bounded canary. It is not guaranteed: adjudication noise and counterfactual action effects remain hard. This claim is **UNVERIFIED**; the experiment itself is the settlement.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and alternatives considered

### Accepted architecture

The Traceable Compute Meter passes the architectural anti-overfit gate *as designed*: one small decision hook, versioned tabular calibration/config, existing actions on the hook/skill/MCP bus, and no second orchestration runtime. It does not require a vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Keeping it under roughly 200 lines is **likely (55-80%)** if offline fitting/export is handled by an existing analysis workflow and the online component only loads a table, records features, selects from existing actions, and emits a certificate. This is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a line-counted spike implementing only the specified online interface. If it cannot fit, prefer a simpler consequence-tier table rather than promoting it to BUILD-LARGE.

### Rejected: full GUM arithmetic over agent “uncertainties”

Rejected because input quantities have no defensible common unit, distributions, or independence model. It would create impressive but false numerical precision. Reconsider only after empirical evidence establishes calibrated component distributions and dependencies across the intended workload.

### Rejected: model self-confidence as the router

Rejected as the sole or primary signal because RLHF model confidence can be overconfident and calibration varies by task/elicitation. It may remain one ablated feature.

### Rejected: always use a larger model or more samples on “hard” tasks

Rejected because larger models are not necessarily better calibrated, self-consistency performs differently across task types, and “hardness” does not identify which next evidence action has value ([Huang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.785/)).

### Rejected: new metrology/observability service (BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification))

This is explicitly rejected, not proposed. A daemon would violate the anti-overfit gate and add a second operational surface before the small hook has demonstrated value. No evidence found in this research justifies it.

### Proposal — UNVERIFIED: consequence-tier budget table (CONFIG (settings/JSON only))

This is a proposal and the required control: route low/medium/high-consequence tasks to fixed verification bundles. It lacks adaptive marginal-value estimation but is transparent and cheap. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to be easier to operate than the calibrated meter, based on its static-table design, and **roughly even (45-55%)** to match the meter's cost–loss frontier; the latter is **UNVERIFIED** and must be settled by the shadow replay. If it matches, choose the table and discard the more elaborate borrow.

## 7. Minimal implementation contract

The online hook should do only this:

1. classify into a declared calibration stratum or `OOD`;
2. read observable budget rows and current action history;
3. load a versioned calibration/action-value table;
4. apply consequence target, guard band, repeat cap, and conservative fallback;
5. recommend one existing action or stop/defer;
6. emit the calibration certificate;
7. never authorize an otherwise unauthorized or destructive action.

It must not:

- invent probabilities when the stratum lacks sample support;
- treat repeated model samples as independent by default;
- bypass existing permission/approval rules;
- turn logging into a new orchestration runtime;
- silently retrain itself online;
- claim metrological or SI traceability;
- optimize token count alone while ignoring latency, scarce quota, or consequence;
- suppress the raw evidence needed to audit its decision.

## 8. Gaps and claims deliberately not made

- The local subject tree was not inspected because this unit explicitly forbids local grounding. Therefore I do **not** claim that the current harness lacks adaptive budgets, calibration records, guard bands, check tasks, or any other mechanism. Determining presence requires the two-signal static-plus-behavioral audit specified above.
- No owner-specific labeled cost/quality dataset was available to this research lens. All workload benefits remain inline-tagged **UNVERIFIED** with settling experiments.
- I did not establish that owner corrections are unbiased ground truth. They are one reference channel and must be combined with executable postconditions and independent adjudication where applicable.
- I found no basis for converting answer-quality contributors into physical-style standard deviations. The recommendation explicitly rejects that transfer.
- The cited agent-calibration studies mainly cover QA/long-form generation, not full tool-using coding and operations trajectories. Extrapolation is therefore limited and marked with confidence bands.
- Monetary prices, model versions, and quota behavior are time-varying; the proposal requires current values but makes no claim about them.

## 9. Bottom line

Metrology's transferable insight is: **precision is not an undifferentiated virtue; it is a budgeted property tied to a use, a reference chain, an uncertainty model, a decision rule, and evidence that calibration still holds.** For an agent harness, translate that into empirical residual-loss calibration and marginal value of the *next distinct evidence action*. Do not translate it into SI rhetoric or algebra over self-reported confidence.
