# K04 — Reconciling contradictions over time

## Executive verdict

The harness does not need another memory product, agent runtime, or hook. It needs a small temporal truth-maintenance layer inside the existing event-ledger/basic-memory/open-loops bus: append every preference assertion as an immutable event; distinguish the time the preference was valid from the time the harness learned it; scope it to the context in which it applies; materialize one current head per preference and scope; retain the displaced value and its reason; and make every affected open thread inherit an explicit `recheck`, `replan`, `blocked`, or `still-valid` disposition. The model may propose a reconciliation, but only an external signal—an exact owner statement, a later owner choice, an execution result, a fresh-context second model, or an owner confirmation—may promote or verify it.

This is a targeted evolution, not a replacement. The local harness already has the right buses and most of the primitives:

* The shared contract says durable decisions and failures go through `note.py`, and superseding milestones retire failures (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:28-34`).
* Basic Memory is declared canonical for semantic continuity, while the event ledger is objective turn history (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:85-91`).
* The current temporal convention writes `superseded_by` on the old note and `valid_as_of` on the new note (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/CLAUDE.md:5-11`).
* The recall index already extracts `superseded_by`, `valid_as_of`, recency, and importance (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/recall_index.py:40-46,222-248,285-314`).
* Open loops already have a durable JSONL store, context predicates, TTL, close semantics, and fail-open behavior (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/_common.py:2-26,41-48,109-175`; `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/add.py:15-24,83-105`).
* Open-loop resurfacing is already folded into the SessionStart dispatcher and emits at most three items, so extending its record/reducer does not consume a seventh hook (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:1-4,6-70`; `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/resurface.py:38-40,51-95`).

The missing unit is not “more recall.” It is deterministic reconciliation between versions and scopes, plus an external-signal promotion rule. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce stale-preference application and forgotten downstream work because the failure mechanism is directly visible locally and the proposed state transitions are testable without trusting same-context introspection; the exact reduction is **UNVERIFIED** until the replay experiment in Finding 7 is run.

## Grounding and evidence discipline

### What the corpus establishes

The corpus covers 11,633 transcript files and 10.62 GB, with zero file-read failures, one bad line, and 32 oversized lines; its own warning says samples are capped and absence claims require checking those counters (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). This is strong coverage for aggregate counts, not a license to treat a mined candidate as an owner preference.

The correction miner saw 8,441 owner-message-shaped records and emitted 2,268 candidates, but explicitly says it is recall-biased and that an unfiltered smoke sample produced 41/41 false positives because agent-authored prompts were misattributed (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10371-10374`). Therefore:

1. `candidate_count=2268` is evidence of a large adjudication surface, not evidence of 2,268 true corrections.
2. No preference may be promoted merely because the miner matched phrases such as “actually,” “no,” “stop,” or “remember.”
3. The exact source span, speaker provenance, timestamp, and later behavior must remain attached to every candidate.

The capped samples nevertheless demonstrate the forms the temporal model must represent without assuming broader frequency: an explicit cancellation (“actually never mind, stop the upload, and delete it”) at a recorded timestamp (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11177-11180`); a scoped preservation command (“wait do not delete the installers”) after tool use (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11201-11204`); and a device-specific exception (“for cross device: i dont want the media to be shared”) (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11207-11210`). These are not proven members of one contradiction chain because the distilled file omits full surrounding turns. They are concrete evidence that cancellation, object scope, and context-specific exceptions exist in the input stream.

The owner also explicitly asks agents not to forget durable findings (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11579-11580`), while the existing open-loop implementation labels forgotten threads as the owner’s primary felt pain (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/_common.py:2-16`). The first signal is a timestamped corpus utterance; the second is an independently implemented durable mechanism. Together they justify treating thread preservation as a hard design invariant.

The corpus’s hooks section is unusable: it is marked `DETECTOR_STATUS: BROKEN` and explicitly says to treat the section as no data (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:13260-13263`). Nothing below infers hook effectiveness or non-use from it.

Finally, a compact deterministic format matters here: StructuredOutput failed on 846 of 3,694 calls (22.9%) (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:103-108`). That supports a flat additive event schema and tolerant reducer, not an over-strict nested fan-out schema.

### What static inspection establishes, with a detector control

A bounded search across the temporal convention, ledger annotator, recall index, and recall consumer found the known-present fields `superseded_by` and `valid_as_of`, proving the detector could find temporal fields. The same output showed no `valid_to`, `recorded_at`, or `preference_scope` in those inspected paths; the independent `note.py` interface accepts only free text, optional preconditions, and failure sequence supersession (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/note.py:25-42`), while the independent recall index emits only path/title/summary/permalink/keywords/mtime/importance/supersession/failure-head/poison fields (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/recall_index.py:300-314`). These two mechanically independent data paths support the limited conclusion: the inspected deployed preference path does not yet encode bitemporal validity or applicability scope as first-class fields. This is not a claim about every file on the Windows machine.

Static inspection also identifies a concrete current-head defect: recall labels either a superseded note or a current failure head as `*contradiction*` (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1656-1673`) and then applies the same 1.6 boost to either (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1693-1710`). It does not follow `superseded_by` to the successor in that function. The indexer independently recognizes that a superseded link is not a chain head (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/recall_index.py:222-248`). Those are two different implementations whose semantics disagree; this claim does not rely on the earlier audit’s assertion.

## Design invariants

Every finding below obeys these invariants:

* **External closure:** no generator grades or promotes its own inference in the same context. Deterministic reducers, execution receipts, a fresh-context model, or the owner close the loop.
* **No new hook:** runtime work folds into existing event-ledger capture, recall, SessionStart/open-loops, or persist-nudge paths. The raw hook count does not increase.
* **Fail-open:** parsing, projection, or resurfacing errors return no injection and exit 0; the immutable source event remains available for later repair.
* **Never drop a thread:** a preference revision may alter or block an open thread, but cannot silently close or garbage-collect it.
* **Prompt-fatigue budget:** owner interruption is reserved for material ambiguity at a decision boundary. Routine revisions are receipted quietly and coalesced.
* **Anti-overfit:** all state is append-only JSONL/Markdown plus deterministic projection on the buses already present. No second orchestrator, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is introduced.

## Findings — inferred-surprise first

### Finding 1 — Preference changes must propagate into open threads, not merely memory search

**improvement:** Add a dependency-preserving “preference impact set” to each open loop and re-adjudicate affected loops whenever a preference head changes.

**what it is:** A temporal preference system is incomplete if it updates what the agent recalls but leaves plans, deferred work, and prospective triggers based on the old preference unchanged. Each open thread gets a compact `depends_on: [preference_key...]` list. When a preference event supersedes a head, the deterministic reducer emits one disposition per dependent loop:

* `still-valid` — no plan change;
* `replan` — preserve the objective, replace affected steps;
* `blocked-by-preference` — keep visible, do not execute;
* `cancel-proposed` — only the owner or an exact explicit cancellation may close it.

The old loop, its original reason, and the change receipt remain in history. A newer preference is therefore an invalidation event, not a deletion event.

**why-inferred:** “Never drop a thread” is a hard constraint in this unit. Independently, the owner asked not to forget findings in the corpus (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11579-11580`), and the installed open-loop layer was built specifically around forgotten-thread stress (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/_common.py:2-16`). The surprising step is to connect preference revision to work-graph invalidation: remembering the new preference alone does not protect already-planned work.

**how-to-build:** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend the existing open-loop record additively with `depends_on`, `basis_event_ids`, and `disposition`. Add a pure function to the existing ledger reducer: `changed_preference_keys -> affected_open_loop_ids`. Append `thread_reconciled` events; never edit or delete history. Fold the bounded reconciliation summary into the already-wired `open-loops/resurface` SessionStart check (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:53-60`), capped by the existing `MAX_ITEMS=3` (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/resurface.py:38-40`). No new hook. Keep ambiguous cancellation as `cancel-proposed`; use AskUserQuestion only at the next natural boundary, not mid-task.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** A deterministic integration fixture creates preference P1, opens threads T1/T2 that cite P1, appends superseding P2, and reads the materialized store from a fresh process. Pass only if both threads still exist, both reference the P1→P2 receipt, and neither is `closed` without an exact owner-cancellation event. A second signal is a fresh-session replay that surfaces the changed thread before execution. Neither verifier is the generating model checking itself.

**SOTA citation with URL:** LongMemEval explicitly treats knowledge updates and temporal reasoning as separate long-term-memory abilities and finds large degradation over sustained histories ([Wu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)). Microsoft’s validated human-AI guidelines separately require remembering interactions, adapting cautiously, conveying consequences, and notifying users about changes ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233)). The local proposal combines those ideas at the work-thread boundary.

**value x effort:** transformative x medium.

**novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy:** just-do-it for `still-valid`, `replan`, and `blocked-by-preference`; confirm-first for permanent cancellation when there is no exact owner cancellation.

**confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent a class of discussed-then-forgotten work because both stores and their identifiers already exist and the verification is deterministic. The reduction in owner stress is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a four-week count of owner “you forgot/why did you stop” corrections per 100 sessions before versus after rollout.

**anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is an additive join between two existing append-only stores, not a graph database or new orchestration runtime.

### Finding 2 — Use bitemporal, scoped preference events: “true when” and “learned when” are different

**improvement:** Replace the single `valid_as_of` convention with an append-only bitemporal preference event whose scope and provenance are explicit.

**what it is:** Every preference assertion becomes an immutable record with two clocks:

```json
{
  "kind": "preference",
  "preference_key": "delivery.interruptions",
  "value": "no_mid_task_questions",
  "reason": "preserve_focus",
  "scope": {"project": "*", "task_class": "long_horizon", "risk": "reversible"},
  "valid_from": "2026-07-19T00:00:00Z",
  "valid_to": null,
  "recorded_at": "2026-07-19T00:02:11Z",
  "source": {"speaker": "owner", "transcript": "...jsonl", "ts": "...", "span_hash": "..."},
  "supersedes": ["pref-event-id"],
  "status": "active"
}
```

`valid_*` says when the preference applies in the owner’s world; `recorded_at` says when the harness learned it. This supports late corrections (“that has been my preference since June”), prospective exceptions (“for this run only”), and audit-time reconstruction (“what did the agent know when it acted?”). Supersession closes only the overlapping scope interval; it never overwrites the prior value or reason.

**why-inferred:** The local convention has only `valid_as_of` plus a pointer (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/CLAUDE.md:11`), while corpus samples contain cancellation and object-specific exceptions (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11177-11180,11201-11210`). The owner wants a partner that grows with them; that requires distinguishing genuine evolution from delayed disclosure and from a local exception.

**how-to-build:** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add `preference` to the existing event-ledger kinds, add flags to `note.py` for key/value/reason/scope/valid-from/valid-to/supersedes, and extend the existing reducer to materialize current heads by `(preference_key, normalized_scope)`. Mirror a human-readable current view plus immutable history pointer into Basic Memory through the already-existing persistence path. Use additive optional fields and tolerate unknown/missing values, avoiding the corpus’s over-strict StructuredOutput failure mode (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:103-108`).

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** A command-level test inserts (a) a global preference, (b) a project exception, and (c) a retroactively valid correction recorded later. A separate process queries `as_known_at` and `valid_at` independently; expected results are literal fixtures, not model judgments. For a live event, the quoted owner span and its timestamp/hash provide the second independent provenance signal.

**SOTA citation with URL:** System-versioned temporal tables preserve prior values and record the time range in which each version was current; on update, the previous row is moved to history rather than erased ([Microsoft SQL Server temporal tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/tables/temporal-tables?view=sql-server-ver17)). HorizonBench’s central failure mode is failure to track updated user state over long histories, and it supplies provenance for every preference change ([Li et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17283)).

**value x effort:** transformative x medium.

**novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy:** just-do-it for exact owner statements and deterministic time/scope extraction; confirm-first if the proposed `valid_from` is retroactive or the scope changes a destructive/expensive action.

**confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** to resolve newer-versus-older statements without losing reasons because bitemporal history is a mature data pattern and the local buses are append-only. Whether the proposed flat schema stays under 200 changed lines is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with a no-write implementation spike against a copied ledger and line-count the patch before approval.

**anti-overfit gate:** passes. Bitemporal fields are a record convention and reducer rule, not a temporal database service.

### Finding 3 — Scope is part of the preference identity; newer does not mean globally stronger

**improvement:** Resolve preferences using a deterministic applicability lattice before recency.

**what it is:** Define precedence as:

1. safety/permission constraints;
2. exact current-turn instruction;
3. exact active preference in the most specific matching scope;
4. broader explicit preference;
5. externally verified inferred preference;
6. default behavior.

Within the same lane and overlapping scope, newer valid time wins. Across disjoint scopes, both remain current. A “no media sharing on CrossDevice” exception must not become “never share media anywhere”; a “no check-ins during this autonomous run” instruction must not suppress a safety confirmation for an irreversible action.

Suggested scope dimensions are deliberately small and enumerable: `project`, `task_class`, `artifact_or_resource`, `audience`, `risk`, and optional `expires_on_thread`. Do not infer dozens of latent persona traits.

**why-inferred:** The corpus supplies a concrete device-specific statement (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11207-11210`) and an interruption-sensitive long-work preference appears in the shared behavior contract (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:7-12`). The owner wants proactive personalization without prompt fatigue; selectivity is what prevents personalization itself from becoming noise or danger.

**how-to-build:** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement a pure `applies(preference, context)` function and a fixed specificity tuple. Derive context from existing event fields—cwd/project, tool/action risk, active goal/thread—not a model-generated persona. If two active records overlap and neither dominates, emit `CONFLICT` and degrade gracefully: use the safer/default path or AskUserQuestion at the decision boundary. Store the resolution receipt `{considered_ids, chosen_id, rule}` in the event ledger.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Table-driven tests cover global default, project override, task-only exception, expired thread scope, and safety conflict. A fresh-context model receives only the resolved preference packet and task, while an independent expected-action oracle checks the resulting tool arguments or output rubric. Any disagreement becomes a candidate, not an auto-correction.

**SOTA citation with URL:** PERSONAMEM finds current models struggle to track dynamic preference evolution even with interaction histories ([Jiang et al., COLM 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14225)). BenchPreS specifically evaluates whether persistent-memory models apply or suppress stored preferences according to recipient-task context, rather than maximizing preference application everywhere ([BenchPreS dataset and metric card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sangyon/BenchPreS)). Microsoft HAX says to scope services when uncertain and to support global controls ([HAX Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)).

**value x effort:** transformative x medium.

**novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy:** just-do-it when one record deterministically dominates; confirm-first on unresolved overlap before a material action.

**confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** to reduce preference over-application because context selectivity is directly benchmarked and the corpus contains scoped utterances. Exact transfer from communication benchmarks to coding/research work is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with the scope-ablation arm in Finding 7.

**anti-overfit gate:** passes. Six fixed dimensions and a pure comparator are intentionally smaller than a learned user model or knowledge graph.

### Finding 4 — Treat inferred preferences as revisable hypotheses, never as owner commands

**improvement:** Introduce an evidence ladder with promotion, contradiction, and demotion rules.

**what it is:** A preference record carries one of four evidence classes:

* `explicit-owner` — an authenticated owner utterance with exact span and timestamp;
* `owner-choice` — a choice between concrete alternatives or an explicit correction;
* `behavioral-hypothesis` — a repeated observed choice, still non-authoritative;
* `ambient` — agent, teammate, web, MCP, or imported text; never promoted to owner preference.

Exact owner instructions can become active immediately in their scope. A behavioral hypothesis requires two mechanically independent signals across distinct interactions—for example, repeated choice plus a later successful outcome—and remains `hypothesis` until then. A second model may classify or pair candidates, but cannot promote them; its output is only another candidate signal. One explicit owner contradiction immediately retires the hypothesis in the overlapping scope.

**why-inferred:** The local correction miner’s 41/41 false-positive smoke sample proves that speaker/provenance mistakes are not hypothetical (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10371-10374`). The known live defect in the supplied input shows a fabricated constraint propagated into roughly 15 prompts and pushed work toward a less reliable mechanism (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-155`). The harness must learn, but the cost of treating inference as authority is already concrete.

**how-to-build:** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add `evidence_class`, `evidence_ids`, `status`, and `promotion_rule` to preference events. Extend the existing persist-nudge/basic-memory write contract, not the hook roster. Candidate extraction may run offline or in a fresh model context; the deterministic reducer enforces promotion rules. Never feed agent-authored subagent prompts back as owner evidence. Store a source-role allowlist and exact transcript pointer.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** Seed fixtures containing one real owner statement, one agent-authored prompt shaped like an owner message, one teammate message, and two consistent owner choices. A deterministic provenance parser must promote only the explicit owner record; a fresh-context second model may label candidates, but expected fixtures grade it. In production, a later owner correction or observed successful choice is the only promotion/demotion signal.

**SOTA citation with URL:** PRefine represents latent preferences as evolving hypotheses and emphasizes preserving the reasons behind choices, rather than treating every past action as a fixed preference ([Yoon et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17886)). Microsoft’s guidelines pair learning from user behavior with cautious adaptation, efficient correction, granular feedback, and consequence disclosure ([Amershi et al., 2019 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)).

**value x effort:** high x low.

**novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy:** just-do-it for candidate capture and demotion on exact owner contradiction; confirm-first before promoting a behavioral hypothesis that would alter cost, privacy, destructive actions, or external communication.

**confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that provenance gating is necessary, based on the corpus’s measured 41/41 smoke-sample failure and the documented fabricated-constraint propagation. The best two-signal promotion thresholds are **UNVERIFIED**; settle them with precision/recall on owner-labeled candidates before enabling behavioral promotion.

**anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is a four-state evidence enum and deterministic gate, not a trained critic.

### Finding 5 — Separate preference, instruction, belief, constraint, and authorization lanes

**improvement:** Add a typed conflict adjudicator so recency cannot make unlike statements supersede one another.

**what it is:** “I prefer no check-ins,” “stop the upload,” “the editor cannot touch battle state,” “do not delete installers,” and “you may push” have different semantics. Store them as:

* `preference` — default behavior under underspecification;
* `instruction` — current task objective/action;
* `belief` — falsifiable claim about the world;
* `constraint` — boundary that shapes a plan;
* `authorization` — permission for a scoped external or risky action.

Only records in the same lane can supersede directly. A new preference cannot erase an open instruction; an owner belief can be corrected by execution evidence without pretending the owner “changed preferences”; authorization expires with its scope and is never generalized. Safety and platform permissions remain above all five lanes.

**why-inferred:** The corpus samples contain cancellation, preservation, and scoped configuration language (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11177-11210`), while the current `note.py` event kinds are generic decision/failure/goal/milestone/selection/note and accept free text (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:61-65`; `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/note.py:25-42`). Typed lanes preserve why two apparent contradictions are not the same kind of change.

**how-to-build:** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add the five record types as a backwards-compatible `semantic_lane` field rather than proliferating hook logic. Use deterministic lexical forms only for explicit high-precision cases (`prefer`, `for this task`, `you may`, `do not`); ambiguous classification remains a candidate reviewed by a fresh-context model or owner. The reducer refuses cross-lane supersession and appends a conflict receipt.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** A fixture matrix supplies paired utterances whose expected lanes and supersession relations are owner-authored gold labels. A separate process runs the reducer. For beliefs, an execution receipt—not the model—settles truth. For authorization, the actual permission subsystem or owner settles whether an action may proceed.

**SOTA citation with URL:** Human-AI teaming research finds that explicit shared goals and action-related communication help develop shared team cognition ([“Let’s Think Together,” CSCW 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3492832)). Microsoft HAX distinguishes correction, scoping under doubt, explanation, learning, cautious adaptation, and global control rather than collapsing them into one generic preference channel ([HAX Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)).

**value x effort:** high x medium.

**novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy:** just-do-it for explicit unambiguous typing; confirm-first when an ambiguous type changes authorization or irreversible action.

**confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent false supersession because the categories have different operational effects and external verifiers. The lexical classifier’s coverage is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it on an owner-labeled 100-message sample and deploy only if precision is at least 95%, otherwise store ambiguous records untyped.

**anti-overfit gate:** passes. The enum and reducer replace free-form ambiguity without introducing a guardrail framework.

### Finding 6 — Retrieve the current head first; reveal history and reason on demand

**improvement:** Make temporal recall successor-aware and contradiction-selective.

**what it is:** When a query matches a retired record, follow `superseded_by` to the active head, verify the scope, and inject only:

`current preference | scope | confidence/evidence class | changed because <short reason> | history pointer`

The retired value is available when the user asks “why did this change?” or when a conflict must be adjudicated, but it is not independently ranked as current guidance. Broken chains, cycles, missing successors, or overlapping heads produce `CONFLICT` and fail open to neutral/default behavior.

**why-inferred:** The current recall consumer labels and boosts a note carrying `superseded_by` without following the successor (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1656-1673,1693-1710`), while the indexer separately knows that such a note is not a head (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/recall_index.py:222-248`). That can preferentially surface retired material after the v2 fields are available. The owner wants both the latest truth and the reason for change, not amnesia about the old view.

**how-to-build:** **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** In `RecallModule`, build an in-memory permalink map, follow successor pointers with a small hop limit and visited set, scope-filter the head, and deduplicate by preference key. Replace the current shared contradiction boost with separate treatment: current conflict head boosted; retired link suppressed unless its successor is missing, in which case surface a bounded repair warning. Keep the existing max-results and 2,400-character budget (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:1656-1681`). No new retrieval service.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it:** A three-link chain fixture and a cycle fixture are indexed, then queried from a fresh process. Exact output must name only the current head for the chain and emit `CONFLICT` for the cycle. A second fresh-context agent receives the packet and must choose the current fixture action; an execution-result oracle grades that action.

**SOTA citation with URL:** LongMemEval reports that temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention are distinct required abilities; its time-aware query expansion and structured memory design improve long-history retrieval ([Wu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)). HorizonBench finds that when models err on evolved preferences, they often select the original value rather than updated state ([Li et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17283)).

**value x effort:** high x low.

**novelty:** user-asked.

**autonomy:** just-do-it for acyclic, single-head chains; confirm-first only when competing active heads affect a material decision.

**confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that current code can surface a retired note as preferred evidence because the consumer’s exact boost expression and absent traversal are visible. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that pointer traversal fixes that mechanism; task-level benefit remains **UNVERIFIED** until Finding 7’s replay.

**anti-overfit gate:** passes. This corrects the existing lexical recall path and preserves its fixed budget.

### Finding 7 — UNVERIFIED: Build a temporal reconciliation canary from owner-verified pairs

**improvement:** Turn real contradiction histories into a small cross-context regression suite before enabling automatic preference application.

**what it is:** Construct 30–50 gold cases covering:

* direct replacement;
* scoped exception;
* temporary override then reversion;
* retroactive correction;
* preference versus belief correction;
* preference versus authorization;
* unchanged preference with new wording;
* false candidate caused by agent/teammate text;
* changed preference with one or more dependent open threads;
* insufficient evidence, where abstention is correct.

## Minimal end-to-end architecture

The smallest coherent architecture is:

```text
owner utterance / owner choice / execution receipt
                    |
                    v
         provenance-aware candidate capture
                    |
       exact owner? | inferred?
          activate  | require independent signal
                    v
        append preference event to event ledger
                    |
                    v
       deterministic temporal + scope reducer
          |               |                |
          v               v                v
   Basic Memory      bounded recall    open-loop impact set
  semantic mirror     current head      replan/block/retain
          \               |                /
           \--------------v---------------/
               action + compact receipt
                    |
                    v
       execution result / owner correction / fresh replay
```

The model participates in extraction and planning, but no arrow from model output back to “verified preference” exists without an external signal. That is the architectural enforcement of the unit’s law.

### Resolution algorithm

For a task context `C` and preference key `K`:

1. Load immutable events for `K` whose `valid_from <= now < valid_to` or whose interval is open.
2. Reject ambient records and unpromoted hypotheses.
3. Filter by deterministic `scope_applies(event.scope, C)`.
4. Partition by semantic lane; never compare unlike lanes as supersession.
5. Apply safety/permission precedence.
6. Within a lane, select the most specific scope, then latest valid time, then latest recorded time only as an audit tie-breaker.
7. Follow successor links to the current head; detect missing targets and cycles.
8. If exactly one head remains, emit it with reason and history pointer.
9. If multiple incomparable heads remain, abstain or ask at the next material decision boundary.
10. If the head changed, append thread-impact and change-receipt events.

This algorithm is intentionally deterministic. A language model may suggest scope or lane, but uncertain suggestions stay candidates until a fresh-context classifier, execution evidence, or the owner settles them.

## Rollout order and quality gates

1. **Correct current-head traversal first.** It is a directly visible defect and a prerequisite for any richer temporal schema. Run chain/cycle fixtures from a copied state root.
2. **Add additive event fields and reducer in shadow.** Do not change action behavior; compare its selected head with the existing path.
3. **Build owner-verified canary.** No automatic behavioral preference promotion before this exists.
4. **Enable exact-owner preference activation only.** Keep all inferred preferences as hypotheses.
5. **Wire thread impact in shadow, then enforce “never close silently.”** This invariant can enforce before broader personalization because it is conservative.
6. **Enable scoped application after the canary beats existing recall.** Abort rollout if clarification count rises or thread retention falls.
7. **Enable behavioral promotion only if owner-labeled precision meets the predeclared threshold.** Otherwise keep hypothesis capture for explanation/search only.

Every runtime phase rides an existing hook event or direct CLI/MCP call. If implementation threatens to become a seventh raw hook, fold it into the existing event dispatcher or replace the relevant older check; do not add the hook.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Full replacement with a temporal knowledge graph

Rejected. It violates the anti-overfit gate by introducing a previously rejected vector-DB/KG memory class, duplicates Basic Memory, creates another service health surface, and is unnecessary for a preference stream whose queries are key/scope/time joins. The base Mem0 paper reports gains from a graph variant, but only roughly two percentage points over its base approach in its reported aggregate result, while the local need is deterministic provenance and supersession rather than open-ended relationship search ([Mem0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413)). A flat append-only event stream is the smaller hypothesis.

### “Newest statement always wins”

Rejected. It destroys valid history, mishandles local exceptions, confuses late disclosure with preference change, and lets newer low-authority text override explicit owner statements. HorizonBench’s findings motivate state tracking, not indiscriminate timestamp sorting ([Li et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17283)).

### Let the same agent reconcile contradictions in its prompt

Rejected by construction. It violates the unit law and reproduces the locally observed same-context failure mode. The only acceptable model role is candidate generation; a different context, deterministic result, second model, or owner must adjudicate.

### Ask the owner about every contradiction

Rejected. It makes the owner repair the harness’s memory and directly violates prompt-fatigue. Ask only on unresolved material overlap, authorization, destructive action, or global scope expansion; everything else is deterministic, passive, or coalesced.

### Age-based deletion or confidence decay for explicit preferences

Rejected. Old explicit preferences can remain valid indefinitely, and silence is not contradiction. Only inferred hypotheses decay; explicit records are superseded, scoped, undone, or explicitly forgotten.

### Use the 2,268 correction candidates as training truth

Rejected. The corpus itself reports 41/41 false positives in an unfiltered smoke sample (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10371-10374`). They are search leads only.

### Add a dedicated preference hook

Rejected. The ~6-hook ceiling is binding, and the existing dispatcher, ledger, recall, persist-nudge, and open-loop surfaces already cover capture, materialization, recall, and resurfacing. No proposal here needs a seventh hook.
