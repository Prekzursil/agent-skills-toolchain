# X006 — Clinical medicine → orchestration: prescribe a worker dose, then reassess

## Executive verdict

**The borrow transfers, but only as a control policy—not as a biological analogy or a ready-made formula.** Clinical medicine has a mature mechanism for acting under uncertainty without assuming that “more intervention is better”: choose an initial dose from the indication and risk context; explicitly balance benefit against harm; record contraindications; observe the response; and stop, continue, narrow, or escalate at a prespecified reassessment point. Drug dose–response guidance says precisely that the useful questions are the starting dose, how to adjust it for the individual, and the point beyond which more dose is unlikely to add benefit or would create unacceptable side effects ([FDA/ICH E4](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e4-dose-response-information-support-drug-registration)). Adaptive-trial guidance adds prospectively planned interim modification from accumulating observations, including stopping for success, futility, or harm ([FDA adaptive-design guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/adaptive-design-clinical-trials-drugs-and-biologics-guidance-industry); [FDA Bayesian-device guidance, §4.10](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-use-bayesian-statistics-medical-device-clinical-trials)). Antibiotic stewardship makes the operational pattern especially concrete: begin empirically when delay is dangerous, then perform a “timeout” once the clinical picture and diagnostic evidence are clearer, asking whether therapy can stop or narrow ([CDC hospital stewardship, pp. 22–23](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/media/pdfs/hospital-core-elements-508.pdf)).

The corresponding harness concept is a **Worker-Dose Timeout**: prescribe an initial concurrency level from task features, launch only that wave, measure marginal information and iatrogenic cost, and allow one of four outcomes—stop-success, stop-futility, narrow/merge, or escalate. This is **very likely (80-90%; evidence: the clinical mechanisms above are structurally about bounded intervention under uncertain benefit, while controlled agent-system evidence finds architecture- and task-dependent positive and negative returns)** to be a better default than a fixed global worker count. Current agent evidence is not merely rhetorical: a 2025/2026 controlled study reports that multi-agent coordination improves parallelizable tasks but degrades sequential ones and identifies efficiency, overhead, error amplification, and redundancy as predictive quantities ([Google Research summary](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/); [underlying paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

The transfer stops there. A worker is not a patient, output quality is not a biological endpoint, adding a worker is not pharmacokinetically equivalent to increasing a dose, and worker outputs are often correlated. Number-needed-to-treat (NNT) arithmetic cannot be copied per task. The mechanism should therefore be borrowed as a disciplined policy and measurement vocabulary, then calibrated by randomized harness experiments. **It is almost certain (90-99%; evidence: the endpoint, independence, and exposure assumptions differ directly as detailed in “Disanalogies”) that literal clinical thresholds would be invalid.**

## The specific source-field mechanism

I call the source mechanism **adaptive minimum-effective intervention with benefit–harm stopping**. It is a synthesis of four linked clinical practices, not a claim that one medical checklist already contains agent scheduling:

1. **Differential diagnosis / action thresholds:** maintain competing explanations, estimate whether additional evidence could change the action, and test only in the intermediate region. Pauker and Kassirer’s threshold model defines a testing threshold and a test–treatment threshold from test reliability, test risk, and treatment benefits and risks: withhold below the lower threshold, test between thresholds, and treat without more testing above the upper threshold ([Pauker & Kassirer, *NEJM*, 1980](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJM198005153022003)). An NCBI methods review states the key value-of-information rule plainly: perform a test only if its result could move the probability far enough to cross the treatment threshold ([NCBI, “The Use of Diagnostic Tests”](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)).

2. **Dose–response and the therapeutic window:** increasing exposure can improve efficacy, add no further benefit, or create unacceptable adverse effects. ICH E4 frames dose selection around the effectiveness and undesirable-effect curves, the appropriate starting dose, adjustment to the individual, and a dose beyond which increases are unlikely to help or create unacceptable side effects ([FDA/ICH E4](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e4-dose-response-information-support-drug-registration)). This is not “find the largest tolerable number”; it is “find the exposure whose benefit–risk balance is appropriate.”

3. **Iatrogenics, contraindications, and medication review:** an intervention can itself cause the problem, and combinations introduce interaction risk. WHO identifies polypharmacy, high-risk situations, and transitions of care as medication-safety priorities, and recommends systematic, patient-centered medication review to retain benefit without harm ([WHO polypharmacy technical report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-UHC-SDS-2019.11)). FDA labeling defines contraindications as situations where risk clearly outweighs possible therapeutic benefit and the medicine must not be used; serious drug-interaction contraindications are cross-referenced to interaction details ([FDA prescription-label FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fdas-labeling-resources-human-prescription-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-about-labeling-prescription-medicines); [FDA contraindications guidance](https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Warnings-and-Precautions--Contraindications--and-Boxed-Warning-Sections-of-Labeling-for-Human-Prescription-Drug-and-Biological-Products-%E2%80%94-Content-and-Format.pdf)).

4. **Absolute benefit/harm and scheduled reassessment:** NNT expresses an absolute effect: the expected number receiving experimental rather than comparator treatment for one additional person to incur or avoid an event during a stated time frame ([Cochrane Handbook, ch. 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)). Laupacis, Sackett, and Roberts introduced the clinically useful treatment-consequence measure in the setting of scarce time and resources ([*NEJM*, 1988](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJM198806303182605); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3374545/)). Cochrane also warns that “number needed to harm” can mislead and prefers NNT for an additional beneficial outcome (NNTB) or additional harmful outcome (NNTH), always tied to comparator and time horizon ([Cochrane Handbook, ch. 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)). Adaptive trials preplan interim looks to stop enrollment for success, futility, or harm or to drop arms/doses ([FDA Bayesian-device guidance, §4.10](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-use-bayesian-statistics-medical-device-clinical-trials)). In routine care, the CDC’s antibiotic timeout reassesses indication, diagnostics, spectrum, route, and duration after more evidence arrives; the CDC notes that a trial improved appropriateness of antibiotic selection but did not reduce overall use, an important reminder that a timeout prompt is not automatically effective ([CDC hospital stewardship, pp. 22–23](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/media/pdfs/hospital-core-elements-508.pdf)).

These practices solve a general clinical control problem: **act soon enough to capture benefit, expose the patient to no more intervention than current evidence warrants, and force new evidence to update the plan.** They do not eliminate judgment. They make its inputs, stopping points, and harms explicit.

## Structural mapping to orchestration

| Clinical object | Harness translation | Exact operational meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Patient / episode | One owner task or one independently scoreable task phase | The unit whose outcome, elapsed time, and resource consumption can be evaluated |
| Working diagnosis / differential | Task phenotype plus uncertainty hypotheses | Examples: parallel evidence collection, independent implementation modules, sequential stateful edit, adversarial verification, or ambiguous research question |
| Indication | Reason another worker could change the result | A named, non-overlapping question, artifact, test, or falsification target—not “help with the task” |
| Drug | Worker role/model/tool-access profile | Researcher, implementer, verifier, domain specialist, or independent replicate |
| Dose | Concurrent worker count and total worker-wave exposure | `k` live workers in this wave; cumulative worker-seconds/tokens across all waves is separately recorded |
| Therapeutic response | Incremental decision-relevant contribution | New supported claim, caught defect, independently passing implementation shard, reduced critical-path time, or improved blinded rubric score |
| Adverse effect / iatrogenesis | Error and coordination burden caused by adding workers | Contradictory edits, duplicate evidence, propagated false premise, merge repair, owner interruption, token/cost increase, or delayed critical path |
| Contraindication | Task/context in which a topology or worker combination is prohibited | Examples proposed below: concurrent writers to the same mutable files; independent workers whose work necessarily depends on a not-yet-produced state; spawning when no distinct indication can be named |
| Interaction | Pair/group effect not predictable from each worker alone | Shared stale premise, edit collision, duplicated tool calls, inconsistent schemas, or cross-worker context contamination |
| Therapeutic window | Range of concurrency with positive net marginal utility | Counts for which expected added value exceeds coordination/harm cost for this task phenotype |
| NNTB / NNTH | Population-level orchestration effect, never a within-run guarantee | Across comparable tasks: how many tasks must receive `k+1` rather than `k` workers for one additional task to cross a prespecified success or harm threshold |
| Timeout | A scheduled evidence review between waves or at an early worker milestone | Decide stop-success, stop-futility, narrow/merge, or escalate |
| De-escalation | Cancel queued work, stop redundant workers, or collapse to one integrator | Preserve the useful narrow contribution while ending unnecessary exposure |

The mapping is **likely (55-80%; evidence: every mapped field has an observable harness analogue, but several endpoints require owner-specific calibration)** to be useful as an implementation schema. The lower band reflects the main unresolved issue: clinical outcomes are defined externally, whereas agent quality is often judged by the same orchestrator that generated the work.

## Proposed harness change — UNVERIFIED: Worker-Dose Timeout

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** This proposal assumes the existing bus exposes worker start, completion/cancellation, token or cost, and a place to attach a small structured record. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by a synthetic run that starts two workers and asserts that the hook receives two distinct start IDs, two terminal states, timestamps, and usage counters.** If those events are unavailable, this line-budget/value tag must be revisited; this unit does not recommend a new daemon.

### 1. Write a one-record “prescription” before spawning

The orchestrator emits a compact record:

```json
{
  "task_id": "…",
  "phenotype": "parallel-research | modular-build | sequential-stateful | adversarial-check | unknown",
  "indications": [
    {"worker": "w1", "question": "…", "unique_deliverable": "…"}
  ],
  "initial_dose": 1,
  "max_dose": 4,
  "timeout": {"event": "first_wave_terminal", "wall_seconds": 180},
  "success_boundary": "…",
  "futility_boundary": "…",
  "harm_boundary": "…",
  "contraindications_checked": ["same-write-set", "sequential-dependency", "no-distinct-indication"]
}
```

The fields are deliberately few. A prescription is rejected if there is no separate indication/deliverable for each worker. This copies the clinical insistence on indication and dose documentation, not medical terminology for decoration. The CDC notes that documenting antibiotic indication, dose, and duration supports later modification or discontinuation ([CDC hospital stewardship, p. 18 and pp. 22–23](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/media/pdfs/hospital-core-elements-508.pdf)).

**Forward claim:** this gate is **very likely (80-90%; evidence: duplicate or non-decomposable workers cannot pass a distinct-indication check, and agent-system evidence reports redundancy and overhead as predictive factors)** to prevent obviously unjustified fan-out before it consumes resources. It will not detect two differently worded assignments that are semantically duplicates; that requires outcome measurement, not a larger prompt.

### 2. Use a small contraindication registry, with hard and soft entries

The registry should be a versioned JSON table evaluated before launch:

| Pattern | Severity | Default action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two workers have overlapping mutable write sets | Hard | Serialize or give one writer and one read-only reviewer | Prevents edit collision and ambiguous ownership |
| A worker’s input depends on another worker’s not-yet-produced result | Hard for parallel launch | Convert dependency to next wave | The work is sequential, not concurrent |
| No unique deliverable or falsifiable question per worker | Hard | Do not spawn | There is no indication for exposure |
| Tool-heavy task with a strong single-worker baseline | Soft | Start at one; escalate only after timeout | Controlled agent evidence reports a tool-coordination trade-off and capability saturation ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)) |
| Independent replicate uses the same model, prompt, sources, and context | Soft | Diversify one of those dimensions or count it as a replicate, not independent confirmation | Homogeneous outputs may be correlated; independence must not be assumed |
| High-uncertainty, genuinely decomposable evidence search with disjoint source classes | Positive indication | Permit an initial multi-worker wave | Parallel search can reduce critical-path latency and broaden coverage |

The first three can be static checks. The last three are reviewable policy. In medicine, a contraindication is stronger than a warning: FDA describes it as a context where risk clearly outweighs possible benefit and the product must not be used ([FDA prescription-label FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fdas-labeling-resources-human-prescription-drugs/frequently-asked-questions-about-labeling-prescription-medicines)). The harness should preserve that distinction: “hard” blocks launch; “soft” lowers the starting dose or requires an explicit override reason.

The listed registry entries are **UNVERIFIED recommendations; settle them with a factorial experiment crossing task phenotype, overlap status, dependency depth, and worker count, while measuring blinded quality, critical-path time, total tokens, collisions, and merge-repair time.** The same-write-set and sequential-dependency entries are **very likely (80-90%; evidence: their causal mechanisms follow directly from concurrent mutation and unmet data dependencies)** to be net beneficial. The numerical boundaries and any exceptions are only **roughly even (45-55%; evidence: no owner-specific randomized results are available in this ideation unit)** until that experiment is run.

### 3. Run in waves; perform a timeout before adding dose

Default policy:

1. Start with the phenotype-specific initial dose, usually one or two—not a universal number.
2. At the earliest of first-wave completion or the wall-time boundary, collect partial/terminal worker cards containing: claims or artifacts produced; evidence IDs; changed-file set; blockers; dependencies discovered; and a one-sentence “what another worker could still change.”
3. Compute the observed marginal card for each added worker:
   - unique accepted contributions;
   - duplicate contribution fraction;
   - contradictions requiring adjudication;
   - critical-path seconds saved or added;
   - total tokens/cost;
   - merge/repair seconds;
   - terminal quality evidence available so far.
4. Choose exactly one action:
   - **stop-success:** adequacy boundary met;
   - **stop-futility:** remaining workers are unlikely to change the decision/artifact enough to justify cost;
   - **narrow/merge:** retain one integrator or a single uncovered role, cancel redundant work;
   - **escalate:** launch the next indicated worker(s), up to the task-specific cap.

This mirrors the antibiotic “start smart, then focus” shape particularly well: initial action is allowed under urgent uncertainty, but its continuation is not presumed. The UK antimicrobial-stewardship toolkit requires review of the diagnosis and continuing need by 48–72 hours and a documented stop/switch/narrow/continue decision ([UKHSA, “Start smart then focus”](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/antimicrobial-stewardship-start-smart-then-focus/start-smart-then-focus-antimicrobial-stewardship-toolkit-for-inpatient-care-settings)). The elapsed times do **not** transfer; the harness timeout should be event-based and measured in seconds/minutes appropriate to agent work.

An adaptive wave policy is **likely (55-80%; evidence: clinical adaptive designs show the validity of preplanned evidence-driven modification, and agent results show task-dependent scaling)** to beat fixed fan-out on combined quality/cost/latency. It is not more confidently rated because waiting for a first wave can destroy the latency benefit on tasks that are obviously parallel. For those tasks, the learned initial dose may be greater than one.

### 4. Estimate worker NNTB/NNTH only across comparable tasks

For task phenotype `p`, compare policy `k+1` against `k` workers over a fixed evaluation window:

```text
absolute_benefit_delta(p,k) = P(success | p,k+1) - P(success | p,k)
worker_NNTB(p,k)            = 1 / absolute_benefit_delta(p,k)

absolute_harm_delta(p,k)    = P(harm | p,k+1) - P(harm | p,k)
worker_NNTH(p,k)            = 1 / absolute_harm_delta(p,k)
```

“Success” must be a prespecified, blinded or mechanically scored threshold; examples are tests passing without regression, all required source classes represented with claim-level support, or owner acceptance without correction. “Harm” must likewise be prespecified; examples are edit collision, accepted false claim, missed deadline, cost ceiling exceeded, or owner correction caused by coordination.

This is a reporting lens, not the runtime decision by itself. NNT depends on baseline risk, comparator, endpoint, and time frame; Cochrane’s definition expressly includes the comparator and time frame ([Cochrane Handbook, ch. 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)). Pooling all task types would erase precisely the heterogeneity the policy needs. Report confidence intervals and raw natural frequencies (`18/100` versus `14/100`) beside NNTB/NNTH; Cochrane recommends natural-frequency presentations because they are less prone to misinterpretation ([Cochrane Handbook, ch. 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

Using worker NNTB/NNTH to choose a global concurrency cap would be **very unlikely (5-20%; evidence: task baseline, endpoint, model, topology, and latency budget alter both absolute effects)** to remain valid across task classes. Using phenotype-stratified NNTB/NNTH as a compact historical effect report is **likely (55-80%; evidence: it preserves absolute benefit and harm against a named comparator, but task scoring may still be noisy)** to improve owner comprehension of the trade-off.

### 5. Use a prospective stopping rule, not post-hoc storytelling

For a candidate extra wave at dose `k+1`, define net incremental utility:

```text
ΔU = wq·Δquality + wt·Δcritical_path_time
     - wc·Δcost - wh·Δharm - wm·Δmerge_repair
```

The weights belong to the task contract; they are not universal. The runtime can use simple boundaries before any Bayesian sophistication:

- do not escalate unless a named uncovered indication remains;
- stop for futility when all uncovered indications are below a minimum decision-value threshold;
- stop for harm on a hard contraindication, write collision, or cost/deadline breach;
- stop for success when the acceptance evidence is complete;
- otherwise launch the smallest next wave capable of covering the highest-value indications.

Later, only if enough randomized observations exist, estimate `P(ΔU > 0 | phenotype, k, topology)` and escalate when it crosses a predeclared threshold. Repeatedly peeking and inventing a threshold after seeing the outputs would reproduce the bias that group-sequential designs address through preplanned stopping rules; CONSORT’s explanation notes that trials stop early for benefit, harm, or futility and discusses formal group-sequential rules ([CONSORT explanation, BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/340/bmj.c869)).

The usefulness of the scalar `ΔU` is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by preregistering weights for a held-out task set, comparing its decisions to blinded owner pairwise preferences, and reporting calibration and regret.** A simple lexicographic rule (hard safety/deadline, then acceptance, then time/cost) may outperform a scalar. It is **roughly even (45-55%; evidence: scalarization is auditable but owner preferences may be discontinuous)** that the weighted formula is the best implementation. The stopping categories remain useful even if the scalar is rejected.

## What this would catch that fixed or intuition-only orchestration misses

The following are predicted catches, not claims about the local harness; this unit intentionally did not inspect it.

1. **Redundant fan-out disguised as confirmation.** Two workers return the same evidence because they share model, prompt, context, and source pool. A distinct-indication check catches obvious duplication before launch; unique-contribution and duplicate-fraction measurements catch it afterward. This is **very likely (80-90%; evidence: redundancy is explicitly measured in controlled agent scaling research and homogeneous workers share causal inputs)** to reveal wasted “independent” work.

2. **The orchestration prescribing cascade.** One worker produces an ambiguous or erroneous result; the orchestrator reacts by adding reviewers; reviewers inherit the same premise; an integrator is then added to reconcile contradictions created by the intervention. Medicine calls the analogous pattern a prescribing cascade when an adverse effect is mistaken for a new condition and treated with another drug. The harness timeout exposes whether the new worker is treating the original task or a coordination harm caused by prior workers. This catch is **likely (55-80%; evidence: explicit lineage and contradiction fields expose the causal chain, but semantic attribution can be ambiguous)**.

3. **Sequential work mislabeled as parallel.** If B needs A’s output, launching both creates speculation, stale assumptions, or rework. The dependency contraindication converts B to a later wave. This is **very likely (80-90%; evidence: a data dependency is mechanically incompatible with useful simultaneous execution unless B can proceed on a stable interface)** to reduce rework on stateful tasks.

4. **More workers improving gross quality while worsening net value.** A final answer can be marginally better yet arrive later, cost much more, or require substantial merge repair. Recording incremental quality, critical path, cost, harm, and repair separately reveals the trade. This is **very likely (80-90%; evidence: the metrics are direct components of net operational value, and agent research reports coordination overhead and task-dependent negative returns)** to identify cases hidden by quality-only evaluation.

5. **Iatrogenic error amplification.** Independent agents can propagate unchecked errors; the 2025 scaling study reports topology-dependent error amplification, with centralized coordination containing more error than independent architectures in its evaluated configurations ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). A harm boundary keyed to shared unsupported premises and contradiction lineage can stop escalation and switch to a verifier with genuinely different evidence. Generalizing the paper’s exact magnitude to this owner is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting known false premises into randomized single, independent, and centralized worker topologies and measuring final acceptance of the falsehood.** Detection is **likely (55-80%; evidence: provenance makes propagation observable, while semantic premise matching remains imperfect)**.

6. **Premature maximal dose.** Fixed fan-out spends the full budget before learning whether one worker already meets the acceptance boundary. A first-wave timeout can cancel queued exposure. Savings are **likely (55-80%; evidence: early success makes later work causally unnecessary, but cancellation support and worker startup latency determine realized savings)**.

7. **Under-treatment of truly decomposable work.** The mechanism is not a “fewer agents” rule. A high-value differential with disjoint deliverables can justify a larger initial dose, and repeated positive marginal response can justify escalation. Avoiding systematic under-parallelization is **likely (55-80%; evidence: dose–response permits escalation and agent evidence finds strong gains on parallelizable tasks)** if phenotype and response estimates are calibrated.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Where the structure genuinely matches

1. **Both are sequential decisions under uncertain response.** The decision-maker chooses an intervention level, observes noisy evidence, and may continue, stop, or change course. FDA adaptive-design guidance explicitly defines prospective modifications based on accumulating data ([FDA adaptive-design guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/adaptive-design-clinical-trials-drugs-and-biologics-guidance-industry)). A harness can observe worker outputs and costs between waves.

2. **Both have non-monotone net benefit.** ICH E4 explicitly recognizes doses beyond which added benefit is unlikely or side effects unacceptable ([FDA/ICH E4](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e4-dose-response-information-support-drug-registration)). Controlled agent experiments report that coordination can help parallelizable tasks and hurt sequential ones ([Google Research summary](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). Thus “more” is not a safe monotonic prior in either field.

3. **Indication and contraindication can be represented before exposure.** Task dependencies and write-set overlap are observable before workers start in many workflows, just as clinical context can make a treatment indicated or contraindicated. The analogy is policy-level, not biological.

4. **Absolute effects matter more than relative slogans.** “Multi-agent improves by X%” does not say how many additional owner tasks cross an acceptance boundary or how many suffer harm. NNTB/NNTH forces a comparator, endpoint, and time horizon ([Cochrane Handbook, ch. 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

5. **A timeout creates a correction point.** CDC stewardship does not assume that an initially reasonable empiric choice remains reasonable after diagnostic evidence arrives ([CDC hospital stewardship](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/media/pdfs/hospital-core-elements-508.pdf)). Agent assignments likewise become obsolete as early outputs resolve uncertainty or expose dependencies.

These similarities make the policy transfer **very likely (80-90%; evidence: the matched variables are intervention level, uncertain response, measurable harm, and sequential updating—not superficial vocabulary)** to be legitimate.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **No pharmacokinetics or biological dose equivalence.** Two workers are not “twice the exposure.” Model capability, role, tool access, context, and topology change the intervention qualitatively. Therefore worker count alone is an inadequate dose; the prescription must record worker composition and topology. Importing a smooth dose–response curve is **almost certainly not (1-5%; evidence: heterogeneous workers violate exchangeability and equal-dose assumptions)** valid.

2. **Worker outcomes are correlated and interactive.** NNT is estimated from populations under a comparator; within one run, agents can copy the same source, inherit a shared prompt error, or alter each other’s state. `1 / success_probability` for one worker is not “workers needed to solve” and should never be reported as such. Literal within-run NNT is **almost certainly not (1-5%; evidence: NNT’s population-level absolute-risk definition and the lack of independent Bernoulli trials)** valid.

3. **The endpoint is endogenous.** Mortality or a lab result exists independently of the clinician’s prose. Agent “quality” may be judged by an orchestrator sharing the workers’ biases, and some tasks lack a mechanical oracle. Benefit estimates can therefore reward stylistic agreement. This threatens calibration **very likely (80-90%; evidence: evaluator and producer can share model/context, unlike externally observed clinical outcomes)** unless blinded owner judgments, executable tests, or source-grounded rubrics are used.

4. **Latency changes the value of staging.** Clinical reassessment can occur after enough biological/diagnostic time passes. Agent workers may finish in minutes, but waiting for wave one before launching an obvious independent shard forfeits parallel speedup. A universal start-at-one rule is **very unlikely (5-20%; evidence: independent critical-path tasks gain directly from simultaneous execution)** to be optimal.

5. **Cancellation differs from withdrawing a drug.** A cancelled worker may already have incurred most token/tool cost, may have written state, or may leave a partial artifact. De-escalation only saves future exposure; it cannot reverse prior actions. This makes early, read-only, or isolated worker phases more valuable.

6. **Clinical thresholds encode ethics and population evidence.** Trial stopping boundaries control statistical error and protect human participants. Harness thresholds optimize owner utility and system risk. Borrowing numeric clinical cutoffs, phase-I escalation algorithms, or “48–72 hours” would be category error. Such direct parameter transfer is **almost certainly not (1-5%; evidence: different loss functions, observation times, and ethical/statistical constraints)** justified.

7. **Contraindications are not eternal facts.** A same-write-set prohibition may become unnecessary if the underlying platform supplies isolated branches and conflict-safe merges; a sequential dependency may be speculatively executable if a stable contract exists. The registry must record rationale, evidence version, exception conditions, and expiry/review date. A permanent unversioned registry is **unlikely (20-45%; evidence: harness capabilities and model behavior change)** to remain correct.

8. **Clinical evidence itself warns against ritualized mechanisms.** The CDC reports that antibiotic timeouts improved appropriateness but did not reduce overall antibiotic use and says they supplement rather than replace prospective audit and feedback ([CDC hospital stewardship, pp. 22–23](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/media/pdfs/hospital-core-elements-508.pdf)). Merely prompting “reassess workers” is unlikely to alter behavior without measurable boundaries and feedback. A prompt-only timeout is **unlikely (20-45%; evidence: the cited clinical trial changed appropriateness but not total use, and no harness enforcement would exist)** to control concurrency reliably.

### Net result of the adversarial test

The analogy passes if implemented as **(a) explicit indication, (b) task-conditioned initial exposure, (c) contraindication checks, (d) measured benefit and harm, and (e) a prespecified reassessment action.** It fails if implemented as medical branding around a fixed heuristic such as “always spawn two, then double,” a literal NNT calculation inside one run, or a universal concurrency cap.

## Minimal validation program — UNVERIFIED recommendation

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the randomized program below. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** for instrumentation and policy selection, plus existing evaluation scripts for analysis. If randomized assignment or outcome capture requires a separate service, stop: that would become BUILD-LARGE and is outside this proposal’s justification.

### Experiment A — detector control

Before evaluating policy, prove the event detector:

- known-present control: launch two labeled no-op/read-only workers and verify two start records and two terminal records by worker ID;
- independent signal: compare event records with the orchestrator’s returned worker handles and terminal statuses;
- negative control: run a single-worker task and confirm no phantom second worker;
- usage control: compare recorded total usage with the provider/session usage field, allowing documented rounding.

No claim that the policy is “running” or “complete” is permitted without these two mechanically independent signals.

### Experiment B — randomized worker-dose response

Stratify a held-out task set by predeclared phenotype and randomly assign `k ∈ {1,2,4}` plus topology where applicable. Keep model, tools, total deadline, and acceptance rubric fixed; record both concurrent count and total compute. Primary endpoints:

- task success at a prespecified acceptance threshold;
- wall-clock critical path;
- total token/tool cost;
- owner correction or executable-test regression;
- merge/repair time;
- duplicate contribution fraction.

Secondary endpoints:

- unsupported premise propagation;
- number of contradictions;
- cancelled-work cost already incurred;
- proportion of workers with zero accepted unique contribution.

Report raw frequencies, absolute differences, confidence intervals, NNTB/NNTH where defined, and the full dose–response by phenotype. Do not pool task classes unless interaction tests and effect plots support pooling.

### Experiment C — timeout policy against fixed policies

Compare:

- fixed one worker;
- fixed owner-current/default concurrency;

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Always use N workers”

Rejected. It ignores task phenotype, marginal response, and staging cost. Controlled agent evidence directly reports positive returns on parallelizable work and degradation on sequential work ([Google Research summary](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). A global optimum is **very unlikely (5-20%; evidence: heterogeneous task dependence)** to exist beyond a coarse safety cap.

### “Spawn until outputs agree”

Rejected. Agreement among correlated agents is not independent confirmation and can amplify a shared false premise. The policy also lacks a benefit/cost boundary. It is **unlikely (20-45%; evidence: common prompt/model/context create correlated errors)** to provide calibrated confidence.

### “Use NNT = 1 / per-worker success probability”

Rejected as a category error. NNT is the inverse of an absolute difference between intervention and comparator over a specified population and time frame, not the inverse of one arm’s success rate ([Cochrane Handbook, ch. 15](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)). This formula is **almost certainly not (1-5%; evidence: it omits the comparator and assumes an inappropriate endpoint model)** valid.

### “Launch maximum parallelism, then select the best output”

Rejected as the default, retained as an experimental arm. It can be correct when latency dominates and tasks are genuinely independent, but it spends maximum exposure before observing response and makes selection quality the bottleneck. As a universal policy it is **unlikely (20-45%; evidence: tool-heavy/sequential tasks incur measured coordination penalties while parallel tasks may benefit)** to dominate.

### “Build a learned orchestration controller now”

Rejected. Without randomized dose-response data, a learned controller would fit historical task assignment bias and evaluator bias. It would also approach a trained critic/second runtime, both excluded by the anti-overfit gate. A learned controller now is **very unlikely (5-20%; evidence: absent counterfactual labels and forbidden architectural weight)** to justify its complexity.

### “Copy a clinical phase-I algorithm”

Rejected. Phase-I rules address human toxicity under biological dose assumptions and trial-statistical constraints; agent workers are heterogeneous, interactive, cheap to observe relative to patients, and evaluated on different loss functions. Literal transfer is **almost certainly not (1-5%; evidence: broken exchangeability, endpoint, and ethics assumptions)** valid.
