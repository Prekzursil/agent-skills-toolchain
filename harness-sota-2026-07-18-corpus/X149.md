# X149 — Cross-domain borrow: cartographic generalisation for agent trust calibration

## Executive result

The useful cartographic borrow is **scale- and purpose-dependent constrained generalisation**, coupled to an explicit statement of the scale at which a representation is valid. It is not merely “show less detail.” Cartography starts from a more detailed representation, chooses a target scale and purpose, applies operators such as selection, simplification, aggregation, displacement, and exaggeration, and evaluates the result against competing constraints: it must remain legible while preserving important characteristics. The U.S. Geological Survey describes generalisation as reducing information and detail to make a topographic map legible at a particular scale, while selecting content and symbolisation appropriate to that scale ([USGS, “Generalization”](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)). Harrie describes constraint-based generalisation as a compromise between readability and preservation constraints, with weights expressing their relative importance ([Harrie 2003](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/weight-setting-and-quality-assessment-in-simultaneous-graphic-gen/)). OGC’s Maps standard makes scale operational rather than rhetorical: an implementation computes a scale denominator and may use it to apply scale-dependent symbology rules ([OGC API — Maps — Part 1](https://docs.ogc.org/is/20-058/20-058.html)).

The transfer verdict is **likely (55-80%) useful as a small trust-calibration mechanism**, based on the structural match between (a) a map representation being valid only for a stated scale and purpose and (b) an agent reliability estimate being valid only for a stated task class, stakes class, and evidential resolution. It is not a basis for a universal agent score. The verdict remains **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the prospective, pre-registered “multiscale trust card” trial specified below**, comparing task-conditioned trust cards with a scalar agent score and no trust display.

The concrete harness change is a **multiscale trust card**. Each agent has a task-conditioned reliability surface backed by adjudicated outcomes. At a coarse “route-planning” view it shows only adequately supported task families and a conspicuous unknown region. At a finer “street-level” view it shows task subtype, consequence class, verification method, recency, sample count, error modes, and the exact evidence retained or omitted. A request is matched to the closest supported cell; the harness then presents the cell’s evidence at detail appropriate to the decision and refuses to silently interpolate across unsupported territory. This proposal is **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** and is **likely (55-80%) to prevent false transfer from an agent’s strong aggregate record to a weak or unmeasured task subtype**, based on cartography’s scale-specific representation principle and ISO’s application-specific view of geographic-data quality ([ISO 19157-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78900.html)). It is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a replay in which evaluators choose whether to accept, verify, or reroute outputs using the new card versus a global success rate**.

This is deliberately not a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It is a compact derived view over ordinary outcome records plus a pre-task lookup and post-task update on the existing bus. It passes the anti-overfit gate by keeping execution unchanged and making trust evidence more honest about resolution and omission.

## 1. What cartography has actually solved

### 1.1 A representation has a scale and purpose; “more detail” is not universally better

USGS states that smaller-scale maps cover larger areas and therefore must reduce information and detail to remain legible. It also says generalisation selects content appropriate to a scale and symbolises that content effectively ([USGS generalisation programme](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)). This establishes three points in cartography’s own field:

1. The source world or source dataset contains more potential detail than the display can use.
2. The appropriate representation depends on the scale and mapping purpose.
3. Omission is an intended transformation, not automatically an error.

This is more disciplined than a single “accuracy” badge. ISO 19157 defines principles for evaluating and reporting geographic-data quality so that a user can judge whether data are sufficient for a *particular application*; it explicitly does not prescribe one universal minimum acceptable quality level ([ISO 19157:2013 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)). The current edition is ISO 19157-1:2023 ([ISO 19157-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78900.html)). Thus, cartographic quality is use-relative while still being evidence-bearing: the producer reports measured qualities, and the user or product specification supplies fitness thresholds.

The OGC Maps standard supplies the operational counterpart. Its `scale-denominator` relates real-world units to pixel units, and when the caller omits it, the implementation computes it for purposes including scale-dependent symbology ([OGC API — Maps — Part 1, Core](https://docs.ogc.org/is/20-058/20-058.html)). A layer or rule therefore need not pretend to be equally meaningful at every zoom level.

### 1.2 Generalisation is a family of explicit operators, not free-form summarisation

Cartographic generalisation uses identifiable transformations. A recent peer-reviewed account lists selection (omitting features), aggregation (combining features), simplification (reducing complexity), and displacement (moving features to resolve conflicts) as core techniques for scale-aware maps ([Takahashi et al. 2024, DOI 10.3154/jvs.44.171_13](https://doi.org/10.3154/jvs.44.171_13)). USGS similarly frames automated generalisation as deriving more generalised scales from highly detailed data while tailoring the result to mapping and modelling needs ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/cegis/science/generalization)).

These operators matter because each loses or changes information in a different way:

- **Selection** removes an object from the displayed representation.
- **Simplification** retains an object but reduces its internal detail.
- **Aggregation** replaces several objects with a collective representation.
- **Displacement/exaggeration** knowingly changes apparent geometry so important objects remain distinguishable.

The source representation is not thereby rewritten as if the display transformation were ground truth. This distinction between retained evidence and derived portrayal is the piece worth borrowing.

### 1.3 Quality is evaluated as a constrained compromise

Harrie’s formulation treats generalisation as simultaneous satisfaction of competing constraints. Readability pushes toward change; characteristic-preservation constraints push toward retaining important properties; the constraints cannot all be completely satisfied, so weights determine the compromise ([Harrie 2003](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/weight-setting-and-quality-assessment-in-simultaneous-graphic-gen/)). Bard’s quality-assessment method compares initial and final states at multiple analysis levels, evaluates quality from those comparisons, and only then aggregates the measures ([Bard 2004, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9671.2004.00168.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9671.2004.00168.x)).

That order is important: characterise locally, compare before/after, then aggregate. An aggregate does not substitute for the lower-resolution evidence from which it was produced.

USGS research also illustrates that the preservation/readability balance needs target-scale tests. Its discernibility work notes that geometric-change metrics alone can miss whether lines visually coalesce, and proposes locating discernibility conflicts at the target map scale ([Kronenfeld et al. 2024](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70273748)). A generalisation can therefore be geometrically conservative yet fail its actual display purpose.

### 1.4 Deliberate omission is legitimate only relative to the map’s job

Generalisation does not license arbitrary deletion. The purpose is to retain essential or characteristic information while making the representation usable. The cited constraint literature describes the tension as readability versus preservation, not brevity versus verbosity ([Harrie 2003](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/weight-setting-and-quality-assessment-in-simultaneous-graphic-gen/)). ISO’s application-relative quality framing also means an omission acceptable for one use can be disqualifying for another ([ISO 19157:2013](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)).

The lesson is not “hide complexity.” It is “declare the intended use, transform by named rules, preserve the source, and evaluate whether what mattered survived.”

## 2. Concrete translation to trust calibration

### 2.1 Correspondence, with limits

| Cartography | Agent harness translation |
|---|---|
| Geographic source data | Adjudicated task outcomes, verification records, and error taxonomy |
| Map purpose | Decision the owner must make: accept, inspect, reroute, or prohibit autonomous action |
| Map scale | Trust-decision resolution: agent family → task family → subtype → consequence/verification context |
| Selection | Show only evidence relevant to the request; explicitly count excluded evidence |
| Simplification | Collapse repeated outcomes into rates or intervals while preserving denominators and definitions |
| Aggregation | Combine sufficiently similar task cells; never combine merely to fill sparse data |
| Exaggeration | Elevate rare catastrophic failures or recent regressions so an average does not make them invisible |
| Displacement | Separate overlapping categories or conflicting signals in the display; do not alter underlying records |
| Scale range | Applicability statement for a reliability estimate: which task taxonomy, stakes, verifier, and time window it covers |
| Legend / quality metadata | Definitions, sample size, recency, verification provenance, missingness, and known omissions |
| Zoom | Request progressively finer evidence without changing the underlying outcome log |

The phrase “scale” must remain an analogy in the target domain. Geographic scale has a mathematical denominator and spatial neighbourhood; trust resolution is a designed taxonomy with no natural metric. Treating task labels as physical distance would be false precision.

### 2.2 Proposal — multiscale, task-conditioned trust cards — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Create one compact JSON-derived trust profile per agent, computed from adjudicated outcomes already available to the harness. The source outcome events remain canonical. The card is a derived portrayal and can always link back to its included event IDs.

Minimum cell key:

```text
(agent_id, task_family, task_subtype, consequence_class,
 verification_class, environment_epoch)
```

Minimum displayed payload:

```text
applicability: exact | parent-only | out-of-domain
outcomes: verified-correct / verified-incorrect / unresolved
n: adjudicated count; unresolved_n: count
time window and last observation
verification provenance: test, owner correction, independent checker, none
dominant failure modes
rare-severe failures: separate count, never averaged away
omitted evidence: count by reason
nearest supported parent and distance in taxonomy
```

No single numeric “trust score” is required. If the UI needs a first-glance symbol, use three portrayal states—supported, weak evidence, unknown—then expose counts and outcomes on expansion. A confidence interval may be shown only where the outcome definition and sampling unit are stable. “Unknown” must not be rendered as neutral or average.

The card applies cartographic zoom levels:

- **Overview:** task families with adequately supported evidence; unknown regions remain visible.
- **Operational:** matching subtype, consequence class, verification mode, recent outcome counts, and important failures.
- **Audit:** individual adjudications, inclusion/exclusion reasons, taxonomy version, and derivation code version.

This mechanism is **likely (55-80%) to improve routing and verification choices**, because it prevents an overview aggregate from masquerading as street-level evidence and follows Bard’s local-characterisation-before-aggregation order ([Bard 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9671.2004.00168.x)). It is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment A below**, measuring decision loss and misplaced acceptance under three display conditions.

### 2.3 Proposal — an omission ledger as part of every portrayal — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** This is part of the same trust-card implementation, not a separate service.

Every derived cell should report what its generalisation omitted:

- unresolved outcomes;
- outcomes outside the selected time window;
- outcomes using a materially different tool/runtime/model epoch;
- task labels too ambiguous to assign;
- owner corrections without independent adjudication;
- inherited evidence from a broader task class;
- censored tasks that were aborted or never verified.

The coarse view may compress these into `omitted: 37`, but expansion must disclose the reason counts. This is analogous to keeping the source database separate from its selected portrayal, but it improves on ordinary maps by making omission machine-auditable.

The omission ledger is **very likely (80-90%) to prevent a specific bookkeeping error—silently treating excluded or unresolved cases as successes—if all derived denominators are computed from the ledger**, based on the arithmetic fact that explicit mutually exclusive outcome buckets can be reconciled to the source-event total. This implementation claim is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a conservation test that asserts `included + omitted + unresolved = all eligible source events` for every card build**, plus mutation tests that drop each bucket in turn. The broader claim that users will make better decisions from seeing the ledger is only **likely (55-80%)**, based on ISO’s emphasis on reported quality for fitness-for-use rather than a universal quality threshold ([ISO 19157:2013](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)); it is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment A settles it**.

### 2.4 Proposal — scale-break rules that change required evidence with stakes — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** The evaluator and hook bus already assumed by the trust card consume a small policy table; this proposal adds no runtime.

Define explicit “scale breaks” for the *decision*, not for prose length. For example:

```yaml
low_consequence_reversible:
  admissible: parent task-family evidence
  minimum_adjudicated_n: 5
  action: allow, show weak-evidence marker
high_consequence_or_irreversible:
  admissible: exact subtype + matching verification class
  minimum_adjudicated_n: 20
  severe_failures: 0 within current epoch
  action_if_unmet: require independent verification or reroute
```

These numbers are examples, not recommended defaults. Choosing them without loss data would be false precision. The policy must be fitted to owner loss preferences and then versioned.

Scale-break rules are **likely (55-80%) to catch inappropriate trust transfer across consequence classes**, because the same nominal task can demand a different evidence resolution when the cost of error changes; ISO likewise leaves acceptability thresholds to the product/application rather than declaring one universal quality floor ([ISO 19157:2013](https://www.iso.org/standard/32575.html)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment B**, a retrospective threshold sweep followed by a prospective holdout.

### 2.5 Proposal — preservation constraints before aggregation — UNVERIFIED

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Define invariants that no summary or parent-cell aggregation may erase:

1. verified failures remain failures;
2. unresolved is never converted to success;
3. rare-severe failures remain separately visible;
4. model/tool/environment epoch boundaries are not crossed unless explicitly requested;
5. an aggregate must retain its component-cell counts and heterogeneity;
6. task cells with materially different verification strength are not pooled by default.

This proposal directly translates cartography’s preservation constraints. Harrie’s work states that readability and characteristic preservation compete and cannot all be fully satisfied, making the constraints and their weights explicit ([Harrie 2003](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/weight-setting-and-quality-assessment-in-simultaneous-graphic-gen/)).

These invariants are **very likely (80-90%) to catch invalid aggregation when violations are mechanically asserted**, because each invariant is testable against the source outcome records. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with property-based tests generating mixed task cells and confirming that prohibited merges fail closed**. Whether those constraints improve end-owner calibration is **likely (55-80%)**, not certain; **Experiment A settles it**.

## 3. What this catches that scalar or agent-global trust misses

These are failure classes the design is intended to catch; this unit makes no claim that the local harness presently exhibits them because the task explicitly excludes local-repository grounding.

1. **Ecological fallacy across task types.** An agent can have a strong overall record because common easy tasks dominate, while a rare subtype is weak. A fine-scale request cannot inherit the overview colour without an applicability warning.
2. **False certainty from sparse cells.** One success in a niche task remains weak evidence rather than a perfect score. Sample count and unknown territory are first-class portrayal elements.
3. **Simpson’s-paradox-like aggregation.** A global rate can reverse or hide subgroup patterns when task mix differs. Component counts and heterogeneity must remain available before aggregation.
4. **Verification-strength laundering.** Self-reported completion, test-verified completion, and owner-adjudicated correctness do not become interchangeable successes merely because their labels share “done.”
5. **Epoch drift.** Old performance under a different model, toolchain, or environment is visibly outside the current applicability range instead of silently pooled.
6. **Catastrophic-tail erasure.** A rare irreversible failure is exaggerated into its own symbol/count rather than averaged into a high success rate.
7. **Selective-verification bias.** Tasks that were never checked appear in `unresolved_n`; the card does not infer correctness from lack of correction.
8. **Omission laundering.** Filtered, ambiguous, stale, or censored events remain reconcilable through the omission ledger.
9. **Decision-scale mismatch.** An overview estimate may be adequate for choosing which agent to ask for a reversible draft but inadequate for authorising an irreversible action.
10. **Detail theatre.** A long explanation or many citations do not manufacture task-specific outcome evidence; trust portrayal is derived from adjudicated outcomes, not output fluency.

The system is **likely (55-80%) to detect more of these classes than a single global success rate**, based on the fact that it retains the conditioning variables that a scalar discards. This comparative benefit is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment A must include seeded cases for all ten classes and score detection separately**, rather than reporting only an average.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy fails

### 4.1 There is no natural trust denominator

Map scale has a defined relationship between represented distance and real-world distance; OGC gives a concrete physical-meters-per-pixel relationship for scale-dependent portrayal ([OGC Maps](https://docs.ogc.org/is/20-058/20-058.html)). “Task scale” has no corresponding natural unit. Task taxonomies are contestable, overlapping, and owner-dependent.

Therefore the harness must not generate a pseudo-continuous trust surface or interpolate between task categories as if they were neighbouring coordinates. The safe transfer is discrete levels of evidence resolution plus explicit applicability, not geometric mathematics.

### 4.2 Geographic neighbourhood does not imply epistemic similarity

Nearby objects on a map are spatially related. Two tasks with similar names can require different capabilities, tools, or failure tolerances. “Edit documentation” and “edit deployment policy” may share surface verbs while having radically different consequence and verification structures.

The analogy would fail if taxonomy distance were used as evidence of capability transfer. Parent-cell evidence may be displayed as context, but exact-cell absence must stay visible. It is **very likely (80-90%) unsafe to infer reliability solely from label proximity**, based on the absence of a validated metric connecting task-name distance to outcome correlation; this statement is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring held-out outcome correlation under candidate taxonomies and comparing it with a task-embedding or random baseline**.

### 4.3 A map’s source terrain is relatively external; agent performance is reflexive

Displaying a map usually does not alter the mountain. Displaying trust can alter routing, which changes which tasks an agent receives, which changes its observed task mix and future trust estimate. Verification may also concentrate on agents already thought weak, creating differential label quality.

This feedback loop means a trust card is not a passive map. It needs exploration or audit sampling if it is ever used for routing. A random audit sampler would be **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**, but adding it now is **UNVERIFIED and only roughly even (45-55%) worth the operational cost**, because no local routing or label-selection evidence is in scope; the settling experiment is a shadow-routing simulation measuring how quickly deterministic routing collapses coverage versus a small random-audit rate. This is not a core recommendation until that experiment supports it.

### 4.4 Cartographic omission can preserve navigational function; factual omission can invert truth

Leaving a small pond off a national road map may be harmless. Leaving a failed test or contraindicating fact out of an agent summary can reverse the decision. The target domain therefore needs stricter preservation invariants and an omission ledger. “Readable” is never sufficient evidence of “trustworthy.”

This is why the proposed card borrows controlled abstraction but rejects silent omission. The source records remain accessible and totals must reconcile.

### 4.5 Stable representation rules meet non-stationary agents

A road does not usually change because a map-rendering library was upgraded. Agent behaviour can shift with a model version, prompt, tool permission, context load, or environment. Pooling history across these boundaries can make a precisely rendered card precisely wrong.

Epoch segmentation is **likely (55-80%) necessary for useful calibration**, based on the causal plausibility that changed models and tools change outcomes, but it is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with change-point tests on adjudicated outcome sequences plus a prospective comparison of pooled versus epoch-specific prediction loss**. If no statistically or operationally meaningful shift appears, segmentation should remain coarse to avoid sparse-data overfit.

### 4.6 Maps have mature perceptual conventions; trust cards do not

Users broadly understand zooming and legends, but colours and map-like surfaces can imply continuity and authority that the evidence lacks. A red/amber/green badge can conceal denominator, class imbalance, and asymmetric loss.

For that reason, the first implementation should be a textual/structured card with progressive disclosure, not a choropleth heatmap. A graphical “trust map” is **unlikely (20-45%) to add net value before the taxonomy and calibration are validated**, based on the risk of falsely implying spatial continuity and the lack of target-domain perceptual evidence. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it only after Experiment A, with a follow-on comprehension test comparing the textual card and graphical view for false interpolation and denominator recall**.

## 5. Experiments that would settle the UNVERIFIED premises

### Experiment A — pre-registered trust-display trial

Construct a frozen set of historical or synthetic agent-task cases with adjudicated ground truth. Seed all ten failure classes listed above. Randomly assign evaluators to:

1. no reliability display;
2. one scalar agent success rate;
3. multiscale trust card with omission ledger and preservation constraints.

For each case, require `accept`, `verify`, or `reroute`, plus an estimate of correctness. Primary outcomes:

- decision loss under owner-specified asymmetric costs;
- Brier score or log loss for correctness estimates;
- false acceptance on high-consequence cases;
- correct recognition of “unknown”;
- detection rate for each seeded failure class;
- time to decision.

Pre-register subgroup analyses by evidence sparsity and consequence class. The proposal advances only if the card reduces decision loss and high-consequence false acceptance without an unacceptable time penalty. Merely increasing subjective confidence is not success.

### Experiment B — scale-break threshold selection without leakage

On a training period, sweep minimum sample sizes, time windows, exact-versus-parent applicability, and severe-failure rules against owner-specified loss. Freeze the chosen table. Evaluate on a later holdout period and then a prospective period. Compare with always-allow, always-verify, and scalar-score thresholds. Report coverage as well as error: a policy that “succeeds” by refusing every task is not useful.

### Experiment C — conservation and mutation tests

For every generated card, mechanically assert:

```text
all eligible source events
= included adjudicated events
+ included unresolved events
+ omitted events (partitioned by reason)
```

Mutate the builder to drop, duplicate, relabel, or cross an epoch boundary for one event at a time. Tests must fail for each mutation. This establishes bookkeeping integrity, not behavioural calibration; Experiment A remains necessary.

### Experiment D — taxonomy validity and drift

Have two independent annotators classify a stratified task sample; measure disagreement and adjudicate. Then test whether cells predict held-out outcomes better than agent-only and broad-family baselines. Run change-point analyses across model/tool/environment updates. Split a cell only when it improves held-out decision loss or isolates a materially different severe failure mode. Merge cells only when preservation constraints remain satisfied.

The complete package is **likely (55-80%) to be testable with existing task outcomes if those outcomes contain stable agent IDs, task labels, timestamps, and independent adjudication**, but this data-availability premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a schema audit counting non-null coverage and label provenance for those four fields**. If independent adjudication is sparse, the correct result is an extensive unknown region, not inferred trust.

## 6. Minimal implementation shape and anti-overfit gate

The smallest viable implementation has four pieces:

1. a versioned YAML task taxonomy and consequence/verification classes;
2. a deterministic script that derives cards from outcome events;
3. a pre-task hook that selects the most specific supported card and renders it into the decision context;
4. a post-adjudication hook that appends an outcome event and rebuilds only affected cells.

No learned trust model is required. No daemon is required. No vector store is required. No agent scheduler is replaced. No model is asked to grade itself. The aggregation function should be transparent enough to reproduce from the event list.

A first implementation is **likely (55-80%) to fit within roughly 200 lines excluding tests and static taxonomy data**, based on the narrow operations—group, count, partition omissions, match a taxonomy path, render JSON/text—but this estimate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a time-boxed spike that implements one event schema, three zoom levels, and the conservation test**. If integration requires a new service or state store, stop: it fails this unit’s anti-overfit gate and should not be smuggled in as “small.”

## 7. Rejected transfers and dead ends

### Rejected: a literal two-dimensional trust map

A 2-D map would force agents and tasks into invented coordinates, implying meaningful distances and interpolation. No evidence here validates that geometry. This does not transfer.

### Rejected: one “trust scale” analogous to 1:24,000

Trust is multidimensional: task subtype, consequence, verification strength, recency, and environment can all matter. Compressing these into one denominator recreates the scalar-score failure the borrow is meant to avoid.

### Rejected: generalise the agent’s prose and call that calibration

Summarising an output may improve readability but says nothing about whether the output is correct. The source layer for trust must be adjudicated outcomes, not the response being judged.

### Rejected: conceal low-salience failures for a clean overview

Cartographic selection is purpose-relative. In trust calibration, rare severe failures can be decision-dominant even when statistically infrequent. They require exaggeration or a separate constraint, not omission.

### Rejected: build a geospatial/vector database or new orchestration service

The analogy does not justify cartographic infrastructure. A versioned task tree and derived JSON views are sufficient to test the idea. A new runtime would violate the anti-overfit gate and add operational risk before value is demonstrated.

### Rejected: use confidence language emitted by the same agent as the trust source

Self-reported confidence may be retained as a feature for later study but is not an adjudicated outcome. Treating fluency or professed certainty as terrain would make the “map” a redraw of the agent’s own claim.

## 8. Final judgment

The analogy transfers at the level of **representation discipline**, not geometry. Cartography contributes four durable rules:

1. fitness is conditional on declared scale and purpose;
2. abstraction uses named operators and preserves the detailed source;
3. readability and preservation are competing constraints that must be evaluated;
4. an overview must not masquerade as evidence at a finer resolution.

Translated faithfully, those rules yield a small harness mechanism: task-conditioned trust cards with explicit applicability, progressive evidence detail, omission accounting, and preservation constraints. It would not tell the owner “Agent A is 87% trustworthy.” It would say, in effect: “For this task subtype and consequence class, here is the adjudicated territory we have actually mapped; here is the coarser evidence nearby; here is what was omitted; and here is where the map ends.”

The overall recommendation is **likely (55-80%) worth a bounded prototype**, based on the cited cartographic mechanisms, the clear failure modes of scalar aggregation, and the ability to ride the existing hook/config bus without a new runtime. It remains **UNVERIFIED; promotion beyond prototype requires Experiment A to beat both the scalar-score and no-display controls on pre-registered decision loss, while Experiments C and D establish bookkeeping and taxonomy validity**.
