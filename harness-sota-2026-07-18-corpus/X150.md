# X150 — Archival science → reliability

## Research question and result

Can archival science contribute a mechanism that prevents an agent from confidently reporting a wrong conclusion after one unvalidated probe?

**Result: the analogy transfers, but only partially.** The useful borrow is the InterPARES **graduated presumption of authenticity plus contextual verification** method, implemented with a small PREMIS-like evidence record. It should not be imported as a claim that provenance makes an answer true. Archives distinguish an authentic record from an accurate statement of fact; the harness must preserve that distinction.

My proposal is an **Evidence Passport and Assertion Gate**. For high-risk conclusion types—especially *absent*, *broken*, *complete/done*, and exhaustive counts—the agent may not emit an unqualified conclusion from a single probe. It must retain the probe's identity and context, demonstrate that the detector recognizes a known-present control, and obtain a second signal whose failure mode is mechanically independent. If it cannot, it emits an explicitly scoped `UNVERIFIED` result and the experiment needed to settle it.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this gate would prevent the measured class of confident single-probe false negatives, because it makes the two missing conditions—detector validation and independent corroboration—machine-checkable before final wording is allowed. That forecast is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E1, “single-probe replay,”** defined below, would settle it.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that this mechanism would materially improve wrong positive conclusions, bad synthesis from correct evidence, or time-sensitive facts, because the borrowed method primarily strengthens identity, integrity, and evidentiary context rather than semantic reasoning. This boundary follows both the archival distinction between authenticity and factual trustworthiness and the proposed gate's narrow trigger set; **experiment E2, “wrong-positive mutation suite,”** would quantify the residual benefit.

## The mechanism in its own field

### 1. Authenticity is not accepted from the face of one digital object

InterPARES defines an authentic electronic record as one that is what it purports to be and is free from tampering or corruption. Because electronic records can be altered intentionally or inadvertently, a preserver must establish both the record's **identity** and its **integrity**, not merely inspect the bytes or trust a label. Identity includes creator participants, dates, the action or matter to which the record belongs, attachments, and the *archival bond* linking the record to other records in the same action. See the InterPARES Authenticity Task Force's [Requirements for Assessing and Maintaining the Authenticity of Electronic Records](https://www.interpares.org/display_file.cfm?doc=ip1_authenticity_requirements.pdf), especially the conceptual framework and Benchmark Requirement A.1.

This is a contextual account of evidence. A record does not authenticate itself. Its relation to a creator, procedure, system, related records, and custody history contributes to the assessment.

### 2. The presumption is graduated and cumulative

InterPARES does not reduce authenticity to one magic test. Its benchmark requirements support a **presumption** inferred from known facts about how a record was created, handled, and maintained. The requirements are cumulative: the more requirements satisfied, and the better each is satisfied, the stronger the presumption. When that basis is weak or insufficient, the method moves to **verification**. The official task-force report describes verification as correspondence between known facts about the record, its contexts, and the proposed fact of authenticity; possible evidence includes separately preserved copies, registers, audit trails, forensic or textual analysis, and trusted-third-party testimony. See [Authenticity Task Force Report, pp. 21–22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf).

That is the key mechanism for this unit: confidence is earned from a bundle of relevant controls, and weak contextual evidence triggers another method rather than a confident declaration.

### 3. Chain of preservation makes controls continuous

InterPARES calls the lifecycle-wide system of controls that protects identity and integrity through changes in storage or presentation the **chain of preservation**. The chain matters because system boundaries and transformations are precisely where ordinary controls can stop applying. See InterPARES' [Principles and Criteria](https://www.interpares.org/display_file.cfm?doc=ip1_principles_criteria.pdf). The project's summary also reports that authenticity measures are generally procedural and that electronic records are embedded in juridical-administrative, provenancial, procedural, documentary, and technological contexts; see the official [InterPARES 1 Authenticity Domain](https://www.interpares.org/ip1/ip1_atf).

In other words, the field does not rely only on a final checksum. It asks what procedure created the record, what happened to it, who or what performed those events, and whether identity and integrity remained controlled.

### 4. PREMIS makes provenance operational rather than narrative

PREMIS supplies an implementable preservation-metadata model around Objects, Events, Agents, and Rights. The Library of Congress describes PREMIS as the international standard for metadata supporting preservation and long-term usability and publishes its schemas and controlled vocabularies at the [PREMIS maintenance site](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/). Its event vocabulary includes fixity checks, validation, message-digest calculation, ingestion, migration, replication, and digital-signature validation; see the [PREMIS preservation-event collection](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/pif-presentations/rebecca-SKOS/preservationEvents-PREMIS.html).

The important point is not XML. It is the normalized relationship:

`object/result ← event/probe ← agent/tool`, with outcome, time, parameters, and related objects recorded.

Digital-preservation practice also warns against overclaiming from one integrity device. A checksum detects whether a bitstream changed but does not say where it changed; repeated checks, logs, multiple copies, and repair from a known-good copy form a stronger system. The Digital Preservation Coalition explains these limits and the increasing levels of practice in [Fixity and checksums](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/technical-solutions-and-tools/fixity-and-checksums). Its acquisition guidance says that without a verifiable manifest, comprehensive integrity verification may be impossible and only a sampled indication of completeness and quality may be available; see [Acquisition and appraisal](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/organisational-activities/acquisition-and-appraisal).

### 5. The crucial archival limit: authenticity is not truth

An authentic record can faithfully preserve a false or mistaken statement. InterPARES treats trustworthiness as comprising accuracy, reliability, and authenticity, rather than treating those words as synonyms; see its [terminology entry for trustworthiness](https://www.interpares.org/ip2/ip2_terminology_db/trustworthiness/1413). The International Council on Archives is even more explicit: reliability is a record's trustworthiness as a statement of fact, while authenticity is trustworthiness *as a record*—that it is what it purports to be and is free of tampering or corruption; see ICA's [Digital Records: Authenticity and Reliability](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2023/12/0102_slides_digital_records_authenticity_and_reliability.pdf).

This distinction controls the translation. Provenance can show exactly which command produced `0 matches`, with which scope and exit status. It cannot by itself show that the target item is absent.

## Structural mapping

| Archival-science element | Agent-harness analogue | Reliability role |
|---|---|---|
| Record identity | Claim ID, exact proposition, subject, scope, time, revision/worktree, environment | Prevents evidence gathered for one scope from silently supporting another |
| Record integrity | Raw tool result hash plus retained exit status, stderr, truncation flag, and parser status | Detects altered, partial, or misparsed evidence |
| Provenance | Tool/agent, command or API parameters, working directory, timestamp, permissions, source endpoint | Makes hidden scope and execution assumptions reviewable |
| Archival bond | Explicit links from claim to every probe, control probe, related claim, and contradiction | Stops a final sentence from becoming detached from its evidence |
| Benchmark requirements | Claim-type policy: required fields and minimum evidentiary conditions | Converts “confidence” from prose into inspectable prerequisites |
| Weak presumption → verification | Failed/unknown control or only one method forces an independent probe or `UNVERIFIED` | Prevents confident closure when evidence is inadequate |
| Chain of preservation | Append-only events from probe execution through parsing, summarization, and final claim | Exposes transformations that can drop errors or negate meaning |
| Appraisal/retention | Retain compact passports for risky claims; discard or summarize low-risk raw chatter after the task | Keeps decisive evidence without building general long-term memory |

The mapping is **likely (55-80%)** to remain useful across shell, filesystem, API, MCP, and structured-output probes because each produces an identifiable result through an event and an agent/tool. This generality is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E3, “five-bus portability trial,”** would implement the same passport on one task from each bus and measure missing or bus-specific fields.

## Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

### Evidence Passport and Assertion Gate

Build one small hook plus a final-response rule. It is deliberately not a second orchestrator, memory service, vector database, knowledge graph, critic model, durable-execution runtime, guardrail framework, or observability service.

#### A. Create a compact passport only when a risky claim is forming

The agent records a short structured object adjacent to its working state:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "c17",
  "claim": "No matching skill exists in scope S",
  "claim_type": "absence",
  "scope": {"roots": ["S"], "revision": "R", "time": "T"},
  "signals": [
    {
      "method_family": "filesystem_name_enumeration",
      "tool": "...",
      "parameters": "...",
      "exit_status": 0,
      "stderr_present": false,
      "truncated": false,
      "result_digest": "sha256:...",
      "result_summary": "0 candidates"
    }
  ],
  "positive_control": {
    "target": "known-present fixture in the same effective scope",
    "detected": true,
    "method_family": "filesystem_name_enumeration"
  },
  "independence": {
    "second_family": "registry_or_content_manifest",
    "shared_failure_modes": []
  },
  "verdict": "verified | contradicted | unverified",
  "settling_experiment": "..."
}
```

The digest is useful for integrity and auditability but explicitly earns **zero** semantic-confidence credit. This prevents checksum theater.

#### B. Trigger on semantic claim classes, not keywords alone

The final-response hook examines an agent-supplied `claim_type` field for:

- absence/nonexistence: “there is no…”, “not installed”, “no matches”;
- broken/nonfunctional: “the hook is broken”, “service is dead”;
- completion/exhaustiveness: “done”, “all files”, “nothing remains”;
- exact global counts used to justify a conclusion.

A lightweight phrase matcher may warn when prose appears to contain an untyped risky claim, but it must not be the sole detector. It is **likely (55-80%)** that requiring explicit claim typing plus a warning matcher will have better recall than keywords alone without requiring semantic-model judging, because generation supplies the type and the matcher catches omissions. This is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E4, “claim-trigger corpus test,”** would label 300 historical conclusion sentences and measure precision/recall for explicit typing, matching, and their union.

#### C. Validate the first detector with a known-present control

Before a negative result counts, run the same detector against an item known to be present in the same effective scope and representation. A control in another directory, account, revision, endpoint, or parser path is invalid. Record the control target before running the negative probe where practical, so the agent cannot opportunistically select an easy control afterward.

Examples:

- Before concluding a directory contains no `SKILL.md`, confirm the enumeration finds a known `SKILL.md` within the same searched root.
- Before concluding an API returns no objects of a type, confirm the same credentials, endpoint, pagination code, and parser can retrieve a known object of that type.
- Before concluding a hook never fired, inject or locate a known event that the event detector must report.
- Before concluding a build has no errors, demonstrate that the diagnostic parser recognizes a controlled failing fixture or a known diagnostic line, without contaminating the real build.

This control validates *detection capacity*, not the target conclusion. Passing it does not count as the second confirming signal.

#### D. Require mechanically independent corroboration

Two executions of the same command, two wrappers around the same library, or two parsers consuming the same truncated stream are one signal. The passport must name each method family and list shared failure modes. Accept the second signal only when its acquisition path and dominant failure mode differ.

Examples of acceptable pairs:

- filesystem enumeration **and** an independently generated registry/manifest;
- API list endpoint **and** direct retrieval of a deterministically named object;
- runtime event capture **and** durable application-side event state;
- compiler exit status/stderr **and** independently inspected output artifact or test execution;
- declared configuration **and** observed runtime behavior.

The gate cannot mechanically prove independence in general. A short allowlist of common method-family pairs can reject obvious duplicates; ambiguous pairs remain `UNVERIFIED`. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this conservative rule will reject repeated same-path probes, because equality of family identifiers is trivial to test. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that the initial allowlist will correctly classify subtler shared dependencies, because two apparently different tools may share an index, cache, credential, or upstream service. Both forecasts are **UNVERIFIED; experiment E5, “dependency-confound injection,”** would seed shared cache, permission, truncation, stale-index, and parser failures across nominally distinct probes and score classifications.

#### E. Fail open on task execution, fail closed on epistemic wording

If the gate lacks sufficient evidence, it does not block all work. It blocks only the unqualified risky conclusion. The permitted output must say, inline:

> `UNVERIFIED: probe P found no matches in scope S, but detector control C failed/was unavailable and/or no independent signal was obtained. Experiment X would settle it.`

This preserves progress while preventing false certainty. It is **likely (55-80%)** to avoid the common failure mode where rigid harness enforcement causes retry loops or task abandonment, because the work continues and only the confidence form changes. This is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E6, “gate-friction A/B,”** would compare completion rate, retries, latency, and unsupported-closure rate with hard task blocking versus wording-only blocking.

#### F. Retention schedule

Retain the passport and small normalized summaries through the task and any immediate review/retry. Retain raw output only when it is the decisive evidence, is not already durably available, and fits a size cap; otherwise retain a digest, locator, truncation status, and bounded excerpt. Dispose of ephemeral low-risk passports at task close under an explicit schedule. Promote only owner corrections, contradictions, and verified high-impact findings to longer-lived regression fixtures.

This borrows archival appraisal rather than “save everything.” It is **likely (55-80%)** to keep storage and prompt cost bounded because passports are created only for risky claims and raw results are capped. This is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E7, “10k-task retention simulation,”** would replay task traces and measure bytes, token rehydration cost, and forensic sufficiency under candidate schedules.

## What it catches that a normal tool transcript misses

1. **The detector returned zero because its scope was wrong.** The passport exposes roots, revision, working directory, endpoint, credentials context, and time. The same-scope positive control is likely to fail.
2. **The detector itself is broken.** A known-present control is not detected, so the negative conclusion cannot pass.
3. **The command succeeded but its output was truncated or parsing failed.** Exit status, stderr, truncation, and parser outcome are separate required fields; a clean exit alone is insufficient.
4. **Two “confirmations” share one failure mode.** Method-family and shared-dependency declarations prevent reruns and aliases from masquerading as independent evidence.
5. **The evidence changed meaning during summarization.** The event chain retains the raw-result digest and the successive result summary/claim relationship.
6. **Configuration was mistaken for runtime behavior.** Independence rules demand an observational signal for behavioral claims.
7. **A local or time-bounded result became a global assertion.** Claim identity preserves scope and time, and the gate compares the wording's scope to the probes' scope.
8. **A negative conclusion is plausible but not adequately verifiable.** The output remains useful but must carry `UNVERIFIED` inline with a settling experiment.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that cases 1–4 are detectable when the required passport fields are truthfully populated, because each corresponds to a direct gate predicate. It is **likely (55-80%)** that cases 5–7 are detectable, because scope and transformations are recordable but semantic comparison is less mechanical. These predictions are **UNVERIFIED; experiment E1, “single-probe replay,”** would report per-case detection rates and false blocks.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy fails

### Disanalogy 1: archives authenticate records; agents assert facts

The archival method asks whether a record is what it purports to be and has retained identity and integrity. The harness needs to know whether a proposition about the world is correct. Those are not equivalent. A perfectly authentic API response can be stale, incomplete, access-filtered, or wrong at its source. Therefore the import must include independent empirical verification, not just pristine provenance.

**Consequence:** PREMIS-like logging alone is rejected. The proposal counts evidence only when detector control and method independence are present.

### Disanalogy 2: archival objects are often stabilized; agent environments are live

An archive can reason over accessioned objects and controlled transformations. An agent probes changing worktrees, eventually consistent APIs, expiring credentials, caches, and concurrent processes. A second signal taken later may disagree because reality changed rather than because either detector failed.

**Consequence:** every passport includes time and environment/revision identity. Contradiction yields `UNVERIFIED` or a temporal explanation, never automatic majority vote. It is **likely (55-80%)** that time/snapshot binding will distinguish many genuine changes from detector disagreement, but **UNVERIFIED; experiment E8, “concurrent-mutation trial,”** would mutate targets between probes and measure correct temporal attribution.

### Disanalogy 3: custodial trust does not map cleanly to tool independence

Archives may rely on a trusted custodian and controlled chain. In an agent harness, apparently separate tools can share a filesystem index, network proxy, permission boundary, model-generated parameter, or stale cache. Naming two tools is not proof of independence.

**Consequence:** independence is about failure modes and upstream dependencies, not tool count. Ambiguity lowers the verdict rather than being wished away.

### Disanalogy 4: archival appraisal tolerates delayed judgment; agents face latency budgets

Full diplomatic analysis is expensive. Requiring it for every sentence would add tool calls, tokens, and new failure surfaces, and would violate the minimal-architecture gate.

**Consequence:** apply the mechanism only to high-damage conclusion types and permit scoped `UNVERIFIED` wording. Do not create a universal provenance graph.

### Disanalogy 5: archival benchmark requirements are domain and custody controls, not a universal numeric confidence score

Counting passport fields would invite false precision: five weak correlated signals do not beat two independent strong ones. InterPARES' cumulative presumption motivates escalation, but it does not license a context-free agent confidence percentage.

**Consequence:** use a small categorical verdict—`verified`, `contradicted`, `unverified`—plus explicit evidence, not a scalar “truth score.”

### Verdict on transfer

The analogy **does transfer** at the level of procedure: identify the evidentiary object, preserve its context and transformation history, treat assurance as conditional, and escalate weak presumptions to verification from other sources. It **does not transfer** as a guarantee that authentic evidence entails a true conclusion.

The proposed design respects that boundary. It is not “archival provenance proves truth”; it is “archival discipline makes unsupported closure visible and forces an independent check.”

## Anti-overfit gate

The proposal passes the stated anti-overfit gate:

- It rides the existing hook/skill/MCP/tool bus.
- It is **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.
- It adds no orchestration runtime.
- It adds no vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or general guardrail framework.
- It is claim-triggered and bounded rather than always-on exhaustive provenance.
- It uses deterministic checks for required evidence shape and obvious non-independence; it does not ask another model to “judge truth.”

The line-count estimate is **likely (55-80%)** if implemented as one schema validator, a small phrase-warning table, and a final-response hook, because no service or UI is required. It is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E9, “minimal prototype,”** would implement the hook against the actual bus and count non-generated source lines. If the actual integration requires a daemon or broad transcript interception, this proposal should be reclassified **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and rejected absent fresh justification.

## Experiments and acceptance criteria

### E1 — Single-probe replay (primary settling experiment)

Construct a frozen suite containing the known false-negative patterns plus mutations for wrong root, wrong revision, hidden permission failure, stale index, truncated output, parser-empty-on-error, pagination omission, and same-command rerun. Include valid negative controls. Run the proposed gate without giving it expected labels.

Accept if:

- at least 95% of seeded unsupported absence/broken/done claims are blocked from unqualified wording;
- all historically measured single-unvalidated-probe failures in the suite are blocked;
- at least 90% of valid, independently verified negative conclusions pass;
- every blocked conclusion can still be emitted with inline `UNVERIFIED` and a concrete settling experiment.

The thresholds are design targets, not evidence that performance will reach them.

### E2 — Wrong-positive mutation suite

Seed authentic but stale, access-filtered, semantically misleading, and source-incorrect results. Measure how often passports and corroboration help. Expected benefit is deliberately low; use this to prevent claims of general factual verification.

### E3 — Five-bus portability trial

Apply passports to filesystem, shell/compiler, HTTP API, MCP, and structured-output tasks. Record which fields are universal, which require bus adapters, and whether any adapter exceeds the small-build budget.

### E4 — Claim-trigger corpus test

Human-label 300 final-answer sentences for risky conclusion type and qualification status. Compare explicit claim typing, phrase matching, and their union. Reject automatic enforcement if the union misses more than 5% of harmful closures or falsely gates more than 10% of ordinary descriptive prose.

### E5 — Dependency-confound injection

Make nominally separate tools share a stale cache, index, credential, proxy, parser, or source manifest. Test whether the independence classifier rejects the pair. Expand the allowlist only from demonstrated dependency structures.

### E6 — Gate-friction A/B

Compare wording-only blocking with full task blocking on completion, tool retries, latency, and unsupported closure. Retain wording-only behavior unless hard blocking demonstrates a materially better reliability/throughput tradeoff.

### E7 — 10k-task retention simulation

Replay traces under three schedules: all raw evidence, passport-only, and risk-tiered raw retention. Select the smallest schedule that still reconstructs at least 95% of adjudicated high-risk conclusions.

### E8 — Concurrent-mutation trial

Change target state between the first probe, control, and second probe. Verify that time/revision binding prevents false claims of detector disagreement and prevents merging evidence from different states.

### E9 — Minimal prototype

Implement the schema, claim gate, and three method-family pairs on the existing bus. Count handwritten lines and dependencies. Reject or rescope if it exceeds roughly 200 lines because integration requires a resident service or generalized semantic judge.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just require two tool calls”

Rejected. Two calls can share command, parser, scope error, cache, permissions, or upstream source. Mechanical independence is the property that matters.

### “Store every trace forever”

Rejected. Custodial maximalism adds cost without improving the key decision. Archival appraisal supports selective retention; the task needs decisive evidence passports and regression fixtures, not total memory.

### “Hash every output and call it verified”

Rejected. Fixity proves sameness relative to a baseline, not correctness, completeness, or adequate scope. A hash of a wrong empty response is faithfully wrong.

### “Have a critic model approve conclusions”

Rejected. This introduces a trained/agentic critic, adds correlated model failure, and violates the anti-overfit gate. The useful checks here are structural and testable.

### “Use majority vote over many probes”

Rejected. Correlated probes can produce unanimous error, and a changing target can generate legitimate disagreement. Two well-characterized independent signals plus a validated detector are more defensible than an arbitrary vote count.

### “Adopt the complete PREMIS schema”

Rejected. PREMIS demonstrates the event/object/agent/provenance pattern, but its preservation scope is much broader than task-time claim verification. A tiny profile is sufficient and avoids schema-induced reliability failures.

## Gaps

- **UNVERIFIED: no empirical estimate is available here for how many historical wrong conclusions would have been caught by each individual predicate; experiment E1 would settle it.** The pinned brief states the observed class, but this ideation unit intentionally did not inspect the local corpus or repository.
- **UNVERIFIED: method-family independence may be expressible accurately enough without a dependency graph; experiment E5 would settle it.** If not, prefer conservative `UNVERIFIED` outcomes over building a graph service.
- **UNVERIFIED: final-response hooks on the target buses can access claim metadata at the right point; experiment E9 would settle it.** If they cannot, use a skill/rule that produces a passport block before finalization rather than adding a new runtime.
- **UNVERIFIED: a same-scope known-present control exists for every negative query; E3 would measure coverage.** When none exists, the correct behavior is independent verification plus inline qualification, not a fabricated control.
- This unit used external archival-science sources and did not ground claims in the local subject tree, as required by the ideation lens.

## Bottom line

Archival science has already solved a closely related procedural problem: do not treat a decontextualized digital result as self-authenticating; build a cumulative presumption from identity, integrity, provenance, procedural controls, and related evidence, then escalate weak cases to independent verification. The exact transfer is an Evidence Passport and Assertion Gate for risky agent conclusions.

The borrow is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce confident wrong conclusions caused by a single unvalidated negative probe, because those conclusions would lack both a demonstrated detector control and an independent second signal. That central effectiveness claim remains **UNVERIFIED; E1, the single-probe replay, is the experiment that would settle it.** It is not a general truth engine, and presenting it as one would repeat the very overclaiming failure this unit is meant to prevent.
