# X083 — Mechanism design → reliability

## Executive verdict

The cross-domain borrow transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form. The useful mechanism is **random spot-checking against independently established ground truth, coupled to a strictly proper scoring rule for the agent's stated confidence**. Mechanism design uses this combination to make costly information acquisition and truthful probability reporting privately worthwhile when exhaustive verification is too expensive. The harness analogue is to make every consequential conclusion a resolvable claim, require the agent to commit a probability before learning whether it will be audited, secretly sample some claims for independent resolution, and use the resulting proper scores to select prompts/models/policies over repeated runs.

My transfer judgment is **very likely (80-90%)**: this mechanism is structurally useful as a *policy-evaluation and selection loop*, because the target setting has repeated reports, costly checking, resolvable claims, and configurable selection pressure—the same four ingredients used in the source mechanisms. The evidence is the formal truthfulness result for proper scores and the limited-ground-truth spot-check result described below. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to work as a literal economic incentive applied to a single transient LLM invocation, because an invocation has no demonstrated stable utility over future routing or reputation. That disanalogy is decisive: the harness must apply the mechanism to repeated agent policies/configurations, not pretend the current token stream is a rational contractor.

The mechanism does **not** replace the supplied two-mechanically-independent-signals rule. A random audit cannot catch every bad conclusion at decision time. The immediate safety control remains a deterministic admission gate for high-impact claims; spot-checking and proper scoring make the gate measurable, difficult to Goodhart, and improvable over time.

## 1. What mechanism design has already solved

### 1.1 Strictly proper scoring rules: make honest uncertainty optimal

A scoring rule maps a reported predictive distribution and the eventual outcome to a score. Gneiting and Raftery define a rule as proper when a forecaster maximizes expected score by reporting its actual belief distribution, and strictly proper when that maximizer is unique. Their treatment connects proper scores to decision theory, forecast evaluation, and estimation ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). Brier's original paper introduced verification of probabilistic forecasts rather than forcing categorical forecasts ([Brier, 1950, *Monthly Weather Review*](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%281950%29078%3C0001%3AVOFEIT%3E2.0.CO%3B2)).

For a binary event with resolved outcome `y ∈ {0,1}` and reported probability `p`, the quadratic/Brier loss can be written:

```text
L(p,y) = (p-y)^2
```

If the forecaster's actual belief that `y=1` is `q`, expected loss is:

```text
E[L] = q(1-p)^2 + (1-q)p^2
     = q(1-q) + (p-q)^2
```

The second term is uniquely minimized at `p=q`. This is the incentive-compatibility result in miniature: exaggerating a 0.7 belief to 1.0 or hiding it at 0.5 worsens expected score. The mechanism elicits uncertainty; it does not manufacture better evidence.

Three source-field qualifications matter:

1. **There must be an outcome.** Proper scoring resolves a report against an exogenous observation. It cannot establish truth from the report itself. Gneiting and Raftery's definition explicitly scores a forecast against an observation ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).
2. **The classic monetary interpretation assumes expected-score maximization.** Risk aversion can pull reports toward 0.5 under ordinary proper scores ([Peysakhovich & Plagborg-Møller, 2012](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2019078)). Binarized scoring rules can remove dependence on risk preference for human subjects by awarding a fixed prize according to a randomized comparison ([Hossain & Okui, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdt006)). For this harness, risk preference is not the principal issue; the more basic issue is whether an LLM invocation optimizes any persistent score at all.
3. **Accuracy and decision cost are different objects.** A proper rule elicits `p`; a separate decision policy should decide what probability is acceptable given the cost of a false conclusion. Making the scoring rule itself reward overconfidence on “important” claims would destroy propriety. This follows directly from the unique-optimum property above ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).

### 1.2 Random spot-checking: induce effort with limited ground truth

Information elicitation adds a harder problem: acquiring the signal itself costs effort, and the principal cannot afford to verify every report. Gao, Wright, and Leyton-Brown study exactly this structure. Their simple mechanism compares a report to trusted, noisy-but-unbiased ground truth with some probability and gives an unconditional reward otherwise. They show that this can provide stronger incentive guarantees with less ground-truth access than a broad class of peer-prediction mechanisms ([Gao, Wright & Leyton-Brown, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042); [author-hosted paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~kevinlb/papers/2016-ECDS-PeerPrediction.pdf)).

The source-field logic is:

1. The worker chooses cheap/low-information or costly/high-information effort.
2. The worker commits a report without knowing whether this task will be checked.
3. With audit probability `a`, the principal resolves the report against trusted ground truth and pays according to accuracy; otherwise the principal uses a report-independent baseline payment.
4. Effort is privately worthwhile when the expected audit-dependent score advantage exceeds effort cost. In schematic form, `a × Δ(expected score from effort) > effort cost`.
5. Audit assignment must not be manipulable from the report. If “uncertain” reports were never audited, strategic agents would learn to emit uncertainty merely to avoid checking.

This is not merely an informal appeal to auditing. It is a mechanism for converting *limited, unpredictably allocated verification* into incentives across a repeated population. Work on costly verification likewise formalizes the principal's tradeoff between checking private reports and the cost of checking them ([Ben-Porath, Dekel & Lipman, 2014, AER](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.12.3779)).

### 1.3 The rejected cousin: peer prediction / Bayesian truth serum

Peer prediction scores a rater using another rater's correlated report. Miller, Resnick, and Zeckhauser show honest reporting as a Nash equilibrium by applying proper scoring rules to the posterior distribution of a peer's signal ([Miller, Resnick & Zeckhauser, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0379)). Prelec's Bayesian truth serum handles subjective questions without objective ground truth by rewarding answers that are more common than the population predicts, rather than merely rewarding the majority answer ([Prelec, 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15486294/)).

**Do not import either as the primary harness mechanism.** Independent-looking agents can share the same model weights, prompt, search index, tool outage, filesystem view, or flawed detector. Their errors are therefore often correlated for reasons unrelated to truth. Agreement would reward the very common-mode failure the harness needs to expose. Gao et al. specifically show that peer-prediction mechanisms can have attractive low-effort, uninformative equilibria when agents coordinate on cheap signals, while limited trusted spot checks avoid that failure ([Gao et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)).

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that two runs of the same model against the same failed probe are not a trustworthy substitute for ground truth; the evidence is structural identity of their observation channel plus Gao et al.'s result that correlated cheap signals can sustain uninformative agreement. The residual uncertainty is that independently diversified models/tools may sometimes supply useful corroboration. “Two agents agree” must therefore never satisfy the two-signal gate unless their data-generating channels are mechanically independent.

## 2. Structural transfer test

| Source-field assumption | Harness counterpart | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| A principal wants accurate private information | Owner/harness wants conclusions that survive verification | Direct match |
| Information acquisition is costly | Extra probe, control query, alternate API, or human resolution costs tokens/time | Direct match |
| Reports repeat across tasks | Models, prompts, skills, and policies execute repeatedly | Direct match at policy level |
| Some ground truth becomes observable | Many filesystem, process, configuration, test, and API claims can be independently resolved | Partial match; not every semantic claim is resolvable |
| Agent chooses effort to maximize expected payment | An LLM invocation follows conditioning and decoding, with no proven persistent utility | Does **not** directly match |
| Selection/reward changes future behavior | Harness can route future work toward configurations with better verified score | Match at population/selection level |
| Audit is independent of report | Harness can sample after a claim is committed, using a hidden seed | Direct match if implemented carefully |
| Ground truth is trusted or at least unbiased | Resolver must use validated controls and independent channels | Conditional match; oracle quality is the central engineering risk |

The analogy therefore survives only after changing the unit of agency. Treat a versioned `(model, system prompt, skill policy, tool policy)` as the “bidder/forecaster.” It emits many claims. The harness observes sampled outcomes and changes routing or promotion decisions. Do not treat a one-off conversational persona as a bank account that fears a future penalty.

This distinction is also the Goodhart defense. Manheim and Garrabrant separate regressional, extremal, causal, and adversarial Goodhart effects; all arise when optimization breaks the relationship between a proxy and the actual goal ([Manheim & Garrabrant, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Counting evidence fields would be a weak proxy and invite checkbox evidence. Scoring independently resolved claims is closer to the true goal, while secret spot checks reduce the ability to optimize only the audited subset. Neither eliminates oracle failure or distribution shift.

### 3.2 Synchronous admission gate [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Before a high-impact negative/broken/done claim reaches the owner or authorizes a mutation, a hook validates:

1. two evidence objects exist;
2. their channel families differ according to a small explicit independence table;
3. each detector has a known-present positive control, recorded before absence is inferred;
4. neither probe ended in timeout, parse failure, permission denial, truncation, or ambiguous empty output;
5. the claimed scope does not exceed the scope actually probed.

If validation fails, the hook does not silently rewrite the claim. It returns a structured request for the missing control/channel or forces the conclusion to be labeled unresolved. This gate deterministically catches the *form* of the target failure—one unvalidated negative probe—before output. It cannot determine whether two plausible probes share an unknown common cause.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that this gate catches claims containing only one declared probe, because that is a syntactic invariant. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to catch disguised common-mode dependence; evidence is that an explicit channel taxonomy catches known same-family cases, while unknown dependencies remain outside any static taxonomy. The latter premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E2, the common-mode mutation suite in §6**.

### 3.3 Secret post-commit spot checks [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** After the claim contract is immutable, sample claims for audit with a seed unavailable to the producing agent. Sampling probability should be a monotone function of impact and novelty, with a nonzero random floor for ordinary claims. Never let reported low confidence reduce audit probability to zero.

The resolver must establish an outcome using a channel not used by the original claim, validate that channel on a known-present control, and preserve `UPHELD / REFUTED / UNRESOLVABLE / ORACLE-FAILED`. Only the first two outcomes enter a proper score. `UNRESOLVABLE` must not be coerced to false; `ORACLE-FAILED` must not be treated as evidence about the claim.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that post-commit hidden sampling reduces selective compliance at the policy-selection level; evidence is the source spot-check mechanism's reliance on probabilistic ground-truth comparison and report-independent audit selection ([Gao et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)). Whether current models anticipate and respond to that selection pressure within a prompt is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 settles it** and is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because the economic agent assumption does not transfer directly.

### 3.4 Proper score and calibration ledger [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** For every resolved sampled claim, store `(policy_version, claim_class, p, y, Brier loss, resolver provenance)`. Compute:

- mean Brier loss by policy and claim class;
- calibration bins, such as whether claims near 0.8 are upheld about 80% of the time;
- coverage: fraction resolvable and fraction audited;
- gate violations and oracle-failure rate separately from accuracy.

Do not collapse these into a single leaderboard number. A configuration could improve Brier loss by emitting 0.5 for everything, reducing usefulness; calibration must be read alongside sharpness and task completion. Forecasting research explicitly treats calibration and sharpness as separate desirable properties ([Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x)).

Use the ledger first for shadow evaluation and regression detection, then for promotion/routing only after minimum sample sizes and a held-out audit stream exist. It is **likely (55-80%)** that a class-stratified ledger reveals policies that are specifically overconfident on absence/broken/done claims; evidence is the mathematical sensitivity of Brier loss to confident errors and the repeated-task structure. The magnitude and required sample size are **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 settles them**.

### 3.5 Selection, not fictional punishment [UNVERIFIED]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Selection actions should be bounded and reversible:

- demote a policy from high-impact negative-claim work after statistically credible degradation;
- route that claim class to a policy with better held-out resolved score;
- require stronger evidence or human review when a class is poorly calibrated;
- promote only on a held-out audit stream not exposed during policy iteration.

Never tell a single invocation that it has “lost points” and assume reliability is solved. A score changes system behavior only when connected to routing, gating, prompt selection, or model selection. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that population-level selection is a more faithful transfer than invocation-level rewards; evidence is the repeated forecast-evaluation setup assumed by proper scoring and the absence of a demonstrated persistent utility function for an isolated generation. Outcome improvement remains **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 settles it**.

## 4. What the design catches that a bare two-probe instruction misses

1. **Confident single-probe absence.** The synchronous gate rejects the claim contract because it has one channel or lacks a positive control. This catch is deterministic given honest schema population.
2. **Detector failure masquerading as an empty set.** A detector that cannot find the known-present control is marked failed; its empty target result cannot count as absence evidence.
3. **Two textual probes backed by one mechanism.** The independence table can reject, for example, two wrappers around the same API or two agents reading the same cached result.
4. **Scope inflation.** A search of one directory cannot support “nowhere on the machine”; the evidence scope and claim scope mismatch is gate-visible.
5. **Error-to-absence coercion.** Timeout, permission error, truncation, and parse failure get distinct statuses rather than an empty-result status.
6. **Chronic overconfidence that passes individual gates.** Sampled outcomes expose a policy whose 0.95 claims hold only 0.7 of the time, even when every claim contains two nominal probes.
7. **Selective ritual compliance.** Hidden random audit creates evidence about ordinary cases, not just the cases a model or developer expects to be reviewed.
8. **Bad verifier health.** `ORACLE-FAILED` is measured separately, and every absence resolution itself needs a known-present control. The verifier is not axiomatically trusted.

Items 1–5 are **almost certain (90-99%)** to be detectable when their relevant fields are truthfully populated, because each is an explicit schema/gate predicate. Items 6–8 are **likely (55-80%)** to become visible over repeated samples; evidence is proper-score sensitivity, random sampling, and explicit oracle statuses, but the required incidence and sample size are **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1 settles them**.

## 5. Adversarial analysis and Goodhart resistance

### 5.1 Failure: the “ground truth” is just a third correlated probe

This is the largest risk. If producer and resolver use the same underlying source, a proper score faithfully rewards agreement with a broken oracle. Mechanism design does not solve oracle construction. Mitigation is provenance-based channel separation, positive controls, resolver failure states, and periodic human adjudication of a blind sample.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that oracle common-mode failure would erase much of the mechanism's value; evidence is that proper scores guarantee truth-telling relative to the resolved outcome, not correspondence between that outcome and reality ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).

### 5.2 Failure: optimize the evidence count

If the target metric is “two probes,” agents can duplicate one source under two names, choose weak controls, or narrow claims after seeing results. This is adversarial Goodhart: optimizing a proxy breaks its relationship with the goal ([Manheim & Garrabrant, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Mitigations are typed channel provenance, post-commit sampling, outcome scoring, and held-out resolver audits. Never reward probe count directly.

### 5.3 Failure: safe 0.5 everywhere

The Brier-optimal strategy for a policy with no information may be honest indecision. That is desirable elicitation but useless execution. Do not corrupt the scoring rule to force confidence. Instead, measure sharpness/coverage and apply a decision rule: unresolved high-impact claims escalate, gather more evidence, or stop the mutation. Calibration subject to sharpness is established forecast-evaluation practice ([Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x)).

### 5.4 Failure: audit gaming

If sampling is predictable, a policy can spend effort only on likely audits. Sample after commitment with a hidden seed; maintain a random floor; rotate canary tasks; and keep a held-out audit stream. It is **likely (55-80%)** that these measures deter simple selective compliance, based on the report-independent random-audit structure in Gao et al. ([Gao et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)). Resistance to an adaptive policy with side-channel access to the sampler is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E3, the audit-prediction red team in §6, settles it**.

### 5.5 Failure: score selection causes distribution shift

Policies promoted on yesterday's claim mix may fail on novel tools or environments—an extremal/regressional Goodhart risk ([Manheim & Garrabrant, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Stratify by claim class, retain novelty sampling, use time-split holdouts, and report uncertainty intervals. Do not authorize autonomous high-impact mutations from a global average score.

### 5.6 Failure: the model is not strategic

If a model does not alter evidence gathering in response to the evaluation regime, spot-check incentives do no causal work. The audit still supplies measurement, and the deterministic gate still blocks malformed claims. This is why the design remains useful even if the economic-agent analogy fails at runtime, but the claimed incentive effect must remain UNVERIFIED until E1.

## 6. Falsification experiments

### E1 — preregistered replay plus shadow trial (settles primary UNVERIFIED premise)

Build a benchmark of consequential claims with adjudicated outcomes, including single-probe empties, detector errors, genuine absences, genuine presences, and ambiguous/unresolvable cases. Before running, preregister:

- baseline policy and mechanism-conditioned policy;
- claim classes and impact tiers;
- independence taxonomy;
- resolver and oracle-failure rules;
- primary endpoint: confidently wrong consequential claims per 100 resolvable claims;
- secondary endpoints: Brier loss, calibration error, sharpness, task completion, added tool calls, latency, false blocks, and owner corrections;
- stopping rule and minimum sample size.

Run two stages. First, replay the fixed corpus of tasks to test detection without affecting behavior. Second, shadow-randomize live eligible tasks between baseline and claim-contract/gate conditions, while keeping any routing consequence disabled. Use an adjudicator blind to condition. The detector needs two mechanically independent validation signals: (a) performance on seeded known-bad and known-good claims, proving it can find present defects and avoid false positives; and (b) blind adjudication of naturally occurring claims. Re-running the same seeded suite is not the second signal.

Success requires lower confidently wrong claim rate without an unacceptable fall in task completion or explosion in verification cost. If probability reports do not improve calibration, retain the gate and audit ledger but reject the “incentive-compatible elicitation” interpretation.

### E2 — common-mode mutation suite

Inject paired probes that look different textually but share a backend, cache, parser, permission boundary, or source snapshot. Also include genuinely independent pairs. Measure precision and recall of the channel-independence taxonomy. A known-present control validates that the mutation detector can recognize at least one seeded shared backend before any claim of “no common-mode dependence” is allowed.

### E3 — audit-prediction red team

Give an adversarial policy all public sampling rules and task metadata but not the secret seed. Ask it to minimize effort while maximizing audited score. Compare effort/accuracy between audited and unaudited claims using a separate hidden human-resolved sample. If the gap is material, revise sampling features or abandon score-linked selection. A second independent signal is canary leakage testing: seed metadata correlated with audits in a sandbox and prove the monitor detects that known leak before trusting a negative leakage result.

### E4 — oracle disagreement audit

For a random subset of resolved claims, obtain two adjudications from mechanically distinct sources, one of which is human where feasible. Estimate resolver disagreement and condition scores on resolver version. If resolver disagreement dominates policy differences, do not use the ledger for routing.

## 7. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

Every proposal rides the existing configuration/hook/skill/tool bus. The design adds a claim schema, a small admission hook, a sampler, and an append-only score record. It does **not** propose a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. A flat JSONL or existing run-event store is sufficient for the experiment; analysis can be batch/offline.

This passes the anti-overfit gate because reliability enforcement remains local and syntactic at decision time, while learning is a thin evaluation loop over existing policies. If E1 does not show meaningful benefit, remove the scoring/selection layer and retain only the deterministic two-signal gate. No architectural sunk cost is needed.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Majority vote among agents — reject

Agreement is not truth under shared failures. Peer-prediction research demonstrates that cheap correlated signals can support uninformative equilibria ([Gao et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)). Majority vote may be useful only after explicit channel diversification; it is not a verifier.

### Bayesian truth serum — reject for objective reliability claims

Truth serum is designed for subjective data whose objective truth is unknowable ([Prelec, 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15486294/)). Filesystem/process/configuration claims are often objectively resolvable. Replacing ground truth with population surprise would throw away the stronger signal and could reward unusual errors.

### Prediction market — reject as unnecessary architecture

Multiple persistent utility-bearing traders and a settlement oracle would add machinery without resolving oracle construction. Proper scoring plus sampled resolution supplies the relevant elicitation property with less structure. A market would also approach the explicitly rejected second-runtime pattern.

### Reward “number of checks” — reject

This directly Goodharts the proxy and encourages duplicated or ceremonial probes. Score resolved truth and validate channel independence instead ([Manheim & Garrabrant, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)).

### Audit every claim — reject as the mechanism-design borrow

Exhaustive checking can be appropriate for a small critical surface, but it eliminates the costly-verification optimization problem rather than solving it. The spot-check mechanism is valuable where full resolution is too expensive ([Ben-Porath, Dekel & Lipman, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.12.3779)). High-impact mutation gates may still require universal synchronous evidence checks.

### Proper score alone — reject

A score without independent settlement is meaningless, and a score without a consequence is only telemetry. The complete mechanism needs commitment, independent sampled resolution, proper scoring, and policy-level selection.

> commit a probabilistic claim; secretly sample it for independent ground-truth resolution; score resolved claims with a strictly proper rule; and apply the score to repeated policy selection.

Pair it with a deterministic, pre-output two-independent-signals gate for consequential negative/broken/done claims. Reject peer agreement as ground truth. Keep accuracy, calibration, sharpness, completion, and verification cost separate. Run E1 in shadow mode before scores affect routing.

The recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce the target failure while preserving a minimal harness, based on the direct costly-verification mapping and the proper-scoring result.
