# X147 — Cross-domain borrow: cartography → synthesis

## Executive result

**Verdict: the analogy transfers, but only at the level of representation architecture and quality constraints—not as an automatic rule for deciding what is true or important.** The specific cartographic mechanism worth borrowing is **constraint-checked, multi-scale generalisation from a single detailed source model, with linked representations that support logical zoom from overview to evidence**. Cartography does not merely shorten a detailed map. It creates a representation for a stated purpose and scale by applying distinct operators—selection, classification, aggregation, simplification, typification, displacement, and exaggeration—while trying to preserve semantic, structural, and topological properties. Monika Sester describes the intended result as linked abstraction levels that permit “coarse-to-fine” movement, quick comprehension, and processing at the level of detail appropriate to the task; she also stresses that choosing and sequencing operators remains difficult to automate ([Sester, *Cartographic Generalization*, Journal of Spatial Information Science](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/127)). The International Cartographic Association likewise defines multi-scale cartography as automated creation and display at multiple levels of detail for different tasks, environments, and devices ([ICA Commission on Multi-Scale Cartography](https://generalisation.icaci.org/)).

Translated into the harness, this becomes an **evidence-preserving Decision Atlas**: one normalized, append-only finding ledger is compiled into several explicitly different scales—a one-screen decision map, issue-level maps, and evidence-level cards. Every generalized assertion retains links to its source findings; every omission is counted and classified; every merge exposes its membership; and each scale is checked against both readability constraints and preservation constraints. The overview is therefore an index into evidence, not a replacement for evidence.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce important-finding loss and duplicate-driven salience in large synthesis jobs, because linked levels, explicit generalisation operators, and preservation checks address those failure modes directly; the evidence is cartography’s established multi-scale practice and constraint literature, not a harness trial. **UNVERIFIED — the settling experiment is a blinded A/B evaluation on at least three completed 500+ finding syntheses, comparing a conventional long-summary pipeline with the proposed compiler on decision recall, unsupported-claim rate, duplicate-weighting errors, contradiction recall, time-to-decision, and token consumption.** The recommendation below inherits that unverified premise.

The proposal passes the anti-overfit gate. It is a small compiler/report skill on the existing skill/hook bus. It is not another orchestration runtime, memory daemon, vector database, knowledge graph, trained critic, durable-execution engine, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. The mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 A map at a smaller scale is a different representation, not a shrunken copy

Cartographic scale creates a hard representation budget. A smaller-scale map covers more territory in the same display area, so preserving every object and every geometric detail would make the map illegible. Generalisation deliberately changes the representation to suit the target scale and purpose. Sester states that generalisation operators include simplification, aggregation, classification, and displacement; important objects are preserved or enhanced while application-specific irrelevant information is ignored ([Sester, pp. 6–7](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/127)). The ICA’s current commission describes the same problem as producing geographic information at multiple levels of detail for tasks ranging from planning to phone navigation ([ICA Commission](https://generalisation.icaci.org/)).

The “purpose” clause matters. Swisstopo publishes a series rather than one universal map: its 1:10,000 product emphasizes detailed traffic, settlements, terrain, and vegetation, while 1:500,000 is an overview that retains the complete railway network and major roads ([Swiss Federal Office of Topography, National Maps](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/national-maps)). The facts in the territory have not changed; the selection appropriate to the user’s task and scale has.

This provides the first useful synthesis principle: **there is no context-free best summary length**. A decision map, a thematic diagnosis, and an audit record are different products derived from the same evidence, not successively more verbose versions of one prose summary.

### 1.2 The specific operator vocabulary

The operator vocabulary prevents “summarization” from being an opaque single action. Roth, Brewer, and Stryker’s ScaleMaster typology separates operations including class selection, reclassification, collapse, combination, simplification, amalgamation, enhancement, displacement, elimination, and typification ([Roth et al., “A typology of operators for maintaining legible map designs at multiple scales”](https://cartographicperspectives.org/index.php/journal/article/view/cp68-roth-et-al)). These operations do different kinds of representational work:

1. **Selection/elimination** decides which features appear at a target scale.
2. **Classification/reclassification** changes categorical granularity.
3. **Aggregation/combination/amalgamation** replaces several related objects with a group object.
4. **Simplification** removes internal geometric detail while trying to retain characteristic shape. Douglas and Peucker’s classic line-reduction work explicitly starts from the observation that digitisation records more points than accurate reproduction or analysis needs and presents algorithms to reduce them ([Douglas and Peucker, 1973, DOI 10.3138/FM57-6770-U75U-7727](https://doi.org/10.3138/FM57-6770-U75U-7727)).
5. **Typification** retains representative instances and the pattern of a collection rather than drawing every member.
6. **Displacement** moves objects to resolve visual conflict while attempting to preserve relative relationships.
7. **Enhancement/exaggeration** increases the visibility of a feature that would otherwise disappear at the target scale.

The important borrow is the *separation* of these operators. A prose summarizer can silently perform all seven in one generation: drop a finding, broaden a category, fuse claims, paraphrase detail, choose one example, move a caveat to the end, and emphasize a striking result. Once those transformations are named and logged, each can receive a different validity test.

### 1.3 Multiple representations remain linked to a detailed source model

Multi-representation databases address the maintenance problem created by scale-specific products. Çobankaya describes 1:25K through 1:500K representation levels, object matching between representations, and the goal of propagating updates from the master landscape model to lower-resolution models ([Çobankaya, International Cartographic Conference 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/530.html)). Sester states that abstraction levels must be linked so a user or process can move from overview to detail and back ([Sester](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/127)). Swisstopo’s production account similarly reports that its 1:25K–1:100K national maps are based on a 1:10K Topographic Landscape Model and that model and cartographic generalisation procedures prepare the base data for the required scale ([Hurni, “How do the Swiss produce their New National Map Series?”](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/1/402/2019/)).

The operative pattern is therefore:

```text
detailed source model
      │
      ├── generalisation specification for scale/purpose A ──> representation A
      ├── generalisation specification for scale/purpose B ──> representation B
      └── generalisation specification for scale/purpose C ──> representation C

Every derived object has a correspondence back to source object(s).
```

It is not:

```text
12 MB → summary → shorter summary → executive summary
```

The latter is serial lossy compression: once an intermediate summary omits or distorts an item, downstream summaries cannot recover it. The former re-derives every scale from the evidence ledger and keeps correspondence links.

### 1.4 Generalisation is constrained compromise, not lossless compression

Cartographic evaluation recognizes conflicting objectives. Harrie’s optimization work describes legibility constraints that require change and characteristic/position constraints that seek preservation; because goals compete, the result is necessarily a compromise ([Harrie, *An Optimisation Approach to Cartographic Generalisation*, Lund University](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/an-optimisation-approach-to-cartographic-generalisation/)). Automated-evaluation research recommends pairing a readability constraint with a corresponding preservation constraint, checking that required entities still exist, and evaluating group or feature-class preservation so deletion cannot produce an over-optimistic score ([Zhang, *Automated evaluation of generalized topographic maps*, University of Twente](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/automated-evaluation-of-generalized-topographic-maps/)).

That paired-constraint idea is the most important guard against a polished but deceptive synthesis:

| Cartographic objective | Synthesis translation |
|---|---|
| Legibility: reduce symbol collision and density | Fit the target decision budget; remove repetition; use stable categories |
| Entity preservation: required features must remain represented | All critical/blocking/high-impact findings must appear or be explicitly accounted for |
| Structural preservation: retain patterns and relative relationships | Preserve causal chains, dependency edges, disagreement, and theme distribution |
| Topological preservation: retain connectivity/containment/adjacency | Preserve “A blocks B,” “claim contradicts claim,” “evidence supports claim,” and scope containment |
| Position constraint | Do not detach caveats, uncertainty, or counterevidence from the claim they qualify |
| Scale/purpose specification | Compile a view for a declared decision and audience, not a generic summary |

Pure compression optimizes readability. Cartographic generalisation explicitly balances readability against preservation. That is the transferable mechanism.

## 2. Concrete translation: an evidence-preserving Decision Atlas

### 2.1 Proposal [UNVERIFIED — settle with the blinded A/B experiment specified in the executive result]

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Build a deterministic `decision-atlas` compiler skill, invoked after research units produce finding records and before the final synthesizer writes recommendations. The compiler should use ordinary files and Markdown/JSON outputs. It should not own a queue, schedule agents, retain hidden memory, or become a daemon.

The under-200-line estimate is **roughly even (45-55%)**, based on the deliberately narrow scope—schema validation, grouping, counts, link emission, and invariant checks—but it is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by implementing a spike against one real finding set and counting non-test source lines after format and schema choices are fixed**. If it exceeds the boundary, keep the mechanism on the existing bus and split deterministic validation from model-assisted prose; do not promote it into a new runtime.

### 2.2 Source model: the “territory” remains findings, not prose

Each research unit emits one or more newline-delimited JSON finding records. A minimal record is:

```json
{
  "id": "stable-unit-local-id",
  "claim": "one falsifiable sentence",
  "theme": "controlled taxonomy path",
  "decision": ["decision-id"],
  "kind": "observation|defect|proposal|counterevidence|gap",
  "severity": "critical|high|medium|low",
  "confidence_band": "likely (55-80%)",
  "evidence": [{"locator": "URL-or-path:line", "signal_family": "documentation"}],
  "independence_group": "shared-root-cause-or-source-id",
  "supports": ["claim-id"],
  "contradicts": ["claim-id"],
  "blocks": ["decision-id-or-action-id"],
  "scope": ["component-or-workflow"],
  "owner_correction": false,
  "status": "verified|unverified|disputed"
}
```

The ledger is not a knowledge graph service. The few arrays are explicit cross-references in flat records; a script can check them with maps/sets in memory. This distinction is important to the anti-overfit gate.

The schema forces a difference between (a) how many units repeat a claim, (b) how many mechanically independent evidence signals support it, and (c) how severe it would be if true. It also makes gaps and counterevidence first-class rather than leaving them buried in narrative.

### 2.3 Three scales, all freshly derived from the ledger

#### Scale S0 — decision map (one screen or approximately 800–1,200 words)

Purpose: permit the owner to choose a direction without reading the full corpus.

Required content:

- the decision question and options;
- the recommended option and its confidence band;
- no more than seven decisive reasons, selected by decision relevance × impact × evidence independence;
- the strongest counterargument and any disputed premise;
- critical blockers and irreversible risks;
- coverage legend: total source records, represented directly, aggregated, typified, and omitted at S0;
- links to S1 issue cards for every assertion.

No S0 assertion may be free prose without member IDs. The model may write connective language, but each bullet carries a machine-generated provenance footer such as `from F12,F18,F40; 2 independent signal families; 1 counterfinding → C07`.

#### Scale S1 — issue maps (one card per decision-relevant theme)

Purpose: let the decision-maker inspect why an S0 statement exists.

Each card contains:

- exact synthesized claim;
- member findings and independence groups;
- evidence for and against;
- causal/dependency chain;
- affected scopes;
- alternative interpretations;
- omitted-member count and reason;
- links to S2 records.

Themes with high internal disagreement must not be merged into a single consensus statement. They render as a split card.

#### Scale S2 — evidence atlas (all normalized records)

Purpose: auditability and recovery.

This scale includes every accepted record, validation errors, orphan records not selected into any S1 card, and backlinks from each record to the S1/S0 objects that use it. It does not reproduce the 12 MB verbatim; its evidence locators point to the underlying artifacts.

A fourth “source” click can open the original artifact. That is not a further generalized scale; it is the territory reference.

### 2.4 Translation of cartographic operators into compiler operations

| Operator | Harness operation | Required audit trace | Failure it exposes or prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selection | Choose records relevant to a declared decision at S0/S1 | selected ID, rejected IDs, reason code, selection score components | A high-impact minority finding vanishing because it is infrequent |
| Classification | Map unit-specific labels into a controlled theme tree | old label → new label, taxonomy version | Synonyms fragmenting one defect; broad categories hiding distinct mechanisms |
| Aggregation | Create one issue from genuinely equivalent findings | member IDs, equivalence key, evidence-independence count | Repeated claims being counted as independent proof; many symptoms masquerading as many root causes |
| Simplification | Shorten a claim without changing predicates, scope, polarity, modality, or confidence | source proposition fields and output fields; changed-field diff | “May fail under X” becoming “fails”; scope or qualifier loss |
| Typification | Show representative examples while retaining population count, range, and outliers | representative-selection rule, hidden member count, min/max severity, outlier IDs | A pleasant example making a heterogeneous cluster look uniform |
| Displacement | Move a decisive caveat or blocker into the limited S0 visual budget | original theme, displayed position, reason | Important but low-frequency evidence remaining “in the middle” of a long report |
| Enhancement/exaggeration | Promote critical, irreversible, owner-corrected, or independently verified exceptions | promotion rule and original frequency | Severity being confused with prevalence |
| Collapse | Represent a dense collection as a named issue marker | member count and expandable membership | Token exhaustion caused by listing every near-duplicate |

One operator should be forbidden: **unlogged elimination**. A record can be absent from S0 or S1, but it must remain at S2 with a reason such as `not decision-relevant`, `duplicate of`, `insufficient evidence`, or `out of scope`. This converts “the summary did not mention it” from an unknowable state into a queryable decision.

### 2.5 Constraint checks before the decision map is accepted

The compiler should fail closed on structural errors, and report softer quality warnings separately.

**Hard preservation invariants**

1. Every source record is present at S2 exactly once.
2. Every S0 claim links to at least one S1 card; every S1 claim links to at least one S2 finding.
3. Every critical blocker appears at S0 or has an explicit, machine-readable waiver.
4. Every `disputed` cluster renders counterevidence adjacent to the synthesized claim.
5. Every `UNVERIFIED` premise retains the settling experiment in the same card.
6. Every aggregation exposes all member IDs and reports independent-signal count separately from record count.
7. Every contradiction edge has both endpoints; neither endpoint may be silently eliminated from the issue card.
8. Every recommendation exposes the premises on which it depends and their lowest confidence band.
9. No confidence band is strengthened by simplification or aggregation unless new independent evidence is added and the trace records why.
10. Coverage arithmetic balances: `direct + aggregated + typified + omitted = total eligible` for each scale and theme.

**Readability constraints**

1. S0 respects its word/item budget.
2. Duplicate semantic claims do not occupy multiple S0 slots.
3. Each bullet expresses one decision reason.
4. Each technical label has one stable name.
5. Provenance is compact at S0 and expanded at S1/S2.

**Balanced evaluation**

Passing only readability constraints would reward aggressive deletion. Passing only preservation constraints would recreate the 12 MB input. Following Zhang’s cartographic guidance, every readability metric should be paired with a preservation metric—for example, S0 token count paired with critical-finding recall, or duplicate reduction paired with minority-counterevidence recall ([Zhang](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/automated-evaluation-of-generalized-topographic-maps/)).

### 2.6 Model work versus deterministic work

Use a model only where semantic judgment is unavoidable:

- propose equivalence clusters;
- suggest theme classification;
- draft scale-specific claims;
- identify potential contradictions and dependencies.

Use deterministic code for:

- schema and confidence-band validation;
- referential integrity;
- counts and coverage arithmetic;
- propagation of critical/disputed/unverified flags;
- preservation of source strings and evidence locators;
- word/item budgets;
- rendering links and audit traces.

The model never deletes a record. It proposes an operator application, and the compiler records and validates it. This division is **very likely (80-90%)** to make omissions more diagnosable than an end-to-end prose synthesis, because selection and membership become enumerable file fields rather than latent generation choices; the evidence is the mechanical inspectability of the proposed format. Its effect on final decision accuracy remains **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the blinded A/B experiment specified above**.

### 2.7 Hook/skill placement

The mechanism should ride the existing bus in two places:

1. A research-unit output skill supplies the finding-record template and validates required fields on unit completion.
2. A synthesis skill compiles S0–S2, runs invariants, and hands the S0 map plus requested S1 cards to the final decision-writing agent.

An optional completion hook can reject a synthesis artifact when hard invariants fail. This is **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** if the existing hook bus can invoke a local validator at the relevant completion event; that availability is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by enumerating supported hook events and running a known-present canary validator on the target installation**. If no relevant native event exists, invoking validation as the final step of the synthesis skill remains **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

No model should receive all S2 records by default. It receives the S0 map and the decision-relevant S1 cards, then follows stable IDs to S2 only when a claim is disputed, weakly supported, or material to the decision. This is logical zoom, not a second retrieval system.

## 3. What this catches that flat synthesis tends to miss

The assigned problem states that thousands of findings must become a decision without an agent reading approximately 12 MB. The following are targeted failure modes, not claims that each has been measured in the local harness; this unit intentionally did not inspect the subject tree.

### 3.1 Duplicate density masquerading as evidential strength

Twenty units may repeat one upstream audit claim, while one unit supplies two independent operational signals against it. A prose synthesis sees 20 mentions versus one. The atlas aggregates by equivalence and displays both `record_count=20` and `independence_groups=1`; the counterfinding remains a split card. The compiler therefore catches correlated repetition before it becomes a vote.

This detection is **very likely (80-90%)** if independence groups are populated correctly, because the comparison is deterministic. Correct population is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by labeling a stratified sample of 200 findings with two human reviewers and measuring agreement and duplicate-cluster precision/recall**.

### 3.2 Severe outliers erased by majority patterns

Ordinary aggregation favors the mode. Cartographic enhancement preserves features that would otherwise vanish at scale. The synthesis translation promotes critical, irreversible, or blocking outliers while showing that they are rare. This is not permission to inflate confidence; visual/positional salience and evidential confidence remain separate fields.

The design is **likely (55-80%)** to improve recall of rare blockers, because the hard invariant explicitly checks their S0 representation; **UNVERIFIED — settle it by seeding known rare blockers into historical finding sets and measuring blinded reviewer recall and false promotion rate**.

### 3.3 Scope, polarity, or modality dropped during paraphrase

“Tool X may fail on Windows when condition Y holds” can become “Tool X is broken.” The simplification trace decomposes the proposition into subject, predicate, polarity, modality, scope, and confidence; a changed-field diff flags semantic movement. This is analogous to retaining characteristic shape, not retaining every vertex.

Automated detection of all semantic drift is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because natural-language proposition parsing is itself fallible; evidence is the semantic rather than geometric nature of findings. **UNVERIFIED — settle it with a mutation suite that systematically deletes or alters scope, negation, modality, quantities, and confidence in 300 real claims, then measure validator recall and precision.**

### 3.4 Contradictions collapsed into false consensus

A theme cluster containing `supports` and `contradicts` edges cannot render as a single unqualified S1 statement. The invariant requires both endpoints and adjacency of counterevidence. A flat summary has no analogous referential-integrity check.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent *known, correctly linked* contradictions from disappearing, because the render rule is deterministic. Discovering previously unlinked contradictions is **likely (55-80%)**, based on model-assisted semantic matching but no harness result; **UNVERIFIED — settle it using a human-labeled contradiction set with hard cases involving scope and time.**

### 3.5 Orphan evidence and invisible omission

The coverage equation exposes findings that reached S2 but no issue or decision card. Some orphans will be legitimately irrelevant; others reveal taxonomy failure or an uncomfortable minority finding. Either way, omission becomes visible.

The compiler is **almost certain (90-99%)** to enumerate syntactic orphans if all unit records enter the ledger, because referential coverage is a finite set comparison. The premise that all records enter the ledger is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with dual controls: compare unit-completion manifests with ledger unit IDs, and compare artifact-listed finding IDs with parsed ledger IDs, after proving both detectors on a known-present canary record.**

### 3.6 A stale overview after evidence changes

Because every scale is re-derived from the detailed ledger rather than summarized from the previous scale, a changed finding invalidates and rebuilds affected cards. Stable membership links reveal which S0/S1 objects depend on it. This borrows incremental multi-representation maintenance without building a durable workflow engine.

Selective rebuilds are **likely (55-80%)** to reduce stale-summary mismatches if dependency links are complete; **UNVERIFIED — settle it by mutating findings across severity, confidence, contradiction, and category fields and checking whether every affected card changes while unrelated cards remain byte-stable.** A simpler first version can rebuild the entire Markdown atlas, which is safer and still small.

## 4. Adversarial transfer test

### 4.1 Structural correspondences that do transfer

1. **Bounded display/context:** both fields must represent more detail than the immediate medium can carry.
2. **Purpose-dependent relevance:** a hiking map and a road overview legitimately retain different features; an implementation decision and a reliability decision legitimately retain different findings.
3. **Multiple useful levels:** overview and detail are both necessary, and links permit coarse-to-fine inspection. Sester explicitly identifies linked abstraction levels and logical zoom as essential ([Sester](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/127)).
4. **Preservation versus readability tension:** both become useless at one extreme—everything retained or too much deleted.
5. **Many-to-one representation:** several buildings can become an urban-area symbol; several symptoms can become one root-cause issue, provided membership remains inspectable.
6. **Danger of local correctness with global distortion:** individually reasonable omissions can erase a network, pattern, or minority class. Group/class preservation checks exist in cartographic evaluation for this reason ([Zhang](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/automated-evaluation-of-generalized-topographic-maps/)).

These correspondences make the representation architecture a legitimate borrow.

### 4.2 Disanalogies that prevent a literal transfer

#### Geography has metric and topological structure; findings do not arrive with one

Road connectivity and polygon containment are mathematically defined. Semantic dependence, causal relation, and “same defect” in research findings are inferred and contestable. A line simplifier can measure perpendicular distance; a synthesis compiler cannot compute a universally meaningful distance between claims.

**Consequence:** do not borrow Douglas–Peucker as a claim-selection algorithm, embedding distance as truth, or spatial clustering as evidence. Borrow the idea of explicit simplification plus invariant checks. Human/model judgment proposes semantic relations; deterministic code verifies only the relations that have been declared.

#### A map represents a world; a decision synthesis argues about a world

Findings include proposals, uncertain forecasts, value judgments, and conflicting causal theories. They are not homogeneous observed features. An atlas must preserve epistemic type (`observation`, `proposal`, `counterevidence`, `gap`) and must not aggregate across types merely because wording is similar.

**Consequence:** the mechanism organizes evidence but cannot choose the owner’s utility function or prove a recommendation. The decision remains a reasoned act above the atlas.

#### Omission has a higher action cost

A user who notices ambiguity on an interactive map can zoom. An agent may execute from S0 without noticing that it ought to zoom. Cartography’s ability to relax presentation requirements when users can zoom is explicitly noted by Sester ([Sester](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/127)); that assumption is unsafe for autonomous action.

**Consequence:** critical/blocking/disputed/unverified items require forced promotion and hard invariants. “Available at S2” is insufficient for an action-critical fact.

#### Salience is not importance

Large roads and cities often deserve visual prominence because of map purpose and spatial role. A frequent or rhetorically vivid finding is not necessarily decision-important. Importance depends on consequences, reversibility, evidence, owner preferences, and the current decision.

**Consequence:** never rank only by frequency, text similarity, or model attention. Expose separate dimensions and require the decision specification to supply weights or rules.

#### Source independence has no simple cartographic analogue

Two roads drawn twice are duplicate geometry; two reports making the same claim might be independent corroboration or copied from one source. Provenance dependence must be modeled explicitly.

**Consequence:** `record_count` and `independence_group_count` must never be conflated.

#### Automated generalisation itself is not solved end-to-end

Sester reports satisfactory algorithms for many individual operators but emphasizes that analyzing a situation and choosing adequate operators and sequence requires experience and creativity and has been hard to automate ([Sester](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/127)). The analogy therefore cannot support the claim “cartography solved automatic synthesis.” It supports a vocabulary, architecture, and evaluation discipline.

### 4.3 Frontier check: this transfer has already been proposed, but not validated

Wu and Gartner’s March 2026 preprint, *Context Cartography*, explicitly proposes reconnaissance, selection, simplification, aggregation, projection, displacement, and layering for LLM context governance ([arXiv:2603.20578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20578)). This is important prior art: the broad analogy is no longer novel. The paper describes interpretive evidence across four systems and proposes ablations and a diagnostic benchmark; it also explicitly acknowledges that geographic space is continuous and low-dimensional while contextual space is sequential and high-dimensional, and says transfer across that gap needs further investigation.

Accordingly, that paper is **UNVERIFIED as evidence of effectiveness — settle it by running its proposed operator-specific ablations or the Decision Atlas A/B benchmark in this artifact**. It increases confidence that the operator vocabulary is coherent, but it does not establish that this concrete harness design improves decisions. The useful novelty here is narrower: multi-scale derivation from a flat evidence ledger, paired preservation/readability constraints, explicit independence counts, and coverage arithmetic, all deployable without a new memory or orchestration substrate.

## 5. Minimal experiment and rejection criteria

### 5.1 Experiment design

Use at least three historical synthesis tasks with 500+ findings and known final decisions. Freeze the underlying artifacts. Have two independent reviewers create a reference set for:

- decision-critical findings;
- true duplicate/equivalence clusters;
- mechanically independent support groups;
- contradictions;
- blockers and irreversible risks;
- unsupported claims in the historical final synthesis.

Randomly assign blinded decision reviewers to:

- **Control:** the current conventional synthesis artifact under the same initial reading budget;
- **Treatment:** S0 Decision Map, with on-demand S1/S2 logical zoom and the same initial reading budget.

Measure:

1. critical-finding recall;
2. contradiction recall;
3. unsupported conclusion rate;
4. duplicate-weighting errors;
5. calibration of confidence bands;
6. decision agreement with a post-hoc full-evidence panel—not merely agreement with the historical decision;
7. time and tokens to a stated decision;
8. number and depth of zooms;
9. false promotion rate for rare but irrelevant findings;
10. coverage/invariant failures detected before reviewer exposure.

Run ablations: remove correspondence links, remove independence groups, remove enhancement of blockers, and remove paired preservation checks. This distinguishes whether the cartographic mechanism adds value or whether formatting alone does.

### 5.2 Success threshold

Adopt only if treatment improves critical-finding and contradiction recall without increasing unsupported conclusions, and either reduces time/tokens or holds them roughly constant. A reasonable provisional gate is +15 percentage points critical-finding recall, no more than +2 points unsupported-claim rate, and no more than 10% additional decision time. Those numeric thresholds are **UNVERIFIED policy choices — settle them with the owner’s risk tolerance before running the benchmark**.

### 5.3 Kill criteria

Reject or substantially redesign the borrow if any of the following occurs:

- reviewers act from S0 without zooming and miss more critical facts than control;
- independence grouping is too unreliable to beat raw frequency;
- provenance footers create enough visual load to erase the readability gain;
- maintaining finding records costs more than the reading it saves;
- the compiler’s model-assisted clustering introduces systematic viewpoint suppression;
- decisions vary more with taxonomy choices than with evidence changes;
- implementation pressure turns the compiler into a second orchestrator, durable runtime, vector/KG memory system, trained critic, SaaS telemetry layer, or guardrail framework.

The chance that at least one substantial redesign is needed after the first trial is **likely (55-80%)**, because semantic clustering, evidence independence, and decision relevance lack cartography’s metric geometry; the evidence is the disanalogy above and Sester’s account of the difficulty of operator selection. That is a reason to run a small compiler experiment, not a reason to create a larger platform.

## 6. Alternatives considered and dead ends

### 6.1 “Just make a hierarchy of summaries” — reject

Serial summaries are not multiple representations. They propagate prior omissions and supply no source-object correspondence or coverage arithmetic. They borrow the visual appearance of zoom without cartography’s source model and constraints.

### 6.2 Douglas–Peucker over embeddings — reject

The classic algorithm is elegant because a polyline has ordered points and geometric deviation. Findings have no trustworthy total order or metric semantic distance. Embedding distance also cannot distinguish copied corroboration from independent corroboration, or low-frequency catastrophe from noise. This is the most tempting forced analogy and should not be built.

### 6.3 A vector database or knowledge graph “map of all findings” — reject under the anti-overfit gate

The necessary links fit in flat JSON records, and the synthesis run is bounded. A database/service would introduce another persistent subsystem without evidence that indexed semantic retrieval is the bottleneck. It would also make coverage harder to audit than a finite manifest plus set comparisons. If future evidence shows ledgers no longer fit comfortably in memory, a storage change can be evaluated then; it is not justified by this lens.

### 6.4 A trained cartographic critic — reject under the anti-overfit gate

There is no labeled harness dataset showing that a learned critic can recognize preservation failures better than explicit invariants. It would reintroduce a previously rejected component and hide evaluation in another model. Deterministic checks plus blinded human benchmarks are the appropriate first step.

### 6.5 One universal S0 map — reject

Cartography’s central lesson is purpose dependence. Compile separate S0 maps for distinct decisions, even when they share the same S2 ledger. A reliability decision, migration decision, and capability-expansion decision can legitimately select different issue cards. A universal executive summary would recreate the overloaded map.
