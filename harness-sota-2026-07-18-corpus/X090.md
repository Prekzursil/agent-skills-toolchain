# X090 — Cross-domain borrow: mechanism design → failure recovery

## Executive verdict

The broad analogy **does not transfer**: mechanism design has not solved how to reconstruct computation after a worker, session, or machine dies. That is a state-durability problem, and the relevant systems literature solves it with checkpoints and logs, not incentives. Elnozahy et al. classify rollback recovery into checkpoint-based and log-based protocols and explain that log-based protocols record the determinants of nondeterministic events; Chandy and Lamport show how to record a consistent global state in an asynchronous system ([Elnozahy et al., *A Survey of Rollback-Recovery Protocols in Message-Passing Systems*](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs614/2004sp/papers/EAWJ02.pdf); [Chandy and Lamport, *Distributed Snapshots*](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/areas/os/Qual/papers/snapshots.pdf)). No payment rule can recreate an unlogged tool result, an unflushed edit, or reasoning that died only in volatile context.

A narrower borrow **does transfer**: use a **costly-state-verification contract** to decide when a recovery claim deserves an expensive audit, and use a **strictly proper scoring rule** to evaluate the predecessor's probabilistic claims about what a successor will actually be able to verify. This is a mechanism for making recovery metadata more informative and for allocating verification effort—not a recovery substrate.

My bottom line is **likely (55-80%)** that this narrow mechanism would reduce wasted resume attempts and premature trust in stale checkpoints; the evidence is (a) the incentive-compatibility result for proper scores, (b) the costly-state-verification result that concentrates verification in exception states, and (c) empirical evidence that verbalized confidence from RLHF language models can be meaningfully calibrated on question-answering benchmarks. The range is deliberately wide because no cited experiment tests crash recovery in an agent harness ([Gneiting and Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437); [Townsend 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(79)90031-0); [Tian et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)). It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that this mechanism alone cannot recover state that was never durably recorded; the two mechanically independent signals are the checkpoint/log taxonomy and the distributed-snapshot construction in the two systems papers above.

## 1. The specific mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 Costly state verification: audit the exception, not every state

Robert Townsend's costly-state-verification model studies contracting when one party privately observes a realized outcome and the other can learn it only by paying a verification cost. The contract chooses both transfers and when verification occurs. Townsend's original paper formulates optimal contracts under costly verification ([Townsend 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(79)90031-0)). Gale and Hellwig's one-period model derives the familiar debt-like form under its assumptions: the borrower promises a fixed repayment; ordinary states do not need a full audit; failure to meet the promise triggers verification and a state-contingent recovery action ([Gale and Hellwig 1985, PDF](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d14b3bef315c03f6686229f644eb470d9f182aa2&repid=rep1&type=pdf)). A Federal Reserve summary independently states the result as an optimal incentive-compatible standard debt contract under costly state verification ([Federal Reserve, 2011](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2011/201104/index.html)).

The mechanism's useful shape is therefore:

1. Define an ex-ante, observable obligation rather than accepting an unconstrained narrative about private state.
2. Let the agent satisfy that obligation cheaply in ordinary states.
3. Treat inability to satisfy it as “default.”
4. On default, pay for an audit that reveals the state and determines the recovery transfer.

This is not “randomly inspect everything.” Its value is that the verification boundary is written before the private outcome is reported. The fixed obligation reduces the profitable report space, while costly verification is reserved for the branch where misreporting would otherwise matter. The statement is confined to the assumptions of the cited models; the literature itself examines how debt optimality changes when those assumptions change ([Townsend 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(79)90031-0); [Gale and Hellwig 1985](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d14b3bef315c03f6686229f644eb470d9f182aa2&repid=rep1&type=pdf)).

### 1.2 Strictly proper scoring: make honest probabilities optimal

A scoring rule pays a forecaster as a function of a reported distribution and the event that later occurs. It is *proper* when reporting the forecaster's actual belief maximizes expected score, and *strictly proper* when that maximizer is unique. Gneiting and Raftery give the general definition, representation results, and examples including logarithmic and quadratic/Brier scores ([Gneiting and Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437); [author-hosted PDF](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)).

For a binary event `y ∈ {0,1}`, one convenient higher-is-better quadratic score is:

`S(p,y) = -(p-y)^2`.

If the forecaster's belief is `q`, its expected score from reporting `p` is

`E[S] = -q(1-p)^2 - (1-q)p^2 = -q(1-q) - (p-q)^2`.

The unique optimum is `p=q`. Saying “100%” is attractive only when the forecaster really assigns probability one; otherwise the larger miss penalty dominates in expectation. This is the exact incentive-compatibility property being borrowed, not the vague practice of asking a model to append a confidence number.

The important limitations already exist inside the home field. Standard properness assumes the forecast does not change the outcome; Oesterheld et al. explicitly start from this limitation and design extensions for performative predictions ([Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a.html)). Risk-neutral scoring rules can distort reports from risk-averse forecasters ([Peysakhovich and Plagborg-Møller 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2012.05.030)). Winner-take-all selection can also destroy the incentive compatibility of simply ranking average proper scores ([Witkowski et al., *Incentive-Compatible Forecasting Competitions*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4410)). Those are direct warnings against treating “Brier-score the agents and choose the winner” as a theorem.

### 1.3 Goodhart and multitask incentives: why the obligation must not become the task

The recovery contract will itself become a target. Manheim and Garrabrant distinguish multiple mechanisms by which optimizing a proxy stops improving the underlying goal; the importance increases with optimization pressure ([Manheim and Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Holmström and Milgrom's multitask principal-agent analysis shows why strong incentives on well-measured activities can divert effort from poorly measured ones ([Holmström and Milgrom 1991](https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24); [OUP PDF metadata and link](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1363107368239517824?lang=en)).

Translated back into the home-field logic: rewarding “manifest emitted,” “all boxes checked,” or “confidence above 0.9” invites effort toward the manifest, boxes, or number. Proper scoring avoids rewarding *high* confidence, but it does not make the chosen recovery predicates complete. That is why the proposed mechanism scores eventual verification outcomes, uses multiple task-relevant predicates, and keeps the actual recovery success criterion outside the predecessor's control.

## 2. Concrete translation: a recovery covenant with default-triggered audit

### Proposal A — recovery covenant and audit ladder — UNVERIFIED: settle with the crash-injection experiment in §6

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

At each naturally durable boundary—successful write/edit, external side effect, test completion, subtask completion, and orderly stop—a hook appends one small **recovery covenant** to stable storage. The covenant is not a prose diary. It is a fixed, versioned record of claims a successor can test:

```json
{
  "task_id": "...",
  "sequence": 17,
  "predecessor": {"session": "...", "model": "...", "policy": "..."},
  "durable_anchor": {"kind": "git-or-content", "id": "..."},
  "milestone": "parser accepts escaped Windows paths",
  "next_action": "run focused regression, then inspect stderr branch",
  "artifacts": [
    {"uri": "...", "digest": "sha256:...", "role": "modified-source"}
  ],
  "external_effects": [
    {"system": "...", "idempotency_key": "...", "receipt": "..."}
  ],
  "predicates": [
    {"id": "artifact-digests-match", "p": 0.98},
    {"id": "focused-test-passes", "p": 0.82},
    {"id": "no-unrecorded-external-effect", "p": 0.90},
    {"id": "successor-can-continue-without-reconstruction", "p": 0.75}
  ],
  "known_unknowns": ["full suite not run"],
  "previous_record_digest": "sha256:..."
}
```

The append record should be written atomically and chained to its predecessor. That recommendation comes from recovery engineering rather than mechanism design: checkpoint/log protocols require recovery information to survive the failure being recovered from ([Elnozahy et al. 2002](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs614/2004sp/papers/EAWJ02.pdf)). It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a record kept only in the dying conversation would fail the central durability requirement; evidence is the systems distinction between volatile process state and checkpoints/logs on stable storage. “Very likely,” rather than “almost certain,” allows platforms that already persist full conversation and tool state transactionally.

On resume, a small validator applies the costly-state-verification pattern:

1. **Cheap covenant payment.** Verify schema/version, chain continuity, artifact existence and hashes, durable anchor, and receipts/idempotency keys. Run only the narrow predicates explicitly named by the record.
2. **Accept.** If the fixed obligation passes, give the successor the milestone, known unknowns, and next action. Passing does not mean “task complete”; it means “this recovery state is admissible.”
3. **Default.** Any missing covenant, broken chain, mismatched digest, absent receipt, failed predicate, or unsupported validator version is a default—not permission to guess.
4. **Costly audit.** Inspect the worktree/durable artifacts, replay relevant read-only checks, reconcile external receipts, and reconstruct a new covenant. Escalate to the owner only for unresolved irreversible side effects or an intent choice.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this ladder would reduce unnecessary full-context reconstruction on clean resumes while routing suspicious resumes to deeper checks; the evidence is the sparse-verification logic of Townsend/Gale-Hellwig plus the established distinction between checkpoint-only and checkpoint-plus-log recovery. This remains UNVERIFIED for agent work; the crash-injection experiment in §6 measures it.

The mechanism would target failure classes that a bare “last message” or “checkpoint exists” test cannot distinguish:

- a syntactically valid checkpoint whose referenced artifact changed after it was written;
- a checkpoint that says a test passed but names neither the command/predicate nor a durable result;
- an external side effect that may have occurred before death but lacks an idempotency key or receipt;
- a stale record separated from the current durable anchor;
- a missing or torn final record, exposed by a digest-chain discontinuity;
- a successor tempted to repeat an irreversible call because the predecessor left only prose;
- “done” asserted when only the checkpoint-writing metric was satisfied.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that hashes, chain continuity, and receipts would detect their corresponding concrete mismatches when those fields are present; evidence is direct equality/continuity checking, not an ML judgment. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that the covenant would expose a semantically important omission, because an omitted artifact or side effect generates no self-authenticating field; the evidence against confidence is Goodhart/proxy incompleteness and multitask incentive substitution ([Manheim and Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585); [Holmström and Milgrom 1991](https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24)). Randomly auditing a small sample of accepted resumes against broader reconstruction is therefore part of the design, not optional decoration.

### Proposal B — score recovery forecasts after the successor resolves them — UNVERIFIED: settle with the calibration arm in §6

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

The harness records each probability `p` from the covenant and later records the successor-observed binary outcome `y`. It computes quadratic score and calibration by predicate, model, policy/template version, task class, and failure type. The score changes harness policy only over a sufficiently large history; it does not award a live agent points, and a single high score never declares a task complete.

Possible policy responses are deliberately low-powered:

- lower the trust threshold or require a broader audit for historically overconfident predicate/model pairs;
- prefer a checkpoint prompt/template with better out-of-sample Brier score when costs are comparable;
- expose calibrated probability and consequence to the successor, which then chooses accept, audit, or owner escalation;
- retain a random audit sample, including high-confidence records, so the score cannot eliminate observations of its own failures.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that post-hoc scoring would identify systematic overconfidence across repeated, resolvable predicates; the evidence is the strict-properness result and Tian et al.'s finding that verbalized RLHF-model confidence was measurably calibratable on several QA benchmarks ([Gneiting and Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437); [Tian et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)). It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that raw per-checkpoint model probabilities would be calibrated enough to drive irreversible resume decisions without empirical calibration; the evidence is domain shift from QA to agent recovery and the risk/performative limitations in the scoring literature ([Peysakhovich and Plagborg-Møller 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2012.05.030); [Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a.html)).

**Important Goodhart constraint:** do not optimize for covenant frequency, field count, predicted confidence, or even in-sample Brier score. The decision target is eventual useful recovery under cost and safety constraints. Hold out crash scenarios, rotate latent audit predicates, and report a frontier of recovered useful work, unsafe duplicate effects, audit cost, and time-to-first-valid-action. This follows the proxy-failure taxonomy and multitask warning rather than claiming that a richer metric is immune to gaming ([Manheim and Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585); [Holmström and Milgrom 1991](https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24)).

## 3. Where the analogy transfers

The structural correspondence is real in four limited places:

| Mechanism-design object | Recovery object | Why the mapping is structurally valid |
|---|---|---|
| Privately observed realized cash flow | Predecessor's partially private task state | The predecessor has context, intentions, and observations the successor does not automatically possess. |
| Fixed repayment/covenant | Fixed, machine-checkable recovery predicates | Both replace a free-form self-report with an ex-ante admissibility condition. |
| Verification cost | Tool calls, replay, artifact inspection, external reconciliation, owner attention | Checking every claim can consume material time and tokens; verification is not free. |
| Default-triggered audit | Deep recovery/reconstruction path | Failure of the cheap obligation is informative and can route scarce verification effort. |
| Proper forecast score | Ex-post score of “successor will verify predicate X” | Both have a later-resolved event against which a probability can be scored. |

The mapping is strongest when predicates are objectively resolvable after failure: hash matches, test command passes, receipt exists, idempotency key was consumed, or successor completed the named next action without reconstruction. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that these binary events fit the mathematical input expected by a binary proper score; evidence is their direct mapping to `y ∈ {0,1}` in the quadratic rule. This says nothing about whether the predecessor's reported `p` equals a stable internal belief.

## 4. Where the analogy fails

### 4.1 A language-model worker is not automatically a strategic economic agent

The mechanism-design theorem assumes an agent with preferences over transfers that chooses a report to maximize expected utility. A dying model invocation does not own future score, and a fresh invocation is not necessarily the same utility-bearing entity. Prompt language such as “you will be scored” does not manufacture the theorem's incentive assumptions.

The transfer is therefore institutional, not psychological: the harness can use scores to select templates/models, allocate verification, and alter future execution policy. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that model-level selection pressure plus prompts yields more informative checkpoint probabilities than simple elicitation; evidence is positive QA calibration results but no recovery-domain causal experiment ([Tian et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)). The experiment in §6 settles this UNVERIFIED premise.

### 4.2 A contract reveals existing state; it does not make state durable

If death occurs before the record is durably appended, the mechanism has nothing to score or audit. If hidden reasoning was never externalized, an audit cannot seize it as a lender seizes realized cash flow. If external systems are not queryable, the “true state” may not be verifiable even at high cost. Elnozahy et al.'s checkpoint/log distinction and Chandy-Lamport's explicit recording protocol independently establish the missing systems requirement ([Elnozahy et al. 2002](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs614/2004sp/papers/EAWJ02.pdf); [Chandy and Lamport 1985](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/areas/os/Qual/papers/snapshots.pdf)).

Accordingly, the recovery covenant must ride on a durable event log/checkpoint facility. Building only the scoring layer would be category error.

### 4.3 Verification is imperfect and semantics are multidimensional

Classical costly-state-verification results commonly assume that an audit reveals the underlying state. Agent work has semantic properties—correct interpretation of owner intent, architectural coherence, safety—that may remain contestable after inspection. A passing test is only a proxy. Holmström-Milgrom predicts effort substitution when measurable and hard-to-measure tasks compete; Goodhart variants predict selection and causal failures under optimization ([Holmström and Milgrom 1991](https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24); [Manheim and Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)).

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that no finite generic covenant schema captures every future-relevant semantic fact; evidence is the open-ended task domain plus the cited multitask/proxy results. The proposal therefore treats covenant admission as “safe and useful place to resume,” never “proof of task correctness.”

### 4.4 Forecasts can affect their own resolution

A low predicted chance of recovery may trigger a deep audit, making recovery succeed; a high prediction may cause a cheap resume, making hidden defects surface. Thus the report changes the action and potentially the outcome. Standard proper scoring assumes non-performativity; Oesterheld et al. identify exactly this failure and develop specialized rules for performative settings ([Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a.html)).

The safe initial measurement is to score atomic predicates under a precommitted randomized audit policy, not score the downstream outcome “recovery succeeded” after policy reacted to `p`. It is **likely (55-80%)** that this reduces the performativity bias relative to deterministic confidence-triggered audits; evidence is precommitment breaking the direct `p → audit → y` path, but residual selection remains. The calibration arm in §6 must compare always-audit ground truth on a random sample.

### 4.5 The debt-contract optimality result is not portable as an optimality theorem

The financial model's cash-flow ordering, limited liability, transfer utility, distributional knowledge, and perfect verification do not map cleanly to task state. “Audit on default” is a design heuristic here, not a proof that the resulting harness policy is optimal. The cited models' conclusions are conditional on their environments ([Townsend 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(79)90031-0); [Gale and Hellwig 1985](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d14b3bef315c03f6686229f644eb470d9f182aa2&repid=rep1&type=pdf)).

## 5. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

The proposals pass the requested anti-overfit gate:

- They ride existing stop/tool hooks plus ordinary stable files or an existing event sink.
- They need a small schema, atomic append helper, validator, and score aggregator—not a second orchestration runtime.
- They do not require a vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- Predicate resolution prefers deterministic commands/receipts over an LLM judge.
- The policy remains a short accept/default/audit ladder, not a general workflow engine.

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** for covenant append/validation and scoring if the harness already exposes lifecycle hooks and a stable append target. The line estimate is **likely (55-80%)** for the mechanism logic alone; evidence is the small fixed schema and four-branch algorithm, while platform adapters, Windows atomic-write behavior, and external receipt integrations could push the total beyond 200 lines. This is UNVERIFIED; settle it with a throwaway implementation spike that counts nonblank noncomment lines separately for core and adapters.

Do **not** introduce a durable-execution engine to implement this proposal. If reliable append/checkpoint primitives do not exist, that missing substrate should be handled as a separate recovery-engineering decision, because mechanism design supplies no justification for a new runtime.

## 6. Falsifiable experiment

### Crash-injection and calibration trial

Run the same stratified task set under three randomized arms:

1. **Control:** existing resume information.
2. **Covenant:** fixed recovery covenant plus cheap accept/default validation, no forecast-based policy.
3. **Covenant + score:** covenant with per-predicate probabilities, random audit holdout, and calibration learned only from earlier folds.

Inject process death at seeded boundaries: before append, during append, after local edit, after test, before/after an idempotent external call, during multi-agent handoff, and after a misleading but syntactically valid checkpoint. Preserve a hidden evaluator manifest so the predecessor cannot optimize directly against every criterion.

Primary outcomes:

- fraction of runs reaching the first *valid useful action* without owner intervention;
- time/tool calls/tokens to first valid useful action;
- duplicated or contradictory external side effects;
- amount of work unnecessarily replayed;
- false acceptance rate: cheap path accepted but hidden audit found a material inconsistency;
- false default rate: deep audit was triggered although the state was admissible;
- Brier score and reliability diagram per predicate, model, and task class;
- semantic task success after resume, measured separately from covenant validity.

Mechanically independent ground-truth signals should be used before declaring a recovery successful or broken. For example, (1) deterministic artifact/test/receipt verification and (2) downstream task completion against hidden acceptance criteria are independent fields of evidence; rerunning the same model judgment is not. Detector controls should include a known-good covenant with an intentional known-present receipt and a known-bad covenant with a deliberate digest mismatch before interpreting zero detections.

Precommit failure criteria:

- Reject Proposal A if it does not improve useful recovery or reduce replay at matched unsafe-side-effect rate, or if false acceptance rises.
- Reject confidence-driven policy in Proposal B if held-out Brier score does not beat the base-rate forecast, if reliability is unstable across task classes, or if always-audit holdouts show policy-induced miscalibration.
- Reject the BUILD-SMALL classification if core mechanism code exceeds approximately 200 lines without counting tests or platform adapters; re-evaluate rather than relabeling complexity away.
- Stop the mechanism-design branch entirely if most injected failures are pre-append losses; that result says checkpoint/log placement, not incentive-compatible reporting, is the dominant bottleneck.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this trial would distinguish “better recovery metadata” from “more checkpoint ceremony,” because it measures downstream valid action, replay, side effects, false acceptance, and calibration rather than manifest production. The evidence is the randomized arms and separately measured semantic outcome. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that one task suite would establish broad generality; evidence is task-class heterogeneity and the Goodhart risk from a fixed hidden evaluator. Replicate on held-out task families before promotion.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Reward checkpoint frequency

Rejected. Frequency is an easily optimized proxy and encourages tiny checkpoints or ceremony. It does not establish atomicity, completeness, or recoverability. This is directly exposed by Goodhart's proxy analysis and Holmström-Milgrom effort substitution ([Manheim and Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585); [Holmström and Milgrom 1991](https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24)).

### Reward high confidence or “percent complete”

Rejected. Proper scores reward calibrated reports after resolution; rewarding the report's magnitude destroys that property. “Percent complete” also lacks a uniquely resolvable event.

### Winner-take-all model selection by average Brier score

Rejected. Forecasting-competition research shows that selecting only the top scorer can change incentives relative to paying every forecast by a proper rule ([Witkowski et al. 2022](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4410)). Use score as one low-powered policy signal and validate out of sample.

### Random audits alone

Retained only as a control and exploration sample. Random audits protect against blind spots, but do not make individual recovery records self-describing or cheaply admissible. Default-triggered audit and a small random holdout have complementary roles.

### VCG, auctions, peer prediction, or prediction markets

Rejected. Failure recovery generally has no natural allocation/payment problem, no stable population of self-interested bidders, and usually has eventual objective predicate resolution. Adding a market would manufacture agents and transfers without recovering state. Proper scoring is the smaller mechanism that matches the available observation.

### A new durable workflow runtime

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate for this unit. It could solve a genuine systems problem, but it is not a mechanism-design borrow and would reintroduce a previously rejected durable-execution component. The checkpoint/log literature should inform any separately justified substrate work ([Elnozahy et al. 2002](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs614/2004sp/papers/EAWJ02.pdf)).

## 8. Recommendation — UNVERIFIED: settle with the crash-injection and calibration trial in §6

Proceed only with a bounded experiment of the **recovery covenant + default-triggered audit**. Treat proper scoring as offline calibration and policy evidence until held-out crash trials establish reliability. Do not claim incentive compatibility for a prompted model, do not equate an admissible covenant with task correctness, and do not let this layer substitute for durable checkpoints/logs.

This recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to be worth the small experiment, based on strong theoretical fit for the *information-verification subproblem*, a small-bus implementation shape, and clear falsification criteria. It is not a recommendation for full deployment: that remains **UNVERIFIED; the crash-injection and calibration trial in §6 is the settling experiment**.

The most valuable result may be negative: if injected failures mostly occur before durable append, or if model probabilities fail base-rate calibration, stop borrowing from mechanism design. Invest instead in atomic state capture, write-ahead logging of tool effects, and idempotent recovery—the mechanisms that actually address lost computation.
