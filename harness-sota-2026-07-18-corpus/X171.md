# X171 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → agent reliability

## Executive result

The borrow transfers, but only in a constrained form. The useful mechanism is not “make the agent do science” and not “run every probe twice.” It is a **prespecified, controlled probe protocol** for high-risk epistemic claims: define the claim and decision rule before observing the result; prove the detector can see a known-present control; run a negative control where applicable; obtain a second signal whose failure mode is mechanically independent; and separate exploratory findings from conclusions. This maps closely to preregistration, assay controls, blinding, and prospective power planning.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this protocol would reduce confidently wrong `absent`, `broken`, and `done` conclusions in an agent harness. Evidence: (1) the owner reports six or more measured single-unvalidated-probe errors plus a seventh caught by a positive control; (2) experimental-design literature identifies prespecification, blinding, controls, and independent validation as bias/error countermeasures; and (3) LLM self-correction research finds that unaided reflection may fail or degrade answers, while reliable external feedback is materially different ([Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IkmD3fKBPQ); [Pan et al., TACL 2024](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00713/125177/When-Can-LLMs-Actually-Correct-Their-Own-Mistakes)). The size of the reduction is **UNVERIFIED — the preregistered replay experiment in “Validation plan” would settle it**.

The analogy does **not** justify literal statistical significance tests for ordinary deterministic shell probes, a fixed “two probes means truth” rule, or a second agent that merely rereads the first agent’s conclusion. Those versions violate the source field’s assumptions about sampling, independence, and control comparability. The translated design must reason about *failure-mode independence*, not merely count calls.

## The target failure, stated as an experimental-design problem

The target is a decision made after one observation:

> Run detector `D` once; observe zero, an error-like result, or an apparently successful terminal state; infer “the item is absent,” “the subsystem is broken,” or “the work is done.”

This bundles at least four hypotheses that a single observation often cannot distinguish:

1. The world-state hypothesis: the target is genuinely absent, broken, or complete.
2. The measurement hypothesis: the detector correctly measures the relevant state.
3. The scope hypothesis: the detector inspected all relevant locations, branches, identities, or time windows.
4. The execution hypothesis: the probe itself ran successfully and its output was not truncated, cached, filtered, misparsed, permission-limited, or routed to the wrong environment.

The experimental-design framing is useful because a measurement is not self-validating. A null result is interpretable only when the assay had a realistic opportunity to detect the phenomenon, its controls behaved as expected, and the result was not selected after trying multiple analyses. NIH’s NINDS rigor guidance explicitly calls for blinding/masking, randomization, prespecified inclusion/exclusion, all-result reporting, target-engagement checks, and planned independent validation or replication ([NINDS, “Rigorous Study Design and Transparent Reporting”](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/funding/preparing-your-application/preparing-research-plan/rigorous-study-design-and-transparent-reporting)).

## What experimental design has already solved

### 1. Preregistration separates prediction from post-hoc storytelling

#### In its own field

Preregistration records a research and analysis plan before observing the relevant data. The Center for Open Science describes it as specifying a plan in advance and submitting it to a registry; its purpose is to distinguish planned from unplanned work and prevent the same data from silently serving both hypothesis generation and hypothesis testing ([COS, “Preregistration”](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg)). COS also explicitly permits deviations, but requires them to be disclosed and treats unplanned analyses as exploratory rather than confirmatory. Conditional decision trees can be prespecified as `IF–THEN` branches rather than improvised after results are visible ([COS, “Preregistration,” conditional analyses](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg)).

The mechanism is temporal separation. Before knowing which result is convenient, the researcher commits to:

- the primary hypothesis;
- the primary outcome and measurement;
- inclusion/exclusion rules;
- the analysis and decision threshold;
- sample size or stopping rule;
- planned contingencies.

This does not prohibit learning from surprises. It prevents a flexible, result-dependent path from being presented with the evidential status of a fixed test. Registered Reports strengthen this by reviewing the design before outcomes are known and offering in-principle publication independent of whether the result is positive or null ([COS, “Registered Reports”](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports)).

#### Translation to the harness

Before executing a probe that may support a high-risk conclusion, the agent writes a compact **claim contract** into structured turn state:

```yaml
claim_id: C17
claim_class: absent | broken | done | exclusive | zero-count
claim: "No executable named X is available in the active environment"
scope: "current host, active user, inherited PATH, shell A and command resolver B"
primary_probe: "resolver A lookup"
positive_control: "resolver A lookup of known-present executable Y"
negative_control: "resolver A lookup of generated impossible name Z"
independent_probe: "resolver B lookup, different implementation and output parser"
decision_rule: "conclude absent only if both probes return clean not-found,
  positive controls are found, negative controls are not found, and scopes agree"
failure_actions:
  probe_error: "report indeterminate, not absent"
  control_failure: "invalidate detector; choose another measurement path"
  disagreement: "report conflict and investigate"
deviations: []
```

The contract is intentionally small. It binds the conclusion to a declared scope and makes an error, empty output, or deviation a different state from evidence of absence. It also preserves exploration: the agent can try extra probes, but it labels them exploratory and cannot silently substitute the most convenient output for the declared decision rule.

This translation is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent post-result rationalization in the targeted claim classes, because the decision rule and error handling are fixed before the output is seen, directly matching preregistration’s mechanism. Evidence is the temporal structure described by COS and the owner’s target pattern of conclusions drawn after a single unvalidated observation. Its actual benefit under tool latency and token pressure is **UNVERIFIED — the randomized replay experiment below would settle it**.

### 2. Positive and negative controls validate the measurement, not the hypothesis

#### In its own field

A negative control repeats the relevant experimental structure under a condition where the hypothesized causal mechanism cannot produce the target effect. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen explain that a useful negative control should reproduce the sources of bias while excluding the hypothesized mechanism; an association in the negative control therefore signals bias ([Epidemiology, “Negative Controls: A Tool for Detecting Confounding and Bias in Observational Studies”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). Arnold and Ercumen give the corresponding formal requirement: a negative-control outcome shares potential bias sources with the primary outcome but cannot plausibly be caused by the treatment ([JAMA, “Negative Control Outcomes”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428075/)).

The comparability requirement matters. A convenient control that does not traverse the same measurement path is reassuring theatre, not a control. Recent work on detection bias emphasizes that an appropriate negative-control outcome must share the same unmeasured determinants of ascertainment as the outcome of interest ([BMJ Epidemiology, “Detection bias and the role of negative control outcomes”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12172073/)).

A positive control is complementary: apply the assay to a condition known to contain the target signal. If the positive control is not detected, a null primary result cannot establish absence because the measurement’s sensitivity failed in the same run. NINDS expresses this general principle as verifying that the intervention or tool reached and engaged its intended target before interpreting outcomes ([NINDS rigor guidance](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/funding/preparing-your-application/preparing-research-plan/rigorous-study-design-and-transparent-reporting)).

#### Translation to the harness

For detector-like probes, attach two controls:

- **Known-present positive control:** a target known, before this probe, to exist in the same scope and detectable representation. Example: before claiming a command is unavailable using a command resolver, resolve a baseline command known to ship in that active environment through the *same resolver*. Before claiming a JSON query found no skills, query a fixture key inserted into the same parsed document or a known-present record established by a different field.
- **Known-absent negative control:** a collision-resistant sentinel constructed to be absent, passed through the same detector and parser. If it appears, the detector, cache, parser, or matching rule is unsound. For a state transition, the negative control may be an intentionally unperformed transition whose status must remain pending.

The key is **path equivalence**. A file-search positive control in another directory does not validate permissions or traversal in the target directory. A command located via shell builtin does not validate a different process-spawning path. A known-present field fetched from a cached index does not validate a live service query. The claim contract must name which failure modes each control shares.

This would catch cases where:

- an empty output is actually a failed or truncated probe;
- a glob or recursive traversal never reached the target scope;
- a parser discards valid records;
- credentials or permissions hide both target and control;
- a stale cache yields a plausible but false zero;
- an API routes to the wrong account, workspace, or environment;
- an overbroad matcher “finds” a deliberately impossible sentinel;
- the detector succeeds syntactically but has zero sensitivity to the representation being sought.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that a failing known-present control invalidates an absence conclusion from that detector. Evidence: this is a deductive consequence of the control being genuinely known-present in the same scope and measurement path, plus the experimental-control literature above. The residual 1-10% uncertainty concerns mistakes in establishing “known-present” or path equivalence; the mutation suite below tests those assumptions.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that a negative control adds material value to ordinary filesystem and configuration searches beyond a positive control. Evidence: negative controls detect false positives and contamination, while the reported target is dominated by false-negative/invalid-null behavior. The incremental rate is **UNVERIFIED — an ablation arm in the replay experiment would settle it**.

### 3. Blinding reduces result-conditioned measurement and interpretation

#### In its own field

Blinding keeps people collecting or assessing outcomes unaware of treatment assignment so expectations cannot systematically change measurement or interpretation. NIH defines blinding as keeping investigators unaware of sample designations until experiment completion and, where possible, analysis; it describes blinding and randomization as central to reducing selection and other biases ([NIH, “Rigor and Reproducibility Training Module 2: Blinding and Randomization”](https://grants.nih.gov/policy/reproducibility/training/blinding-randomization.pdf)). NC3Rs similarly recommends that outcome measurement occur while the assessor is unaware of treatment received until final statistical analysis ([NC3Rs, “Key elements of a well-designed experiment”](https://nc3rs.org.uk/3rs-resources/key-elements-well-designed-experiment)).

Blinding does not make an instrument accurate. It removes one route by which expectations influence choices: where to look, what to exclude, how to code an ambiguous observation, or whether to stop.

#### Translation to the harness

The first result should not author the second measurement. On creation of a claim contract, the harness selects or instantiates the second probe from a prespecified template **before exposing the primary output to the reasoning step that will interpret both**. The verifier receives:

- the exact claim and scope;
- raw outputs and exit/status metadata from both probes;
- control outcomes;
- the prespecified decision rule;
- no prose conclusion generated from probe one.

The verifier then classifies the evidence as `supported`, `refuted`, `conflicted`, or `indeterminate`. Only after that does the main agent narrate the result. This is “outcome-assessment blinding,” not full agent blinding.

If a single agent has already seen probe one and freely invents probe two, calling the process blinded would be false. The model can select a second probe that confirms its emerging story. A hook can enforce temporal ordering without introducing another model or runtime: freeze the probe plan, execute both, then reveal the bundle.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that this limited blinding will reduce confirmation-seeking in probe selection. Evidence: blinding’s established role in preventing expectation-conditioned assessment and the structural opportunity for an agent to choose follow-up probes after seeing an answer. Direct evidence in this harness is **UNVERIFIED — compare prespecified-hidden versus adaptively chosen second probes in the validation experiment**.

### 4. Power analysis prevents interpreting an insensitive null as evidence of no effect

#### In its own field

Statistical power is the probability that a test rejects a false null hypothesis under specified assumptions. A priori power analysis chooses sample size using a meaningful effect size, outcome variance, significance threshold, and desired false-negative risk. Ledolter and Kardon explain that small samples may miss meaningful effects and that power rises with sample size and effect size but falls with variance ([“Statistical Design of Experiments and Sample Size Selection Using Power Analysis”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425741/)). NC3Rs operationalizes the same idea by combining formal sample-size calculation with designs that control variation ([NC3Rs, “Key elements of a well-designed experiment”](https://nc3rs.org.uk/3rs-resources/key-elements-well-designed-experiment)).

Power analysis is prospective. It asks whether the design could reliably detect an effect worth detecting *before* using a null result as evidence.

#### Translation to the harness — and a necessary limit

Most shell and filesystem probes are not random samples from a stable population. Repeating the identical command twice often has no defensible binomial interpretation: the same quoting bug, wrong scope, cache, permissions, or parser can deterministically reproduce the same error. Therefore, conventional power calculations generally **do not transfer** to a single deterministic probe.

What does transfer is the prospective sensitivity question:

> If the target were present, which plausible states or representations would this probe detect, and which would it miss?

Implement this as a compact **fault-model coverage table**, not a fake p-value:

| Candidate failure / representation | Primary probe | Positive control | Independent probe | Residual gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| target in expected scope and format | detects | validates path | detects | low |
| target in alternate configured scope | misses | does not test | detects | low |
| primary parser drops valid row | misses | catches if same representation | independent parser detects | low |
| shared permissions hide target | misses | catches only if control shares ACL | may also miss | explicit gap |
| shared stale upstream index | misses | may pass | may also miss | explicit gap; live query needed |

For genuinely stochastic probes—flaky network calls, sampled model judgments, eventually consistent APIs—numerical planning may be legitimate, but only after estimating error rates and dependence. A binomial “two successes” calculation is invalid when retries share a server, cache, credential, or time window.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that the sensitivity-table translation is more valid than literal power analysis for deterministic agent tools. Evidence: formal power depends on a sampling model, effect size, variance, and error probabilities, while deterministic shared-cause failures violate those assumptions. Whether agents will complete the table accurately without excessive burden is **UNVERIFIED — mutation coverage and task-cost measurements in the validation experiment would settle it**.

### 5. Independent replication tests a different route to the same claim

#### In its own field

Replication and independent validation test whether a finding survives a new execution and, ideally, variation in investigators, materials, instruments, or setting. NINDS asks for plans to determine robustness and reproducibility, including independent validation or replication ([NINDS rigor guidance](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/funding/preparing-your-application/preparing-research-plan/rigorous-study-design-and-transparent-reporting)). The National Academies distinguishes reproducibility and replicability and notes that rigorous, transparent design makes successful replication more likely but does not guarantee it ([National Academies, *Reproducibility and Replicability in Science*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547546/)).

The value comes from changing error opportunities, not ceremonial repetition.

#### Translation to the harness

Require two **mechanically independent signals** before emitting a high-risk conclusion. Independence must be declared along at least one relevant dimension:

- different executable or API implementation;
- different data field or authoritative source;
- different traversal algorithm;
- different parser;
- direct state inspection versus behavioral test;
- pre-state/post-state comparison versus terminal status;
- remote authoritative view versus local cached view;
- artifact existence/content check versus producer exit code.

The second signal is invalid if it merely wraps the same underlying source. Examples of false independence include two shells calling the same missing executable, two queries reading the same stale index, two LLM passes over the same evidence, or two parsers fed the same truncated output.

For `done`, use outcome triangulation: (1) the requested artifact/state exists and meets an invariant, and (2) an independent consumer or verification command accepts it. For `broken`, distinguish service unavailability from client configuration by combining a client-side observation with a direct reachability/health or authoritative configuration signal. For `absent`, combine a scope-complete search with a different index or direct resolver, after controls validate both.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch shared *model* reasoning errors only when at least one signal is external and mechanically grounded. Evidence: the TACL survey reports that intrinsic self-correction may not improve and can degrade several task types, while reliable external tools or knowledge change the correction setting ([Pan et al., TACL 2024](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00713/125177/When-Can-LLMs-Actually-Correct-Their-Own-Mistakes)). Two model-generated opinions without new evidence are **unlikely (20-45%)** to supply the required independence; evidence is the same survey plus Huang et al.’s finding that unaided self-correction struggles on reasoning tasks ([Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IkmD3fKBPQ)). Exact harness-specific rates are **UNVERIFIED — the replay experiment would settle them**.

## Concrete harness design

### Proposal A — Claim-contract rule

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a short, high-priority reliability rule activated only for claims in a narrow lexicon and semantic class: `absent`, `not installed`, `never`, `zero`, `broken`, `disabled`, `did not fire`, `complete`, `done`, `fixed`, `only`, and equivalent categorical statements. The rule requires the agent to create the claim contract before probing, and forbids mapping `error`, `timeout`, `empty output with failed controls`, or `unknown scope` to `absent`.

The rule’s key state machine is:

```text
UNTESTED
  -> PLAN_FROZEN
  -> PRIMARY_MEASURED
  -> CONTROLS_VALID | DETECTOR_INVALID
  -> INDEPENDENT_MEASURED
  -> SUPPORTED | REFUTED | CONFLICTED | INDETERMINATE
```

Only `SUPPORTED` may produce the categorical claim. `INDETERMINATE` must name the missing evidence and use calibrated uncertainty. This is **likely (55-80%)** to improve compliance by itself because explicit rules alter the agent’s decision surface, but prompt-only enforcement can be forgotten under long context. Evidence: the mechanism is directly aligned with preregistration; the weakness is that the same model both applies and polices the rule. Its standalone compliance is **UNVERIFIED — log claim-contract completion in the replay experiment**.

### Proposal B — Controlled-probe gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a small hook that receives structured claim receipts. It does not reason about truth. It performs mechanical checks:

1. Detect a proposed categorical claim using explicit structured output from the agent; regex over arbitrary prose is only a fallback.
2. Require a frozen claim contract timestamped before primary results.
3. Require primary probe status, raw-output reference, and scope.
4. Require a positive-control receipt and verify it passed.
5. Require a negative-control receipt when the contract says one is applicable.
6. Require a second receipt with a different declared `mechanism_id` and `source_id`.
7. Reject duplicated source lineage, failed controls, probe errors mislabeled as not-found, or unresolved disagreements.
8. Return a compact correction telling the agent exactly which evidence is missing.

Suggested receipt:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "C17",
  "phase": "independent_probe",
  "probe_id": "get-command",
  "mechanism_id": "powershell-command-discovery",
  "source_id": "active-process-environment",
  "scope": "host/user/PATH fingerprint",
  "status": "found|not_found|error|timeout|partial",
  "exit_status": 0,
  "raw_output_digest": "sha256:...",
  "control_for": null,
  "observed_at": "..."
}
```

The gate stores only turn-local receipts; it is not a memory system, daemon, critic, or orchestration runtime. It can be bypassed explicitly for exploratory narration, but then the output cannot use categorical evidential language. A user override remains possible and is logged.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that this can stay below roughly 200 lines if the harness already exposes a hook callback plus turn-local structured state. That premise is **UNVERIFIED — a 90-minute spike implementing only receipt validation on the actual hook API would settle it**. If no such structured callback exists, the proposal inherits **UNVERIFIED — the same spike would determine whether it must be reduced to CONFIG or exceeds BUILD-SMALL**.

### Proposal C — Probe-pattern library

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Provide a small declarative library of patterns, not a general planner:

```yaml
command_presence:
  primary: resolver_A
  positive_control: known_present_command
  negative_control: random_impossible_command
  independent: resolver_B
  independence: different resolver implementation

file_absence:
  primary: direct stat when exact path known
  positive_control: known sibling or injected fixture through same access path
  negative_control: random impossible sibling
  independent: directory enumeration with explicit scope and error audit
  independence: direct lookup vs enumeration

operation_done:
  primary: producer reports success
  positive_control: expected output invariant
  negative_control: forbidden stale/incomplete marker absent
  independent: consumer validation or test
  independence: producer status vs consumer behavior
```

The patterns reduce the burden of inventing controls and independence arguments on every turn. They must remain examples with explicit scope placeholders; hard-coding platform-specific commands would overfit.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to improve protocol adherence compared with prose rules alone, because it turns abstract requirements into executable slots. Evidence is the role of structured design tools such as NC3Rs’ Experimental Design Assistant, which provides stepwise planning plus support for randomization, blinding, and sample-size calculation ([NC3Rs EDA](https://nc3rs.org.uk/our-portfolio/experimental-design-assistant-eda)). Harness-specific uplift is **UNVERIFIED — the replay experiment’s CONFIG-only versus CONFIG-plus-library arms would settle it**.

### Proposal D — Blinded evidence bundling for consequential claims

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For consequential claims only, the hook freezes the two probe templates, executes or schedules both through the existing tool bus, and reveals their evidence together. It does not launch a second agent. If execution cannot be hidden from the model in the existing event loop, retain plan freezing and drop the word “blinded”; transparency is better than a false methodological label.

This is **roughly even (45-55%)** to be implementable below roughly 200 lines because tool-event interception and deferred reveal capabilities are unknown. This is **UNVERIFIED — an API capability spike that demonstrates freeze → execute both → atomic reveal would settle it**. The core protocol does not depend on this proposal; prespecification plus controls and independent signals remain useful without true blinding.

## What this catches that a generic “double-check your work” instruction misses

| Failure pattern | Generic self-review | Controlled probe protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Probe exits nonzero but emits empty stdout | May read empty as zero results | Receipt status is `error`; categorical absence is blocked |
| Detector cannot find anything in the target scope | May repeat same detector | Known-present control fails; detector is invalidated |

## Adversarial examination of the analogy

### Structural similarities that make the borrow legitimate

1. **Both infer latent state from fallible measurement.** An experiment infers an effect; an agent probe infers system state. Neither output validates its own sensitivity or scope.
2. **Both permit analyst degrees of freedom.** Researchers can change outcomes or analyses after seeing data; agents can change scopes, commands, parsers, and stopping points after seeing tool output.
3. **Both suffer asymmetric interpretation of nulls.** “No significant effect” can be mistaken for “no effect”; “zero matches” can be mistaken for “target absent.” Prospective sensitivity/control checks address the shared logic.
4. **Both can use controls that share nuisance paths.** A negative control is informative only when it shares the relevant bias structure; a harness control is informative only when it shares permissions, parser, scope, and transport relevant to the claim.
5. **Both benefit from independent measurement.** A result that survives a genuinely different measurement path is stronger than repeated observation through the same failure mode.

These similarities make the core transfer **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally sound. Evidence is the one-to-one correspondence between measurement validity, degrees of freedom, null interpretation, control comparability, and independent validation in the cited methods literature.

### Disanalogies and limits

1. **No population sampling in many tool calls.** A deterministic command is not a randomized trial. Classical alpha, beta, confidence intervals, and sample-size formulas are usually inapplicable. The translation uses fault-model coverage unless a real stochastic model exists.
2. **The agent changes the environment.** Probes may mutate caches, timestamps, locks, or external state. Later probes may not measure the same world. The contract therefore marks probes read-only where possible and records observation order and state fingerprints.
3. **Independence is causal, not nominal.** Two tools can share an upstream API, index, credential, parser library, or filesystem view. “Different command” is not enough. Source lineage must be explicit.
4. **Known truth is scarce.** A positive control asserted by the same mistaken agent may itself be false. Controls should be fixtures, invariants, or known-present items established through a separate source. If none exists, the result remains indeterminate.
5. **Adversarial software failures differ from biological noise.** Permission errors, quoting bugs, truncation, and stale caches can be systematic and perfectly repeatable. Replication alone does not average them away.
6. **Blinding is partial in a single-agent loop.** Once the model sees a result, it cannot unsee it. Plan freezing is achievable; genuine blinded interpretation requires evidence withholding by the harness.
7. **Cost is per claim, not per study.** Applying a full protocol to every mundane statement would create latency and ritual compliance. Activation must be risk-based and narrow.
8. **Controls can share the wrong nuisance structure.** A positive control accessible under permissive ACLs does not validate access to a restricted target. A negative control that takes a different parser branch cannot diagnose target parsing.
9. **A successful control does not prove completeness.** It establishes some sensitivity, not exhaustive coverage. Conclusions must remain scoped.

The analogy therefore supports a gate for categorical, consequential conclusions; it does not support universal bureaucracy or numerical confidence theatre. I am **very likely (80-90%)** that forcing classical power calculations onto deterministic probes would reduce clarity and create false precision. Evidence is the absence of the sampling assumptions required by the cited power-analysis methods.

## Validation plan: preregister the harness intervention itself

The intervention should be evaluated using its own borrowed discipline before broad rollout.

### Hypotheses and arms

Preregister the primary hypothesis: the controlled-probe gate reduces the proportion of false categorical conclusions that escape to the final answer, without an unacceptable increase in task failure or latency.

Randomly assign replayable tasks within strata (`absent`, `broken`, `done`, `exclusive/zero`) to:

1. Baseline instructions.
2. Claim-contract CONFIG only.
3. CONFIG + positive control + independent signal gate.
4. Full protocol including negative controls and, where supported, frozen/hidden probe planning.

Use identical model/version, temperature, starting state, tool fixtures, and task ordering blocks across arms. Where exact replay is impossible, use matched task pairs and randomize arm order.

### Case set and controls

Construct three sources of cases:

- the six or more measured owner-correction incidents and the caught seventh case, redacted into replayable fixtures;
- a stratified sample of correct categorical conclusions, to measure nuisance blocking;
- mutation-generated cases with known ground truth.

Mutations should include: forced nonzero exit with empty stdout; truncated output; wrong account/workspace; permission-denied subtree; stale cache; malformed parser input; alternate representation; shared-upstream “independent” probes; false-positive matcher; incomplete output artifact; and a genuine clean absence.

Every detector must first demonstrate it finds a known-present fixture and rejects a known-absent fixture. This also validates the evaluation detector rather than assuming it works.

### Outcomes

Primary outcome:

- false categorical conclusion escape rate per adjudicated high-risk claim.

Secondary outcomes:

- detector-invalid cases correctly converted to `indeterminate`;
- disagreement cases surfaced rather than cherry-picked;
- false-block rate on correct claims;
- task completion rate;
- median added tool calls, tokens, and wall time;
- fraction of supposed independent pairs that share a source lineage;
- protocol compliance by component;
- owner correction rate in a shadow deployment.

Adjudicators should be blinded to arm and see raw fixtures plus evidence, not the agent’s persuasive prose. Resolve disagreements with a second adjudicator and a prespecified rule.

### Power and stopping

Do not invent a sample size from the reported “6+” cases. Estimate baseline escape rate and paired-arm discordance in a pilot that is excluded from the confirmatory set, choose the smallest operationally meaningful reduction, then calculate sample size for the selected paired or stratified proportion test. Freeze exclusion rules, analysis code, and stopping criteria before the confirmatory run. This directly follows prospective power analysis rather than using the observed result to choose a favorable threshold.

The appropriate sample size and expected effect are **UNVERIFIED — a blinded pilot estimating baseline event rate and discordant-pair frequency, followed by a preregistered power calculation, would settle them**.

### Success rule

Before looking at confirmatory outcomes, set explicit thresholds for:

- minimum relative and absolute reduction in escaped false conclusions;
- maximum false-block rate;
- maximum median latency and tool-call overhead;
- no regression in ordinary task completion.

If the full arm fails but the positive-control-plus-independent-signal arm succeeds, ship the smaller arm. If no arm clears the threshold, reject the borrow in implementation even though the conceptual analogy remains sound.

## Rollout and failure containment

1. Start in shadow mode: generate gate decisions without blocking answers.
2. Audit every disagreement between gate and agent against ground truth.

## Rejected translations and dead ends

### “Always run the same command twice” — reject

This is replication in name only. Systematic quoting, permission, scope, cache, parser, and upstream failures repeat. It adds cost without a new error opportunity.

### “Require two agents to agree” — reject as the default

Agreement is not measurement independence. Two agents may share model priors and the same flawed evidence. A second agent is useful only if it obtains a mechanically independent external signal; at that point the external signal, not agent plurality, supplies the epistemic gain.

### “Assign a p-value to absence” — reject

Without a defensible stochastic data-generating process, repeated deterministic probes do not yield a valid significance test. Use scoped claims and fault-model coverage.

### “A positive control proves the detector is complete” — reject

It proves detection of that control through that path. It does not prove all encodings, scopes, identities, or permissions were covered.

### “Block every uncertain sentence” — reject

This would overfit the harness to epistemic ritual and penalize exploration. Activate on categorical claims whose falsity would materially redirect action.

### “Hide all evidence from the main agent until finalization” — reject

Adaptive diagnosis is valuable. Only the confirmatory decision path needs freezing; exploratory follow-ups remain allowed and labeled.

## Bottom line

Experimental design has a genuine mechanism to lend: **make the measurement earn the conclusion**. Prespecification prevents the first result from rewriting the test; positive controls establish sensitivity; negative controls expose contamination and false positives; limited blinding prevents the first result from choosing its own confirmation; prospective sensitivity analysis asks whether a null was detectable; and independent replication changes the failure path.

The transfer succeeds because agent reliability and experimental inference share a latent-state-from-fallible-measurement structure. It stops where sampling assumptions, true blinding, or control comparability fail. The recommended harness change is a narrow claim contract plus a small mechanical receipt gate, evaluated with preregistered replay and mutation tests. It is not a new runtime and not a generic critic.
