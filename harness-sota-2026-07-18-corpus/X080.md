# X080 — law of evidence → tool design

**Unit:** X080  
**Lens:** cross-domain borrow from admissibility, authentication, chain of custody, hearsay-within-hearsay, and burdens of production into agent tool interfaces  
**Target defect:** tool interfaces and diagnostics that induce misuse, with 1,631 unmatched-quote/EOF shell failures supplied as measured ground truth in the unit brief  
**Research boundary:** external evidence-law and shell authorities only. Per the unit instruction, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation.

## Executive judgment

The analogy **transfers partially, not wholesale**.

The transferable structure is this: a court does not accept an item merely because its endpoint looks plausible. The proponent must establish what the item is; when reliability depends on multiple nested statements, each layer needs its own justification; and an exclusion ruling identifies the specific ground while preserving what was offered. Federal Rules of Evidence 901, 805, and 103 embody those three mechanisms respectively ([Rule 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901), [Rule 805](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805), [Rule 103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_103)). A shell call assembled through model text, JSON, a host process API, PowerShell or Bash, and sometimes another `-c` string has the same relevant topology: identity can be lost at a transformation boundary, and validating only the outermost text does not validate an inner layer.

The non-transferable structure is equally important. Evidence law manages uncertainty in adversarial fact-finding; a command runner must produce exact bytes/arguments and then the operating system either accepts them or does not. Courts can admit imperfectly authenticated evidence and let a fact-finder reduce its weight. A runner cannot sensibly assign “reduced weight” to an unterminated quote. Chain-of-custody paperwork is retrospective and can coexist with contamination; shell tooling needs prevention before execution. Hearsay doctrine also addresses perception, memory, narration, sincerity, and confrontation—human reliability concerns that have no direct equivalent in deterministic tokenization.

**Conclusion:** it is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on (1) Rule 805’s explicit per-layer requirement and (2) Microsoft’s documentation that PowerShell uses multiple parsing modes and may hand arguments to another parser, that a *per-parser-layer admission contract* is a sound design borrow. It is only **likely (55-80%)**, based on available native parse-only facilities but no harness implementation inspection, that the full proposal below can materially reduce this harness’s quoting failures without unacceptable false positives. The replay experiment specified below would settle that second claim.

Do not build a “legal evidence ledger.” Build a small argv-first adapter, a parser-boundary manifest for the remaining shell-text cases, and evidence-ruling-style errors.

## 1. What the source field has already solved

### 1.1 Authentication: require a foundation for the identity being claimed

In its own field, authentication is a threshold question: Federal Rule of Evidence 901(a) requires the proponent to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the offered item is what the proponent claims it is. Rule 901(b)(9) specifically permits authentication through evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result ([Rule 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). This is a burden of **production**, not a guarantee of ultimate truth. The proponent supplies a foundation; the judge decides whether the threshold has been met.

That separation is useful. “This string looks like a command” is not the relevant identity claim. The useful claim is: “the target program will receive this ordered vector of arguments, under this shell dialect and expansion policy.” The tool-call proponent—the model/tool client—should supply that claim structurally. The executor should authenticate it by parsing or by bypassing shell parsing, rather than infer it from an opaque string.

Rule 104(b) supplies a related conditional mechanism: when relevance depends on a fact, proof sufficient to support a finding of that fact must be introduced; admission may be conditional on later foundation ([Rule 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)). Translated carefully, a shell-text call whose safety depends on “this payload is Bash, not PowerShell” should be admitted only after the caller declares the dialect and the corresponding parser accepts it.

### 1.2 Chain of custody: record each transfer and transformation, not just endpoints

In its own field, chain of custody tracks movement through collection, safeguarding, and analysis by recording who handled evidence, when, and why ([NIST definition](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/chain_of_custody)). NIST’s evidence-preservation handbook says the record is chronological and covers collection, custody, control, transfer, and disposition; it also says derived evidence should have its own chain ([NIST biological evidence handbook, p. 25](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/NIST-IR-7928.pdf)). That last point matters most here: a derivative is not made trustworthy merely because its source was trustworthy.

The tool-design translation is a **transformation chain**, not a people-access log:

`intent fields → JSON strings → host-language strings → argv or shell source → nested interpreter source → target argv`

At each edge record the input representation, output representation, responsible encoder, target grammar, and validation result. Preserve the canonical source rather than only a prettified display rendering. This resembles chain of custody because the question is continuity of identity across transformations. It differs because most transformations are deterministic and therefore can often be tested exactly.

### 1.3 Hearsay within hearsay: every nested layer needs an independent basis

Federal Rule of Evidence 805 does not allow a reliable outer record to launder an unreliable inner statement. Each part of a combined hearsay statement must independently conform to an exception ([Rule 805](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805)). The Advisory Committee’s example is a hospital record containing information supplied by a patient’s wife: the business-record basis for the hospital entry does not automatically establish the inner statement; the inner statement needs its own exception.

This is the sharpest borrow for quoting failures. A JSON-valid tool call can contain invalid Bash. Valid Bash can contain an invalid `python -c` program. Valid PowerShell can produce the wrong native argument vector. Outer-layer success does not authenticate the inner layer.

Microsoft documents that PowerShell tokenizes commands using expression and argument modes, that `$()`, quotes, braces, and other metacharacters create additional interpretation, and that native commands introduce further argument-passing behavior ([PowerShell about_Parsing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/microsoft.powershell.core/about/about_parsing?view=powershell-7.6)). GNU Bash documents that `-n` reads commands without executing them and can check syntax ([GNU Bash manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/bash/manual/bash.html)). PowerShell’s parser API returns an AST, tokens, and parse errors for input text ([Parser.ParseInput](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.management.automation.language.parser.parseinput?view=powershellsdk-7.4.0)). These are concrete means to test individual layers rather than trusting the outer envelope.

### 1.4 Best evidence: preserve and operate on the canonical object

Federal Rule of Evidence 1002 requires an original writing, recording, or photograph when a party seeks to prove its content, subject to the Rules’ exceptions ([Rule 1002](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1002)). The doctrine is narrower and more exception-filled than the slogan “always demand the original,” so the analogy must not be overstated.

The useful design principle is nevertheless concrete: when exact content controls behavior, do not reconstruct it from a lossy display. For multiline or nested shell source, preserve the exact script as the canonical execution object and make logs a derived view. For direct program calls, the canonical object is `program + argv[] + environment delta + cwd`, not a rejoined command line.

### 1.5 Offers, objections, and rulings: retain the proffer and state the curable ground

Rule 103 requires a timely objection to state its specific ground, and when evidence is excluded the offering party preserves its substance through an offer of proof. The court may state the character or form of the evidence, the objection, and the ruling ([Rule 103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_103)). The Advisory Committee explains that these devices alert the decision-maker to the proper corrective action and allow the other side to take corrective measures.

A bare “unexpected EOF” is a poor ruling because it announces a result while omitting the actual representation layer, dialect, source span, expected closer, and safe cure. An evidence-ruling-style tool error is proposed to preserve the rejected proffer, identify the exact failed condition, and offer a structurally different cure; its improvement over current diagnostics is **UNVERIFIED and roughly even (45-55%); experiment E4 below settles it by comparing next-attempt recovery.**

### 1.6 Regularly conducted records and negative evidence: useful only with a validated recording duty

Rule 803(6) treats regularly made records as an exception only under conditions: contemporaneity or a knowledgeable source, regular keeping, regular practice, qualified foundation, and no shown lack of trustworthiness. Rule 803(7) permits absence from such records to prove nonoccurrence only when a record was regularly kept for that kind of matter ([Rule 803](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

This directly reinforces the program’s two-signal honesty rule. “No quoting failure appears in telemetry” is probative only after showing that this class of attempt is always recorded and that a known quoting failure is detected. A sparse or broken recorder cannot establish absence. This is not merely metaphor: Rule 803(7)’s logic depends on a demonstrated duty/practice to record the event class.

## 2. Concrete translation: the Parser-Custody Contract

## UNVERIFIED recommendation A — make direct execution argv-first

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a structured mode to the execution tool:

```json
{
  "program": "git",
  "args": ["show", "origin/main:path with spaces/file.txt"],
  "cwd": "C:\\work",
  "stdin": null,
  "shell": false
}
```

The runner must pass the argument vector without routing it through a shell. It may render a human-readable command for logs, but must label that rendering `display_only` and never execute the rejoined text.

This is authentication by process: the claimed identity is the actual argv. It removes quoting from ordinary program invocation rather than teaching the model more quoting rules. Its target classes are argument-boundary loss, paths with spaces, accidental wildcard expansion, shell metacharacter interpretation, and injection through argument values; the confidence and experiment for that prediction are stated immediately below.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with experiment E1: replay a stratified sample of successful direct-program calls and unmatched-quote failures through an argv adapter, comparing the child process’s observed argv with an instrumented echo-argv executable on Windows PowerShell 7 and Git Bash.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to remove quote balancing from direct executable invocations because an argv API does not ask a shell to parse the model’s command text; Microsoft’s documentation independently shows why PowerShell/native argument transitions are otherwise parser-sensitive. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to fix commands that genuinely require pipelines, redirection, globbing, conditionals, or shell expansion, because those semantics are not representable by a single direct argv call.

## UNVERIFIED recommendation B — require a per-layer manifest for genuine shell source

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Keep a raw-script mode, but make its parser boundaries explicit:

```json
{
  "script": "...exact source...",
  "dialect": "bash",
  "mode": "file",
  "layers": [
    {"grammar": "bash", "validator": "bash -n", "status": "pending"}
  ]
}
```

If the outer script contains `bash -c`, `pwsh -Command`, `cmd /c`, `python -c`, or another recognized string-evaluating boundary, the runner extracts or receives the inner payload and adds a layer. Each declared layer must either:

1. pass its native parse-only validator;
2. be converted to a file/stdin form that avoids one quoting boundary; or
3. carry an explicit `unvalidated_reason` and require the escape-hatch policy described below.

The first implementation should recognize only a small allowlist of high-frequency interpreters. Unknown boundaries remain executable through an escape hatch; they are not silently claimed safe.

This is Rule 805 translated literally at the structural level: outer validity does not launder inner invalidity. Its target classes are balanced outer quotes that expose malformed inner source, a Bash payload sent to PowerShell, unterminated here-documents/here-strings, and nested `-c` payloads whose delimiters were consumed one layer too early; the confidence and experiment for that prediction are stated immediately below.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with experiment E2: build a read-only shadow validator and run it over known failing unmatched-quote calls plus a time-matched sample of successful shell calls, then separately run property-generated nested payloads through the native parsers.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch a meaningful subset of the measured unmatched-quote failures because Bash provides `-n` and PowerShell provides parser errors, while the measured fingerprint is syntactic. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to validate arbitrary `cmd.exe` quoting or program-specific mini-languages accurately because not every interpreter exposes a parse-only API and extraction of dynamically constructed strings can be undecidable without execution.

## UNVERIFIED recommendation C — spill complex source to a canonical script file

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

When a call is multiline, contains a recognized nested interpreter, or crosses a configurable quote-complexity threshold, write the exact payload to a per-call temporary `.ps1` or `.sh` file and invoke the interpreter’s file mode. Record a content hash, dialect, encoding, newline convention, and deletion outcome. Pass data as argv, stdin, or environment values instead of interpolating it into source wherever feasible.

This is the narrow “best evidence” borrow: the validated bytes are the executed bytes. It removes one host-string-to-`-c` encoding layer and makes native line/column diagnostics meaningful.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with experiment E3: execute semantically equivalent generated scripts in current string mode and file mode across quotes, Unicode, CRLF/LF, trailing backslashes, here-documents, and environment expansion, comparing exit status, stdout/stderr, and side effects inside a disposable fixture.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to eliminate failures caused solely by one `-c` quoting boundary because file execution bypasses that boundary, based on the Bash manual’s file-mode behavior and PowerShell’s documented parser modes. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to preserve intended semantics across both Windows shells because encoding, newline, policy, profile, and working-directory behavior can differ; E3 must test those explicitly.

## UNVERIFIED recommendation D — return an “evidentiary ruling,” not a generic parser complaint

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Normalize shell-tool rejection errors to fields such as:

```json
{
  "status": "rejected_before_execution",
  "ground": "UNTERMINATED_SINGLE_QUOTE",
  "layer": 2,
  "dialect": "bash",
  "source_span": {"line": 3, "column": 17},
  "expected": "'",
  "proffer_hash": "sha256:...",
  "proffer_preserved": true,
  "safe_cures": [
    "pass this value in args[]",
    "move the inner payload to script/file mode"
  ],
  "retry_equivalence_key": "bash:L2:UNTERMINATED_SINGLE_QUOTE:<normalized-shape>"
}
```

The error must say whether the child process was never started, started and rejected source, or ran and returned nonzero. It must preserve the exact rejected content or a secure reference/hash where content may contain secrets. It must recommend a different representation, not merely “fix quoting.” On a repeated equivalence key, the next retry should be forced toward argv/file/stdin form or explicitly acknowledge an override.

The proposed control-level target is retrying the same malformed topology with cosmetic edits. The schema also distinguishes a host-envelope failure from an inner-parser failure and supplies the information needed to choose a non-equivalent repair. Whether the current harness lacks equivalent information is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by inspecting real error payloads in the neighboring grounding unit**, so this unit makes no absence claim.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with experiment E4: A/B replay failed-call continuations using current stderr versus the structured ruling, measuring next-attempt syntactic validity and repeated-equivalence-key rate without executing destructive payloads.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce immediate same-shape retry loops because Rule 103’s successful source-field mechanism is precisely specific grounds plus preserved substance, and the proposed error exposes a machine-actionable alternative. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** whether wording alone improves end-to-end task success; E4 must separate parser recovery from task correctness.

## UNVERIFIED recommendation E — use a graduated burden, not universal blocking

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Configure the admission policy around representation risk:

- Direct `program + args[]`, no shell: admit after schema and executable-resolution checks.
- One declared Bash or PowerShell file: require the native parser to accept it.
- Multiple recognized interpreter layers: require a validator result for every static layer or convert inner payloads to files/stdin.
- Dynamic or unknown layers: allow only through an explicit escape hatch that records the unvalidated condition.
- Destructive or externally consequential actions: retain the harness’s existing authorization controls; parser validity must never be treated as authorization.

This borrows the *placement* of a burden from authentication, not courtroom proof standards wholesale. The caller produces the intended representation; the runner checks the foundation. The burden increases with parser ambiguity because the cost of supplying structure is low while the expected correction cost is higher.

This recommendation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with experiment E5: shadow-score the policy on successful and failed historical calls, then choose thresholds against a predeclared false-block ceiling and quote-failure recall floor.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to avoid the overblocking of a universal raw-string ban because it preserves an explicit escape hatch and applies stricter proof only when layers multiply. No deployment claim is warranted until E5 measures false blocks.

## 3. What this design catches—and what it does not

| Failure class | Mechanism | Expected disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Unmatched quote or incomplete here-document in top-level Bash | `bash -n` on canonical file | **Very likely (80-90%)** to reject before execution; GNU documents `-n` as a syntax check. |
| PowerShell parse error, including incomplete strings or script blocks | PowerShell parser API | **Very likely (80-90%)** to reject before execution because the API returns parse errors and source extents. |
| Valid outer shell, invalid static inner `bash -c`/`pwsh -Command` | Per-layer manifest and validator | **Likely (55-80%)** to reject; effectiveness depends on reliably extracting the static payload. |
| Path/argument split caused by spaces or metacharacters | Direct argv mode | **Very likely (80-90%)** to eliminate for direct executables because no shell tokenization is requested. |
| Shell dialect mismatch | Required `dialect` plus native validator | **Likely (55-80%)** to reject when syntax differs; syntactically valid but semantically different constructs remain possible. |
| Repeated cosmetic retries of the same malformed structure | Equivalence key plus representation-changing cure | **Likely (55-80%)** to identify; whether the agent follows the cure is UNVERIFIED and is settled by E4. |
| Variable expands to an unintended but syntactically valid value | None of the syntax gates | **Very unlikely (5-20%)** to catch without semantic/domain assertions; do not claim otherwise. |
| Correctly parsed destructive command | None of the syntax gates | **Almost certainly not (1-5%)** prevented by parser custody alone; authorization and destructive-action controls remain separate. |
| Program-specific expression inside a quoted argument (regex, SQL, `jq`, compiler flags) | Only if that grammar has an explicit adapter | **Unlikely (20-45%)** to catch in the initial allowlist. |
| Runtime-dependent quoting generated by command substitution/eval | Static validation | **Unlikely (20-45%)** to prove safe; policy should prefer removing `eval`/nested string interpretation or mark it unvalidated. |

The important boundary is **syntax identity, not semantic correctness**. The mechanism can establish that exact source reached the declared parser and was grammatically accepted. It cannot establish that the command expresses the owner’s intent, that referenced files exist, or that effects are safe.

## 4. Adversarial transfer test

### Structural matches

1. **Multiple transformation layers exist in both domains.** Evidence may move through handlers and derivatives; commands move through serializers, host languages, shells, and child interpreters. NIST explicitly treats derived evidence as needing its own custody record, while Rule 805 independently tests nested statements.
2. **Endpoint inspection is insufficient in both domains.** A plausible exhibit may not be authentic; a plausible display string may not correspond to the argv actually received.
3. **The proponent is best placed to state the claim.** Rule 901 places the production burden on the proponent. The model/client knows whether it intends literal arguments, shell expansion, or a script and can state that choice.
4. **Specific grounds enable correction.** Rule 103’s specific objection and preserved proffer correspond to layer-specific, source-located, representation-changing error feedback.

### Structural disanalogies

1. **Probabilistic credibility versus deterministic parsing.** Evidence may be admitted on a threshold showing while ultimate weight remains disputed. Parsing is not weighted: a token boundary either matches the intended argv or it does not. Therefore, do not implement numerical “evidence confidence” for syntax.
2. **Adversarial parties versus a cooperative stack.** Evidence rules allocate burdens between parties with conflicting interests. The agent and runner nominally share an objective. The useful borrow is division of responsibility, not adversarial ceremony.
3. **Retrospective audit versus prospective prevention.** Chain of custody primarily supports later authentication. A tool wrapper can test before execution. Logging without a preflight gate would imitate the least useful part of the source field.
4. **Human testimony versus formal languages.** Hearsay exceptions rely on circumstances thought to indicate human trustworthiness. Shell grammars can often be parsed exactly. Do not invent “exceptions” when a native parser is available.
5. **Tolerance for gaps.** Courts sometimes treat chain defects as affecting weight rather than admissibility; tool execution cannot safely downgrade an incomplete command to a warning when it will fail or mutate the wrong target. The harness policy must be more binary for known syntax defects.
6. **Best-evidence doctrine is not a general integrity law.** Rule 1002 applies when proving content and has exceptions. Its contribution here is only the canonical-object principle; it does not justify a heavyweight immutable ledger.

**Adversarial verdict:** the analogy survives only after discarding courtroom confidence scoring, ceremonial custody logs, and human credibility exceptions. What remains is a compact engineering rule: authenticate the intended representation, validate each parser layer independently, execute the canonical object, and issue a specific ruling on rejection. That surviving core is **very likely (80-90%)** structurally sound because each element has both a source-law mechanism and a concrete parser/tool counterpart documented by GNU or Microsoft.

## 5. Minimum viable experiment and proof standard

The design must not be declared “working” from one detector. Use two mechanically independent signals, with a known-present control for each:

### Signal 1 — historical replay

Run the shadow adapter against:

- a stratified sample of the supplied 1,631 unmatched-quote/EOF failures;
- successful shell calls matched by shell, length, nesting depth, and time period;
- known-present controls: one deliberately unterminated Bash quote and one deliberately unterminated PowerShell string.

Record true pre-execution catches, false blocks, unclassified failures, parser layer, and whether argv/file mode offers an equivalent representation. This signal tests ecological validity against real agent output.

### Signal 2 — generated boundary tests

Generate argument vectors and scripts spanning spaces, empty arguments, single/double quotes, backticks, dollar expansion, nested `-c`, Unicode, CRLF/LF, trailing backslashes, here-documents/here-strings, and JSON escapes. Compare:

- the intended argv/payload;
- the adapter’s canonical representation and hash;
- the argv observed by a dedicated echo-argv child;
- native parser acceptance and actual non-destructive execution output.

This signal is mechanically independent because it tests exact round trips and generated edge cases, not corpus classification.

### Predeclared gates

The numeric thresholds below are design proposals, not evidence-backed facts:

- **UNVERIFIED gate G1:** at least 90% recall on known-present top-level unmatched-quote controls and the labeled replay subset; settle by E2.
- **UNVERIFIED gate G2:** below 1% false rejection on matched successful direct-executable calls; settle by E1/E5.
- **UNVERIFIED gate G3:** zero observed argv mismatches in the generated direct-execution suite, with the suite size and seed published; settle by E1 plus Signal 2.
- **UNVERIFIED gate G4:** statistically reported reduction in same-equivalence-key retry after structured errors, without a drop in eventual parser recovery; settle by E4.
- **UNVERIFIED gate G5:** both known-present controls detected before any claim that a failure class is absent; settle on every detector run.

Passing these gates would make it **likely (55-80%)**, based on independent real-history and generated-round-trip evidence, that the adapter addresses syntactic quoting failures. It would not prove semantic correctness or production safety. Those require separate task-level and authorization tests.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

Every proposed component rides the existing tool/hook bus and is deliberately small. This design does **not** introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Rejected alternatives:

- **Legal-style append-only evidence service:** rejected. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to prevent quoting failures because retrospective custody records do not alter parser boundaries, and it would be a BUILD-LARGE component without proportional benefit.
- **LLM critic that reviews every command:** rejected. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to outperform native parsers on grammar while adding latency and another probabilistic failure mode. It also approaches the previously rejected trained-critic pattern.
- **Universal ban on raw shell:** rejected. It is **likely (55-80%)** to block legitimate pipelines and shell-native workflows because those operations genuinely require a shell grammar. A graduated escape hatch is less brittle.
- **More prompt rules about escaping:** rejected as the primary fix. It is **likely (55-80%)** to leave the structural defect intact because PowerShell, Bash, JSON, and nested interpreters have different escaping rules; Microsoft’s parsing documentation demonstrates this multiplicity. Prompt guidance may supplement but cannot authenticate the received argv.
- **Parse once at the outer layer:** rejected. Rule 805 supplies the exact counterexample: outer admissibility does not establish inner admissibility. Technically, JSON validity says nothing about Bash validity, and Bash validity says nothing about a nested Python or PowerShell payload.
- **Hashing alone:** rejected. NIST describes hashes as integrity checks for digital evidence ([NIST digital evidence preservation](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ssd/software-quality-group/computer-forensics-tool-testing-program-cftt/digital-evidence)), but a hash can prove that malformed source was preserved perfectly. Hashes support canonical-object identity; they do not establish syntactic or semantic correctness.

## 7. Recommended implementation order

1. **Argv-first direct execution (Recommendation A).** Highest leverage and least analogy-dependent: eliminate a parser rather than police it.
2. **Canonical file mode plus native parse checks (Recommendations B/C).** Cover commands that truly need shell features and preserve exact source.
3. **Structured ruling errors and retry equivalence (Recommendation D).** Make failures curable and measurable.
4. **Shadow-mode graduated policy (Recommendation E).** Measure false blocks before enforcement.

This order is **likely (55-80%)** to maximize early value per line of implementation because A removes an entire error-producing boundary, B/C reuse native parsers, and D/E operate on the resulting structured metadata. That prioritization is UNVERIFIED; E1-E5 settle it by reporting avoided failures, false blocks, retry recovery, and implementation size for each increment.

## Final answer to the cross-domain question

Law of evidence has already solved a closely matching *composition problem*: authentication requires a foundation for identity; chain of custody accounts for transformations and derivatives; Rule 805 refuses to let a valid outer layer launder an invalid inner layer; Rule 1002 favors the canonical content when content itself matters; and Rule 103 makes rejection specific and preserves the offered substance for correction and review.

The borrow is valid only at that structural level. The harness should implement a **Parser-Custody Contract**: structured argv for direct execution, exact script files for real shell programs, a declared and independently validated chain for every static parser layer, and machine-readable rejection rulings that identify layer, ground, span, preserved proffer, and a representation-changing cure. It should not import courtroom confidence scoring, ceremonial ledgers, or human hearsay exceptions.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** a better conceptual fit than “teach the model to quote more carefully,” because the former removes or authenticates parser boundaries while the latter leaves all boundaries implicit; the evidence is the per-layer logic of Rule 805 together with GNU Bash and Microsoft PowerShell’s native parsing facilities. The magnitude of improvement in this owner’s harness remains **UNVERIFIED; experiments E1-E5, especially matched historical replay plus mechanically independent generated round trips, are required before deployment or a claim of completion.**
