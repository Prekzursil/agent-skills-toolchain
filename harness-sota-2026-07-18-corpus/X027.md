# X027 — Cross-domain borrow: aviation safety → agent-claim verification

## Executive conclusion

The aviation mechanism worth borrowing is **not “use a checklist” in the generic sense** and it is not “ask a second agent.” It is a three-layer assurance pattern:

1. **Challenge–verification–response (CVR) at consequential transition points:** challenge with a short list of critical state variables, inspect the actual indication, and answer with the observed value rather than “done” or “checked.”
2. **Recorder-derived assurance:** preserve an event trace produced by the operating substrate, then evaluate objective outcome parameters from that trace instead of treating the operator’s account as evidence.
3. **Sample normal operations, not only declared incidents:** use routine-operation sampling to discover unsafe adaptations and verification gaps that outcome-triggered review never sees.

This composite transfers. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the shared structure that both aircraft operation and tool-using agents change externally observable state through fallible procedures, that it will catch a useful class of false “done,” wrong-target, stale-state, partial-completion, and silent-degradation claims. The claim remains **UNVERIFIED;** the settling experiment is the replay study in §10, comparing detection and false-block rates on a labeled set of real agent runs.

The analogy transfers only if the verifier reads evidence independently of the acting agent’s narrative. A second language-model pass over the same transcript is not the aviation mechanism: it is closer to two pilots reading back the same mistaken mental model while neither looks at the instrument. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the FAA’s explicit requirement to answer with actual system status and the NASA checklist report’s explicit “verification” step, that prose-only review omits the defining safety property of the borrowed mechanism ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.4, PDF p. 20](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf); [NASA CR-177549, pp. 19–20](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)).

The proposed first implementation is deliberately small and rides existing tool/hook mechanisms. It does not introduce a second orchestrator, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

**Proposal classification — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

This is an ideation unit. Per the unit boundary, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. “Currently miss” below means failure classes that a normal agent transcript or self-report cannot establish, not a claim that a particular local component is absent.

## 1. The source-field mechanisms, in their own field

### 1.1 Challenge–verification–response: a checklist is a redundant state inspection

The NASA human-factors study of normal flight-deck checklists distinguishes two methods that are often blurred together:

- In **call-do-response**, the list leads the action: one crewmember calls an item, another sets it, and then announces the new status.
- In **challenge–verification–response**, crews first configure the aircraft through the normal operational flow. The checklist is then a backup: one pilot calls the item, both verify the setting, and the other states the verified status. This creates both *configuration redundancy* (perform first, check afterward) and *mutual redundancy* (crew cross-checking). The report warns that a do-list loses configuration redundancy and that an interrupted sequence can let a mistake pass unnoticed ([NASA CR-177549, pp. 19–20](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)).

The FAA’s later SOP guidance sharpens what “response” means. It says the response should be the **actual system status, switch/lever position, or specific parameter value**, and that generic responses such as “set” or “checked” are less informative. It also recommends short, prioritized lists because interruption probability rises with checklist length. Its error list includes omitted items, failure to verify visually, interruption, incomplete or wrong checklists, and difficulty confirming the action ([FAA AC 120-71B, §§5.3–5.6, PDF pp. 20–21](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)).

This mechanism does not prove every fact about a flight. It verifies a deliberately selected set of safety-critical configuration claims at phase boundaries. The checklist study explicitly describes the design tension: listing everything makes a checklist long enough to invite poor use; listing only critical items can let omitted configuration errors pass ([NASA CR-177549, p. 21](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/NASA%20Ames%20Rpt%20CR%20177549%20.pdf)). That tension is central to the transfer, not an inconvenience to hide.

### 1.2 Flight data recorders and FOQA: reconstruct behavior from substrate data

A flight data recorder measures operating conditions such as time, altitude, airspeed, heading, attitude, flap position, autopilot mode, and alerts. The NTSB uses recorder data alongside other evidence to reconstruct an accident; it does not ask the flight crew to summarize those values after the fact ([NTSB, “Cockpit Voice Recorders and Flight Data Recorders”](https://www.ntsb.gov/news/Pages/cvr_fdr.aspx)).

Flight Operational Quality Assurance (FOQA) moves the recorder idea from rare accidents to ordinary operations. FAA describes FOQA as collection and analysis of digital flight data from normal line operations and emphasizes that it supplies objective information unavailable through other methods ([FAA, FOQA program description](https://www.faa.gov/about/office_org/headquarters_offices/avs/offices/afx/afs/afs200/afs260/descriptions)).

Two important limits are visible in aviation’s own practice:

- Recorder data is one input, not a self-interpreting oracle. The NTSB says recorders are used “in conjunction with other information”; measurements can be incomplete or require validation ([NTSB recorder overview](https://www.ntsb.gov/news/Pages/cvr_fdr.aspx)).
- Objective traces answer many **what/when** questions but often not **why**. Aviation therefore also uses narrative reporting, operational observation, physical evidence, and domain experts.

### 1.3 LOSA: observe normal work, where latent hazards live

Line Operations Safety Assessment (LOSA) is a structured observational program for normal operations, not merely an investigation after a mishap. FAA’s implementation guidance lists peer-to-peer observation, confidential/non-punitive collection, and voluntary participation among its essential characteristics. It says normal-operation monitoring identifies at-risk behavior proactively and that peers are less intrusive and more locally knowledgeable than conventional auditors ([FAA Maintenance and Ramp LOSA Implementation Guideline, pp. 11 and 68](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/about/initiatives/maintenance_hf/losa/losa_implementation_guideline.pdf)).

LOSA’s purpose is distribution-level learning: find recurring threats, errors, recoveries, and adaptations across ordinary work. It is not strong evidence that one particular flight claim is true. This distinction matters in the target domain.

### 1.4 Crew monitoring and cross-checking: useful redundancy, with a vigilance limit

FAA guidance calls crew monitoring and cross-checking a last barrier that may break an accident chain, while also noting that humans are poor at continuously monitoring rare errors under both high workload and low-workload boredom/complacency ([FAA AC 120-71A, Appendix 19](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AC120-71A_0.pdf)). Aviation’s answer is not “watch harder”; it is SOPs, explicit role allocation, phase-specific monitoring, and state-based callouts.

### 1.5 Mode awareness and automation surprise: expose current and future behavior

Sarter and Woods describe automation surprise as the automation taking unexpected action, or failing to act as anticipated, because the operator lacks awareness of its current and future status/behavior. Their A320 work treats this as a mismatch in human-machine properties rather than simply pilot carelessness ([NASA NTRS record 20040112190](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040112190)). NASA’s formal-methods program gives concrete design probes: search modeled states for inputs with no visible effect, and check operator-provided scenarios against an executable behavioral model ([NASA Langley, “Mode Awareness”](https://shemesh.larc.nasa.gov/fm/fm-collins-mode.html)).

Mode awareness is adjacent to verification: a verifier cannot interpret evidence correctly if it cannot tell whether the harness is in “planned,” “executing,” “retrying,” “rolled back,” “partially applied,” or “verified” mode.

### 1.6 Just culture and confidential reporting: obtain evidence, but do not confuse it with proof

FAA’s compliance program defines just culture as valuing self-disclosure and considering honest mistakes while retaining strict accountability for intentional or reckless conduct ([FAA Compliance Program](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/cp)). NASA’s ASRS is voluntary, confidential, and non-punitive; reports are de-identified and used to identify hazards and lessons ([NASA ASRS overview](https://www.nasa.gov/human-systems-integration-division/aviation-safety-reporting-system-overview/)).

This solves an incentive problem: people reveal precursors and mistakes they might otherwise conceal. It does not make a report true. FAA’s own voluntary-reporting order describes these reports as subjective and incomplete, usually categorized from the submitter’s perspective ([FAA JO 7200.20A, §§2-1–2-2](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/docs/JO_7200.20A.pdf)).

## 3. Recommended mechanism: Evidence Challenge–Verification–Response (E-CVR) — UNVERIFIED

**Proposal classification — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because useful contract coverage and acceptable false-block rate have not been measured. The experiment in §10 settles both premises.

### 3.1 The claim envelope

Before emitting a consequential completion claim, the agent/harness produces a compact envelope:

```json
{
  "claim": "artifact X exists and contains the required terminator",
  "risk": "high",
  "contract": "file-finalized/v1",
  "expected": {
    "path": "/resolved/absolute/path/X.md",
    "terminator": "UNIT-COMPLETE: X027"
  },
  "action_receipts": ["event:1841", "event:1842"],
  "state": "AWAITING_VERIFICATION"
}
```

The challenge names the variable, like the label-facing phraseology in AC 120-71B. The response contains actual observed values, not a boolean summary:

```json
{
  "contract": "file-finalized/v1",
  "observed": {
    "resolved_path": "/resolved/absolute/path/X.md",
    "stat_type": "regular-file",
    "byte_count": 24193,
    "last_line": "UNIT-COMPLETE: X027",
    "content_sha256": "..."
  },
  "signals": [
    {"kind": "filesystem-metadata", "receipt": "event:1847"},
    {"kind": "independent-content-read", "receipt": "event:1848"}
  ],
  "verdict": "VERIFIED",
  "scope": ["existence", "resolved-target", "terminator"]
}
```

The word `VERIFIED` is forbidden without a scope. A successful write receipt proves an attempted operation returned successfully; it does not by itself prove durable content at the intended resolved path.

### 3.2 Mechanical independence rule

For claims of `done`, `absent`, or `broken`, require two signals that do not share the same failure mode. Examples:

| Claim | Signal A | Signal B | Not independent |
|---|---|---|---|
| File finalized | metadata from resolved path (`stat`) | fresh content read + terminal invariant/hash | running the same `ls` twice |
| Test passed | test runner exit plus structured result count | independently confirm expected test target/collection and no skipped-all condition | trusting “PASS” in agent prose plus the same console output pasted into prose |
| Remote ref updated | mutation API receipt/ID | fresh remote read through a separate GET/list operation | local branch state plus local `git log` |
| Service healthy | process/port ownership | protocol-level request with expected semantic response | two process-list commands reading the same OS table |
| Item absent | validated detector first finds a known-present control | second query through a different index or direct target lookup finds no target | two spellings of one search command |

The verifier should record a small dependency label for each signal (`filesystem-metadata`, `file-content`, `runner-process`, `remote-read-api`, etc.) and reject same-label pairs by default. This is not proof of statistical independence; it is a cheap guard against identical detectors masquerading as corroboration.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on aviation’s use of configuration plus mutual redundancy and the distinct failure modes in the examples above, that dependency-labeled dual evidence will reduce false completion claims more than a second prose critique at similar cost. This is **UNVERIFIED;** the §10 ablation comparing dual probes, single probes, and second-agent review settles it.

### 3.3 Risk-tiered use, not universal ceremony

Borrow aviation’s selectivity:

- **Tier 0 — reversible/read-only:** no gate; retain the event receipt.
- **Tier 1 — ordinary artifact claim:** one fresh postcondition probe; sampled shadow audit.
- **Tier 2 — consequential completion, absence, breakage, external write, or destructive precondition:** short E-CVR contract plus two mechanically independent signals.
- **Tier 3 — irreversible or human-impacting action:** E-CVR before and after action; require explicit authority separately. Verification never manufactures authorization.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the checklist-length findings and the large difference in consequence among agent actions, that gating every tool call would create needless latency and checklist fatigue. The exact tier thresholds are **UNVERIFIED;** settle them by plotting defect yield and latency per contract during the §10 shadow phase.

### 3.4 State machine and mode annunciation

Expose one explicit claim state:

```text
PROPOSED → ACTING → AWAITING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED(scope)
                     ↘ INCONCLUSIVE(reason)
                     ↘ CONTRADICTED(observation)
          ↘ ABORTED | PARTIAL | ROLLED_BACK
```

The user-facing answer must not collapse `PARTIAL`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, or `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` into “done.” The state is computed from receipts and contract results, not chosen freely by the agent. Also display the active target, authority scope, and whether a retry changed either. This is the direct response to automation-surprise research: reveal current mode and the next allowed transition.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on NASA’s mode-awareness findings and the analogous hidden state in multi-step tool use, that explicit state annunciation will prevent some mistaken finalization and retry-on-wrong-target errors. This is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it by injecting interrupted, timed-out, partial, and target-switched operations and measuring incorrect `VERIFIED` transitions.

## 4. Recorder-derived evidence receipts — UNVERIFIED

**Proposal classification — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The tool/hook bus should emit a compact append-only receipt per action and verification probe:

- event ID, monotonic sequence, and timestamps;
- tool identity and operation class;
- resolved target or target digest (with secret-safe redaction);
- authority/approval context;
- exit/transport status distinct from semantic status;
- stable output/artifact digest and returned remote object ID where available;
- parent event and retry lineage;
- claim-state transition caused by the event.

The receipt is the FDR analogue, not a full observability platform. Raw content need not be duplicated; pointers and hashes preserve linkage while limiting secret exposure. The agent can cite a receipt but cannot mint one.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on NTSB’s use of recorder parameters to reconstruct events and FOQA’s use of routine digital flight data, that substrate receipts will make post-run reconstruction materially more reliable than free-form summaries. Whether the existing event bus exposes enough pre-redaction target information without leaking secrets is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it with a prototype that evaluates 100 heterogeneous tool events for reconstructability and secret exposure.

What this catches:

- a claim refers to path/repository/account A while the action receipt resolved to B;
- an agent narrates success after transport success but before semantic acknowledgement;
- a retry succeeds on a different target and the lineage is hidden in prose;
- a claimed test result came from an unintended target, zero-test collection, or stale output;
- a final answer cites an action event but no postcondition event;
- an operation was partial or rolled back after the success line the agent noticed.

These are predicted detection targets, not claims about observed local defects. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the direct mismatch each target creates between receipts and claim envelopes, that the mechanism detects them when the relevant fields are observable. It is **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on the semantic limits of substrate data, that receipts alone detect a subtly wrong design, a plausible but false factual answer, or a requirement the contract author forgot.

## 6. Blameless/just-culture incident learning: useful support, not verifier — UNVERIFIED

**Proposal classification — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a structured, non-punitive incident record whenever E-CVR returns `CONTRADICTED` or `INCONCLUSIVE`:

- intended action and actual observed state;
- environmental and interface contributors;
- contract or detector weakness;
- recovery that worked;
- proposed system-level countermeasure;
- explicit separation of mistake, risky shortcut, and intentional policy bypass.

For an autonomous model, “blamelessness” has little direct incentive effect; the model does not fear discipline. It can matter for humans who curate prompts, tools, and runs. The direct verification benefit is therefore **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on ASRS being subjective self-report and the absence of a model fear incentive. The organizational learning benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, based on FAA/NASA evidence that protected reporting exposes otherwise hidden precursors, but this target-domain benefit is **UNVERIFIED;** settle it by measuring report volume, unique precursor yield, and remediation closure before/after protected reporting.

Never upgrade self-report to evidence. A post-mortem explains a mismatch; recorder receipts and fresh probes establish whether the mismatch exists.

## 7. What not to borrow

### 7.1 “Two agents agree, therefore verified”

Reject. Aviation mutual redundancy assumes separately attentive humans who look at an external indication. Two agents with the same model family, context, prompt framing, and faulty tool output have strongly correlated epistemic inputs. Agreement can increase confidence only after measuring conditional independence; it cannot substitute for a fresh state probe.

### 7.2 A checklist for every possible requirement

Reject. Aviation research itself documents the long-checklist tradeoff. A universal completion schema would become an over-strict fan-out interface, encourage boilerplate, and eventually be bypassed. Use short contracts keyed to claim type and risk.

### 7.3 Full cockpit-style continuous monitoring

Reject for the first design. Continuous semantic oversight would require a second orchestration runtime or large service and would violate the anti-overfit gate without evidence that small transition probes are inadequate. The minimal mechanism intercepts claim transitions, reads existing receipts, and runs bounded postcondition probes.

### 7.4 Accident-investigation bureaucracy

Reject. NTSB’s party system separates factual reports from later analysis and asks parties to verify factual accuracy, which is a useful epistemic distinction ([NTSB, “The Party System”](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/process/Pages/partysystem.aspx)). But importing boards, formal parties, and manual docket production would not fit agent-task frequency or latency. Borrow the separation of **facts → interpretations → recommendations**, not the institution.

### 7.5 Treating absence of an alert as safety

Reject. Neither aviation recorders nor agent hooks cover uninstrumented variables. Every verdict must name detector coverage; `no contradiction observed` is not `verified`. Before any absence claim, validate the detector on a known-present control and add a mechanically different lookup.

## 8. Concrete evidence contracts and catches

| Agent claim | Challenge | Actual-value response | Independent corroboration | Failure caught |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| “I wrote the requested artifact.” | Resolved path? type? required terminal invariant? | canonical path, type, bytes, last line, hash | fresh content read separate from mutation receipt | wrong path, directory/file confusion, truncation, missing terminator, self-report after failed write |
| “Tests pass.” | Which test target and how many collected/executed/skipped? | runner ID, target, counts, exit, duration | independent discovery/collection or artifact/test-result parse | wrong suite, zero tests, all skipped, stale PASS text |
| “The remote change exists.” | Which remote object and revision? | server-returned ID, revision, current state | fresh read API distinct from mutation call | local-only change, wrong account/project, eventual rollback, stale client state |
| “The service is healthy.” | Which endpoint and semantic invariant? | resolved address, status, schema/value, latency | process/port ownership or separate dependency check | running-but-broken process, wrong service on port, superficial 200 |
| “No matching item exists.” | Can the detector find the control? which scopes searched? | control hit, searched scopes, target miss | direct target lookup or second index | broken detector, ignored scope, permission-filtered false absence |
| “The task is complete.” | Which acceptance claims are machine-observable? | per-claim verdict and exact scope | requirement-to-contract coverage report | partial work hidden by a global success label |
| “The citation supports the claim.” | What exact proposition and source location? | URL/document ID, section/lines, extracted bounded support | second source or direct primary-data check for consequential claims | citation exists but is irrelevant, circular summaries, unsupported extrapolation |

The citation contract is weaker than a filesystem contract: semantic entailment may still require a model or human. It should therefore emit `SUPPORTED(scope, confidence)`, not `VERIFIED`, unless the claim is a direct machine-checkable datum. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, based on open-ended language and source interpretation, that a small deterministic hook can verify arbitrary semantic entailment without human/model judgment.

## 9. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

The minimal viable implementation is one transition hook plus a small contract registry:

```text
tool/hook event bus ──> receipt normalizer ──> claim-transition hook
                                                │
                                  contract lookup + risk tier
                                                │
                                  bounded fresh probe(s)
                                                │
                            scoped verdict + evidence receipt IDs
```

No new scheduler is needed. The acting runtime remains authoritative for orchestration. The hook only blocks or annotates a completion transition when a configured contract applies. Unknown claim types return `UNVERIFIABLE`, not an invented check.

**Anti-overfit gate result: passes.** The proposal rides the existing hook/tool/skill/MCP bus and introduces none of the rejected component classes. Its value derives from independent measurement and explicit state transitions, not from a more elaborate reasoning scaffold. If a prototype cannot remain approximately under 200 lines of executable logic excluding declarative contracts and tests, split contracts into existing skills rather than promote the hook into a service. Whether that size target is attainable is **UNVERIFIED;** the settling experiment is implementing the three contracts `file-finalized/v1`, `tests-passed/v1`, and `remote-object/v1` and counting executable lines plus dependencies.

The likely performance cost is one or two fresh reads at completion, not another full reasoning trajectory. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the bounded probe design, that median latency overhead remains below a second for local file/test-metadata claims but not for remote systems. This is **UNVERIFIED;** instrument p50/p95 latency separately for local, process, and remote contracts during shadow deployment.

## 10. Settling experiment: replay, inject, shadow, ablate

### Phase A — build a blinded labeled set

Select at least 300 completed runs stratified across file writes, tests, process/service state, remote mutations, absence claims, and citation claims. A human labels only the concrete acceptance claims using raw state/artifacts, not the agent conclusion. Seed an additional mutation set with:

- wrong resolved path or wrong repository/account;
- success exit with missing semantic postcondition;
- zero-test/all-skipped suite;
- stale output reused after a retry;
- partial write and truncated final line;
- timeout followed by eventual success or eventual rollback;
- broken absence detector that also fails on a known-present control;
- same-target dual probes versus genuinely different evidence paths;
- unsupported citation whose URL resolves;
- hidden mode change during retry.

### Phase B — compare four arms

1. Agent self-report only.
2. A second agent critiques the same transcript.
3. One fresh deterministic postcondition probe.
4. E-CVR with recorder receipt plus two dependency-distinct signals where required.

Measure precision, recall, false-block rate, unresolved rate, latency, token cost, and conditional overlap of errors. Predeclare that E-CVR advances only if it materially improves detection over arm 3 without an operationally unacceptable false-block rate. The numeric acceptance threshold is a product choice and is intentionally not fabricated here.

### Phase C — shadow normal operations

Run contracts without blocking for 30 days. Manually adjudicate every `CONTRADICTED` result and a random sample of `VERIFIED` results. This validates both the detector and its false-negative estimate. Use a known-present positive control before crediting any absence detector.

### Phase D — limited enforcement

Enable blocking only for contract/risk cells with measured high precision. Preserve `INCONCLUSIVE` as a first-class result. Re-run injected cases after every contract or tool-bus change.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the experiment’s labeled ground truth, mutation testing, ablation, positive controls, and shadow adjudication being mechanically distinct signals, that this design can distinguish “useful transfer” from an attractive analogy. The remaining uncertainty is ecological validity: injected defects may not match live defect distribution. The live shadow phase addresses but cannot eliminate that limitation.

## 11. Adversarial failure analysis

1. **Goodharting the contract.** An agent may satisfy the checked projection while violating the real requirement. Example: the terminator exists but the report is nonsense. Mitigation: never let a representative item stand for a broader semantic claim unless the implication is mechanically valid. FAA permits representative items only where the indication entails completion of the represented subset ([FAA AC 120-71B, §5.2.4, PDF p. 19](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_120-71b.pdf)). It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on proxy gaming in any projection-based check, that poorly scoped contracts will produce false assurance; require scope-labelled verdicts and mutation tests.

2. **Correlated detectors.** `stat` and `ls` may share the same filesystem metadata and permission failure. Mitigation: dependency labels, known-present controls, and direct content/semantic probes.

3. **Verifier changes state.** Some “read” APIs consume messages, refresh tokens, trigger builds, or alter access timestamps. Mitigation: contracts declare probe side effects and forbid destructive verification. It is **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on most but not all read APIs being operationally non-mutating, that this is common; the premise is **UNVERIFIED** and is settled by side-effect audits per connector.

4. **Eventual consistency.** A fresh remote GET immediately after mutation may return stale state. Mitigation: bounded backoff with an `INCONCLUSIVE(timeout)` terminal state and recorded consistency window, not an infinite retry or fabricated success.

5. **Recorder tampering or omission.** If the acting model can forge receipts, or the hook omits direct mutations, the FDR analogy fails. Mitigation: receipt issuance below the model layer and coverage tests with known tool events. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the definition of independent evidence, that agent-authored receipts would provide no meaningful independence.

6. **Secret leakage.** Capturing full commands, headers, or outputs can expose credentials. Mitigation: stable hashes, typed identifiers, and allowlisted evidence fields; raw sensitive payload stays in the existing protected store. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on tool events commonly containing sensitive arguments, that naive full-event recording creates unacceptable exposure; prototype with seeded secrets and scan receipts before deployment.

7. **Checklist fatigue by automation.** Even automated gates impose latency and confusing blocks. Mitigation: risk tiers, short contracts, sampling, and deletion of low-yield checks. This follows aviation’s warning that long lists invite poor use.

8. **Semantic overclaim.** A passing test does not prove the user’s intent was met; a valid citation does not prove an inference. Mitigation: scope-labelled evidence and `UNVERIFIABLE`/`SUPPORTED` distinctions.

9. **Automation surprise in the verifier itself.** Hidden contract selection or silent fallback can make the user believe a claim was verified when no contract ran. Mitigation: display contract ID, version, active mode, scope, and receipt IDs in the final outcome.

10. **Verification becomes authorization.** Evidence that an action would work or did work does not establish permission. Mitigation: keep authority checks orthogonal and earlier in the transition graph.

## 12. Final judgment

The aviation borrow survives adversarial comparison, but in a narrower form than the slogan “aviation uses checklists.” The transferable invariant is:

> At a consequential transition, challenge a small set of critical variables, observe the underlying system through an evidence path distinct from the action/narrative, answer with actual values, preserve substrate receipts, and learn from sampled normal operations.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on direct structural correspondence and the cited aviation mechanisms, that this is worth prototyping as a small completion-transition hook. It is not yet evidence that the prototype will have adequate contract coverage, low false-block rate, or acceptable latency; those premises are **UNVERIFIED**, and the replay/injection/shadow/ablation experiment in §10 is required before enforcement.

The analogy explicitly does **not** support a second agent runtime, continuous semantic surveillance, universal schemas, or treating blameless narratives as truth. Those additions would discard the very features that make aviation’s mechanism work: external indications, bounded critical items, independent evidence channels, and clear separation between observation and interpretation.
