# F011 — Frontier research: uncertainty quantification and abstention in LLM agents

**Unit:** F011  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** What is established, promising, or hype in uncertainty quantification (UQ) and abstention for tool-using LLM agents, and what this owner's harness should adopt.  
**Mode:** Research and design only. The subject tree was read-only; this artifact is the only write.  

## Executive verdict

The frontier does **not** support putting a universal numeric confidence meter in front of an autonomous agent and treating it as probability of correctness. It does support three narrower propositions:

1. On labeled, sufficiently stable task distributions, post-hoc confidence signals can rank errors and can be calibrated; the result is method-, model-, prompt-, task-, and distribution-dependent. Verbal confidence, self-evaluation, sample consistency, token likelihood, and semantic entropy each work on some benchmarks and each fail on others. The clearest cross-paper result is not that one estimator wins, but that held-out calibration and deployment-facing risk–coverage evaluation are indispensable ([Kadavath et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221); [Tian et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/); [Xiong et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063); [Farquhar et al., Nature 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)).
2. Abstention is a **decision policy**, not a sentiment in prose. It must trade residual risk against coverage and the cost of asking, delaying, verifying, or escalating. For agents, it must be scored over the trajectory: saying “I cannot proceed” after an irreversible tool call is not abstention. The very recent AgentAbstain preprint operationalizes this distinction with paired act/abstain tasks and finds post-hoc abstention in real tool trajectories, but its numerical results are **UNVERIFIED outside the authors' v1 study; independent reproduction on the released environments would settle it** ([AgentAbstain, arXiv v1, 11 July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059); [project and code page](https://agentabstain.github.io/)).
3. For this harness, observable preconditions and receipts are stronger than introspective confidence. A missing authorization, contradictory instruction, dead tool, failed test, absent source, or stale receipt is an actionable uncertainty state. The harness already has the relevant lifecycle bus and a receipt-aware completion gate, while its file named `compute-confidence-gate` is actually a deterministic long-compute checklist, not a calibrated UQ implementation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/compute-confidence-gate/gate.py:1-21,54-81,212-240`; `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2000-2137,2197-2228`).

**Recommendation in one line:** build a small, typed, pre-side-effect abstention policy on the existing hook and event-ledger bus; log act/clarify/verify/escalate/stop outcomes; evaluate it on paired local cases; retain numeric or semantic UQ only as an advisory shadow signal until it demonstrates lower risk at useful coverage on held-out owner tasks.

This direction is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve safety and epistemic honesty without substantial harness complexity because it composes native PreToolUse control, current event receipts, and deterministic missing-precondition checks; the evidence is the native hook contract, established selective-prediction theory, the local receipt implementation, and early agent-trajectory evidence. The exact reduction in harmful or wasted actions is **UNVERIFIED; a prospective paired replay described in Recommendation 3 would settle it**.

## Scope, terminology, and evidence standard

### What “uncertainty” means here

The literature often collapses distinct objects into one word. This report keeps them separate:

- **Predictive confidence:** a score intended to approximate `P(answer/action is correct | available information)`.
- **Calibration:** across cases assigned confidence `p`, about fraction `p` are correct. Calibration is a population property, not proof that a particular answer is right ([Guo et al., ICML 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)).
- **Failure discrimination:** whether wrong cases rank as more uncertain than right cases. AUROC, AUPR, and risk–coverage measures address ranking/selective performance, not the same property as calibration. Ding et al. show why ECE and AUROC alone can mislead and recommend area under the risk–coverage curve when underlying predictors differ ([Ding et al., CVPRW 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w1/Ding_Revisiting_the_Evaluation_of_Uncertainty_Estimation_and_Its_Application_to_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf)).
- **Ambiguity / aleatoric uncertainty:** multiple actions or answers may remain acceptable even with complete information; asking the owner may be the only resolution.
- **Epistemic uncertainty:** relevant facts, evidence, capability, or knowledge are missing and may be reducible by reading, searching, testing, or calling a tool.
- **Operational uncertainty:** tool availability, permissions, environment state, side effects, or the success criterion is unknown. This is central for agents and mostly absent from QA-only UQ work.
- **Abstention:** withholding the proposed answer or action and choosing an alternative such as clarify, verify, defer, escalate, or stop. In an agent, the non-action must occur **before** an irreversible side effect.

### Evidence hierarchy used

“Established” below means replicated conceptually across more than one primary study or supported by mature statistical theory; it does **not** mean universal across frontier models or this owner's workload. Peer-reviewed proceedings and official documentation carry more weight than arXiv-only claims. Very recent 2025-2026 preprints are kept in “promising” unless an older independent line of evidence supports the same narrow claim.

The pinned audit findings are claims, not facts. I used them only to locate source code, then checked the unpacked subject. ## 1. Established

### 1.1 Calibration, discrimination, and selective risk are different targets

A confidence system can be calibrated yet useless for choosing which individual cases to reject: for example, the same base-rate score for every case can be calibrated but cannot rank errors. Conversely, a score can rank cases well and still need recalibration before its numeric values can be read as probabilities. This separation follows the definitions in calibration and selective-classification work, and is empirically important because common ECE binning can hide high-confidence errors while AUROC can be misleading when comparing predictors of different base accuracy ([Guo et al., 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html); [Ding et al., 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w1/Ding_Revisiting_the_Evaluation_of_Uncertainty_Estimation_and_Its_Application_to_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf)).

Therefore an agent-abstention evaluation needs, at minimum:

- task success/error at full coverage;
- coverage, the fraction on which the agent acts without escalation;
- selective risk among acted cases at several coverage levels;
- false abstention on cases that were safe and sufficiently specified;
- false action on cases that required abstention;
- calibration diagnostics if numeric probabilities are exposed;
- latency, token/tool cost, and owner-interruption cost;
- trajectory-side-effect correctness, not only final prose.

Risk–coverage is not a decorative metric. Selective classification has long formalized the trade: reject more cases to reduce error among retained cases, with the operating point chosen against the costs of error and rejection ([Geifman & El-Yaniv, NeurIPS 2017](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html); [SelectiveNet, ICML 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html)). Those papers are classification studies, not LLM-agent trials; what transfers is the decision and evaluation framework, not their numerical thresholds.

### 1.2 LLMs expose useful uncertainty signal, but no elicitation is universally reliable

Several independent primary studies establish that model behavior contains nonzero signal about correctness:

- Kadavath et al. found encouraging calibration and scaling for `P(True)`—asking a model to assess a proposed answer—and found that considering multiple samples improves self-evaluation. They also found `P(IK)` struggled to remain calibrated on new tasks ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)).
- Tian et al. found that verbalized confidence from RLHF models such as ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude was usually better calibrated than conditional token probabilities on TriviaQA, SciQ, and TruthfulQA, often cutting ECE by roughly 50% relative—not eliminating error ([ACL record and abstract](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/)).
- Xiong et al. evaluated verbal confidence, repeated-sample consistency, and aggregation across five dataset types and five LLMs. Models were generally overconfident; consistency and prompting helped, but no method consistently won and all struggled on difficult professional-knowledge tasks. Their reported white-box versus black-box failure-prediction AUROC example was 0.605 versus 0.522, evidence of signal but not a production-quality oracle ([ICLR 2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)).

The established conclusion is modest: introspective and behavioral signals can improve ranking or calibration on measured distributions. The evidence rejects both extremes—“models know nothing about when they are wrong” and “just ask for a probability and trust it.”

### 1.3 Semantic diversity detects confabulation, not all wrongness

Semantic entropy clusters sampled answers by meaning before measuring dispersion. This corrects a real defect in token- or surface-level entropy: multiple paraphrases of one meaning should not imply uncertainty merely because their strings differ. Farquhar et al. report average AUROC 0.790 over 30 task/model combinations versus 0.691 for naive entropy, 0.698 for `P(True)`, and 0.687 for embedding regression; they used ten generations for the estimator. They also report stable 0.78-0.81 AUROC over the studied LLaMA, Falcon, and Mistral scales and a black-box discrete variant for GPT-4 ([Nature paper, results and methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)).

Equally important, the paper explicitly says the method does **not** address systematic wrong answers, systematic reasoning errors, misleading objectives, or confident errors learned from training. A model that repeats the same false claim has low semantic dispersion. The study's sentence-length correctness labels also relied on GPT-4 comparison to references, with a manual validation subset, and the paragraph study used 150 manually checked claims from 21 generated biographies. The result is strong evidence for detecting a specific class—confabulation—in QA-like generation, not a general guarantee for tool-using trajectories.

### 1.4 A refusal or low-confidence sentence is not agentic abstention

Tool-using systems have two channels: words and environment effects. A final message can claim restraint even after a transfer, cancellation, deletion, or disclosure has occurred. AgentAbstain's paired framework explicitly scores both action behavior and verbal behavior and reports 115 post-hoc cases in its evaluated trajectories, 98 of which a prose-only judge would have missed. This numerical claim is from a **very recent, non-peer-reviewed v1 preprint and is UNVERIFIED; reproducing the public benchmark with frozen harness/model versions would settle it** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059); [project page](https://agentabstain.github.io/)).

The broader principle is established independently of that benchmark: a pre-action control point is required to prevent a side effect. Claude Code's official hook lifecycle fires `PreToolUse` before every tool call and allows `allow`, `deny`, `ask`, or `defer`; `ask` prompts the owner, while `deny` prevents execution ([official hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks#pretooluse-decision-control)). A Stop-time uncertainty linter cannot undo an already executed tool.

### 1.5 Conformal guarantees are real but conditional and usually marginal

Conformal prediction is not hype in its proper scope. With fresh calibration data and exchangeable test cases, it can provide finite-sample marginal coverage without assumptions about the predictive model. “Marginal” means coverage for a random future case from the same distribution, not equally strong coverage for every subgroup or every individual input. The standard introduction states the same-distribution condition and notes that basic methods can under-cover hard subgroups and over-cover easy ones ([Angelopoulos & Bates, current tutorial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)).

Conformal Language Modeling extends the idea to sampled response sets, calibrating stopping and rejection rules so the set contains at least one acceptable response under its formal conditions; it demonstrated applications in open-domain QA, summarization, and radiology reports ([Quach et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/31421b112e5f7faf4fc577b74e45dab2-Abstract-Conference.html)). KnowNo applied conformal prediction to LLM robot planners, returning candidate action sets and asking a human when ambiguity remains, across simulated and real robot setups ([Ren et al., CoRL 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)).

What is established is the theorem under its assumptions and the feasibility of narrow applications. It is **not** established that an evolving personal-agent workload—with adaptive prompts, model upgrades, changing tools, correlated multi-step trajectories, and sparse labels—is exchangeable with a historical calibration set.

### 1.6 Evidence and uncertainty are complements, not substitutes

An executable verifier can establish a narrower proposition—tests pass, a file exists, a source supports a claim, a service returned success—more directly than model confidence. It cannot prove untested properties, and imperfect verifiers create their own false-positive risk, but where a task-native check exists it should dominate introspection for that checked claim.

This harness already embodies that distinction. `ClaimWarningModule` detects completion language, queries fresh typed receipts after the last mutation, and injects when evidence is absent (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2000-2137,2197-2228`). The local file named `compute-confidence-gate`, by contrast, detects a narrow list of long-compute command strings and asks for registry, live-state, parameters, cost, success criterion, and a high/medium/low label (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/compute-confidence-gate/gate.py:129-163,212-240`). It is a useful preflight forcing function, but its three-bin self-rating is not trained, held-out calibrated, or connected to an empirical risk–coverage curve; calling it “confidence” overstates what it measures.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 Paired, trajectory-native abstention evaluation

AgentAbstain pairs a should-act task with a minimally perturbed should-abstain task, across query ambiguity, conflicting constraints, insufficient tool capability, runtime state, and high-stakes actions. This paired construction helps prevent an always-act or always-refuse system from looking good. The paper reports 263 pairs, 42 executable sandboxes, 17 frontier models in four harnesses, and best paired accuracy of 59.5% ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059)).

It is **likely (55-80%)** that paired local tests will expose harness failures missed by ordinary task-success evals. Evidence: the design controls the act/abstain trade and observes side effects, while selective-prediction theory requires measuring both risk and coverage. **UNVERIFIED for this owner's tasks; create 30-50 owner-derived pairs and compare current versus proposed policy to settle it.** Confidence is not higher because the agent-specific paper is eight days old, v1, not peer-reviewed, partly uses semantic LLM judging, and this unit did not verify an independent reproduction.

### 2.2 Semantic consistency as a black-box escalation signal

Repeated generation plus semantic clustering is API-compatible and can identify variable confabulations without model logits. It is **likely (55-80%)** to add useful signal for high-stakes, open-ended factual synthesis where no deterministic verifier exists. Evidence: peer-reviewed cross-model QA results outperform several baselines, and Xiong et al. independently found repeated-sample consistency helpful ([Farquhar et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0); [Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)). **UNVERIFIED for long research artifacts and agent plans; a shadow experiment should sample five independent claim-level answers, cluster them, then measure AURC against human/source adjudication.**

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to justify always-on use across this harness. Evidence: the Nature method used ten generations, targets confabulation rather than systematic error, and depends on semantic entailment/clustering quality. This workload contains many deterministic coding, filesystem, reverse-engineering, and operations tasks where a test or direct read is cheaper and stronger.

### 2.3 Hybrid evidence fusion

A small policy combining objective signals—fresh receipts, tool failure state, source disagreement, missing authorization—with a model consistency or self-evaluation score is **very likely (80-90%)** to rank risk better than a naked verbal confidence score. Evidence: no black-box elicitation method consistently wins in Xiong et al.; semantic entropy and `P(True)` detect different failures; this harness already records objective outcome classes. **UNVERIFIED quantitatively; a preregistered ablation over held-out paired cases (receipts only vs. self-score only vs. fused) would settle the local gain.**

The promising part is fusion as a compact feature rule on the existing bus. A learned calibrator is not yet justified because the harness lacks a verified, prospective label stream and models/tools drift.

### 2.4 Conformal action sets and risk control

Returning a set of plausible next actions and escalating when the set has size other than one is an attractive analogue of KnowNo. It is **likely (55-80%)** to be useful in a stable, repeated subdomain with clear acceptable-action labels—e.g., a fixed deployment runbook or a bounded computational workflow. Evidence: KnowNo shows real/simulated planner feasibility and conformal theory supplies finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability ([KnowNo](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html); [conformal tutorial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). **UNVERIFIED for this harness; freeze one task family, collect at least a prospective calibration set, predefine loss, then test coverage under a later time slice.**

It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that one conformal threshold over the owner's entire task distribution would retain its advertised guarantee. Evidence: the workload spans unrelated domains and changes models, prompts, tools, and environments; standard conformal coverage assumes calibration and future cases are exchangeable or handled by a justified shift-aware extension. A global wrapper would pool incomparable losses and conceal subgroup failure.

### 2.5 Native `ask` and `defer` as abstention actuators

Using Claude Code's native `permissionDecision: "ask"` for selected uncertain, consequential tool calls is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent some premature side effects because the host pauses before execution and surfaces a reason to the owner ([official hook semantics](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks#pretooluse-decision-control)). **UNVERIFIED under this owner's `bypassPermissions` settings and cross-provider dispatcher; a synthetic, reversible smoke test must prove the prompt appears and the tool remains unexecuted before relying on it.** The local gate source itself notes this unresolved interaction (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/compute-confidence-gate/gate.py:30-40,100-108`).

### 2.6 Teaching models to abstain

Fine-tuning or reinforcement learning can optimize an explicit abstention policy, and recent work continues to report gains. For this harness, adoption is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat a small external policy in the near term. Evidence: the owner uses changing closed and frontier models, while training creates model-specific maintenance, data, and evaluation burdens; the older established results already show prompting/calibration behavior is model- and task-dependent. **UNVERIFIED; only a matched-cost local experiment against an external hook policy would settle it.** A trained critic is also explicitly rejected by the owner's anti-overfit gate, so it is outside the acceptable architecture absent extraordinary evidence.

## 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

### 3.1 “The model says 87%, therefore failure probability is 13%”

**Tell:** a precise number with no frozen model/prompt, held-out calibration set, reliability diagram, Brier/NLL or adaptive calibration diagnostic, and no task-stratified result. Verbal confidence can reduce ECE on selected QA benchmarks, but models are often overconfident and methods reverse rank across tasks ([Tian et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/); [Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)). Precision of formatting is not statistical precision.

### 3.2 “Self-consistency is a truth detector”

**Tell:** the system equates agreement among samples with correctness and lacks a consistent-but-wrong test set. Semantic entropy's own discussion excludes systematic errors and misleading behavior ([Farquhar et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)). Correlated samples from one model can confidently repeat the same misconception.

### 3.3 “Our AUROC proves safe abstention”

**Tell:** only AUROC/ECE is reported; there is no risk at the intended coverage, coverage at the required risk, false-action rate, false-abstention rate, or cost-weighted operating point. AUROC averages over thresholds that may never be deployed, and Ding et al. show metric comparisons can be misleading when base predictors differ ([Ding et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w1/Ding_Revisiting_the_Evaluation_of_Uncertainty_Estimation_and_Its_Application_to_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf)).

### 3.4 “Distribution-free guarantee” with the assumptions in small print

**Tell:** no fresh calibration set, no exchangeability argument, no loss definition, no distinction between marginal and conditional coverage, and no drift test. Standard conformal prediction gives powerful finite-sample marginal coverage for a fresh same-distribution point; it can still under-cover hard subgroups, and an uninformative score can yield valid but uselessly large sets ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). “Distribution-free” does not mean “assumption-free” or “valid after arbitrary agent/model/tool changes.”

### 3.5 “The agent abstained” based on final text

**Tell:** evaluation reads the answer but not the tool trace or environment diff. Post-hoc recognition after a bank transfer or deletion is failure, not restraint. The recent AgentAbstain study makes this exact distinction, although its rates remain **UNVERIFIED pending independent benchmark reproduction** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059)).

### 3.6 “More agents make confidence calibrated”

**Tell:** multiple agents share the same model family, prompt, retrieval, or mistaken premise; disagreement is suppressed to one vote; there is no outcome label; token cost is excluded. A workshop demonstration of multi-agent calibration is publication, not establishment, and generic model judging has known hard-task limitations. Panels may expose alternatives, but votes do not manufacture independent evidence. The owner's anti-overfit gate also rejects a trained critic and a second orchestration runtime.

### 3.7 “Always ask when uncertain”

**Tell:** refusal or clarification rate is celebrated without paired should-act cases or owner-interruption cost. A system can appear safe by doing nothing. Selective prediction is explicitly a risk–coverage trade, and useful abstention must preserve autonomy on clear, low-risk tasks ([Geifman & El-Yaniv](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html)).

### 3.8 “One confidence threshold for every domain”

**Tell:** coding, medical research, shell operations, creative writing, reverse engineering, and scientific simulation share one number despite different labels, costs, verifiers, and error types. Xiong et al. found every elicitation strategy struggled on harder professional tasks, and `P(IK)` calibration degraded on new tasks in Kadavath et al. A global threshold hides domain shifts rather than controlling them.

## 4. Specific things this harness should adopt

Every proposal below uses exactly one required implementation tag and explicitly applies the anti-overfit gate. Expected effects are forward-looking and therefore carry confidence bands and settling experiments.

### Recommendation 1 — NATIVE: put native `ask` in front of known consequential actions — UNVERIFIED under current permission mode

**NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Use static, narrowly scoped native ask rules for known consequential command/tool families—force push, external send/publish, destructive cloud/database mutations, irreversible account actions—where owner confirmation is appropriate regardless of model confidence. Do not trigger by scanning arbitrary prose; match the tool verb and structured arguments. The official host supports PreToolUse `ask`, shows the reason to the user, and applies precedence `deny > defer > ask > allow` ([official documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks#pretooluse-decision-control)).

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent some premature irreversible acts with little code because enforcement occurs before execution and uses a native mechanism. **UNVERIFIED under the owner's `bypassPermissions` combination and for Codex/Antigravity; run a reversible canary whose side effect is an append to a temporary file, reject at the prompt, and independently verify both prompt receipt and unchanged file.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It uses the native permission/hook bus, adds no runtime, memory store, critic, SaaS, or guardrail framework. Keep rules few and semantic to the tool/argument; D1 shows payload-wide prose scanning causes self-amplifying false positives (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:7-71`).

### Recommendation 2 — BUILD-SMALL: add a typed pre-action abstention module — UNVERIFIED local thresholds

**BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add one shared PreToolUse module that evaluates only high-consequence actions and returns one of:

```text
ACT       required facts and authority observed
VERIFY    a cheap read/test/tool call can settle a named premise
CLARIFY   two materially different owner intents remain
ESCALATE  action is consequential and authorization/precondition is missing
STOP      known contraindication or contradiction makes the action wrong
```

The module should not ask the model for a free-floating percentage. It should inspect typed, observable predicates:

- authorization scope present/missing;
- target resolved to a concrete non-broad resource;
- reversibility and blast radius;
- contradictory instructions or state;
- required tool available versus failed/dead;
- fresh source/read/receipt present after the last mutation;
- cheaper settling probe available;
- known deterministic contraindication matched in executable fields.

For `VERIFY`, inject the exact cheapest experiment and allow only that read-only action. For `CLARIFY`/`ESCALATE`, use native `ask` where proven; otherwise deny with actionable context before the side effect. For `STOP`, block only deterministic known-bad conditions. Emit `reason_code`, predicate values, intended action fingerprint, and decision to the existing event ledger—never raw secrets.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to outperform the current “high/medium/low, honestly” checklist on consequential tool decisions because it replaces uncalibrated introspection with falsifiable state and a typed action. Evidence: native pre-action control, the local receipt bus, mature selective-decision framing, and the early trajectory benchmark. **UNVERIFIED on the owner's distribution; Recommendation 3's paired replay is the promotion gate.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is one composable module in `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py` and the existing dispatcher, not a second orchestrator or policy daemon. It reuses the ledger rather than adding a vector database/KG or durable engine. It is deliberately rule-small: only consequences and observable missing preconditions, not a general “AI safety” framework.

### Recommendation 3 — BUILD-SMALL: create a paired abstention golden set and promotion gate — UNVERIFIED case count sufficiency

**BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Construct 30-50 initial pairs from this owner's real corrections and failures. Each pair shares a task and environment but changes one fact:

- explicit authorization present vs. absent;
- safe path vs. broad/destructive target;
- live tool vs. dead/disconnected tool;
- sufficient schema vs. ambiguous/mismatched schema;
- fresh read/receipt vs. stale or missing evidence;
- compatible constraints vs. a contradiction discovered mid-run;
- reversible draft vs. external publication/send;
- adequate capability vs. missing required tool.

Replay in a sandbox and record:

```text
paired_accuracy = both act and abstain side correct
false_action_rate = acts on should-abstain cases
false_abstain_rate = refuses/asks on should-act cases
post_hoc_rate = side effect occurred before abstention signal
selective_risk@coverage
owner_interruptions / 100 tasks
settling_probe_success_rate
latency, tokens, tool calls
```

Require two independent signals for side-effect success/absence: tool return plus environment diff/state, with a known-present canary validating each detector. This directly incorporates the owner's honesty instrumentation and D2 (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:107-130`).

A 30-50-pair suite is **likely (55-80%)** to catch high-value regressions but **UNVERIFIED as statistically sufficient; sequentially add cases until confidence intervals around false-action and false-abstention rates are decision-useful, and retain a never-tuned holdout.** The pair construction is supported by AgentAbstain, while the exact local sample size is a pragmatic seed rather than a published optimum.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is data plus a thin evaluator on the existing eval/replay path. It adds no orchestration runtime or learned critic. Prefer owner-correction cases and deterministic environment assertions over LLM-generated/judged cases.
