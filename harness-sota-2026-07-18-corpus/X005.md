# X005 — Cross-domain borrow: clinical medicine → finite context over a long horizon

## Executive finding

The useful transfer is **not** “let the model diagnose its context.” That would be a decorative metaphor. The useful transfer is a decision discipline that clinical medicine uses when it must act under uncertainty while information, interventions, and harms accumulate:

1. build a terse **problem representation** and an explicit **differential** rather than collapsing immediately to one explanation;
2. perform **medication reconciliation at every transition**, treating unexplained omissions, additions, and changes as discrepancies to resolve;
3. model **iatrogenic harm and contraindications**, because an otherwise useful intervention can become harmful in combination or in a changed patient state; and
4. estimate **absolute benefit and absolute harm over a defined horizon**, rather than accepting a context item because it sounds relevant.

The combined translation is a **context regimen**: a small, typed, transition-reconciled working set whose entries have an indication, scope, provenance, expiry/review trigger, competing hypotheses they bear on, and known conflicts. Context is admitted, compressed, refreshed, quarantined, or evicted as an explicit decision; none of those states silently means “forgotten.”

This transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to improve long-horizon agent reliability, based on (a) the close structural match between medication transitions and agent handoffs/compactions, (b) evidence that long-context models do not use all positions uniformly, and (c) clinical evidence that contextualized interaction checking reduces low-value alerts. The exact improvement is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the prospective, randomized context-policy ablation specified in §8**. The confidence is not higher because medicine has externally observable outcomes, calibrated tests, stable drug identities, and accountable clinicians; agent tasks often lack all four.

The strongest single borrow is **reconciliation at transitions**, not differential diagnosis and not NNT. It addresses loss, duplication, and stale-state errors at the exact moments when a long-horizon harness rewrites context. The second strongest is a contraindication registry for mutually incompatible or scope-mismatched context. NNT is valuable only as an evaluation lens; literal item-level NNT is too unstable and interaction-blind to drive online eviction by itself.

No second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is proposed. Every build rides the existing prompt/context assembly, hook, skill, or evaluation bus.

## 1. What clinical medicine has actually solved

Clinical medicine has not solved a generic finite-memory eviction problem. It has developed several mechanisms for choosing which facts remain decision-active, which alternatives must not be prematurely discarded, and when an intervention’s side effects outweigh its benefit. Those mechanisms are separable; their value comes from composing them without pretending they are one algorithm.

### 1.1 Problem representation, illness scripts, and the differential diagnosis

In its own field, a **problem representation** is a compact interpretive summary of the patient’s presentation. It preserves discriminating features such as clinical context, risk factors, and temporal pattern rather than reproducing the entire chart. A **working diagnosis** remains explicitly unconfirmed, and a **differential diagnosis** compares two or more plausible conditions while additional history, examination, or tests confirm or refute them. A 2022 scoping review gives these definitions and describes hypothesis-driven history and examination as a purposeful search for evidence bearing on the differential ([Daniel et al., *Journal of Advanced Nursing*, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9805128/)).

The supporting cognitive structure is the **illness script**: a stored representation linking enabling conditions, the underlying fault/pathophysiology, expected consequences, and management. A new case activates one or more scripts; when several match, the clinician compares alternatives analytically ([ten Cate and Custers, NCBI Bookshelf, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK543763/); [Custers, NCBI Bookshelf, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK543757/?report=printable)). The National Academies’ diagnostic-process model makes the Bayesian aspect explicit: findings revise probabilities, base rates matter, and further information gathering is chosen in light of expected harms, benefits, and preferences ([National Academies, *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care*, 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)).

This is not “keep every possible diagnosis.” A usable differential is pruned by probability and consequence. A low-probability but catastrophic condition may remain “must not miss,” while a plausible alternative may be dropped after a genuinely discriminating negative result. The diagnostic process is iterative: new evidence can reopen an alternative. The safety value is resistance to **premature closure**, the error of accepting a diagnosis before adequate verification; explicit differentials and diagnostic time-outs are among the recommended countermeasures ([StatPearls/NCBI Bookshelf, *Medical Error Prevention and Root Cause Analysis*, updated 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK570638/)).

#### Concrete translation

A harness should maintain a **bounded context problem representation**, not a rolling narrative summary. Its fields should be:

- objective and current success test;
- hard owner constraints and permissions;
- current phase and last irreversible/external effect;
- verified facts, each with source/provenance;
- active hypotheses, each with supporting evidence, disconfirming evidence, probability band, and next discriminating probe;
- “must-not-miss” risks whose severity justifies retention despite low probability;
- unresolved decisions and who has authority to resolve them;
- temporal markers: when each fact became true, last checked, and review/expiry trigger.

The representation should be generated from the full event stream but should not replace it. It is the clinical one-line summary plus problem list, while the raw transcript is the chart.

**Proposal — typed problem representation and live differential — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a compaction/handoff hook that serializes the fields above into a bounded Markdown or JSON block. Require every active hypothesis to name one observation that would lower its probability. Pin only the representation, owner constraints, and must-not-miss risks; retrieve raw evidence on demand by provenance pointer.

This mechanism is **likely (55-80%)** to catch premature narrative collapse—e.g., “tests fail because the implementation is wrong” surviving after evidence points to an environment mismatch—because competing explanations and disconfirming evidence remain explicit. Evidence for the direction comes from medicine’s use of differentials and the known tendency of long-context models to use relevant information less effectively when it is buried in the middle ([Liu et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)). The harness-specific effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting two-cause debugging tasks, compacting midway, and measuring whether the agent preserves and tests the non-leading cause versus a rolling-summary control**.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** to make unsupported closure detectable by a machine check, because “hypothesis without disconfirming observation” is a syntactic invariant. This says detectability, not that the agent will heed the record. Whether detection reduces task failure is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the same randomized ablation and separately report alert compliance**.

### 1.2 Medication reconciliation at vulnerable transitions

In its own field, **medication reconciliation** is a formal process for ensuring accurate and complete medication information transfer at interfaces of care. On admission, clinicians obtain a Best Possible Medication History, compare it with intended orders, identify discrepancies, and resolve whether each change is intentional. Verified information is then transferred to the next provider. WHO identifies admission, internal transfer, discharge, and cross-facility handoff as vulnerable moments ([WHO High 5s Medication Reconciliation Implementation Guide](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/patient-safety/high5s/h5s-guide.pdf)).

At internal transfer, reconciliation accounts for the pre-admission list, the transferring unit’s list, and new orders. The aim is not blind continuity: the new care setting may make a prior drug inappropriate. The clinician intentionally continues, changes, resumes, or discontinues it. Reconciliation is meant to reveal therapeutic duplication, omission, unnecessary medication, and confusion (same WHO guide, §6). WHO’s later transitions-of-care report recommends measuring outstanding **unintentional** discrepancies, not merely whether a reconciliation form was completed ([WHO, *Medication Safety in Transitions of Care*, 2019](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-UHC-SDS-2019.9)).

That distinction is the mechanism worth borrowing: **change is allowed; unexplained change is the defect**.

#### Concrete translation

Long-horizon agents have analogous vulnerable transitions:

- initial prompt → plan;
- exploration → implementation;
- implementation → verification;
- context compaction or model switch;
- primary agent → subagent and return;
- local work → external action;
- interrupted run → resumed run;
- task completion → handoff/report.

At each transition, reconcile three lists:

1. **prior active context regimen** — constraints, decisions, facts, hypotheses, commitments, and pending verification;
2. **observed event record** — what actually happened since the last transition, with tool-result provenance;
3. **proposed next-phase regimen** — what the next model call will receive.

Every difference must be classified `continue`, `add`, `change`, `suspend`, `evict-to-chart`, or `retire`, with a reason and source event. `Evict-to-chart` means removed from immediate prompt but retrievable; `retire` means known obsolete or disproven. An unclassified difference blocks automatic compaction but need not block all work.

**Proposal — context medication reconciliation hook — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement a deterministic diff over stable item IDs at compaction and handoff. Emit only discrepancies and require the next context assembler to carry forward unresolved ones. Store the reconciliation record alongside the existing transcript/event log; do not create a new persistence service.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch silent omission, duplication, and resurrection of stale instructions at handoffs, because those are direct analogues of the discrepancies the diff enumerates. The evidence is the structural identity of the compared sets and WHO’s explicit transition process. Whether these discrepancies are a major source of this owner’s context failures is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by shadow-running the hook for 200 real transitions and adjudicating a stratified sample against raw transcripts**.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent some completion errors in which “implemented” crosses a transition but “verification pending” does not, because pending checks become first-class regimen items. Prevention is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying historical episodes with deliberately dropped pending-verification entries and comparing final-state claims**.

### 1.3 Iatrogenics, contraindications, and interaction-aware decision support

**Iatrogenic** harm is harm caused by medical care itself. The important conceptual move is that an intervention’s presence is not automatically beneficial: dose, duration, route, patient state, and other interventions can turn benefit into harm. Medication-safety practice therefore combines reconciliation with prescribing checks, drug-allergy alerts, drug–drug interaction rules, and high-risk-state monitoring. AHRQ lists computerized order entry plus decision support and medication reconciliation at transitions among strategies to prevent adverse drug events ([AHRQ PSNet, *Medication Errors and Adverse Drug Events*, 2024](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/medication-errors-and-adverse-drug-events)).

Naive contraindication registries also fail. Pair-only interaction checking generates too many irrelevant warnings, causing alert fatigue. AHRQ reports that contextualized DDI algorithms incorporating patient circumstances cut simulated alerts by 52% for eight high-priority interactions, while preserving alerts where harm was plausible ([AHRQ Digital Healthcare Research, *Creating Meaningful Decision Support to Reduce Drug-Drug Interactions*](https://digital.ahrq.gov/program-overview/research-stories/creating-meaningful-decision-support-reduce-drug-drug)). AHRQ also notes that dose, route, duration, and concomitant therapies can determine whether a nominal interaction matters ([AHRQ, *Individualized Drug Interaction Alerts*](https://digital.ahrq.gov/ahrq-funded-projects/individualized-drug-interaction-alerts)). Broad, repetitive alerts are commonly overridden; PSNet summarizes prior DDI override rates as high as 90% and describes expert tiering of low-priority interactions to non-interruptive status ([AHRQ PSNet summary of Phansalkar et al.](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/drug-drug-interactions-should-be-non-interruptive-order-reduce-alert-fatigue-electronic)).

#### Concrete translation

Context itself can be iatrogenic. Examples include:

- a once-correct instruction whose scope expired but which still overrides a newer request;
- two individually useful rules that conflict in a particular shell, repository, or action phase;
- a stale “test passed” fact suppressing necessary re-verification after code changes;
- a verbose error log crowding out the single owner constraint needed for the final action;
- duplicated warnings producing habituation, so the one truly blocking constraint is ignored;
- an untrusted retrieved passage placed at the same authority level as an owner instruction.

**Proposal — context contraindication registry — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Define a small, auditable registry of high-severity predicates over typed context items, not a semantic knowledge graph. Initial rule classes:

- `supersedes`: newer owner decision replaces an older decision in the same scope;
- `invalidates`: file mutation invalidates a prior test/read result about that file;
- `requires`: completion claim requires named verification evidence;
- `conflicts`: two instructions cannot both govern the same action and need authority resolution;
- `taints`: untrusted content cannot become an instruction or authority source;
- `expires_on`: phase, time, branch, working-directory, tool, or artifact-state change ends applicability;
- `dose_limit`: cap repeated logs, retries, examples, or reminders while retaining a count and provenance pointer.

**Proposal — severity-tiered interaction check at context assembly — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Apply the registry when assembling each next-turn context. Only `blocker` interactions interrupt; `review` items appear once in the transition reconciliation; `info` items are logged outside immediate context. Deduplicate identical alerts by rule, item IDs, and scope. Re-alert only when the relevant state changes.

The registry is **likely (55-80%)** to catch stale-validity and authority-conflict failures that relevance-only retrieval misses, because semantic relevance does not encode invalidation, supersession, or authority. This is a logical consequence of the rule types, but the frequency and value are **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a shadow-mode scan of 1,000 context assemblies followed by blinded adjudication of precision, severity, and missed conflicts**.

Severity tiering is **very likely (80-90%)** to be necessary for sustained use, based on clinical alert-override evidence and the direct cost of adding warnings to a finite prompt. The best tier thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; settle them through a four-week shadow period measuring rule-level precision and a subsequent randomized interruptive-versus-noninterruptive trial for borderline rules**.

### 1.4 NNT, NNH, thresholds, and decision curves

In its own field, **number needed to treat (NNT)** is the inverse of absolute risk reduction for a defined outcome and time horizon. It turns a trial’s difference into “how many patients receive the treatment for one additional patient to benefit.” Cook and Sackett introduced it as more clinically interpretable than relative effects and emphasized its dependence on baseline risk ([Cook and Sackett, *BMJ*, 1995](https://www.bmj.com/content/310/6977/452)). Its harm analogue is number needed to harm (NNH), the inverse of absolute risk increase.

NNT is not a magic scalar. It varies with baseline risk, comparator, outcome definition, and follow-up duration; it needs uncertainty intervals; and it collapses outcome severity. NICE therefore recommends communicating absolute risks, a fixed denominator, and a defined time period, with both positive and negative framing ([NICE NG197, 2021](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197/chapter/recommendations)). NICE’s evidence review found moderate-quality evidence from one comparison that absolute risk reduction may be understood better than NNT, a warning against fetishizing the reciprocal ([NICE Evidence Review D](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197/evidence/d-risk-communication-pdf-9142344257)).

Medicine’s more general solution is **decision threshold/net benefit analysis**. Pauker and Kassirer formalized testing and test-treatment thresholds: below one threshold, do not test; between thresholds, gather information; above the upper threshold, act ([Pauker and Kassirer, *NEJM*, 1980](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJM198005153022003)). Decision-curve analysis evaluates a prediction strategy by placing true-positive benefit and false-positive harm on a common scale across plausible thresholds, comparing it with “treat all” and “treat none” ([Vickers and Elkin, *Medical Decision Making*, 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2577036/); [Vickers et al., *BMJ*, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4724785/)). Critically, accuracy alone is not utility.

#### Concrete translation

Define a context policy’s effect using controlled episodes and a fixed horizon:

- **absolute context benefit (ACB)** = failure rate without an item/class minus failure rate with it;
- **number needed to context (NNC)** = `1 / ACB` when ACB is positive;
- **absolute context harm (ACH)** = failure rate with the item/class minus failure rate without it for adverse endpoints attributable to distraction, conflict, latency, or excess tool use;
- **number needed to distract (NND)** = `1 / ACH` when ACH is positive.

These are proposed engineering names, not established clinical or agent-evaluation terms.

Do not compute NNC for arbitrary individual snippets. Estimate it for policy classes such as “retain owner constraints through compaction,” “include last 200 raw tool lines,” “carry two competing hypotheses,” or “inject retrieved historical preference.” Pre-register the endpoint and horizon: task success at final handoff, owner correction within the next five turns, invalid external action, token cost, or latency. Always report raw absolute rates and confidence/credible intervals beside the reciprocal.

**Proposal — randomized context-policy ablation evaluator — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Given a frozen episode set, construct paired prompts differing only in one context policy; randomize order/seed where supported; score task outcomes blind to arm; report ACB, ACH, uncertainty, token/latency burden, and subgroup results by task phase and baseline risk. This is an offline script invoked through the existing evaluation/replay path, not a runtime or trained critic.

**Proposal — admission thresholds based on net benefit — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Classify candidate context as:

- **retain/pin** when omission risk is high or consequence severe (owner constraint, authorization boundary, unresolved destructive action);
- **retrieve/test** when relevance is plausible but uncertain (fetch provenance-backed raw evidence or run a discriminating tool probe);
- **evict-to-chart** when expected benefit is below distraction/token burden but future retrieval remains plausible;
- **retire** only when invalidated, superseded, or disproven with provenance.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that policy-level randomized ablation will identify context routinely carried at net negative value, because long-context performance is position- and distractor-sensitive rather than monotonic. Liu et al. found a U-shaped use pattern in multi-document QA, with relevant information often used less effectively in the middle ([Liu et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)); Chroma’s 2025 controlled report found performance degradation with increasing context across tested models and tasks ([Chroma, *Context Rot*, 2025](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). The harness benefit is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with §8’s experiment**.

Literal online NNC-driven eviction is **unlikely (20-45%)** to work reliably, because context effects are heterogeneous, sequential, and interactive; evidence for one policy on one task population does not identify the causal value of one snippet in a new episode. The settling experiment would be a held-out, temporally later evaluation comparing an NNC-threshold policy with a simpler typed recency/authority baseline. Until that wins, NNC belongs in evaluation dashboards, not the runtime hot path.

## 2. The combined design: the Context Regimen

The four medical mechanisms compose into one minimal architecture.

### 2.1 Data model

Each regimen item has:

```text
id                 stable across summaries and handoffs
kind               constraint | fact | hypothesis | decision | commitment | risk | evidence
statement          bounded canonical text
authority          owner | developer | environment | tool-result | inference | retrieved-untrusted
provenance         transcript/tool/event pointer
scope              task, phase, path, branch, tool, or action class
observed_at        event/time
review_on          transition/state-change predicate
expires_on         optional explicit predicate
status             active | suspended | evicted-to-chart | retired | unresolved
supports/refutes   hypothesis IDs
severity           blocker | review | info
conflicts_with     rule or item IDs
last_reconciled    transition ID
```

The stable ID prevents a paraphrased constraint from becoming a duplicate “new fact.” `Authority` prevents a retrieved document from silently becoming an instruction. `Scope` and `expires_on` prevent stale global application. `Supports/refutes` makes the differential operational. `Review_on` forces re-evaluation after the state changes that can make a fact unsafe.

**Proposal — Context Regimen schema — UNVERIFIED. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Keep this as a small schema consumed by existing compaction/handoff hooks. Do not introduce embeddings, a graph database, or a separate memory server.

The schema is **very likely (80-90%)** to make provenance, authority, and expiry representable because those are explicit fields. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that models will populate all fields accurately without deterministic extraction for high-risk event types, based on the general unreliability of unconstrained model-generated state and no harness-specific trial. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by double-annotating 100 transitions and measuring field-level precision/recall against deterministic event metadata**.

### 2.2 Online flow

1. **Observe:** append raw user, tool, and environment events to the existing transcript/event log.
2. **Represent:** update the bounded problem representation and differential; do not rewrite raw evidence.
3. **Check interactions:** apply high-severity contraindication predicates using authority, scope, state, and freshness.
4. **Reconcile at transitions:** diff prior regimen, observed events, and proposed next regimen; classify every change.
5. **Assemble context:** pin high-consequence items, include the compact representation, and retrieve provenance-backed evidence required for the current hypotheses.
6. **Act or test:** when the evidence lies between action thresholds, gather a discriminating observation instead of adding broad background.
7. **Evaluate offline:** use paired ablations to update policy-level benefit/harm estimates; never mutate rules automatically from a handful of episodes.

This design is **likely (55-80%)** to remain below the anti-overfit threshold because it is a schema, deterministic diff, predicate list, and offline evaluator riding existing buses. The line-count estimate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an implementation spike that counts nonblank, noncomment lines for each component and rejects any component exceeding ~200 lines without a new review**.

## 3. What this catches that common context policies miss

This unit intentionally did not inspect the local harness. Therefore each “currently miss” statement below is a mechanism-class prediction, not a claim that a named local component lacks the feature.

### 3.1 Silent loss during compaction or handoff — UNVERIFIED locally

Rolling summaries can omit a pending test, a negative result, or a low-probability/high-severity risk without recording that an eviction occurred. Reconciliation catches the set difference and demands an intentional disposition. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect such loss when stable IDs exist, based on deterministic set comparison. Local prevalence is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the 200-transition shadow study in §8**.

### 3.2 Stale truth after state mutation — UNVERIFIED locally

Relevance retrieval may return “tests pass” after files changed because the statement remains semantically relevant. An `invalidates(file mutation, verification result)` predicate changes its status to suspended until re-tested. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect declared file/result dependencies, because the trigger is deterministic. Coverage of undeclared dependencies is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by mutation testing known file-to-test relationships**.

### 3.3 Premature closure on one causal story — UNVERIFIED locally

A narrative summary tends to preserve the selected explanation and discard why alternatives remained possible. The live differential retains support, refutation, and the next discriminating probe. This is **likely (55-80%)** to improve alternative recovery after compaction, based on the structural role of a clinical differential. The agent outcome is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with two-cause debugging trials**.

### 3.4 Individually relevant but jointly incompatible context — UNVERIFIED locally

Top-k retrieval scores items independently. It can select an old permission and a new restriction, or platform-specific instructions for two shells. Typed `supersedes`, `scope`, and `conflicts` rules check the set, not each item alone. Detection is **likely (55-80%)** for registered rule classes; open-ended semantic contradictions remain **UNVERIFIED; settle coverage by seeding a labeled suite of authority, temporal, platform, and phase conflicts**.

### 3.5 Alert/context overdose — UNVERIFIED locally

Repeated warnings can consume attention and tokens until they are functionally ignored. Severity tiers, deduplication, and state-change-based re-alerting copy the clinical response to alert fatigue. Reduced alert volume is **very likely (80-90%)** by construction; preserved safety is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring severe-conflict recall and unsafe-action rate, not alert count alone**.

### 3.6 Relative-relevance theater — UNVERIFIED locally

A retriever can improve a ranking metric while worsening task outcomes. Absolute benefit/harm and net-benefit curves force comparison with simple policies such as “pin all owner constraints,” “carry none of the old logs,” and “retrieve on demand.” It is **likely (55-80%)** that this reveals policies whose benchmark relevance does not translate into utility, based on medicine’s distinction between predictive accuracy and clinical net benefit. The frequency is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with paired task-level ablations**.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy transfers

### 4.1 Transition reconciliation transfers strongly

The source and target share the essential structure: an evolving active set crosses a boundary; the new phase has different needs; both accidental omission and blind continuation can cause harm; and the raw prior record remains available. The mechanism does not require drugs and context items to behave identically. It requires only stable identities, an observed event record, and an intentional classification of differences.

Transfer strength is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by this structural correspondence and WHO’s explicit distinction between intentional change and outstanding unintentional discrepancy. Effect size remains **UNVERIFIED; the shadow-and-randomized experiments in §8 settle it**.

### 4.2 Differential diagnosis transfers moderately

Debugging, planning, and research genuinely involve latent competing explanations updated by evidence. Preserving alternatives counters premature closure. But many agent tasks are constructive rather than diagnostic: there may be no hidden “disease,” and hypotheses can alter the artifact they predict. The borrow should therefore apply only when uncertainty concerns causal state, requirements interpretation, or failure explanation—not to every checklist item.

Transfer strength is **likely (55-80%)**, supported by the shared abductive/Bayesian structure but reduced by endogenous task state. The scope boundary is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by stratifying evaluation into diagnostic, constructive, and mixed tasks**.

### 4.3 Contraindication checking transfers selectively

Authority conflicts, stale facts, and state-dependent invalidation are rule-like and can be checked. However, drugs have standardized identities and extensively studied interaction mechanisms; free text has combinatorial semantics and context-sensitive pragmatics. A comprehensive “context interaction database” would become a brittle knowledge graph or guardrail framework—the exact overfit this program rejects.

Transfer is **likely (55-80%)** for a small registry of deterministic, high-severity relations and **very unlikely (5-20%)** for a comprehensive semantic contraindication system, based on the difference between typed event metadata and open-ended language. The small registry’s useful coverage is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with labeled seeded-conflict recall and real shadow precision**.

### 4.4 NNT transfers as evaluation, not bedside control

Both fields need absolute, horizon-specific estimates of benefit and harm. But clinical NNT usually estimates a treatment policy over a population; an agent context item’s effect depends on its position, wording, neighboring items, model, task phase, and earlier outputs. Stable-unit treatment value and limited interference assumptions are weak. Reciprocals also become unstable near zero effect.

Therefore NNT’s **measurement discipline** transfers, while literal per-item NNT eviction does not. This conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on NNT’s baseline-risk dependence and the documented position sensitivity of LLM context. A policy-level NNC may generalize within a task stratum; that is **UNVERIFIED; settle it only on temporally later held-out episodes and a different model version**.

## 5. Failure modes and safeguards

1. **The regimen becomes another bloated summary.** Enforce a hard token budget and typed fields; raw evidence stays behind pointers. A hard budget is **very likely (80-90%)** to bound prompt cost by construction, while preservation of critical content is **UNVERIFIED; test must-not-miss recall under adversarial budget pressure**.

2. **The differential becomes an excuse never to decide.** Require a next discriminating probe and explicit action threshold. This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce idle hypothesis accumulation, based on the threshold model; outcome impact is **UNVERIFIED; measure tool calls and time-to-commit**.

3. **Stable IDs falsely merge distinct facts.** IDs must bind canonical claim plus scope, not surface similarity. Collision rate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with annotated paraphrase, scope-change, and contradiction cases**.

4. **The contraindication registry causes alert fatigue.** Start with a handful of high-severity deterministic rules, tier output, deduplicate, and measure overrides. This safeguard is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce alert volume relative to interrupting on every pair, based on AHRQ’s contextualization and tiering evidence; safe threshold values are **UNVERIFIED**.

5. **Model-generated reconciliation launders omissions as intentional.** The diff must be deterministic; only the reason may be model-authored, and high-severity retirements require provenance or user authority. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose rather than hide set differences, by construction. Correct reason classification is **UNVERIFIED; audit against human labels**.

6. **Ablations leak or are confounded by stochasticity.** Use paired frozen inputs, randomized arm order, repeated seeds where possible, blinded scoring, pre-registered endpoints, and uncertainty intervals. This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce bias, based on experimental design principles; residual model nondeterminism is **UNVERIFIED and quantified by repeated-control variance**.

7. **Population benefit hides catastrophic subgroup harm.** Report by authority level, task phase, and risk class; keep hard safety constraints pinned even when mean NNC looks poor. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal measured subgroup differences when sample sizes suffice; adequate power is **UNVERIFIED and must be reported rather than assumed**.

8. **The medical vocabulary creates false confidence.** Use ordinary engineering names in implementation (`context_item`, `transition_diff`, `conflict_rule`, `policy_ablation`); retain the clinical terms only in design rationale. This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce metaphor-driven overreach, based on making acceptance tests operational rather than analogical. The behavioral effect is **UNVERIFIED; review implementation proposals for claims supported only by analogy**.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 6.1 “Keep everything; context windows are large now” — reject

This ignores both finite cost and nonuniform use. Lost-in-the-middle results show that presence is not equivalent to effective use ([Liu et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)). Chroma’s controlled experiments likewise report degradation as input grows, although that report is not a peer-reviewed universal law ([Chroma, 2025](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). It is **very likely (80-90%)** that unlimited accumulation is not a robust policy across models and tasks, based on these two independent experimental programs. ## 8. Experiments that settle the unverified premises

### Experiment A — transition discrepancy shadow study

- Sample 200 real compaction, phase-change, resume, and agent-handoff transitions.
- Establish a known-present control item for each detector run (e.g., a seeded active constraint with a stable ID), proving the detector can find present items before interpreting absence.
- Signal 1: deterministic stable-ID set diff between prior regimen and proposed next regimen.
- Signal 2: independent human review of the raw event span and next prompt, blinded to the diff classification.
- Primary measures: unintentional omissions per transition, duplicate/resurrected items, stale items carried, precision by severity, and adjudicator agreement.
- Promotion gate: high recall for seeded controls, useful real discrepancy yield, and blocker precision high enough that interruptive use will not recreate alert fatigue.

This experiment satisfies the two-mechanically-independent-signals rule for absence claims: a set comparison and a human reconstruction from raw events are different detectors, and the seeded control validates detection.

### Experiment B — competing-hypothesis compaction trial

- Construct debugging and research episodes with two plausible causes, one initially salient but wrong, and a later discriminating observation.
- Randomize to rolling narrative summary versus typed problem representation plus differential at equal token budget.
- Score preservation of both hypotheses, retrieval of disconfirming evidence, selection of the discriminating probe, final correctness, tool calls, and latency.
- Repeat on constructive tasks as a negative-control stratum; a good mechanism should not add overhead where no diagnostic uncertainty exists.

### Experiment C — contraindication registry shadow and seeded-conflict trial

- Seed labeled authority, temporal, platform, phase, invalidation, and completion-without-verification conflicts.
- Prove known-present detection for every rule class.
- Independently adjudicate 1,000 natural context assemblies.
- Measure per-rule precision/recall, alert deduplication, token cost, and whether blocker-tier alerts predict unsafe action.
- Only then randomize borderline rules between interruptive and noninterruptive presentation.

### Experiment D — context-policy ACB/ACH/NNC evaluation

- Freeze a representative episode set before inspecting outcomes.
- Compare at least: current/default assembly; pin everything; summary-only; typed regimen; typed regimen without differential; typed regimen without contraindication checks.
- Pair prompts and randomize arm order/seed; score blind.
- Pre-register binary outcomes and horizon. Report absolute rates, differences, intervals, NNC/NND only when denominators are meaningfully nonzero, token cost, latency, and owner-correction rate.
- Validate temporally and on a different model version. A policy does not graduate from evaluation to runtime on in-sample NNC.

### Experiment E — catastrophic subgroup gate

- Define high-severity episodes: destructive actions, external communications, authorization boundaries, and completion claims.
- Compare omission and unsafe-action rates under each policy even if total task success rises.
- Retain hard constraints if subgroup uncertainty is too wide; lack of statistical power is not evidence of safety.

## 9. Success and stop conditions

Proceed from shadow mode only if two independent signals agree that transition discrepancies or registered conflicts are real and actionable. A successful design must:

- reduce unintentional transition discrepancies;
- preserve or improve task success on held-out episodes;
- not increase high-severity unsafe actions;
- keep the pinned regimen within its fixed token budget;
- show better net benefit than simple “carry all,” “carry none,” and summary-only baselines;
- preserve raw provenance so eviction remains reversible;
- avoid a new daemon, database, trained critic, or orchestration runtime.

Stop or roll back a mechanism if blocker precision produces alert fatigue, if the differential adds cost without benefit outside diagnostic strata, if policy-level benefits fail temporal/model validation, or if schema population cannot be made reliable with deterministic event metadata. These stop conditions are configuration/evaluation decisions, not a request for a larger architecture.
