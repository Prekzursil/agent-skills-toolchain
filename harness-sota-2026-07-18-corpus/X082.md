# X082 — Cross-domain borrow: law of evidence → task-conditioned agent trust

## Executive finding

The useful borrow is **not** “put the agent on trial,” nor is it a single courtroom-style confidence threshold. It is a compact four-part control pattern:

1. **Foundation before weight:** establish what an output is, where each material claim came from, and whether the producing agent/process is qualified for this task before deciding how persuasive the output is.
2. **Every relay must carry its own warrant:** a trustworthy wrapper cannot cure an untrustworthy inner assertion. This is the important structure behind hearsay-within-hearsay.
3. **Trust is purpose- and task-limited:** evidence admissible for one purpose is not thereby valid for every purpose; likewise an agent's coding record must not become a global reputation score for legal research, UI judgment, or completion claims.
4. **The acceptance standard follows the cost and reversibility of error:** the burden is allocated according to what happens if the decision is wrong, rather than according to how authoritative the speaker sounds.

This mechanism transfers **likely (55-80%)**, because both adjudication and agent use involve decisions under incomplete, mediated, sometimes interested information; the legal rules directly separate identity, source reliability, scope, credibility, and decision threshold. The evidence is the distinct treatment in Federal Rules of Evidence 104, 105, 702, 803, 805, 901, and 902, and the Supreme Court's explanation that a standard of proof allocates error risk in *Addington v. Texas*. The range is deliberately not higher: courts add oath, sanctions, discovery, opposing counsel, a neutral judge, and institutional appeal, none of which a typical agent loop fully reproduces.

The recommended harness primitive is a small **claim-evidence passport plus a task-conditioned trust ledger and consequence gate**, implemented on the existing hook/skill bus. It must remain advisory for ordinary reversible work and become a stop/escalate gate only for explicitly high-consequence actions. It does not require a second orchestrator, a vector database, a knowledge graph, a durable-execution engine, a trained critic, an observability service, or a guardrail framework.

## 1. What the source field has actually solved

This report uses the U.S. Federal Rules of Evidence and U.S. Supreme Court decisions as a concrete, internally coherent example. These rules are not claimed to be universal evidence law, and this is not legal advice.

### 1.1 Authentication is a threshold question, not a truth certificate

Federal Rule of Evidence 901(a) requires the proponent to produce enough evidence to support a finding that an item is what the proponent claims it is. The rule lists nonexclusive ways to do that, including testimony by someone with knowledge, comparison with an authenticated specimen, and distinctive characteristics considered with the circumstances ([FRE 901(a)-(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)).

The important mechanism is modest: authentication answers **identity and provenance**, not ultimate truth. Rule 104 gives the court preliminary responsibility for qualification and admissibility, treats fact-dependent relevance as conditional, and expressly preserves later attacks on weight and credibility ([FRE 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)). Authentication therefore opens the door to consideration; it does not tell the factfinder how much to believe the content.

For electronic material, Rule 902 provides shortcuts where the production process itself supplies a sufficient foundation. It allows certified records generated by an electronic process that produces an accurate result and certified copied data authenticated through digital identification; it also requires notice and an opportunity to inspect/challenge certified business records ([FRE 902(11), (13), and (14)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902)). Rule 902's committee note also says self-authentication never forecloses the other side from disputing authenticity. This is a useful warning against treating a signature or hash as proof of semantic correctness.

**Harness translation:** an agent answer should first establish “this claim came from this agent/process, using these inputs, source artifacts, tools, and transformations.” A digest can establish that the cited bytes did not change after capture; it cannot establish that the page was true, current, correctly interpreted, or applicable.

### 1.2 Chain of custody preserves continuity, while gaps normally affect weight

Chain of custody is the continuity story for an item as it passes among handlers: who possessed it, what happened to it, and whether the offered item is the relevantly unchanged item. The Supreme Court in *Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts* recognized the prosecution's obligation to establish chain of custody, while explaining that not every handler must testify and that gaps normally go to weight rather than admissibility ([*Melendez-Diaz*, 557 U.S. 305, 311 n.1 (2009)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/557/305/)).

This avoids two symmetric errors: “there is one gap, discard everything” and “the final analyst is credible, so ignore what happened upstream.” The legal system can admit an imperfectly documented chain while exposing the gap to the factfinder and reducing weight.

**Harness translation:** record the lineage of a claim through source → retrieval → excerpt/observation → interpretation → synthesis → action. Mark a missing hop; do not silently fill it and do not necessarily discard the whole answer. The consumer can still use the claim for low-stakes orientation while refusing to use it for an irreversible action.

### 1.3 Hearsay rules inspect the source of assertions, not just the final speaker

Rule 801 defines hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered to prove what it asserts ([FRE 801(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801)). Its relevance here is functional, not literal: an agent often reports a webpage's report of a vendor's or another agent's assertion. The final agent usually did not perceive the underlying event and may only be relaying language.

Rule 805 supplies the critical recursion rule: nested hearsay is not excluded only when **each part** of the combined statement independently conforms to an exception ([FRE 805](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805)). Rule 806 then allows attacks on the absent declarant's credibility as if that declarant had testified ([FRE 806](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_806)). The source does not become immune to scrutiny merely because another speaker repeated it.

Rule 803(6)'s business-record exception shows what a process-based reliability foundation looks like: the record is made at or near the time by someone with knowledge, kept in a regularly conducted activity, regularly made, supported by a custodian/certification, and still rejectable when source or preparation suggests untrustworthiness ([FRE 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)). Rule 803(7) is especially important for absence claims: absence from a record supports nonoccurrence only when that kind of matter was regularly recorded ([FRE 803(7)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

**Harness translation:** “agent B says agent A found no failures” has at least two reliability links: A's detector and B's faithful transmission/interpretation. B's good history cannot cure A's broken detector. An absence claim requires proof that the relevant detector normally records known-present events; a blank log is not self-validating.

### 1.4 Expert qualification is question-specific and method-specific

Rule 702 allows expert opinion only when the witness is qualified and the proponent shows, more likely than not, that the specialized knowledge will help, the testimony rests on sufficient facts/data, the method is reliable, and the method was reliably applied to the facts ([FRE 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)). The advisory note identifies a nonexclusive reliability checklist including testability, peer review, known or potential error rate, standards/controls, and general acceptance. It also cautions that the factors do not fit every kind of expertise.

The mechanism is not “credentialed person equals truth.” It asks separately whether this producer is qualified **on this issue**, whether the method is inspectable and tested, and whether the method was applied properly in this instance.

**Harness translation:** maintain performance by `(agent identity/version, task type, verification regime)`, not a single Elo-like “trustworthiness” number. A model that reliably edits localized TypeScript under tests has not thereby established reliability for detecting configuration absence, interpreting law, or declaring an external deployment complete.

### 1.5 Relevance and limiting purpose prevent reputation leakage

Rule 401 defines relevance relationally: evidence must change the probability of a consequential fact ([FRE 401](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401)). The committee note is unusually apt: relevance is not an inherent property of an item, but a relation between the item and what is to be proved. Rule 105 requires evidence admitted for one purpose but not another to be restricted to its proper scope ([FRE 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_105)). Rule 403 permits exclusion where probative value is substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice, confusion, delay, waste, or cumulative presentation ([FRE 403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_403)).

**Harness translation:** a prior success is evidence only to the extent that it bears on the current task and conditions. Displaying a global 93% “agent reliability” badge invites exactly the misuse Rule 105 guards against: valid for one purpose, silently generalized to another. Lengthy provenance also has a Rule 403 analogue—if every trivial claim dumps a full trace into the user's view, the evidence can obscure the decision it is meant to support. Preserve the trace, but show the shortest material summary by default.

### 1.6 Burdens and standards of proof allocate the risk of being wrong

The clearest primary statement is *Addington v. Texas*. The Supreme Court explained that a standard of proof tells the factfinder the degree of confidence appropriate for a type of decision, allocates error risk, and reflects the importance of the decision. It described preponderance in ordinary civil disputes as roughly equal allocation of error risk, beyond reasonable doubt in criminal matters as putting almost all error risk on society, and clear-and-convincing evidence as an intermediate standard for more important individual interests ([*Addington*, 441 U.S. 418, 423-25 (1979)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/441/418/)). The Court selected a higher standard for involuntary commitment because the harm of erroneous commitment was not fairly shared, but rejected the highest standard partly because the underlying diagnosis was too uncertain for it and because that threshold could defeat legitimate decisions ([*Addington*, 441 U.S. at 425-33](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/441/418/)).

**Harness translation:** acceptance is a decision-policy question, not merely an estimated-accuracy question. The same 0.8-supported claim can be enough to choose which harmless diagnostic to run first and not enough to delete data, send a consequential message, or assert legal/medical/financial facts. The harness should set a burden according to asymmetric harm, reversibility, detectability, and available review.

## 2. The proposed mechanism

### 2.1 UNVERIFIED proposal A — Claim-evidence passport

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add an optional structured footer/sidecar for material claims. A post-response hook validates it and stores it beside the normal run record. It does not replace the prose response and is not a new execution runtime.

Minimal schema:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "c7",
  "claim": "deployment health check returned 200",
  "purpose": "prove post-deploy reachability",
  "task_type": "deploy.verify.http",
  "producer": {"agent": "...", "model": "...", "prompt_rev": "...", "tool_rev": "..."},
  "claim_kind": "observed",
  "lineage": [
    {"source": "tool-call:...", "locator": "response.status", "captured_at": "...", "digest": "..."}
  ],
  "transformations": ["selected response.status"],
  "scope_limits": ["does not prove background jobs or database migrations"],
  "verification": {
    "status": "corroborated",
    "signals": [
      {"class": "http-probe", "ref": "..."},
      {"class": "service-log", "ref": "..."}
    ]
  },
  "counterevidence": [],
  "self_confidence": "very likely (80-90%)"
}
```

Required behavior:

- Material factual claims are typed as `observed`, `derived`, `reported`, `predicted`, or `absence`. This is provenance, not a claim that any type is automatically true.
- Every `reported` claim identifies the upstream declarant/source; nested reports create nested lineage nodes rather than collapsing into one citation.
- Every `derived` claim states the transformation or inference.
- Every `absence` claim identifies (1) a detector control that found a known-present item and (2) a mechanically independent signal. Two agents reading the same blank field are one signal, not two.
- A hash/digest may set `integrity=matched`; it may not set `truth=verified`.
- Missing lineage yields `foundation=gap`, not fabricated metadata and not automatic rejection.
- The visible response shows a compact badge such as `observed / two independent signals / scope: HTTP only`; full lineage is available on demand.

This will catch provenance laundering, stale or misidentified source artifacts, unsupported completion claims, unvalidated absence claims, and summaries that silently strengthen their source **very likely (80-90%)**, because each failure directly violates a required schema field or independence check. This outcome is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a 200-output blinded replay containing seeded missing-hop, same-source-corroboration, stale-source, and overbroad-completion defects, comparing hook detection with the present workflow and measuring precision/recall plus added tokens/latency.**

It will improve the substantive truth of well-provenanced but false sources only **unlikely (20-45%)**, because provenance reveals the relay but does not independently test the world's facts. The evidence for the low band is the legal distinction between authentication and weight in Rules 901/104 and Rule 902's continued right to dispute authenticity.

### 2.2 UNVERIFIED proposal B — Task-conditioned trust ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Maintain a small append-only JSONL/SQLite-free ledger computed from existing run outcomes. Identity must include model/prompt/tool/harness revisions so a model upgrade does not inherit an obsolete persona's record. Key rows by a controlled task taxonomy, for example:

```
(producer_revision, task_type, claim_kind, verification_regime)
  n_matured
  supported
  contradicted
  unresolved
  owner_corrected
  coverage_rate
  mean_brier_score_if_probabilities_exist
  last_observed_at
```

Rules:

1. Do not score unresolved outcomes as successes. Preserve them as censored/unresolved.
2. Do not treat “no correction” as correctness unless the outcome matured and a defined verifier ran.
3. Report sample size and interval/lower bound beside every rate. For sparse cells, display `insufficient evidence`; do not borrow a global score invisibly.
4. Keep task granularity coarse enough to accumulate outcomes but narrow enough to preserve causal relevance. Version and publish taxonomy changes.
5. Separate `claim accuracy`, `evidence coverage`, `calibration`, and `instruction compliance`; do not average them into one number.
6. When a current claim depends on upstream agent output, consult each relevant task cell. The final agent's score does not overwrite the upstream score.
7. Permit impeachment: attach known counterexamples, correction clusters, and regime changes to the cell shown to the decision maker.

A trust card might read: `deploy.verify.http | n=84 matured | 80 supported, 3 contradicted, 1 unresolved | evidence coverage 91% | current revision n=12: insufficient evidence`. That is more honest than “Agent Q: 95% trusted.”

This will reduce cross-task reputation leakage and expose confidence based on tiny or stale samples **very likely (80-90%)**, because the UI refuses to produce an unqualified global score and always surfaces the current cell's `n` and revision. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying routing/acceptance decisions from historical tasks with task labels hidden, then compare global-score, task-cell, and no-score conditions on false acceptance, false rejection, and abstention.**

It will predict future correctness better than a global baseline **likely (55-80%)**, supported conceptually by Rule 401's relational relevance and Rule 702's issue/method-specific qualification, but the gain depends on having enough mature outcomes in each cell. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a time-split evaluation in which only earlier outcomes build the ledger and later independently verified outcomes measure Brier score, log loss, calibration error, and selective accuracy.**

### 2.3 UNVERIFIED proposal C — Consequence-sensitive burden gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Define operational tiers. These are intentionally not named “preponderance,” “clear and convincing,” or “beyond reasonable doubt,” because those verbal legal standards are not calibrated numeric probabilities and transplanting their names would create false precision.

| Harness tier | Decision profile | Minimum evidence procedure | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 orient | no state change; cheap and reversible | provenance or explicit `unverified` | continue, label |
| T1 act-reversible | local/recoverable change; verifier exists | authenticated direct observation **or** deterministic test, scope-limited | run verifier or abstain |
| T2 material | external communication, material cost, difficult rollback | two mechanically independent signals; task cell not contradicted; counterevidence surfaced | pause for additional evidence |
| T3 protected | destructive/irreversible, credentials, legal/medical/financial reliance, safety-critical | T2 plus explicit owner authorization and a domain-appropriate authoritative source/test | stop and escalate |

“Mechanical independence” is determined from the passport's provenance graph. Two agents quoting the same page, two retries of the same detector, or a summary plus the record it summarizes are correlated and count as one signal. An API response plus an independently sourced service-side state record can count as two when they do not share the same failure mode. Exact independence classes should be configured per task type.

The gate asks four questions before setting a tier: `harm_if_false_accept`, `harm_if_false_reject`, `reversibility`, and `later_detectability`. It raises the required process when false acceptance is worse, and allows lower thresholds when delay or false rejection is worse. This is the direct structural borrow from *Addington*: standards allocate error risk rather than merely naming confidence.

This will prevent a self-confident single-agent assertion from authorizing high-consequence work **very likely (80-90%)**, because T2/T3 mechanically require independent support and T3 requires owner authorization. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a shadow-mode evaluation over at least 100 prospective decisions, pre-registering consequence labels and measuring prevented unsafe false accepts, unnecessary escalations, and time-to-completion before enabling enforcement.**

The proposed tier boundaries will have acceptable friction on real work only **roughly even (45-55%)**, because the analogy does not supply the owner's actual loss function and independence classes can be hard to specify. This is **UNVERIFIED; the same shadow-mode experiment settles it, with an acceptance criterion set in advance for escalation rate and added latency.**

### 2.4 UNVERIFIED proposal D — Challenge, scope, and “weight not exclusion” UI

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a compact trust panel to a response or handoff:

```
FOUNDATION: authenticated | one lineage gap
TASK RECORD: 31 matured | 27 supported | current revision n=4
SCOPE: proves file content; does not prove runtime behavior
BURDEN: T2 | unmet (only one independent signal)
CHALLENGE: 2 counterexamples | inspect / request verification
DISPOSITION: usable for orientation; not sufficient to act
```

The panel keeps `admissibility/foundation` separate from `weight`, as Rules 104 and 901 do. It keeps purpose limits visible, as Rule 105 does. It lets the user inspect counterevidence and prior inconsistent outcomes, analogous to Rule 806 impeachment. A lineage gap lowers the displayed weight or limits permissible action; it does not delete potentially useful material. The full trace stays collapsed unless requested, following the practical concern behind Rule 403's treatment of confusion, waste, and cumulative evidence.

This will make uncertainty and scope failures easier for an owner to notice **likely (55-80%)**, because it puts the missing condition next to the proposed disposition rather than burying it in logs. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a randomized comprehension test using paired agent outputs, measuring whether users correctly identify what is proved, what is not, and whether action is permitted, plus task time.**

## 3. End-to-end decision procedure

For each consequential claim or completion assertion:

1. **Define the proposition and purpose.** “The command succeeded” is not interchangeable with “the deployment is healthy.” A passport must state the exact consequential proposition.
2. **Authenticate the artifact and producer.** Capture source locator, time, producer revision, tool call, and digest where useful. Do not infer truth from integrity.
3. **Expand all relays.** If the claim is reported, recursively list the upstream source and transformation. Every material link needs a warrant, verifier, or visible gap.
4. **Qualify by task.** Retrieve only the matching producer/task/claim-kind cell. If it is sparse, say so.
5. **Test conditional facts.** If the conclusion is valid only if a service is connected, a branch is current, or a detector covers the target, test that condition. Do not bootstrap the condition from the conclusion.
6. **Seek disconfirmation.** Surface contradictory results, prior correction fingerprints, and scope limits. Challenge the upstream source as well as the synthesizing agent.
7. **Classify consequence.** Determine T0-T3 using error harm, reversibility, and detectability.
8. **Count independent support.** Collapse signals sharing a source or likely failure mode. For absence claims, validate the detector on a known-present control and obtain a second independent signal.
9. **Render a disposition, not a charisma score.** Examples: `consider with gap`, `sufficient for reversible action`, `insufficient for external action`, `owner authorization required`.
10. **Close the outcome later.** When the verifier matures, append supported/contradicted/unresolved to the correct task cell. Never rewrite history silently.

The whole procedure can run as response metadata plus a hook and a tiny reporter. It need not make another model call. Selective use is essential: require full passports only for material claims, claims offered as “done,” absence/broken assertions, cross-agent relays, and T2/T3 decisions. Ordinary brainstorming can remain T0 and simply label conjecture.

## 4. What this catches that ordinary agent-level trust misses

| Missed pattern | Why one agent score fails | Evidence-law control | Harness detection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reliable synthesizer repeats a bad upstream claim | trust is assigned to the last speaker | Rule 805 inspects every statement layer | nested lineage has an unwarranted node |
| A hash-matched artifact contains false content | integrity is confused with truth | Rules 901/902 authenticate identity but preserve challenge | `integrity=matched`, `truth=unverified` |
| Agent is strong at coding but weak at absence detection | reputation leaks across purposes | Rules 401, 105, 702 make relevance/qualification issue-specific | missing or weak task cell |
| Two agents cite the same broken detector | headcount is confused with independence | chain and source analysis expose common origin | provenance graph collapses both signals |
| Empty log is offered as proof nothing happened | detector coverage is assumed | Rule 803(7) requires a record normally kept for that matter | known-present control absent or coverage unknown |
| “Tests passed” becomes “feature is complete” | evidence is used beyond its scope | Rule 105 limiting purpose | scope mismatch between claim and evidence |
| High confidence drives an irreversible action | belief strength is confused with acceptable risk | *Addington* ties burden to allocation of error harm | T3 gate demands evidence plus authorization |
| Sparse new model inherits an old model's reputation | legal identity/qualification foundation is missing | Rules 901 and 702 | producer revision creates a fresh sparse cell |
| One missing lineage hop causes wholesale discard | admissibility is confused with weight | *Melendez-Diaz* treats normal chain gaps as weight issues | disposition limits use while preserving trace |
| Huge evidence dump overwhelms the owner | more evidence is assumed always better | Rule 403 balances probative value against confusion/waste | compact panel; full trace on demand |

The schema will detect these **very likely (80-90%)** when the relevant metadata exists, because each row maps to an explicit validator or display rule. The claim is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the seeded replay described under proposal A, ensuring at least ten examples of every row and reporting per-pattern recall and false-positive rate.** It will detect agents that fabricate internally consistent passports only **very unlikely (5-20%)** without an independent verifier, because self-authored metadata is not independent evidence. The evidence is the central disanalogy below: legal systems use opposing parties and sanctions, while a lone agent can generate both claim and foundation.

## 5. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 5.1 Structural features that do transfer

- **Mediated assertions:** courts and harnesses both consume reports of events that the decision maker did not personally observe. Source relays and transformations matter.
- **Purpose dependence:** the same information can be useful for one inference and invalid for another. This directly supports task-conditioned rather than global trust.
- **Correlated evidence:** repeated assertions can trace to one origin. Counting speakers without tracing lineage double-counts support.
- **Asymmetric consequences:** accepting a wrong claim and rejecting a true claim can have very different costs. A single threshold is therefore inappropriate.
- **Need for contestability:** credibility changes when counterexamples, inconsistent statements, or unreliable preparation become visible.

These structural matches make a process-level borrow **very likely (80-90%)** to be conceptually valid, supported by the direct correspondence between Rules 104/105/702/805/901 and the proposed fields. Conceptual validity does not establish empirical benefit; all effectiveness recommendations above remain inline UNVERIFIED with settling experiments.

### 5.2 Disanalogies that forbid a literal transplant

1. **No genuinely adverse party by default.** Litigation assumes motivated opposition. Two cooperating agents can share model priors, source failures, prompts, and incentives. Asking the same model to “criticize itself” is not cross-examination and does not establish mechanical independence.
2. **No oath, perjury sanction, discovery duty, or professional discipline.** A legal witness may face institutional consequences for deception; a model does not. Requiring an agent to “certify” its own trace supplies syntax, not the incentive foundation behind certification.
3. **The factfinder is different.** Juries/judges weigh admitted evidence; a harness often both produces and gates its own evidence. This creates bootstrapping unless independent tools or the owner resolve high-consequence claims.
4. **Legal admissibility optimizes more than truth.** Evidence rules also protect privilege, constitutional rights, fairness, jury comprehension, institutional legitimacy, and trial efficiency. A useful harness should not copy exclusions whose rationale is courtroom policy rather than epistemic reliability.
5. **Legal verbal standards are not probabilities.** *Addington* itself noted the limited empirical understanding of how jurors distinguish standards. Mapping “clear and convincing” to 75% or “reasonable doubt” to 95% would be invented calibration, not a legal result.
6. **Ground truth and feedback differ.** Trials culminate in a legally final decision, not necessarily truth. Agent tasks may have deterministic tests, delayed external outcomes, subjective preferences, or no observable resolution. A judgment or lack of correction cannot automatically label the agent correct.
7. **Scale and latency differ.** A trial can afford extensive foundation and challenge; an agent harness handles many trivial actions. Applying full evidentiary process to every sentence would impose delay and encourage ceremonial metadata.
8. **Agent identity drifts.** A human expert's identity is relatively continuous. Model weights, system prompts, context, tools, and harness revisions alter the producing process; historical trust must be version-conditioned.

Therefore the analogy supports a **minimal procedural architecture**, not courtroom simulation. It does not justify legalistic prompts, a mock judge agent, a permanent prosecutor/defender multi-agent loop, numerical conversions of proof standards, or universal claim-by-claim bureaucracy.

### 5.3 Explicit rejection of attractive but invalid borrows

- **Reject a global “admissible/inadmissible” output gate.** Useful but imperfect evidence should often remain visible with reduced weight and scope.
- **Reject automatic trust from cryptographic signing.** Signing establishes producer/integrity only; it does not prove factual truth or sound inference.
- **Reject “three agents agree” as corroboration.** Agreement is not independent when all use the same source/model family/tool path.
- **Reject a judge-agent or trained critic.** It would add another fallible model and risk circular self-validation; it also reintroduces a previously rejected trained-critic/second-runtime direction.
- **Reject legal labels as numeric cutoffs.** The owner needs operational procedures tied to harm, not borrowed courtroom rhetoric.
- **Reject treating final task success as proof of every intermediate claim.** A correct result can coexist with false reasoning; claim-level outcomes and task-level outcomes should remain distinct.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and architecture budget

The proposal passes the anti-overfit gate in design:

- It rides existing response metadata, hooks, skills, and run records.
- It adds one small schema validator/reporter and one small outcome aggregator; no always-on daemon is required.
- It uses ordinary JSONL (or the existing run store) rather than a vector DB/KG.
- It invokes no durable workflow engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- It does not require multi-agent deliberation. Independent evidence can come from deterministic tests, different system fields, source-side state, or owner inspection.
- It applies selectively to material claims and high-consequence decisions.
- It can be removed without changing the underlying agent runtime.

Keeping the total implementation under roughly 400 lines across two independent small bus riders is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the deliberately narrow schema validation and aggregation behavior, but no target harness API was inspected in this ideation unit. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a one-day spike against the actual hook event contracts, counting non-generated executable lines and measuring whether any required event is unavailable.** If the hook bus cannot expose producer revision, source references, verifier outcomes, and final disposition, this line budget is **unlikely (20-45%)** to hold; the same spike settles that premise.

Do not combine the two small components into a new orchestration service. If prospective evaluation shows poor precision or high friction, retain the passport as optional provenance and abandon automatic gating. The law-of-evidence borrow is not valuable enough to justify a second runtime.

## 7. Evaluation plan and falsification criteria

### Phase 1 — static seeded replay

Construct 200 prior or synthetic outputs with blinded labels and at least ten instances of each failure pattern in section 4. Seed both obvious and subtle defects. Compare:

- baseline output;
- passport only;
- passport plus task ledger;
- passport, ledger, and consequence disposition.

Measure per-pattern precision/recall, false rejection, false acceptance, metadata completion, token overhead, validation latency, and user comprehension. Include adversarially well-formed but false passports to demonstrate the boundary of self-attestation.

### Phase 2 — temporal trust prediction

Use only outcomes before a cutoff to compute task cells. Predict independently verified outcomes after the cutoff. Compare against global producer score, self-confidence alone, and a no-history baseline. Report Brier/log loss only where agents issue probabilities; otherwise report selective accuracy, coverage, contradiction rate, and calibration by the mandated verbal bands. Never tune and evaluate on the same interval.

### Phase 3 — prospective shadow gate

Run T0-T3 classification without blocking. Pre-register consequence labels and acceptable thresholds. Human reviewers independently label whether evidence sufficed before seeing the gate. Measure prevented unsafe false accepts, unnecessary escalation, disagreement, added latency, and correlation of supposedly independent signals.

### Phase 4 — limited enforcement

Enable only T3 stop/escalate first. T2 remains advisory until shadow data shows acceptable precision. Roll back if any of the following pre-registered falsifiers occurs:

- provenance completion rises while false acceptance does not fall;
- agents learn to generate plausible metadata with no increase in external verification;
- over 10% of routine reversible tasks are escalated without a corresponding defect catch;
- the task taxonomy produces mostly sparse cells or becomes unstable under routine model revisions;
- users misunderstand integrity/authentication badges as truth certificates;
- nominally independent signals repeatedly share a hidden failure source.

The combined design will deliver net value only **likely (55-80%)**, because it targets recognizable epistemic failure structures with a small mechanism, but its friction, taxonomy sparsity, and self-attestation risks are material. This is **UNVERIFIED; phases 1-3 settle whether enforcement is warranted, and failure of the pre-registered criteria should kill or narrow the proposal rather than trigger a larger architecture.**

## 8. Bottom line

Law of evidence has not solved “which agent is truthful” with a master score. Its transferable achievement is better: it decomposes trust into **identity/foundation, source-chain reliability, issue-specific qualification, purpose-limited relevance, contestable weight, and a consequence-sensitive decision standard**. That decomposition directly fits agent outputs, where a polished final answer may contain observations, reports, inferences, and predictions with different provenance and different permissible uses.
