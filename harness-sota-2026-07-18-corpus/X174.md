# X174 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → agent memory

## Unit remit and boundary

This unit asks what experimental design has already solved that can help decide what an agent remembers across sessions, when it forgets, and how it detects staleness. It is deliberately an ideation lens. I did not inspect the local harness, corpus, audit findings, settings, hooks, or memory implementation, because the unit explicitly says not to ground in the local repository. Consequently, statements below describe failure classes and a candidate design, not claims that a named local component is absent or broken.

“What we currently miss” below means what a conventional write/retrieve memory loop misses when it has no prospective evaluation contract. Whether this particular harness already implements any element is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a read-only inventory of every cross-session-memory writer, retriever, expiry rule, evaluator, and hook registration, followed by one known-present control for each detector**. None of the recommendations depends on absence: duplicate mechanisms should be reconciled rather than added.

## Bottom line

The strongest transferable object is not “run more A/B tests.” It is a **prospectively registered memory lifecycle**: before a candidate memory can influence normal work, record the claim it is expected to help, its target scope, primary outcome, minimum worthwhile benefit, known contraindications, invalidation evidence, review schedule, and promotion/retirement rule. Then evaluate its effect with randomized, preferably paired memory-on/memory-off runs whose outcome assessor is blinded to condition. Keep exploratory findings, but label them exploratory and require a new prospective test before promotion.

This transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce hindsight-driven retention and premature forgetting in an agent harness. Evidence: preregistration explicitly separates hypothesis generation from hypothesis testing ([Nosek et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1816418115)); Registered Reports move methodological review and acceptance before results are known ([Center for Open Science](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports)); and a non-randomized comparison found 96% positive first hypotheses in standard psychology reports versus 44% in Registered Reports, while explicitly warning that selection and editorial confounding preclude a causal estimate ([Scheel, Schijen & Lakens, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459211007467)). The confidence is not higher because agent tasks are adaptive, non-IID, and model-mediated, and I found no direct trial of this complete mechanism for cross-session agent memory.

The transfer is deliberately partial:

* **Preregistration / Registered Reports:** transfers strongly at the *policy and evaluation* layer, not as bureaucracy for every note.
* **Power and precision planning:** transfers as “how much evidence is enough before promotion or retirement,” but classical IID power calculations do not transfer unchanged.
* **Blinding:** transfers to output assessment and adjudication, not to the working agent, which must receive the memory to use it.
* **Negative controls:** the logic transfers, but a literal prompt-injected sham memory generally fails the required no-causal-effect assumption. It should not be called a valid negative control.

The proposed architecture rides the existing hook/skill/evaluation bus and uses ordinary files plus a small deterministic policy hook. It does not introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. That passes the stated anti-overfit gate.

## 1. What experimental design solved in its own field

### 1.1 Preregistration: bind the question and decision rule before seeing the outcome

In confirmatory research, an investigator can make many defensible choices after seeing data: change the outcome, exclusions, subgroup, stopping point, transformation, or hypothesis. That flexibility makes an apparently confirmatory result partly exploratory. Preregistration creates a time-stamped protocol before data collection or analysis, documenting hypotheses, methods, outcomes, exclusions, sampling, and analysis. Its key epistemic function is not to prohibit deviations; it is to expose which decisions preceded the result and which followed it. Nosek and colleagues describe the benefit as increasing the distinction between hypothesis generation and hypothesis testing ([PNAS, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1816418115)).

Registered Reports add a stronger incentive mechanism. A journal reviews the question and method before outcomes exist, grants in-principle acceptance, and later publishes regardless of result direction if the approved protocol and quality conditions were followed. The format therefore separates selection on methodological quality from selection on an attractive result. The [Center for Open Science overview](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports) and [Scientific Reports policy](https://www.nature.com/srep/journal-policies/registered-reports) specify the two-stage process, including hypotheses, procedures, analysis pipeline, and sampling/power plan at Stage 1.

Important limit: preregistration alone is not a magic causal guarantee. Scheel et al. explain that ordinary preregistration does not stop an investigator from file-drawering an unhelpful study; Registered Reports address that by committing to publication before outcomes ([Scheel et al., sections “Methods to Mitigate Bias” and “Limitations”](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459211007467)). Their 96% versus 44% comparison is strong evidence of a distributional difference, but **likely (55-80%)**, not almost certain, evidence that the format itself caused most of it: the authors identify non-random assignment, author selection, editorial policy, and coding limitations.

### 1.2 A priori power and precision: decide what “enough evidence” means before looking

Power analysis asks how many observations a design needs to detect a prespecified effect with a chosen Type II error rate, conditional on assumptions about effect size and data generation. The valuable mechanism is prospective calibration: define the *smallest effect size of interest* and the acceptable error rates before observing a favorable or unfavorable sequence.

Lakens emphasizes two details especially relevant here:

1. Power is conditional on an assumed effect size; it never guarantees power for the unknown true effect.
2. A design can plan both to detect benefit and to reject benefits large enough to matter using an equivalence test. Failure to find a significant benefit is not evidence that useful benefit is absent.

The paper also says every primary test needs a sample-size justification and multiplicity corrections must be included in that calculation ([Lakens, “Sample Size Justification,” 2022](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267)). This solves two common reasoning errors: declaring victory after a lucky small sample and declaring “no effect” after an uninformative small sample.

Adaptive experimental designs show that prospectivity does not require a frozen fixed sample size. The FDA defines an adaptive design as one with *prospectively planned* modifications based on accumulating data that preserve integrity and validity ([FDA adaptive-design guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/adaptive-designs-medical-device-clinical-studies)). The transferable principle is prespecified interim looks and stopping/adaptation rules, not a perpetual license to change thresholds after every observation.

### 1.3 Blinding: keep treatment knowledge away from subjective outcome judgment

Blinding prevents a participant, operator, analyst, or assessor from letting treatment assignment influence behavior or measurement. For memory, the closest analogue is the outcome assessor: if it knows “this answer used our new memory feature,” expectations can affect a subjective quality score.

The empirical literature is nuanced, which matters for the analogy. A review of 24 trials with both blinded and non-blinded assessors of the same subjective scale outcome reported that non-blinded assessment exaggerated pooled effect size by 68%, with a wide 95% interval from 14% to 230% ([Hróbjartsson et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.120744)). A later large meta-epidemiological study found no evidence of an average association between blinding and estimated effects across its sample and stressed variation and limitations in blinding classification ([Moustgaard et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l6802)). Therefore, it is **likely (55-80%)** that label-blinding improves subjective agent-output comparisons when evaluators have a stake or expectation, supported by the within-trial evidence and direct bias pathway, but the effect size in agent evaluation is **roughly even (45-55%)** to be operationally large because the broader clinical literature is heterogeneous and the domain differs.

Allocation concealment is distinct from assessor blinding: the person enrolling or selecting cases must not foresee assignment. The [CONSORT 2010 statement](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c332) requires reporting the actual concealment mechanism rather than a vague assurance. In agent evaluation, deterministic but hidden condition assignment prevents task selection from drifting toward examples favorable to the memory.

### 1.4 Negative controls: test whether the apparatus produces an effect where causality says it cannot

A negative control is not merely “some unrelated example.” It is an exposure or outcome selected so that it shares the bias-producing pathways of the target comparison but lacks the causal path being tested. If an effect appears on the negative control, the apparatus has detected confounding, selection, measurement error, recall bias, or an analytic flaw.

Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen distinguish negative-control exposures and outcomes and state demanding structural conditions ([Epidemiology, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)):

* A negative-control outcome should share as nearly as possible the common causes of the exposure and real outcome, but not be caused by the exposure.
* A negative-control exposure should share the uncontrolled causes of the real exposure, but not cause the real outcome.
* Approximate comparability can yield either false alarms or false reassurance.
* A failed negative control detects that some bias exists but generally does not identify the bias or quantify it.

Subject-matter causal knowledge, not superficial similarity, establishes control validity. This is the most important caveat for memory.

## 2. Translation: a Registered Memory Lifecycle (RML)

### 2.1 The conceptual mapping

| Experimental-design object | Memory object | Why the mapping is structural rather than metaphorical |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothesis | “For tasks in scope S, recalling candidate M improves primary outcome Y without exceeding harm guardrail H.” | Both are prospective, falsifiable effect claims. |
| Intervention | Inject/retrieve M versus suppress M | Memory exposure is manipulable at retrieval time. |
| Unit of assignment | Session, task, or paired replay | Condition assignment can occur before the answer is generated. |
| Primary outcome | Owner correction, factual error, completion, regression, or cost metric | Prespecification blocks swapping to whichever metric improved. |
| Smallest effect of interest | Minimum benefit worth context, latency, privacy, and staleness risk | It defines when retention is worthwhile rather than merely detectable. |
| Exclusion / protocol deviation | Invalid run, unavailable tool, contaminated pair, manual override | Deviations stay possible but become visible. |
| Interim analysis | Scheduled evidence review / source-change trigger | A memory can be reevaluated without optional-stopping theater. |
| Equivalence / non-inferiority margin | Evidence that any remaining benefit is too small to justify retention, while safety is not worse | It prevents “not significant” from being treated as “useless.” |
| Blinded assessor | Judge sees answer pair and rubric, but not memory condition, feature name, or preferred variant | It cuts the direct expectation-to-score path. |
| Registered Report | Candidate memory is accepted for a fair trial before outcomes, and every result—including no benefit or harm—is retained in the ledger | It prevents a “file drawer” containing only successful memories. |

The causal unit is the *retrieval event*, not the stored record. A memory can exist without being retrieved; retrieval can expose multiple memories; and an answer can itself create later memories. The design must log assignment, actual exposure, co-retrieved memories, model/version, task family, and outcome. An intention-to-treat view (“assigned to memory-on”) estimates policy effect; a per-exposure view (“memory actually appeared in context”) answers a different and more confounded question. Keeping both is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent misleading attribution, because retrieval failures and co-retrieval are direct post-assignment selection mechanisms; this confidence rests on causal structure rather than a memory-specific trial.

### 2.2 The prospective record

#### UNVERIFIED integration premise — candidate record and policy hook

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a versioned `memory-trials.jsonl` ledger plus a deterministic admission/review hook. The premise that the available hook bus exposes memory-candidate creation, retrieval assignment, and session outcome is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a four-event smoke test that emits a nonce candidate, assignment, actual retrieval exposure, and outcome, then verifies all four reach a temporary ledger**. If any event is unavailable, implement only the observable part and record the gap; do not create a daemon.

Each candidate version should contain:

```text
memory_id, version, content_hash
claim                    # atomic factual/procedural/preference claim
evidence_refs            # source identifiers, timestamps, hashes when available
scope                    # task/repo/user/tool/model applicability
utility_hypothesis       # expected benefit and mechanism
primary_outcome          # exactly one default decision metric
secondary_outcomes       # reported, not silently substituted
harm_guardrails          # factual error, privacy, cost, latency, owner override
minimum_worthwhile_effect
assignment_unit          # session/task/paired replay
strata                   # task family, model, repository, etc.
invalidators             # observable facts that would make content stale
review_triggers          # source drift, contradiction, error, age fallback
promotion_rule
retirement_rule
planned_sample_or_precision
interim_look_schedule
created_at, next_review_at
status                   # quarantine | trial | active | suspect | retired
amendments[]             # timestamp, old/new value, reason, before/after outcome access
```

The content and protocol are separate versions. Correcting the claim creates a new content version; changing the metric or threshold creates a protocol amendment. Old versions remain auditable. “Forget” means suppress from retrieval and preserve a compact tombstone with reason and evidence, not immediate destructive deletion. Hard deletion remains available for privacy, security, owner request, or policy requirements and should not await an experiment.

This record catches:

* **retention HARKing:** a memory is credited with whatever later success happens to look favorable;
* **metric switching:** factual quality worsens but token savings becomes the post-hoc justification;
* **scope laundering:** a local rule becomes a universal rule after one successful use;
* **silent policy drift:** the reason and threshold for retention change after outcomes are seen;
* **success-only memory history:** failed candidates disappear, so the system repeatedly rediscovers them;
* **unfalsifiable staleness:** no one wrote down what evidence would invalidate the memory.

A small deterministic ledger is **likely (55-80%)** to deliver most of this value at low complexity because the mechanism is prospective disclosure, not sophisticated storage. The lower bound reflects **UNVERIFIED operational fit; the settling experiment is the hook smoke test above plus a 30-candidate pilot measuring missing fields, operator overrides, and added prompt/runtime cost**.

### 2.3 What should be remembered

#### UNVERIFIED operational thresholds — quarantine and promotion

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Define a policy that new inferred memories begin in `quarantine`, while explicit durable owner instructions may enter `active` immediately with provenance and scope. The exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with a prospective pilot stratified by explicit owner instruction, observed correction, inferred preference, factual environment claim, and reusable procedure, comparing false-promotion and missed-benefit costs**.

Promotion requires all of the following:

1. **Provenance adequacy.** The source is recorded. An assertion inferred from behavior is not silently upgraded into an owner preference.
2. **Scope.** The record says where it applies and where it does not.
3. **Falsifiability.** At least one observable invalidator or a documented reason why none exists.
4. **Prospective utility claim.** The primary outcome and minimum worthwhile effect were fixed before evaluation.
5. **Evidence adequate for the decision.** Either benefit clears the promotion threshold, or the item belongs to a policy class allowed immediate activation (for example, an explicit owner instruction).
6. **Guardrails.** No prespecified unacceptable harm appears.

This policy deliberately distinguishes *truth* from *utility*. A claim can be true but too narrow or costly to retrieve. A procedure can improve average completion while being factually stale in one environment. Promotion therefore needs content validation and effect evaluation; one cannot substitute for the other.

### 2.4 How much evidence before remembering or forgetting

#### UNVERIFIED statistical model — precision-planned, paired evaluation

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a trial planner that randomizes memory-on/off within prespecified strata, prefers paired replay when safe and representative, and reports effect intervals plus guardrails. Classical power calculations are **UNVERIFIED for these non-IID streams; settle the model with a replay study that estimates within-task correlation, between-task heterogeneity, time drift, carryover, and model-version interaction, then simulation-calibrates false-promotion and false-retirement rates**.

Use this decision structure:

* Specify the smallest benefit that repays retrieval cost and risk.
* For promotion, require the benefit interval or posterior decision criterion to clear that margin and all harm guardrails.
* For retirement due to “no utility,” require an equivalence-style result showing that benefits large enough to matter are implausible. A non-significant benefit alone is inconclusive.
* For retirement due to harm or falsity, allow faster prespecified stopping. Safety and source invalidation need not wait for utility equivalence.
* Set interim looks in advance or use a validated anytime-valid method; do not inspect daily and stop at the first favorable fluctuation.
* Treat task families and model versions as strata, not IID rows. Report heterogeneity; do not let a large easy-task class erase harm in a rare critical class.
* Preselect one primary outcome. Correct or hierarchically gate promotion when many memories or outcomes are tested, because multiplicity otherwise produces “winning” memories by chance.

Precision planning is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent both premature deletion and immortal low-value memories. Evidence: the error is exactly the one equivalence testing and a priori sample justification address in experiments ([Lakens, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267)); confidence remains bounded because task dependence, carryover, and changing models can make nominal intervals miscalibrated.

It catches:

* forgetting after two unlucky uses;
* keeping a memory forever because “absence of benefit was never proved”;
* optional stopping after a lucky streak;
* selecting the best of many outcomes without multiplicity control;
* a globally positive average that masks a harmful subgroup;
* judging an uninformative trial as proof of no effect.

### 2.5 How to notice that memory has gone stale

#### UNVERIFIED trigger coverage — prespecified invalidation and scheduled review

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Require each factual or procedural memory to declare invalidators and review triggers at creation. Trigger coverage is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by sampling historical changes from source-controlled facts and measuring whether proposed triggers would have fired before the first stale retrieval, using a held-out set when choosing trigger templates**.

Use a three-channel stale detector:

1. **Direct invalidation (truth channel).** Source hash/version changes; command or capability is removed; authoritative value conflicts; owner explicitly corrects the memory; a scoped environment identifier changes. These move the item to `suspect` immediately.
2. **Behavioral invalidation (effect channel).** Prespecified errors, corrections, regressions, or guardrail breaches rise in memory-on relative to memory-off. This catches harmful content even when the nominal source has not changed.
3. **Calendar backstop (coverage channel).** `next_review_at` forces reconsideration when no machine-readable source or invalidator exists. Age alone is not proof of staleness; it is evidence that validation is due.

At review, choose among `revalidated`, `narrowed scope`, `superseded`, `retired`, and `hard delete`. Record the adjudicating evidence. Never refresh `last_verified_at` merely because the memory was retrieved without an obvious failure; silent success is not a truth check.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect some stale memories earlier than TTL-only expiry, because direct source drift and explicit contradictions contain information that age discards. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to achieve high recall, because many world changes have no observable local signal and because an invalidator list can itself be incomplete. The held-out historical-change experiment above settles recall and lead time.

It catches:

* a fact that changes one day after a long TTL begins;
* a permanently valid owner preference mistakenly expired by age;
* a memory whose source looks unchanged but whose observed effect becomes harmful after a model/tool update;
* “freshness by use,” where frequent retrieval indefinitely renews an obsolete claim;
* a correction applied to content without narrowing the scope that caused the error.

### 2.6 Blinded evaluation and allocation concealment

#### UNVERIFIED evaluator susceptibility — masked pairwise adjudication

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** For offline or shadow evaluation, show the assessor randomly ordered answer pairs with feature names, memory IDs, condition labels, and protocol preference removed. Preserve a sealed mapping for later analysis. The evaluator’s susceptibility is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a randomized crossover in which the same answer pairs are scored once with condition labels and once without, counterbalancing order and measuring label-by-score interaction**.

Condition assignment must occur before task execution from a committed task list or hidden random seed. The selector must not choose the “good demo” after learning which condition it will receive. Log unblinding: answers may reveal that they contain a remembered fact, so masking is not always successful.

Do **not** blind the working agent to memory content. That destroys the intervention: an agent cannot use a hidden instruction. Do not hide provenance from safety checks either. Blinding belongs at subjective assessment and, where feasible, task/condition allocation.

This catches:

* evaluators rewarding the expected “memory-on” answer;
* cherry-picking tasks after condition assignment;
* a feature-name halo effect;
* post-hoc rubric changes favoring the preferred architecture.

Masked assessment is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce expectation bias in subjective ratings, based on the direct causal pathway and blinded-assessor literature cited above. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to provide complete masking because distinctive remembered details can reveal condition; the settling crossover must report guessed condition as a diagnostic, not treat a guess test as proof of perfect blinding.

## 3. Negative controls: where the analogy breaks

### 3.1 Why a “sham memory” is not a valid negative-control exposure

The tempting design is to inject a same-length, same-style but irrelevant memory and expect no effect. That does not satisfy the no-causal-path condition. Any text added to an LLM context can causally alter token attention, instruction interpretation, latency, truncation, or output distribution. A semantically irrelevant paragraph is therefore not inert. It may share context-cost pathways with real memory, but those are causal effects of the control itself, not only confounding pathways.

The claim that a prompt-injected sham memory is a valid negative control is **very unlikely (5-20%)** under ordinary transformer inference. Evidence: the control necessarily changes the model input and token budget, while valid negative-control exposure logic requires that the control not cause the target outcome ([Lipsitch et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). The residual probability covers a narrowly proven invariant apparatus where a masked or out-of-band field demonstrably cannot reach generation; that premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by information-flow inspection plus randomized equivalence tests across adversarial tasks and context lengths**.

Accordingly:

* Call an injected irrelevant memory a **placebo/context-interference stress arm**, not a negative control.
* If it changes outcomes, conclude only that context injection or content interference matters; do not infer that the real memory’s measured benefit is confounded by the same amount.
* If it does not change outcomes, do not infer that the real comparison is unbiased. Approximate controls can miss a confound, exactly as Lipsitch et al. warn.

### 3.2 What can be salvaged from negative-control logic

#### UNVERIFIED causal exclusion — shadow negative-control outcomes

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add prespecified *diagnostic outcomes* that are outside the memory’s intended scope, but label them “negative-control candidates” until causal exclusion is defended. For example, a repository formatting memory might be tested against an unrelated arithmetic outcome only if the design can argue that retrieval cannot affect arithmetic through attention, truncation, latency, or instruction competition. That argument will usually fail for in-context injection.

Validity is **UNVERIFIED for every candidate; settle each with an explicit causal diagram, an information-flow review, and equivalence data showing the proposed control remains insensitive to the memory while a known-positive control demonstrates that the detector can respond**. This meets the two-signal requirement for any later absence claim: structural exclusion is one signal, calibrated behavioral equivalence is another, and the known-positive control validates detector sensitivity.

The useful retained discipline is:

1. State the causal path the control lacks.
2. State the nuisance paths it shares with the real outcome/exposure.
3. Include a known-positive control to show the diagnostic apparatus can detect a deliberately induced failure.
4. Treat control failure as an alarm, not a diagnosis or bias correction.
5. Treat control success as narrow evidence, not proof of no bias.

This discipline is **likely (55-80%)** to catch evaluator leakage, metric bugs, and selection artifacts when a genuine out-of-band control exists; evidence is the established negative-control causal logic. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to yield many valid controls inside the generation context because prompt changes usually have plausible causal routes to output.

### 3.3 A better control family for this target

Rather than force negative controls, use four explicit arms when cost permits:

* **Memory off:** baseline policy.
* **Memory on:** candidate intervention.
* **Context-interference stress:** matched token/placement payload, interpreted only as a stress test.
* **Known-positive failure:** a deliberately stale or contradictory memory in a sandboxed, non-production task that the detector is expected to flag.

#### UNVERIFIED safety premise — sandboxed positive control

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Run the known-positive failure only in isolated evaluation tasks with no external side effects. Isolation is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by a dry-run trace proving tools and writes are disabled, followed by a nonce canary that would reveal any escape without changing subject state**.

This control family does not magically identify causal bias. It does validate the stale-memory detector on a known failure, separates generic context overhead from candidate-specific behavior, and prevents a zero-alert detector from being celebrated without a sensitivity control. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch completely inert detectors in the sandbox because the known-positive item supplies a ground-truth expected alert; it is only **likely (55-80%)** to diagnose partial detector failures because real stale memories are more heterogeneous than the synthetic control.

## 4. End-to-end decision protocol

### Stage A — admission

1. Capture the candidate and immutable provenance.
2. Classify it: explicit owner instruction, inferred preference, fact, procedure, failure lesson, or ephemeral context.
3. Write scope, contraindications, invalidators, and expiry/review backstop.
4. Declare the prospective utility claim, primary outcome, minimum worthwhile effect, guardrails, assignment unit, planned evidence, and stopping rule.
5. Hash the protocol and test set before outcome access.
6. Put inferred candidates in quarantine; allow explicit owner policy to activate under the configured exception.

### Stage B — fair trial

1. Assign memory-on/off before execution, stratified by task family and model version.
2. Log assignment and actual exposure separately.
3. Preserve co-retrieved memory IDs and protocol deviations.
4. Score objective outcomes mechanically where possible.
5. For subjective outcomes, use masked, randomized answer order and a fixed rubric.
6. Report every candidate, including null and harmful results; do not maintain only a “successful memories” table.

### Stage C — decision

* **Promote** if prospective benefit and guardrails pass.
* **Continue trial** if evidence remains uninformative and expected value of more information justifies cost.
* **Narrow scope** if benefit/harm is heterogeneous in a reproducible, prospectively confirmable way.
* **Retire for no value** only after the equivalence/no-worthwhile-benefit condition passes.
* **Suspend or retire for harm/falsity** immediately when a prespecified safety or direct invalidation condition passes.
* **Amend** transparently when exploration reveals a better hypothesis; treat the amended claim as a new confirmatory test.

### Stage D — continuing validity

1. Watch direct source/version/owner-correction invalidators.
2. Watch memory-on versus memory-off behavioral guardrails at planned interim points.
3. Review at the calendar backstop if no direct signal arrives.
4. On model, tool, repository, or environment boundary change, re-stratify rather than silently pooling old evidence.
5. Retain a compact tombstone after ordinary retirement so the same disproven candidate is not repeatedly relearned.

This protocol is **likely (55-80%)** to improve decision traceability even if it does not improve task success, because every disposition has a prospective criterion and retained outcome. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** to improve aggregate task success without careful triage, because evaluating low-impact memories can consume more cost and context than it saves. The settling experiment is a time-boxed randomized pilot measuring correction rate, severe error rate, completion, token/latency cost, operator burden, and repeated rediscovery against the existing policy.

## 5. Adversarial transfer audit

### Assumption 1: stable treatment

Experiments often assume a well-defined intervention. A memory’s effect depends on model version, surrounding prompt, retrieval order, other memories, and task. “Memory M” is not one stable treatment across all contexts.

**Consequence:** version content and retrieval policy; record co-exposures; stratify by model/task; do not transport an effect estimate across a boundary without revalidation. The analogy survives only locally.

### Assumption 2: independent or exchangeable units

Sessions can share user state, generated artifacts, and newly written memories. Replaying a task may contaminate later runs. Task streams drift, and easy repetitive tasks can dominate counts.

**Consequence:** randomize at the highest unit needed to avoid carryover, use paired replays only in side-effect-free sandboxes, cluster uncertainty, and simulate operating characteristics from observed dependence. Naive binomial “20 wins out of 30” power is not justified.

### Assumption 3: outcomes are measured after treatment but do not rewrite treatment

Agent outcomes can create new memory, alter repositories, or prompt owner corrections that influence later runs. This feedback is central, not incidental.

**Consequence:** distinguish immediate answer effect from longitudinal policy effect. Freeze candidate-writing during a paired offline evaluation or treat the whole adaptive policy as the intervention in a cluster/time-block experiment.

### Assumption 4: withholding treatment is ethical and safe

Suppressing a security instruction, explicit owner constraint, or known correctness rule for an A/B test may be unacceptable.

**Consequence:** never randomize mandatory safety, privacy, legal, or explicit owner constraints. Evaluate those in replay or compare delivery mechanisms while preserving the constraint. Experimental rigor does not override authority or safety.

### Assumption 5: there is an inert placebo

As shown above, injected text is not inert. This disanalogy is fatal to literal negative-control memory injection.

**Consequence:** downgrade sham prompts to stress arms and demand causal exclusion before using the negative-control label.

### Assumption 6: the cost of preregistration is small relative to the experiment

Scientific trials may justify a long protocol. Agents can generate thousands of tiny candidate memories; full preregistration per note would be bureaucratic overfit.

**Consequence:** triage. Ephemeral session context is not cross-session memory. Explicit owner constraints use a short predefined protocol class. Only inferred durable claims with material expected reuse receive a trial. Templates populate defaults. This makes the mechanism **likely (55-80%)** to remain under the anti-overfit threshold; evidence is that the architecture is one ledger and hook, but operator burden remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the 30-candidate pilot and reject the design if median manual handling exceeds the prespecified budget**.

### Assumption 7: “forgetting” means deleting data

In experimentation, rejecting a treatment does not erase the trial record. In memory systems, ordinary forgetting should stop retrieval, while provenance and a tombstone prevent rediscovery. But privacy erasure may require actual deletion.

**Consequence:** separate retrieval retirement, archival compaction, and hard deletion. No experiment delays a mandatory deletion.

### Overall verdict on transfer

The analogy **does transfer, but only at the decision-governance and evaluation layers**. That verdict is **likely (55-80%)**, supported by exact structural matches—prospective hypotheses, manipulable retrieval, outcomes, assessor expectation, repeated looks—and bounded by treatment instability, dependence, feedback, and safety constraints. Literal transplantation of IID power formulas, runtime blinding, or sham-memory negative controls is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be valid without additional proof, for the disanalogies above.

## 6. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

### Proposed minimal components

1. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** — one schema/policy defining candidate classes, default outcomes, invalidators, margins, review triggers, and safety exemptions.
2. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** — one deterministic hook that appends candidate, assignment, exposure, outcome, amendment, and disposition events to JSONL and computes due reviews.
3. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** — existing evaluator configuration for random condition assignment, masked answer order, a fixed rubric, and a sealed mapping.
4. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)** — where the native platform already exposes session hooks, subagent isolation, or task replay, use those facilities rather than a second runner. Exact native availability is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by consulting the installed version’s official capability documentation and a nonce smoke test before assigning this tag in implementation**.

The fourth item is a conditional preference, not a claim that a specific native facility exists locally. If the event bus cannot support the design, the correct next step is to reduce scope or report the gap, not introduce a service by default.

### Components explicitly rejected

* A vector database or knowledge graph to model trials: unnecessary; the proposal needs append-only structured events and small indexes.
* A durable-execution engine: unnecessary; overdue review can be checked at session start/end.
* A second orchestration runtime: unnecessary and risks interacting policies.
* A trained critic: unnecessary; use objective outcomes and fixed masked rubrics first.
* Observability SaaS: unnecessary; local structured events are sufficient for the decision record.
* A guardrail framework: unnecessary; guardrails are fields and deterministic stopping conditions in the existing bus.

No rejected component is reintroduced. The proposal’s novelty is epistemic discipline—prospective contracts, honest uncertainty, and calibrated controls—not infrastructure.

## 7. Recommended experiments, in order

### Experiment 0 — integration feasibility (no production memory change)

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** In a temporary evaluation scope, emit a nonce candidate, random assignment, actual retrieval exposure, and outcome. Verify the event chain and a known-present hook/control through mechanically independent signals: event-bus trace and ledger record. Pass only if both agree and a deliberately omitted event is detected as missing.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to resolve whether the minimal architecture can ride the bus, because it tests the exact required events end to end. It will not establish usefulness.

### Experiment 1 — retrospective trigger coverage

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Sample historical source changes and corrections; develop invalidator templates on one split and measure stale-detection recall, false-alert rate, and lead time on a held-out split. Include known-present changed items to validate the detector and use both source-diff evidence and correction/error evidence before declaring a stale item detected.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to identify which invalidators are worth automating; it may underrepresent world changes never recorded locally.

### Experiment 2 — blinded-assessor crossover

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Have evaluators score identical answer pairs labeled versus masked, with order counterbalanced and condition guesses collected. Pre-register score difference and inter-rater agreement as outcomes.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to quantify label bias in the actual evaluator population, which is more relevant than transporting a clinical meta-analysis effect size.

### Experiment 3 — 30-candidate prospective pilot

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Stratify 30 candidates across owner instructions, preferences, facts, procedures, and failure lessons. Freeze outcomes and thresholds before exposure. Measure missing protocol fields, useful promotions, harmful promotions, correct retirements, inconclusive cases, operator minutes, context cost, and repeated candidate rediscovery. Preserve all outcomes.

Thirty is an operational pilot size, **not** a powered efficacy sample. Whether it is enough to estimate workflow burden is **likely (55-80%)** if the candidate mix is deliberately stratified; whether it is enough to estimate rare severe harm is **very unlikely (5-20%)**. Set the subsequent sample by simulation from pilot heterogeneity rather than retroactively claiming power.

### Experiment 4 — randomized policy evaluation

**Proposal — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Compare the registered lifecycle with the existing policy at the session or task-cluster level, preserving mandatory memories in both arms. Pre-register one primary utility outcome, safety guardrails, minimum worthwhile effect, interim rules, model/version strata, and an equivalence retirement analysis. Use a committed task stream and masked adjudication where possible.

The experiment is **likely (55-80%)** to settle whether the policy earns its overhead in the measured task distribution, provided contamination is controlled. Generalization to new models or task distributions remains **unlikely (20-45%)** without revalidation because treatment effects can interact with both.

### Rejection criteria

Reject or radically simplify RML if the pilot shows any prespecified condition such as:

* required events cannot be observed without a new daemon;
* manual protocol burden exceeds the value of prevented corrections;
* masking almost always fails and objective outcomes are unavailable;
* task dependence makes practical uncertainty unresolvable at affordable sample sizes;
* invalidator templates have poor held-out recall and high alert burden;
* randomized withholding would cover too few non-mandatory memories to answer the question.

These criteria keep the borrow falsifiable. Experimental-design language must not become a ceremonial wrapper around untestable memory decisions.

## 8. Failure taxonomy: what this lens newly catches

| Failure | Why an ordinary memory store misses it | Experimental-design mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| A memory is retained because a later success is narratively attributed to it | No counterfactual or prospective outcome | Registered utility hypothesis + randomized exposure |
| A failed memory vanishes and is proposed again months later | Only successful items survive | Registered-Report-style all-results ledger + tombstone |
| No observed benefit is mistaken for no useful benefit | Low exposure count is treated as evidence | A priori precision + equivalence margin |
| A memory stays forever because every noisy interim look can be rationalized | No stopping rule | Prespecified interim and retirement rule |
| A local exception becomes a universal rule | Scope decided after outcomes | Preregistered scope and strata |
| Evaluator prefers the memory feature it helped design | Treatment label reaches subjective assessor | Masked answer order and fixed rubric |
| Retrieval changes after favorable tasks are selected | Assignment is foreseeable | Allocation concealment / committed task list |
| Age-based TTL misses a source change tomorrow | Time is used as a proxy for invalidation | Prespecified direct invalidators + calendar backstop |
| Frequent use refreshes a false claim | “No complaint” is treated as validation | Separate retrieval from truth verification |
| Detector reports no stale memories because it is inert | No sensitivity control | Sandboxed known-positive failure + independent event evidence |
| Irrelevant prompt changes are misread as proof of confounding | Sham text is mislabeled as inert | Negative-control causal assumptions reject the inference |
| One average conceals harm in a critical task family | IID pooling | Stratified outcomes and heterogeneity guardrails |
| Metric or threshold changes after seeing results | No protocol version | Timestamped amendments and confirmatory reset |

No table row asserts that this local harness presently exhibits the failure. Local incidence is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the read-only inventory and controlled pilot stated above**.

## 9. Confidence ledger

* **Registered lifecycle reduces hindsight and metric switching — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: direct procedural match to preregistration plus Registered Report distributional evidence; bounded by lack of randomized agent-memory evidence and local integration data.
* **Explicit invalidators plus scheduled review beat TTL alone for some stale changes — very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: direct source/owner contradiction is more specific than elapsed time; bounded because not all changes emit observable signals.
* **High stale-detection recall from invalidators — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: multiple channels cover source, behavior, and age; bounded by incomplete causal knowledge. Settling experiment: held-out historical-change replay.
* **Equivalence-style retirement reduces premature forgetting — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: it directly prevents interpreting an underpowered null as absence; bounded by non-IID calibration.
* **Masked assessment reduces subjective evaluator bias — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: plausible direct pathway and within-trial blinded-assessor evidence; bounded by heterogeneous meta-epidemiology and possible condition revelation.
* **Literal injected sham memories are valid negative controls — very unlikely (5-20%).** Evidence: changed prompt/context is itself a causal intervention, violating exclusion.
* **A JSONL ledger plus one small hook is enough — likely (55-80%).** Evidence: decision state is small and append-only; integration is UNVERIFIED. Settling experiment: four-event nonce smoke test.
* **The full policy improves aggregate task success — roughly even (45-55%).** Evidence: it targets real decision biases but imposes evaluation and operational cost; settling experiment: randomized policy evaluation.
* **The design remains useful after a model/task-distribution change without revalidation — unlikely (20-45%).** Evidence: memory effects depend on model, prompt, retrieval, and task interactions.

## 10. Gaps and evidence limits

1. Direct evidence for this complete mechanism in cross-session LLM memory was not located in the source-field research reviewed here. The proposal is a structural translation, not an established agent-memory best practice.
2. No local harness capability or absence claim was tested by design. All local integration, duplication, and incidence premises are marked UNVERIFIED inline with settling experiments.
3. Registered Report evidence is not a randomized causal estimate. The strongest cited comparison explicitly reports selection and editorial confounds.
4. Clinical blinding effects are heterogeneous; they motivate an actual local crossover rather than supplying a borrowed effect size.
5. Classical power numbers cannot be responsibly supplied before estimating task clustering, carryover, drift, and the minimum worthwhile effect.
6. Negative-control validity is candidate-specific and causal. No generic in-context negative control is endorsed.
7. Privacy and mandatory deletion are outside experimental optimization: authority and policy override retention trials.
