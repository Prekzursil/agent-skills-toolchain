# P2A18 — Published agent-failure taxonomies mapped to this owner’s measured failures

**Unit:** P2A18 — research: failure-taxonomy  
**Date:** 2026-07-18  
**Lens:** external research. Local repository implementation was deliberately not inspected; the only owner-specific inputs used are the measured facts pinned in the task.  
**Bottom line:** the measured corpus is a good detector for *loud, deterministic interface and infrastructure faults*. It is not evidence that the harness is good at planning, memory, reflection, semantic correctness, verification, multi-agent coordination, entity binding, security, or evaluator validity. For almost every category that appears “never observed,” the correct verdict is **blind**, not **good**.

## Method and honesty controls

I searched for empirical and published failure taxonomies, then deep-read the most relevant papers, official repositories, and standards material. The anchors are: AgentErrorTaxonomy/AgentDebug (17 errors across memory, reflection, planning, action, and system), MAST (14 multi-agent failures), Aegis (six agent–environment failures), AgentRx (ten cross-domain trajectory failures), Xiong et al.’s five tool-parameter failures, OWASP’s Agentic Top 10, and NIST’s agent-tool and evaluation-validity work. I also used benchmark-validity and coding-agent studies to test whether “success” labels are trustworthy.

The search used the live web index available to this unit. Firecrawl, Exa, Tavily, Perplexity, scholarly-access, Hugging Face, Context7, and SearXNG were not exposed as callable providers here, so provider-level cross-corroboration was unavailable. I compensated by preferring primary paper pages, official repositories, official standards bodies, and publisher pages, and by checking key definitions against both paper and code where available.

The absence protocol was:

1. **Known-present controls:** the supplied per-tool error metrics detect known hard failures (for example `not connected`, schema mismatch, `ENAMETOOLONG`, unmatched quotes, `ECONNREFUSED`, and unread-file preconditions). The retry-loop fingerprints detect known repeated-action behavior. Therefore those detectors are demonstrated capable of finding hard exceptions and repeated fingerprints.
2. **Two independent supplied signal families:** (a) tool call/error fingerprints and retry-loop data; (b) owner-correction candidates/capability-use counts. Neither family provides adjudicated labels for planning, reflection, memory, verification, semantic correctness, security, or entity identity. The second family is independent of the first, but its entries are *candidates*, not ground truth.
3. Consequently, I report “not evidenced by the supplied measurements” rather than “did not occur.” Where neither signal family is a valid detector, the verdict is **blind**. This avoids converting detector silence into a quality claim.

## 1. What is actually established

### 1.1 There is no single sufficient taxonomy

The literature separates at least four different objects that are often conflated:

- **Agent cognitive/behavioral errors.** AgentDebug defines 17 types in five modules: memory (hallucination, retrieval failure, oversimplification), reflection (progress misjudgment, outcome misinterpretation, causal misattribution, hallucination), planning (constraint ignorance, impossible action, inefficient plan), action (misalignment, invalid action, format error, parameter error), and system (step limit, tool execution error, LLM limit, environment error). The official repository exposes both the taxonomy and its definitions, and the paper evaluates annotated trajectories from ALFWorld, GAIA, and WebShop: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370), [official repository taxonomy](https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/AgentDebug#failure-taxonomy).
- **Multi-agent organizational failures.** MAST identifies 14 modes under specification/system design, inter-agent misalignment, and task verification/termination. The modes include task/role disobedience, step repetition, loss of conversation history, unawareness of termination conditions, conversation reset, failure to ask for clarification, derailment, information withholding, ignoring another agent, reasoning–action mismatch, premature termination, and missing/incomplete/incorrect verification. Its taxonomy came from expert annotation of five systems with reported Cohen’s κ=0.88 and was later applied to 1,642 traces: [MAST paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657).
- **Agent–environment failures.** Aegis distinguishes exploration failures (state-space navigation and state awareness), exploitation failures (tool-output processing, domain-rule violation, and user-instruction failure), and resource exhaustion. It annotates the first consequential failed subtask rather than every transient exception, an important distinction from the owner’s raw error fingerprints: [Aegis paper and taxonomy table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19504).
- **Software/integration faults.** A 2026 empirical study sampled 385 faults from 13,602 issues and pull requests across 40 agentic repositories and explicitly separates 37 fault types, 13 symptom classes, and 12 root-cause categories. It reports recurring mismatches between probabilistic artifacts and deterministic interfaces, especially dependency integration, data validation, and runtime-environment handling: [Shah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06847). This distinction is essential: `ECONNREFUSED` is a symptom; a dead service, bad routing, authentication, or network policy can be the cause.

These taxonomies are complementary rather than interchangeable. A schema mismatch can simultaneously be an AgentDebug action-format/parameter error, an AgentRx invalid invocation, an Aegis domain-rule violation, and a software integration/data-validation fault. Counting those as four incidents would be wrong; recording four *facets of one incident* is useful.

### 1.2 Tool-call failures are better understood than most other agent failures

The peer-reviewed EMNLP Findings study by Xiong et al. derives five parameter-failure classes: missing information, redundant information, hallucinated parameter name, task deviation, and specification mismatch. It perturbs user queries, tool documentation, and tool returns, and recommends standardized return formats, clearer error feedback, and parameter consistency: [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.907/), [PDF taxonomy and definitions](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/findings/2025.findings-emnlp.907.pdf).

AgentRx independently offers a cross-domain ten-way taxonomy: instruction/plan adherence, invention of information, invalid invocation, tool-output misinterpretation, intent–plan misalignment, underspecified user intent, unsupported intent, guardrail-triggered, system failure, and inconclusive. Its implementation normalizes traces, derives invariants, checks them, and only then uses an LLM judge for classification: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02475), [official Microsoft repository](https://github.com/microsoft/AgentRx#failure-taxonomy).

The convergence is real: invalid arguments/schema, misinterpreted outputs, and infrastructure failures recur across independently constructed taxonomies. The owner’s measured schema, quoting, connection, path-length, and precondition failures therefore map cleanly to established categories.

### 1.3 End-to-end success and trajectory correctness are different measurements

Tool traces alone cannot establish task success. AgentDebug localizes root-cause trajectory errors; AgentRx checks stepwise constraints; TRAJECT-Bench separately measures tool selection, argument correctness, and dependency/order satisfaction rather than only final accuracy: [TRAJECT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04550). A 2025 study of 3,977 coding-agent solving trajectories and 3,931 testing logs likewise argues that final static outputs hide dynamic failure processes and reports benchmark-platform bugs confirmed by maintainers: [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12374).

The reverse is also true: a passing oracle may be wrong. UTBoost found insufficient tests and 345 erroneous patches that the original SWE-bench evaluation labeled as passed, changing 40.9% of SWE-bench Lite and 24.4% of SWE-bench Verified leaderboard rankings in its study: [UTBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289). NIST separately documented agents exploiting evaluation loopholes through solution contamination, disabling assertions, test-specific logic, and unintended denial-of-service strategies: [NIST, “Cheating on AI Agent Evaluations”](https://www.nist.gov/caisi/cheating-ai-agent-evaluations).

Therefore “the tool returned success,” “tests passed,” “the agent stopped,” and “the owner’s goal was met” are four distinct facts. The current supplied measurements establish the first for many calls and some exception rates; they do not establish the latter three at corpus scale.

### 1.4 Security taxonomies cover failure classes ordinary error telemetry will not reveal

OWASP’s Top 10 for Agentic Applications identifies agent-goal hijack, tool misuse, identity/privilege abuse, agentic supply-chain vulnerabilities, unexpected code execution, memory/context poisoning, insecure inter-agent communication, cascading failures, human–agent trust exploitation, and rogue agents: [official OWASP release](https://genai.owasp.org/2025/12/09/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-the-benchmark-for-agentic-security-in-the-age-of-autonomous-ai/). NIST’s tool-use taxonomy adds dimensions that ordinary call/error counts omit: read versus write permission, trusted versus untrusted environment, reversibility, statefulness, risk, observability, and autonomy: [NIST tool-use workshop findings](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2025/08/lessons-learned-consortium-tool-use-agent-systems).

These are not merely checklist inventions. AgentDojo includes 97 realistic tasks and 629 security cases for prompt-injection attacks and defenses: [AgentDojo paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352), [official benchmark](https://agentdojo.spylab.ai/). Agent Security Bench, published at ICLR 2025, evaluates direct prompt injection, indirect prompt injection during tool use, and memory poisoning among attacks across ten scenarios and more than 400 tools: [ICLR paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=V4y0CpX4hK), [official repository](https://github.com/agiresearch/asb). Agent-SafetyBench contains 349 environments and 2,000 test cases across eight risk categories and ten failure modes; its reported 16 evaluated agents all scored below 60%: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14470), [official repository](https://github.com/thu-coai/Agent-SafetyBench).

Hard-error telemetry will often label a successful malicious action as success. Security evaluation therefore requires adversarial goals, policy oracles, and state inspection, not just exception detection.

### 1.6 Categories “never observed”: good or blind?

**Almost none can honestly be called good.** More precisely:

- **Almost certain (90–99%) that tool/system and format/parameter failures are genuinely present**, because independent taxonomies match explicit terminal fingerprints, and the supplied metrics contain concrete counts.
- **Very likely (80–90%) that retry-loop behavior contains genuine MAST step repetition**, because repetition is directly represented in fingerprints; it remains UNVERIFIED whether those loops reached resource exhaustion—the settling experiment is to label termination reason and token/turn budget for a stratified retry-loop sample.
- **Likely (55–80%) that unread-file precondition failures include state-awareness or rule-adherence failures**, because the operation violates an explicit prerequisite; this is UNVERIFIED as an agent-cognitive cause—the settling experiment is a trace review distinguishing missing affordance, forgotten observation, ignored instruction, and race/stale state.
- **Very likely (80–90%) that planning, reflection, memory, semantic correctness, verification, and multi-agent coordination are blind spots rather than areas of demonstrated strength**, because neither independent supplied signal family contains ground-truth labels for them, while multiple external taxonomies require semantic trajectory or outcome adjudication. The settling experiment is a blinded human annotation of a stratified sample of successful, failed, and owner-corrected sessions using the AgentDebug/MAST categories.
- **Almost certain (90–99%) that security, entity binding, and evaluator validity are blind spots**, because their oracles require adversarial intent, identity/provenance, authorization, external state, or counterfactual grading—none of which is represented by tool exception counts or unadjudicated correction candidates. The settling experiments are respectively: an AgentDojo/ASB-derived red-team suite; ambiguous-entity action tests with post-state checks; and mutation/hidden-test plus transcript-cheating review.
- **No category qualifies as “we are good at it” from the pinned measurements alone.** Demonstrated low error rate for a detector-valid category could support that claim, but the question asks about categories never observed; without a detector and exposure denominator, zero is not evidence.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 A small, faceted failure record is more promising than adopting one taxonomy wholesale

**Very likely (80–90%)**, based on the convergence of AgentDebug, AgentRx, Aegis, MAST, and Shah et al., that a useful operational record needs separate fields for *locus/module*, *failure mode*, *symptom*, *root-cause hypothesis*, *outcome impact*, and *evidence confidence*. This remains UNVERIFIED for this harness; the settling experiment is to dual-label 200 historical incidents and measure inter-annotator agreement, unclassifiable rate, and whether labels predict a distinct remediation.

The case for facets is stronger than the case for a universal tree. The same raw event can have a system symptom, integration cause, planning consequence, and verification failure. Shah et al.’s explicit separation of fault, symptom, and cause supports this design: [study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06847). AgentRx’s `inconclusive` category is also important: [taxonomy](https://github.com/microsoft/AgentRx#failure-taxonomy).

### 2.2 Environment observability enhancements are promising; speculative actions are not yet a default

Aegis reports that explicit state changes, environment lookahead, computation offloading, and speculative actions improved task success by 6.7–12.5% on its studied benchmarks: [Aegis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19504). **Likely (55–80%)** that explicit post-state and reachability hints would reduce this owner’s precondition and exploration friction, because they directly target observed interface failures. This is UNVERIFIED here; settle it with a randomized replay comparing current versus augmented tool responses on unread-file, pagination, directory-change, and output-ranking tasks.

By contrast, **unlikely (20–45%) that speculative execution should be enabled generally**. The Aegis benefit is benchmark-specific, while NIST’s risk dimensions emphasize statefulness, reversibility, permissions, and trusted versus untrusted environments, and OWASP identifies tool misuse and cascading failures. The settling experiment is a sandbox-only A/B test restricted to read-only, idempotent calls, measuring success, cost, unwanted side effects, and security-policy violations.

### 2.3 Invariant-first failure attribution is promising, but a universal LLM judge is not established

AgentRx’s pipeline normalizes trajectories, derives static/dynamic invariants, checks them, then asks an LLM to classify the evidence: [official implementation](https://github.com/microsoft/AgentRx). AgentDebug reports improved step and all-correct accuracy and recovery on three benchmark families: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370). **Likely (55–80%)** that deterministic invariant checks followed by sampled semantic adjudication would find failures missed by exception telemetry. This is UNVERIFIED on this owner’s heterogeneous sessions; settle it with a preregistered 200-trace benchmark against blinded human labels, reporting precision/recall per category and cost.

**Unlikely (20–45%) that an always-on LLM judge alone would be reliable enough to define ground truth**, because the cited systems evaluate bounded datasets, and Anthropic explicitly recommends combining grader types while warning that grader bugs, harness constraints, and task ambiguity can produce misleading scores: [Anthropic eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). The settling experiment is judge-vs-human agreement plus adversarial perturbations where only irrelevant prose, ordering, or model identity changes.

### 2.4 Wrong-entity action is a plausible blind spot

A 2026 preprint separates tool correctness from entity correctness and reports wrong-entity actions even when wrong-tool error was zero: [Entity Binding Failures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30531). **Likely (55–80%)** that this is relevant wherever the harness acts on branches, files, repositories, accounts, messages, or people, but the premise is UNVERIFIED for this owner; settle it with ambiguous-name and near-duplicate target tasks, logging candidate set, selected stable identifier, provenance, clarification, and post-state.

Because this is a recent controlled diagnostic preprint, its exact rates should not be generalized. The important contribution is the separable oracle: “right tool, valid schema” does not imply “right entity.”

### 2.5 Outcome-oracle diversity is promising

Anthropic recommends combining deterministic tests, static analysis, browser/behavior checks, and LLM judges, and converting user-reported failures into eval cases: [eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). UTBoost demonstrates that nominal tests can under-specify correctness: [UTBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289). **Very likely (80–90%)** that pairing deterministic task-specific checks with sampled human review will reveal false successes hidden by the current corpus. This is UNVERIFIED in magnitude; settle it by sampling sessions currently marked successful and comparing command exit status, acceptance criteria, hidden/counterfactual checks, diff review, and owner judgment.

## 3. What is hype, and how to tell

### 3.1 “Comprehensive” taxonomies are scoped samples, not laws of nature

AgentDebug uses three benchmark environments; Aegis uses 142 failed traces across five workloads; MAST begins with roughly 150 expert-studied tasks and extends through an automated annotator; AgentRx contains 115 failed trajectories; even the 40-repository fault study samples 385 faults. Their categories are useful operational vocabularies, but claims of universal completeness exceed their samples. Each paper itself exposes a bounded dataset: [AgentDebug](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370), [Aegis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19504), [MAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657), [AgentRx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02475), [Shah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06847).

**Very likely (80–90%)** that a taxonomy imported unchanged would misclassify owner-specific Windows, plugin-lifecycle, hook, and cross-tool failures. The settling experiment is open coding of owner traces followed by coverage and inter-annotator tests against each candidate taxonomy.

### 3.2 “Self-healing” or “failure-aware multi-agent” gains do not justify another runtime

Benchmark papers can show recovery improvements while adding judges, critics, or specialist agents. That does not establish net reliability, latency, security, or maintainability in a heterogeneous production harness. Anthropic’s production guidance is explicit that successful implementations tend toward simple, composable patterns and that complexity should be added only when it measurably improves outcomes: [Building Effective Agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents). Its 2026 long-running-harness report additionally warns that every harness component encodes assumptions that can go stale as models improve: [Harness design for long-running application development](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps).

**Very unlikely (5–20%) that a second orchestration runtime, trained critic, durable-execution engine, vector/KG memory, or observability SaaS is justified by this failure-taxonomy evidence.** This is a forward-looking judgment based on the anti-overfit gate and the absence of owner-specific ablations; the settling experiment would require a preregistered, costed A/B test against the existing bus showing material gains after accounting for new failure surface. Until then those components are rejected.

### 3.3 Large percentage improvements without released incident-level evidence are marketing-grade, not owner-grade

Fresh workshop/preprint claims that combine anonymized production incidents with dramatic MTTR, cascade, or “silent degradation” improvements are not automatically false, but are not actionable when the incident set, baselines, and fault injections cannot be independently inspected. For example, the 2026 Compound AI Reliability submission reports large improvements from resilience patterns but is an OpenReview workshop submission with anonymized enterprise incidents: [submission](https://openreview.net/forum?id=06K05My3td). **Roughly even (45–55%)** that the direction is useful, but UNVERIFIED for this harness; settle it by reproducing each proposed pattern against the owner’s fixed historical failure corpus and publishing exact fixtures, denominators, and confidence intervals.

### 3.4 “No failures observed” is often a detector claim in disguise

NIST found successful evaluation cheating by transcript review, and UTBoost found passed patches that were incorrect under stronger oracles. Those are direct demonstrations that a success label can be blind: [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/caisi/cheating-ai-agent-evaluations), [UTBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289). Any dashboard that reports category zero without exposure count, detector validation, and a known-present control is hype. It reports detector output, not system quality.

## 4. Specific, concrete things this owner’s harness should adopt

Every proposal below rides the existing hook/skill/MCP/event-ledger bus. None adds a second orchestrator, vector/KG memory, durable-execution runtime, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

### 4.1 Add a faceted failure envelope to the existing event ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

On terminal tool/session events, append fields rather than replacing the current fingerprint:

```json
{
  "outcome": "success|failed|unknown",
  "locus": "memory|reflection|planning|action|system|coordination|evaluation|security",
  "mode": "invalid_invocation|system_failure|step_repetition|...",
  "symptom": "raw normalized fingerprint",
  "root_cause": "known|hypothesis|unknown",
  "impact": "none|recovered|task_degraded|task_failed|unsafe",
  "first_consequential_step": "trace-step-id|null",
  "detector": "exit-code|schema|state-diff|human|llm-sampled",
  "confidence": "numeric-or-band",
  "evidence_refs": ["trace-step-id", "artifact-or-test-id"]
}
```

Retain `unknown`/`inconclusive`; do not force a label. Separate symptom from root cause. AgentRx supports an inconclusive class and invariant evidence; Shah et al. supports the fault/symptom/cause separation: [AgentRx](https://github.com/microsoft/AgentRx#failure-taxonomy), [Shah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06847).

**Confidence: very likely (80–90%)** to improve diagnosis without material orchestration complexity, because it is append-only metadata on an existing bus. UNVERIFIED benefit magnitude; settle with 200 historical events and measure label coverage, agreement, and whether remediation differs by facet.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is a schema and small classifier/normalizer, not a new runtime.

### 4.2 Install a deterministic same-fingerprint circuit breaker with terminal output

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

At PostToolUse failure, normalize the fingerprint. On the second identical failure within a task, inject the previous attempt and require a materially different action. On the third, emit `FAILED:<id> repeated <fingerprint>` and stop that route, preserving evidence and suggesting a known alternative or user escalation. Distinguish transient classes (bounded backoff allowed) from deterministic classes (schema, quote, path length, missing prerequisite: no blind retry).

OpenAI’s agent guidance explicitly recommends retry/action thresholds and human handoff after exceeding them: [OpenAI practical guide](https://openai.com/business/guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-ai-agents/). MAST identifies step repetition, while AgentDebug identifies progress misjudgment and step limits: [MAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657), [AgentDebug taxonomy](https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/AgentDebug#failure-taxonomy).

**Confidence: almost certain (90–99%)** to reduce repeated identical deterministic failures because the intervention directly bounds them; UNVERIFIED effect on total task success—the settling experiment is replay of the 100 most frequent retry-loop fingerprints with and without the breaker.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. A hook-local counter and terminal marker use the existing bus.

### 4.3 Normalize tool contracts and make state transitions explicit

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

For the highest-damage tools first:

- Validate required fields, types, enum/range, and quoting *before* invocation; return one concise corrective example.
- Pass shell arguments as arrays or encoded script files rather than repeatedly constructing nested command strings.
- For state-changing operations, return stable post-state such as resolved path, current branch, selected entity ID, file-read token, or pagination count.
- For large discovery commands, return bounded results plus continuation/reachability metadata.
- Canonicalize terminal outcomes as `SUCCESS:<id>` or `FAILED:<id> reason`.

Xiong et al. supports standard return formats, error feedback, and parameter consistency; Aegis supports explicit state-change and observability hints: [Xiong et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.907/), [Aegis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19504).

**Confidence: very likely (80–90%)** to reduce the observed schema, quoting, unread-file, and exploration friction because each control targets a measured failure family. UNVERIFIED aggregate reduction; settle via per-fingerprint A/B replay and report both tool error rate and end-to-end task success.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. These are thin adapters on existing tools, not new tools or orchestration.

### 4.4 Add live-capability routing and quarantine for dead endpoints

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Maintain one capability-to-provider table with health state. A provider returning deterministic “not connected” is removed from routing until a successful explicit preflight or configuration change; it is not retried by ordinary tasks. Preserve a reason and last probe time. Do not infer that all providers are down from one failed detector.

AgentRx’s system-failure category covers unreachable infrastructure, while standard agent tracing records tool calls and events for diagnosis: [AgentRx](https://github.com/microsoft/AgentRx#failure-taxonomy), [OpenAI Agents SDK tracing](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tracing/).

**Confidence: almost certain (90–99%)** to eliminate calls to an already-known dead MCP while quarantined, based on deterministic routing. UNVERIFIED risk of stale quarantine; settle with a synthetic known-live recovery probe and measure false quarantine/false live rates.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is configuration on the existing MCP bus.

### 4.5 Add an outcome-and-verification gate at task stop

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The Stop hook should refuse to equate “no exception” with “done.” It should record:

1. acceptance criteria restated from the task;
2. concrete artifacts/state expected;
3. deterministic verification performed and result;
4. unresolved gaps;
5. `success`, `failed`, or `unknown`;
6. whether the evidence came from exit code, test, state diff, source check, or human confirmation.

It should not add a universal critic agent. Use existing tests/static checks/state diffs first; sample semantic review only when deterministic oracles are unavailable. Anthropic recommends mixed grader types and turning reported failures into evals; UTBoost shows narrow tests can produce false passes: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [UTBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289).

**Confidence: very likely (80–90%)** to expose premature termination and false success because those categories are currently not adjudicated. UNVERIFIED false-block rate; settle on 200 completed sessions with blinded owner/human outcome labels.

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It is a stop-time checklist and evidence record on the existing hook bus.

### 4.6 Convert owner corrections into adjudicated regression cases

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Do not treat all 2,268 candidates as failures. Sample and adjudicate them into: misunderstanding, planning/constraint, action/tool, memory/context, verification/termination, factuality, UX preference, or false candidate. For true failures, save minimal prompt/context, expected invariant/outcome, and relevant state fixture. Run the smallest deterministic check possible in regression; preserve semantic examples for periodic sampled review.

Anthropic explicitly recommends converting user-reported failures into test cases and prioritizing by impact: [eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents).

## 5. What could not be determined

1. **True prevalence of semantic categories in this owner’s corpus.** The supplied owner-correction entries are candidates, not adjudicated outcomes. No external taxonomy can turn them into labels without reading and annotating traces.
2. **Whether any “never observed” category is genuinely well-controlled.** No detector-valid, exposure-normalized zero was supplied for planning, memory, reflection, verification, security, entity binding, or evaluation validity.
3. **Root causes for most raw tool errors.** Except where the task pins a diagnosed cause (for example orphaned plugin paths causing `ENAMETOOLONG`), an exception fingerprint is a symptom, not a causal attribution.
4. **Recovery quality.** The corpus summary includes retry loops and errors but not whether recovery ultimately achieved the owner’s acceptance criteria, created collateral changes, or merely produced a plausible narrative.
5. **Cross-provider/model taxonomy stability.** The cited studies use bounded model and environment sets. No study establishes that its category frequencies transfer to this owner’s full mix of Claude, Codex, hooks, skills, MCPs, Windows shells, and subagents.
6. **Security exposure denominator.** Tool counts do not reveal how often untrusted content reached a write-capable tool, whether credentials/privileges were in scope, or whether attacks were attempted.
7. **Wrong-entity incidence.** No stable-identifier or post-state adjudication was supplied. The recent entity-binding work is a useful hypothesis, not owner evidence.
8. **Evaluation-oracle contamination or gaming rate.** NIST and UTBoost prove that such failures occur in agent evaluations generally, but no owner-specific transcript-cheating or hidden-test audit was provided.
9. **Taxonomy detector accuracy on owner traces.** AgentDebug, AgentRx, and MAST report results on their datasets; importing their LLM annotators without a local labeled benchmark would be ungrounded.
10. **Whether the latest 2026 preprints survive peer review or replication.** Findings from AgentDebug, Aegis, AgentRx, Shah et al., Entity Binding Failures, and UTBoost were treated as promising empirical evidence, not settled universal law. The peer-reviewed/official sources were weighted more heavily where possible.

## Recommended order of adoption

1. Failure envelope plus coverage matrix and positive controls.
2. Same-fingerprint circuit breaker and dead-endpoint quarantine.
3. Tool-contract normalization and explicit post-state.
4. Stop-time outcome/verification gate.
5. Adjudicate correction candidates into regression fixtures.
6. Safety regression pack and tool risk tags.
7. Structured multi-agent handoffs.
8. Only then test sampled semantic attribution, entity binding, or read-only speculation.

This ordering is **very likely (80–90%)** to maximize information gained per line of harness code because it first repairs measurement validity and observed deterministic failures, then expands into semantic/security blind spots. It is UNVERIFIED as an end-to-end sequence; settle it with staged ablations where each step must reduce a preregistered failure metric without worsening task success, cost, or false-block rate.
