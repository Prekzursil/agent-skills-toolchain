# X091 — mechanism design → tool design

## Executive result

The cross-domain borrow is useful, but only after cutting it down to its non-strategic core. The specific mechanism is the **incentive-compatible direct-revelation mechanism**, operationalized here as a **direct-intent execution tool**: solicit the program, argument atoms, working directory, environment delta, and standard input directly, then let the harness perform platform-specific serialization. In mechanism design, a direct mechanism asks agents to report their private types and chooses an outcome from those reports; incentive compatibility makes truthful reporting an equilibrium. The revelation principle says that an equilibrium outcome of an arbitrary mechanism can be replicated by an incentive-compatible direct mechanism. This is the formulation in the Royal Swedish Academy’s mechanism-design survey, including its explicit scope over private information, unobserved actions, and multistage mechanisms ([Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, *Mechanism Design Theory*, pp. 4–6](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2007.pdf)).

The theorem itself **does not transfer**. An LLM constructing a command is not an expected-utility-maximizing bidder with a private type; no transfer/payment makes an argument array a dominant strategy; and the harness cannot infer arbitrary shell semantics from a stated goal. What transfers is the interface-design move: replace an indirect action language with a direct message space wherever the desired outcome can be represented directly. This conclusion is **almost certain (90-99%)**, because (a) the economic theorem’s assumptions and conclusion are explicit in the Academy survey, and (b) operating-system/tool documentation independently shows the engineering distinction between shell text and explicit argument lists ([Nobel survey](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2007.pdf); [Node `spawn(command, args)` documentation](https://nodejs.org/download/release/latest-v6.x/docs/api/child_process.html); [.NET `ProcessStartInfo.ArgumentList`](https://learn.microsoft.com/de-de/dotnet/api/system.diagnostics.processstartinfo?view=netframework-4.8.1)).

## 1. The mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 Direct revelation and incentive compatibility

Mechanism design begins with a designer who wants an outcome but does not possess all relevant information. Each participant has a private “type,” an arbitrary mechanism gives participants some message/action space, and an outcome rule maps messages/actions to allocations and possibly payments. A **direct** mechanism makes the message space the type space: each participant reports a type. It is **incentive compatible** when reporting the true type is an equilibrium under the selected equilibrium concept. The revelation principle then allows the designer, under its assumptions, to search direct incentive-compatible mechanisms without losing implementable equilibrium outcomes ([Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, pp. 4–6](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2007.pdf)).

The key detail is not “make the form nicer.” The direct mechanism internalizes the translation from type to outcome. In the proof sketch, a mediator receiving reported type `t` plays the equilibrium message `m(t)` that the participant would have played in the original indirect mechanism. A profitable lie in the direct mechanism would correspond to a profitable deviation in the original mechanism, contradicting equilibrium ([Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, p. 5](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2007.pdf)). Thus truthfulness comes from the relationship between the outcome rule and participants’ payoffs, not from an instruction saying “be truthful.”

This matters for the translation: an agent-facing schema alone is not incentive compatibility. The harness must itself turn the direct message into the intended process invocation; otherwise it has merely relocated quoting into a JSON string.

### 1.2 Proper scoring rules are a narrower incentive-compatible mechanism

A strictly proper scoring rule elicits a probability distribution by paying a score after the outcome is observed. If the forecaster’s belief is `Q`, expected score is uniquely maximized by reporting `P = Q`. Gneiting and Raftery define propriety and strict propriety in exactly these expected-score terms and stress that propriety depends on the forecaster maximizing expected reward ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007, pp. 359–360](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)). This is a one-agent incentive-compatible elicitation mechanism, not a general cure for malformed actions.

The assumptions are fragile. Competitive preferences can make a forecaster optimally exaggerate even when the underlying rule is proper ([Lichtendahl & Winkler 2007, abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.1070.0729)), and risk aversion can shade reports away from true probabilities under a nominally proper rule ([Armantier & Treich 2013, abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176512002984)). Those examples are useful warnings: “proper” describes a mechanism under a utility model, not an unconditional property of a feedback number.

### 1.3 Goodhart is a constraint, not the selected mechanism

Goodhart’s original observation arose from monetary control: a statistical regularity can break when it is used for control. Later work separates several causal, extremal, regressional, and adversarial ways that a proxy ceases to track a goal ([Manheim & Garrabrant 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Here, “minimize shell parse errors” is only a proxy for “complete the owner’s task correctly and safely.” An interface that suppresses shell errors by refusing useful operations, hiding errors, or pushing failures into semantic mis-execution would improve the proxy while harming the goal. Therefore the evaluation below pairs error counts with end-to-end outcome and argument-fidelity signals.

## 2. Structural mapping

| Mechanism-design object | Tool-design counterpart | Transfer status |
|---|---|---|
| Private type: information held by participant | Agent’s intended process invocation: executable, ordered arguments, input, environment, and desired shell features | Partial analogy. The invocation is representable information, but not a preference type. |
| Indirect mechanism/action space | Free-form Bash or PowerShell source text | Strong engineering correspondence: the agent must encode intent into a grammar whose parser has behavior independent of the target process ([POSIX shell command language](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/utilities/V3_chap02.html); [PowerShell parsing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/microsoft.powershell.core/about/about_parsing?view=powershell-7.5)). |
| Direct report | `{program, argv[], cwd, env_delta, stdin}` | Strong correspondence for a single process invocation; weak for pipelines, redirections, loops, and expansions whose semantics are the desired computation. |
| Outcome function | Harness validates fields, resolves the executable, serializes arguments for the platform, spawns, and returns a typed result | Strong correspondence if serialization is owned by the harness. Node exposes command plus an argument array with shell use off by default; .NET exposes an `ArgumentList` whose strings need not be pre-escaped ([Node](https://nodejs.org/download/release/latest-v6.x/docs/api/child_process.html); [.NET](https://learn.microsoft.com/de-de/dotnet/api/system.diagnostics.processstartinfo?view=netframework-4.8.1)). |
| Transfers/payments | Tool friction, retry permission, or prompt-visible score | Poor correspondence. These are not stable utilities and do not establish a dominant strategy. |
| Incentive-compatible truth telling | Agent emits the intended argument boundary directly | **Does not follow.** It is an affordance/error-surface claim, not an equilibrium claim. |

Windows makes the engineering issue unusually sharp. `CreateProcess` ultimately accepts a single command-line string with a documented 32,767-character limit, and applications may parse that string into `argv` ([Microsoft `CreateProcess`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-createprocessa)). That does **not** invalidate an argument-array tool: it means the adapter, rather than the model, must own the unavoidable Windows serialization convention. PowerShell adds another parser before native invocation and documents expression mode, argument mode, metacharacters, quote handling, and version-dependent native argument passing ([PowerShell `about_Parsing`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/microsoft.powershell.core/about/about_parsing?view=powershell-7.5)). POSIX shell similarly performs tokenization, quoting, expansions, and quote removal before execution ([POSIX.1-2024, Shell Command Language](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/utilities/V3_chap02.html)).

The transferable design principle is therefore: **when the outcome is “spawn this program with these exact arguments,” ask for those atoms; when the outcome genuinely is a shell program, ask for shell source and name the dialect.** This is direct revelation as a design heuristic, not revelation-principle equivalence.

## 3. Proposed harness mechanism — UNVERIFIED (`QUOTE-1631-DIRECT-EXEC-A/B`)

### 3.1 Two lanes, with the direct lane first

**Proposal — direct process tool. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add or expose this contract:

```json
{
  "program": "git",
  "argv": ["show", "origin/main:path with spaces/file.txt"],
  "cwd": "C:\\Users\\Prekzursil\\source\\repo",
  "env_delta": {"NO_COLOR": "1"},
  "stdin": null,
  "timeout_ms": 30000
}
```

Normative behavior:

1. `program` and every `argv` element are atomic strings. The agent never supplies a joined command line in this lane.
2. `cwd` is a distinct field. Environment changes are a key/value delta, not `NAME=value command` text. Standard input is a distinct byte/text field, not an `echo ... |` construction.
3. The adapter sets shell execution off and uses the runtime’s argument-list interface. Node documents `shell: false` as the default for `spawn()` and separately warns against unsanitized input when shell execution is enabled ([Node child processes](https://nodejs.org/download/release/latest-v6.x/docs/api/child_process.html)). On .NET, use `ProcessStartInfo.ArgumentList`, whose elements do not need pre-escaping, rather than the `Arguments` string property ([.NET `ProcessStartInfo`](https://learn.microsoft.com/de-de/dotnet/api/system.diagnostics.processstartinfo?view=netframework-4.8.1)).
4. The returned record includes the resolved executable, an indexed display of argument atoms, exit code, stdout/stderr, duration, truncation metadata, and a stable error code. The display is diagnostic only and must never be copied back into an executable string.
5. The tool schema forbids both `argv` and `command_text` in the same call. This makes the safe path structurally distinct rather than a boolean that silently changes the meaning of one field.

This direct lane eliminates unmatched shell quotes **by construction for calls represented in this lane**: quote characters inside an argument are data rather than delimiters. It also preserves spaces and empty arguments as boundaries rather than requiring the model to remember dialect-specific escaping. Python’s standard-library documentation independently recommends an argument sequence and notes that breaking a complex shell command into arguments may itself be non-obvious ([Python `subprocess`](https://docs.python.org/3/library/subprocess.html)).

**Proposal — explicit shell-program tool. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Retain raw shell execution for computations that actually require a shell, but require:

```json
{
  "dialect": "pwsh-7",
  "script": "Get-ChildItem | Where-Object Length -gt 0",
  "cwd": "C:\\work",
  "required_feature": ["pipeline", "cmdlet"]
}
```

`dialect` must be an enum such as `bash`, `pwsh-7`, or `cmd`, never inferred from syntax. `required_feature` is not a moral justification and should not be scored; it is a capability declaration that makes routing errors inspectable. Valid values should describe semantics such as pipeline, redirection, control flow, expansion, shell builtin, or PowerShell cmdlet. A false or unnecessary declaration has no punitive consequence, because attaching a target to “low shell usage” would invite metric gaming.

Making direct execution the default is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce quoting failures materially, based on the removal of shell parsing for eligible calls and official process APIs that accept argument lists; **UNVERIFIED — `QUOTE-1631-DIRECT-EXEC-A/B` must measure the eligible fraction and net failure displacement**. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to eliminate most failures if historical calls are dominated by true shell programs, because those calls remain in the shell lane; the same experiment settles that uncertainty.

### 3.2 Error messages are the mechanism’s outcome channel

**Proposal — phase-typed error envelope. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Every failure should report:

```json
{
  "phase": "parse",
  "code": "UNCLOSED_SINGLE_QUOTE",
  "dialect": "bash",
  "span": {"start": 41, "end": 42},
  "message": "Bash reached end of script inside a single-quoted word.",
  "repair": {
    "kind": "use_direct_exec",
    "reason": "This script appears to contain one simple external command."
  },
  "same_call_will_change_outcome": false
}
```

The phase enum should distinguish `schema`, `resolve`, `parse`, `serialize`, `spawn`, `run`, and `policy`. The code should identify a condition, not merely repeat stderr. For parse errors, return the parser’s span and dialect. A suggested direct call may be shown only when conversion is unambiguous; otherwise return a minimal dialect-specific explanation and preserve the original parser diagnostic.

This would catch an unclosed quote before a side-effecting process starts when the chosen shell offers a parse-only API, and would turn a generic end-of-file message into a repairable condition. That preflight coverage is **very likely (80-90%)** for syntactically malformed PowerShell because PowerShell exposes `Parser.ParseInput`, which returns an AST plus tokens and parse errors ([Microsoft `Parser.ParseInput`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.management.automation.language.parser.parseinput?view=powershellsdk-7.4.0)), and **likely (55-80%)** for the Bash subset accepted by the selected parser; **UNVERIFIED — experiment `DIALECT-PARSER-CONTROL-MATRIX` must run known-valid and known-invalid controls across the exact installed Bash and PowerShell versions before enabling blocking**. These probabilities are intentionally not claims that a detector already exists or works.

Do not auto-rewrite shell text after a parse failure. Automatic repair is **likely (55-80%)** to alter semantics in some ambiguous cases, because a missing delimiter has multiple possible insertion points; **UNVERIFIED — experiment `QUOTE-REPAIR-SEMANTIC-EQUIVALENCE` would need an oracle corpus of intended scripts to quantify this**. The tool should instead expose atoms when it can prove the call is a simple process invocation, or return a localized diagnostic.

### 3.3 One bounded retry friction, not a second orchestrator

**Proposal — repeated-failure receipt. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Hash the normalized tool name, structured fields, and stable error code. After two identical failing calls, a third identical call returns `UNCHANGED_RETRY` without execution and names the fields that must change. This is not claimed to make the agent incentive compatible; it mechanically removes one useless action from the message space.

It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce literal retry loops without harming successful recovery, because it blocks only an identical normalized call after the same stable failure; **UNVERIFIED — experiment `IDENTICAL-RETRY-RECEIPT-REPLAY` must compare loop termination and eventual task success on historical retry sequences**. Nondeterministic errors, rate limits, connection failures, and explicitly retryable status codes must be exempt.

## 4. What the design catches, and what it does not

### Caught by construction in direct execution

- An unmatched quote cannot be a syntax error because there is no shell syntax. A literal quote is part of one `argv` element.
- Spaces, empty strings, wildcard characters, dollar signs, semicolons, parentheses, and ampersands remain data unless the target program interprets them. PowerShell documents these as argument-mode metacharacters in shell text, illustrating the parsing surface removed ([PowerShell `about_Parsing`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/microsoft.powershell.core/about/about_parsing?view=powershell-7.5)).
- `cwd`, environment, and stdin cannot accidentally fuse into the command language because they are separate typed fields.
- The harness can report which exact argument index failed schema validation before attempting a process start.

### Newly diagnosable in the explicit shell lane

- Dialect mismatch becomes a first-class field rather than an inference from an error string.
- A parse failure can carry a source span, parser code, and named dialect.
- A raw shell call that declares no actual shell feature becomes measurable as a migration candidate. This is not proof it is safely convertible.
- A repeated identical malformed call can be stopped with a stable receipt rather than producing another opaque EOF.

### Not caught

- Syntactically valid but semantically wrong arguments.
- A correctly delimited destructive command.
- Target-program-specific mini-languages inside an argument, such as regular expressions, SQL, `jq`, or `awk` source.
- Pipelines whose individual stages are valid but compose incorrectly.
- Windows programs with idiosyncratic command-line parsing. Windows supplies one process command-line string, and the receiving application may implement its own interpretation ([Microsoft `CreateProcess`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-createprocessa)). The adapter can centralize known serialization rules but cannot guarantee every target’s parser.
- Task failure hidden by exit code zero.

These exclusions are why “quote-error rate” cannot be the sole objective.

## 5. Proper scoring rule: useful instrumentation, rejected as the production mechanism

One could ask the model for `p_success` with each call and score it using a Brier or logarithmic score after observing success. Under the proper-scoring assumptions, truthful belief reporting maximizes expected score ([Gneiting & Raftery](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)). In principle, the resulting calibration curve is the signal of whether reported uncertainty tracks quoting outcomes; the empirical usefulness of that signal is assigned a confidence band and settling experiment below.

I reject this as a production routing gate. The model is not trained online against the harness’s score, prompt-visible “points” are not demonstrated utility, “success” is only partially observable, and routing extra checks based on the reported probability changes the event-generating process. A low confidence report could trigger assistance and thereby succeed, confounding calibration; a high report could avoid checks. The assumptions required for strict propriety therefore do not hold as stated.

**Proposal — offline confidence probe only, UNVERIFIED (`TOOL-CALL-BRIER-OFFLINE`). NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** On a non-actionable evaluation copy of calls, ask for probability of (a) schema acceptance, (b) parse success, and (c) nonzero exit separately, then score each against its mechanically observed outcome. Never expose the score as a task reward or use it to decide whether the call executes. This probe is **roughly even (45-55%)** to reveal useful self-knowledge, because proper scoring gives a principled evaluation metric but the model has no assured incentive or stable calibration under the elicitation prompt; the named offline experiment settles it. Even a positive result would support a risk estimator, not replace structured execution.

### 6.2 Randomized comparison

- Control: current free-form shell interface and current errors.
- Treatment A: direct process tool plus explicit shell lane.
- Treatment B: Treatment A plus phase-typed errors.
- Optional Treatment C: Treatment B plus identical-retry receipt.

Randomize condition order and use multiple samples per episode. Do not translate historical malformed strings automatically for the treatment; let the model choose the new interface from the original task context, or the experiment would measure a handcrafted translator rather than tool affordance.

### 6.3 Primary and guardrail outcomes

Primary outcomes:

- end-to-end fixture task success;
- exact received-`argv` fidelity against the fixture oracle;
- unmatched-quote parser failures per eligible attempt;
- successful recovery within the next two tool calls.

Guardrail outcomes:

- total tool calls, latency, and tokens to completion;
- wrong-command and wrong-argument executions;
- refusal/abandonment rate;
- fraction routed to shell and fraction of shell calls that genuinely use a declared feature;
- new error-code distribution, especially schema mismatch and serialization failure;
- side-effect attempts, captured by the fixture rather than executed.

An acceptance claim needs two mechanically independent signals: (1) typed tool telemetry showing fewer parser failures and (2) the echo fixture or semantic oracle showing the intended arguments/task outcome. Re-running the same log query is not a second signal. The detector must first pass both known-invalid and known-valid controls. Only then may the unit claim “caught,” “absent,” or “fixed.”

### 6.4 Decision rule

Adopt the direct lane only if it reduces quote failures **and** does not degrade fixture task success or exact argument fidelity beyond a pre-registered non-inferiority margin. The margin must be selected before viewing results. Do not set a target such as “90% fewer shell calls”; shell usage is legitimate for pipelines and shell-native computations. Report migrations, refusals, semantic errors, and displaced schema errors alongside the headline quote count.

If Treatment A wins, retain it even if B or C does not. If A merely converts parse errors into schema errors without improving task success or argument fidelity, reject the mechanism as Goodharted. If only error-envelope Treatment B improves recovery, ship B independently. This factorial separation prevents the cross-domain story from forcing an all-or-nothing architecture decision.

## 7. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### Disanalogy 1: there is no strategic private type

The model is generating an action representation, not concealing a valuation. A malformed quote is generally a competence or representation error, not a profitable misreport. Mechanism-design truthfulness proofs therefore supply no behavioral guarantee. This is the largest disanalogy and rules out claims such as “structured argv is strategy-proof.”

### Disanalogy 2: utility and transfers are missing

Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility compares participant utilities across reports. Tool errors, token costs, and retry friction are not known utilities for an LLM. A model may imitate score-seeking language without altering the underlying error distribution. Proper scoring rules explicitly depend on expected-score maximization ([Gneiting & Raftery](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)). Hence the proposal uses structural elimination and mechanical validation, not motivational points.

### Disanalogy 3: the “type” need not contain a complete invocation

If the model is uncertain which program, argument boundary, or shell feature will accomplish the task, there is no complete invocation to report. Direct revelation cannot reveal information the agent does not possess. The structured interface can expose representational choices, but it cannot create command knowledge.

### Disanalogy 4: directness is domain-relative

For `git status`, program plus arguments is direct. For `Get-ChildItem | Where-Object ...`, the PowerShell program is itself the intended computation; decomposing it into `argv` is not more direct. A universal no-shell policy would force a false analogy and introduce a second orchestration language.

### Disanalogy 5: Windows never truly receives an argument array

The Windows API boundary is a command-line string with a length limit ([Microsoft `CreateProcess`](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-createprocessa)). The benefit is centralized, tested serialization—not metaphysical removal of quoting from the entire stack. Target-specific parsers remain.

### Why the narrowed borrow still holds

The borrow holds at the interface boundary because a large, identifiable subset of intended outcomes is already a tuple `(executable, argv, cwd, env, stdin)`, and mainstream process APIs expose those components separately above the OS boundary ([Node](https://nodejs.org/download/release/latest-v6.x/docs/api/child_process.html); [.NET](https://learn.microsoft.com/de-de/dotnet/api/system.diagnostics.processstartinfo?view=netframework-4.8.1)). For that subset, requiring the model to synthesize shell source introduces an additional grammar and an additional parser without adding desired semantics. Removing that avoidable action space is an engineering consequence, not an equilibrium theorem.

My final transfer judgment is **very likely (80-90%)** that direct-intent execution is the right mechanism-design-inspired candidate to test, based on the direct-mechanism literature, documented shell parsing layers, and documented argument-list APIs. It is only **likely (55-80%)** that it will materially reduce the owner’s measured 1,631 quoting failures, because this unit was deliberately forbidden from local-repo grounding and the eligible historical fraction is **UNVERIFIED — `QUOTE-1631-ELIGIBILITY-LABEL` settles it**.

## 8. Anti-overfit gate and alternatives rejected

The proposal rides the existing tool/hook/MCP bus as one small typed adapter, one schema split, and optional small error normalization. It does not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It keeps raw shell available where shell semantics are the task. Therefore it passes the specified anti-overfit gate.
