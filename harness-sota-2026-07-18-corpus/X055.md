# X055 — Control theory → synthesis

**Unit:** X055  
**Lens:** control theory (observability/controllability, feedback stability, anti-windup, dead-band design)  
**Target:** collapse thousands of findings into a defensible decision without requiring an agent to read the full corpus  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** this is an external, cross-domain design study. Per the unit brief, it does not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. The measured facts in the brief establish the scale and reliability problem; they are not re-derived here.

## Executive result

The literal analogy—“thousands of prose findings are a dynamical system, therefore apply balanced truncation”—**does not transfer**. Classical balanced truncation assumes a stable linear time-invariant (LTI) state-space model, defined inputs and outputs, superposition, and a norm in which approximation error is meaningful. A findings corpus has none of those properties by default. Assigning it matrices and Hankel singular values would manufacture precision rather than earn it.

A narrower control-theoretic mechanism **does transfer as an engineering pattern**: admit a finding to the decision surface only after independently scoring (1) how well the claim is observed from evidence and (2) whether any available harness action can control the outcome it describes; preserve the small set that is high on both axes; route the two asymmetric failure classes instead of discarding them; and feed the *actual admitted output* back to the accumulator so overflow cannot silently build behind a fixed context limit. Add a hysteretic re-open rule so small evidence fluctuations do not repeatedly eject and restore the same issue.

I call the resulting mechanism a **Balanced Decision Surface (BDS)**. It borrows the *dual questions* and the *saturation feedback topology*, not balanced truncation's theorem or vocabulary as decoration.

**Overall judgment:** it is **very likely (80-90%)** that a dual-certified, saturation-aware decision surface will produce a more auditable synthesis than one-pass ranking or free-form summarization. Evidence: control theory treats sensing and actuation as distinct structural properties; anti-windup explicitly feeds actuator saturation back into hidden accumulated state; and the proposed fields are directly testable. The settling experiment is a blinded replay in which BDS and the current synthesis process receive the same historical finding bundles, with human adjudicators measuring decision recall, unsupported-decision rate, and reading volume.

It is only **likely (55-80%)** that BDS will materially reduce total agent reading while preserving every decision-relevant minority finding. Evidence: explicit admission budgets and provenance pointers reduce rendered text, but natural-language dependencies are nonlinear and rare hazards can evade any scoring rubric. The settling experiment is the same replay plus an adversarial rare-but-catastrophic finding suite and a requirement that no seeded catastrophic item be lost.

## 1. What control theory actually solved

### 1.1 Observability and controllability are different questions

For an LTI system

\[
\dot{x}=Ax+Bu, \qquad y=Cx+Du,
\]

controllability asks whether inputs can move the state; observability asks whether the internal state can be reconstructed from outputs. MIT's graduate notes define controllability as the existence of an input that moves any initial state to any final state in finite time ([MIT, *Underactuated Robotics: Controllability*](https://underactuated.mit.edu/acrobot.html#controllability)). Stanford's model-reduction notes define the finite-horizon reachability Gramian

\[
P(T)=\int_0^T e^{A\tau}BB^*e^{A^*\tau}d\tau
\]

and observability Gramian

\[
Q(T)=\int_0^T e^{A^*\tau}C^*Ce^{A\tau}d\tau
\]

([Stanford AA216/CME345, *Projection-Based Model Order Reduction—Balanced Truncation*, pp. 14ff](https://web.stanford.edu/group/frg/course_work/CME345/CA-AA216-CME345-Ch6.pdf)). The crucial point is not merely that there are two scores: a mode can be easy to excite but hard to observe, or easy to observe but impossible to influence with the available input channels.

This separation prevents a common category error. More sensor evidence does not create an actuator, and more actuator authority does not prove the state estimate. In the Kalman decomposition, the state space is partitioned by these structural properties; a minimal input-output realization retains the controllable and observable part. MIT provides dedicated notes on the two concepts and their decomposition ([MIT OCW 12.864, *Controllability and Observability*](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/12-864-inference-from-data-and-models-spring-2005/resources/contrl_obser_4_6/)).

### 1.2 Balanced truncation reduces states according to joint input-output significance

For an asymptotically stable LTI system, balancing changes coordinates so reachability and observability Gramians are equal and diagonal. Their diagonal values are the Hankel singular values. Small values identify state directions that are jointly difficult to reach and difficult to observe; truncating those directions gives a lower-order input-output model. A Berkeley technical report describes the method exactly this way: balance controllability and observability, then truncate states hard to control and observe ([Berkeley EECS-2012-217, §2.3.3](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2012/Archive/EECS-2012-217.pdf)). Stanford's course notes give the classical reachability and observability constructions ([Stanford AA216/CME345](https://web.stanford.edu/group/frg/course_work/CME345/CA-AA216-CME345-Ch6.pdf)).

The appeal is not just compression. In its valid domain, balanced truncation preserves stability and has an input-output error bound based on discarded Hankel singular values. MIT's balanced-truncation lecture states the lower and upper error-bound treatment and notes that the theorem is especially useful when singular values decay rapidly ([MIT 6.241, Lecture 17](https://web.mit.edu/course/6/6.241/ameg_www_fall2006/www/images/L06baltr.pdf)); KTH lecture notes display the familiar bound

\[
\sigma_{r+1}\le \lVert G-G_r\rVert_\infty\le 2\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\sigma_i
\]

and describe eliminating the states with small Hankel singular values ([KTH EL2520 lecture 10](https://www.kth.se/social/upload/518244fef27654191c3866a3/lec10_4up.pdf)).

That theorem is important here mainly as a boundary marker: control theory's compression guarantee comes from a precise plant model and norm. We do **not** possess an analogous guarantee for prose findings.

### 1.3 Anti-windup corrects hidden accumulation when the actuator saturates

Integral feedback accumulates error over time. If an actuator reaches a limit, additional controller command cannot change the plant, yet the internal integral term can continue growing. Åström and Murray explain that saturation breaks the effective feedback loop, allows the integral to build, and causes long transients even after the error changes sign ([Åström & Murray, *Feedback Systems*, §10.4](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/am08-pid_19Jul11.pdf)).

Two standard repairs are:

1. **Clamping / conditional integration:** stop integration while the output is saturated and the new error would drive it farther into saturation.
2. **Back-calculation:** feed the difference between unsaturated command and actual saturated output back into the integrator, actively unwinding internal state.

MathWorks documents both topologies and explicitly says back-calculation uses an internal tracking loop to unwind the integrator; it also states the assumptions that saturation limits are known and that the controller is the sole signal feeding the actuator ([MathWorks, *Anti-Windup Control Using PID Controller Block*](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simulink/slref/anti-windup-control-using-a-pid-controller.html)). Its discrete PID reference states the exact feedback quantity: saturated output minus unsaturated command ([MathWorks, *Discrete PID Controller—Anti-windup method*](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simulink/slref/discretepidcontroller.html)).

### 1.4 Dead bands, hysteresis, and dwell time prevent chatter

A dead band deliberately ignores sufficiently small deviations; hysteresis uses different enter and exit thresholds so a noisy signal near a boundary does not toggle the actuator repeatedly. This is not free: the accepted steady error increases. In networked control, event-triggered schemes similarly trade transmissions against tracking fidelity. A comparative study of sampled-data, deadband, and model-based event-triggered control derives approximation-error and minimum inter-sampling-time bounds ([Miskowicz, *Comparison between sampled-data control, deadband control and model-based event-triggered control*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1474667015371664)). Work on event-triggered control emphasizes a positive lower bound on inter-event time to exclude Zeno behavior—infinitely dense triggers that make an otherwise elegant rule unusable ([Adloo & Shafiei, *Zeno-free adaptive event-triggered control design*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0142331218799147?download=true)).

The synthesis analogue is not “ignore small findings.” It is “do not reopen a settled decision for a tiny score fluctuation; require a materially stronger reversal signal, while allowing a separate hard-hazard trigger to bypass the band.”

## 2. Translation: the Balanced Decision Surface

### Proposal A — Dual-certified finding records

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Require every normalized finding to carry two independent certificates before it competes for scarce synthesis space.

**Observation certificate `O`:**

- concrete evidence pointers, not copied prose;
- detector identity and detector control result (a known-present item it successfully found);
- number and type of mechanically independent signals;
- contradiction count and freshness;
- confidence band and any inline `UNVERIFIED` experiment.

**Control certificate `C`:**

- named actuator on the existing harness bus: setting, rule, hook, skill, MCP registration, cleanup operation, or owner decision;
- exact outcome the actuator can change;
- authority and reversibility;
- expected leading indicator and lagging outcome;
- verification experiment and rollback condition.

These are not multiplied into a single magic score. The record stays in a four-way partition:

| Class | Meaning | Synthesis treatment |
|---|---|---|
| high `O`, high `C` | well-supported and actionable | candidate decision card |
| high `O`, low `C` | real but currently uncontrollable | constraint / escalation / actuator-gap card |
| low `O`, high `C` | tempting action without enough sensing | experiment card; do not decide yet |
| low `O`, low `C` | neither demonstrated nor actionable | archive with provenance; no decision surface slot |

This partition catches two errors that a severity-only ranking misses: (a) a vivid, easy fix whose premise is poorly observed, and (b) a highly evidenced defect that is repeatedly summarized but has no named mechanism capable of changing it.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that explicit asymmetric routing will expose evidence gaps and actuator gaps that a scalar priority score conceals. Evidence: observability and controllability are structurally distinct in state-space theory, and the schema preserves rather than averages that distinction. The settling experiment is to hand reviewers mixed high/low `O`/`C` cases and compare classification accuracy against a one-number priority rubric.

The numerical calibration of `O` and `C` is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to label a historical set for evidence sufficiency and executable actionability, fit only monotone rubric thresholds on a training half, then report calibration and error types on a held-out half**. Consequently, any recommendation to auto-reject records based only on numeric cutoffs is also **UNVERIFIED** and is not made here.

### Proposal B — Decision cards plus residual ledger, not a giant summary

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add a deterministic reducer on the existing artifact/skill bus. It consumes normalized records and emits two outputs:

1. A bounded **decision surface** of cards an agent reads.
2. A complete **residual ledger** the agent normally does not read, but every card links into.

Each decision card contains:

```text
decision_id
decision_statement
O_certificate_summary + evidence pointers
C_certificate_summary + actuator pointer
independent_supporting_families
strongest_contradiction
blast_radius / reversibility
expected_measurement and time horizon
confidence band
UNVERIFIED experiment, if any
supersedes / blocked_by / conflicts_with
residual_ids represented by this card
```

The reducer may deduplicate records only when their proposed decision, causal mechanism, and actuator are the same. Textual similarity alone is insufficient. It must preserve the strongest contradiction, union provenance, and maintain a count plus IDs of represented residuals. A hard-hazard lane bypasses the ordinary card budget when a finding alleges irreversible loss, credential exposure, destructive scope, or a detector-control failure.

The card budget is not “top N findings.” It is allocated across the four dual-certificate classes and across independent failure families. This prevents a large cluster of near-duplicate errors from consuming every slot and also prevents an uncontrollable but important constraint from disappearing because it cannot yet yield a patch.

This would catch:

- **false consensus from duplication:** many fingerprints from one causal family no longer masquerade as independent evidence;
- **silent minority contradictions:** the strongest dissent must be rendered on the card;
- **orphaned recommendations:** every recommended change needs a named actuator and outcome measurement;
- **unmeasured actions:** a decision with no post-action signal is visibly low-`O` for closure even if its initial diagnosis was well supported;
- **context-limit disappearance:** everything omitted remains addressable in the residual ledger, with reason and parent card.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that these rules will expose at least one of those five failure classes in a large historical replay. Evidence: each class has a distinct invariant that the reducer can check (family independence, rendered contradiction, named actuator, declared measurement, or lossless residual membership). The settling experiment is to seed one instance of each class into blinded fixture bundles and report per-class detection and false-positive rates.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a sub-200-line deterministic reducer can perform schema validation, exact-key grouping, budget allocation, and Markdown rendering. Evidence: these are bounded transforms over records and require no semantic daemon; however, causal-equivalence labeling must be supplied upstream or conservatively left unmerged. The settling experiment is a throwaway implementation against a representative fixture, with line count, deterministic golden output, and mutation tests for contradictions and provenance loss.

Semantic causal merging is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a blinded pairwise evaluation of proposed merges, requiring zero merges across different actuators or causal mechanisms before enabling it**. Until then, the safe default is under-merging.

### Proposal C — Saturation-aware synthesis with back-calculation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Treat context and decision-card count as actuator limits. The synthesizer can request an unbounded “ideal” set of cards, but the renderer admits only a bounded actual set. Feed the difference back into the next synthesis pass:

```text
requested = all cards currently above admission policy
admitted  = cards actually rendered under token/card/family budgets
residual  = requested - admitted

for every residual card:
  retain reason_not_admitted
  retain best evidence and strongest contradiction
  increment persistence only if new independent evidence arrived
  never increment merely because another summarizer repeated the claim
```

This is the back-calculation analogue: the accumulator learns the actual output limit rather than continuing to inflate invisible priority behind a saturated prompt. A persistent residual does not gain infinite urgency from age alone. It gains admission pressure only from a new independent signal, increased consequence, a newly available actuator, or invalidation of a currently admitted card.

This would catch a failure mode ordinary summarization obscures: internal “importance” can keep accumulating for omitted findings even though the output channel is fixed, producing later abrupt displacement or endless re-summarization. The residual feedback makes saturation explicit and inspectable.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that explicit saturation feedback will reduce repeated processing of unchanged omitted findings. Evidence: anti-windup works by reconciling internal command with actual actuator output, and the proposed persistence rule does the same bookkeeping operation; the analogy lacks a stability theorem because agent synthesis is nonlinear. The settling experiment is a multi-round replay with a fixed card budget, measuring unchanged-card reconsiderations, decision churn, and recovery when a genuinely new independent signal arrives.

Automatic tuning of a back-calculation gain is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a parameter sweep over historical multi-round runs, rejecting settings that either starve residuals or cause excessive card churn**. The first implementation should use no floating-point gain at all—only explicit event predicates.

### Proposal D — Hysteretic decision reopening with a hazard bypass

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Use different thresholds for admission and eviction. For example, a card might enter only after it has two independent evidence families and a named actuator, but remain admitted until evidence is invalidated, the actuator disappears, or a competing card exceeds it by a material margin. Impose a minimum dwell of one synthesis generation before rank-only eviction. New hard-hazard evidence bypasses both hysteresis and dwell time.

The purpose is to suppress chatter caused by tiny rescoring changes, not to suppress corrections. Every held decision must expose why it is being held and which signal would reopen it.

This catches oscillating recommendations—enable/disable, keep/replace, build/do-not-build—that alternate because near-tied scores or duplicated evidence move slightly between passes. It also ensures a genuinely new destructive-risk signal is not trapped behind a “stability” rule.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that asymmetric thresholds and a one-generation dwell reduce decision churn without materially delaying ordinary corrections. Evidence: deadband/event-trigger mechanisms trade small tolerated error for fewer activations, while a bypass protects discontinuous hazards. The settling experiment is a replay with noisy score perturbations and seeded reversals, comparing churn, reversal latency, and missed-hazard count.

The correct threshold gap is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to sweep the gap on held-out replays and choose the smallest gap that materially reduces churn while preserving a predeclared reversal-latency ceiling**.

### Proposal E — Post-decision observability contract

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

No decision card may claim “done” merely because an actuator was invoked. Closure requires two mechanically independent post-action signals, and each detector must first demonstrate a known-present control. The card declares these signals before action. If the outcome cannot be observed, the result is “action issued; outcome unobservable,” not “fixed.”

This is the most direct translation of observer design: the harness must arrange measurements that distinguish the intended state from plausible alternatives. It catches configurations that were written but not loaded, hooks that exist but do not execute, cleanups whose target count changed but whose operational failure persists, and tool fallbacks that hide a dead primary path.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that predeclared post-action signals reduce false closure claims. Evidence: without distinguishable output measurements, state cannot be inferred in the control-theoretic sense; the unit brief independently mandates two mechanically independent signals and detector controls. The settling experiment is a fault-injection suite containing “write succeeded/load failed,” “hook registered/did not fire,” and “proxy metric improved/user outcome did not,” scored for false closure.

## 3. Concrete end-to-end flow

The minimal flow is:

```text
existing findings/artifacts
        |
        v
schema validation: evidence, detector control, actuator, measurement
        |
        v
four-way O/C partition
        |
        +--> low O / high C --> experiment queue
        +--> high O / low C --> constraint + actuator-gap queue
        +--> low O / low C  --> residual ledger only
        |
        v
high O / high C causal-family grouping
        |
        v
bounded decision cards <---- actual-admission feedback ---- residual ledger
        |
        v
agent/owner decision
        |
        v
existing actuator bus
        |
        v
two independent post-action observations
        |
        +--> observed outcome: close
        +--> contradiction/new hazard: reopen immediately
        +--> small score drift only: hysteresis hold
```

The reducer is intentionally not an autonomous second planner. It validates fields, performs conservative grouping, allocates a bounded view, and maintains lossless pointers. Judgment remains in the agent/owner decision step.

## 4. Adversarial transfer audit

### 4.1 Where the analogy holds

1. **Separate sensing from influence.** Findings really do have an evidence side and an intervention side. Treating them separately is operationally meaningful even without an LTI model.
2. **A synthesis channel really is bounded.** Prompt tokens, attention, and owner reading time impose actual output saturation. Hidden accumulation behind that limit is a real bookkeeping hazard.
3. **Repeated decisions form a feedback process.** Later observations can confirm, contradict, or fail to distinguish the effects of earlier changes. Predeclared output measurements are therefore useful.
4. **Threshold chatter has a direct software analogue.** Small score changes can toggle a recommendation. Asymmetric enter/exit criteria are implementable without claiming a differential-equation model.

These structural correspondences make the pattern-level borrow **very likely (80-90%)** to be legitimate. Evidence: each correspondence maps to an explicit data field, limit, or transition that can be observed in a replay; none depends on fitting prose to a transfer function. The settling experiment is to instrument those transitions and falsify any mapping that cannot be observed distinctly.

### 4.2 Where the analogy fails

1. **No linear state space.** A finding is not a coordinate of an LTI state vector. Findings interact logically, causally, and institutionally; superposition fails.
2. **No stationary plant.** The harness, models, tasks, and owner preferences change. A matrix estimated yesterday may not describe tomorrow's synthesis process.
3. **No canonical input/output norm.** “Decision loss” mixes safety, cost, correctness, reversibility, and owner preference. There is no justified `H∞` norm over those outcomes.
4. **No valid Hankel singular values.** Frequency, severity, confidence, and actionability are not Gramians. Multiplying them does not recover balanced truncation.
5. **Rare hazards violate energy intuition.** A mode with little historical “energy” may represent a catastrophic one-off event and must not be truncated.
6. **Human/agent readers are part of the loop.** Their behavior is path-dependent, strategic, and susceptible to framing. The controller is not external to a fixed plant.
7. **Evidence sources are correlated.** Thousands of findings can descend from one mistaken detector. Count is not independent excitation.

Therefore it is **almost certain (90-99%)** that applying classical balanced-truncation equations directly to finding embeddings or score vectors would yield unjustified guarantees. Evidence: the cited theorems require stable LTI realizations, Gramians derived from `A,B,C`, and an input-output norm; the target supplies none by default. The experiment that could overturn this judgment would require defining a predictive state-space model in advance and demonstrating out-of-sample linearity, stationarity, controllability/observability ranks, and error-bound coverage across changing harness tasks. That is a much larger research program, not a prerequisite for BDS.

### 4.3 Failure modes of BDS itself

- **Goodharting the certificates.** Producers may fill actuator and evidence fields perfunctorily. Countermeasure: executable pointer validation and random blinded audits.
- **Schema-induced blindness.** A novel issue may not fit known actuator classes. Countermeasure: high-`O`/low-`C` is a first-class queue, not a discard bucket.
- **Correlated evidence masquerading as independence.** Countermeasure: evidence-family IDs based on detector, raw source, and failure mechanism; repeated prose never increases independence.
- **Hysteresis entrenchment.** A bad incumbent decision can persist. Countermeasure: contradictions and hard hazards bypass the band; every hold states its reopening condition.
- **Residual graveyard.** A lossless ledger can still become unread. Countermeasure: residual aging reports based on *new information and missing actuator*, not raw age, plus sampling audits.
- **Over-compression.** Cards may omit causal nuance. Countermeasure: pointers to full evidence and a red-team test that asks reviewers to reconstruct each decision from the card plus linked residuals.
- **False numerical authority.** `O` and `C` can look scientific while encoding judgment. Countermeasure: ordinal rubric categories, displayed components, no opaque combined score, and calibration experiments.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** whether certificate gaming becomes the dominant BDS failure after adoption. Evidence: structured mandatory fields improve inspectability but also create incentives to satisfy the form; no local deployment data exists. The settling experiment is a prospective audit of the first 100 produced cards, with independent reviewers checking whether pointers execute and whether evidence families are genuinely independent.

## 5. Alternatives considered

### Rejected: literal balanced truncation over embeddings

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)**

One could embed findings, infer latent “states,” estimate transition matrices, compute empirical Gramians, and truncate low singular-value modes. This reintroduces a second modeling/runtime layer and provides no trustworthy control-theoretic guarantee because the learned model is nonlinear, task-dependent, and weakly identified. It also risks discarding rare hazards. Rejected.

### Rejected: one scalar priority score

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

`severity × confidence × actionability` is compact but collapses the asymmetry between “well observed, no actuator” and “easy actuator, weak observation.” Equal products can demand opposite treatments. Rejected as the primary representation; component values can still assist ordering within a partition.

### Rejected: LLM-only recursive summarization

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Recursive summaries reduce tokens but do not inherently preserve provenance, contradictions, actuator gaps, or omitted-item reasons. They may be used to draft card prose only after deterministic admission and provenance rules. Rejected as the control mechanism.

### Rejected: vector database / knowledge graph memory

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)**

This is explicitly a previously rejected architecture class under the anti-overfit gate. Retrieval may help access, but it does not solve admission, dual certification, output saturation, or closure observability. Rejected.

### Retained: deterministic stratified sampling audit

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Sample residuals across `O/C` class, causal family, age, and consequence tier for human/agent review. This is not the main reducer; it is a detector-control mechanism for the reducer itself. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to reveal systematic omission classes if strata and random seeds are recorded. Evidence: every residual remains enumerated and stratification deliberately covers the reducer's own categories. The settling experiment is to seed known misclassifications into each stratum and measure detection probability across repeated samples.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate

BDS passes the stated gate:

- It rides existing artifact, skill, hook, or MCP buses; it does not require a second orchestration runtime.
- It uses JSON/Markdown records and a deterministic reducer, not a vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- The only proposed executable component is deliberately bounded at roughly 200 lines and can be replaced by a native renderer if the host acquires equivalent capabilities.
- The residual ledger preserves raw pointers, so a stronger future model can ignore the cards and re-synthesize from source without migration lock-in.
- Control-theory language does not become an extra abstraction layer in runtime. Runtime fields should be plain (`evidence`, `actuator`, `measurement`, `not_admitted_reason`), not `Gramian`, `plant`, or `Hankel mode`.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this architecture is smaller than a learned retrieval or orchestration service while addressing the specific synthesis failure. Evidence: the design adds a schema, a deterministic transform, and transition rules only. The settling experiment is an implementation spike that reports lines of code, dependencies, runtime state, and failure recovery; if it exceeds the BUILD-SMALL boundary, the design must be cut rather than silently retagged.

## 7. Recommended validation protocol

### Phase 0 — falsify the schema before building

Take a stratified historical sample and have two reviewers independently assign `O/C` classes. Measure agreement and catalogue disagreements. If reviewers cannot distinguish evidence sufficiency from actuator availability, stop: the transfer has failed operationally.

Success criterion is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to pre-register an agreement threshold before labeling and reject the schema if held-out agreement falls below it**. A threshold is not invented here because no baseline reviewer data exists.

### Phase 1 — shadow reducer

Generate cards and residual ledger without changing decisions. Check mechanically:

- every input ID appears exactly once as a card or represented residual;
- every card has executable evidence pointers;
- every merge shares causal mechanism and actuator;
- strongest contradiction survives;
- hard-hazard seeds always bypass the budget;
- output is byte-for-byte deterministic for the same ordered input and configuration.

### Phase 2 — blinded decision replay

Randomize reviewers between full-corpus/current synthesis and BDS cards. Measure:

- decision recall against an adjudicated gold set;
- unsupported decision rate;
- missed seeded hazard count;
- time and tokens read;
- ability to trace a decision to evidence;
- ability to name the actuator and post-action measurement;
- inter-round decision churn under score noise;
- reversal latency after genuine contradictory evidence.

### Phase 3 — shadow feedback

Run multiple synthesis generations with a fixed card budget. Feed actual admissions back to residual state, but do not alter live harness actions. Measure repeated processing of unchanged findings and whether new independent evidence reliably promotes the right residual.

### Phase 4 — narrow live use with rollback

Use BDS for one reversible decision family. Predeclare two independent success signals and rollback conditions. Do not generalize until rare-hazard and contradiction tests pass.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this sequence can distinguish a useful pattern transfer from a persuasive metaphor. Evidence: it directly tests inter-reviewer meaning, losslessness, decision quality, saturation behavior, and live observability in increasing-risk stages. The remaining uncertainty is external validity across task families; settle it by repeating the replay on at least three materially different families before broad adoption.

## 8. Final recommendation

### Adopt the pattern, reject the theorem claim

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build a shadow-mode Balanced Decision Surface consisting of:

1. a dual observation/control certificate in each finding record;
2. a deterministic four-way router;
3. bounded decision cards plus a complete residual ledger;
4. actual-admission feedback that prevents priority windup;
5. hysteretic reopening with an immediate hazard/contradiction bypass;
6. two-signal post-action closure contracts; and
7. stratified audits of what the reducer did not render.

Do **not** compute Hankel singular values, claim balanced-truncation error bounds, fit a latent plant, or introduce a new runtime. The control-theory contribution is disciplined separation of sensing from influence, explicit handling of saturation, and stable event thresholds—not a mathematical certification of prose compression.

The recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)** to be worth a shadow-mode experiment. Evidence: every added mechanism corresponds to a known control problem and to a mechanically testable synthesis failure; the implementation stays on the existing bus and preserves all source pointers. Production adoption remains **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the staged protocol in §7, with production blocked unless the BDS arm preserves adjudicated decision recall, misses no seeded hard hazard, and materially reduces reading volume without increasing unsupported decisions**.

## GAPS

- This artifact establishes no formal equivalence between natural-language findings synthesis and balanced model reduction. It therefore makes no theorem-level transfer claim; establishing one would require the predictive-model experiment specified in §4.2.
- No local harness files were inspected, by instruction. Therefore the proposal does not claim that any named schema, reducer, or hysteresis mechanism is currently absent. Whether an equivalent mechanism already exists is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a separate implementation audit using two mechanically independent signals after first validating each detector on a known-present mechanism**.
- Exact token/card budgets, rubric thresholds, dwell intervals, and hazard taxonomy boundaries are not set. Each requires empirical calibration; invented constants would be false precision.
- The cited control results apply under assumptions stated in their own domains. They support mechanism explanations, not outcome guarantees for agent synthesis.
