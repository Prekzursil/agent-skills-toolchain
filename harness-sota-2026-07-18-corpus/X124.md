# X124 — Cross-domain borrow: forensic-science controls for high-volume synthesis

**Unit:** X124  
**Source field:** forensic science (contamination control, blind verification, blind proficiency testing)  
**Target:** synthesis of thousands of findings into a decision without requiring the deciding agent to read the full multi-megabyte corpus  
**Lens constraint:** external research and translation only; no local-repository grounding was performed, as directed.  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19 UTC

## Executive verdict

The useful borrow is a **three-control protocol**, not a forensic metaphor:

1. **Linear Sequential Unmasking–Expanded (LSU-E)** controls which information reaches a decision-maker, in what order, and requires the decision-maker to record an initial judgment and later changes.
2. **Blind verification** gives a second examiner the evidence but not the first examiner's conclusion or identity, so agreement is not manufactured by exposure to the answer being checked.
3. **Blind proficiency testing** inserts realistic, known-answer work into the ordinary pipeline without telling the operator which case is a test, thereby testing the operational system rather than test-taking behavior alone.

This bundle transfers **very likely (80-90%) as a control of anchoring, correlated review, and untested compression loss**, because the target has the same causal structure: ambiguous evidence is filtered by humans/models; early conclusions can alter attention to later evidence; a verifier who sees the first conclusion is not independent; and declared tests do not exercise ordinary behavior. The evidence is (a) LSU-E's explicit sequencing rationale and criteria, (b) controlled fingerprint studies in which independent examinations exposed errors, and (c) Houston's operational blind-QC program, which routed 973 known-answer samples through routine workflows and kept 850 of 901 completed cases blind to analysts [Dror & Kukucka 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8385162/), [Ulery et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093498/), [Moral et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7317955/).

The bundle does **not** transfer as a truth machine. Strategic recommendations usually lack a specimen-level ground truth. Blind agreement can establish reproducibility but not correctness; the forensic literature states exactly this limitation for blind verification [Mejia, Cuellar & Salyards 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/). Known-answer synthetic tests can measure only the failure classes represented in the tests. Accordingly, this unit recommends a small protocol on the existing artifact/skill/hook bus, not a new orchestrator, knowledge graph, vector database, critic model, or durable-execution system.

The highest-value concrete translation is a **staged decision packet with an independent shadow ranking and covert, known-answer synthesis canaries**. It is **likely (55-80%) to catch material omissions that a single summarizer misses**, based on independent fingerprint review detecting every false positive and most false negatives in one large controlled study, plus Houston's evidence that realistic blind cases can exercise an end-to-end operational pipeline [Ulery et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093498/), [Moral et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7317955/). The lower band reflects two disanalogies: synthesis errors are often correlated across models, and many findings do not have objective truth labels.

## 1. What forensic science actually solved

### 1.1 LSU-E: control contamination by ordering exposure, not by pretending context is irrelevant

In its own field, LSU began with comparative examinations. The examiner first examines and documents the questioned crime-scene material by itself, before seeing the suspect/reference material. The reason is directional: the often-poor and ambiguous crime-scene item is more malleable than a clean reference, so seeing the target first invites circular reasoning—working backward from the target to the evidence. LSU-E generalizes this beyond comparisons. It instructs the expert to begin with raw data/evidence alone, then reveal task-relevant context in a deliberate sequence. It ranks information using three interacting criteria: **biasing power**, **objectivity**, and **relevance**. The expert records what was seen, when it was seen, and how the opinion changed [Dror & Kukucka 2021, §§1.1, 2–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8385162/).

This is not ordinary redaction and not permanent deprivation. The mechanism is a one-way information valve:

```text
questioned evidence alone
    -> document initial observations/judgment
    -> reveal less-biasing, more-objective, more-relevant context
    -> document delta
    -> reveal more-subjective or more-biasing context only when useful
    -> document final judgment and causal changes
```

The important solved problem is **contamination provenance**. When an opinion changes after contextual information is revealed, the record shows the exposure that preceded the change. It does not prove causation, but it prevents the final narrative from erasing its own path.

A 2024 operational pilot in Costa Rica gives useful, though non-randomized, implementation evidence. A case manager acted between judicial authorities and examiners, sorted incoming information, withheld irrelevant or prematurely biasing material, and used an LSU-E worksheet to control release. The pilot reports 161-plus clarifications handled without exposing assigned examiners to the underlying contextual traffic. The authors explicitly caution that information management cannot turn subjective judgment into objective judgment [Quigley-McBride et al. 2024, §§3.2.1–3.2.2, 3.4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11720873/).

### 1.2 Blind verification: prevent the verifier from inheriting the answer

In blind verification, a second examiner analyzes the same evidence without seeing the first examiner's conclusion (and, in the Costa Rica pilot, without seeing the examiner's identity). Only after both records are fixed are they compared; discrepancies trigger resolution [Quigley-McBride et al. 2024, §3.2.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11720873/). This differs mechanically from peer review in which the reviewer starts from the draft conclusion.

The controlled latent-fingerprint evidence illustrates both value and limits. In a study of 169 examiners making 17,121 decisions, five examiners made six false positives and false negatives occurred in 7.5% of comparisons of mated pairs. No two examiners made the same false-positive error; independent examination of the same comparisons—analogous to blind verification—would have exposed every false positive and most false negatives in that study. The authors also estimated that blind verification could materially reduce false negatives, while warning that outcomes share dependence on item difficulty and that resource costs matter [Ulery et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093498/). A follow-up retest found that independent examinations would have disagreed with 81% of original false-negative errors, which would send those decisions to conflict resolution [Ulery et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3299696/).

The transfer-relevant lesson is not “two votes are true.” It is: **do not reveal the candidate answer before eliciting the supposedly independent signal**. The 2024 Costa Rica pilot performed 201 blind verifications and observed 24 non-critical disputes, four critical disputes, and two technical-manager interventions, showing that blinding produced actionable disagreement in routine operation; the study design does not establish a counterfactual error reduction [Quigley-McBride et al. 2024, §3.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11720873/).

The frontier warning is unusually sharp. A June 2026 PNAS critique reports that, in the firearm-verification evidence it reviewed, odds of disagreement were five times higher when the verifier was blind than when the verifier saw the first conclusion; it concludes that nonblind verification lacks a scientific foundation. The same paper warns that proficiency results can be distorted by unrepresentative difficulty, consensus rather than independent ground truth, inconsistent scoring of inconclusives, unequal test items, and contextual bias [Scurich & Albright 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2528192123). That critique is directly relevant to synthesis: apparent agreement after showing the reviewer the draft is weak evidence.

### 1.3 Blind proficiency testing: test ordinary operation with realistic, known-answer cases

A declared proficiency test tells the analyst that the item is a test. A blind proficiency test makes the test look like ordinary work, routes it through ordinary intake and processing, and keeps the expected answer with an organizationally separate quality function. This assesses more than the final analytical step: it can expose problems in submission, handling, assignment, analysis, policy conformance, review, and reporting [NIJ, “Proficiency Testing”](https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/principles-forensic-dna-officers-court/05-assuring-quality-dna-testing/introduction-quality-assurance/proficiency-testing), [Peterson & Gaensslen 2001](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/developing-criteria-model-external-dna-proficiency-testing-final-report).

Houston Forensic Science Center's implementation is the strongest concrete operational example found. Its Quality Division, organizationally separate from testing sections, created samples with known expected answers, mimicked ordinary packaging/submission/workflow, and inserted them across toxicology, seized drugs, firearms, latent prints, biology, and multimedia. From 2015–2018, 973 blind samples were submitted, 901 completed, and analysts detected 51 as tests. The program could exercise order-of-operations and cross-discipline handoffs that declared tests did not [Moral et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7317955/).

Realism is essential and expensive. Houston analysts detected early tests from neat handwriting, atypical packaging, implausible materials, and familiar firearms. A multi-laboratory workshop likewise recorded that a crowbar's implausible print placement exposed a test and estimated that Houston spent about as much on blind as declared proficiency testing. It also emphasizes that blind verification without known ground truth measures consistency, not accuracy, and that blind proficiency tests are not themselves operational error-rate studies [Mejia, Cuellar & Salyards 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/).

## 2. Structural mapping—and the adversarial disanalogies

| Forensic structure | Synthesis analogue | Transfer status |
|---|---|---|
| Ambiguous questioned evidence can be reshaped by a salient reference | A terse or uncertain finding can be interpreted to fit the first architecture narrative | Strong process analogy. Initial evidence-only recording blocks retrospective fit. |
| Task-irrelevant case context biases an examiner | Author prestige, unit rhetoric, favored architecture, frequency, or an existing executive summary biases prioritization | Strong process analogy when these fields can be separated from evidence. |
| A verifier who sees the first conclusion can echo it | A reviewer editing an existing synthesis can rubber-stamp its framing and omissions | Strong process analogy. Blind parallel ranking yields a mechanically separate judgment. |
| A declared proficiency specimen changes behavior and tests only part of the process | A labeled evaluation set elicits extra care and may bypass routine extraction, deduplication, prioritization, and reporting | Strong process analogy if synthetic cases traverse the same artifact path. |
| Known-source specimens provide ground truth | Most strategic claims and trade-offs have no objective answer | **Major disanalogy.** Only designed canaries and a small class of factual checks have ground truth. |
| Physical chain of custody preserves a bounded specimen | Findings are interpretations that can be copied, merged, and semantically transformed | Partial analogy. Provenance IDs help, but semantic loss cannot be detected by hashes alone. |
| Two examiners may have partly independent perceptual errors | Two runs of the same foundation model may share training, prompt, and salience biases | **Major disanalogy.** Blindness removes answer leakage, not correlated model error. |
| A laboratory can sample a reasonably stable stream of case types | Frontier research topics and architecture choices drift rapidly | Partial analogy. Canary banks and strata require versioning and retirement. |

### Why the borrow passes, narrowly

The analogy passes because the proposed mechanisms intervene on **information flow and evaluation design**, not on domain content. Ordering effects, anchoring, answer leakage, Hawthorne/test-awareness effects, and end-to-end pipeline gaps can occur whether the evidence is a fingerprint or a finding card. That causal correspondence is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by LSU-E's cross-domain cognitive rationale, the fingerprint blind-review studies, and blind-QC operational evidence cited above.

The analogy would fail if used to claim that two synthesizers agreeing makes a recommendation correct, that a source citation is equivalent to a known-source specimen, or that canary recall estimates the real-world synthesis error rate. Those claims are **almost certainly not (1-5%) justified** by the cited evidence, because the forensic sources themselves distinguish consistency from accuracy and warn that test difficulty, scoring, and representativeness govern valid inference [Mejia, Cuellar & Salyards 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/), [Scurich & Albright 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2528192123).

## 3. Concrete harness translation

### Proposal A — Evidence-first staged decision packet — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a packet assembler plus a synthesis skill. Do not ask the deciding agent to read the 12 MB. Instead, require every upstream unit to emit one or more compact, structured **finding cards at creation time**, next to its narrative artifact:

```yaml
finding_id: stable unique ID
claim: one falsifiable sentence
decision_relevance: what choice it changes
evidence:
  - URL or artifact pointer with exact locator
evidence_class: measured | observed | primary-source | audit-claim | inference | proposal
confidence_band: required vocabulary
verification_state: verified | unverified
severity: consequence if ignored
scope: affected subsystem(s)
contradicts: [finding_id...]
supersedes: [finding_id...]
source_unit: provenance only; hidden in early stages
```

This avoids a second lossy extraction pass: the unit that actually read the source creates the card while its evidence is live. A deterministic assembler validates required fields, rejects dangling evidence locators, groups cards by decision and contradiction cluster, and counts every input card exactly once as `presented`, `deferred with reason`, or `duplicate/superseded with pointer`. The full narrative remains drill-down evidence, not mandatory context.

Release the packet in fixed stages:

1. **Stage Q (questioned evidence):** measured/observed facts, primary-source propositions, evidence locator, scope, verification state; hide source-unit identity, recommendations, popularity/frequency, and prior executive summaries. The synthesizer records candidate failure modes and an initial priority ordering.
2. **Stage C (comparison/context):** contradiction clusters, consequence/severity, independent corroboration counts, and explicit uncertainty. The synthesizer records changes as a delta, not by overwriting Stage Q.
3. **Stage R (recommendations):** proposed solutions, costs, owner preferences, implementation constraints, and author/unit identity only if needed. The synthesizer records which evidence changed which decision.
4. **Stage D (drill-down):** retrieve full artifacts only for contested, high-impact, sampled-minority, or provenance-failing cards. The agent reads a bounded evidence slice rather than the entire corpus.

The premise that upstream units can reliably produce sufficiently atomic cards is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X124-E1 Card Sufficiency` settles it**: draw a risk-stratified sample of at least 200 cards across evidence classes, have assessors blind to the cards extract decisions from the original artifacts, and measure claim recall, evidence-locator validity, polarity reversals, and decision-impact agreement. Do not deploy packet-only synthesis unless predeclared thresholds are met (suggested initial gates: ≥95% materially correct claim/polarity, ≥99% resolvable locators, and 100% coverage accounting; these numeric gates are design choices, not literature-derived facts).

This proposal is **likely (55-80%) to reduce anchoring and “narrative capture”**, because it implements LSU-E's evidence-first sequencing and immutable opinion-delta record. The evidence is the LSU-E mechanism and the Costa Rica case-manager implementation. The band stops below “very likely” because finding-card construction may itself bake in the source unit's framing.

What it would catch that a monolithic read or one-shot summary can miss:

- a high-frequency recommendation whose evidence is weak or circular;
- a measured defect hidden behind a confident prior executive summary;
- a late contextual claim that flips an initially evidence-driven priority;
- an orphaned or non-resolving evidence pointer;
- a claim silently dropped as “duplicate” without a supersession pointer;
- a minority, high-severity finding drowned by raw frequency;
- contradictions that prose summarization smooths into false consensus.

### Proposal B — Blind shadow synthesis and discrepancy docket — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Run two existing agent invocations against the same Stage Q and Stage C cards, with randomized card order within strata. Neither invocation sees the other's ranking, rationale, or selected decisions. Freeze both outputs before comparison. A deterministic comparer then opens a **discrepancy docket** containing:

- top-priority items selected by only one run;
- polarity or action conflicts for the same finding/decision;
- cards deferred by one run but promoted by the other;
- contradiction clusters resolved differently;
- evidence locators used by only one run;
- large rank differences, reported as differences rather than averaged away.

Only the discrepancy docket goes to adjudication. Do not use majority vote, and do not ask one agent to “critique” a visible first draft; both recreate the decision from evidence independently. If both runs use the same model, vary prompt framing and card order, but report shared-model dependence explicitly.

The premise that blind parallel synthesis adds enough non-correlated signal to justify its token cost is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X124-E2 Blind-v-Visible Review` settles it**: construct a benchmark with adjudicated factual findings and planted omissions; randomly assign review to (a) visible-draft critique or (b) blind reconstruction; compare unique material-error detection, false challenges, and tokens per caught error. Stratify results by same-model versus different-model pairs.

Blind reconstruction is **likely (55-80%) to reveal more disagreements than visible-draft review**, supported by the forensic studies in which independent examinations exposed errors and the 2026 report of substantially greater disagreement under blind firearm verification. Whether those extra disagreements are useful rather than noise is only **roughly even (45-55%)** until `X124-E2`, because synthesis lacks pervasive known ground truth and same-model failures may correlate.

What it catches:

- review that agrees because it inherited the proposed answer;
- entire decision branches absent from the first draft;
- alternative causal explanations the first narrative foreclosed;
- a “consensus” that disappears when judgments are elicited independently;
- sensitivity to incidental within-stratum card order.

### Proposal C — Shadow-mode blind synthesis proficiency tests — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Maintain a versioned canary bank owned by the evaluation layer, not by the synthesis prompt authors. For a randomly selected **shadow copy** of an ordinary synthesis job, insert realistic finding cards with known scoring outcomes. The production decision never sees the altered packet; only the shadow run does. This preserves operational blindness without contaminating a real decision.

Initial canary families should test the pipeline rather than trivia:

1. **Known duplicate:** two paraphrased cards with the same evidence ID; pass if merged with provenance retained, fail if counted as independent corroboration.
2. **Known contradiction:** two cards assert opposite polarities and cite distinct locators; pass if a conflict is surfaced, fail if prose silently reconciles them.
3. **Unsupported authority:** a confident recommendation with no resolvable evidence locator; pass if quarantined, fail if promoted.
4. **Minority catastrophic risk:** one low-frequency, high-consequence measured finding among many cosmetic findings; pass if it reaches the discrepancy/decision docket.
5. **Superseded evidence:** an older card explicitly superseded by a newer primary source; pass if recency/provenance is handled without double counting.
6. **Instruction-shaped evidence:** a card body contains text that attempts to change the synthesis procedure; pass if treated as quoted evidence, not executable instruction.
7. **Locator decay:** plausible claim with a deliberately non-resolving locator; pass if marked unverified rather than accepted.
8. **Order pair:** matched shadow runs with opposite order inside one stratum; score rank/polarity stability, not a single “correct” recommendation.

Each canary needs an independently specified scoring rule, difficulty label, date introduced, retirement date, and realism audit. Seeders must not score their own ambiguous cases. Report per-family detection and false-positive rates; never collapse them into a single “synthesis accuracy” number. Rotate surface forms so the harness cannot memorize signatures. Keep a clean holdout bank that prompt and workflow authors cannot inspect.

The premise that these cards are indistinguishable from real cards and representative of real synthesis failures is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X124-E3 Canary Blinding and Generalization` settles it**: first ask agents to classify mixed cards as real/test and require discrimination near chance with confidence intervals; then compare canary-family performance with independently adjudicated naturally occurring failures over at least three workflow versions. A test family that is easily recognized or does not predict its corresponding natural failure is retired, not celebrated.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%) to detect failures in deduplication, contradiction surfacing, provenance validation, and instruction/data separation**, because those cases have programmable ground truth and traverse the routine synthesis path. It is **unlikely (20-45%) to quantify overall strategic-decision quality**, because canaries cannot represent unknown unknowns or settle genuine trade-offs; the forensic proficiency-testing literature supplies the same warning about representativeness and inference.

What it catches:

- a “working” synthesis pipeline that only succeeds when told it is under evaluation;
- a schema or summarizer update that silently stops propagating minority risks;
- duplicate findings inflated into false corroboration;
- contradiction handling that converts disagreement into fluent consensus;
- evidence text that escapes its data boundary and controls the agent;
- broken locator verification hidden by plausible prose;
- degradation after a model, prompt, or packet-schema change.

### Proposal D — Coverage ledger and risk-stratified drill-down — **UNVERIFIED**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Use deterministic accounting, not model self-report, to prove that every card reached one terminal state. Then select full-artifact drill-down with a predeclared mixture:

- all unresolved high-severity contradictions;
- all cards with missing/broken provenance;
- all singleton catastrophic-risk cards;
- a random sample from every decision/evidence stratum;
- a novelty sample from low-frequency clusters;
- a sample of items marked duplicate or superseded.

This is contamination control by sampling: the deciding agent need not read all narratives, but the process cannot silently equate “not selected” with “not present.” Random sampling also provides a route for discovering extraction defects that priority-only sampling would never inspect.

The premise that the proposed strata capture the tail risks important to this owner is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X124-E4 Stratified Coverage Audit` settles it**: for a historical set with adjudicated owner corrections, compare recall under frequency-only, risk-only, and mixed stratified drill-down at equal context budgets. Freeze strata before evaluation to prevent tuning on the answer set.

Mixed sampling is **likely (55-80%) to outperform top-frequency-only reading for rare severe omissions**, because it guarantees representation of singleton/high-severity and randomly sampled strata by construction. Whether the severity labels are trustworthy is **roughly even (45-55%)** until `X124-E1` and `X124-E4`, because upstream authors may inflate or misclassify severity.

## 4. End-to-end operating protocol

```text
UPSTREAM UNIT (while evidence is live)
  narrative artifact + finding cards + exact locators
                    |
                    v
DETERMINISTIC INTAKE
  schema validation -> provenance resolution -> duplicate/contradiction candidates
  -> coverage ledger (every card accounted for)
                    |
                    v
PACKET CASE MANAGER
  Stage Q evidence-only -> freeze initial decisions
  Stage C context/conflicts -> freeze deltas
                    |
          +---------+---------+
          |                   |
          v                   v
 BLIND SYNTHESIS A      BLIND SYNTHESIS B
          |                   |
          +---------+---------+
                    v
DISCREPANCY DOCKET + RISK-STRATIFIED DRILL-DOWN
                    |
                    v
Stage R recommendations/constraints -> adjudicated decision + dissent

Separately, on randomly selected shadow copies:
  hidden canary seeder -> same ordinary pipeline -> scored known-answer results
  (never feed altered packet/output into production decision)
```

The case-manager role should be mechanical wherever possible. A model deciding what the synthesizer is allowed to see could simply relocate bias upstream. Rules for evidence class, field masking, card accounting, randomization, and reveal order should therefore be deterministic; model judgment is reserved for semantic clustering and decision-making, with its mappings exposed for review.

## 5. Acceptance metrics and stop conditions

These metrics are proposals, not claims of validated thresholds:

| Control | Measure | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Card sufficiency | material claim/polarity recall against source; locator resolution | packet-only decision loses or reverses source meaning |
| Coverage | `input cards = presented + deferred + duplicate/superseded`; all terminal states carry reason/pointer | any unaccounted ID or silent drop |
| LSU-E delta | decisions changed after each reveal, with cited triggering cards | final answer overwrites history or changes without evidence |
| Blind verification | unique material discrepancies found before answer exchange | visible critique matches blindly reconstructed performance, making second pass wasteful |
| Canary blindness | agent ability to distinguish canary from ordinary card | above-chance discrimination means the test is declared in practice |
| Canary performance | detection and false-positive rate per family/version/difficulty | aggregate score hides a failing family or realism drift |
| Stability | rank/polarity changes across within-stratum order permutations | large changes without substantive evidence change |
| Drill-down yield | material source/card defects found per full artifact read | sampling budget produces no useful discoveries over several preregistered evaluations |
| Cost | tokens and wall time per unique material error caught | extra pass costs more than the errors justify under an owner-set exchange rate |

Stop or roll back a control if its preregistered test shows no incremental material-error detection at matched budget; if canaries become recognizable; if cardization produces unacceptable polarity/provenance loss; or if blind shadow runs create only unactionable disagreement. This is **very likely (80-90%) to constrain harness overfitting** because every added control has a named error class, a matched comparison, and a deletion condition; the evidence is architectural rather than empirical, so the residual uncertainty concerns whether teams enforce the stop rules.

## 6. Failure modes the analogy does not solve

1. **Shared-source error.** If all cards faithfully repeat a false primary source, staged exposure and blind review may reproduce the error. No claim of correctness follows.
2. **Cardizer contamination.** The upstream unit can omit inconvenient evidence before the case manager sees it. Random source audits reduce but cannot eliminate this.
3. **Correlated agents.** Two prompts on one model are not two independent scientific instruments. Disagreement is informative; agreement has limited evidentiary weight.
4. **No ground truth for values.** A canary can prove that a catastrophic singleton was surfaced, not that its preferred remediation is optimal.
5. **Synthetic-test gaming.** Repeated families can become recognizable. Realism testing, holdouts, rotation, and shadow-only insertion mitigate but do not solve distribution drift.
6. **Schema Goodharting.** Required confidence, severity, and evidence fields can induce superficially compliant but meaningless entries. Source audits must score semantic adequacy, not field presence.
7. **False objectivity from counts.** Five derivative cards are not five independent signals. Evidence identity and dependency must survive deduplication.
8. **Dissent overload.** Surfacing every discrepancy can recreate the original context problem. The docket needs consequence/evidence thresholds and a bounded drill-down budget.
9. **Packet assembler as hidden oracle.** If a model silently chooses clusters, labels, or reveal strata, the system merely moves synthesis upstream. Expose mappings and sample their source validity.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### One giant-context synthesis pass

Rejected because it offers no independent signal, no immutable pre-context judgment, no coverage accounting, and no operational proficiency measure. Larger context may improve recall, but it does not by itself control order effects or answer leakage. The claim that a giant-context pass is worse on this owner's workload is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X124-E5 Full-Context Baseline` settles it** by comparing it with the proposed protocol at equal total tokens on adjudicated historical decisions.

### Reviewer critiques the first draft

Rejected as the primary verification mechanism because seeing the first conclusion compromises independence. It may remain useful after blind discrepancies are fixed. The direct forensic evidence favors independent examination for error exposure [Ulery et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093498/) and the 2026 PNAS critique specifically challenges nonblind verification [Scurich & Albright 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2528192123).

### Majority vote or consensus score

Rejected because agreement does not establish ground truth, correlated models can agree, and consensus scoring is one of the current proficiency-test validity problems identified by Scurich and Albright. Preserve disagreement and adjudicate it against evidence.

### Permanent context redaction

Rejected because LSU-E is sequencing, not ignorance. Relevant context may be necessary to interpret consequence, feasibility, and action. Reveal it later and record the delta.

### New orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, trained critic, or observability service

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. The proposed controls are artifact schemas, deterministic packet operations, ordinary agent invocations, and shadow tests on the existing bus. A new runtime would add a failure surface without being necessary to test this borrow.

### Canaries in the production decision packet

Rejected because synthetic claims could contaminate a real architectural decision even if removed later. The safer design copies the packet into an isolated shadow run. This sacrifices true live-run coverage but preserves the decision's evidence boundary.

## 8. Minimum experiment sequence

1. Run `X124-E1 Card Sufficiency` first. If finding cards do not preserve claims, polarity, and locators, stop; every downstream control would be validating a corrupted representation.
2. Run `X124-E5 Full-Context Baseline` and `X124-E2 Blind-v-Visible Review` on the same adjudicated set. This establishes whether staged packets and blind reconstruction add error detection per token.
3. Build only four simple canary families initially: duplicate, contradiction, unsupported authority, and locator decay. Run `X124-E3` for blindness and natural-failure correlation before adding more.
4. Run `X124-E4` to tune sampling allocation without changing the preregistered evaluation set.
5. Only then test the full protocol prospectively in shadow mode across workflow/model changes. Promotion to decision support requires a demonstrated incremental catch rate and bounded false-challenge cost; it must not rely on confidence or fluency judgments.

This order is **very likely (80-90%) to fail cheaply if the central representation is unsound**, because E1 tests the prerequisite before parallel-agent or canary costs are incurred. It is **likely (55-80%) to yield a useful minimal protocol if the borrow transfers**, because each later component has a distinct error target and ablation; the evidence is the explicit dependency structure, not an already-run harness experiment.

## 9. Gaps and evidence limits

- No local harness files, corpus fields, audit findings, or hook behavior were inspected, per the explicit ideation-lens instruction. Therefore this unit makes no claim that the proposed card schema or hooks fit the subject tree as currently implemented.
- The Costa Rica pilot is operational evidence without a randomized counterfactual. Its reported disputes and case-manager activity establish feasibility and observable intervention, not an error-rate reduction.
- The fingerprint studies have known-source image pairs and bounded categorical decisions. Their error-detection rates must not be numerically transferred to open-ended synthesis.
- Houston's 2015–2018 program establishes operational feasibility in one well-resourced organization and exposes realism costs. It does not establish that a text-card canary will remain blind in this harness.
- The June 2026 PNAS paper is a critique of forensic proficiency-test design, not an agent-synthesis experiment. Its warnings about ground truth, difficulty, scoring, and nonblind verification transfer conceptually; the proposed experiments must validate the translation.
- “Double-blind” is deliberately not claimed for the proposed harness. The synthesis agents are blind to each other's answers and canary identity, but the canary seeder knows the ground truth and packet construction. Calling this double-blind would overstate the control.
