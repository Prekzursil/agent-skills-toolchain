# X101 — Cross-domain borrow: metrology → synthesis

**Unit:** X101  
**Source field:** metrology (calibration, traceability, measurement uncertainty, reference standards)  
**Target problem:** collapsing thousands of findings into a decision without requiring an agent or owner to read 12 MB  
**Research mode:** external ideation lens only; no local repository or subject-tree inspection  
**Date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive judgment

The useful borrow is a **traceable decision certificate**, built from four linked metrology mechanisms rather than from “better summarization”:

1. define the measurand before measuring;
2. relate every result to an explicit reference through a documented calibration/traceability chain;
3. carry an uncertainty budget, including shared dependencies, with the result;
4. apply a declared conformity decision rule that exposes false-accept and false-reject risk.

In metrology, this combination allows a user to act on a compact result without personally repeating every calibration or reading every laboratory notebook. The compact result is not merely a number: the VIM defines a measurement result as the values attributed to a measurand **plus relevant information**, and notes that it is generally expressed with measurement uncertainty ([JCGM VIM 2.9](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.9.html)). Metrological traceability is specifically a property of a result connected to a reference by a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing uncertainty ([JCGM VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). An uncertainty budget records the components, their calculation, and their combination ([JCGM VIM 2.33](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.33.html)). Conformity assessment then turns the result into an accept/reject action under a stated decision rule and makes false acceptance and false rejection explicit ([JCGM 106:2012, §9.3.2](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_106_2012_E.pdf)).

**Transfer verdict:** a constrained structural transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve audit synthesis, because the target has the same information-topology problem—many transformations between primary evidence and a consequential decision—and the cited metrology mechanisms explicitly preserve reference, uncertainty, dependence, and decision policy across such transformations. The numerical GUM calculus is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** valid if applied directly to free-form audit findings, because those findings are not commensurate observations of a stable physical quantity, lack empirically justified probability distributions, and often share prompts, sources, or model failure modes. Therefore this unit recommends a provenance-and-decision protocol, not “confidence arithmetic.”

The concrete output would be a small deterministic compiler that consumes machine-readable **finding certificates** and emits a short **decision docket** plus a complete reconciliation ledger. Humans read the exceptions and decision-critical evidence; machines check coverage, conflicts, reference lineage, detector validation, and dependency clusters. The proposal is **UNVERIFIED — experiment X101-E1 (a blinded replay against a completed large audit, specified below) would settle whether it improves decision accuracy and reading load**. Its expected benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the strong structural match but reduced by the absence of a universal reference standard and calibrated numerical uncertainty in the target domain.

## 1. What metrology solved in its own field

### 1.1 Define the measurand, not merely the topic

Metrology begins by specifying the quantity intended to be measured. The VIM calls that quantity the **measurand** and says its specification requires the kind of quantity and the relevant state of the phenomenon, body, or substance ([JCGM VIM 2.3](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.3.html)). The notes make an important distinction: what a measuring system actually affects or observes can differ from what was intended, requiring a correction in the measurement model.

This solves a deceptively central synthesis problem in metrology: two values cannot be meaningfully reconciled merely because they share a label. “Length” at different temperatures, or voltage under load versus open-circuit voltage, can be different measurands. A crisp measurand prevents a synthesis procedure from averaging answers to different questions.

The direct lesson is not “give every audit topic a score.” It is: **name the exact decision quantity before accepting inputs**. A finding about “tool reliability in historical sessions,” a finding about “configuration correctness today,” and a recommendation about “future owner utility” do not measure the same thing. A synthesis system that merges them under “tool quality” commits the analogue of a measurand-definition error.

### 1.2 Make the compact result carry its lineage

The VIM defines metrological traceability as a property of a measurement result by which it can be related to a reference through a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, with every calibration contributing to uncertainty ([JCGM VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). It defines a traceability chain as the sequence of standards and calibrations used to relate the result to that reference ([JCGM VIM 2.42](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.42.html)). A calibration hierarchy is a sequence in which each calibration outcome depends on the previous outcome, and its uncertainty necessarily grows down the chain ([JCGM VIM 2.40](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.40.html)).

Several details matter for synthesis:

- The reference is explicit; “traceable” is not a synonym for “has provenance.”
- The chain is attached to the result, not kept only as ambient institutional knowledge.
- The chain can branch or form a network when several input quantities contribute, and the VIM says effort should be commensurate with each input’s contribution ([JCGM VIM 2.41, Notes 3-4](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)).
- Traceability alone does not prove fitness for purpose or absence of mistakes ([JCGM VIM 2.41, Note 5](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). This is a critical anti-analogy safeguard: perfect citations do not make a claim correct.

NIST’s traceability policy makes the operational burden concrete. Merely using an artifact calibrated by NIST is not sufficient; the provider must document the measurement process, the calibration chain, the result and uncertainty, the reference used, and measurement-assurance status ([NIST, “Metrological Traceability: Frequently Asked Questions and NIST Policy,” §5.2.1](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)). This is why a recipient can inspect a compact certificate and selectively traverse only the links that matter.

### 1.3 Carry an uncertainty budget rather than a naked confidence score

The VIM defines an **uncertainty budget** as a statement of measurement uncertainty, its components, and their calculation and combination. Its note says the budget should include the measurement model, estimates, input uncertainties, covariances, probability distributions, degrees of freedom, evaluation types, and coverage factor ([JCGM VIM 2.33](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.33.html)). The GUM’s summary procedure likewise requires identifying all significant contributors, evaluating covariance for correlated inputs, calculating the combined uncertainty, and reporting how the result and uncertainty were obtained ([JCGM 100:2008, Clause 8](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/GUM/JCGM100/C045315e-html/C045315e_FILES/MAIN_C045315e/08_e.html)).

The dependence treatment is especially relevant. The GUM states that the independent-input formula is valid only for independent or uncorrelated inputs; significant correlations must be included. It gives use of the same instrument, reference standard, reference datum, or method as common causes of correlation ([JCGM 100:2008, §§5.2.1-5.2.5](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/GUM/JCGM100/C045315e-html/C045315e_FILES/MAIN_C045315e/05_e.html)). Metrology therefore does not treat ten readings from one biased chain as ten independent confirmations.

The mechanism compresses responsibly because the result is accompanied by a structured account of what could move it and how dependencies were handled. It allows a reviewer to focus on dominant uncertainty contributors instead of replaying every operation.

### 1.4 Use reference comparisons to test the measuring organizations

Metrology does not rely only on paper lineage. Interlaboratory comparisons use a common artefact or measurand, a comparison reference value with uncertainty, and a **degree of equivalence** for each participant. A current BIPM quick reference guide defines a degree of equivalence as the participant’s deviation from the comparison reference value together with expanded uncertainty, and requires reports to state the individual results, how the reference value was calculated, and how degrees of equivalence were calculated ([BIPM CCQM Quick Reference Guide to Comparisons, §§2.5-2.6](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2078587/CCQM-Quick%20Reference%20Guide%20to%20comparisons.pdf/d960051a-be80-461c-df21-3a84b078b906)). ISO 13528:2022 describes statistical methods for proficiency testing by interlaboratory comparison and says the procedures can assess even expert judgments when outputs can be compared objectively with an independent reference value or consensus statistic ([ISO 13528:2022 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/78879.html)).

NIST measurement assurance adds temporal controls: a check standard is measured over time, and control procedures detect persistent shifts in bias or variability that can indicate changed standards, procedures, environment, handling, or instrumentation ([NIST Engineering Statistics Handbook, §2.2.2](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section2/mpc22.htm)). A traceable calibration made last month does not excuse an instrument that drifted today.

These are two complementary checks:

1. **reference comparison / proficiency test:** can this laboratory recover a result whose reference is independently established?
2. **check standard over time:** has this local measurement process drifted since it last demonstrated competence?

### 1.5 Separate measurement from the decision rule

JCGM 106:2012 treats conformity assessment as a decision under measurement uncertainty. With a binary decision rule, it distinguishes valid acceptance, false acceptance, valid rejection, and false rejection. It names the probability of false acceptance consumer’s risk and false rejection producer’s risk ([JCGM 106:2012, §9.3.2](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_106_2012_E.pdf)). ILAC G8:09/2019 exists specifically to guide laboratories in using decision rules when issuing conformity statements ([ILAC G8 publication page](https://ilac.org/publications-and-resources/ilac-guidance-series/)).

The key mechanism is separation of concerns:

- a measured result and its uncertainty describe evidence;
- a tolerance or requirement describes what “conforming” means;
- an acceptance interval and decision rule describe what action to take;
- the gap between those constructs determines the two kinds of wrong decision.

That separation stops a report writer from silently turning “some evidence supports this” into “ship it,” and it permits different risk preferences to act on the same evidence.

### 1.6 The metrological compression pattern

Metrology’s answer to “I cannot read every notebook” is therefore not lossy prose compression. It is a small, standardized result object whose fields preserve:

`measurand → value/result → reference chain → uncertainty budget and dependencies → decision rule → conformity statement`

The reference chain and budget allow selective expansion. The decision rule makes the final reduction inspectable. Reference comparisons and check standards test whether the organizations producing the certificates remain competent. A BIPM key-comparison display demonstrates the compact form: the reference value and its uncertainty are reported, then each laboratory’s degree of equivalence is represented by its deviation and expanded uncertainty ([BIPM KCDB example, CCEM-K2.2012](https://www.bipm.org/kcdb/comparison?id=1010)).

## 2. Translation to an agent harness

### 2.1 Mapping table

| Metrology construct | Harness translation | What must remain explicit |
|---|---|---|
| Measurand | One named decision question or evaluand | scope, time, owner objective, state under evaluation, and success criterion |
| Measurement standard/reference | Versioned owner intent, requirement, benchmark oracle, or accepted test procedure | exact reference ID/version; reference type; who can revise it |
| Measuring system | Agent + prompt/skill + tools + detector/query + model/runtime version | identity, version, execution context, known applicability limits |
| Calibration | Running the measuring system against reference cases with known adjudicated outcomes | case ID, expected output, observed output, discrepancy, date |
| Measurement result | Finding certificate | claim, polarity, scope, evidence pointers, uncertainty/limitations, lineage |
| Uncertainty budget | Structured uncertainty ledger | source weakness, detector coverage, transfer assumptions, staleness, conflicts, shared dependencies |
| Covariance/correlation | Dependency cluster | same source, same detector, same prompt/model, copied claim, common upstream summary |
| Key comparison / proficiency test | Multiple units independently analyze a small blinded gold case | deviations from adjudicated answer and disagreement pattern |
| Check standard | Fixed canary corpus replayed periodically through ingestion and synthesis | missed seeded facts, false absences, polarity errors, drift over time |
| Tolerance/specification | Owner-authored acceptance criteria and hard constraints | pass conditions, vetoes, trade-off policy |
| Decision rule | Deterministic policy that maps qualified findings to accept/reject/defer/escalate | thresholds, guard band, risk owner, treatment of missing/conflicting evidence |
| Certificate | Decision docket plus complete machine-readable reconciliation ledger | decision, decisive evidence, exceptions, unresolved uncertainty, reversible next action |

The word “calibration” in this translation must remain narrow. A prompt passing a few gold cases demonstrates performance on those cases; it does not establish universal competence. Calling prompt tuning itself “calibration” would confuse adjustment with calibration, a distinction the VIM explicitly warns about ([JCGM VIM 2.39, Notes 1-3](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.39.html)).

## 3. Proposed build

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: versioned Finding Certificate schema

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Require every research/audit unit to emit one JSON object per atomic finding in addition to prose. This proposal is **UNVERIFIED — experiment X101-E1 would test whether units can populate the schema consistently without reducing substantive finding recall**. Schema compliance is **very likely (80-90%)** to be mechanically enforceable because required fields and enumerations are ordinary JSON validation; semantic correctness remains only **roughly even (45-55%)** without reference-case calibration because an agent can fill valid fields incorrectly.

Minimum certificate:

```json
{
  "finding_id": "stable-id",
  "decision_question_id": "DQ-...",
  "claim": "one falsifiable proposition",
  "polarity": "supports|opposes|qualifies|unknown",
  "scope": {
    "subject": "...",
    "time": "...",
    "conditions": ["..."]
  },
  "reference": {
    "id": "owner-rule/test/oracle/version",
    "type": "owner_intent|requirement|benchmark|procedure|none",
    "version_or_date": "..."
  },
  "evidence": [
    {
      "pointer": "URL or path:line or command-output artifact",
      "observation": "what the source directly establishes",
      "signal_family": "static|runtime|history|control|external",
      "content_hash": "optional"
    }
  ],
  "detector_validation": {
    "required_for": "absent|broken|done|not_applicable",
    "positive_control": "known-present item and observed detection",
    "independent_signal_ids": ["signal-a", "signal-b"]
  },
  "uncertainty_ledger": [
    {
      "component": "source|detector|scope|transfer|staleness|model|conflict",
      "direction": "could_overstate|could_understate|unknown",
      "magnitude": "dominant|material|minor|unknown",
      "evidence": "why"
    }
  ],
  "dependency_keys": [
    "source:...", "detector:...", "prompt:...", "upstream-summary:..."
  ],
  "confidence_band": "one owner-mandated exact band",
  "confidence_evidence": "evidence for the band",
  "severity_if_true": "critical|high|medium|low",
  "reversibility": "easy|moderate|hard",
  "settling_experiment": "required when UNVERIFIED",
  "artifact_pointer": "full unit prose"
}
```

Important design constraints:

- `claim` is atomic. A certificate that asserts both cause and remedy must be split.
- `observation` separates what a source directly shows from the unit’s inference.
- `reference.type = none` is allowed and visible; the compiler must not invent a reference.
- `detector_validation` implements the owner’s two-independent-signal and positive-control rule for absence/broken/done claims.
- `uncertainty_ledger` is categorical. Confidence bands must not be converted to point probabilities.
- `dependency_keys` expose shared upstream causes. Ten findings with the same source and detector remain one independence cluster for corroboration.
- Full prose remains addressable via `artifact_pointer`; the schema is an index/certificate, not a replacement for source work.

What this catches: findings attached to different decision questions but merged by vocabulary; claims with no governing reference; naked confidence; single-detector absence claims; and copied evidence masquerading as independent corroboration. Detection of these structural omissions is **almost certain (90-99%)** once schema validation is mandatory, because each condition corresponds to a required or enumerable field. Detection of semantically dishonest values is **unlikely (20-45%)** from schema alone, because syntax cannot establish truth.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: deterministic Decision Certificate Compiler

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build a deterministic reducer—no second agent orchestration runtime—that ingests certificates and emits:

1. a one- to three-page decision docket;
2. a machine-readable reconciliation ledger covering every input ID;
3. an exception queue containing conflicts, invalid certificates, unsupported decisive claims, and boundary cases.

This proposal is **UNVERIFIED — experiment X101-E1 would compare it with unconstrained agent-written synthesis on decision correctness, unsupported conclusions, conflict retention, and reading time**. A deterministic compiler is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce evidence-dropping and false consensus, because total input reconciliation and dependency grouping are mechanically testable; it is only **roughly even (45-55%)** to improve the substantive choice without owner-authored decision rules, because a compiler cannot supply missing values or adjudicate genuinely normative conflicts.

#### Compiler algorithm

**Stage 0 — validate and quarantine**

- Validate schema and allowed confidence vocabulary.
- Quarantine certificates that assert absent/broken/done without a positive control and two signal families.
- Quarantine `UNVERIFIED` recommendations without a named settling experiment.
- Retain quarantined IDs in the reconciliation ledger; never silently drop them.

**Stage 1 — partition by decision question and scope**

- Never combine certificates with different `decision_question_id` values.
- Within a question, separate incompatible subject/time/condition scopes.
- Require an explicit scope-translation certificate before evidence from one scope can decide another.

This is the measurand-definition gate.

**Stage 2 — construct reference and evidence lineage**

- Form a DAG from decision question → requirement/reference → finding → evidence pointer → upstream summary/source.
- Flag missing reference versions, broken pointers, cycles, and findings that ultimately cite another summary rather than primary evidence.
- Compute reachability so every final decision can list all decisive leaf sources.

This is the traceability-chain analogue. It must be called **claim traceability**, not metrological traceability, because no calibration to an SI realization exists.

**Stage 3 — group dependencies before counting corroboration**

- Union certificates sharing a source hash, detector ID, prompt lineage, or upstream-summary ID.
- Report both raw finding count and independent-cluster count.
- Do not upgrade a confidence band merely because several members of one cluster agree.
- If dependency metadata is unknown, label independence `unknown`; do not assume independence.

The GUM’s covariance equations are not imported. The transferable rule is only that shared causes must be represented and cannot be treated as independent ([JCGM 100:2008, §5.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/GUM/JCGM100/C045315e-html/C045315e_FILES/MAIN_C045315e/05_e.html)).

**Stage 4 — retain conflict, do not average it away**

- For each atomic proposition, list supporting, opposing, qualifying, and unknown clusters.
- Mark conflict `reference`, `scope`, `observation`, `causal`, or `value`.
- Auto-resolve only mechanical conflicts covered by a declared precedence rule, such as newer evidence about a mutable current-state fact when both scopes and detectors are valid.
- Route unresolved high-severity conflicts to the exception queue.

Metrological compatibility gives a useful question—are results sufficiently compatible given their uncertainties?—and explicitly notes that correlation affects compatibility ([JCGM VIM 2.47](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.47.html)). The harness must not copy its numeric formula; it lacks standard uncertainties on a shared quantity.

**Stage 5 — apply a declared decision rule**

Each decision question supplies:

```yaml
outcomes: [accept, reject, defer, escalate]
hard_requirements: [...]
veto_conditions: [...]
minimum_evidence:
  positive_controls_for_absence: true
  independent_signal_families: 2
  decisive_claim_minimum_band: very likely
guard_band:
  if_conflict_on_hard_requirement: escalate
  if_dependency_unknown_for_decisive_claim: defer
  if_reference_missing: defer
risk_owner:
  false_accept: owner/team/system
  false_reject: owner/team/system
```

- Rules operate on enumerated states, not sums of confidence values.
- Hard requirements and vetoes dominate soft preferences.
- A boundary/guard-band case becomes `defer` or `escalate`, never an optimistic pass.
- The docket names both consequences: what happens if the system accepts a bad proposal, and what happens if it rejects a good one.

This borrows conformity assessment’s separation between result, tolerance, acceptance interval, and decision risk without pretending to calculate consumer/producer probabilities.

**Stage 6 — emit a bounded decision docket**

For each decision:

- outcome and exact rule clause that fired;
- decision question/scope/reference version;
- decisive independent evidence clusters;
- dominant uncertainty components;
- opposing evidence and unresolved conflicts;
- false-accept and false-reject consequence owners;
- smallest reversible next action;
- pointers to full certificates and prose.

The reconciliation ledger records each input ID as `decisive`, `supporting`, `duplicate/dependent`, `out-of-scope`, `superseded`, `conflicting`, `quarantined`, or `unresolved`, with a reason. Completeness means every input has exactly one disposition; it does not mean every finding appears in the short narrative.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: synthesis check standards and proficiency cases

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Maintain a small versioned fixture pack, executed before or alongside every large synthesis:

- a known-present item to validate absence detectors;
- two contradictory findings sharing one source, to verify dependency clustering;
- two genuinely independent agreeing findings, to verify corroboration is retained;
- a stale reference versus a current reference;
- a high-confidence but out-of-scope claim;
- a boundary case that must defer under the guard band;
- a seeded severe minority finding that must remain in the exception queue;
- one malformed or circular lineage chain.

Add a blinded proficiency set in which several synthesis agents receive the same compact certificate batch and their outputs are compared with an adjudicated reference docket. Keep the adjudication and scoring procedure versioned; do not use consensus alone as truth.

This proposal is **UNVERIFIED — experiment X101-E2 would run the fixtures through at least three model/runtime versions and measure seeded-failure recall, false alarms, and decision drift**. Fixed check standards are **very likely (80-90%)** to detect regression on the exact seeded failure classes, because each has an explicit oracle; generalization from those fixtures to unseeded real failures is only **unlikely (20-45%)** without periodic fixture renewal, because agents and prompts can overfit a small known pack. Blinding and holdout rotation are therefore part of the proposal.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: reference and rule calibration workshop

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Before a large research program, use the existing interactive agent/user surface to elicit a versioned decision contract: decision questions, hard requirements, tolerable trade-offs, risk ownership, and examples of accept/reject/defer. “NATIVE” here refers only to collecting and writing the contract through the existing interaction surface; it does not claim the current harness already provides metrology-style calibration semantics.

This proposal is **UNVERIFIED — experiment X101-E3 would have the owner adjudicate a stratified sample twice, then measure whether the contract reduces inter-run disagreement and whether the owner’s own decisions are stable**. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce normative ambiguity because it externalizes acceptance criteria before synthesis, but it is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a single workshop yields a stable reference because owner preferences can legitimately change with newly revealed consequences.

The version is therefore mandatory. A changed owner decision is not automatically “instrument drift”; it may be a changed reference or changed measurand. The system must distinguish those cases.

## 4. What this would catch that ordinary summarization misses

The following are detection capabilities of the proposed protocol, not claims about the present harness:

### 4.1 False consensus from correlated evidence

Five agents can independently phrase the same upstream claim while all relying on one source, one broken detector, or one generated summary. Dependency keys collapse these into one corroboration cluster. This detection is **very likely (80-90%)** when exact source hashes or lineage IDs are present, because equality and graph reachability are mechanical; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** for undeclared semantic copying, which requires a separate similarity or provenance experiment and must remain `dependency unknown`.

### 4.2 Wrong-question synthesis

A historical rate, a present configuration observation, and a future design forecast may share vocabulary but answer different decision questions. Scope partitioning prevents an automatic merge. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** for explicitly distinct scope fields and only **roughly even (45-55%)** when the unit mislabels scope; X101-E1’s adjudicated sample would measure the latter.

### 4.3 Detector blindness masquerading as absence

The positive-control field plus two independent signal families blocks a single failed query from becoming “absent.” Structural enforcement is **almost certain (90-99%)** for certificates that honestly declare an absence conclusion, because the validator can reject a missing control or signal; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** to catch a conclusion euphemistically phrased to evade the enum, so prose-to-certificate consistency must be sampled in X101-E1.

### 4.4 Confidence laundering through layers of summaries

Claim lineage exposes when a final recommendation points only to an intermediate summary or when a transformation silently upgrades a band. The compiler can forbid band upgrades without a new evidence certificate. Mechanical band-transition detection is **almost certain (90-99%)** when all transformations are certificates; end-to-end capture is **likely (55-80%)** until every synthesis path emits them, a premise UNVERIFIED — X101-E1 would compare the ledger’s input IDs with the source manifest.

### 4.5 Buried minority evidence and unresolved contradiction

The exception queue retains severe opposing clusters even when most findings support a proposal. This is **very likely (80-90%)** for explicitly encoded polarity and severity because the rule is deterministic. Whether it prioritizes the *right* minority finding is only **roughly even (45-55%)** until severity and reference semantics are calibrated against owner adjudications in X101-E3.

### 4.6 Stale or changed reference standards

Every finding and decision names the reference version and time. Mixed versions become a conflict rather than a silent merge. Detection is **almost certain (90-99%)** when version fields are populated because mismatches are mechanically comparable; semantic changes without version changes remain **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be detected by this mechanism alone and need governance or content hashing.

### 4.7 Decision instability near a boundary

A guard band converts borderline evidence, missing references, decisive dependency unknowns, or hard-requirement conflicts into defer/escalate. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent an automatic pass for enumerated boundary states because the reducer is deterministic. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** to choose an owner-optimal guard band initially; X101-E3 must calibrate false-accept versus false-reject consequences.

### 4.8 Synthesis-process drift

Check-standard fixtures reveal that a model, prompt, parser, or rule change now misses a seeded severe finding, counts duplicates, or mishandles polarity. Exact-fixture regression detection is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by the same control-standard logic NIST uses to monitor persistent process shifts ([NIST §2.2.2](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section2/mpc22.htm)); extrapolation to all real findings remains **unlikely (20-45%)** for the reasons already stated.

## 5. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 5.1 Structural assumptions that do transfer

1. **There is a transformation chain.** Primary evidence becomes a unit claim, then an aggregate claim, then a decision. Errors and qualifications can be introduced at every link. This matches the traceability-chain topology.
2. **Some inputs share upstream causes.** Agents can share sources, prompts, models, and detectors just as measurements can share instruments or standards. The GUM’s warning that shared references induce correlation is directly relevant at the structural level ([JCGM 100:2008, §5.2.4](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/GUM/JCGM100/C045315e-html/C045315e_FILES/MAIN_C045315e/05_e.html)).
3. **A compact result must remain expandable.** Both domains need a decision-maker to inspect dominant contributors and selectively drill down rather than replay the entire process.
4. **Decision policy is not evidence.** Conformity assessment’s separation of measurement from acceptance policy maps cleanly to audit evidence versus owner trade-offs.
5. **The process can be tested with controls.** Known-reference cases can reveal parser, detector, and synthesis regressions, provided their scope is not overgeneralized.

These structural correspondences make the protocol-level transfer **very likely (80-90%)** to be legitimate, supported by the cited definitions and operational guidance. That confidence does not extend to numeric uncertainty propagation.

### 5.2 Structural assumptions that do not transfer

1. **No common physical quantity.** Audit findings include facts, causal hypotheses, normative preferences, forecasts, and design options. They cannot be expressed in one unit or combined as repeated measurements of one measurand.
2. **No SI-like invariant reference.** Owner intent, software state, threats, and available models change. A “reference standard” here is a governed requirement or adjudicated example, not a realization of an invariant unit.
3. **No justified probability model.** The mandated confidence bands are epistemic communication labels, not demonstrated standard deviations. There are no established sensitivity coefficients, distributions, degrees of freedom, or covariance estimates for most findings.
4. **Errors are strategic and semantic.** An LLM may rationalize, copy, omit, or misunderstand. These failure modes are neither independent nor necessarily stationary.
5. **The act of synthesis can change the target.** A newly surfaced consequence can legitimately change owner preference; this is not necessarily measurement error.
6. **Severity is not a quantity value.** “Critical/high/medium/low” is ordinal and context-dependent. Arithmetic over severity labels would manufacture meaning.

Therefore:

- Computing a weighted mean of finding confidence is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** defensible, because the prerequisites for commensurability and calibrated uncertainties are absent.
- Translating confidence bands to mid-point probabilities and applying Bayes is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** defensible without empirical calibration and independence modeling.
- Calling the claim DAG “metrological traceability” is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be semantically safe; use **claim traceability** so users do not infer SI-level assurance.
- Treating a multi-agent majority as a key-comparison reference value is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be reliable, because shared model/source biases violate independence and consensus is not truth. An independently adjudicated oracle is required for proficiency cases.

### 5.3 Net verdict: partial transfer, with an explicit stop line

The analogy transfers through the point of **typed result certificates, explicit references, lineage graphs, dependency budgets, control cases, and predeclared decision rules**. It stops before **numerical uncertainty propagation, confidence averaging, consensus truth, or probabilistic conformance claims**.

If the program cannot supply atomic decision questions and versioned acceptance rules, it should still use certificates for traceability and conflict retention, but it must stop at an evidence docket and ask the owner to decide. It must not synthesize a normative “best harness” by inventing a scalar objective. That abstention is the analogue of refusing to report a measurement when the measurand is unspecified.

## 6. Anti-overfit and architecture gate

The proposed design rides existing file/schema and hook/skill/MCP interfaces. It does not introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph service, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The “graph” above is an in-memory DAG constructed during one deterministic compilation, serialized as ordinary JSON; it is not a graph database or memory subsystem. The check-standard pack is a small fixture directory, not a learned critic. The reducer should use standard JSON parsing, validation, union-find or maps for dependency clusters, and template rendering.

The complete reducer being under roughly 200 lines is **likely (55-80%)** if schema validation is delegated to an existing JSON Schema library and reports are simple Markdown/JSON; this estimate is UNVERIFIED — X101-E4 would implement a spike and count nonblank, noncomment lines plus dependencies. If it grows beyond that, the correct response is to reduce rule expressiveness or split generated views, not to build a daemon.

No BUILD-LARGE component is justified. The marginal value comes from disciplined result representation and deterministic reconciliation, not from a new runtime.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 7.1 One giant LLM summarizer

Rejected as the primary mechanism. It can produce readable prose, but it does not mechanically prove total input disposition, preserve dependency clusters, or expose the exact decision rule. It can remain a presentation layer over the deterministic docket. Using it as the authoritative reducer is **unlikely (20-45%)** to meet the traceability goal without certificates, because the goal requires inspectable coverage and lineage rather than linguistic fluency; X101-E1 could measure this directly.

### 7.2 Numeric “evidence scores” or weighted voting

Rejected. The inputs are heterogeneous and confidence labels are not calibrated uncertainties. Weighting can give correlated duplicates more influence and hide value judgments inside coefficients. This is the failed part of the metrology analogy.

### 7.3 Full Bayesian evidence network

Rejected under both the analogy and anti-overfit gate. It would require justified priors, likelihoods, dependency structure, and empirical calibration that the target does not presently guarantee. It also risks becoming a second complex inference runtime. A categorical uncertainty ledger and explicit defer states preserve honesty with much less machinery.

### 7.4 Vector database or knowledge graph memory

Rejected by the stated anti-overfit gate and because the essential graph is small and run-local. Exact IDs, hashes, and pointers solve the provenance problem more directly. Semantic retrieval may help discover related prose but cannot establish lineage or independence.

### 7.5 Majority vote among agents

Rejected as a truth mechanism. It remains useful as a **disagreement detector** on blinded reference cases. Shared prompts, models, and sources can make votes correlated; metrology’s own treatment of shared instruments/reference data warns against assuming independence ([JCGM 100:2008, §5.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/GUM/JCGM100/C045315e-html/C045315e_FILES/MAIN_C045315e/05_e.html)).

### 7.6 Require the final agent to read all prose

Rejected as the normal path, not forbidden as a fallback. It does not scale and creates another opaque synthesis step. The docket should route only decisive evidence, unresolved conflicts, dominant uncertainty, and a stratified audit sample to deep reading. Full prose stays available when a pointer is challenged.

## 8. Settling experiments

### X101-E1 — blinded retrospective synthesis trial

**Question:** Does the certificate/compiler protocol improve decisions and reduce reading without hiding important findings?

**Design:**

1. Select a completed large audit with an owner-adjudicated final decision and a manifest of all source findings.
2. Stratify findings by severity, polarity, evidence type, source lineage, and whether they affected the adjudicated decision.
3. Have one team convert findings to certificates; separately sample and adjudicate certificate semantic fidelity while blinded to compiler output.
4. Compare three synthesis arms: unconstrained LLM summary, LLM using certificate JSON, and deterministic compiler plus LLM presentation.
5. Blind adjudicators to arm identity.
6. Measure:
   - correct final decision against owner adjudication;
   - recall of decision-critical minority findings;
   - unsupported conclusion count;
   - conflict retention;
   - false independence/corroboration count;
   - percentage of input IDs with an auditable disposition;
   - owner/agent reading tokens and wall-clock review time;
   - number of drill-downs needed to validate the decision.
7. Run an ablation without dependency keys and another without decision rules to determine which mechanism supplies value.

**Success gate:** predeclare a non-inferiority margin for decision correctness, require zero loss of critical-finding recall, require complete ID disposition, and require a material reading reduction chosen by the owner. The exact margin is a value decision and is UNVERIFIED — the owner must set it before seeing results.

### X101-E2 — check-standard drift trial

Construct the eight seeded cases in Proposal C, hold back variants, and execute across at least three model/runtime or prompt versions. Score exact expected dispositions and false alarms. Then introduce unseen paraphrases and dependency structures. This separates fixture memorization from mechanism generalization.

**Success gate:** 100% detection on known severe canaries, no automatic pass on boundary fixtures, and predeclared acceptable false alarms on holdouts. The false-alarm threshold is UNVERIFIED — owner cost preference must set it.

### X101-E3 — reference/decision-rule calibration

Ask the owner to adjudicate a stratified set of decision vignettes, elicit the rationale and false-accept/false-reject consequences, then repeat after a washout period with ordering changed. Encode the stable parts as decision rules; preserve unstable cases as explicit escalation classes.

**Success gate:** the encoded rules reproduce stable owner judgments on a holdout and identify unstable cases rather than forcing agreement. This experiment distinguishes measurement disagreement from changing owner values.

### X101-E4 — minimal implementation spike

Implement JSON validation, dependency clustering, reconciliation, four-state decision rules, exception routing, and Markdown/JSON emission using an existing runtime. Count code, runtime, dependencies, and failure modes on 10,000 synthetic certificates. Kill the design if it requires a daemon, external state service, or opaque inference to perform core reconciliation.

## 10. Gaps and non-claims

1. **Current-harness presence/absence:** not tested. This unit makes no claim that finding schemas, dependency tracking, control fixtures, or decision rules are absent or broken.
2. **Bus integration details:** not tested because local grounding was out of scope. The BUILD-SMALL tags specify the required architecture boundary, not confirmed filenames or hook events.
3. **Owner decision stability:** unknown until X101-E3. A mutable reference is expected in design work and must be versioned rather than disguised as calibration noise.
4. **Confidence calibration:** no dataset was found or evaluated here that maps the mandated verbal bands to empirical correctness for this owner’s agents. The design deliberately refuses numeric propagation.
5. **Certificate authoring cost:** unknown until X101-E1. Structured emission could reduce later synthesis work but increase unit effort; both must be measured.
6. **Semantic provenance:** exact hashes and IDs catch declared or byte-identical ancestry, not all paraphrased copying. A similarity detector might improve recall, but proposing one now would be premature; mark dependency unknown and test first.
7. **Reference-oracle quality:** proficiency cases are only as sound as their adjudication. Consensus without an independent oracle must not be labeled calibration.

## Conclusion

Metrology did not solve “read less” by trusting a shorter narrative. It solved a more precise problem: make a result compact **and still traceable to a declared reference, explicit about uncertainty and dependence, tested against controls, and separable from the rule that turns evidence into action**.

That architecture maps well to large-scale harness synthesis as a Finding Certificate → deterministic Decision Certificate pipeline. The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** sound at the structural level for the reasons and authoritative mechanisms cited above, but direct numerical uncertainty propagation is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** sound without new empirical calibration and commensurate measurands. The honest build is consequently small: schemas, an in-process deterministic reducer, check-standard fixtures, and owner-authored decision rules. The system should produce a short exception-focused docket while retaining a complete reconciliation ledger and drill-down links to every source artifact.

The overall recommendation remains **UNVERIFIED — adopt only after X101-E1 demonstrates non-inferior decision correctness, zero loss of critical-findings recall, complete reconciliation, and materially lower reading cost; use X101-E2 through E4 to test drift, owner-rule stability, and implementation size**.
