# X089 — Mechanism design → synthesis

## Executive result

The borrow transfers only in a deliberately weakened form. The useful mechanism is **precommitted random spot-checking plus a strictly proper scoring rule**. It does not make a population of same-owner LLM calls economically incentive-compatible. It does turn a small, independently verified sample of atomic claims into (1) a calibration record, (2) an evidence-based ranking of claim sources and claim classes, and (3) a compact decision queue. The literal “prediction market among agents” analogy fails because model instances have neither persistent utility nor genuinely independent private information.

My recommendation is therefore **not** a market and **not** peer voting. It is an audit lottery over structured claim forecasts, scored with the Brier rule after independent resolution. The resulting synthesis packet presents actions, decisive claims, calibrated probabilities, impact, dependencies, and a small residual-uncertainty queue. It does not ask a final agent to read every finding.

This transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to improve the precision of the short list presented to the final decision-maker, because strict propriety supplies the correct loss for probabilistic claims [Gneiting and Raftery, pp. 359-360](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf), random ground-truth checks avoid the peer-agreement failure characterized by [Gao, Wright, and Leyton-Brown](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042), and LLM confidence can be recalibrated on labeled data [Detommaso et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/detommaso24a.html). The exact gain here is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` below would settle it**.

It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that this mechanism alone can establish that the chosen architecture is globally best, because it ranks submitted, resolvable claims rather than discovering omitted alternatives; that limit follows from the mechanism’s input set, not from a tested harness result. Experiment `X089-OMISSION-CHALLENGE` below would measure the residual omission rate.

## 1. The target problem as a mechanism-design problem

The synthesis bottleneck has four separable operations:

1. elicit what each research unit believes;
2. distinguish evidence from confidence theater;
3. aggregate overlapping and conflicting reports;
4. allocate a scarce resource—the final decision-maker’s attention—to claims and actions.

That resembles information elicitation under costly verification more closely than it resembles ordinary summarization. A research unit privately observes a noisy signal after spending effort. Full verification is expensive. The center must choose an outcome from reports. That is the same abstract shape used in mechanism design: participants send messages and a prespecified rule maps messages to outcomes; the Nobel committee’s account of Hurwicz’s framework describes exactly those elements ([Nobel Prize, “Mechanism Design Theory,” 2007](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2007/popular-information/)).

The analogy immediately breaks in one important place. An LLM invocation is not an economic person with durable wealth, stable preferences, or a reason to maximize later token allocations. A prompt that says “you will lose points” does not create utility. Therefore it would be false to call the proposed harness dominant-strategy incentive-compatible. The only real optimizing entities are (a) any human who changes behavior in response to the score, (b) an automated selector that routes future work toward historically better methods, and (c) a training process if scores ever become training rewards. This unit proposes only (b), a selection rule.

The transferable object is thus the **measurement and allocation rule**, not the game-theoretic equilibrium claim. This distinction is central: strict propriety tells us what score rewards honest probabilities *for a risk-neutral expected-score maximizer*; it does not prove that a prompted model reports its latent belief.

## 2. The specific mechanism in its own field

### 2.1 Strictly proper scoring rules

A probabilistic scoring rule assigns a numerical value after a forecasted event resolves. Let a forecaster report probability `p` for a binary event and let `y` be 1 if it occurs and 0 otherwise. A Brier loss is:

```text
L(p, y) = (p - y)^2
```

Lower is better. If the forecaster’s true subjective probability is `q`, expected loss is uniquely minimized by reporting `p=q`. More generally, Gneiting and Raftery define a rule as proper when reporting distribution `Q` maximizes expected score under `Q`, and strictly proper when that maximizer is unique ([definition and inequality (1), pp. 359-360](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)). Their review gives the multicategory quadratic/Brier score and its squared-Euclidean divergence ([Example 1, p. 363](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)).

The important mechanism-design property is not “correct guesses earn points.” A zero-one accuracy score makes all reports on the same side of 0.5 equivalent. A strictly proper rule makes exaggerating 0.60 into 0.99 costly when the event fails, while still rewarding warranted certainty. It elicits a probability distribution rather than a winner label.

Strict propriety has assumptions:

- the event eventually receives a trustworthy outcome;
- the forecaster acts to maximize expected score;
- utility is compatible with the score (the elementary result usually assumes risk neutrality or an appropriate payment construction);
- the resolution criterion was not changed after seeing the forecast;
- the forecast does not itself change the event in an unmodeled way.

The proposed transfer keeps the first and fourth assumptions by design, approximates the second through downstream selection, and does not claim the literal utility result for models.

### 2.2 From one forecast to group aggregation: market scoring rules

Hanson’s market scoring rule makes a proper scoring rule sequentially shared. A participant changes the public probability from an old report to a new report and is paid the difference between the new and old scores after resolution. Hanson describes the result as allowing anyone to change the official report and be paid according to that revision; the mechanism combines individual probability elicitation with consensus estimates ([Hanson, “Logarithmic Market Scoring Rules,” pp. 7-8](https://hanson.gmu.edu/mktscore.pdf)).

That is a genuine aggregation mechanism when participants possess different information and respond to payoffs. It is attractive as an analogy because later informed reports can correct earlier ones without the center rereading all underlying arguments.

It is not the recommended implementation here. Sequential LLM “trading” is **very likely (80-90%)** to add path dependence and duplicated token expense, because every trade is another ordered inference call, while its fake currency supplies no demonstrated model utility; experiment `X089-MARKET-ABLATION` would settle whether any aggregation gain offsets that cost. More seriously, it is **likely (55-80%)** that same-model agents share enough training-induced errors to make apparent consensus overstate independent support; the evidence is shared model, prompt family, and source material, while experiment `X089-CORRELATION-MATRIX` would measure residual error correlation. A market price would then encode correlated repetition as if it were aggregation.

### 2.3 Why random spot-checking is the decisive addition

When most judgments lack direct ground truth, peer-prediction mechanisms pay an evaluator based on agreement patterns with other evaluators. Prelec’s Bayesian Truth Serum is a sophisticated example: it rewards answers that are more common than the population collectively predicted, rather than simply rewarding the modal answer ([Prelec, *Science* 2004 abstract and DOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15486294/)). This can elicit subjective information under its Bayesian population assumptions.

Those assumptions are a poor match here. Same-family model instances are **almost certainly not (1-5%)** a large population of conditionally independent human signals with a meaningful common prior, because they are neither humans nor independently sampled persistent respondents; this is a category mismatch, not an empirical estimate of error correlation. It is **likely (55-80%)** that they can converge on a familiar but false explanation when model, prompt, or source ancestry is shared; experiment `X089-CORRELATION-MATRIX` would settle the rate. Findings also concern externally checkable technical facts, so treating truth as unknowable would discard available evidence.

Gao, Wright, and Leyton-Brown study the closer case: costly evaluation with only limited access to trusted reports. Their negative result matters more than the optimistic peer-prediction analogy. Under their stated conditions, adding peer prediction does not reduce the spot-check probability needed to make truthful effort attractive; a peer-insensitive mechanism that pays only against spot-checked trusted reports can require no more ground truth ([Theorem 3 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)). Their paper also shows how a low-quality equilibrium can exist and dominate truthful effort when unchecked peer agreement is rewarded.

The mechanism to borrow is therefore:

1. commit to a random audit policy before reports are known;
2. require probabilistic, resolvable reports;
3. obtain trusted outcomes for the sampled reports using a verifier independent of the reporter;
4. apply a strictly proper score only where trusted resolution exists;
5. use the resulting out-of-sample calibration to govern attention allocation;
6. do not award epistemic credit merely because peers agree.

This is “mechanism design” in the modest but concrete sense that report format, audit rule, scoring rule, and downstream allocation are fixed before messages arrive.

## 3. Does the structural analogy transfer?

### 3.1 Assumption-by-assumption judgment

| Source-field assumption | Synthesis analogue | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Reports contain dispersed private information | Units inspect different sources or use different lenses | Partial. Different source material can be dispersed; shared model/prompt ancestry makes consequential residual correlation **likely (55-80%)**; experiment `X089-CORRELATION-MATRIX` would settle it. |
| Verification is possible but costly | Re-running commands, opening cited sources, and constructing controls costs time/tokens | Strong for factual technical claims; weak for taste and long-horizon architecture effects. |
| Participant maximizes expected payment | Model follows a prompt | Fails literally. Only automated selection based on scores supplies a real consequence. |
| Outcome is exogenous and well specified | “File exists at revision R” or “test T passes” | Strong for frozen-state facts; weak for reflexive recommendations whose adoption changes outcomes. |
| Reports are probabilistic | Unit supplies calibrated probability/confidence band | Implementable, but raw self-reports require calibration. |
| Agents are independent or their dependence is modeled | Many units may share model, prompt, and sources | Independence is **unlikely (20-45%)** when ancestry is shared; experiment `X089-CORRELATION-MATRIX` would settle it. Track provenance families and cap duplicates. |
| Designer can commit to rules | Harness fixes schema, random seed commitment, resolver, and scoring version | Commitment is **very likely (80-90%)** to be technically feasible because immutable policy/version/seed hashes are ordinary file operations; a one-run prototype would settle it. |
| Enough outcomes resolve to learn quality | A stratified sample can be independently checked | **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X089-RESOLUTION-RATE` below would settle whether enough claims are resolvable.** |

### 3.2 Verdict

The scoring-rule component transfers mathematically to **resolved atomic propositions**. The market-equilibrium and peer-truth components do not transfer. Random spot-checking transfers as an audit-sampling design, while its incentive theorem does not transfer to stateless model calls.

This partial transfer is still potentially useful. The center does not need to make models “want truth.” It needs a loss function that distinguishes calibrated from theatrical confidence and an allocation policy that spends verification and reading effort where estimated decision regret is largest.

The expected benefit is **likely (55-80%)**, conditional on at least several dozen heterogeneous claims resolving per provenance/domain stratum; the evidence is the strict-propriety result, Gao et al.’s ground-truth comparison, and empirical LLM multicalibration results cited above. That condition is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X089-RESOLUTION-RATE` would settle it**.

If the resolution-rate experiment finds fewer than 30 determinate audited claims in the major strata, the calibrated-source weighting should not ship. That threshold is a proposed engineering guardrail, not a theorem. Whether 30 is adequate is **roughly even (45-55%)**; experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` with bootstrap confidence intervals would settle it.

## 4. Concrete harness translation

### 4.1 Proposal — CONFIG (settings/JSON only): atomic claim contract

Every finding-producing unit emits, alongside prose, a small machine-readable record per decision-relevant claim:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "stable-id",
  "claim": "One falsifiable proposition",
  "claim_type": "current_fact | causal_prediction | design_judgment",
  "probability": 0.78,
  "confidence_band": "likely (55-80%)",
  "decision_if_true": ["action-id"],
  "decision_if_false": ["action-id"],
  "impact": 4,
  "evidence": [{"locator": "URL or immutable path/revision", "signal_kind": "..."}],
  "resolution": {"procedure": "...", "deadline": "..."},
  "provenance_family": "model+prompt+source-family",
  "dependencies": ["claim-id"]
}
```

Probability and impact are deliberately separate. A near-certain cosmetic fact must not outrank a 65% chance of catastrophic data loss. “Decision if true/false” forces a value-of-information test: if neither outcome changes any action, the claim normally does not deserve final-context budget.

The claim schema is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce synthesis ambiguity because it makes proposition, confidence, evidence, resolution, and consequence separately addressable; this is an inference from the definition of a proper forecast and from the target bottleneck, not a harness measurement. It is **UNVERIFIED; the schema arm of experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` would settle it**.

Do not reject a whole unit because one record is malformed. Store malformed records in a quarantine count and retain the prose artifact. This avoids turning schema conformance into the new target.

### 4.2 Proposal — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus): committed audit lottery and resolver

A small command performs these steps:

1. Before units finish, record a hash commitment to the sampling seed and audit policy version.
2. After claim records arrive, reveal the seed.
3. Sample claims with a mixture of:
   - uniform random coverage;
   - stratification by provenance family, domain, and confidence band;
   - risk-weighted oversampling by stated impact;
   - disagreement oversampling where materially different claims imply different actions.
4. Send each sampled claim to an independent resolver. “Independent” means a different evidence channel, not a second prompt over the same text. Each resolver first proves its detector on a known-present control, then applies the test.
5. Label the outcome `true`, `false`, or `indeterminate`, with immutable evidence. Never silently map `indeterminate` to false.
6. Compute Brier loss on determinate binary claims. Report resolution coverage and indeterminate rate separately.

The random component mechanically withholds the selected claim identities until seed reveal. Stratification mechanically reserves sample slots by domain, and risk weighting mechanically allocates more verification probability to higher stated impact. Whether those controls alter report quality is **UNVERIFIED and likely (55-80%)** under the selection-pressure analogy; experiment `X089-GAMING-REDTEAM` would settle it. These are design rationales, not equilibrium guarantees.

The audit lottery is **likely (55-80%)** to expose high-confidence false claims that a consensus summarizer preserves, because a false 0.95 claim incurs much greater Brier loss than a false 0.55 claim when sampled. The harness-specific rate is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` would settle it**.

The seed commitment matters only if report producers or their operators could infer or influence the final sample. If all generation is complete before sampling and logs are immutable, ordinary post-hoc random sampling is sufficient.

### 4.3 Proposal — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus): calibration ledger and diversity caps

Maintain an append-only, run-local ledger keyed by `(provenance_family, claim_type, domain, confidence_band)`. For each adequately populated cell, record:

- count resolved / sampled / indeterminate;
- mean Brier loss;
- observed truth frequency by submitted band;
- false-positive rate for high-confidence claims;
- mean verification cost;
- calibration interval, not just a point estimate.

Use the ledger to recalibrate reports, not to blacklist a named agent forever. Material cross-domain accuracy variation is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the task-conditional multicalibration evidence in Detommaso et al.; experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` would measure it here. Detommaso et al. motivate calibration across intersecting groups rather than marginal calibration alone and report improved calibration and accuracy on LLM QA benchmarks ([ICML 2024 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/detommaso24a.html)).

Cap duplicate influence by provenance family. Ten reports from the same model/prompt/source family count as corroboration metadata, not ten independent votes. Require at least two genuinely different signal kinds before labeling a disputed claim corroborated.

This ledger is **likely (55-80%)** to catch provenance-specific overconfidence after enough resolved samples, based on multicalibration’s empirical LLM results and the Brier score’s properties. It is **UNVERIFIED for research-agent findings; experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` would settle it**.

Do not use a vector database or knowledge graph. A flat JSONL/SQLite ledger, deterministic claim IDs, explicit dependencies, and a generated Markdown packet are enough. This remains on the existing hook/skill bus and passes the anti-overfit gate.

### 4.4 Proposal — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus): decision packet by expected regret, not finding count

The synthesizer groups exact duplicate claim IDs and explicit dependencies, applies calibration only where the ledger has adequate support, and produces a bounded packet:

1. **Decision table:** candidate action, estimated upside, downside, reversibility, calibrated support, and decisive claim IDs.
2. **Do-now queue:** high-impact reversible actions supported by resolved or well-calibrated claims.
3. **Verify-next queue:** unresolved claims with the largest estimated decision flip value.
4. **Conflict table:** mutually inconsistent claims, provenance families, and the cheapest discriminating experiment.
5. **Unknown/omission register:** domains without coverage and audit strata with high indeterminate rates.
6. **Evidence appendix:** only the atomic records and evidence for claims that can change a selected action.

An illustrative priority score is:

```text
priority_to_verify = P(decision flips after resolution)
                     * consequence_if_wrong
                     / estimated_verification_cost
```

The numeric inputs need not pretend to cardinal precision. Coarse ordinal consequence and cost tiers are acceptable if the packet shows them. The priority expression is a triage heuristic, not a proper scoring rule and not a proof of expected utility.

This packet is **likely (55-80%)** to let a final agent act without reading the full corpus while preserving traceability for decisive claims; evidence is the explicit claim-to-action mapping and bounded evidence appendix, but the result is **UNVERIFIED; experiment `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` would settle it**.

## 5. What the mechanism would catch that ordinary synthesis misses

The catch-rate claims in this section are collectively **likely (55-80%)** for the listed adversarial examples because each example targets a field represented explicitly in the proposed record or audit, but they are **UNVERIFIED; the adversarial slice of `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` and `X089-GAMING-REDTEAM` would settle each rate**. Arithmetic and schema-routing descriptions are deterministic consequences of the specified rules, not empirical forecasts.

### 5.1 Confidence inflation

An ordinary summary can preserve “definitely broken” and “possibly broken” as equally prominent bullets. A proper score makes those reports distinguishable after audit. A 0.99 false forecast has Brier loss `0.9801`; a 0.60 false forecast has loss `0.36`. Over repeated resolutions, unjustified certainty becomes visible.

### 5.2 Correlated consensus

Twenty units may repeat one attractive explanation because they share a model prior or upstream source. Peer voting strengthens that error. Provenance-family caps plus independent spot checks mark it as one correlated signal until a different detector resolves it. Gao et al.’s low-quality-equilibrium result is the direct warning against paying for agreement ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)).

### 5.3 Citation laundering

A claim can cite a real source that does not entail the proposition. The audit resolver follows the prespecified resolution procedure and records entailment/outcome, rather than counting URLs. High citation count produces no score by itself.

### 5.4 Unresolvable rhetoric

“This design is robust” cannot receive a legitimate Brier score without a horizon, workload, and failure criterion. The contract forces it to become a testable claim or remain `design_judgment`. A high indeterminate rate is itself surfaced in the decision packet rather than hidden as uncertainty.

### 5.5 Easy-claim farming

A producer can inflate accuracy with many trivial claims. The synthesizer does not rank by number correct. It separates impact, uses provenance/domain strata, and allocates final context by decision relevance. Audit statistics must publish both score and the distribution of claim impact/type.

### 5.6 Unsupported minority signals

Simple majority aggregation suppresses a rare but correct observation. Random audits give minority claims a nonzero path to resolution; disagreement and impact strata give consequential minority claims additional audit probability. This is one reason not to use consensus as truth.

### 5.7 Hidden detector failure

A resolution procedure that returns “absent” is not trusted until the same detector finds a known-present control, and absence requires a second mechanically independent signal. The mechanism record makes detector controls part of the outcome evidence, so a final summary cannot silently launder a failed probe into a fact.

### 5.8 Decision-irrelevant volume

Thousands of true observations may have no bearing on the architecture choice. Requiring `decision_if_true` and `decision_if_false` exposes claims whose resolution changes nothing. They remain searchable but leave the bounded decision packet.

## 6. Goodhart analysis and countermeasures

Goodhart effects are not one failure. Manheim and Garrabrant distinguish regressional, extremal, causal, and adversarial variants of proxy failure ([“Categorizing Variants of Goodhart’s Law”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)). Each has a direct analogue here.

### Regressional Goodhart

Selecting only the historically best Brier cell also selects positive noise, especially with small samples. Countermeasure: minimum cell sizes, uncertainty intervals, shrinkage toward the global baseline, and no permanent winner designation.

### Extremal Goodhart

A score calibrated on routine file-existence claims is **likely (55-80%)** to transfer poorly to rare architectural predictions because the outcome distribution and resolution process differ; `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B` would settle the size of this domain shift. Countermeasure: never extrapolate a cell’s weight across claim type/domain; show “no calibration data” rather than a synthetic number.

### Causal Goodhart

Once source weights govern future context, reporting adaptation is **likely (55-80%)** if human operators inspect the scores and **unlikely (20-45%)** for stateless model calls absent prompt or selector changes; `X089-GAMING-REDTEAM` would settle observed easy-claim and claim-splitting rates. Countermeasure: jointly report calibration, coverage, impact mix, indeterminate rate, and omitted-domain probes; do not optimize a single leaderboard.

### Adversarial Goodhart

A producer aware of the metric is **likely (55-80%)** to find at least one profitable proxy exploit under an explicit red-team reward, by the adversarial-Goodhart mechanism categorized by [Manheim and Garrabrant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585); `X089-GAMING-REDTEAM` would settle which exploit works. Candidate attacks include easy-resolution phrasing, probability bunching, and provenance spoofing. Countermeasure: hidden-until-commit sampling seed, immutable provenance assigned by the runner, audits of claim atomicity, and periodic holdout “sentinel” claims whose labels are not available to the reporting stage.

The system must never promote “mean Brier score” to the owner’s objective. The objective is better decisions under a reading and verification budget. Brier loss is one diagnostic of forecast quality.

## 7. Explicitly rejected borrows

### 7.1 Prediction market among LLM agents — rejected

This fails the incentive assumption: tokens are not money to a stateless model, and repeated same-model agents do not guarantee independent private information. Building an exchange would also approach a second orchestration runtime. It fails the anti-overfit gate.

### 7.2 Bayesian Truth Serum / peer prediction as the truth engine — rejected

BTS is elegant for subjective data without objective truth: answers receive information scores based on being more common than collectively predicted ([Prelec 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15486294/)). Here, many important propositions are externally verifiable, while the common-prior/population assumptions are dubious. The peer-prediction negative result makes a direct-ground-truth audit preferable when trusted checks are available ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07042)).

### 7.3 Quadratic voting over recommendations — rejected

Quadratic voting is designed to express intensity under a constrained credit budget. Model instances have no authentic preferences or opportunity cost for credits. It would aggregate prompted rhetoric, not owner value.

### 7.4 One scalar reputation per agent — rejected

Quality is conditional on domain, claim type, source access, and resolution difficulty. A global scalar invites regressional and extremal Goodhart and conceals where evidence is absent.

### 7.5 Learned critic, vector DB/KG memory, durable workflow engine, or observability SaaS — rejected

None is needed for the mechanism. Structured records, a seeded sampler, direct resolvers, a flat ledger, and Markdown generation ride the existing bus. Adding those components would violate the anti-overfit gate without evidence of incremental value.

## 8. Experiments that settle the unverified premises

### `X089-RESOLUTION-RATE`

Purpose: determine whether enough claims can receive trustworthy outcomes for scoring.

1. Draw 200 claims, stratified by claim type, domain, provenance family, and stated confidence.
2. Freeze each claim and its resolution criterion before verification.
3. Give independent resolvers a fixed budget and require known-present detector controls.
4. Record `true/false/indeterminate`, time, tool calls, and inter-resolver disagreement.
5. Success gate: at least 70% determinate overall, at least 30 determinate claims in every stratum whose calibration will affect decisions, and less than 10% unresolved resolver disagreement after adjudication.

Those thresholds are engineering proposals. Their adequacy is **roughly even (45-55%)** because no harness-specific cost/error distribution was examined in this ideation unit; the follow-on A/B and bootstrap sensitivity analysis would settle whether decisions are stable across threshold choices.

### `X089-AUDIT-LOTTERY-A/B`

Purpose: test whether the mechanism improves a bounded synthesis decision.

Use several completed research batches with frozen artifacts and later independently established outcomes.

- Control: a strong synthesis agent receives the existing summaries under a fixed context/token budget.
- Treatment: the same model receives the generated decision packet under the same budget. The claim extraction stage runs before assignment and is shared where possible so the test isolates ranking/auditing rather than extra compute.
- Blind evaluators: experts unaware of condition assess top-20 claim precision, recall of known critical defects, unsupported-claim rate, action regret on reversible replay tasks, calibration/Brier loss, and time/tokens to decision.
- Adversarial slice: inject correlated duplicate claims, real-but-nonentailing citations, one rare critical minority claim, and an unresolvable high-confidence recommendation.
- Precommit primary outcome: critical-claim recall subject to no increase in unsupported high-impact recommendations.

Ship the calibrated weighting only if treatment improves the primary outcome across batches and the interval excludes a practically negligible effect chosen before the run. Do not ship merely because Brier score improves.

### `X089-OMISSION-CHALLENGE`

Purpose: measure what a submitted-claim mechanism cannot see.

1. Give a red-team unit only the problem and primary evidence, not the submitted findings.
2. Ask it to produce architecture-changing claims absent from the decision packet.
3. Independently verify those claims.
4. Report verified high-impact omissions per batch and add an “uncovered domain” probe to the packet if omission clusters recur.

The mechanism is **unlikely (20-45%)** to materially reduce omissions by itself because it operates after claims are generated; this is a structural inference. `X089-OMISSION-CHALLENGE` would quantify the rate.

### `X089-GAMING-REDTEAM`

Purpose: test Goodhart resistance.

Give red-team producers the public schema and scoring rule, but not the committed sample seed. Reward them for achieving a good score while misleading the final decision. Test easy-claim flooding, claim splitting, confidence bunching, provenance spoofing, citation laundering, and correlated consensus. The design passes only if the decision packet exposes the attack through coverage/impact/provenance diagnostics or spot checks.

## 9. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

The minimum viable architecture is:

```text
unit prose + claim JSONL
          |
          v
seeded stratified sampler --> independent resolver evidence
          |                            |
          +-------------> score/coverage ledger
                                        |
                                        v
                   bounded decision packet + evidence appendix
```

It requires no second orchestrator, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The proposal consists of one output contract and a few small bus-riding commands. If implementation cannot remain in that shape, the mechanism should return for review rather than silently expanding into a platform.

Keep prose as the source artifact and quarantine malformed records rather than failing work.

## Gaps

- This was explicitly an ideation lens and did not inspect the local harness or corpus. No claim here establishes current implementation presence or absence.
- No local research batch was sampled, so resolution rate, stratum size, verification cost, and effect size are all unmeasured; each recommendation depending on them is marked UNVERIFIED with a settling experiment.
- The literature establishes incentive properties for expected-score maximizers under specified assumptions, not for prompted LLM instances. This artifact intentionally makes no “LLMs truthfully reveal beliefs” claim.
- Proper scoring evaluates submitted resolvable forecasts; it does not measure prose quality, value pluralism, omitted hypotheses, or owner preference. The omission challenge and explicit owner-value fields remain necessary.
