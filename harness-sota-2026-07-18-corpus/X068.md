# X068 — Epidemiology → failure-recovery

## Executive conclusion

The borrow transfers, but only for a bounded and important part of failure-recovery: epidemiology has a mature method for finding incompletely observed events, classifying uncertain reports without pretending uncertainty away, maintaining a longitudinal case register, spending scarce investigation effort according to sensitivity/specificity and base rate, and auditing whether the detector itself misses cases. It has **not** solved reconstruction of arbitrary lost computational state, exactly-once side effects, or split-brain execution. Those require ordinary checkpointing plus distributed-systems leases/fencing. Calling the whole recovery design “epidemiological” would therefore overclaim.

My concrete borrow is a **stranded-work surveillance system**: a small, purpose-tuned case definition with `suspect → probable → confirmed → recovered/closed` states; passive reports plus an active sweep; a durable line list; independent confirmation before takeover; and measured sensitivity and positive predictive value (PPV), stratified by failure mode. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve discovery and triage of silently stranded work, because those jobs and disease cases share the relevant structure—latent events, incomplete reporters, costly investigations, and changing base rates—and because the proposal retains the existing hook/skill/state bus. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to improve end-to-end successful resumption, because that additionally depends on checkpoint completeness and side-effect safety, which surveillance does not supply. Those bands are evidence-based judgments from the structural mapping and the CDC mechanisms cited below, not measurements on this harness.

The strongest design lesson is not “add another health dashboard.” It is: **separate a sensitive discovery definition from a specific takeover definition, and continuously estimate both missed-case rate and alert yield against independently obtained truth.** A lone “heartbeat expired” rule collapses detection, diagnosis, and intervention into one brittle predicate.

## Scope and method

This is an IDEATION lens as assigned. I did not inspect or make claims from the local subject tree. I used public-health guidance and methodological literature to establish what the source field actually does, then tested whether its causal and observational assumptions survive translation.

The target event is deliberately narrow:

> A unit of owner-authorized work has no live, legitimate executor capable of continuing it, while the task has neither reached a verified terminal outcome nor been intentionally abandoned.

“Worker process died,” “session disappeared,” and “machine became unavailable” are signals, not the case definition. A worker can disappear after completing the work but before reporting completion; a heartbeat can lapse while useful execution continues; a session can die while a child worker remains healthy; and a replacement can be unsafe while the old worker is merely partitioned. This distinction between observed indicator and underlying event is exactly where epidemiological reasoning helps.

## What epidemiology already solved

### 1. Surveillance is an action-oriented loop, not merely telemetry

CDC defines the purpose operationally: surveillance collects, consolidates, analyzes, and interprets data so that disease can be prevented or controlled. Its objectives determine required timeliness, completeness, sensitivity, and specificity. A tuberculosis system intended to get individual patients treated needs more timely and complete data than one intended only to track a long-term trend. CDC explicitly asks what event is under surveillance, its case definition, population, frequency, data sources, routing, analysis, and recipients ([CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 5 §2](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section2.html)).

The transferable mechanism is **purpose-first detector design**. Epidemiology does not maximize “events collected.” It states what action follows a report and tunes collection to that action. For failure-recovery, the actions differ:

- “show reliability trends” tolerates delayed aggregate data;
- “notify an owner” needs timely but not necessarily highly specific detection;
- “reassign work and permit side effects” requires much stronger confirmation and fencing.

That prevents a common category error: using the same low-specificity heartbeat alarm both for observability and automatic takeover.

### 2. A working case definition makes uncertainty explicit and revisable

In outbreak investigation, a case definition is a standard, consistently applied set of clinical and time/place/person criteria. CDC warns not to put the hypothesized exposure into the definition, because doing so biases the subsequent test of that exposure. When diagnosis is uncertain, investigators use suspected, probable, and confirmed categories. Early investigations may use a loose, sensitive definition to characterize extent; later analytic work tightens the definition because false positives can mislead inference ([CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 6 §2, “Construct a working case definition”](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson6/section2.html)). WHO likewise publishes explicit surveillance case definitions rather than leaving each reporter to improvise them ([WHO COVID-19 case definition](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-2019-nCoV-Surveillance_Case_Definition-2022.1)).

Three details matter in translation:

1. A “suspected” case is not an error; it is a deliberately inclusive queue for more evidence.
2. Confirmation is a state transition supported by a stronger kind of evidence, not merely the same weak signal repeated.
3. Definitions change as knowledge and available tests change, so the definition version must travel with every record.

### 3. Passive, active, and sentinel ascertainment compensate for different blind spots

CDC distinguishes:

- **passive surveillance**, where providers initiate reports under known rules;
- **active surveillance**, where health-department staff contact providers to solicit cases, usually for selected conditions and limited periods;
- **sentinel surveillance**, where a stable, prearranged sample reports consistently when universal reporting is incomplete;
- **registries**, permanent longitudinal records that can include diagnosis, treatment, outcome, and periodic active updates.

These are described together in [CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 5 §4](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section4.html). CDC also notes that secondary-use data are efficient but may be late or lack the detail needed for the surveillance objective. The important mechanism is not “use every data source”; it is **combine cheap routine reports with selective active ascertainment, then preserve cases and outcomes in a longitudinal line list**.

Translated signals are:

- passive: worker/session hooks report `started`, `checkpointed`, `completed`, or `abandoned`;
- active: a session-start or scheduled hook scans the authoritative active-task registry and probes cases whose lease or progress age is abnormal;
- sentinel: a small fixed suite of injected crash scenarios runs on each harness release, giving a stable trend even when production workload mix shifts;
- registry/line list: one durable record per task attempt, including definition version, evidence, classification transitions, takeover, outcome, and duplicates.

### 4. Sensitivity, PPV, and base rate make detector quality an empirical question

CDC’s surveillance-evaluation guideline defines sensitivity at case level as the proportion of true cases detected, and also considers outbreak-level sensitivity. It says sensitivity measurement needs truth data external to the surveillance system and validation of collected data. It defines PPV as the proportion of reported cases that truly have the event; low PPV wastes investigation resources and can create pseudo-outbreaks. Crucially, PPV reflects case-definition sensitivity, specificity, **and prevalence** ([CDC, Updated Guidelines for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems, MMWR RR-50-13](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm)). The same guideline warns that raising sensitivity can lower PPV and that greater complexity can damage acceptability, timeliness, and flexibility.

Base-rate reasoning is decisive for crash recovery. In an illustrative population of 10,000 task windows where 1% are truly stranded, a detector with 95% sensitivity and 95% specificity finds about 95 true cases but also about 495 false cases: PPV is only about 16%. These numbers are a thought experiment, not harness measurements. **UNVERIFIED: the harness’s actual stranding prevalence, sensitivity, specificity, and PPV are unknown; settle this by fault-injecting labeled worker/session/machine failures plus matched healthy long-running controls, then reconcile detector output to the injected truth and manually adjudicated production samples.** The illustration proves why seemingly impressive sensitivity and specificity are insufficient when the event is rare; it does not estimate current performance.

### 5. Surveillance treats detector changes as possible artifacts

CDC lists changes in reporting procedure, case definition, diagnostic test, awareness, and batch reporting as causes of apparent incidence changes. It advises considering an apparent increase real for protective action while investigating whether it is artifactual ([CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, Lesson 5 §5](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section5.html)). Timely dissemination back to reporters also improves collaboration and reporting ([CDC, Lesson 5 §6](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson5/section6.html)).

For a harness, every line-list entry therefore needs `case_definition_version`, `detector_version`, and source timestamps. A jump in “stranded tasks” after shortening the lease must not be presented as a reliability regression until the definition artifact is separated from a real change. Conversely, the system should respond conservatively to a confirmed high-impact case while that analysis proceeds.

## Concrete translation: Stranded-Work Surveillance (SWS)

### Proposal A — versioned stranded-work case definition — UNVERIFIED implementation premise

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Define the population at risk as every accepted task with a stable `task_id`, one or more `attempt_id`s, and a declared terminal policy. Define observable criteria without embedding a presumed cause such as “machine crash.”

| Classification | Consistently applied criteria | Permitted action |
|---|---|---|
| `suspect` | Task is recorded `active`, no verified terminal outcome exists, and one sensitive indicator fires: lease age exceeded, expected session missing, progress age exceeded, or nonterminal process exit. | Gather evidence; do not re-execute side effects. |
| `probable` | `suspect` plus a mechanically independent signal from a different failure domain, e.g. scheduler says executor absent **and** durable checkpoint remains nonterminal; or session registry lacks the session **and** the worker endpoint fails a liveness challenge. | Prepare a resume packet; notify or queue review. |
| `confirmed-stranded` | `probable`, a bounded recheck window elapsed, no authorized executor can renew the fenced lease, and no independent completion evidence exists. | A successor may acquire a higher fencing token and resume from a validated checkpoint. |
| `recovered` | Successor acquired ownership, revalidated prerequisites, and produced a terminal result whose existence is independently checked. | Close incident; retain outcome and recovery latency. |
| `false-positive` | Original executor is healthy/authorized, task was intentionally paused/abandoned, or completion is independently established. | Close without takeover; feed PPV measurement. |

“Time, place, person” becomes “time window, execution domain, task/attempt identity.” The definition must specify lease horizon by workload class; an interactive edit and a 12-hour build cannot share one elapsed-time threshold.

The two-signal requirement is deliberately about mechanical independence, not two spellings of the same fact. A heartbeat file and a “last seen” field derived from that file are one signal. Scheduler membership plus a checkpoint stored on a separate durable channel are two. A worker’s `completed=true` and its prose “done” are one self-report, not independent confirmation.

This classification is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce unsafe takeover compared with a one-signal timeout, because it improves specificity before the highest-cost action and mirrors the documented surveillance separation between sensitive case finding and confirmation. Its actual false-negative cost is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the labeled fault matrix described under validation, including failures that corrupt or delay one evidence channel.**

### Proposal B — tiny passive reporter plus active sweeper — UNVERIFIED implementation premise

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Use an existing lifecycle hook to append compact, atomic records to a durable task line list:

```json
{
  "task_id": "...",
  "attempt_id": "...",
  "event": "checkpoint|terminal|lease-renewal|intentional-pause",
  "definition_version": "sws/v1",
  "fencing_token": 7,
  "checkpoint_ref": "content-addressed-ref",
  "checkpoint_digest": "...",
  "side_effect_ledger_ref": "...",
  "observed_at": "...",
  "source": "worker-hook"
}
```

A second small scanner, invoked by the existing session-start/periodic hook bus—not a new daemon—walks only active records, applies the versioned case definition, performs bounded confirmation probes, and emits transitions. It must be able to recover an incomplete final append by checksum/record framing. Compaction can snapshot closed cases while retaining raw records for detector evaluation.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to remain under roughly 200 lines per component because the mechanism is an append, a scan, a finite-state classifier, and a few adapters; evidence is the deliberately narrow interface and absence of a new scheduler or database. **UNVERIFIED: actual line count and whether the available hook bus exposes every needed lifecycle event; settle it with a spike implementing `accept/checkpoint/terminal`, one scanner, and two independent liveness adapters, then count non-test source lines and run the fault matrix.** If the spike requires a resident coordinator, this proposal fails the anti-overfit gate and must be reduced rather than quietly becoming BUILD-LARGE.

### Proposal C — resume packet and fenced takeover — UNVERIFIED implementation premise

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Every meaningful checkpoint should be a small *resume packet*, not a transcript dump:

- owner intent and acceptance criteria;
- last verified durable artifacts and their content hashes;
- completed steps and evidence;
- next safe action;
- unresolved decisions/assumptions;
- environment/dependency identifiers needed to validate replay;
- outstanding or completed external side effects, with idempotency keys where available;
- lease/fencing token and definition version.

On confirmation, a successor performs compare-and-swap acquisition of a monotonically increasing fencing token, validates referenced artifacts and prerequisites, checks the side-effect ledger, and resumes from the last *verified* boundary. A stale worker presenting an older token is rejected by cooperating mutating adapters.

The epidemiological contribution ends at “confirmed case with a documented history and outcome.” Checkpoint content, compare-and-swap ownership, fencing, and idempotency are distributed-systems controls. They are included because surveillance without them would detect an incident and then risk making it worse.

This will **likely (55-80%)** permit useful continuation after a worker or session death when the durable store survives and checkpoints occur at semantic boundaries; evidence is that the required intent, artifacts, next action, and side-effect state are explicitly preserved. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to recover transparently after total machine loss if the only line list and checkpoints live on that machine; evidence is direct loss of the only recovery evidence. **UNVERIFIED: whether an already-authorized, cross-machine durable artifact channel exists in the target harness; settle it by writing a canary checkpoint, killing or isolating the originating machine, and reading/verifying it from a replacement host using only existing buses.** If no such channel exists, cross-machine recovery remains a stated gap; this unit does not justify adding a database or durable-execution service.

### Proposal D — detector evaluation as a first-class recovery test — UNVERIFIED implementation premise

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Create a fixed fault-injection sentinel suite with labeled truth:

1. kill a worker before any checkpoint;
2. kill after a checkpoint but before reporting it;
3. kill after an external side effect but before terminal reporting;
4. terminate the parent session while a child remains healthy;
5. partition heartbeat/liveness while execution continues;
6. delay line-list writes and deliver them out of order;
7. corrupt the newest checkpoint while preserving the previous one;
8. restart a stale worker after takeover;
9. intentionally pause/abandon a task;
10. complete the artifact but lose the completion event;
11. make the whole originating machine unavailable while a shared durable channel survives;
12. make both machine and sole local checkpoint unavailable.

For each detector version and workload stratum, report:

- case sensitivity = detected true strandings / injected true strandings;
- takeover PPV = confirmed strandings / automated takeovers;
- false takeover count, never hidden inside an average;
- median and tail detection/recovery latency;
- fraction resumed from a valid checkpoint;
- duplicate side effects;
- unrecoverable cases by reason;
- classification transitions and definition version.

Production truth cannot be obtained only from the detector being evaluated. Sample both alerts and “healthy” non-alerts for independent adjudication, and reconcile terminal artifacts, scheduler records, and owner corrections. A stable sentinel suite provides trend sensitivity; production sampling checks representativeness.

This suite is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose false-death and stale-resurrection defects that happy-path restart tests miss, because cases 4, 5, 8, and 10 explicitly separate task truth from individual reporting channels. Whether those defects occur in the present harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by running the suite against the current and proposed recovery paths and preserving the confusion matrix.**

## What this would catch that a typical crash detector misses — UNVERIFIED for the current harness

The heading is marked because this ideation unit did not inspect the local implementation, and no supplied measurement establishes which of these classes the current harness detects. The proposal would distinguish and catch the following classes by construction; **UNVERIFIED: which are currently missed; settle that premise by replaying each labeled scenario through the current recovery path before implementing SWS.**

| Failure class | Why a single heartbeat/session-exit detector misses or mishandles it | SWS evidence/action |
|---|---|---|
| Silent abandonment without a clean crash | No dramatic death event occurs. | Active registry sweep finds an active nonterminal task whose lease/progress has aged. |
| False death during pause, overload, or partition | Liveness disappears although execution remains legitimate. | `suspect` only; independent scheduler/worker challenge prevents takeover. |
| Session dies, child survives | Session status is not task status. | Separate task, session, and worker identities; worker can renew the fenced lease. |
| Worker dies after completing an artifact but before reporting | Exit suggests stranded work, but replay may duplicate work/side effects. | Independent artifact digest and side-effect ledger can classify completion or choose the prior safe boundary. |
| Worker dies after side effect but before checkpoint | Transcript/state alone cannot prove whether replay is safe. | Case is confirmed but held for reconciliation; no claim of automatic recovery. |
| Corrupt or partial latest checkpoint | “Checkpoint exists” is a weak signal. | Digest/framing validation falls back to the last verified checkpoint. |
| Stale worker resurrects after replacement | Both workers may believe they own the task. | Monotone fencing token rejects mutations from the stale attempt. |
| Detector regression after threshold/schema change | Counts change even if real stranding does not. | Versioned definitions plus sentinel sensitivity/PPV reveal the artifact. |
| A whole stratum is invisible to passive hooks | No report is generated by the failed component. | Active sweep and negative-sample adjudication measure the passive channel’s blind spot. |
| “Recovered” asserted but outcome unusable | Executor self-report is mistaken for truth. | `recovered` requires an independent artifact/test/consumer signal. |

## Adversarial transfer test

### Where the analogy genuinely holds

1. **Latent event versus imperfect observation.** Disease status is not identical to a report; stranded-work status is not identical to a missed heartbeat. Both require a case definition over fallible indicators.
2. **Multiple ascertainment channels.** Clinicians, labs, and registries have distinct blind spots; workers, schedulers, sessions, durable artifacts, and owner observations do too.
3. **Asymmetric intervention cost.** Missing a severe case can be costly, but investigating every false report also consumes capacity. In recovery, missed strandings waste owner time while false takeovers can duplicate or corrupt work.
4. **Changing base rates and definitions.** Outbreak prevalence changes PPV; workload class and infrastructure state change stranding prevalence. Detector/config changes can manufacture apparent trends in both fields.
5. **Longitudinal outcomes matter.** A notification without treatment/outcome is incomplete for action-oriented surveillance; a crash alert without resumed-or-closed outcome is incomplete for recovery.

These structural correspondences make the bounded transfer **very likely (80-90%)** valid. The evidence is not superficial vocabulary but a shared inference problem: classify a hidden state from noisy, incomplete, differently biased observations, then allocate a costly response.

### Where it breaks

1. **Humans are not replayable processes.** Epidemiology observes and intervenes; it does not reconstruct an exact pre-disease computational state. A case report cannot substitute for a checkpoint.
2. **Disease confirmation does not create exactly-once semantics.** A laboratory confirmation has no analogue to preventing a duplicate payment, message, deployment, or file mutation. Recovery needs idempotency and fencing.
3. **Contagion models usually do not transfer.** Worker failures can share infrastructure causes, but “transmission” among tasks is not generally biological contact. I reject epidemic reproduction numbers and compartment models as default recovery controls.
4. **Time scales and feedback differ.** Software can emit near-instant high-cardinality telemetry and can change behavior immediately after a hook/config update. Public-health reporting delays and biological incubation do not map cleanly.
5. **The denominator can be more knowable in software.** If all accepted task IDs are durably registered, the at-risk population is explicit; epidemiology often estimates it. This makes direct sensitivity testing preferable to population estimation.
6. **Automatic intervention is more dangerous in one particular way.** A false disease alert generally triggers investigation before treatment; a naive recovery detector may immediately launch a second writer. The translation must therefore preserve the staged classification and forbid automatic mutation at `suspect`.

The analogy would fail if used to justify a general recovery runtime, probabilistic “disease spread” model, or dashboard detached from action. It succeeds as a detector/triage/evaluation pattern layered on a minimal checkpoint-and-fencing substrate.

## Attractive epidemiological mechanisms explicitly rejected

### Reject capture–recapture as the production estimate of missed tasks

Capture–recapture estimates unobserved cases from overlaps among incomplete lists. CDC mentions it as one way to assess surveillance sensitivity ([CDC MMWR RR-50-13](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5013a1.htm)). However, two-source methods require independence of registers, and heterogeneity/dependence can badly distort infectious-disease estimates ([van Hest et al., *Epidemiology & Infection*, 2008/2010 PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2870770/)).

Worker, session, scheduler, and checkpoint detections are strongly causally coupled: one crash can suppress several reports, one control plane may populate multiple registries, and some task types are much easier to observe than others. The critical assumptions therefore **very likely (80-90%)** fail, based on the shared origins and heterogeneous capture probabilities of these channels. Use cross-source disagreement to find cases, but use injected truth and independent adjudication—not capture–recapture—to estimate sensitivity.

### Reject syndromic anomaly detection as the first recovery trigger

Anomaly detection is useful when the case is unknown, but the target here is an individual active task needing a safe decision. Aggregate spikes can prioritize an investigation; they cannot establish that a particular task is safe to take over. Adding learned baselines would also add complexity and workload-drift sensitivity without solving fencing. A deterministic, versioned case definition plus measured thresholds is **very likely (80-90%)** the better first mechanism, based on the explicit task registry denominator and the high cost of false takeover. **UNVERIFIED: unusual correlated failures might only be visible in aggregate; settle this with the sentinel suite plus historical replay comparing per-task rules against a simple count/rate threshold before considering any statistical detector.**

### Reject a central epidemiology-inspired service

No second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is proposed. A new “surveillance service” would duplicate scheduling/state ownership and violate the anti-overfit gate. The useful borrow fits in versioned JSON, append-only records, hook-triggered scanning, and a fault suite.

## Failure policy and safe stopping points

The design must be willing to say “detected but not automatically recoverable.” In particular:

- no durable checkpoint: open a confirmed incident with owner intent and available artifacts, but do not fabricate state;
- ambiguous external side effect: reconcile through the external system or request human decision before replay;
- no independent liveness signal: remain `suspect/probable` and do not seize ownership;
- no surviving cross-machine durable channel: machine-loss case is unrecoverable until that machine/storage returns;
- unsupported mutating adapter that ignores fencing tokens: allow read-only reconstruction, not concurrent mutation;
- conflicting task identity or definition versions: quarantine the record and retain raw evidence.

This conservative boundary is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce catastrophic duplicate actions relative to unconditional restart, because each stop condition corresponds to missing evidence required to distinguish safe replay from double execution. It may increase manual review; PPV and outcome data should determine whether that trade is acceptable rather than hiding it.

## Minimal experiment that can falsify the recommendation

Build no service. In an isolated test environment:

1. Define `sws/v1` and its two independent signal adapters.
2. Implement the append-only reporter, active scanner, resume packet, and fencing-token check on one representative mutating adapter.
3. Run at least the 12 sentinel scenarios above across short interactive, long-running, child-worker, and side-effecting tasks.
4. Include healthy controls with slow progress, transient disconnects, and intentional pauses so specificity/PPV are measurable.
5. Blindly adjudicate a sample of alerts and non-alerts from artifacts plus scheduler/control-plane evidence not consumed by the detector.
6. Compare against the current recovery path on sensitivity, takeover PPV, time to useful continuation, duplicate effects, and unrecoverable fraction.
7. Change a threshold and a case-definition field deliberately; verify that the reporting layer attributes the resulting count shift to detector version.
8. Disable each reporting channel in turn; verify that the evaluator notices the sensitivity loss rather than concluding there are no cases.

Go forward only if it improves detected-and-usefully-resumed cases without any unfenced duplicate mutation in the test matrix. A reasonable initial acceptance threshold is **UNVERIFIED and must not be treated as a fact**: 100% prevention of duplicate mutating side effects in the finite injected suite, at least 90% sensitivity for recoverable injected strandings, and a takeover PPV high enough that review load is acceptable to the owner. Set the operational PPV target only after measuring the real base rate and cost ratio.

The recommendation would be falsified if (a) independent signals share one hidden failure domain, (b) semantic checkpoints are too expensive or too incomplete to improve continuation, (c) the active registry lacks a durable denominator, (d) the bus cannot enforce fencing on important mutations, or (e) classification latency costs more than immediate owner-led restart. In those cases retain only the line list and fault suite as measurement tools; do not force automatic recovery.

## Final recommendation — UNVERIFIED until the fault matrix passes

Adopt the epidemiological borrow as a **small surveillance layer around recovery**, not as a replacement orchestration architecture:

1. version a staged stranded-work case definition;
2. combine passive lifecycle reports with an active sweep;
3. keep a durable task-attempt line list with classifications and outcomes;
4. require mechanically independent confirmation before takeover;
5. resume only through validated packets and fenced ownership;
6. measure sensitivity and takeover PPV against injected and independently adjudicated truth;
7. stratify results by workload and detector version;
8. stop safely when checkpoint or side-effect truth is missing.

This package passes the anti-overfit gate: it rides existing hooks/state/skills, proposes only CONFIG and BUILD-SMALL components, and explicitly avoids every rejected architectural category. The bounded detection-and-triage transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** sound for the structural reasons and source-field evidence above.
