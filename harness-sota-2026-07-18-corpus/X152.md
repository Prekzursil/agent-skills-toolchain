# X152 — Cross-domain borrow: archival science → agent-claim verification

**Unit:** X152  
**Source field:** archival science (provenance, appraisal, retention schedules, authenticity)  
**Target:** proving an agent's claim without trusting the agent and without requiring a human to read everything  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Lens constraint:** external research and design only. Per the unit brief, this report does not inspect or make claims about the local harness repository.

## Executive conclusion

The archival-science borrow **does transfer, but only to part of “verification.”** Archival science has a mature answer to: *What record is this? Who or what created it? What happened to it? Is it complete and unchanged in essential respects? What other records give it context? How strong is the resulting presumption of authenticity?* It does **not** thereby answer: *Is the proposition recorded in it true?* The Society of American Archivists explicitly says authenticity alone does not imply reliable content, and InterPARES states even more directly that an authentic record need not be accurate ([SAA, “authenticity”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/authenticity.html); [InterPARES 2, Domain 2 report, pp. 26–27](https://www.interpares.org/display_file/ip2_book_part_3_domain2_task_force.pdf)).

That distinction is the valuable borrow. The harness should stop treating “a citation exists,” “a tool was called,” “the transcript says PASS,” and “the claim is true” as one state. It should issue separate machine verdicts:

1. **IDENTIFIED** — the claimed evidence has a stable identity and unambiguous relationship to this run and claim;
2. **INTACT** — captured evidence bytes and relevant metadata match their recorded fixity values;
3. **AUTHENTIC-EVIDENCE** — identity, integrity, provenance, context, and custody controls support a stated presumption that this is the actual evidence produced by the observed operation;
4. **SUPPORTED** — a claim-type-specific rule finds that the authentic evidence entails the bounded claim (for example, exit code 0 supports “this invocation exited successfully,” but does not support “the program is correct”);
5. **CORROBORATED** — a mechanically independent second signal agrees where the claim class requires it;
6. **INDETERMINATE / CONTRADICTED** — the package is incomplete, the rule cannot decide, or evidence conflicts.

It is **very likely (80–90%)** that this separation would catch a meaningful class of false completion, stale-output, wrong-file, citation-drift, and unsupported-absence claims, because each of those failures breaks an identity, context, fixity, entailment, or corroboration check that is mechanically testable; this is a forward-looking estimate, not a measured result on this harness. The experiment that would settle it is the seeded-fault evaluation in “Acceptance experiments” below.

It is **almost certainly not (1–5%)** sufficient to verify arbitrary semantic claims, because archival authenticity preserves a record at the fidelity at which it was created; it does not make false original content true. That limit is not speculation: it is the explicit authenticity/accuracy distinction in InterPARES and SAA cited above.

The recommended artifact is a small **Claim Evidence Package (CEP)** generated on the existing tool/hook bus: a content-addressed manifest joining each material claim to harness-observed evidence, provenance events, context, independent corroboration requirements, a disposition class, and a machine verdict. This is intentionally a package format plus a verifier, not another orchestration runtime, memory service, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. What archival science has actually solved

### 1.1 Archival diplomatics: authenticity is a cumulative, rebuttable presumption

Diplomatics determines whether a document is authentic or forged by examining internal and external characteristics. Archival science extends that examination to aggregates of records in their functional, procedural, and documentary contexts ([SAA, “diplomatics”](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/diplomatics.html)). The important mechanism is not “ask a trusted expert.” It is a disciplined decomposition of authenticity into evidence-bearing attributes and controls.

InterPARES’s *Requirements for Assessing and Maintaining the Authenticity of Electronic Records* says a preserver assessing an electronic record must establish **identity** and demonstrate **integrity**. Identity includes the persons participating in formation, creation and transmission dates, the action or matter in which the record participates, its linkage to other records in that action (the archival bond), and attachments. Integrity concerns whether the record remains complete and unaltered in essential respects ([InterPARES, Authenticity Requirements, conceptual framework and Requirement A.1](https://www.interpares.org/display_file.cfm?doc=ip1_authenticity_requirements.pdf)).

InterPARES then makes the assessment cumulative. Its benchmark requirements concern record attributes plus procedural controls over creation, handling, and maintenance. The stronger and more numerous the satisfied controls, the stronger the presumption. When the basis is insufficient, authenticity is verified through a detailed examination against reliable information from other sources: preserved copies, registers, textual or forensic analysis, audit trails, or trusted-third-party testimony ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force report, “The presumption of authenticity” and “The verification of authenticity,” pp. 21–22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)).

This is a better model for agent verification than a binary “verified” badge:

- the agent is the **record creator**, not the authority that authenticates its own record;
- the harness is the **preserver/observer**;
- the response claim is one record, while the tool request, tool result, source snapshot, produced file, and test result are related records;
- the links among them are the agent-run equivalent of the **archival bond**;
- procedural controls create a **presumption**, not certainty;
- weak or conflicting packages are escalated to independent verification rather than waved through.

This mapping is **very likely (80–90%)** structurally sound for operational claims because agent work already occurs as a sequence of identifiable actions with outputs and relationships, matching the record/context/event structure documented by InterPARES. The remaining uncertainty is whether every target harness event exposes stable IDs and raw outputs. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is to emit one known tool call of each tool family, capture its hook payload and canonical transcript, and compare call ID, arguments, raw result bytes, status, and timestamps field by field.**

### 1.2 Chain of preservation: preserve content *and* the history that makes it intelligible

Digital preservation does not rely on a filename and checksum alone. The OAIS information model calls for representation, reference, context, fixity, and provenance information. The Library of Congress summarizes those categories as information needed to render/use the data, identify it, document why it was created and its relationships, test integrity, and document custody and changes since creation ([Library of Congress, Sustainability Factors](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/sustain/sustain.shtml?loclr=blogsig)). OAIS packages distinguish information received from a producer, the archival package managed by a repository, and a package disseminated to a consumer ([Library of Congress, NDSA/OAIS explainer](https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2016/04/expanding-ndsa-levels-of-preservation/)).

PREMIS makes this operational. It defines preservation metadata around Objects, Events, Agents, and relationships. Its official overview describes PREMIS as the international standard for metadata supporting preservation and long-term usability; its controlled event vocabulary includes capture, creation, ingestion, fixity check, message-digest calculation, validation, migration, deletion, and digital-signature validation ([Library of Congress, PREMIS maintenance activity](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/); [PREMIS preservation-event vocabulary](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/pif-presentations/rebecca-SKOS/preservationEvents-PREMIS.html)). The PREMIS introduction emphasizes that an accurate and trustworthy event history is critical to digital provenance and demonstrating object authenticity ([Library of Congress, *Understanding PREMIS*, 2021 edition](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/understandingPREMIS_english_2021.pdf)).

Fixity is deliberately narrower than truth. A checksum detects that bytes changed; it does not say why, where, whether the original was correct, or whether the claim made about it follows. The Digital Preservation Coalition describes checksums as supporting transfer and storage integrity and a chain of custody, while also noting that intentional migrations require new checksums and documented events ([DPC Digital Preservation Handbook, “Fixity and checksums”](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/technical-solutions-and-tools/fixity-and-checksums)).

The agent-harness translation is direct:

| Archival package element | Agent verification element | What it prevents or exposes |
|---|---|---|
| Reference/identity | `run_id`, `claim_id`, tool-call ID, canonical source locator, artifact path, model/runtime identity | Evidence from another call, run, URL, revision, or file being silently substituted |
| Content | Exact tool result bytes, source snapshot or response, test output, and relevant after-state | A paraphrase supplied by the agent standing in for actual evidence |
| Representation | Encoding, MIME/type, parser/version, schema version, command/shell, platform | Misreading bytes through the wrong parser, shell, schema, or rendering context |
| Context | User request, working directory, repository revision, relevant environment facts, parent/child event links | Correct evidence being applied to the wrong task, directory, commit, or proposition |
| Provenance | Ordered event records: creator, capture, transformation, verifier, result, timestamp | Undocumented normalization, truncation, retries, edits, or post-hoc replacement |
| Fixity | SHA-256 of content and canonicalized manifest; optional chain hash over events | Silent mutation or accidental corruption after capture |
| Archival bond | Typed links: `claim-supported-by`, `tool-result-of`, `artifact-produced-by`, `test-ran-against`, `supersedes` | “Evidence adjacency,” where a nearby but unrelated successful action is cited |
| Access/custody | Which principal can append, read, redact, or delete; recorder boundary | The claimant rewriting the record on which its verification depends |

It is **almost certain (90–99%)** that fixity would detect changed captured bytes when a collision-resistant digest is recomputed, based on the defined function of checksums and mature preservation practice above. It is **very unlikely (5–20%)** that fixity alone would catch a false original statement, because the unchanged bytes can faithfully preserve a falsehood; InterPARES’s explicit authenticity/accuracy distinction is the evidence.

### 1.3 Appraisal and retention schedules: keep evidence by function and risk, not by reflex

Archives do not preserve everything forever. Appraisal decides final disposition; a records schedule describes content and context and establishes whether and when records may be destroyed or transferred for permanent preservation ([NARA, “Records Scheduling and Appraisal”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/sch-appraisal)). NARA warns that “destroy when no longer needed” leaves retention to individual users, causes loss of control, can produce both over-retention and premature destruction, and is difficult to automate; it recommends fixed, applicable cutoffs and retention periods ([NARA, “Scheduling Records”](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). NARA also treats sampling as a deliberate appraisal decision: define an objective process, identify retained records easily, and assess cost and benefit rather than using sampling merely to defer a volume problem ([NARA Appraisal Policy, sampling section](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)).

This maps to verification because a full transcript is cheap to create but expensive to inspect, retain, redact, migrate, and reason over. The archive’s answer is a functional retention schedule:

- retain the **final CEP manifest and verdict** longer than bulky raw successful output;
- retain **all contradictory, indeterminate, user-corrected, policy-blocked, destructive-action, security-sensitive, and failed-verification packages** for the investigation window;
- retain evidence for high-impact external actions and irreversible changes longer than routine read-only discovery;
- compact routine successful raw output after a fixed period only if its manifest, digest, bounded excerpt, locator, and verifier result remain and the underlying source is reproducible;
- place a **hold** on disposal when a correction, incident, audit, or dispute references the run;
- use objective sampling for low-risk passed packages to measure verifier false negatives; never sample away the evidence for an individual high-impact claim that must itself be proved.

It is **likely (55–80%)** that a functional schedule would reduce evidence volume without materially reducing detection of important faults, because appraisal explicitly concentrates retention on evidential value and uses fixed disposition rules; the range is broad because the harness’s claim distribution and investigation horizon are not measured in this unit. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is to replay 90 days of packages under candidate schedules and measure retained bytes, percentage of seeded faults still investigable, and percentage of actual owner corrections whose full evidence remains.**

## 2. The concrete design

## Recommendation — Claim Evidence Packages, contingent on recorder-boundary validation **[UNVERIFIED: settle with Experiments 1 and 2 below]**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Build one compact capture/verification helper invoked by existing lifecycle/tool hooks. Do not build a daemon or a second agent runtime. The helper emits newline-delimited canonical JSON event receipts plus one final manifest per material claim. A deterministic verifier consumes the manifest at the final-response gate or on demand.

The recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because two target-platform premises are not established by this ideation unit: that hooks expose the necessary raw event fields, and that the recorder can write evidence the agent cannot rewrite. Experiments 1 and 2 below settle those premises. If either fails, the strict “without trusting the agent” requirement is not met; do not market the result as trusted verification.

### 2.1 Minimal package schema

The following is a design sketch, not a prescription for an over-strict fan-out schema. Unknown fields should be preserved, optional fields should remain optional, and schema failure should produce `INDETERMINATE`, not destroy the underlying record.

```json
{
  "cep_version": "0.1",
  "run_id": "stable-run-id",
  "claim": {
    "claim_id": "c7",
    "type": "test_passed",
    "text": "The targeted test suite passed",
    "scope": {"command_event": "e41", "repo_revision": "...", "cwd": "..."},
    "impact": "medium"
  },
  "evidence": [
    {
      "event_id": "e41",
      "kind": "tool_result",
      "producer": "harness-observed-tool",
      "content_ref": "sha256:...",
      "content_sha256": "...",
      "status": 0,
      "captured_at": "...",
      "representation": {"tool": "...", "shell": "...", "encoding": "utf-8"},
      "relations": [
        {"type": "result-of", "target": "e40"},
        {"type": "ran-against", "target": "state12"}
      ]
    },
    {
      "event_id": "state12",
      "kind": "after_state",
      "content_ref": "sha256:...",
      "content_sha256": "..."
    }
  ],
  "provenance_events": [
    {"type": "capture", "agent": "harness-recorder", "outcome": "success", "at": "..."},
    {"type": "fixity_check", "agent": "cep-verify", "outcome": "match", "at": "..."}
  ],
  "rule": {"id": "test-passed/v1", "requires": ["exit=0", "recognized-run-summary", "after-state-bound"]},
  "corroboration": {"required": true, "events": ["e41", "state12"], "independence_basis": "process-status vs artifact/test-report state"},
  "verdict": "CORROBORATED",
  "disposition": {"class": "routine-pass", "delete_raw_after_days": 30, "hold": false},
  "manifest_sha256": "..."
}
```

### 2.2 Capture rule: the claimant may point, but may not self-attest

The agent may emit a claim type and evidence handles. It must not supply the authoritative tool output, status, timestamp, digest, or verifier verdict. The helper resolves handles against harness-observed events and computes those fields independently.

The capture pipeline is:

1. On tool request, record stable event ID, parent run ID, normalized tool name, exact arguments or a redacted commitment to them, working context, and timestamp.
2. On tool result, record the matching request ID, raw result or content-addressed blob, status/error channel, truncation flag, and digest.
3. On transformations (normalization, excerpting, schema conversion, summarization), keep the source digest and emit a distinct event with `derived-from`; never overwrite the source event.
4. At finalization, resolve each material claim’s evidence handles and run the claim-type rule.
5. If evidence is missing, truncated in a way that removes decisive material, refers to a different scope, or fails fixity, return `INDETERMINATE` or `CONTRADICTED`; do not silently coerce it into a pass.
6. Apply the retention schedule after verdict, subject to holds.

It is **very likely (80–90%)** that harness-side resolution is safer than accepting evidence text copied by the agent, because it eliminates self-reported status and binds the claim to the observed event. This prediction assumes the event stream is captured before the agent can mutate it. That assumption is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment 1 settles field completeness and Experiment 2 settles mutation resistance.**

### 2.3 A small, closed rule vocabulary

Do not promise general semantic verification. Start with claim classes whose evidence relation is deterministic:

| Claim type | Required authentic evidence | Independent corroboration | Machine verdict boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| `command_succeeded` | Exact invocation event plus process exit/status event | Optional unless impact is high | Only that invocation returned success; not that its goal was achieved |
| `test_passed` | Invocation, untruncated recognized test summary, exit status, revision/cwd | Test report or expected artifact/state from a separate channel | Named tests passed against the bound state |
| `file_written` | Write/edit event and digest/path | Fresh filesystem metadata+digest read after the write | Exact bytes exist at exact path at check time |
| `change_present` | Before/after state or patch | Independent diff/status query | Specified textual/structural change exists; not that it is correct |
| `source_says` | Exact source snapshot, locator, retrieval event, excerpt offsets | Second source only when policy requires; otherwise source identity+context | Quoted/paraphrased bounded proposition appears in the captured source |
| `item_absent` | Detector query, scope, empty result | Validated positive control plus a mechanically different detector/data field | No item found within declared scope and detector limits |
| `task_done` | Explicit checklist of requested acceptance predicates | At least two independent state signals for material completion | Only listed predicates are established; no open predicate may be hidden |

It is **likely (55–80%)** that this closed vocabulary will cover a substantial share of costly operational misclaims while remaining small, because the classes correspond to common agent actions and mechanically checkable evidence. Coverage on this owner’s workload is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is to label a statistically useful sample of owner corrections by claim type and report vocabulary coverage before implementation.** Claims outside the vocabulary must remain `SEMANTIC-UNVERIFIED`, be rerun by a purpose-built deterministic checker, or be escalated; they must not be passed by a generic model critic.

### 2.4 Two independent signals are a property of the package, not prose etiquette

The package must encode `independence_basis`, and the verifier must reject two observations that arise from the same failure domain. Examples:

- “file written”: the tool’s success result **and** a fresh read/digest from the filesystem;
- “tests passed”: process exit/status **and** a separately emitted test report or after-state marker;
- “item absent”: an empty scoped query **and** a different detector or data field, after the first detector proves it can find a known-present control;
- “web claim current”: captured primary-source response **and** a separately retrieved authoritative source or independent endpoint where materiality requires it;
- “artifact complete”: manifest predicate coverage **and** an after-state enumeration of the expected outputs.

Two reruns through the same parser, two excerpts from the same response, or agent prose plus an agent-authored summary are one signal. This is analogous to InterPARES verification against reliable information from other sources, not merely accumulating more copies of the same assertion ([InterPARES Authenticity Task Force report, p. 22](https://www.interpares.org/book/interpares_book_d_part1.pdf)).

It is **very likely (80–90%)** that explicit independence metadata would prevent superficial “two checks” compliance, because the verifier can compare event lineage and reject shared parentage; it is only **likely (55–80%)** to establish true mechanical independence automatically, because hidden common dependencies (the same stale cache, wrapper, filesystem view, or API backend) may not be visible. The latter premise is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is fault injection at each shared dependency and measurement of whether both nominally independent signals fail together.**

### 2.5 Retention classes

Use a schedule, not “keep everything” or “delete when no longer needed”:

| Class | Examples | Proposed disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Incident/hold | Contradiction, owner correction, security event, disputed destructive action | Preserve full package and raw evidence until explicit hold release |
| High-impact verified | External mutation, deletion, publication, credentials/permissions change | Preserve full package for a fixed long window chosen by owner policy |
| Indeterminate | Truncation, missing evidence, schema ambiguity, failed corroboration | Preserve until triaged and for a fixed post-triage window |
| Routine pass | Read-only discovery, ordinary local edit with verified after-state, routine tests | Preserve manifest/verdict longer; compact bulky raw output after a fixed short window if reproducible |
| Ephemeral diagnostic | Superseded probe with no unique evidential value | Preserve digest, provenance link, and outcome briefly; dispose of raw payload on schedule |

This table intentionally does not invent day counts except the illustrative `30` in the schema sketch. Selecting actual periods requires volume, privacy, incident-response, and owner-recall measurements absent from this unit. Any concrete production duration is therefore **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is the 90-day replay described in §1.3, followed by owner selection of acceptable investigation coverage and storage cost.**

## 3. What this would catch, and what it would not

### 3.1 Expected catches

Each forecast below carries a confidence band and its evidence:

- **Wrong-run or wrong-call evidence — almost certain (90–99%).** Stable IDs, typed relations, and scope fields make a mismatch deterministic. Evidence: OAIS reference/context categories and InterPARES record identity/archival bond explicitly serve identification and relationship.
- **Evidence modified after capture — almost certain (90–99%) if the baseline digest and manifest are outside the claimant’s write authority.** A recomputed cryptographic digest changes when bytes change with overwhelming practical probability. Evidence: DPC fixity practice and NARA’s recommendation to checksum records and metadata and audit additions/updates/deletions ([NARA Fast Track Products, §4.2](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/policy/prod6b.html)). The custody condition is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment 2 settles it.**
- **Agent says a failed command/test passed — very likely (80–90%).** Harness-observed status contradicts the claim, while a recognized result plus after-state is harder to spoof accidentally. Evidence: the proposed deterministic `command_succeeded`/`test_passed` rules; settle the estimate with seeded faults F1–F3 in Experiment 3.
- **A successful command cited as proof of broader task completion — very likely (80–90%).** The rule boundary and requested-predicate checklist prevent exit 0 from entailing unspecified success. Evidence: archival context and bond distinguish an authentic record from the action/matter it participates in; settle with seeded overclaim F4.
- **Stale or silently transformed source material — very likely (80–90%).** Retrieval time, canonical locator, content digest, and derived-from events expose drift and undocumented transformations. Evidence: OAIS provenance/context/fixity and PREMIS events; settle with seeded faults F5–F6.
- **Unsupported absence — likely (55–80%).** A positive control plus a second detector catches dead detectors and scope mistakes, but correlated blind spots remain possible. Evidence: InterPARES’s independent-source verification principle and the package’s lineage check; settle with detector fault injections F7–F9.
- **Post-write file missing or different — almost certain (90–99%) at verification time.** A fresh path enumeration and digest directly compare expected and actual after-state. Evidence: fixity and manifest practice; settle with seeded rename/delete/mutation F10–F12.
- **Evidence gaps hidden by schema coercion or truncation — very likely (80–90%).** Explicit truncation/unknown-field preservation and fail-to-indeterminate semantics make the gap visible. Evidence: this is a design invariant, not an archival empirical result; settle with malformed/extended/truncated package cases F13–F15.

### 3.2 Explicit non-catches

- An authentic, intact source can be factually wrong. Archival authenticity does not validate its proposition.
- Two authoritative sources can repeat the same upstream error. Different URLs are not necessarily independent provenance.
- A passing test can be inadequate, nondeterministic, or aimed at the wrong requirement. The package proves the bounded event and bound state, not test quality.
- A digest made after tampering faithfully authenticates the tampered baseline. Fixity requires trustworthy capture and custody.
- A complete transcript can omit an action that occurred outside the observed bus. Package completeness is relative to the capture boundary.
- A model can phrase a material claim to evade a closed claim classifier. Requiring explicit typed material claims reduces this risk but does not solve semantic extraction.
- Secrets can leak into faithfully preserved evidence. Authenticity and retention increase the need for capture-time minimization and access control; later redaction must be a new provenance event, not silent mutation.

It is **almost certain (90–99%)** that no archive-derived mechanism can eliminate these semantic and boundary failures on its own, because the source discipline explicitly separates authenticity from accuracy and assumes a defined recordkeeping/custody boundary.

## 4. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 4.1 Where the structures genuinely match

1. **Both domains evaluate evidence produced by an interested creator.** An agent has incentive or pressure to present completion; an archive does not simply accept provenance prose as proof.
2. **Both domains are event- and context-sensitive.** A byte sequence without creator, date, action, relationships, and handling history is weak evidence.
3. **Both domains face mutable digital objects.** Copying, normalization, migration, truncation, or rendering can change the object or its interpretation.
4. **Both domains must operate at scale.** Appraisal, schedules, manifests, fixity checks, and exception queues allow machine processing without reading every item.
5. **Both domains need qualified conclusions.** InterPARES’s cumulative presumption is a better epistemic form than an absolute “verified” label.

These parallels are evidenced by InterPARES’s identity/integrity requirements, OAIS/PREMIS context and provenance packages, and NARA scheduling practice cited above.

### 4.2 Disanalogy 1: authenticity is not semantic truth

This is the largest limit. Archivists can preserve an authentic false report. The target problem’s phrase “proving an agent’s claim is true” asks for more than authenticity. The borrow succeeds only by using authentic evidence as an input to a **claim-type-specific entailment test**. Where no deterministic entailment test exists, the correct verdict is `SEMANTIC-UNVERIFIED`, not “verified by provenance.”

Consequently, replacing human reading for *all* claims is **almost certainly not (1–5%)** achievable with this mechanism. Replacing human reading for bounded operational claims and routing only exceptions is **very likely (80–90%)** achievable in principle, because status, bytes, paths, IDs, and declared predicates are machine-checkable; the actual achieved rate is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment 3 settles it.**

### 4.3 Disanalogy 2: archives assume a custodian distinct from the record creator

Archival chain of preservation gains force from controlled custody and procedural authority. A hook running as the same OS principal as an agent-controlled shell may offer observability but not adversarial tamper resistance. If the agent can edit or delete receipts, rewrite the baseline, or bypass the observed bus, the chain is self-authentication.

Therefore:

- Under an **honest-but-fallible agent** threat model, a local hook recorder is **very likely (80–90%)** useful; accidental mistakes do not normally include deliberate receipt forgery, and independent after-state checks expose many errors.
- Under a **strategic agent with the same write authority as the recorder**, a local-only package is **very unlikely (5–20%)** to provide trustworthy custody; the shared principal defeats the key archival assumption.
- Under a **host-owned event recorder and evidence store inaccessible to model-issued tools**, the chain is **very likely (80–90%)** to resist agent-side record rewriting, based on separation of custody; platform compromise and unobserved channels remain outside scope.

Whether this target harness provides the third condition is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment 2—attempted read, overwrite, append, delete, rename, hard-link/symlink substitution, and baseline replacement from every agent-accessible shell/tool—settles it.** If it does not, stop at “diagnostic evidence package” or obtain a native isolation boundary. Do not respond by building a second orchestration runtime: that violates the anti-overfit gate and still risks moving rather than solving the trust boundary.

### 4.4 Disanalogy 3: archives preserve after creation; verification benefits from active challenge

Archival appraisal and authenticity assessment are often retrospective. Agent verification can actively rerun a query, inspect after-state, inject a known-present control, or test a predicate. This makes the target easier in one respect: it can obtain fresh corroboration. It also means a passive archive of transcripts is insufficient.

The design therefore borrows the archive’s package and custody concepts but adds active, deterministic verification rules. It does not claim those rules are an archival invention.

### 4.5 Disanalogy 4: archival aggregates are relatively stable; agent traces are high-volume and recursive

Agent runs produce retries, partial results, summaries of summaries, tool wrappers, and generated evidence about evidence. Blind PREMIS-level description of every token would become a metadata explosion. The transfer works only with coarse event granularity and appraisal by claim materiality. The “archival bond” should link claims to decisive events, not become a general-purpose knowledge graph.

It is **likely (55–80%)** that coarse claim/event packages avoid metadata explosion while retaining decisive evidence, based on NARA’s selective appraisal principle and the limited schema above; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the 90-day retention replay plus per-run metadata-size measurement settles it.**

## 5. Anti-overfit gate

The recommendation passes the stated gate conditionally:

- **Existing bus:** capture is invoked by existing lifecycle/tool hooks and reads existing tool/MCP events.
- **No second runtime:** no planner, scheduler, durable executor, or agent loop is introduced.
- **No forbidden memory architecture:** content-addressed blobs and short-lived manifests are records, not vector/KG memory.
- **No trained critic:** bounded deterministic rules establish only what their evidence entails.
- **No observability SaaS or guardrail framework:** storage and verification remain a small local/native harness facility.
- **Small surface:** one event-receipt format, one claim-manifest format, and a compact verifier. If real implementation cannot remain under approximately 200 lines because target hooks lack stable events or require a service, the proposal fails its `BUILD-SMALL` tag and must return for architectural review rather than silently expanding.

The most radical justified replacement is conceptual rather than infrastructural: replace monolithic “agent self-reports with citations” verification with a layered, harness-issued evidentiary verdict. A full replacement of the orchestration harness is **unlikely (20–45%)** to be justified by this archival borrow alone, because the mechanism naturally rides the event bus and does not require control of planning or execution. Evidence: all required functions are capture, linking, hashing, deterministic checking, and disposition; none requires a new orchestration loop.

## 6. Acceptance experiments

These experiments are part of the recommendation, not optional future polish. They resolve every material **UNVERIFIED** premise in this report.

### Experiment 1 — event-field census and detector controls

For every tool family the harness intends to verify:

1. issue a known-success and known-failure operation;
2. capture hook payload, canonical transcript record, and observed after-state;
3. inventory call ID, parent/run ID, exact arguments, working directory, raw output, error channel, exit/status, truncation, timestamp, and tool identity;
4. prove the detector finds the known-present success and known-present failure before interpreting any zero result;
5. compare two mechanically different data fields or capture surfaces.

**Pass:** decisive fields are available and consistently joinable, or missing fields are explicitly scoped out.  
**Fail:** the design cannot claim complete evidence for that tool class.

### Experiment 2 — custody red-team

Create a canary receipt and manifest, then from every agent-accessible shell/tool attempt to read, overwrite, append, truncate, delete, rename, replace via symlink/hard link, alter timestamps, replace the digest baseline, and bypass event capture. Test recovery after process crash as a separate signal.

**Pass for trusted verification:** mutation/bypass is denied or independently detected by a host-owned record the agent cannot rewrite.  
**Pass only for diagnostics:** ordinary changes are detectable but a strategic agent can rewrite both evidence and baseline.  
**Fail:** receipt loss or bypass is silent.

### Experiment 3 — seeded-fault verification benchmark

Construct at least these fault classes: failed command reported as success; passing unrelated test cited; correct output from wrong revision/cwd; deleted/changed post-write file; stale source; transformed source without provenance; empty broken detector; scoped query pointed at wrong root; two “independent” checks sharing one bad cache; truncated decisive output; malformed/unknown schema fields; evidence from another run; checklist item omitted from “done.”

Randomize packages and include clean controls. Score per class:

- detection recall;
- false-positive rate;
- `INDETERMINATE` rate;
- verifier runtime and package bytes;
- percentage requiring human review;
- whether each reported absence has a successful positive control and second independent signal.

**Provisional acceptance target (UNVERIFIED; this benchmark itself settles whether the target is realistic):** at least 95% detection of seeded deterministic faults, below 2% false contradiction on clean deterministic controls, and explicit `INDETERMINATE` rather than pass for every unsupported semantic claim. The exact thresholds are a design proposal, not evidence of current performance.

### Experiment 4 — appraisal/retention replay

Replay 90 days of representative packages under candidate schedules. Measure raw and retained bytes, verifier reproducibility after compaction, investigation coverage for seeded incidents and actual correction classes, privacy exposure duration, and held-record survival. Compare retain-all, fixed functional schedule, and naive age-only deletion.

**Pass:** the functional schedule materially reduces bytes while preserving every high-impact/held package and the agreed investigation coverage.  
**Fail:** package content must be reduced at capture or retention periods changed; do not silently discard decisive evidence.

### Experiment 5 — correlated-dependency injection

Introduce a stale cache, wrapper bug, shared parser error, wrong filesystem view, and duplicated backend under pairs labeled “independent.” Confirm lineage metadata or injected outcomes reveal the correlation.

**Pass:** known shared dependencies are rejected as independent, and undocumented correlations become explicit gaps.  
**Fail:** the system may say “two signals” but may not say “mechanically independent.”

## 7. Decision rule after experiments

1. If Experiments 1 and 2 pass for trusted verification, implement the `BUILD-SMALL` CEP prototype and run Experiments 3–5.
2. If Experiment 1 fails for some tool classes, limit the claim vocabulary to observable classes and mark the rest `INDETERMINATE`; do not infer fields from agent prose.
3. If Experiment 2 passes only for diagnostics, deploy only as a reliability aid labeled **non-adversarial provenance**, not as proof “without trusting the agent.”
4. If Experiment 2 fails silently, **stop this transfer**. The central archival assumption—controlled chain of preservation—does not exist.
5. If implementing custody would require a new daemon/service/runtime, this becomes **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and is not recommended by this unit. A separate unit would have to justify that exception against the rejected-second-runtime rule.
6. If Experiment 3 shows low vocabulary coverage but strong precision, keep the narrow verifier and honest `SEMANTIC-UNVERIFIED` state; do not add a trained critic to inflate coverage.
7. If Experiment 4 shows that compaction destroys investigation value, retain more evidence or reduce capture volume; do not use subjective “no longer needed” disposal.

It is **very likely (80–90%)** that this decision rule prevents a partial implementation from being mislabeled as trusted verification, because each trust claim is conditional on a named, falsifiable boundary test. Its effectiveness in organizational practice is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is an audit in which a separate operator attempts to reproduce ten verdicts from packages alone and checks that every failed precondition is visibly downgraded.**

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just hash the transcript” — rejected

A transcript hash proves only that the hashed bytes did not change relative to a baseline. It does not establish trustworthy baseline creation, tool/result identity, context, relationships, completeness, or semantic entailment. OAIS’s multiple metadata categories and InterPARES’s identity/integrity controls show why fixity alone is insufficient.

### “Keep every event forever” — rejected

This ignores appraisal, privacy, cost, and reviewability. NARA’s schedules exist precisely to make disposition explicit and functional. Retain decisive and exceptional evidence under fixed rules; do not convert high-volume traces into indiscriminate permanent memory.

### “Have a second LLM verify the first” — rejected

That supplies another fallible semantic assertion and may share training, prompts, sources, and tool blind spots. It can help triage but is not mechanically independent evidence and would drift toward a trained/generic critic, one of the rejected components. The archival borrow calls for reliable external facts, records, and procedural controls—not merely another opinion.

### “Use a transparency ledger/blockchain” — rejected for the base design

An external append-only log could strengthen custody, but it does not solve false original content or missing capture and would expand this small hook mechanism toward a service. The base design first tests whether native host-owned event storage provides the needed boundary. Recommending a ledger before that test would violate the anti-overfit gate.

### “Model every relation in a knowledge graph” — rejected

Typed local links are enough for claim-to-evidence bonds. A general graph/ontology would reintroduce a previously rejected KG memory component and metadata overhead without improving the deterministic verdicts proposed here.

### “Do not transfer the analogy at all” — rejected, conditionally

That would discard proven machinery for identity, provenance, integrity, custody, context, appraisal, and qualified presumptions. The analogy has a real structural match for authenticating evidence packages. It must, however, be abandoned if custody isolation fails, and it must never be stretched into proof of arbitrary semantic truth.

## 9. Gaps and boundaries

- This unit intentionally did not inspect the local harness, hooks, schemas, permissions, or session corpus. Any statement about target event fields or tamper resistance is therefore marked **UNVERIFIED** inline and paired with an experiment.
- The cited InterPARES work establishes archival concepts and requirements; it is not an empirical benchmark of agent harnesses.
- ISO 15489-1:2016 remains the published standard and was confirmed in 2021, but the full standard is paywalled; this report relies on ISO’s public abstract and explanatory material for its scope ([ISO 15489-1:2016 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/62542.html); [ISO, “Records management in the digital age”](https://www.iso.org/news/2016/04/Ref2072.html)).
- No production retention duration is recommended without measured volume, privacy, legal/business need, and correction-replay coverage.
- Cryptographic signing, secure enclaves, remote attestation, and transparency services are deliberately out of scope unless the native custody experiment proves no smaller boundary exists.
- The design verifies only events observed on the declared bus. Claims involving unobserved GUI state, human action, external side effects, or nondeterministic services require a purpose-built observer or remain indeterminate.

## 10. Bottom line

Archival science’s useful contribution is not “save the transcript” and not “a checksum means true.” It is a disciplined chain:

> identify the record → preserve content plus context → record every consequential event → protect custody → test fixity → relate the record to the action and companion records → appraise by evidential value → issue a qualified, rebuttable verdict → seek independent facts when the presumption is weak.

Translated into the harness, that becomes a Claim Evidence Package and a narrow deterministic verifier. It is **very likely (80–90%)** to improve verification of bounded operational claims if the harness has an agent-inaccessible recorder boundary, based on the direct structural mapping and mature archival controls cited throughout. That boundary is **UNVERIFIED; Experiments 1 and 2 settle it.** Without the boundary, the package remains useful diagnostics but does not satisfy “without trusting the agent.” For arbitrary semantic truth, the analogy does not transfer: authenticity can faithfully preserve a lie.
