# X178 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → failure recovery

## Executive result

The analogy transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form.

Experimental design has solved an epistemic recovery problem that is structurally similar to part of agent failure recovery: after outcomes are visible, how can another person distinguish what was intended in advance from a plausible story reconstructed afterward? Its answer is to freeze a sufficiently specific protocol before outcomes, preserve departures as protocol deviations, and include controls that travel through the same measurement path. Translated to an agent harness, this becomes a small, task-local recovery contract plus an append-only evidence/deviation journal and recovery canaries. It does **not** become a second workflow engine.

The central recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce false continuation and false-"done" declarations after crashes, because a pre-crash intent record and shared-path controls directly expose those two error classes; this probability is not higher because no harness-specific fault-injection result was supplied to this ideation unit. **UNVERIFIED — settle with the preregistered, blinded crash-injection experiment specified in §8.**

The analogy does not solve exact-once external side effects, atomic storage, credential continuity, or reconstruction of state that was never recorded. Those are execution-semantics problems, whereas preregistration is an inference-discipline mechanism. Treating a protocol as a transaction log would be a category error.

### Scope and evidence boundary

This is the requested ideation lens. I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. In particular, I do not claim that the current harness lacks checkpoints, recovery hooks, or equivalent controls. Any statement about what a proposed mechanism would catch names a failure class, not a verified current implementation defect. Where prioritization depends on present-harness prevalence, it is marked **UNVERIFIED** inline with the experiment that would settle it.

External claims below cite public sources directly. The primary experimental-design anchors are:

1. Nosek et al., “The preregistration revolution,” *PNAS* (2018), full text at [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/) and bibliographic record at [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29531091/).
2. ICH E9, “Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials,” official [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e9-statistical-principles-clinical-trials) and [guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/71336/download).
3. Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen, “Negative Controls: A Tool for Detecting Confounding and Bias in Observational Studies,” full text at [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/) and record at [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20335814/).
4. Lazic, “Focus on Data: Statistical Design of Experiments and Sample Size Selection Using Power Analysis,” full text at [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425741/).
5. The Open Science Framework’s registration documentation, which describes a registration as a frozen version that cannot be edited or deleted and distinguishes preregistration before data collection/analysis from registration at other times: [OSF registration documentation](https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations).

## 1. What experimental design solved in its own field

### 1.1 Preregistration: preserve temporal provenance of intent

Nosek et al. identify a specific inferential failure: prediction and postdiction are easy to conflate once results are known. Hindsight bias makes a result-derived explanation feel as though it had been predicted. Their mechanism is not merely “write a plan.” It is:

1. state the research question, hypotheses, methods, and analysis before observing the relevant outcomes;
2. timestamp/freeze that statement so it cannot silently absorb later knowledge;
3. compare the eventual conduct and analysis with the frozen statement; and
4. label unplanned analysis as exploratory rather than disguising it as confirmatory.

That mechanism preserves the temporal provenance of a decision. Nosek et al. explicitly argue that defining questions and the analysis plan before outcomes distinguishes predictions from postdictions ([PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/)). OSF supplies the operational property: a registration is a frozen version that cannot later be edited or deleted ([OSF documentation](https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations)).

Preregistration is not a promise that the original plan was wise. Nor does it prohibit adaptation. It makes adaptation inspectable. That distinction matters for recovery: the useful property is not obedience to an old plan, but a reliable boundary between **pre-failure intent** and **post-failure reconstruction**.

### 1.2 Protocol and protocol deviations: adaptation without historical revision

ICH E9 treats a protocol as a prospective specification of the trial’s objectives, design, conduct, and planned analysis. It recommends that the principal features of the eventual statistical analysis be described in the protocol and that the statistical analysis plan be finalized before unblinding; it also treats changes and deviations as matters to document rather than silently fold back into the original design ([FDA E9 page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e9-statistical-principles-clinical-trials), [official PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/71336/download)).

The own-field mechanism is therefore a three-way classification:

- **planned**: specified prospectively;
- **deviation/amendment**: changed after the protocol, with timing and rationale visible;
- **exploratory**: learned or chosen after observing outcomes and reported as such.

This is more useful than an undifferentiated activity log. It says not only what happened, but how the epistemic status of an action changed.

### 1.3 Negative controls: make hidden bias produce an observable contradiction

Lipsitch et al. define the essential negative-control pattern precisely: reproduce a condition that cannot involve the hypothesized causal mechanism but is likely to share the same sources of bias. A non-null result in that condition indicates that bias, confounding, measurement error, selection, or analytic error may explain the main result ([PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).

Two details are crucial:

- The control must share the relevant error-generating path. A trivial check outside that path can pass while the real measurement is corrupt.
- A failed negative control is a sensitive but blunt alarm. It indicates that the inference is suspect; it generally does not identify or quantify the contaminating mechanism.

Experimental biology often creates the negative condition by leaving out an essential ingredient or inactivating the hypothesized active ingredient. Epidemiology translates that into negative-control exposures or outcomes chosen to share confounders while lacking the proposed causal link. The transferable abstraction is: **construct an invariant whose violation reveals an otherwise latent error in the same pipeline**.

### 1.4 Blinding: prevent outcome knowledge from steering judgment

ICH E9 describes blinding as one of the most important design techniques for avoiding bias in clinical trials. It also discusses blind review: decisions affecting analysis should be made before the blind is broken, so knowledge of treatment assignments does not steer exclusions, transformations, or analytic choices ([official FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/71336/download)).

In its own field, blinding works because some information is unnecessary for the decision being made but can bias that decision. Blinding is therefore not “withhold context” generically. It is a controlled partition: retain information needed for competent judgment; hide assignment/outcome information capable of contaminating that judgment.

### 1.5 Prospective power analysis: decide in advance what evidence can settle the question

Power analysis links the minimum effect worth detecting, outcome variability, false-positive threshold, desired probability of detection, and required sample size. Lazic explains that required sample size grows when the meaningful effect becomes smaller relative to variability or desired power becomes higher ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425741/)). The NIH’s research-methods resource likewise requires the type-I error rate, desired power, outcome distribution, design, and analysis plan for sample-size calculation ([NIH calculators](https://researchmethodsresources.nih.gov/Calculators)).

The mechanism prevents a common non-result from being overstated: “we did not see a difference” is not evidence of equivalence when the study could not reliably detect the smallest difference that matters. Power analysis is prospective budgeting of evidence, not a ritual performed after seeing results.

## 2. Structural mapping to failure recovery

The mapping is about epistemic continuity, not process continuity.

| Experimental-design object | Recovery object | Shared structure | Transfer strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen preregistration | Immutable task-intent record written before execution | Later observer must separate prior intent from outcome-informed reconstruction | Strong for intent; irrelevant to unrecorded machine state |
| Protocol/SAP | Acceptance criteria, planned phases, verification plan, side-effect policy | A successor needs a prospective decision rule, not only prose about activity | Strong if specific and cheap to update |
| Protocol deviation/amendment | Append-only recovery deviation with reason and evidence | Adaptation is allowed without rewriting what was originally intended | Strong |
| Exploratory label | Recovery hypothesis / newly discovered work | New work can continue without being misrepresented as original scope | Strong |
| Negative control | Known-finished, known-unfinished, and known-forbidden recovery canaries | Shared-path invariant reveals a recovery classifier or verifier that is lying | Moderate to strong, conditional on pipeline comparability |
| Blinding | Evaluator does not know recovery method or checkpoint’s claimed status | Outcome/assignment knowledge can bias adjudication | Strong for evaluation; harmful in live recovery if needed context is hidden |
| Power analysis | Prospective fault-injection sample size and stopping rule | A null test result is interpretable only if the experiment could detect a meaningful regression | Strong for evaluation; no direct runtime benefit |

The key shared assumptions are present:

1. **There is a before/after knowledge boundary.** Before a worker fails, it has intent and observations; after failure, a successor sees artifacts and may rationalize them.
2. **A later actor has discretion.** Both analysts and recovery workers can select interpretations, exclusions, or next actions after seeing partial outcomes.
3. **Historical revision is dangerous.** Rewriting a checkpoint to make the recovered path look planned destroys evidence needed to diagnose drift.
4. **Some errors are latent but can be made observable through controls.** A successor that declares everything complete may look productive until a deliberately unfinished canary is misclassified.

The analogy is therefore **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally valid for detecting reconstruction bias and evidentiary drift; the evidence is the direct correspondence between the before/after information boundary described by Nosek et al. and the pre-/post-crash boundary. It is only **unlikely (20-45%)** to solve the broader reliability problem by itself, because none of the experimental-design mechanisms provide atomic commits, idempotency, locking, or replay semantics; that disanalogy is mechanical rather than empirical.

## 3. Concrete harness design

### Proposal A — task-local preregistered recovery contract

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

At task start, emit a compact `intent.json` beside task artifacts. The record is content-addressed or written with an immutable generation identifier. It should contain only fields required to decide safe continuation:

```json
{
  "schema": 1,
  "task_id": "...",
  "generation": 1,
  "objective": "...",
  "scope": {"allowed": [], "forbidden": []},
  "acceptance": [{"claim": "...", "evidence_required": "..."}],
  "inputs": [{"identity": "...", "version_or_hash": "..."}],
  "phases": [{"id": "...", "exit_evidence": "..."}],
  "side_effects": [{"kind": "...", "idempotency_key": "...", "replay": "never|verify|safe"}],
  "verification": [{"probe": "...", "independent_signal": "..."}],
  "controls": [{"id": "...", "expected": "..."}]
}
```

The contract must be frozen by generation. A legitimate scope change creates `intent.g2.json` referencing the prior generation and recording the reason; it does not mutate `intent.json`. This borrows OSF’s frozen-registration property, not its public hosting model.

The contract is **likely (55-80%)** to help a successor distinguish original scope from post-crash invention, because the comparison is direct and does not depend on the dead worker’s narrative. The evidence is Nosek et al.’s prediction/postdiction distinction and OSF’s immutable snapshot semantics. **UNVERIFIED — settle by injecting crashes after a hidden scope change and measuring successor scope-drift against transcript-only recovery.**

What it catches:

- a successor silently redefining “done” to fit artifacts already present;
- a successor dropping a difficult acceptance criterion;
- a successor treating a newly discovered subproblem as though it had been part of the original mandate;
- replay of an external effect explicitly marked `never` or `verify`;
- recovery against inputs whose identity/version has changed.

What it cannot catch:

- incorrect acceptance criteria frozen at the start;
- work completed but never evidenced;
- external side effects not listed in advance;
- a malicious or careless writer that fabricates both the intent and later journal unless storage provenance is independently protected.

### Proposal B — append-only evidence and protocol-deviation journal

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Use an existing lifecycle hook to append one small NDJSON record at semantic boundaries, not on every token or tool call. Suggested events are:

- `phase_started`;
- `claim_evidenced` with artifact identity/hash and probe result;
- `side_effect_attempted` / `side_effect_observed` with idempotency key;
- `checkpoint` with current phase and explicit next action;
- `deviation` with old rule, new action, reason, and whether the change was known before or after observing the relevant outcome;
- `exploratory_work_opened`;
- `recovery_started` with predecessor generation and journal tail hash.

Each record links to the previous record’s hash. Write a new complete record via the platform’s normal atomic-file primitive or append discipline; never treat a partially written tail as valid. A recovery reader walks to the last valid linked event and labels any later bytes `incomplete`, not `completed`.

This is not event sourcing as a new runtime. The journal is evidence for a model or human successor; it is not an executable command log and must not automatically replay actions.

The journal is **likely (55-80%)** to expose silent plan drift and ambiguous in-flight work, because protocol deviations become first-class records and torn tails fail linkage. **UNVERIFIED — settle with kill-at-boundary and kill-during-write fault injection, comparing false-complete and duplicate-side-effect rates with and without the journal.** The probability is capped because filesystem atomicity and available hook boundaries are implementation-specific and were outside this ideation unit.

What it catches that ordinary “resume from the last assistant message” can miss:

- a tool action began but never produced verified evidence;
- the worker changed method after seeing a failing result;
- a “done” sentence lacks the acceptance evidence specified beforehand;
- the latest checkpoint was torn or belongs to an older intent generation;
- an external side effect has uncertain outcome and therefore must be observed, not retried blindly.

### Proposal C — shared-path recovery controls

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add three control records to each recoverable test task:

1. **Known-finished positive control.** A phase has an artifact plus independent verification evidence. Recovery must classify it `complete-evidenced`.
2. **Known-unfinished negative control.** A phase is intentionally left with `phase_started` and no exit evidence. Recovery must classify it `in-flight/unknown`, never complete.
3. **Known-forbidden side-effect control.** The intent explicitly forbids or marks non-replayable a synthetic effect; recovery must not issue it and must report the prohibition.

These controls must enter through the same intent writer, journal parser, checkpoint selection, and recovery prompt as real phases. A separate unit test that bypasses the model or parser is not a negative control in the Lipsitch sense because it does not share the relevant sources of error.

The controls are **very likely (80-90%)** to catch the degenerate policies “mark every prior phase done” and “retry every incomplete action,” because the expected classifications directly contradict those policies and traverse the same recovery path. The evidence is the negative-control requirement to share bias sources while removing the claimed mechanism ([Lipsitch et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). They are only **likely (55-80%)** to catch subtler recovery defects, because comparability between a synthetic control and real task state may be weak. **UNVERIFIED — settle by seeding a taxonomy of corruptions (torn tail, stale generation, missing artifact, changed input, uncertain external effect) and measuring per-class control sensitivity.**

A failed control is a stop signal, not a diagnosis. Recovery should quarantine the completion claim and report which invariant failed. It should not infer from one failed canary that all work is corrupt, nor infer the magnitude of corruption from the canary; that follows the source field’s warning that negative controls are blunt detectors.

### Proposal D — recovery decision procedure

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Give every recovery worker the same short procedure:

1. Resolve the newest valid intent generation and verify its link to its predecessor.
2. Validate a known-present control first, proving that the detector can recognize evidence.
3. Validate the known-unfinished negative control.
4. Reconstruct phases from evidence records and material artifacts, not from status prose alone.
5. Assign exactly one status to each phase: `complete-evidenced`, `not-started`, `in-flight/unknown`, `deviation`, or `blocked`.
6. For `in-flight/unknown`, inspect the declared replay policy. Observe external state before any retry.
7. Record any changed plan as a new deviation/amendment with rationale.
8. Continue from one explicit next action.
9. Re-run acceptance probes before declaring completion.

Step 2 is intentionally ordered before absence claims. It applies the owner’s two-signal detector rule in recovery form: first prove that the evidence resolver can find a known-present item; then pair journal evidence with a mechanically independent material-state probe before saying an artifact or side effect is absent.

The procedure is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce confident continuation from a bad checkpoint because it forces validation before execution and has an explicit unknown state. **UNVERIFIED — settle by comparing unsafe-action and false-absence rates in randomized, blinded recovery trials.**

## 4. What does not transfer

### 4.1 A preregistration is not a checkpoint

A scientific protocol records planned decisions. A computational checkpoint records sufficient execution state to continue. The former can be semantically excellent while omitting a process identifier, unflushed buffer, lock, environment variable, remote transaction state, or intermediate model output. Therefore:

- do not advertise `intent.json` as restorable machine state;
- do not infer that an action is safe to repeat merely because it was planned;
- do not auto-execute the journal;
- do not claim exactly-once behavior without an idempotency/transaction mechanism supplied by the affected system.

This disanalogy is decisive. The borrow improves the *truthfulness and auditability of recovery decisions*, not the atomicity of execution.

### 4.2 Live recovery should not be blinded

Withholding treatment assignment can remove irrelevant biasing information from a clinical judgment. A recovery worker often needs the original objective, artifacts, environment identity, side-effect history, and uncertainty flags to act safely. Blinding it to those facts would increase risk.

Accordingly, live-recovery blinding is rejected. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to be harmful when it hides replay policy or external-effect evidence, because those fields directly determine whether retrying is safe. This is a structural judgment supported by the semantics of the proposed decision procedure, not a measured harness result. **UNVERIFIED — a sandboxed replay study could settle the magnitude, but no production trial is warranted before sandbox evidence.**

Blinding belongs only in evaluation (§7), where an adjudicator can be kept unaware of treatment assignment and recovery method while still seeing all artifacts required to score predefined outcomes.

### 4.3 Power analysis does not make one recovery safer

Power analysis determines whether a series of trials can detect a meaningful effect. It contributes nothing to a particular crashed task at runtime. Adding sample-size calculations to every checkpoint would be ceremony. Use it only when deciding whether evidence justifies enabling the mechanism broadly.

### 4.4 Negative controls require comparability

A toy canary with no external calls cannot validate recovery of uncertain external side effects. A local text artifact cannot validate recovery across a machine loss if it is stored only on that machine. A control that shares the label but not the failure path can produce false reassurance.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a single generic canary would generalize across materially different task classes, because the negative-control paper makes shared sources of bias a necessary design condition and task classes have different state and side-effect channels. The appropriate response is stratified controls, not a universal “recovery health” bit.

### 4.5 Frozen plans can fossilize mistakes

Preregistration increases transparency, not plan quality. Recovery must allow amendments and exploratory work. A rule that mechanically forces the successor to follow the frozen plan would discard new information and could repeat the dead worker’s error. The contract is a comparison baseline, not a prison.

## 5. Failure classes made visible

The combined mechanism targets the following classes:

| Failure class | Why it is otherwise hard to see | Experimental-design borrow that exposes it | Required independent confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconstructed-intent drift | A coherent post-crash plan can look original | Frozen intent generation | Compare timestamp/hash chain with later deviation record |
| False completion | Narrative status can outrun material evidence | Prospective acceptance criterion + known-unfinished control | Artifact/probe result independent of journal status |
| Silent abandonment | An initiated phase disappears from summary | `phase_started` without exit evidence | Material-state probe for expected output |
| Unsafe duplicate side effect | Crash occurs between remote commit and local acknowledgement | Prospective replay policy + uncertain state | Query remote system by idempotency key; never rely only on local journal |
| Torn/stale checkpoint | Last write exists but is partial or from old scope | Linked immutable generations and journal-tail validation | Intent generation plus artifact/environment identity |
| Post-outcome method switching | Changed approach is rewritten as original | Deviation/amendment classification | Compare frozen plan with timestamped journal |
| Biased evaluation | Evaluator knows which mechanism is “new” | Blinded adjudication | Predefined scoring rubric and concealed assignment |
| Underpowered rollout decision | A few clean demos are treated as proof | Prospective MDE, power, and stopping rule | Held-out crash trials with confidence interval |

Whether these are frequent in the present harness is **UNVERIFIED — settle by labeling a random sample of real resumed tasks and by controlled crash injection at each semantic boundary.** No prevalence conclusion is made here.

## 6. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

All proposals pass the requested anti-overfit gate:

- They ride existing lifecycle hooks, skills, or the MCP/tool bus.
- State is task-local JSON/NDJSON plus hashes of material artifacts.
- There is no second orchestration runtime.
- There is no vector database or knowledge graph.
- There is no durable-execution engine.
- There is no trained critic.
- There is no observability SaaS.
- There is no guardrail framework.

The intentionally omitted “radical” design is a recovery daemon that owns leases, persists every event, and replays workflows. That would be **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and would reintroduce the specifically rejected durable-execution/second-runtime components. The experimental-design borrow supplies no evidence that such infrastructure is necessary. A plain contract, a small append hook, and a recovery skill are the smallest falsifiable version.

The minimal implementation is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve most of the epistemic benefit, because the benefit depends on temporal immutability, explicit evidence, and controls rather than scheduling sophistication. **UNVERIFIED — settle by implementing the minimal version behind an experiment flag and measuring the primary outcomes below before considering any larger runtime.**

## 7. Correct role of blinding: evaluation, not operation

### Proposal E — blinded adjudication of recovery outcomes

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For an evaluation corpus, create paired recovery attempts using baseline and proposed recovery conditions. Randomize opaque condition IDs. Give adjudicators:

- the original task and frozen acceptance rubric;
- final artifacts and externally observed side effects;
- safety violations and elapsed/tool-cost data;
- no label identifying baseline versus proposed method;
- no checkpoint sentence asserting “done.”

Adjudicators score evidence against the preregistered rubric. A separate script reveals assignment only after scores are fixed. If human adjudication is used, randomize order and avoid showing paired outputs adjacent when stylistic signatures could reveal the method. If a model adjudicator is used, rotate presentation order and retain objective probes as the primary evidence; “model-as-judge” is not mechanically independent from another model’s prose.

Blinding is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce evaluator expectancy bias when outputs differ subtly, because treatment identity is unnecessary to score predefined acceptance evidence and ICH E9 identifies blinding as a primary bias-control technique. **UNVERIFIED — settle by measuring inter-rater disagreement and score changes after labels are revealed.** It cannot blind obvious stylistic or structural signatures, so report whether adjudicators guessed condition above chance.

## 8. Preregistered, powered falsification experiment

### Proposal F — fault-injection evaluation with prospective stopping rule

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

The proposal itself should be tested using the source field’s discipline.

### 8.1 Freeze before running

Publish a read-only experiment manifest before any scored trial containing:

- hypothesis and smallest worthwhile effect;
- task sampling frame and exclusions;
- randomized assignment procedure;
- crash-point matrix;
- primary and secondary outcomes;
- scoring code/version;
- sample-size calculation and stopping rule;
- control cases;
- treatment of missing or indeterminate trials;
- planned subgroup analyses;
- rule that any post-result analysis is labeled exploratory.

### 8.2 Crash-point strata

Sample at least these structural boundaries:

1. before any action;
2. after plan creation but before tool execution;
3. after a local write begins;
4. after local write completes but before verification;
5. after an external request is sent but before acknowledgement;
6. after acknowledgement but before local journal update;
7. after evidence is produced but before the completion message;
8. during checkpoint/journal write;
9. after input or environment identity changes;
10. whole-session or simulated machine loss with only declared durable artifacts retained.

Do not pool away the uncertain-external-effect stratum. It is qualitatively different from local idempotent work.

### 8.3 Primary outcomes

Use two co-primary outcomes, with multiplicity handling stated in advance:

- **useful recovery rate**: task ultimately meets all frozen acceptance criteria without forbidden side effects;
- **false-done rate**: recovery declares completion while at least one frozen criterion lacks required evidence.

Safety-critical secondary outcomes:

- duplicate non-idempotent side effects;
- scope drift;
- lost completed work;
- time/tool calls to verified completion;
- abstention/`unknown` rate;
- control sensitivity and false-alarm rate by failure stratum.

Do not optimize only completion rate. A method can “recover” more tasks by confidently repeating uncertain side effects or relaxing criteria.

### 8.4 Negative and positive controls

Include, hidden among real phases:

- a genuinely completed, independently verified phase;
- a started-but-unfinished phase;
- a stale checkpoint from an older intent generation;
- a correctly formed current checkpoint;
- a deliberately torn tail record;
- a synthetic external effect whose remote state says “committed” while local acknowledgement is absent;
- a synthetic effect whose remote state says “not committed.”

The detector must first find the known-present current checkpoint and completed phase. Only then may its absence findings be scored. This both validates detector sensitivity and provides two mechanically independent signals for absence/broken claims.

### 8.5 Power and sample size

Choose the smallest operationally meaningful reduction in false-done rate and improvement in useful recovery rate **before** collecting scored outcomes. Estimate baseline rate and between-task variance from an unscored pilot. Then calculate sample size for the chosen design, alpha/multiplicity rule, and desired power; block or pair by task where feasible. The source-field rationale is that sample size depends on effect size, variance, significance threshold, and desired power ([Lazic](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425741/); [NIH calculators](https://researchmethodsresources.nih.gov/Calculators)).

No numeric sample size is asserted here. A number without baseline variance, task clustering, MDE, and allocation would be fabricated precision. **UNVERIFIED — the unscored pilot and frozen analysis code are the experiment that settle the required N.**

### 8.6 Decision rule

Adopt the mechanism only if it improves useful recovery without worsening the preregistered safety bound for duplicate effects or false-done declarations. Report effect sizes and uncertainty intervals, not only thresholded significance. Preserve deviations and all stopped/indeterminate trials in the result set.

The experiment is **very likely (80-90%)** to falsify at least degenerate recovery policies because hidden known-finished and known-unfinished controls have deterministic expected states. It is **likely (55-80%)** to estimate production benefit if the task and crash-point sampling frame resembles production; the evidence is the blocked, randomized, prospective design, while the remaining uncertainty is external validity. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to establish exactly-once safety for arbitrary external services, because synthetic and sampled services cannot cover every remote transaction contract.

## 9. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just checkpoint the conversation”

Rejected as insufficient. A transcript preserves statements but does not prospectively separate intent, evidence, deviation, and exploration. It also encourages a successor to inherit an unverified “done” claim. It remains useful raw evidence, but not the recovery contract.

### “Snapshot everything after every tool call”

Rejected for this borrow. It increases state volume and couples recovery to low-level execution without providing exactly-once semantics. Semantic boundary records are cheaper and align with protocol decisions. If later fault injection shows that semantic boundaries miss critical state, expand only the demonstrated boundary.

### “Have another model critique the recovering model”

Rejected as the primary control. A critic can share the same narrative bias and is not a mechanically independent signal. Material probes, remote idempotency queries, hashes, and hidden known-state controls are stronger. This also avoids the previously rejected trained-critic architecture.

### “Use one universal recovery score”

Rejected. It would hide asymmetric harms and violate the negative-control requirement for shared error paths. False-done, duplicate effect, useful completion, and abstention must remain separately visible, stratified by crash class.

### “Force strict adherence to the frozen plan”

Rejected. It mistakes transparency for immutability of action. Amendments are necessary; silent revision is the target.

### “Install a durable workflow engine”

Rejected under the anti-overfit gate. It is a **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** proposal and duplicates orchestration. Nothing in the cited experimental-design mechanisms establishes that this cost is necessary to gain the intent/evidence/control benefits.

## 10. Recommended build order and kill criteria

1. **CONFIG (settings/JSON only):** define the minimal intent/deviation schema and recovery status vocabulary.
2. **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** append semantic events and verify generation/hash linkage.
3. **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** add known-finished, known-unfinished, stale, and torn controls to a sandbox test set.
4. **CONFIG (settings/JSON only):** encode the nine-step recovery decision procedure in the existing recovery skill/rules surface.
5. **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** run randomized crash injection and blinded adjudication.
6. Enable more broadly only after the preregistered decision rule passes.

Kill or redesign the mechanism if any of the following occurs:

- writing the journal materially increases task failures or latency beyond the preregistered bound;
- controls do not share real failure paths and therefore have low sensitivity;
- successors follow stale plans despite visible amendments;
- journal presence increases blind replay of external effects;
- false-done does not improve at the smallest worthwhile effect despite adequate power;
