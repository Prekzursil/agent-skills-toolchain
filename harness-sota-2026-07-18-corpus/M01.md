# M01 — Trust / autonomy calibration: irreversible-operation gating at the right level

**Unit:** M01  
**Family:** trust / autonomy calibration  
**Question:** Which real operations in this workspace should proceed autonomously, which should be made reversible first, and which should stop at an owner-controlled commit boundary?  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Subject mode:** read-only; only this artifact was written.

## Executive decision

Do **not** add a general “dangerous command” blocker and do **not** ask about every write. Replace the current underspecified binary distinction with one effect policy enforced inside the existing shared `PreToolUse` dispatcher:

1. **Observe** — no durable mutation: proceed.
2. **Prepare** — private mutation with an independently proven rollback: proceed, checkpoint automatically, then verify through an execution result.
3. **Commit** — action creates a non-private obligation, spends non-trivial money, crosses a trust boundary, changes security authority, or has no independently proven restoration path: ask once on the exact transaction.
4. **Refuse derived catastrophe** — secrets egress, unbounded destructive scope, protected-history destruction, or safety-control disabling may run only when the owner explicitly names that exact action and target; an agent may never infer this authority from “do everything,” “finish,” or a plan approval.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve both autonomy and safety because the local corpus shows enormous volumes of ordinary reads and edits that would be harmed by blanket confirmation, while live incidents prove payload-regex guards manufacture false danger. The evidence is 61,165 `Read`, 28,964 `Edit`, 12,845 `Write`, and 108,121 generic `Bash` calls in the corpus (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:15-52`), alongside the live defect report’s independently diagnosed use-versus-mention false fires (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-22`, `:54-82`). The remaining uncertainty is how much prompt load the proposed boundary removes in live sessions; Finding 8 specifies the external adjudication experiment.

The architecture passes the anti-overfit gate. It adds no orchestration runtime, memory store, vector database, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It folds into the already registered shared dispatcher at `PreToolUse` (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json:139-149`) and uses the existing event ledger, edit checkpoints/rollback, `AskUserQuestion`, and `OPEN-THREADS.md`. It adds **zero hooks** and therefore stays under the approximately six-hook ceiling.

## Evidence discipline and scope

The corpus is unusually complete for transcript-level behavior: it distilled 10.62 GB, saw 11,633 files, failed on zero, and had one bad line (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). Its tool-call counts are ground truth for operations recorded in Claude transcripts. Its `_meta` also says samples are capped and that absence claims require checking failures/bad lines (`:11`); therefore this report uses the corpus to prove presence and relative frequency, not to claim that an unobserved operation is impossible.

The corpus `hooks` section is not used. The pinned brief marks its detector broken, and the live defect report independently says hook telemetry is not established in transcripts (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-147`).

The 2,268 “owner correction candidates” are not treated as 2,268 corrections. The corpus itself warns that the detector is recall-biased and that 41/41 items in an unfiltered smoke sample were false positives (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10371-10374`). The reliable owner signals used here are instead (a) explicit instructions in this unit, (b) the four live defects diagnosed with two independent signals (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:1-5`), and (c) repeated top-pattern requests to continue rather than stop, treated only as supporting evidence because the correction detector is noisy (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10377-10383`).

The prior 140-unit audit is treated as a lead generator, not fact. For example, audit F02 claims authenticated outbound MCP sinks and a no-approval Codex mode (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/audit-findings/F02.md:1-21`), but this artifact rests only on independently visible settings and corpus tool names unless it explicitly says **UNVERIFIED**.

## The real operation placement

The policy unit is not the tool name. It is the resolved operation tuple:

`(verb, target, data provenance, destination/audience, authority change, estimated cost, rollback receipt, scope)`

That distinction matters because `Bash`, `PowerShell`, browser JavaScript, `evaluate_lua`, and meta-tools can each perform either a read or a destructive mutation. The corpus records 108,121 Bash calls at 5.98% error, 19,181 PowerShell calls at 10.59% error, and 660 Cheat Engine `evaluate_lua` calls (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:15-44`, `:159-164`). A tool-name allow/deny list alone therefore cannot place the real action.

## Findings — inferred-surprise first

### Finding 1 — Move the gate from commands to the externally observable commitment

**Improvement.** Introduce the four-state `OBSERVE / PREPARE / COMMIT / REFUSE-DERIVED` effect policy above, and make **COMMIT** the only normal confirmation boundary.

**What it is.** The agent may research, plan, draft, edit locally, test, checkpoint, and prepare an exact outbound transaction without interruption. It pauses immediately before the first effect that cannot be privately and reliably restored: external send/post/upload, protected/shared history change, production release, non-trivial spend, authority expansion, or mutation without a proven rollback. Approval covers the exact action digest—not the agent’s general plan and not later materially different actions.

**Why inferred.** The owner explicitly wants ambitious autonomy but identifies prompt fatigue and false blocking as regressions. The corpus contains 121,809 ordinary `Edit`/`Write`/`Read` calls and 127,302 shell calls (`corpus.json:15-52`); prompting at tool granularity would consume attention on the dominant workload. Conversely, the live report shows prose about destructive commands being mistaken for execution (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-47`). The surprising conclusion is that the right boundary is neither “before tools” nor “before writes,” but at the resolved commitment.

**How to build.** Add an `EffectDecision` record to the existing shared dispatcher output: `effect_class`, `verb`, `resolved_targets`, `sink/audience`, `authority_delta`, `cost_ceiling`, `rollback_receipt`, `policy_rule_id`, and `action_digest`. Use deterministic adapters for known native tools and MCP methods. Shell adapters parse only the executable AST/argv and redirection targets, never quoted payload prose. Unknown mutations without a restoration receipt become `COMMIT`; unknown read-only calls proceed. Keep the host hook fail-open on code error, but inject a visible `GATE-ERROR:<id>` ledger receipt so the owner can see that policy was not applied. This is one module in the existing dispatcher, not a new hook.

**External signal that verifies it.** A table-driven test corpus must contain known-positive real actions and known-negative mentions: the nine live false fires in `INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:54-82`, one real recursive delete, one real protected force-push, one markdown quotation of each, one MCP upload, and one MCP read. Run the classifier as a separate process and compare its machine output to owner-adjudicated labels. Then run provider-native disposable probes: local edit/rollback, owned test-repo branch push, owned HTTP canary upload, and protected-ref simulated block. Same-context model review does not count.

**SOTA.** Google PAIR says automate more when risk is low and retain user control in high-stakes cases, explicitly tying automation to failure tolerance ([PAIR design patterns](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/patterns)). OpenAI’s current agent guide recommends tool risk ratings based on read/write access, reversibility, permissions, and financial impact, with human intervention for sensitive, irreversible, or high-stakes actions ([OpenAI practical guide](https://openai.com/business/guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-ai-agents/)). NIST calls for context-specific risk tolerances and documented human oversight rather than a universal checklist ([NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)).

**Expected effect.** **Very likely (80-90%)** high-value: local frequency evidence and the confirmed use-versus-mention defect both point in the same direction; uncertainty remains around the proportion of shell calls whose effect can be resolved cheaply.

**Value x effort:** transformative x medium  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise  
**Autonomy:** confirm-first (changing the global authority boundary itself requires owner acceptance)  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

### Finding 2 — Convert danger into reversible preparation before asking

**Improvement.** Build “reversibility transformers” that stage the safe form automatically: checkpoint instead of raw overwrite, trash/quarantine manifest instead of deletion, draft instead of send, preview/draft release instead of publish, feature branch instead of protected branch, and bounded job plan instead of immediate expensive submission.

**What it is.** Confirmation is not the first response to a risky intention. The harness first performs every private reversible step that narrows uncertainty, then presents the owner with the exact residual commitment: target list, diff/payload hash, recipient/environment, cost ceiling, rollback status, and strongest evidence against proceeding. If the operation can be fully transformed into a private reversible action, no prompt is needed.

**Why inferred.** The stopped orphan-plugin deletion demonstrates that “delete marked orphans” was not actually known-reversible: 110/169 had no live sibling and one marked directory served a live skill (pinned measured ground truth). The existing harness already has edit checkpoints+/rollback, so the missing capability is not another checkpoint system; it is routing actions through reversible representations before asking. The owner’s quality bar rejects dead or half-wired controls, making a draft-only button insufficient unless the same adapter can commit the staged object after approval.

**How to build.** Extend existing edit-checkpoints with adapters:

- filesystem: copy metadata + content hash to the existing checkpoint store or move to OS trash/quarantine; record a manifest; sample-restore one file before bulk execution;
- git: create owner-scoped branch/worktree and local commit; resolve downstream CI/deploy triggers before push;
- communication: create local/provider draft where supported; hash rendered recipient/body/attachments; approval executes the native send on that hash;
- deploy/publish: create preview/draft and capture provider diff; approval promotes the same immutable artifact digest;
- compute: estimate from provider/job parameters, attach ceiling and cancel condition; submit only if within lease.

**UNVERIFIED** whether every connected communication/deploy provider exposes a true draft or immutable promotion primitive; the settling experiment is a schema inventory followed by one disposable draft/promote probe per active provider. Where it does not, preparation remains local and COMMIT sends the exact hashed payload.

**External signal that verifies it.** Restoration must be tested by a separate executable/process against a disposable tree; remote draft/promotion must return provider IDs and read back matching hashes; compute must return a provider run ID and later terminal/cancel state. A model saying “backup created” is rejected.

**SOTA.** PAIR recommends low-risk reversible experimentation and says automation should retain a way to undo or choose before high-stakes action ([PAIR mental models](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/mental-models/), [PAIR automation patterns](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/patterns)). Microsoft’s validated HAI guidelines require efficient correction/dismissal and scoping the service when uncertain ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)).

**Expected effect.** **Likely (55-80%)** to remove a material fraction of confirmations while improving decision quality; evidence is strong for local/git transformations, but provider draft support is **UNVERIFIED** by the schema/probe experiment above.

**Value x effort:** transformative x medium  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it for preparation; confirm-first for the residual COMMIT  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

### Finding 3 — Approve a transaction digest, never a persuasive agent summary

**Improvement.** Make approval dialogs non-generative and bound to the exact transaction.

**What it is.** The owner sees a compact, deterministic card generated from tool arguments and resolved state: `ACTION`, `TARGET/AUDIENCE`, `DATA CLASS`, `DIFF OR PAYLOAD HASH`, `COST CEILING`, `ROLLBACK`, `WHY NOW`, and `EXPIRES`. The agent may provide optional rationale below it, but cannot alter the authoritative fields. Approval returns a single-use token tied to `action_digest`; any material change invalidates it.

**Why inferred.** This owner runs long autonomous and multi-agent workflows. A plan approval is too broad: child agents and late discoveries can change recipients, targets, or scope. The live defect report also shows an orchestrator-fabricated constraint propagated into about 15 prompts (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`), proving agent-authored framing cannot be authorization ground truth.

**How to build.** Canonicalize operation arguments, resolved target IDs, content/attachment hashes, active account/environment, and policy version into a SHA-256 digest. Call existing `AskUserQuestion` once with the deterministic card; store `{digest, decision, expiry, owner_message_id}` in the event ledger. The executor re-computes the digest immediately before COMMIT. Tokens are single-use by default; an explicit lease may authorize a bounded set (Finding 5). Do not pass fetched text, tool output prose, or agent-generated markdown into authoritative dialog fields.

**External signal that verifies it.** Mutation tests change recipient, attachment, target ref, environment, payload byte, cost, and account after approval; a separate executor must reject all changed digests and accept the exact unchanged digest once. The provider’s post-action ID/read-back closes the real-world loop.

**SOTA.** OWASP recommends complete mediation and downstream approval of high-impact extension actions, including manual review before email send ([OWASP LLM06 Excessive Agency](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/)). OWASP separately documents approval-dialog forging as an attack, reinforcing that the agent’s persuasive prose must not define what is being authorized ([OWASP Lies-in-the-Loop](https://owasp.org/www-community/attacks/Lies_in_the_Loop)).

**Expected effect.** **Very likely (80-90%)** to prevent approval drift because digest mutation tests are deterministic and the orchestrator-propagation incident supplies direct local motivation; usability impact remains to be measured in Finding 8.

**Value x effort:** high x low  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise  
**Autonomy:** confirm-first  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

### Finding 4 — Preserve attention with one just-in-time boundary, not repeated micro-approvals

**Improvement.** Batch causally related commits into one owner decision at the highest strategy level that still has exact bounded effects.

**What it is.** A batch may contain “push feature branch + open draft PR” or “send these three already rendered files to this named recipient,” but may not contain wildcards such as “all future actions in this session.” The card lists every external effect. Low-risk preparation continues without interruption. If the owner does not answer, the commitment remains pending and the agent continues independent safe work; it never converts silence into consent.

**Why inferred.** Prompt fatigue is a hard constraint, not a cosmetic issue. The corpus records 1,329 `AskUserQuestion` calls (`corpus.json:135-140`), while top correction-candidate patterns repeatedly ask the agent to proceed and not stop (`corpus.json:10377-10383`; treated cautiously because `:10374` warns the detector is noisy). The owner’s focus is a scarce safety resource: spending it on reversible edits makes it less available for a true external commitment.

**How to build.** The dispatcher accumulates only already-prepared COMMIT intents with the same goal ID, account/environment, and risk class. At the next unavoidable boundary, render one deterministic transaction card. Cap cards at 3-5 effects; split larger batches by sink or blast radius. Provide `approve exact batch`, `edit scope`, and `deny`—no “always allow everything.” Use native notification once; do not add a hook.

**External signal that verifies it.** Compare two owner-adjudicated shadow cohorts in the event ledger: current prompts versus proposed deduplicated boundaries. Measure prompts per completed goal, false-block overturn rate, approval response time, denied-action catch rate, and unfinished pending threads. Owner adjudication is the truth label; same-model self-rating is forbidden.

**SOTA.** Anthropic reports that repeated prompts create friction and can be tuned out, and describes moving oversight from each action to overall strategy while retaining intervention ([Anthropic, “Trustworthy agents in practice”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/trustworthy-agents)). A 2026 agent-oversight preprint models human attention as finite and reports moderate reviewer agreement (Fleiss’ kappa 0.52) plus an inverted-U between escalation load and realized safety; its fatigue result is a modeling result awaiting a human study, so it is supporting rather than decisive evidence ([“Oversight Has a Capacity”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919)).

**Expected effect.** **Likely (55-80%)** to reduce prompt fatigue without sacrificing material control; Anthropic’s product evidence and local ask/proceed signals support it, while the exact optimum batch size is **UNVERIFIED** and will be settled by the shadow-cohort experiment above.

**Value x effort:** high x low  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it for batching and safe parallel progress; confirm-first for the batch  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

### Finding 5 — UNVERIFIED: use narrow autonomy leases, learned only from explicit owner choices

**Improvement.** Let the owner pre-authorize recurring bounded commitments without teaching the system that mere non-objection equals consent.

**What it is.** A lease is explicit structured policy such as: “for repo X, until 18:00 UTC, may push new `agent/*` branches up to 20 commits; may never merge, release, change secrets, or touch protected refs.” Leases are scoped by goal, provider account, target prefix, effect class, cost/data ceiling, and expiry. They are revocable and visible. The system never generalizes from approvals by itself.

**Why inferred.** Repeated autonomous loops and remote-fleet workflows make asking the same bounded question wasteful, but durable personalization is dangerous when inferred from behavior. Current policy grants broad bypass (`settings.json:26-75`, `:693`) rather than granular temporary authority. The owner wants proactive partnership and low cognitive load, implying a need for *explicitly teachable boundaries*, not invisible learned permissions.

**How to build.** Extend existing CONFIG policy with a small `autonomy-leases.json` projection sourced from owner decisions in the event ledger. Only an explicit “remember this boundary” decision creates a lease. Match exact structured operation fields; deny delegation from widening authority; show active lease ID on each auto-committed receipt. Expire by time and goal completion. Write the canonical decision to existing Basic Memory only if the owner asks for durable preference; otherwise keep it task-local.

**UNVERIFIED premise.** The unpacked bundle does not include the shared dispatcher source or host-native approval-token API, so exact lease enforcement across both Claude and Codex is unverified. The settling experiment is to inspect the dispatcher/adapters, replay one synthetic `PreToolUse` in each host, and prove that a child agent cannot widen a parent lease. Until that experiment passes, leases remain shadow-only and cannot authorize COMMIT.

**External signal that verifies it.** Disposable owned-repo tests exercise allowed branch push, protected-ref denial, expiry, account mismatch, child widening, and revocation. `git ls-remote`/provider API is the outcome signal; owner review of the lease card is the policy signal.

**SOTA.** NIST calls for explicit risk tolerances, differentiated human/AI roles, documented oversight, and risk controls tailored to context ([NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)). PAIR recommends progressively increasing automation under user guidance and keeping levels adjustable ([PAIR design patterns](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/patterns)). The UK NCSC recommends least privilege for the shortest time required and temporary credentials where possible ([NCSC agentic AI guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/thinking-carefully-before-adopting-agentic-ai)).

**Expected effect.** **Likely (55-80%)** to improve autonomy for recurring fleet work if the cross-host enforcement experiment passes; without that external proof, the safe benefit is only the visibility of proposed leases.

**Value x effort:** high x medium  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise  
**Autonomy:** confirm-first to create or broaden a lease; just-do-it inside its exact bounds  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

### Finding 6 — Classify generic and MCP tools from typed effects, not names or payload regex

**Improvement.** Maintain one small effect registry generated from active tool schemas, with explicit adapters for open-ended execution tools.

**What it is.** Each known method declares effects such as `read_local`, `write_local`, `send_external`, `mutate_remote`, `spend`, `change_authority`, or `execute_open_ended`, plus the fields that resolve target/audience/cost. MCP `browser_file_upload`, cloud run submission, memory delete, and backend remove do not inherit the same policy just because all are `mcp__.*`. Generic shell/browser JS/Cheat Engine Lua are parsed by executable verb and target; quoted prose is never scanned as an action.

**Why inferred.** The corpus proves both narrow methods and opaque executors: `mcp__tooluniverse__execute_tool` has 1,416 calls (`corpus.json:127-132`), Cheat Engine `evaluate_lua` 660 (`:159-164`), memory write/edit/delete (`:239-244`, `:335-340`, `:2007-2012`), backend removal (`:1807-1812`), and CLI install (`:2119-2124`). The live D1 incident proves payload matching cannot distinguish mention from use (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:14-22`, `:65-82`).

**How to build.** At capability-catalog refresh, read each active JSON schema and emit a versioned effect manifest. Require hand-curated entries only for open-ended/meta tools and sinks. Unknown methods default to `OBSERVE` only when their schema and native annotation prove read-only; otherwise they prepare but stop at COMMIT. Fold this into the existing semantic capability catalog and shared dispatcher—do not create a second catalog. Validate every entry with one known-positive and one known-negative fixture before activation.

**UNVERIFIED** whether all current MCP schemas expose enough semantics to infer effects; the settling experiment is a complete active-schema inventory reporting resolved, ambiguous, and missing methods. Ambiguous methods must remain explicit adapters rather than model-classified at run time.

**External signal that verifies it.** Compare registry declarations with provider schemas and disposable execution traces. ### Finding 7 — Never drop a pending commitment: make the approval a first-class open thread

**Improvement.** Persist every deferred/denied/unanswered COMMIT as an event-ledger thread linked into the existing `OPEN-THREADS.md`, and close it only on an external terminal signal.

**What it is.** A pending record contains goal ID, action digest, exact safe preparation already completed, why confirmation is needed, expiry, what independent work remains, and the external event that closes it. The agent continues safe work and resurfaces the item once at the next natural boundary or session resume. It does not repeatedly nag, silently discard, or mark completion while a required commitment remains.

**Why inferred.** “Never drop a thread” is a hard constraint because discussed-then-forgotten items cause real stress. The existing event ledger is already designated objective ground truth across resume/compact/clear (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:28-34`, `:85-91`), so a separate queue would duplicate it. Corpus retry loops show 53 repeated Read runs and 14 Bash runs (`corpus.json:10297-10323`); terminal thread state is also an anti-loop mechanism.

**How to build.** Add ledger event types `commit_prepared`, `approval_requested`, `approval_granted/denied/expired`, `commit_executed`, and `commit_verified`. Project unresolved events into the existing `OPEN-THREADS.md`; do not create a new store. `Stop` may report the thread but must exit 0 and must not block. `SessionStart` injects at most the highest-priority unresolved commitment, with a count/link for the rest, protecting attention. A digest change supersedes the old thread rather than mutating history.

**External signal that verifies it.** Kill/restart/compact a disposable session after preparation but before approval. A separate process reads the ledger and projection, proves the exact digest survives, then the provider or filesystem receipt closes it. A thread is “done” only when that independent receipt exists.

**SOTA.** Microsoft’s validated guidelines call for remembering recent interactions, cautious adaptation, efficient invocation/dismissal/correction, and making consequences clear ([Microsoft HAI guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)). ### Finding 8 — Calibrate the gate with owner-labeled shadow evidence, not self-evaluation

**Improvement.** Before enforcement, run the effect policy in shadow and measure selective-classification cost against actual owner adjudications; then promote only rules with independent known-positive controls.

Metrics are false-safe rate, false-block rate, prompts per completed goal, approval latency, ignored/expired approvals, thread loss, and coverage of known-positive canaries. ## Concrete policy pseudocode

This is intentionally small enough to live inside the current dispatcher:

```text
resolve(tool_call) -> EffectDecision

if resolver_error:
    ledger(GATE_ERROR, call_hash)       # fail-open by hard constraint
    allow_with_visible_warning()

elif effect is read_only:
    allow()

elif effect is private_mutation and rollback_receipt independently_verified:
    allow(); require_postcondition_receipt()

elif exact active_lease matches every resolved field:
    allow_once(); require_provider_or_execution_receipt()

elif effect in {external_send, shared_history, publish, production,
                authority_change, unbudgeted_spend, unproven_restore}:
    prepare_safe_form()
    persist_open_thread(action_digest)
    request_owner_once(action_digest)

elif effect in {secret_egress, unbounded_destroy, protected_force_push,
                disable_safety_to_proceed} and exact owner authority absent:
    refuse_derived_action(); persist_open_thread()

else:
    allow_with_context(); require_external_postcondition()
```

No branch asks the same model to judge whether its own work succeeded. Success is a file hash/diff/test result, task/provider status, remote ref, message/resource ID, account scope, owner decision, or a fresh-context independent review tied to execution evidence.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Reject: ask before every write or shell command

This would gate the dominant workload—over 169,000 combined shell/Edit/Write calls—and would repeat the live use-versus-mention failure. It is prompt-fatigue regression, not calibrated safety (`corpus.json:15-52`; `INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-47`).

### Reject: let the model assign its own danger score

It violates the law binding this unit: the same context that proposed an action cannot be its authorizer or verifier. It would also recreate the single-signal failures at `INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`. Deterministic resolution may defer ambiguity to the owner; it may not turn confidence prose into permission.

### Reject: one blanket plan approval

A plan is valuable for strategy but cannot authorize later-changed recipients, attachments, refs, environments, or costs. Anthropic’s plan-level oversight reduces friction ([source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/trustworthy-agents)), but this workspace needs the transaction digest because multi-agent/fan-out prompts have already propagated a fabricated constraint (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`).

### Reject: permanent “always allow” learned from repeated approval

Non-objection is not consent, and context changes risk. Explicit narrow leases provide the useful part without invisible authority creep. No trained personalization policy, critic, or preference model is proposed.

### Reject: a seventh hook or second policy daemon

The shared dispatcher already receives `PreToolUse`, and the local dispatch layer is separately wired (`settings.json:139-149`, `:250-258`). A new hook would breach the ceiling unless it replaced one; a daemon would violate the anti-overfit gate and split policy truth.

### Reject: “reversible means harmless”

Deletion of a sent message does not recall copies; reverting a pushed commit does not erase CI/release effects; canceling a run does not refund cost; reflog recovery does not protect collaborators. The policy therefore includes externality, authority, audience, cost, and independently demonstrated restoration—not only theoretical undo.
