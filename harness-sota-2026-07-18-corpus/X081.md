# X081 — law of evidence → observability without a baseline

## Executive finding

The analogy transfers, but only for a narrower problem than the wording initially suggests.

Law of evidence has not solved causal measurement without a baseline. It has solved a different and valuable problem: how a decision-maker should handle claims when the historical record is incomplete, interested parties select the proof, records can be altered, and the cost of a false conclusion varies. Its transferable mechanisms are (1) a burden of production and a burden of persuasion assigned to the claim's proponent; (2) foundation and authentication before an item receives evidentiary weight; (3) a regular-record rule that makes contemporaneous operational records more trustworthy than accounts produced for the dispute; (4) a stringent predicate for treating the absence of a record as evidence of absence; (5) completeness rights that counter cherry-picked excerpts; and (6) restrained treatment of lost evidence.

The correct harness translation is therefore an **evidence docket for change claims**, not a synthetic-baseline engine. A change with no pre-change baseline is recorded as `CAUSAL EFFECT: NOT PROVEN`; it may still be admitted on a reversible trial under a lower, explicitly named operational standard. From the first trial onward, a small hook-bus component records a prospective comparison contract, immutable-enough provenance, matched treatment/control outcomes, countermetrics, missingness, and a verdict whose strength cannot exceed its evidence tier.

This transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent unsupported “the change helped” conclusions, because the proposed gates directly instantiate Federal Rules of Evidence 901, 803(6), 803(7), and 106, and because a no-baseline claim fails the deliberately assigned burden rather than being filled in by retrospective guesswork. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to improve the quality of harness deployment decisions, because that effect depends on reviewers honoring `NOT PROVEN` and on tasks being comparable; the settling experiment is the shadow evaluation specified in “Validation experiment,” comparing adjudications with and without the docket on the same blinded change packets.

It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** capable of recovering the causal effect of an already-deployed change for which neither baseline nor credible concurrent control exists. The evidence is the causal-inference constraint that a causal effect is a contrast between potential outcomes and one potential outcome is unobserved; Hernán and Robins develop that framework in *Causal Inference: What If* ([free book PDF](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/miguel-hernan/wp-content/uploads/sites/1268/2024/04/hernanrobins_WhatIf_26apr24.pdf)). Evidence doctrine determines what may support a finding and how much proof is required; it does not manufacture the missing counterfactual.

## The mechanism in its own field

This section describes U.S. federal evidence law as the source system. It is a design analogy, not legal advice and not a claim that harness telemetry is legally admissible.

### 1. Burden of production plus burden of persuasion

The burden of proof separates two questions that are easily collapsed in engineering reviews:

1. **Has the proponent produced enough evidence for the claim to be considered?** This is the burden of production.
2. **Has the proponent persuaded the fact-finder to the required degree?** This is the burden of persuasion.

Cornell's Legal Information Institute describes burden of proof as comprising these two concepts and states that the ordinary civil preponderance standard means “more likely than not,” while criminal guilt must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt ([burden of proof](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/burden_of_proof); [preponderance](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/preponderance)). “Clear and convincing” is an intermediate standard under which the contention must be highly probable ([clear and convincing evidence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/clear_and_convincing_evidence)). The important mechanism is not the vocabulary alone. The law assigns the risk of unresolved uncertainty: if the party bearing the burden does not meet it, the proposition is not legally established.

That is exactly what a baseline-free harness review lacks. In ordinary engineering conversation, “we cannot tell” often drifts into “it seems better” or “nothing broke.” A burden rule preserves the third state: **not proven**. It does not invert automatically into “the change hurt.”

### 2. Authentication and foundation

Federal Rule of Evidence 901(a) requires the proponent to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item is what the proponent claims. Rule 901(b)(9) specifically recognizes evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result ([FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). In its own field, this is a gateway question: a screenshot, recording, or data export is not self-proving merely because it looks plausible. The proponent supplies a foundation connecting the exhibit to its claimed source and method.

For digital evidence integrity, NIST defines a hash as a value used to substantiate integrity or perform inclusion/exclusion comparisons ([NIST hash glossary](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/38726)). NIST IR 8387 recommends documenting the original source and creation/transfer path, hashing digital objects with an approved algorithm, and storing the hash separately from the object ([NIST IR 8387, §3.2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2022/NIST.IR.8387.pdf)). A hash can substantiate that bytes did not change after hashing; it cannot establish that the producing instrumentation measured the right event or was truthful at creation. That distinction matters to the translation.

### 3. Regularly conducted records, not dispute-shaped recollection

The hearsay rule generally distrusts an out-of-court statement offered for its truth, but Federal Rule of Evidence 803(6) admits a qualifying record of a regularly conducted activity even when the declarant is unavailable. The rule's predicates are concrete: the record was made at or near the time by, or from information supplied by, someone with knowledge; it was kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity; making it was a regular practice; a custodian or certification establishes these conditions; and the opponent may show that the source or preparation circumstances lack trustworthiness ([FRE 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

The transferable logic is that a record created routinely and contemporaneously, before anyone knows which later dispute it will favor, has a better foundation than a narrative reconstructed after a change attracts scrutiny. The rule does **not** say that all business logs are true. Its express trustworthiness escape hatch is central.

### 4. Absence of a record is evidence only after proving the recording practice

Federal Rule of Evidence 803(7) permits absence from a regularly conducted record to prove nonoccurrence only when a record was regularly kept for a matter of that kind and the opponent does not show circumstances indicating untrustworthiness ([FRE 803(7)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)). Rule 803(10) similarly permits a diligent-search certification for absence of a public record only where the office regularly kept a record of that kind ([FRE 803(10)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

This is the closest direct borrow for observability. “No error event exists” is probative only after establishing that the detector was active, that this event type was within its coverage, and that the record survived. Silence from a detector with unknown coverage is no evidence. The rule has already encoded the asymmetry between a present record and an absent record.

### 5. The rule of completeness

Federal Rule of Evidence 106 allows an adverse party, when a party introduces all or part of a statement, to require contemporaneous introduction of another part or another statement that fairness requires. The Advisory Committee explains that the rule addresses misleading impressions created by taking material out of context and the inadequacy of delayed repair ([FRE 106 and committee notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)).

The mechanism is adversarial and local: the proponent does not get unilateral control of the slice by which a record is understood. In harness evaluation, a latency improvement can be misleading without completion by success rate, retries, token cost, task mix, user correction, and missing-event rate. Completeness does not require dumping every metric; it requires the contextual metric capable of curing the specific misimpression.

### 6. Lost evidence does not automatically prove the worst case

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e) addresses electronically stored information that should have been preserved but was lost because reasonable preservation steps were not taken. Curative measures may address prejudice; the severe step of presuming the lost information unfavorable requires a finding of intent to deprive another party of its use. The Advisory Committee states why: negligent loss may have destroyed favorable or unfavorable information, so negligence alone does not logically support an adverse inference ([FRCP 37(e) and committee notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_37)).

This is a useful brake on the analogy. A missing baseline makes an improvement claim unproved. It does not prove the change regressed. Only evidence of intentional suppression warrants a stronger adverse inference, and even that analogy should be used for governance, not as a numerical estimate of effect.

## Concrete translation: an Evidence Docket on the existing bus — UNVERIFIED on the target harness; settle with the hook-payload spike and blinded shadow evaluation

### Proposal A — prospective change-claim manifest

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Before enabling a behavior-changing harness patch for evaluation, its proponent creates a small, schema-versioned manifest:

```yaml
claim_id: hc-2026-0017
change_digest: sha256:...
claim: "quote-safe command wrapper reduces unmatched-quote failures"
population: "PowerShell/Bash tool calls containing generated shell strings"
primary_outcome: "unmatched_quote_errors / eligible_calls"
countermetrics:
  - task_success_rate
  - p95_wall_time
  - tokens_per_success
  - user_corrections_per_task
exclusions: ["human-authored literal commands"]
assignment: "session-hash A/B, 10% treatment"
minimum_evidence: "operational-preponderance"
decision_window: {eligible_calls: 500, max_days: 7}
rollback: "disable config key quote_wrapper"
owner: "..."
created_at: "..."
```

The manifest is the harness equivalent of pleading a definite proposition and assigning its burden before seeing outcomes. The `change_digest`, population, outcome definition, exclusions, countermetrics, assignment, and threshold are frozen before the first eligible event. Amendments append a new version rather than rewriting the original.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to expose metric switching and post-hoc population changes, because the before-observation manifest can be mechanically compared with the verdict and Rule 106's completeness logic requires named countermetrics. The settling experiment is to seed ten review packets with outcome switching or silently changed exclusions and compare detection rates between manifest-assisted and ordinary review.

The proposal does not solve old changes with no manifest. They enter as `retrospective`, and causal claims remain `NOT PROVEN` unless a separately justified concurrent control or credible natural experiment exists.

### Proposal B — append-only evidence envelopes from existing hooks — UNVERIFIED; settle with the hook-payload spike

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

An existing pre/post tool or session hook emits one JSONL envelope per eligible observation:

```json
{
  "schema":"evidence-envelope/v1",
  "claim_id":"hc-2026-0017",
  "event_id":"uuid",
  "session_pseudonym":"HMAC(...) ",
  "assignment":"control",
  "harness_digest":"sha256:...",
  "model":"...",
  "platform":"...",
  "task_class":"...",
  "started_at":"...",
  "ended_at":"...",
  "outcomes":{"eligible":true,"quote_error":false,"success":true},
  "countermetrics":{"wall_ms":1234,"tokens":890,"corrections":0},
  "detector_health":{"hook_seen":true,"parser_canary":true},
  "prev_hash":"sha256:...",
  "record_hash":"sha256:..."
}
```

The foundation report accompanying a verdict states the producer version, event coverage, schema, assignment method, dropped-event count, canary result, and hash-chain verification result. The artifact hash is anchored outside the data file (for example, in the change manifest's verdict record), following NIST's separation principle. Secrets and raw prompts are excluded; pseudonymous grouping is sufficient for cluster-aware comparison.

Hash chaining detects post-record modification and deletion gaps when sequence continuity is available. It does not prove truthful generation. The independent `parser_canary` addresses detector operability, while cross-checking hook event counts against a different source such as session completion counts addresses coverage. These are mechanically independent signals: internal canary success and external denominator reconciliation.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this envelope can be implemented under roughly 200 lines in a harness that already exposes pre/post hooks, because the operation is bounded JSON serialization, HMAC/hash calculation, and append; **UNVERIFIED — settle by implementing a spike against the actual hook payload and counting non-generated source lines**. Because that premise is unverified, no size-dependent deployment commitment should be made before the spike.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the envelope will distinguish “no matching failures observed” from “the detector was not demonstrably operating,” because every absence claim is conditioned on both a known-present canary and denominator reconciliation. The settling experiment is to disable only the failure parser while leaving event emission live; a conforming verdict must become `DETECTOR INVALID`, not `zero failures`.

### Proposal C — admissibility ladder and explicit burdens

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Each change claim receives two separate decisions: `ADMISSIBILITY` (may these observations be considered?) and `WEIGHT/VERDICT` (what do they establish?). A compact ladder is enough:

| Tier | Predicate | Permitted statement |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Anecdote, retrospective recollection, or unvalidated detector | “Lead for investigation”; no effect claim |
| E1 | Authenticated event records, but no baseline/control | “Observed under version X”; causal effect `NOT PROVEN` |
| E2 | Prospective manifest + authenticated records + detector controls + comparable before/after series | “Associated with change”; causal wording requires explicit confounding analysis |
| E3 | Prospective concurrent randomized or stable hash assignment + complete outcome bundle + acceptable missingness | “Change caused an average effect in the evaluated population,” with interval and limitations |
| EX | Foundation failure, changed primary outcome without declared amendment, broken canary, unreconciled large missingness, or unverifiable artifact | Excluded until cured |

Operational burdens should depend on action cost:

- **Exploration:** E0 may justify investigation; no deployment claim.
- **Reversible, low-blast-radius canary:** E1 plus a reasoned mechanism and rollback may satisfy an `operational-probable-cause` threshold. The verdict must say the benefit is unproved.
- **Default rollout:** require E3 and `operational-preponderance`: the probability of net benefit must be judged greater than the probability of net harm, with the judgment and uncertainty recorded.
- **Irreversible, security-sensitive, or broad rollout:** require E3 plus `operational-clear-and-convincing`: high probability of net benefit, detector validity, and no material countermetric regression.

These labels deliberately borrow the structure, not legal numerical cutoffs. U.S. evidence law does not define a harness deployment threshold, and pretending that “preponderance” always equals a computed 50.0001% would be false precision.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that tiered burdens will reduce premature broad rollout without blocking cheap learning, because the legal mechanism assigns stricter proof to higher-stakes decisions while preserving low-cost provisional action; the settling experiment is a six-week prospective audit comparing rollback frequency and time-to-decision before and after adoption, stratified by rollout class.

### Proposal D — completeness bundles and adversarial review — UNVERIFIED; settle with a renderer spike and red-team packets

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The verdict generator refuses a lone favorable metric. For each primary metric it renders, in the same view:

- eligible treatment/control counts and missing counts;
- absolute rates and effect size, not only relative percentage;
- uncertainty interval or the explicit reason none is valid;
- predeclared countermetrics;
- task/model/platform mix;
- detector health and schema/version changes;
- exclusions and post-manifest amendments;
- adverse observations linked by event ID.

A reviewer can attach a `completion request`: identify the claimed misimpression, name the omitted record needed to cure it, and receive that record beside the original result. This imports Rule 106's narrow corrective logic rather than requiring unlimited metric disclosure.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch cherry-picked wins such as lower latency paired with lower success or a favorable relative percentage hiding a tiny denominator, because the renderer mechanically co-displays the complementary fields. The settling experiment is a red-team set of packets containing five predefined selective-reporting patterns; success requires all five to be surfaced without access to raw prompts.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the deterministic renderer remains under roughly 200 lines when attached to an existing reporting hook, because it only validates required fields and formats a fixed bundle; **UNVERIFIED — settle by building the renderer spike against an actual verdict payload and counting non-generated source lines**.

### Proposal E — missing-evidence disposition, not invented baselines

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

The adjudicator uses this decision table:

| Condition | Disposition |
|---|---|
| No historical baseline and no credible concurrent control | `CAUSAL EFFECT: NOT PROVEN`; start prospective evaluation |
| Event absent, detector canary passes, independent denominator reconciles, and this event type is regularly recorded | Absence may receive weight, with coverage interval |
| Event absent but canary or independent reconciliation fails | `DETECTOR INVALID`; silence receives zero weight |
| Evidence lost despite reasonable preservation | Record prejudice and narrow the claim; do not infer direction |
| Evidence intentionally suppressed, supported by independent proof | Governance adverse inference may apply; do not fabricate an effect size |
| Old telemetry survives and an untreated comparable population exists | Permit retrospective causal analysis only with explicit identification assumptions and sensitivity analysis |

The key result is intentionally austere: the absence of a baseline is not cured. The system creates the conditions under which the next decision has evidence.

This is **almost certain (90-99%)** to prevent the specific logical error “missing baseline implies no regression,” because that inference is prohibited by the table and FRCP 37(e)'s reasoning independently explains why unintentional loss has no determinate direction. The remaining 1-10% reflects bypass or misclassification; the settling experiment is a conformance suite in which every no-baseline fixture must serialize `NOT PROVEN` and every broken-canary fixture must serialize `DETECTOR INVALID`.

## What this catches that baseline-free observability misses

The following are capability claims about the proposed rules, not claims about any particular local repository:

1. **Retrospective success narratives.** A favorable post-change sample with no counterfactual cannot clear E1, so “better” becomes “observed under X; effect not proven.”
2. **Silent detector failure represented as zero incidence.** FRE 803(7)'s predicate becomes a required canary plus an independently reconciled denominator.
3. **Log tampering or accidental mutation after collection.** Event and chain hashes expose byte-level changes or sequence gaps; foundation metadata distinguishes integrity from measurement validity.
4. **Metric and population switching.** The prospective manifest makes later definitions mechanically diffable.
5. **Cherry-picked slices.** The completeness bundle places countermetrics, denominators, missingness, and task mix beside the favorable result.
6. **Confusing admissibility with strength.** An authentic log may be E1 and still fail to prove causation.
7. **Overreaction to missing data.** The system neither treats silence as success nor assumes lost observations were unfavorable without evidence of intentional suppression.
8. **One-size-fits-all proof.** A reversible canary can proceed on less evidence than a broad or irreversible rollout, while its epistemic status remains visible.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy transfers and where it breaks

### Structural matches

**Interested proponents select evidence.** Litigation assumes adversarial selection rather than neutral omniscience. Harness change authors also know which result favors adoption. Burden allocation and completeness therefore transfer directly as governance structures.

**Records are generated before and after a dispute.** FRE 803(6) distinguishes regular contemporaneous records from dispute-shaped accounts. Harness hooks can likewise create routine records before the result is known.

**Digital records require provenance and integrity.** FRE 901(b)(9) asks for evidence that a producing system is accurate, while NIST separates provenance and hashes. Telemetry pipelines have the same need.

**Absence is detector-relative.** FRE 803(7) makes nonoccurrence depend on the regular recording practice. Observability has the identical logical structure: no emitted event is meaningful only relative to coverage and health.

**Decision stakes vary.** Evidence law openly uses different standards of persuasion. Harness operations also distinguish a reversible experiment from an irreversible rollout, so tiered burdens are a coherent transfer.

### Structural breaks

**Courts decide historical propositions; observability asks for causal effects and future performance.** Authentication can establish that a log is genuine. It cannot establish what would have happened without the change or whether the result generalizes to future models and tasks. This is the central disanalogy.

**Legal standards are normative, not estimators.** “Preponderance” allocates decision risk; it is not an A/B estimator, power calculation, confidence interval, or correction for interference. The harness still needs experimental design.

**Adversarial procedure has institutional actors the harness may lack.** A judge decides foundation questions and an adverse party invokes completeness. If the change author is also sole reviewer, the checks must be mechanically enforced or independently reviewed. It is **likely (55-80%)** that mechanical completeness checks retain part of the adversarial benefit, because schema validation cannot waive required countermetrics; whether they change owner decisions is **UNVERIFIED — settle with the blinded shadow-review experiment below**.

**Regular recording can regularize systematic error.** A consistently wrong detector satisfies “routine” but not trustworthiness or accurate-system foundation. Hence the separate known-present canary and independent denominator; mere volume is not validation.

**Chain of custody can become security theater.** A perfect hash chain preserves perfectly wrong data. The design never upgrades an evidence tier on hashes alone.

**Hearsay doctrine contains policy choices unrelated to measurement science.** The translation uses the operational structure of Rule 803(6)/(7), not the proposition that courtroom admissibility implies scientific validity.

**Spoliation sanctions depend on duties and intent.** A harness should not anthropomorphize routine log rotation as culpable conduct. FRCP 37(e)'s restraint transfers; its punitive legal machinery does not.

### Verdict on transfer

The analogy **does transfer** as a claim-governance and evidence-integrity layer. It **does not transfer** as a substitute for baseline creation, randomization, causal identification, or statistical inference. Any design that advertises “legal-grade evidence” as proof of efficacy would force the analogy past its structural support.

## Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

All five proposals ride the existing settings/hook/reporting bus. The state is a manifest plus append-only JSONL and a generated verdict. No second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is introduced.

The minimal architecture is deliberate:

```text
change manifest (before data)
          |
existing hooks -> evidence envelopes -> foundation/completeness check -> verdict
                     |                         |
              canary + denominator       NOT PROVEN is valid
```

Rejected alternatives:

- **Reconstruct a synthetic baseline from unrelated old sessions.** Rejected because task/model/platform drift makes comparability an identification assumption, not a fact. Such analysis may be exploratory E2 only when assumptions and sensitivity checks are explicit.
- **Add a telemetry daemon or observability platform.** Rejected under the anti-overfit gate: the proposed evidence records need no second runtime or SaaS.
- **Store every prompt and response for perfect custody.** Rejected because custody does not require maximal collection, and sensitive content expands risk without solving causal identification.
- **Treat any hash-verified log as dispositive.** Rejected because integrity after capture is not semantic correctness at capture.
- **Use a trained critic as the fact-finder.** Rejected because it reintroduces a previously rejected component and creates another uncalibrated measurement layer. Deterministic predicates plus a recorded human/owner judgment are sufficient.
- **Infer that missing evidence was adverse.** Rejected unless independent evidence supports intentional suppression; FRCP 37(e) expressly explains the directional fallacy.
- **Block every change lacking E3.** Rejected because evidence standards allocate risk rather than prohibit learning; reversible canaries can operate under a lower, explicitly provisional burden.

## Validation experiment — UNVERIFIED recommendation; this experiment is what settles it

The recommendation as a whole is **likely (55-80%)** to improve decision calibration, based on the direct structural matches above. This remains **UNVERIFIED — settle with the following preregistered, blinded shadow-evaluation before making the docket a mandatory rollout gate**.

1. Collect 24 historical or synthetic change packets spanning genuine improvement, regression, no detectable effect, detector outage, selective metric reporting, post-hoc exclusion, and missing baseline. Do not rely on a single detector to label truth: use seeded defects for the synthetic packets and two independent reviewers plus raw-source reconciliation for historical packets.
2. Create two views of every packet: current-style evidence and Evidence-Docket evidence. Randomize order and blind reviewers to condition and seeded truth.
3. Ask reviewers for one of `DEPLOY`, `CANARY`, `REJECT`, `NOT PROVEN`, or `DETECTOR INVALID`, plus a confidence band and cited observations.
4. Primary outcome: rate of false causal conclusions (`DEPLOY` or confident improvement where truth is regression/no-effect/indeterminate).
5. Countermetrics required by completeness: decision time, false rejection of genuine improvements, reviewer disagreement, and packet-preparation effort.
6. Mechanism checks: all missing-baseline packets must become `NOT PROVEN`; all detector-outage packets must become `DETECTOR INVALID`; all selective-reporting packets must surface the omitted countermetric.
7. Adoption criterion, fixed before review: at least a 50% relative reduction in false causal conclusions, no more than a 15% relative increase in false rejection, and median review overhead below ten minutes per packet. These are proposed operational thresholds, not claims derived from evidence law.
8. After shadow validation, run a time-bounded live canary with stable hash assignment. Report absolute effects and uncertainty; do not silently change thresholds after seeing results.

The experiment itself needs a baseline condition, which is the point: evidence-law mechanisms discipline the claim, but only comparison supplies evidence that the mechanism helped.

## Gaps and limits

- This IDEATION unit intentionally did not inspect the local harness or repository. Therefore existing hook payload fields, stable assignment primitives, redaction guarantees, and exact implementation line count are **UNVERIFIED — settle by the implementation spike against the target harness**.
- No claim is made that the proposed records satisfy legal admissibility, preservation duties, privacy law, or forensic standards. The cited rules are source mechanisms for design translation.
- Exact statistical tests and sample sizes depend on metric distributions, clustering by task/session, expected effect, and acceptable error rates. Choosing them without those inputs would be fabricated precision.
- A concurrent A/B test may suffer interference if treatment changes shared caches, MCP state, or owner behavior. Whether stable session-hash assignment is unbiased is **UNVERIFIED — settle with an A/A test, balance checks, and an interference audit before interpreting treatment effects**.
- Historical telemetry may support a credible quasi-experiment in a specific case, but that is **roughly even (45-55%)** absent facts about untreated units and drift; the evidence is only the generic availability of observational identification methods, not evidence about this harness. The settling experiment is to state the causal estimand, draw the assignment mechanism, test pre-change comparability, and run sensitivity analysis on the actual candidate data.
- The proposed confidence bands are decision labels, not substitutes for reported effect intervals or calibrated probabilities.
