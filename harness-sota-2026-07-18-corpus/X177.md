# X177 — Cross-domain borrow: experimental design → synthesis

## Unit boundary and answer in one paragraph

This unit asks whether mechanisms from experimental design can help an agent collapse thousands of findings into a defensible decision without reading a 12 MB synthesis corpus end to end. Per the task, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness or repository; the measured ground truth in the brief is context, not evidence for this ideation lens. The borrow **does transfer, but only partially**. The transferable object is not statistical experimentation as a whole. It is a compact *decision-integrity protocol* assembled from (1) preregistration/Registered Reports, (2) blinded assessment, (3) negative controls, and (4) prospective power or sample-size planning. The first three address degrees of freedom, expectation effects, and pipeline bias; the fourth can size a randomized omission audit. Causal effect estimation, ordinary null-hypothesis testing, and a claim that sampled findings represent the whole corpus do **not** transfer. I am **very likely (80-90%)** that a staged protocol will expose important synthesis failures that an ordinary one-pass summary conceals, because each proposed check targets a concrete, observable failure mode and registered-report and negative-control designs explicitly separate plans from outcomes and test the same measurement pathway with outcome-null cases ([Nature Registered Reports](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/registered-reports), [Lipsitch et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). That expected benefit is **UNVERIFIED in agent synthesis; settle it with the paired replay experiment specified in §8 before adopting the protocol as a gate**.

## 1. What experimental design has already solved

### 1.1 Preregistration: bind the analysis before its answer can influence the method

In its own field, preregistration creates a time-stamped record of hypotheses, outcomes, sampling rules, exclusions, and analyses before the investigator observes the result. Its narrow epistemic benefit is *historical transparency*: a reader can distinguish a planned confirmatory test from an analysis selected after inspecting data. Nosek et al. describe preregistration as a way to make hypothesis generation and hypothesis testing more distinct, not as a ban on exploration ([Nosek et al., “The preregistration revolution,” PNAS 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)). ICMJE states an operationally sharper motivation for prospective clinical-trial registration: preventing selective publication and selective reporting of outcomes, with registration before the first participant is enrolled ([ICMJE clinical-trial registration policy](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/publishing-and-editorial-issues/clinical-trial-registration.html)).

Registered Reports strengthen the mechanism. Nature’s process reviews the question, method, analysis pipeline, sampling plan, and quality checks at Stage 1, before results; Stage 2 checks adherence, requires deviations to be identified, permits separately labelled exploration, and does not condition publication on result direction or significance. Nature also requires all registered analyses to be reported except rare, justified cases ([Nature Registered Reports, process and reviewer criteria](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/registered-reports)). The critical mechanism is therefore not merely “write a plan.” It is:

1. commit to decision rules while outcome-blind;
2. make the commitment inspectable;
3. label deviations rather than silently rewriting history;
4. preserve a lane for exploration; and
5. adjudicate final work against the prior commitment.

This is a solution to *researcher degrees of freedom coupled to observed outcomes*. It does not prove that the chosen hypothesis, measurement, or analysis is good. A bad plan can be faithfully preregistered. It also does not prevent a researcher from writing a vague registration. Nature’s reviewer criterion that the plan contain enough detail to prevent undisclosed flexibility is important precisely because registration alone is not sufficient.

### 1.2 Prospective power analysis: define the smallest effect worth detecting and buy enough observations

In an ordinary hypothesis-testing experiment, power is the probability that the chosen procedure rejects the null under a specified alternative. Prospective power analysis links the design, significance threshold, assumed variance/effect size, and sample size. It forces the investigator to state what magnitude matters and how much data would give the planned test an acceptable probability of detecting it. The NC3Rs Experimental Design Assistant says that formal hypothesis tests should use an appropriate sample-size method such as power analysis and notes that smaller samples reduce power ([NC3Rs, group and sample size](https://eda.nc3rs.org.uk/index.php/experimental-design-group)). Nature likewise asks Stage 1 reviewers to examine statistical power and the sampling plan ([Nature Registered Reports](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/registered-reports)). A worked scientific overview shows the familiar dependence on alpha, beta/power, expected variability, and effect size ([Indrayan et al. 2020, sample-size selection using power analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425741/)).

The deep mechanism is a prospective *sensitivity contract*: “If a real effect of at least δ exists under assumptions A, this design has probability 1−β of exposing it.” It prevents “we looked at whatever was convenient and saw nothing” from masquerading as strong negative evidence. It is not a magic percentage attached after data collection, and it is only meaningful for a specified estimand and sampling model.

### 1.3 Blinding: prevent knowledge of condition or desired answer from entering measurement

In experiments, blinding separates information that is needed to perform a role from information that could bias that role. Blinding participants/providers targets behavioral or co-intervention differences; blinding outcome assessors targets differential measurement or adjudication. A within-trial systematic review found empirical observer bias for subjective scale outcomes and argued that blinded outcome assessment can materially reduce it ([Hróbjartsson et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3589328/)).

The evidence is not a license to say “blinding always fixes bias.” A later meta-epidemiological study found no evidence of an *average* difference in estimated treatment effects between trials with and without blinded patients, providers, or outcome assessors ([Moustgaard et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7190062/)). The results are compatible because effects can depend on outcome subjectivity, implementation, and study context, and because meta-epidemiology is vulnerable to confounding between design features. The defensible field-level statement is narrower: blinding blocks a specific information path by design; its empirical benefit varies and it does not remove other paths of bias.

### 1.4 Negative controls: run the same bias-producing pathway where the hypothesized effect cannot operate

A negative control is not merely a random irrelevant item. In epidemiology, the ideal negative-control outcome cannot plausibly be caused by the exposure but shares the primary outcome’s sources of confounding or measurement bias. Conversely, a negative-control exposure cannot cause the outcome but shares the primary exposure’s confounding structure. If the primary analysis reports an “effect” in that matched null condition, a bias or analytic flaw is implicated.

Lipsitch, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and Cohen give the crucial selection rule: the negative control must reproduce a condition in which the hypothesized causal mechanism cannot operate while retaining the likely sources of bias. They call the desired relationship “U-comparability.” They also explicitly warn that comparability is approximate, so a positive control result does not uniquely identify the bias and a null control result can miss bias that the control does not share ([Lipsitch et al. 2010, especially “Choice of negative controls”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)). A later review similarly emphasizes that a useful control is causally irrelevant to the target relationship but comparable with respect to unmeasured confounding, and that rare or weak controls can themselves be underpowered ([Shi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8118596/)).

The solved pattern is therefore a *falsification probe through the same pipeline*. It detects a class of unsuspected errors without requiring the investigator to name the exact bug in advance.

## 2. Structural mapping to synthesis

The target is a decision such as “which harness mechanisms should be adopted, rejected, tested, or deferred?” The raw material is thousands of heterogeneous findings produced by different units. The dangerous “outcome” is the emerging preferred architecture. Once an agent sees persuasive conclusions, it can unconsciously change inclusion criteria, treat supportive claims as concrete and objections as speculative, count duplicates as corroboration, or stop reading after narrative convergence.

The mappings are:

| Experimental-design object | Synthesis analogue | What remains invariant | What does **not** remain invariant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protocol/hypothesis | Decision question, candidate actions, acceptance/veto criteria, evidence hierarchy, stopping and deviation rules | Rules can be fixed before the answer is visible | There is no single scientific hypothesis and criteria may legitimately evolve |
| Observation/experimental unit | Atomic evidence card or claim-source pair | A finite item can be sampled, scored, and traced | Findings are correlated, duplicated, unequal in severity, and not exchangeable |
| Outcome assessor | Agent assigning relevance, credibility, severity, and action class | Knowledge of author/conclusion can alter a subjective score | Provenance is also legitimate evidence and cannot stay hidden forever |
| Negative-control outcome | Matched evidence packet whose correct action is “no change/no support,” processed identically | The same prompt/parser/model can generate a spurious positive recommendation | It is difficult to guarantee that a synthetic control is semantically matched |
| Statistical power | Probability that a randomized audit detects an omission/error occurring at prevalence p | Sampling mathematics transfers when the audit population and randomization are real | “Power to find the best architecture” has no defined effect, null, or sampling distribution |
| Registered exploratory analysis | Explicit “emergent candidate” lane | Discovery remains allowed without being back-labelled as planned confirmation | Exploration is central to design synthesis, not a secondary nuisance |

This shows why the borrow is real but bounded. All four mechanisms govern *information flow and prospective commitments*, which exist in both domains. Only power analysis depends heavily on statistical-unit assumptions, so its use must be confined to a mechanically defined audit.

### 3.1 UNVERIFIED — Stage 0: preregister a decision protocol

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Before showing the synthesis agent unit conclusions, create a small immutable `decision-protocol.json` containing:

- the exact decision to be made and allowed dispositions (`adopt`, `experiment`, `defer`, `reject`);
- acceptance criteria, e.g. evidence strength, expected damage avoided, implementation size, reversibility, and bus compatibility;
- veto criteria, including reintroduction of prohibited architecture classes;
- severity strata and a rule that rare catastrophic risks receive census review rather than sampling;
- a source/evidence hierarchy and rules for handling unsupported assertions;
- duplicate-family rules so repeated claims do not automatically become independent corroboration;
- contradiction rules: retain both sides and require explicit adjudication;
- the schema for atomic evidence cards;
- control families, expected outputs, and failure thresholds;
- the random-audit population, seed derivation, target detectable prevalence, and confidence;
- stopping conditions;
- an explicit deviation mechanism; and
- the intended final decision-table schema.

Hash the protocol and record the input-manifest hash before synthesis. Do not make it “immutable forever”: later changes are allowed as numbered deviations with author, time, reason, affected criteria, and whether the change was informed by revealed evidence. This mirrors the Registered Reports distinction between adherence and separately disclosed deviations, while retaining the adaptivity required for design work.

What this catches: shifting the definition of “strong evidence” after a favored mechanism appears; silently adding a criterion that rescues a preferred proposal; stopping after a persuasive cluster; and presenting an emergent idea as though it satisfied an a priori test. I am **very likely (80-90%)** that a precise pre-evidence contract makes those shifts detectable, because the before/after objects are directly diffable and Registered Reports use the same temporal separation to expose deviations ([Nature Registered Reports](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/registered-reports)). Whether it materially improves the chosen architecture is **UNVERIFIED; the §8 replay compares decision quality and undisclosed criterion changes**.

### 3.2 UNVERIFIED — Stage 1: make a loss-accounted evidence ledger, not a prose mega-summary

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Normalize each unit into one or more append-only evidence cards. A useful minimum schema is:

```json
{
  "card_id": "stable-id",
  "source_id": "unit-or-document-id",
  "claim": "one decision-relevant proposition",
  "evidence_locator": ["URL-or-source locator"],
  "evidence_kind": "measured|observed|documented|reasoned|speculative",
  "direction": "supports|opposes|qualifies|null",
  "scope": "where the claim applies",
  "severity": "catastrophic|high|medium|low",
  "dependencies": ["premise/card ids"],
  "duplicate_family": "semantic-family-id",
  "extractor_uncertainty": "explicit text",
  "source_span_digest": "hash"
}
```

The ledger must also record every source unit, including `zero decision-relevant cards`, parse failures, and exclusions with reasons. The decisive accounting identity is:

`manifest units = successfully carded units + explicit zero-card units + quarantined failures`.

This is analogous to requiring every registered outcome to appear, including null results. It catches entire dropped files, silent parser failures, “boring” negative findings disappearing, and multiple paraphrases being counted as independent evidence. A card is an index into evidence, not a replacement for evidence; high-severity cards and the adjudication sample must reopen their source spans.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that a ledger will reduce silent omission relative to recursive prose summarization, because manifest reconciliation and stable source links make omission mechanically observable. The magnitude is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by planting known findings across file positions and measuring source-level and claim-level recall in §8**. I am only **roughly even (45-55%)** that automatic atomization preserves enough cross-claim context for final architectural decisions, because interdependent arguments may be damaged when split; the full-source adjudication arm in §8 is required.

### 3.3 UNVERIFIED — Stage 2: two-pass blinding, not permanent provenance removal

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Produce a masked view in which the first assessor sees claim, evidence excerpt or locator class, scope, and dependencies but not unit identity, rhetorical conclusion, author prestige, vote counts, or whether the card came from a favored proposal. The first pass assigns only relevance, damage class, falsifiability, required corroboration, and candidate disposition. A second pass reveals provenance, method, independence/duplicate family, and conflicts so the assessor can legitimately evaluate credibility. Preserve both scores and require an explanation for changes after unblinding.

Permanent blinding would be wrong: whether a statement is a measured observation, an official specification, or an unaudited idea is central to evidence strength. The two-pass design instead asks a diagnostic question: “Did seeing provenance or the unit’s conclusion change the judgment, and why?” Large unexplained shifts are audit targets, not automatic errors.

What it can catch: authority/prestige anchoring, deference to confident prose, preference for the first coherent architecture, and asymmetric skepticism toward supporting versus opposing claims. It can also reveal legitimate provenance effects, which must be retained. I am **likely (55-80%)** that masking conclusion and identity changes some subjective classifications, based on the causal information path and evidence that assessor blinding can matter for subjective outcomes ([Hróbjartsson et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3589328/)). I am only **roughly even (45-55%)** that those changes improve final decisions, because meta-epidemiological evidence on average blinding effects is mixed and provenance carries real information ([Moustgaard et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7190062/)). This is **UNVERIFIED; §8 compares masked-first and provenance-first arms and has human adjudicators score both without being told the arm**.

### 3.4 UNVERIFIED — Stage 3: matched negative controls and positive detector controls

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Seed controls *before* card order is randomized, hide their identifiers from the assessor, and pass them through the exact same prompts, parsers, aggregation, and decision logic as real cards. Use several families because no single control shares every bias path:

1. **Null-recommendation control.** A plausible, stylistically matched card contains no evidence for changing the harness. Expected disposition: `no support` or `defer`, never `adopt`.
2. **Broken-causality control.** Keep the surface form and evidence strength of a real proposal but sever the link between evidence and proposed mechanism. Expected disposition: flag unsupported inference.
3. **Duplicate-count control.** Insert paraphrases of one source under different masked IDs. Expected behavior: one duplicate family, not multiple independent votes.
4. **Provenance-swap pair.** The same substantive card appears with high- and low-prestige metadata in separate blinded runs. Expected first-pass scores are identical; justified second-pass differences are logged.
5. **Direction-flip pair.** Matched support and objection cards use parallel structure. Expected behavior: the same evidentiary threshold applies to each direction.
6. **Known-present positive control.** Seed one explicit, high-severity, decision-relevant finding. This is not a negative control, but it proves the recall detector can find a known-present item before interpreting missing outputs.
7. **Known-absent negative detector control.** Seed a manifest source whose correct result is explicitly zero cards. This checks that the system does not hallucinate findings merely to fill the schema.

The controls must be semantically matched to the real population and must not be trivial “gotchas.” Lipsitch et al.’s U-comparability warning transfers directly: an obviously synthetic nonsense card follows a different cognitive path and teaches almost nothing. A control failure says the pipeline is biased or broken; it does not identify the exact cause or quantify bias in real cards.

What this catches that ordinary summary review misses: recommendation-happy behavior, unsupported inference that nevertheless sounds coherent, duplicate amplification, provenance sensitivity, one-sided burden of proof, recall-detector failure, and schema-induced hallucination. I am **very likely (80-90%)** that well-matched controls detect at least the seeded failure when that failure is deliberately induced, because expected outputs are fixed and the controls traverse the same code path. Generalization from control failures to organic findings is **UNVERIFIED and only likely (55-80%)**, because semantic comparability will be approximate; settle it by inducing each organic failure class in held-out replays and measuring control sensitivity and false alarms (§8).

### 3.5 UNVERIFIED — Stage 4: power a randomized omission audit—and nothing broader

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Define a concrete finite population: all evidence cards in non-catastrophic strata. Randomly sample cards and have an independent assessor reopen the full source span and answer prespecified binary questions: Was the card faithfully extracted? Was its direction preserved? Was it represented in its cluster summary? Would correcting the error change a disposition?

If errors were independent draws and at least prevalence `p`, the probability that a sample of `n` sees one or more is `1 - (1-p)^n`. Thus a simple with-replacement planning bound is:

`n >= log(beta) / log(1-p)`.

For 95% probability of encountering at least one error if the error rate is 1%, `n >= 299`. This is a *detection* design, not an estimator of decision correctness. For a finite population sampled without replacement, use the exact hypergeometric probability. Stratify by evidence kind, direction, and severity. Review every catastrophic card and every control; do not ask a random sample to protect against a unique fatal omission. Cluster by source or duplicate family when dependence is strong, and treat the effective sample size as the number of independent clusters, not raw cards.

Pre-register what happens after a hit: for example, any high-severity omission triggers census review of that stratum; a control failure invalidates the run; and an observed ordinary-card error rate above a threshold triggers re-extraction. Do not keep sampling until a comforting result appears.

This catches prevalent extraction loss, polarity flips, summary omission, and failure modes concentrated in a registered stratum. It cannot establish that the synthesis found the best possible design. I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that the formula gives the stated detection probability under independent random sampling, because it follows directly from the probability of `n` misses. I am only **unlikely (20-45%)** to believe the independence assumption holds for real finding cards, because shared extractors, source units, and duplicate families induce correlation; therefore raw-card binomial “power” must not be reported as calibrated. Cluster bootstrap or exact replay simulation is **UNVERIFIED; settle calibration by injecting correlated error bursts of known prevalence and comparing nominal with empirical detection across at least 1,000 seeded runs**.

### 3.6 UNVERIFIED — Stage 5: outcome-neutral decision table and deviation ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** The final agent receives clustered cards, both blinded/unblinded assessments, control results, audit results, and the registered protocol. It produces a table with one row per candidate mechanism:

- disposition;
- registered criteria passed/failed;
- supporting and opposing card IDs;
- independence/duplicate-family count;
- highest-severity unresolved objection;
- native/build value;
- anti-overfit result;
- required experiment and falsifier;
- protocol deviations affecting the row; and
- confidence band with cited evidence.

Every candidate in the registered manifest must have a row, including rejected and null candidates. Emergent candidates are allowed but labelled `exploratory`; they are not promoted to “confirmed” without a later registered replay. The prose executive summary is generated from this table, never the reverse.

This catches selective omission of losing candidates, evidence laundering through untraceable prose, conclusion-first rationalization, and hidden protocol deviations. I am **very likely (80-90%)** that schema completeness makes missing candidate dispositions and missing evidence links mechanically detectable, because the validator can reconcile stable IDs against the manifest. Whether the resulting table is more useful than a well-written expert narrative is **UNVERIFIED and likely (55-80%)**; §8 should score decision accuracy, traceability, and reader time, not formatting compliance alone.

## 5. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy fails

### 5.1 There is no treatment effect or stable null

A harness decision is not an average treatment effect. There may be one owner, one evolving tool ecosystem, and one non-repeatable decision. “Adopt mechanism X” has no natural control arm or population distribution. Therefore p-values, conventional effect sizes, and claims of statistical significance for the final recommendation do not transfer. Applying “power analysis” to the number of findings read without a defined random population and error event would be cargo cult statistics.

**Verdict:** causal-inference machinery does not transfer. Only randomized audit detection does.

### 5.2 Findings are not exchangeable experimental units

Audit units vary by orders of magnitude in severity and evidentiary quality. Many share sources or repeat the same claim. A single rare security or data-loss issue can dominate thousands of minor findings. Random samples protect against prevalent failures, not singular catastrophic ones. Deduplication itself requires semantic judgment, so even the cluster count is uncertain.

**Verdict:** ordinary-card sampling is acceptable only after severity stratification, source clustering, and a census of catastrophic items. A “299 cards means 95% coverage” statement would be false; 299 only gives the stated detection probability under the registered prevalence and independence model.

### 5.3 Blinding removes biasing information and valid evidence together

In a drug trial, allocation identity is often irrelevant to outcome measurement. In synthesis, provenance, measurement method, and author access can be central to credibility. Masking them permanently would make assessments worse. Even rhetorical conclusion may contain dependencies that the atomized claim omits.

**Verdict:** only staged blinding transfers. First score content properties that should be provenance-invariant; then reveal provenance and explicitly measure its effect.

### 5.4 Preregistration can freeze a bad ontology

Exploratory synthesis is expected to discover categories that were unknowable at Stage 0. A rigid protocol can suppress novelty, reward schema compliance, and cause important out-of-model findings to be discarded. Registered Reports preserve exploratory analyses, and the harness translation must do the same.

**Verdict:** the protocol is a comparison baseline, not a prison. Deviations and exploratory rows are first-class outputs; they simply cannot be silently relabelled as prespecified confirmation.

### 5.5 Negative controls may be unlike real findings

Language models can detect awkward synthetic controls or respond to tiny stylistic cues. Conversely, a supposedly null proposal may contain a real causal route the designer overlooked. The negative-control literature warns that imperfect comparability causes both false reassurance and false alarms ([Lipsitch et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/)).

**Verdict:** control families need held-out construction, style matching, and empirical validation against induced organic failures. A passed control suite is evidence about covered pathways, never a certificate of unbiased synthesis.

### 5.6 Agent runs are adaptive and not independent

The same model, prompt family, and context builder can correlate errors across hundreds of cards. Repeated agents may share training-induced priors; they are not independent replicates. Majority vote can amplify a common bias.

**Verdict:** count independence at the level of differing evidence and mechanically independent checks, not agent count. Power claims based on card-level independence are not calibrated until simulation demonstrates otherwise.

### 5.7 Compression can destroy interactions

Some design conclusions arise only from conjunctions: mechanism A is safe only if B runs before C; two individually weak signals jointly establish a defect; a recommendation’s value depends on an owner constraint in another unit. Atomic cards can sever these relations.

**Verdict:** dependencies and contradiction groups belong in the schema, but schema links may still be inadequate. Full-source reopening for high-severity rows and a random adjudication sample is mandatory. This is the largest unresolved transfer risk.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate

The proposal rides an existing skill/hook/MCP-style bus and uses files, hashes, ordinary model calls, and a small deterministic validator. It does **not** propose a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The append-only ledger is a run artifact, not durable semantic memory. The two assessors are staged invocations within the existing agent mechanism, not a new agent scheduler. The powered audit is a scriptable sampling step, not an experimental platform.

Complexity is deliberately bounded:

- one decision protocol;
- one JSONL evidence ledger;
- one masked derivative;
- a small hidden control set;
- one registered audit sample; and
- one decision table plus deviation log.

The mechanism earns its complexity only if §8 shows decision-level improvement. If controls catch only contrived failures, atomization loses cross-context reasoning, or reader time rises without reducing consequential errors, reject the borrow rather than adding more scaffolding. I am **likely (55-80%)** that a prototype fits the BUILD-SMALL boundary, based on the limited deterministic operations above. This is **UNVERIFIED; the spike’s line count, runtime, and number of moving artifacts settle it**.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 7.1 “Have a stronger model read all 12 MB”

This can be a useful experimental control, but it does not expose criteria drift, selective reporting, or a broken detector. Long-context access also does not guarantee attention to every item. Use it as a baseline arm, not as evidence that synthesis is complete.

### 7.2 Recursive summaries only

Recursive summarization reduces context size but compounds lossy choices and makes omissions hard to trace. Stable card IDs and manifest reconciliation are preferable because they retain an audit route. Narrative summaries can still be generated at the end.

### 7.3 Multi-agent majority vote

Votes are not independent evidence when agents share a model and inputs. Majority vote also hides minority catastrophic objections. Preserve disagreement and independent evidence families instead of treating agent count as sample size.

### 7.4 Full double-blind synthesis

Reject. Source method and provenance legitimately affect credibility. Use a masked first pass followed by explicit unblinding.

### 7.5 Apply power calculations to “percent of corpus read”

Reject. Corpus bytes and finding counts are not exchangeable units, and there is no defined effect. Power only the randomized fidelity audit with a named population and failure event.

### 7.6 Build a knowledge graph/vector memory/second orchestration runtime

Reject under the anti-overfit gate. The decision problem needs traceability and falsification probes, not a new durable substrate. A JSONL ledger with explicit dependency and duplicate-family fields is enough to test the borrow.

### 7.7 A trained critic or learned reward model

Reject. It introduces another opaque model and a training/maintenance burden before the simpler controls have demonstrated value. Matched controls test observable behavior without claiming that a critic is ground truth.

## 8. Decisive validation experiment (required before adoption)

This is the experiment that settles every major **UNVERIFIED** premise above.

### 8.1 Preregistered question

Does RBCS reduce consequential synthesis errors and increase traceability, at acceptable time/cost, versus a strong long-context baseline and recursive-summary baseline?

### 8.2 Corpus and gold construction

Use several completed, decision-rich corpora. Before running synthesis, independent human adjudicators construct a hidden set of consequential findings, contradictions, duplicate families, null units, and cross-file dependency chains. Add controlled perturbations:

- dropped-file and parser-failure cases;
- polarity flips;
- paraphrased duplicates;
- unsupported recommendation cards;
- provenance swaps;
- support/objection direction swaps;
- rare catastrophic findings;
- correlated clusters of extraction errors; and
- interaction-dependent conclusions that fail when atomized.

Controls must be authored separately from the synthesis protocol and held out from prompt tuning. Human adjudicators validating final outputs should be blinded to arm labels where feasible.

### 8.3 Arms

1. Strong-model direct long-context synthesis.
2. Recursive hierarchical summarization.
3. RBCS with ledger, staged blinding, controls, and registered audit.
4. RBCS ablations: remove preregistration, blinding, controls, or audit one at a time.

Use the same underlying model budget where possible and report unavoidable differences. Randomize corpus-to-arm order. Multiple runs are sensitivity analyses, not independent evidence if they share model and prompt.

### 8.4 Primary outcomes, fixed before results

- consequential omission rate at the finding-family level;
- false adoption rate for unsupported mechanisms;
- rate of lost or misresolved contradictions;
- rate of duplicate amplification;
- catastrophic-finding recall (census, not sample estimate);
- decision traceability: fraction of dispositions whose evidence links reopen valid source spans;
- undisclosed criterion changes; and
- blinded human rating of final decision correctness against the full-source adjudication.

Secondary outcomes: wall time, tokens/cost, human review minutes, false-alarm rate, control sensitivity, and reader time to verify a randomly chosen decision row.

### 8.5 Prospective sample planning

Do not invent an effect size. Pilot on a separate corpus to estimate baseline consequential error and between-corpus variance, then register a minimally important reduction and simulate the hierarchical design at the *corpus* level. The number of model runs is not the number of independent corpora. If too few corpora exist for a credible comparative effect estimate, report a feasibility study with intervals and failure-case detection, not a powered superiority claim.

For the within-run omission audit, precompute sample sizes using exact finite-population or cluster simulation. Demonstrate calibration with at least 1,000 seeded correlated-error replays before accepting a nominal detection probability.

### 8.6 Success and rejection gates

Adopt RBCS only if it reduces consequential errors on held-out corpora, retains all rare catastrophic controls, keeps source links valid, and adds an agreed bounded review cost. Reject or simplify it if:

- controls succeed but organic failures do not correlate with control outcomes;
- atomization increases interaction-dependent errors;
- direct full-context review is as accurate and materially cheaper;
- staged blinding changes judgments but worsens human-adjudicated decisions;
- nominal audit detection is badly miscalibrated under clustered errors; or
- the implementation crosses BUILD-SMALL and begins recreating an orchestration runtime.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this experiment can distinguish “mechanically neat” from “decision-improving,” because it includes decision-level outcomes, ablations, held-out controls, and explicit rejection conditions. I am only **roughly even (45-55%)** that available completed corpora provide enough independent decision instances for a well-powered comparative claim; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling measurement is the count of genuinely independent corpora plus pilot-estimated between-corpus variance**.

## 10. Bottom-line judgment

The strongest transferable idea from experimental design is **prospective separation of rules, measurement, and outcome, coupled with matched falsification probes**. It maps cleanly enough to synthesis to merit an experiment. The statistical language must remain narrow: power can size a randomized fidelity audit, not certify the architecture or the completeness of a heterogeneous corpus. Blinding must be staged, not permanent. Negative controls must share the pipeline and plausible bias structure, not merely be obvious decoys. Preregistration must expose deviations while protecting an exploratory lane, not freeze discovery.

My final confidence is **very likely (80-90%)** that this partial borrow is structurally valid: both research analysis and agent synthesis contain outcome-dependent discretion, subjective assessment, and pipelines that can be probed with matched null cases; the cited mechanisms directly address those structures. It is **UNVERIFIED whether RBCS beats a strong full-context model on consequential decisions; only the preregistered, blinded, controlled replay in §8 settles that claim**. If that replay fails, the right result is to keep only manifest reconciliation and source-linked decision tables—or reject the borrow entirely—not to elaborate the scaffold.
