# X069 — Cross-domain borrow: epidemiology → harness observability without a baseline

## Executive verdict

The borrow transfers, but only in a narrow and useful form: **evaluate a harness intervention as a stepped-wedge cluster-randomized rollout, while treating the outcome telemetry as a fallible surveillance system with a predeclared case definition and a blinded verification sample**. Epidemiology's contribution is not a disease metaphor. It is the combination of (1) prospectively manufactured contemporaneous controls, (2) stable rules for what counts as a case, and (3) explicit measurement of ascertainment error and base-rate-dependent positive predictive value.

This mechanism cannot recover the causal effect of a change that was already deployed everywhere before measurement began. It can create a valid baseline prospectively from the first rollout period onward. That distinction is central: “no historical baseline” is tractable; “no untreated observations, no randomizable rollout, and no repeatable workload” is not.

**Overall transfer judgment:** **very likely (80-90%)** to improve the truthfulness of evaluations for reversible harness changes that can be assigned across multiple sufficiently independent workers, repositories, task strata, or time blocks. Evidence: stepped-wedge trials explicitly randomize the date on which clusters cross from control to intervention, letting exposed and unexposed periods replace parallel arms, while surveillance guidance explicitly separates observed reports from true cases and evaluates sensitivity, predictive value positive, representativeness, and timeliness ([Hemming et al., BMJ 2015](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h391); [CDC surveillance evaluation guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/preview/MMWRhtml/rr5013a1.htm)). The wide band reflects an **UNVERIFIED** premise: harness tasks can be partitioned into clusters with low enough cross-cluster contamination and enough events for useful precision. The settling experiment is the two-week feasibility/power pilot specified below, including an intracluster-correlation estimate, a cross-cluster contamination audit, and minimum-detectable-effect simulation.

The analogy must be rejected for a singleton global harness change that instantly affects every task, cannot be switched off, or leaks substantially through shared prompts, caches, models, or human learning. In that setting there is no credible concurrent counterfactual, and epidemiological vocabulary cannot create one.

## 1. The mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 Public-health surveillance observes reports, not reality

Public-health surveillance is a continuous process for collecting, analyzing, interpreting, and disseminating health data for public-health action. Its data-generating system is itself an object of evaluation. CDC's framework asks evaluators to characterize simplicity, flexibility, data quality, acceptability, sensitivity, predictive value positive, representativeness, timeliness, and stability—not merely count notifications ([CDC, Updated Guidelines for Evaluating Public Health Surveillance Systems](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/preview/MMWRhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

That framing matters because a reported case count is the product of at least two processes:

1. the underlying occurrence of the condition; and
2. the probability that the surveillance pipeline notices, classifies, and reports it.

CDC defines surveillance sensitivity at both the case-reporting level—the proportion of true cases detected—and the outbreak level—the ability to detect changes over time. It also notes that a system with imperfect sensitivity can still monitor trends when sensitivity remains reasonably constant over time. This is a strong warning for harness observability: changing the detector at the same time as the intervention can manufacture an apparent improvement or deterioration even if the underlying failure rate is unchanged ([CDC surveillance evaluation guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/preview/MMWRhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

### 1.2 A case definition makes the outcome reproducible

Epidemiological surveillance uses a standard case definition to decide whether an observation is a suspected, probable, or confirmed case. The definition is for consistent counting and comparison, not necessarily for clinical treatment. CDC's published surveillance definitions explicitly describe uniform reporting criteria and caution that they are not sole clinical diagnostic or standard-of-care rules ([CDC, Case Definitions for Public Health Surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/PDF/rr/rr3913.pdf)). A current CDC example separates confirmed, probable, and suspect MIS-C using combinations of clinical, laboratory, and epidemiological criteria ([CDC, MIS Case Definitions and Reporting](https://www.cdc.gov/mis/hcp/case-definition-reporting/index.html)). CDC outbreak guidance likewise says a common case definition standardizes the cases of interest ([CDC, Outbreak and Case Definitions](https://www.cdc.gov/urdo/php/surveillance/outbreak-case-definitions.html)).

Case definitions deliberately trade sensitivity against specificity. A broad “suspected” definition catches more potential cases but produces more false positives. A narrower “confirmed” definition gives stronger evidence at the cost of missing events that cannot be verified. This is why a surveillance report should not silently collapse alerts, probable cases, and adjudicated cases into one number.

### 1.3 Base rates determine whether an alert deserves belief

Predictive value positive (PVP, equivalent here to positive predictive value) is the proportion of reported cases that truly have the event. CDC states that PVP depends on the case definition's sensitivity and specificity **and on prevalence**. It warns that low PVP creates pseudo-outbreaks, unnecessary investigations, and misdirected resources ([CDC surveillance evaluation guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/preview/MMWRhtml/rr5013a1.htm)).

The base-rate consequence is easy to miss. Consider a detector with 90% sensitivity and 95% specificity:

- At a 10% true failure rate, its PPV is about 66.7%: `(.90 × .10) / ((.90 × .10) + (.05 × .90))`.
- At a 1% true failure rate, its PPV falls to about 15.4%: `(.90 × .01) / ((.90 × .01) + (.05 × .99))`.

The detector did not become technically worse; the event became rarer. Therefore, after a successful harness improvement lowers the true failure rate, a fixed anomaly detector can produce a stream dominated by false positives. Raw alert volume is not a stable proxy for harm.

### 1.4 Sentinel surveillance spends verification effort where a census is impractical

Sentinel surveillance collects standardized, higher-quality observations from selected sites rather than pretending to enumerate every case everywhere. WHO influenza guidance describes sentinel systems with standard case definitions and specimens, while warning that catchment denominators may be unavailable and participating sites may not represent the whole population ([WHO, Global Epidemiological Surveillance Standards for Influenza](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/311268/9789241506601-eng.pdf); [WHO, Sentinel Sites and Specimens](https://www.who.int/teams/global-influenza-programme/influenza-covid19/sentinel-sites-and-specimens)). The transferable idea is purposeful, stable sampling plus known limits—not assuming a sentinel sample is a census.

For measurement accuracy, a harness analogue should go one step further than convenience-site surveillance: draw a probability sample of both detector-positive and detector-negative task traces and adjudicate them against a stronger reference standard. Sampling negatives is essential; reviewing only alerts can estimate PPV but cannot estimate missed-case sensitivity.

### 1.5 The stepped-wedge rollout creates the missing counterfactual

In a stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial, all clusters begin in control and cross to the intervention at randomized times until all receive it. The design embeds evaluation into a phased rollout. Each cluster contributes unexposed and exposed observations, while clusters not yet crossed over provide contemporaneous controls ([Hemming et al., BMJ 2015](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h391); [CONSORT extension for stepped-wedge cluster trials](https://www.bmj.com/content/363/bmj.k1614)).

This is the answer to “there is no baseline”: do not compare the new harness to anecdotes from an uninstrumented past. Start stable surveillance now, randomize rollout timing, and compare treated cluster-periods with not-yet-treated cluster-periods while adjusting for calendar time. The intervention date is randomized; the tasks are observed longitudinally.

The design is not self-analyzing. Calendar time is correlated with treatment because more clusters are treated later. Methodological sources explicitly require adjustment for secular trend, clustering, repeated observations, and potentially time-since-treatment; a naive analysis can report a beneficial effect merely because outcomes improved over calendar time ([Hemming et al., BMJ 2015](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h391); [Li et al., mixed-effects overview](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32631142/); [analysis of repeated cross-sectional stepped-wedge trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259174/)).

## 2. Concrete translation: Harness Stepped-Wedge Sentinel Evaluation (HSWSE)

### Proposal A — prospective randomized rollout ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a small rollout allocator on the existing invocation/configuration path. It does not orchestrate agents. It assigns an eligible cluster to `control` or `intervention` according to a precommitted stepped schedule and logs the assignment.

Minimum ledger fields:

```text
study_id
intervention_digest
cluster_id
task_id_hash
eligibility_stratum
assigned_sequence
period_id
actual_exposure
model_id_and_version
harness_config_digest
case_definition_version
timestamp
```

Cluster selection must follow the causal interference boundary, not convenience. Candidate clusters are independent worker processes, isolated worktrees/repositories, or task families whose prompts, caches, and operator feedback do not cross during the study. Individual tool calls are usually a bad randomization unit because a session exposed to a rule or skill cannot plausibly “unlearn” it on its next control call.

Use a deterministic keyed hash to implement the precommitted allocation after a statistician or simulation chooses the sequence. Record both assigned and actual exposure. Analyze assignment as the primary intention-to-treat effect; treat noncompliance as a secondary implementation question. Freeze the intervention digest and outcome definition for the study. Any change starts a new versioned study rather than silently moving the target.

**Forward-looking assessment:** **likely (55-80%)** that a deterministic allocator and append-only ledger fit within 200 lines if the existing hook/config bus can choose configuration per process. Evidence: the required logic is hashing, lookup, validation, and structured emission, not a scheduler or service. This is **UNVERIFIED** because the target bus's per-process configuration granularity is intentionally outside this ideation unit. The settling experiment is a 20-invocation dry run that proves alternating assigned configurations yield distinct configuration digests while an independent post-run trace field confirms actual exposure.

### Proposal B — versioned surveillance case definition

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Before rollout, publish a machine-readable case definition and analysis declaration. A useful three-level outcome hierarchy is:

**Suspected harness-harm case**

- a machine detector fires: tool error, retry loop, timeout, malformed output, excessive context growth, policy violation signal, or abnormal resource cost;
- sensitive, inexpensive, and explicitly not a confirmed defect.

**Probable harness-harm case**

- the suspected signal is joined by a mechanically independent signal: for example, an error plus a later owner correction; a retry loop plus test failure; a claimed file write plus independent filesystem absence; or a malformed output plus downstream parser rejection;
- the two signals must arise from different mechanisms, not the same parser run twice.

**Confirmed task-impact case**

- a blinded adjudicator or deterministic external oracle establishes that the requested outcome was not achieved, was materially wrong, or required avoidable repair;
- acceptable reference signals include hidden tests, artifact validation, reproducible command outcome, or a rubric adjudication performed without treatment assignment;
- treatment-arm knowledge is withheld where feasible.

Predeclare one primary outcome, preferably `confirmed task-impact cases / all eligible tasks`, and a small set of secondary outcomes: failure-free completion, owner repair minutes, task latency, tokens/cost, severe-event count, and suspected/probable/confirmed incidence. Keep all eligible tasks in the denominator, including crashes and early exits. Report benefits and harms together; an intervention that suppresses visible retries by causing silent wrong answers is not successful.

The primary outcome should not be a composite unless every component has comparable importance and the component results are also shown. If a composite is unavoidable, fix its weights in advance. Do not redefine “success” after viewing treatment data.

### Proposal C — blinded verification sentinel

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

At the existing post-task event, sample traces for adjudication using a reproducible seed:

1. sample a weighted fraction of suspected positives, stratified by severity and task family;
2. independently sample detector negatives from every treatment-period stratum;
3. strip assignment labels from the review packet;
4. apply the frozen reference rubric or deterministic oracle;
5. retain sampling probabilities so inverse-probability weighting reconstructs population estimates;
6. estimate detector sensitivity, specificity, PPV, negative predictive value, and their uncertainty by arm and period.

This sentinel catches ascertainment drift: if the intervention changes log formatting, retry visibility, task length, or reporting behavior, detector sensitivity or PPV may change even when true task harm does not. Review of detector negatives catches silent failures. Blinding reduces the chance that reviewers interpret ambiguous traces more favorably after seeing the new harness label.

**Forward-looking assessment:** **very likely (80-90%)** that stratified sampling and packet emission fit within 200 lines when adjudication itself uses an existing skill/hook or deterministic test. Evidence: the new mechanism is selection and redaction, while the expensive judgment remains on the existing bus. This is **UNVERIFIED** for reliable blinding because trace content may reveal the intervention. The settling experiment is a reviewer-guessing test: reviewers predict assignment from 100 redacted packets; if accuracy materially exceeds chance with a prespecified randomization test, label adjudication unblinded and use objective oracles for the primary endpoint.

### Proposal D — base-rate-aware report and promotion gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Generate a local report from the ledger and verification sample. No daemon, vector database, knowledge graph, trained critic, durable workflow engine, external observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is needed.

The report must include:

- eligible tasks, exclusions, missingness, and actual exposure by cluster-period;
- suspected, probable, and confirmed incidence with denominators;
- detector sensitivity, specificity, and PPV with uncertainty, not just alert counts;
- intention-to-treat effect estimate with confidence interval, adjusted for period and clustering;
- absolute risk difference and number of confirmed harms prevented or caused per 1,000 tasks, alongside any relative effect;
- latency, cost, and owner-repair burden, so harms cannot be displaced into another dimension;
- estimates by prespecified task strata and model/config version;
- negative-control outcomes and exposure checks, labeled as bias diagnostics rather than proof;
- missing telemetry and sampling coverage as first-class outcomes.

Promotion requires a predeclared decision rule. Example: promote only if the upper confidence bound excludes a practically unacceptable increase in confirmed harm, cost stays below its bound, detector sensitivity does not materially fall, and no severe-event stopping rule fired. This is a non-inferiority/safety gate, not “p < .05 means ship.” Exact margins require owner utility judgments and pilot variance; inventing them here would be false precision.

**Forward-looking assessment:** **likely (55-80%)** that this gate will prevent more false wins than it delays real improvements for medium/high-volume changes. Evidence: randomization protects exchangeability in expectation, period adjustment addresses secular trend, and verification separates event incidence from detection probability. This is **UNVERIFIED** because task volume, effect size, intracluster correlation, and owner loss function are unknown. The settling experiment is a simulation calibrated by the feasibility pilot, reporting false-promotion and false-rejection rates under null, beneficial, harmful, detector-drift, and workload-drift scenarios.

### Proposal E — negative-control diagnostics

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Predeclare one or two outcomes the intervention cannot plausibly affect but that share relevant measurement or workload causes. Examples must be intervention-specific. For a quoting-rule change, a candidate might be network connection refusal on calls whose arguments are generated outside that quoting path. For a local file-read protocol, a candidate might be remote HTTP status on an independently generated request. A treatment association with a credible known-null outcome signals calendar-time imbalance, workload shift, detector drift, or leakage.

Negative controls do not certify absence of bias. Epidemiology defines them as variables known to be causally unrelated to the exposure/outcome relation of interest; an unexpected association is evidence of bias, while a null association is only absence of evidence under strong comparability assumptions ([Lipsitch et al., Epidemiology 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/); [Shi et al., selective review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8118596/)). Recent work emphasizes that a negative-control outcome can create false reassurance unless it shares the relevant unobserved confounding mechanism and is truly unaffected by exposure ([Pitfalls of Using Negative Control Outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12644173/)).

**Forward-looking assessment:** **roughly even (45-55%)** that a valid negative-control outcome exists for any particular harness intervention. Evidence: harness changes often have broad effects through prompt length, tool selection, latency, or model behavior, violating the “known null” requirement. Every proposed negative control is therefore **UNVERIFIED** until a causal graph and perturbation test support it. The settling experiment is to inject the intended intervention in a replay sandbox and verify that the negative-control measurement is unchanged while deliberately perturbing the shared nuisance factor and verifying that both primary and control measurements respond.

## 3. What this catches that a simple before/after dashboard misses

This section describes failure classes, not claims that any specific local harness currently exhibits them.

1. **Secular improvement mistaken for treatment effect.** A model/provider update, warmer cache, easier task mix, or operator learning can improve outcomes after deployment. Not-yet-treated clusters observed in the same period distinguish this from intervention effect, subject to low spillover.

2. **Regression masked by harder control traffic.** Randomized timing balances expected task difficulty; prespecified strata reveal whether the new harness helps common tasks but harms rare, high-stakes ones.

3. **Pseudo-improvement caused by detector suppression.** If a new rule hides retries or changes error strings, raw error counts fall. The blinded negative-trace sample estimates whether sensitivity fell.

4. **Pseudo-outbreak caused by instrumentation change.** A new detector may increase alert counts without increasing confirmed task harm. PPV and confirmed-case incidence separate measurement from reality.

5. **Base-rate collapse.** As true failures become rare, even a seemingly accurate alert can have poor PPV. The report makes the false-positive burden visible instead of treating every alert as a defect.

6. **Outcome displacement.** A change can reduce tool errors but increase latency, tokens, owner repair, or silent wrong artifacts. A predeclared outcome family and confirmed endpoint expose the trade.

7. **Selective missingness.** If severe failures terminate before the normal completion event, completion-only telemetry looks better. The eligibility ledger supplies the denominator, and missing outcome status is itself reported.

8. **Carryover and learning.** Cluster-level assignment plus exposure-digest checks reveal when control tasks actually saw the treatment. If contamination is high, the study is invalid rather than merely “noisy.”

9. **Rare catastrophic harm hidden by averages.** A severe-case stopping rule and separate tail reporting avoid averaging one destructive event against many trivial wins.

10. **A winning average that fails the owner’s workload.** Stratified sampling and cluster-period reporting make representativeness inspectable. They do not magically guarantee it.

The ability to catch items 1-7 is **very likely (80-90%)** when the ledger is complete, assignment is honored, and verification sampling includes negatives. Evidence: each item maps directly to a measured design component—concurrent control, randomization, stable case definition, reference verification, denominators, or multiple outcomes. This is **UNVERIFIED** as an end-to-end claim; settle it with a seeded-fault validation in which analysts are blinded and must identify injected secular drift, detector suppression, missingness, and outcome displacement from synthetic study data.

## 4. Adversarial transfer audit

### 4.1 Structural correspondences that do transfer

| Epidemiology | Harness evaluation | Why the structure matches |
|---|---|---|
| Disease/event occurrence is partly latent | True task-impact failure is partly latent | Neither raw notifications nor raw tool errors are the target truth |
| Case definition | Versioned failure/success rubric | Both make classification reproducible across observers and time |
| Diagnostic/surveillance sensitivity and specificity | Detector recall and false-positive rate against adjudication | Both concern a fallible ascertainment channel |
| Prevalence affects PPV | True defect base rate affects alert credibility | Same conditional-probability identity |
| Sentinel verification | Stratified blinded trace audit | Both spend scarce high-quality measurement on a designed subset |
| Cluster-randomized staggered rollout | Randomized harness enablement across isolated task/worker clusters | Both create treated and not-yet-treated cluster-periods |
| Secular trend | Model, provider, workload, cache, and operator changes over time | Both confound treatment with calendar time in a stepped rollout |

These are mathematical or design correspondences, not aesthetic similarities.

### 4.2 Disanalogies and their consequences

**Errors do not behave like pathogens.** Harness failures do not have incubation periods, immunity, or biological transmission. Shared prompts and learning can create interference, but an epidemic transmission model would be cargo-cult borrowing. Consequence: borrow surveillance measurement and rollout design only; do not import reproduction numbers or compartmental disease models.

**There is no natural gold standard for “task success.”** Laboratory confirmation can sometimes establish infection more cleanly than a trace reviewer can establish whether an open-ended agent response served the owner. Consequence: deterministic tests and artifact validators should dominate the primary endpoint where available; subjective adjudication must be blinded, duplicated on a calibration subset, and reported with disagreement.

**The intervention can change the observer.** A harness change may alter what is logged, how the owner corrects, which tasks are attempted, or whether a session survives long enough to report. This is analogous to ascertainment changes but can be more tightly coupled than in routine surveillance. Consequence: freeze the detector, maintain an eligibility ledger outside the treated path, and verify both positives and negatives.

**Clusters may not be independent.** A shared model endpoint, global config, plugin cache, owner, or learned correction can spill treatment into controls. Epidemiological cluster trials also face spillover: when treatment affects nominal controls, contamination biases direct-effect estimates ([Halloran and Hudgens, spillover methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5837695/)). Consequence: define clusters at the actual interference boundary and run contamination checks. If that boundary is the entire owner/harness, reject the design.

**Task populations drift faster than many clinical populations.** New models and new work types can arrive during a short experiment. Period effects and task strata help, but treatment-by-time interactions may remain. Consequence: report the study's versions and observation window; do not generalize indefinitely.

**All clusters eventually receive treatment.** This is operationally attractive but removes a long-term untreated control. Delayed harms or learning curves can be weakly identified near the end. Consequence: choose enough periods after each crossover, model time-since-exposure, and retain a parallel randomized holdout when delayed harm is plausible and withholding is acceptable.

**Blinding is porous.** Trace text can reveal the treatment, and the owner cannot forget which harness is in use. Consequence: make objective oracles primary and empirically test whether redaction conceals assignment.

**Low event counts destroy precision.** Cluster randomization reduces effective sample size because outcomes within a cluster correlate. Stepped-wedge power depends on cluster count, observations, steps, and intracluster correlation; calendar adjustment can reduce precision ([Hemming et al., BMJ 2015](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h391)). Consequence: simulate power before claiming a result, publish uncertainty, and prefer paired replay for rare one-off workloads.

### 4.3 Hard rejection conditions

Do **not** use HSWSE to claim causality if any of these holds:

- fewer than a defensible number of independent clusters after measuring the interference boundary;
- treatment must be enabled globally and irreversibly at once;
- control tasks can read or inherit treatment prompts/configuration at meaningful rates;
- the task mix is unique and cannot be randomized, repeated, or stratified;
- outcome recording exists only inside the intervention being evaluated;
- adjudicators can infer treatment and no objective oracle is available for the primary endpoint;
- the event is so rare that the study cannot exclude owner-defined unacceptable harm;
- rollout order is chosen by convenience or expected difficulty rather than randomized as declared.

Under those conditions, a causal “helped” claim is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** justified by the stepped-wedge analogy. Evidence: the design's identifying comparison requires distinct treated and untreated cluster-periods, randomized timing, observable outcomes, and adjustment for time. The remaining 1-5% allows a different valid natural experiment not specified here. The settling experiment is not another statistical model; it is a feasibility audit that demonstrates multiple noncontaminated clusters and valid outcome capture. If it fails, stop.

The fallback is descriptive prospective surveillance: freeze a case definition, begin an eligibility denominator, audit positives and negatives, and report rates without causal attribution. For repeatable tasks, paired replay against frozen inputs can provide engineering evidence, but model nondeterminism and replay mismatch must be reported. This fallback is **likely (55-80%)** to improve measurement discipline but **unlikely (20-45%)** to identify the real-world causal effect on heterogeneous live work. Evidence: it improves ascertainment while lacking a concurrent randomized counterfactual. The **UNVERIFIED** replay premise is that frozen tasks remain representative; settle it by comparing replay and live task-feature distributions plus outcome disagreement on a sampled overlap set.

## 5. Feasibility pilot and decision protocol

### Phase 0: causal and measurement declaration

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Write, timestamp, and freeze:

- intervention digest and hypothesized causal path;
- eligible tasks and clusters;
- interference map (shared owner, prompt, cache, filesystem, model endpoint, and feedback paths);
- primary/secondary outcomes and suspected/probable/confirmed definitions;
- unit of randomization and rollout sequence;
- analysis estimand, period adjustment, clustering method, missing-data policy, and stopping rules;
- practical benefit, non-inferiority, cost, and catastrophic-harm margins;
- subgroup and negative-control analyses;
- minimum sample/precision rule.

### Phase 1: two-week measurement-only pilot

Run the detector and verification sentinel with no treatment variation. This is not an efficacy baseline; it estimates measurement properties and design inputs. Verify the detector with two mechanically independent signals:

1. positive controls: seed known-present benign fault patterns and prove the detector identifies them;
2. reference audit: blinded adjudication of a probability sample of ordinary detector-positive and detector-negative traces.

Estimate event prevalence, detector PPV/sensitivity, task volume, missingness, cluster size, intracluster correlation, reviewer disagreement, and likely contamination routes. A detector that cannot find seeded controls cannot support absence claims.

### Phase 2: simulation gate

Simulate the exact planned stepped schedule under:

- null effect;
- owner-defined minimum benefit;
- unacceptable harm;
- secular improvement and deterioration;
- cluster-specific time trends;
- detector sensitivity drift;
- 5%, 10%, and 20% exposure contamination;
- missing outcomes concentrated among severe failures;
- delayed and decaying treatment effects.

Report interval coverage, false promotion, false rejection, and stopping behavior. Do not run the causal trial if plausible scenarios produce unacceptable error rates.

### Phase 3: randomized rollout

Randomize the order only after clusters consent/qualify, conceal future switch dates where practical, keep surveillance identical across arms, and log actual exposure independently. Use a transition/wash-in period when a skill requires learning or caches need to turn over. Do not score transition periods as fully treated unless predeclared.

### Phase 4: analysis and promotion

Use intention-to-treat as primary. Include categorical period effects and clustering appropriate to the design; model time since exposure when delayed adoption is plausible. Publish absolute effects and uncertainty, measurement accuracy, protocol deviations, and negative results. Treat exploratory subgroup discoveries as hypotheses for the next trial.

The full pilot-to-report path is **likely (55-80%)** feasible without a new runtime if interventions can be selected at worker startup and task completion events already exist. Evidence: allocation, event emission, sampling, and offline analysis can ride existing boundaries. This is **UNVERIFIED** because those integration boundaries were deliberately not inspected in this cross-domain unit. The settling experiment is the Phase 1 dry run plus a source-level line-count and dependency audit of the three BUILD-SMALL components before approval.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate

The architecture is deliberately small:

```text
existing invocation/config path
        → deterministic rollout assignment + digest
existing task/tool telemetry
        → versioned case classifier
existing post-task hook
        → probability sample + blinded review packet
local append-only records
        → offline period/cluster-aware report
```

It rides the existing hook/skill/MCP/configuration bus. It does not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The statistical design is the mechanism; the software is a thin allocator, sampler, and reporter.

A daemon would be **very unlikely (5-20%)** to add enough validity to justify its state, failure modes, and operational burden here. Evidence: causal validity comes from allocation, case-definition stability, independent verification, and analysis—not from a continuously running service. This forward-looking claim is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it only if a thin implementation fails a documented requirement such as atomic cross-worker assignment that cannot be met by append-only records or an existing service. Until such failure exists, BUILD-LARGE is rejected.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Capture-recapture as the primary solution — rejected

Capture-recapture estimates unseen cases from overlap among incomplete case lists. In epidemiology it can estimate cases missed by all sources, but it depends on population closure, correct matching, and assumptions about dependence and heterogeneous capture probabilities; with more than two sources, log-linear models can address some dependencies but not the highest-order interaction ([Santé publique France, capture-recapture principles and limitations](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/docs/article/capture-recapture-method-in-epidemiology-principles-limitations-and-applications); [Hook and Regal, PubMed review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8935735/)).

Harness detectors are strongly dependent: owner corrections are more likely after visible tool errors; retries are caused by errors; test failures provoke both. Case matching can also be ambiguous, and the task population changes continuously. Capture-recapture might bound under-ascertainment in a carefully designed sub-study, but it does not estimate whether a harness intervention caused improvement. As a primary answer it does not transfer.

### Raw sentinel dashboard — rejected

A fixed panel can reveal trends cheaply, but convenience sentinels may not represent the owner's workload and do not create a counterfactual. WHO explicitly warns that sentinel catchment populations and representativeness can be uncertain ([WHO influenza surveillance standards](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/311268/9789241506601-eng.pdf)). Sentinel verification is retained only as the measurement layer inside a randomized rollout.

### Interrupted time series alone — rejected when there is no pre-period

Interrupted time series needs repeated pre-intervention observations to estimate the prior level/trend and remains vulnerable to coincident changes. With literally no baseline, it cannot reconstruct one. A stepped rollout prospectively produces contemporaneous untreated observations; if randomization is impossible, the correct conclusion is weaker evidence, not a more elaborate time-series model.

### Universal A/B randomization per tool call — rejected

It ignores carryover within a session, shared context, and intervention interactions. The treatment unit must be at or above the interference boundary, usually a session/worker/worktree/task-family cluster. More nominal samples do not compensate for invalid independence.

### “More alerts means worse” — rejected

Alert count conflates incidence, sensitivity, specificity, prevalence, and exposure volume. Epidemiological base-rate reasoning directly shows why the mapping fails.

## 8. Compact recommendation

Adopt **HSWSE** for the next reversible, medium/high-volume harness change only after the feasibility pilot demonstrates multiple noncontaminated clusters and adequate precision:

1. freeze a surveillance case definition and denominators;
2. verify a probability sample of detector positives **and negatives** against blinded/objective reference outcomes;
3. randomize cluster crossover dates;
4. analyze intention-to-treat with calendar-period and clustering adjustment;
5. report absolute confirmed-harm effects, measurement accuracy, cost/latency displacement, missingness, and uncertainty;
6. stop causal language if contamination, nonrepresentativeness, low power, or outcome ascertainment fails.

This recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)** superior to an uninstrumented before/after comparison for eligible changes. Evidence: it supplies the missing concurrent counterfactual and separately estimates the surveillance channel's error, directly addressing the two main reasons a no-baseline dashboard lies.
