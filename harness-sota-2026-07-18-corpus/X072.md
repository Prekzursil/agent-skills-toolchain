# X072 — Cross-domain borrow: law of evidence → agent reliability

## Executive judgment

**Verdict: transfer selectively; do not import the courtroom.** The useful legal invention is a layered **foundation-before-weight** discipline: before a factfinder may rely on an item, its proponent identifies the source, authenticates the item or the process that produced it, establishes any prerequisite facts, and bears a stated burden. Evidence law also has an unusually exact rule for negative inference: the absence of an entry supports nonoccurrence only when records of that kind were regularly kept; for public records, the search must be diligent. Those structures map closely to an agent turning a probe result into a claim.

The closest harness translation is an **Evidence Foundation Protocol**: every categorical `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, or `DONE` conclusion must carry a machine-readable claim packet; a final-response gate checks source identity, probe validity, scope, transformation lineage, a known-present detector control, and two mechanically independent signals. A tool error is evidence about the probe, never about the target. A zero-result is evidence about the target only after search coverage and detector sensitivity have foundations.

This selective transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent the specific class of single-unvalidated-probe errors named in the unit brief, because the gate rejects exactly the invalid inference form—`probe returned nothing/errored → target is absent/broken/done`—and Federal Rules 803(7), 803(10), 901(b)(9), and 104(b) independently encode the same prerequisites in their own domain ([Rule 803](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803), [Rule 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901), [Rule 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)). The size of the improvement in live agent work is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is the replay-and-shadow evaluation specified below**.

The full courtroom analogy—LLM prosecutor, LLM defense counsel, LLM judge, formal objections, and a persistent case-management service—does **not** transfer. It creates redundant model calls, role-play correlated errors, and a second orchestration runtime without importing the institutional independence, discovery powers, sanctions, or human factfinder that make adversarial process meaningful. It also fails the program’s anti-overfit gate.

## What evidence law solved in its own field

### 1. Foundation and conditional relevance: prove the prerequisite before the inference

Federal Rule of Evidence 104 divides preliminary admissibility questions from the ultimate assessment of evidence. Under Rule 104(a), the court decides preliminary questions about qualification, privilege, and admissibility. Under Rule 104(b), when relevance depends on a fact, the proponent must introduce proof sufficient to support a finding that the fact exists. The Advisory Committee’s examples are structurally exact: a statement offered to prove notice has no probative value unless the recipient heard it; a letter offered as an admission has no probative value unless the alleged author wrote or authorized it ([Rule 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)).

This separates two questions that ordinary reasoning often collapses:

1. **May this item bear on the proposition at all?** That is the foundation/admissibility question.
2. **If it may, how persuasive is it?** That is the weight question.

An empty search result has weight only after a prerequisite is established: the search actually covered the place where the sought item would appear and the detector recognizes items of that kind. Until then, the result is conditionally irrelevant to absence. Likewise, a test result has weight for `DONE` only if the test executed against the intended revision, environment, and behavior.

Rule 104 does not guarantee that admitted evidence is true. It only prevents the factfinder from using evidence whose logical connection depends on an unsupported predicate. This modesty is a strength of the analogy.

### 2. Authentication: establish identity and process accuracy

Rule 901(a) requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item “is what the proponent claims it is.” Its nonexclusive examples include testimony by a witness with knowledge, distinctive characteristics considered with the circumstances, and—most relevant here—evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result. The Advisory Committee explicitly identifies computer output as a use case for process-or-system authentication ([Rule 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)).

Authentication answers questions such as:

- Is this output from the command and environment claimed?
- Does the result belong to the intended repository, revision, host, account, or service?
- Is the output complete rather than truncated?
- Does the detector or tool produce accurate results for this class of target?

Rule 901’s own notes also warn that authentication is not sufficient for admission; other bars, including hearsay, can remain. In harness terms, a correctly attributed tool output can still be irrelevant, stale, incomplete, or incapable of supporting the conclusion.

### 3. Personal knowledge and hearsay: preserve source distance

Rule 602 permits a witness to testify to a matter only after evidence supports that the witness has personal knowledge. Its Advisory Committee note describes personal knowledge as a common-law insistence on reliable sources and distinguishes knowledge that a statement was made from knowledge that the statement’s content is true ([Rule 602](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_602)).

Rules 801 and 802 define hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered for its truth and generally exclude it unless another rule supplies an exception ([Rule 801](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801), [Rule 802](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_802)). Rule 803’s business-record exception does not simply bless records: it requires contemporaneous creation by or from someone with knowledge, regular keeping, a regular practice of making the record, foundation from a custodian or certification, and no indication that the source, method, or circumstances are untrustworthy ([Rule 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

The transferable insight is not “ban all secondhand agent information.” It is **retain source distance**. A tool’s direct output, an agent’s summary of that output, and a subagent’s assertion about a summary are not equivalent. Each transformation adds an opportunity for scope loss, mistaken attribution, or unsupported generalization. The receiving agent should be able to distinguish:

- direct observation of target state;
- direct observation of a tool response;
- deterministic derivation from retained inputs;
- another agent’s report;
- an inference or judgment.

Legal hearsay doctrine is full of policy-specific exceptions and does not map literally to machines. The narrower source-distance metadata does.

### 4. The law’s exact negative-evidence rule: absence is probative only after regularity or diligent search

Rule 803(7) allows the absence of an entry in a regularly conducted activity to prove that a matter did not occur or exist only when a record was regularly kept for matters of that kind and circumstances do not indicate untrustworthiness. Rule 803(10) similarly permits proof that a diligent search failed to disclose a public record, when offered to show that the record or event does not exist ([Rule 803](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)).

This is the most direct cross-domain borrow in the unit:

> No record is not evidence of no event until one establishes both that the event would ordinarily generate that record and that the relevant record system was searched adequately.

Translated to detection:

> Zero matches is not evidence of absence until one establishes that a present target would ordinarily generate a match, that the detector recognizes a known-present control, and that the intended search universe was covered.

This legal rule does not require two independent searches. The owner’s two-signal requirement is an additional engineering safeguard justified by the cheap repeatability of software probes and the observed error mode, not a doctrine smuggled in from law.

### 5. Chain of custody: retain identity and transformation lineage

NIST defines chain of custody as tracking evidence through collection, safeguarding, and analysis by documenting handlers, collection or transfer times, and purposes ([NIST CSRC glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/chain_of_custody)). NISTIR 7928 describes the chain as a chronological record from collection through disposition, says derived samples should have their own chain, and identifies core fields: description, case identifier, collection and storage locations, possession and purpose, actions performed, and date/time ([NISTIR 7928, section IV](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/NIST-IR-7928.pdf)). NIST’s digital-forensics glossary additionally describes reliable practice as including validated tools, repeatability, and reporting while preserving integrity ([NIST digital-forensics glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/digital_forensics)).

In its own field, chain of custody supports the identity and integrity of an exhibit across handlers and transformations. It does **not** prove that the exhibit’s content is accurate, that the collection was representative, or that the inference drawn from it is sound. A perfectly preserved output from a mis-scoped command remains perfectly preserved bad evidence.

### 6. Burden and standard of proof: allocate error risk by consequence

Evidence law assigns a proponent both a burden to produce support and a burden to persuade to the required level. Rule 702 now states that the proponent of expert testimony must demonstrate that it is more likely than not that the testimony is based on sufficient facts or data, uses reliable principles and methods, and reliably applies them. Its 2023 notes emphasize that an opinion must stay within what its basis and methodology support and caution forensic experts against absolute certainty when a method is subjective or error-prone ([Rule 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)).

Standards change with the cost of error. Congress’s Constitution Annotated explains that ordinary money-damages claims generally use preponderance, while serious interests such as involuntary commitment and termination of parental rights demand clear and convincing evidence; criminal guilt demands proof beyond a reasonable doubt ([Congress.gov](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt14-S1-5-4-9/ALDE_00013758/)). The Supreme Court’s reasoning in *Addington v. Texas* is especially portable at the abstract level: a standard of proof expresses the desired confidence in factual conclusions and allocates the risk of error according to the stakes ([*Addington v. Texas*, 441 U.S. 418](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/441/418/)).

The harness should not pretend legal verbal standards are numerical probabilities. It should borrow only the risk-allocation principle: an exploratory lead can survive on one weak signal if clearly labeled; a destructive action or categorical terminal report demands stronger, independent support.

### 7. Challenge rather than silent authority

Rule 104(e) preserves a party’s ability to introduce evidence relevant to weight or credibility even after a preliminary ruling ([Rule 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)). Rule 702’s reliability factors include testability, known or potential error rate, standards and controls, and reliable application, and its notes explain why gatekeeping remains necessary when the factfinder cannot independently assess a specialist method ([Rule 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)).

The useful engineering property is contestability: a conclusion should expose the probe, its scope, its controls, and its transformations so another check can attack the weakest predicate. The unhelpful imitation is to instantiate multiple LLM personas and call their debate “cross-examination.”

## Concrete translation: the Evidence Foundation Protocol

### Proposal 1 — Claim classes and burden matrix

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a compact policy declaring five claim classes and the support each requires. This is a normative configuration proposal, not a claim about currently supported configuration keys.

| Claim class | Example | Minimum foundation before categorical wording |
|---|---|---|
| `OBSERVED-POSITIVE` | “File X exists at revision Y” | Authenticated direct probe, exact scope, successful execution, retained output reference |
| `DERIVED` | “Error rate is 25.8%” | Authenticated inputs, deterministic formula, transformation record, unit/denominator |
| `REPORTED` | “Subagent says service is down” | Named source distance and citation; may not silently become direct observation |
| `NEGATIVE` | “No hook fired” or “skill is absent” | Search-universe foundation, successful detector control on a known-present item, and two mechanically independent signals |
| `TERMINAL` | “Broken,” “fixed,” or “done” | Operational definition, target-state check, independent behavioral/consumer check, and counterevidence search |

The policy should distinguish **burden of production** (“is there enough foundation to make the claim at all?”) from **strength/weight** (“how confident may the response be?”). If the burden of production is unmet, the agent must say `UNVERIFIED` inline and identify the settling experiment. It must not compensate by adding confident prose.

For risk-sensitive actions, add consequence classes:

- `L0 exploratory`: a lead, no mutation; one qualified signal may be reported.
- `L1 ordinary`: affects explanation or reversible local work; authenticated direct evidence plus scope.
- `L2 terminal/high-impact`: absence, brokenness, completion, destructive or externally consequential action; two mechanically independent signals plus the applicable control.

Adopting this matrix is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve conclusion hygiene because each target failure named by the brief falls into a class with explicit production requirements, and the rule is directly checkable rather than aspirational. The evidence is the close logical match to Rules 104(b), 803(7)/(10), and 901(b)(9). Its effect on false blocks or agent latency is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the shadow evaluation below**.

### Proposal 2 — Session-local evidence envelopes — UNVERIFIED: settle bus compatibility with a one-session hook spike

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

A post-tool hook writes a session-local JSONL envelope for evidence-bearing calls. It is not durable memory and is deleted with the task/session scratch space. Suggested minimal fields:

```json
{
  "evidence_id": "ev-...",
  "observed_at": "RFC3339 timestamp",
  "collector": "agent/tool identity",
  "source_kind": "direct-tool|deterministic-derivation|agent-report|inference",
  "tool_and_version": "...",
  "operation": "normalized operation name",
  "arguments_digest": "sha256:...",
  "scope": {
    "host": "...",
    "platform": "...",
    "cwd": "...",
    "repo": "...",
    "revision": "...",
    "account_or_service": "..."
  },
  "execution": {
    "started": "...",
    "ended": "...",
    "exit_or_status": "...",
    "timed_out": false,
    "truncated": false
  },
  "output_ref": "session-local reference",
  "output_digest": "sha256:...",
  "derived_from": [],
  "transform": null,
  "limitations": []
}
```

The envelope authenticates where an output came from and records its chain through summaries or calculations. It does not claim the output is true. The post-tool hook must explicitly classify nonzero exit, timeout, transport failure, parser failure, truncation, and schema mismatch as `PROBE-FAILED`. A `PROBE-FAILED` envelope may support “this probe failed”; it is inadmissible for “the target is absent,” “the target is broken,” or “the work is done.”

Sensitive arguments and output remain in the existing protected tool transcript; the envelope retains references and digests rather than copying secrets. Hashing supports accidental-mismatch detection, not truth, authorization, or tamper-proofing. Do not add blockchain: NIST IR 8387 notes that distributed-ledger overhead is too large for most evidence units, even in literal digital-evidence management ([NIST IR 8387](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2022/NIST.IR.8387.pdf)).

It is **likely (55-80%)** that an envelope can be implemented below roughly 200 lines if the existing hook bus exposes post-tool metadata and a session-local scratch path; evidence is that the design is append-only field normalization with no database or daemon. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a spike that captures one successful call, one failing call, one truncated call, and one derived summary, then verifies cleanup at session end**. If the bus cannot observe final status and scope, stop: do not build a parallel interception runtime.

### Proposal 3 — Foundation gate for `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, and `DONE` — UNVERIFIED: settle response-interception capability with a synthetic claim suite

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

A pre-response hook or required finalization skill accepts a small claim manifest:

```json
{
  "claim": "No matching skill exists in scope S",
  "class": "NEGATIVE",
  "scope": "S",
  "operational_definition": "what would count as a match",
  "evidence_ids": ["ev-a", "ev-b"],
  "control_evidence_id": "ev-control",
  "counterevidence_search": "ev-counter",
  "disposition": "admit|qualify|exclude"
}
```

The gate enforces the following inference rules:

1. **Errors do not invert into facts.** Timeout, connection refusal, parse error, schema mismatch, permission denial, truncation, wrong shell, or unknown status yields `PROBE-FAILED`, never a target-state conclusion.
2. **Negative claims require detector validation.** Before “none,” the same detector must find a known-present control of the relevant kind. The control must exercise the failure-prone layer: checking that an arbitrary file exists does not validate an MCP capability detector.
3. **Negative claims require search coverage.** The manifest names roots, branch/revision, hidden-file policy, account/tenant, pagination state, filters, and exclusions as applicable.
4. **Two signals must be mechanically independent.** They may not share the same parser, cache, index, wrapper, query expansion, or derived source. Re-running the same command, changing only formatting, or asking another LLM to interpret the same output is one signal.
5. **`BROKEN` requires intended behavior plus a control.** One signal demonstrates failure of the intended operation; another distinguishes target failure from environment/detector failure—for example, a direct health/status source plus a minimal functional operation through a different path.
6. **`DONE` requires state plus behavior.** One signal inspects the target state; the other exercises a consumer, test, or acceptance criterion. Running the same test twice is one signal.
7. **Conflicting evidence is retained.** The response must narrow the claim or report conflict; it may not silently discard the inconvenient signal.
8. **Failure to meet the gate produces a useful qualified sentence.** Example: “UNVERIFIED: search A returned no matches, but the detector was not validated against a known-present control; run experiment B.” The hook does not merely block output.

The gate need not parse arbitrary prose with another model. The agent supplies a tiny structured claim manifest for only three high-risk predicates. A deterministic checker verifies fields and independence declarations. This keeps the mechanism legible and cheap.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that these rules would catch a false absence caused by a broken detector, because the detector cannot pass without finding the known-present control; this follows directly from the gate logic and Rule 803(7)’s regular-record prerequisite. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch error-to-absence conversions because any unsuccessful execution is categorically ineligible. It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch wrong-scope conclusions because explicit scope and independent evidence make wrong host/revision/root visible, but both signals could still share a mistaken scope assumption. These predictions are based on rule coverage, not a live replay. The overall catch rate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the replay suite below**.

Whether a native pre-response hook can inspect or require the manifest is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a synthetic suite that attempts one valid and one invalid claim through every available response/finalization hook**. If no interception point exists, implement the gate as a required finalization skill plus a transcript linter; do not create a daemon.

### Proposal 4 — “Motion to exclude” recipes, not an LLM court

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

For each categorical claim class, configure a deterministic challenge recipe aimed at the weakest foundation:

| Claim | Challenge recipe | What exclusion means |
|---|---|---|
| `ABSENT` | Prove known-present control; enumerate scope; use a second mechanism with a different index/parser | Report search result as incomplete/UNVERIFIED |
| `BROKEN` | Separate target behavior from transport, credentials, permissions, dependency, and detector health | Report observed failure only; do not generalize to component brokenness |
| `DONE` | Restate acceptance criterion; inspect changed state; exercise an external consumer or acceptance test | Report work performed and remaining verification |
| Numerical summary | Recompute denominator and units from retained input using a second implementation or spot-check | Report calculation as UNVERIFIED or corrected |
| Source-based factual claim | Open the primary source and verify quoted scope/date/version | Attribute as secondhand or remove |

This is “motion to exclude” only as a mnemonic: the proponent must show why the evidence bears on the claim, while the challenge targets admissibility predicates. No adversarial persona is needed.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that recipes will produce better independence than a bare “double-check,” because they specify a different failure mechanism rather than merely another attempt. Evidence is the explicit independence constraint and Rule 702’s focus on testability, error rate, standards, controls, and application ([Rule 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)). Actual agent compliance is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by comparing recipe-selected probes with unguided second probes on the replay set and coding shared failure dependencies**.

## Examples: what the protocol catches that a single probe misses

These are synthetic mechanism tests, not claims about local harness behavior.

### Example A: broken capability detector reports zero skills

- Probe A returns an empty list with exit status 0.
- Current unsafe inference: “There are no skills.”
- Foundation gate: `NEGATIVE` requires a known-present control. The operator supplies the name/path of one known-present skill; Probe A still returns nothing.
- Disposition: Probe A is excluded as evidence of absence and admitted as evidence that the detector or its scope is faulty.
- Independent Probe B enumerates the underlying manifest store without Probe A’s parser/index.

The gate catches the precise distinction between absence of a record and failure of the system that would record or reveal it. This is the direct Rule 803(7) transfer.

### Example B: command-line failure becomes “no files”

- A file search fails before execution or returns truncated output.
- Current unsafe inference: “No matching files exist.”
- Envelope records `PROBE-FAILED` or `truncated: true`.
- Disposition: the evidence is ineligible for a negative claim. A second search must use a mechanically different traversal/path and validate a known-present file pattern.

This catches shell quoting errors, argument-length failures, permissions, and output caps without needing bespoke rules for each error string.

### Example C: stale or wrong revision becomes “feature absent”

- Probe A searches a worktree but the relevant path exists only on another revision.
- Current unsafe inference: “The feature does not exist.”
- Envelope forces revision and root into scope; search coverage is incomplete unless the claim itself is narrowed to that revision/root.
- Probe B inspects the authoritative revision through a different access mechanism.

This does not magically discover the correct revision. It prevents a local observation from silently widening into a global claim.

### Example D: transport error becomes “service broken”

- A client receives connection refusal.
- Current unsafe inference: “The service is broken.”
- Gate treats the error as evidence that the client-to-endpoint attempt failed.
- `BROKEN` needs a separate service-health/control-plane signal and a minimal functional check that separates credentials, routing, and client configuration from service state.

If the independent health source says healthy, the proper result is a scoped connectivity failure, not service brokenness.

### Example E: tests pass becomes “done”

- Probe A runs one test command successfully.
- Current unsafe inference: “Done.”
- `DONE` requires the acceptance criterion, authenticated revision/environment, and an independent consumer or state check.
- If no acceptance criterion was supplied, the response can say which tests passed but cannot claim task completion categorically.

This prevents “green command” from being mistaken for full task satisfaction while avoiding an unbounded demand for every conceivable test.

### Example F: two apparent checks share one broken source

- Probe A queries a search index.
- Probe B invokes a wrapper that queries the same index.
- Both return no result.
- Independence metadata reveals a shared index/cache/parser dependency; the pair counts as one signal.

This catches ritual duplication. Evidence count is not independence.

## Adversarial assessment of the analogy

### Structural correspondences that do transfer

1. **Both systems transform observations into consequential factual conclusions.** A tool result is not the conclusion; it is an evidentiary item from which the agent reasons.
2. **Both face source-identity errors.** Rule 901’s “what the proponent claims it is” corresponds to wrong environment, revision, account, tool, or truncated transcript.
3. **Both face conditional relevance.** An empty search matters only if the searched system would expose a present item; Rule 104(b) directly models this dependency.
4. **Both face provenance loss through intermediaries.** Rule 602/hearsay distinctions and chain of custody correspond to direct output versus summaries, subagent reports, and transformations.
5. **Both must allocate false-positive versus false-negative risk.** Stakes-sensitive burdens offer a principled alternative to uniform maximal verification.
6. **Both benefit from contestable records.** Retained scope, process, source, and limitations allow another investigator—or the same agent later—to challenge an inference.

These correspondences are **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally real because each maps a defined legal prerequisite to a defined information-processing dependency, with no need to assume human psychology. The evidence is the rule text cited in the preceding sections and the explicit logical mapping.

### Disanalogies that limit or defeat broader transfer

1. **Evidence law is not an optimal truth engine.** It also protects privilege, constitutional rights, procedural fairness, jury functioning, and trial efficiency. Some reliable material is excluded; some imperfect material is admitted. A harness optimizing factual reliability must not copy exclusions without their policy rationale.
2. **Admissibility is a low threshold, not correctness.** Rule 901 requires enough evidence to support a finding, not conclusive proof. Gaps may affect weight rather than admissibility. Therefore “authenticated” must never render a tool result true by fiat.
3. **The courtroom has institutionally distinct actors.** Opposing parties have incentives, discovery rights, lawyers, sanctions, and a neutral decision-maker. Multiple prompts to the same model share training, context, tools, and often failure modes. Persona debate is not mechanical independence.
4. **Software probes are often cheap and repeatable.** Trials cannot always recreate events or summon perfect evidence, which helps explain permissive thresholds and exceptions. An agent can often run a truly different probe in seconds. This supports safeguards stricter than general evidence law.
5. **Hearsay is anthropocentric.** Cross-examination, oath, demeanor, memory, perception, and sincerity have no literal analogue for deterministic tool output. The transferable unit is source distance and transform lineage, not the hearsay rule’s categories or exceptions.
6. **Chain of custody protects identity/integrity, not semantic validity.** Provenance cannot rescue a bad query, biased sample, invalid detector, or non sequitur.
7. **Law does not generally impose a two-independent-signal rule.** Special corroboration rules exist in narrow contexts; for example, the Supreme Court described the traditional two-witness/corroboration rule for perjury in *Weiler v. United States* ([323 U.S. 606](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/323/606)). Its narrowness is evidence against claiming a general legal doctrine. The owner’s engineering requirement must stand on its own observed failure rate and low cost of independent probing.
8. **Burdens of proof do not convert cleanly to model confidence percentages.** Legal verbal standards guide allocation of error risk; they are not calibrated posterior probabilities. The harness should retain the mandated confidence vocabulary, not label a claim “beyond reasonable doubt” and pretend calibration.
9. **An agent may be both proponent and factfinder.** A deterministic gate can enforce production requirements, but it cannot reproduce an independent judge’s judgment. Escalation to a human is warranted where consequences are irreversible or external.

The selective design survives these disanalogies because it borrows record structure and inference prerequisites, not legal outcomes or institutional role-play. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that importing a full simulated court would add cost without corresponding independence; evidence is the shared-model/shared-context architecture inherent in persona prompting and the absence of distinct legal powers in such prompts. The exact cost and error correlation are **UNVERIFIED; a settling experiment would compare token/tool cost and unique-error discovery between deterministic recipes and three-role model debate on the same seeded cases**. This experiment is lower priority because the full-court design already violates the stated anti-overfit gate.

## Anti-overfit gate

The counted design consists of:

- a claim/burden configuration;
- one small post-tool evidence-envelope hook, conditional on an already available hook event;
- one small deterministic claim-manifest gate or required finalization skill;
- session-local JSONL, not a database;
- verification recipes, not additional autonomous agents.

It rides the existing hook/skill bus and introduces none of the rejected components: no second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. There is no daemon and no durable cross-session memory. Complexity is concentrated at three high-risk predicates rather than applied to every sentence or tool call.

Do **not** build the following:

- a prosecutor/defense/judge multi-agent workflow;
- a general-purpose evidence graph;
- a tamper-proof distributed ledger;
- continuous semantic monitoring of all prose by another model;
- a universal ontology of every possible claim;
- a separate evidence service.

If the existing bus cannot supply status, scope, and a finalization interception point, the correct fallback is policy plus a linter and replay measurement. It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** justified to add a new runtime solely to implement this borrow, because the target error can be addressed by explicit claim structure and independent checks while the program’s anti-overfit gate rejects a second runtime.

## Evaluation plan and falsification criteria

### Phase 1: retrospective replay

Construct a labeled suite from known single-probe conclusion failures plus synthetic controls. For each case retain:

- the claimed proposition and correct disposition;
- the original probe result and hidden failure mechanism;
- whether the error is source identity, execution failure, scope, detector sensitivity, transformation, shared dependency, or unsupported generalization;
- the minimal second signal that would resolve it;
- one valid categorical conclusion that must not be blocked.

Replay the cases with policy only, policy plus envelopes, and policy plus gate. Measure:

- **false categorical conclusion escape rate**: wrong `ABSENT/BROKEN/DONE` claims that pass;
- **false block rate**: well-supported categorical claims rejected;

### Phase 2: shadow mode

- all proposed categorical negatives;
- all `BROKEN` and `DONE` claims;
- all gate disagreements;
- a random sample of gate passes;
- a random sample of outputs in which the gate saw no manifest, to measure evasion/coverage.

The last sample is essential: a perfect checker of submitted manifests is useless if agents simply omit manifests.

## Failure modes of the proposed mechanism

1. **Paper compliance.** An agent can populate fields with vague scope or claim two signals are independent when they are not. Mitigation: enumerate common dependency types and audit a sample; do not add a semantic critic.
2. **Known-present control selection bias.** A control may exercise an easy path while the target uses a different parser, provider, namespace, or permissions. Mitigation: require the manifest to state which failure-prone layer the control validates.
3. **Correlated sources.** Two tools can share the same index, API, cache, or generated file. Mitigation: record dependency notes and prefer origin data plus behavioral observation.
4. **Scope explosion.** Proving universal absence can be impossible. Mitigation: narrow the sentence to the authenticated scope instead of demanding infinite search.
5. **False confidence from hashes.** Digests show byte identity relative to a recorded digest, not source truth. Mitigation: keep authentication, detector validity, and inferential support as separate fields.
6. **Over-strict completion semantics.** Some tasks have no external consumer. Mitigation: require an acceptance criterion and the best mechanically distinct state/behavior checks available; otherwise qualify completion.
7. **Stale evidence.** Correctly captured evidence can age. Mitigation: record time and revision and require freshness appropriate to the claim.
8. **Evidence laundering through summaries.** A subagent’s confident summary may hide failed probes. Mitigation: source-distance classification and `derived_from`; reports without evidence references remain `REPORTED`.
9. **Gate becomes a guardrail framework.** Expansion to every statement and arbitrary semantic policy would violate the anti-overfit rationale. Mitigation: fixed narrow predicates and deterministic fields.
10. **The legal metaphor becomes authority theater.** Terms such as “admissible” can imply correctness. Mitigation: documentation must state that passing foundation permits consideration; it does not establish truth.

## Bottom line

Law of evidence has not solved “truth from two probes.” It has solved a prior and more fundamental problem: **an observation cannot rationally support a conclusion until its identity, producing process, prerequisite facts, source distance, and applicable burden are exposed**. Its rule for negative records is a particularly strong fit: absence is probative only when the record would ordinarily exist and the search is adequate.

That mechanism transfers. The institution around it does not. Build a tiny foundation protocol on the existing bus, keep evidence session-local, enforce known-present controls and mechanical independence for `ABSENT/BROKEN/DONE`, preserve conflicts, and qualify unsupported claims with a named settling experiment. Do not build a court.
