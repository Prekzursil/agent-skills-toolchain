# B006 — Groundedness engineering for causal claims

**Unit:** B006  
**Question:** What evidence standard applies to “X caused Y,” and how can the harness stop reporting correlation as cause?  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Subject access:** read-only Linux bundle at `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle`  
**Only write:** this artifact  

## Executive verdict

The harness must not try to decide causality from prose. It must make the *burden of proof* machine-checkable. A causal sentence is an assertion about an intervention or counterfactual, not merely about two events co-occurring, occurring in order, or sharing a plausible story. The minimum defensible local-software claim is therefore a scoped paired experiment: reproduce Y with X in a recorded state, intervene on X while holding the relevant state fixed, observe Y change, and bind both runs to the same claim. For nondeterministic outcomes, repeat, randomize or block run order, report raw counts and uncertainty, and avoid a deterministic claim. For a general population-level effect, specify the estimand and the identification assumptions; observational logs alone do not silently become a causal study.

The current harness has useful ingredients—normalized events, a ledger, fresh receipts, exact failing-command fingerprints, and shadow-first gates—but its receipts prove only that a command-shaped string received a pass-like result. They do not represent a causal treatment, control, outcome, context, or alternative explanation. Worse, a search or echo can currently mint a passing receipt. A read-only probe returned:

```text
known-positive ('test', 'pass', 2, 0)
search-only ('test', 'pass', 0, 0)
echo-only ('test', 'pass', 0, 0)
contradictory-text ('run', 'pass', 0, 0)
```

The probe called `classify_receipt` directly with a real `pytest -q` control, `rg -n pytest modules.py`, `echo test`, and a zero-exit run whose stdout said `ERROR application failed`. Its mechanism is visible in `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/ledger.py:252-310,354-403`: receipt class comes from tokens in the command, and exit zero is accepted before unnumbered failure text. A `fixed` claim accepts either a `test` or generic `run` receipt at `ledger.py:109-121`; the Stop gate unions classes across claim families at `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2197-2228`. Those are execution receipts, not causal receipts.

The recommended end state is a compact **causal-claim card plus causal receipt** on the existing ledger/hook bus. It explicitly records the treatment X, outcome Y, scope, claim strength, baseline/control run, intervention run, state fingerprints, repetitions, and assumptions. Deterministic validation checks whether the evidence is adequate for the *wording actually used*. A broad language extractor may nominate unmarked causal sentences in shadow mode, but it must never itself block or declare a claim false. This separation is the main precision control: semantic extraction proposes “this may be a causal assertion”; deterministic evidence validation decides only “the declared evidence fields do or do not license this strength.”

This does not eliminate hallucination. It makes unsupported causal claims detectable, reviewable, and cheap to downgrade. Any promise to eliminate causal hallucination would be false because implicit causal language, omitted claim cards, bad measurements, unmeasured confounding, invalid counterfactuals, and model/verifier errors remain possible.

**Overall confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that the typed-evidence design will be materially safer than adding another prose-matching guard. Evidence: four locally observed detector implementations confused use with mention and produced 9+ false fires, including an instruction to discard a successful result (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-87`); the existing receipt bus admits non-probative passes by direct probe; and experimental-design references distinguish association from interventions. **UNVERIFIED** are the local catch fraction, false-positive rate, and cost per causal claim; the settling experiment is the labeled, replayable causal-claim benchmark specified in “Evaluation and promotion gates.”

### 1.3 External research basis

The evidence standard is grounded in primary or authoritative sources:

- Hernán and Robins define causal effects through counterfactual outcomes and show that observational identification requires assumptions such as exchangeability, positivity, and consistency: [*Causal Inference: What If*](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/miguel-hernan/wp-content/uploads/sites/1268/2024/04/hernanrobins_WhatIf_26apr24.pdf), especially Chapters 1-3.
- NIST distinguishes correlation from causality and points to designed experiments for establishing causal relationships: [NIST/SEMATECH, Experiments and Experimental Design](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section1/ppc136.htm). NIST also emphasizes randomization and replication and defines confounding: [DOE glossary](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section7/pri7.htm).
- Causal Testing applies counterfactual executions to software root-cause analysis rather than treating a suspicious execution correlation as a cause: [Johnson, Brun, and Meliou](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06991).
- Fine-grained factuality work decomposes long prose into atomic claims before verification: [FActScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251). VeriScore shows why extracting every sentence as a verifiable fact is itself error-prone: subjective, trivial, vague, and unverifiable content gets over-extracted, and performance varies by domain: [VeriScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19276).
- A causal-language classifier can distinguish no-relationship, correlational, conditional-causal, and direct-causal sentences in a particular scientific corpus, but that is evidence for *candidate extraction*, not proof that the relation is true or that the classifier transfers to coding-agent prose: [Yu, Li, and Wang](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1473/).
- SAFE reports useful but imperfect agreement for automated fact checking and incurs search/model cost: [Google DeepMind, Long-form factuality](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/85420/). This supports escalation as an audit aid, not an always-correct causal arbiter.

### 2.2 A current receipt is not adequate evidence for “caused”

`classify_receipt` extracts any string under `command`, `cmd`, `script`, or `argv`; classifies it by substring/word token; then labels it pass on explicit exit zero before considering generic negative output (`ledger.py:262-310,354-403`). The `has_fresh_receipt` query correctly imposes sequence freshness after the last recorded mutation, but intentionally accepts **ANY** required class (`ledger.py:1093-1117`). Freshness answers “did a pass-shaped event occur after the last recognized edit?” It does not answer “would Y have differed under an intervention on X?”

This distinction is load-bearing:

- `baseline fail → changed bundle pass` supports the narrow claim that the *bundle intervention* changed the measured test outcome in those two executions, subject to determinism and state equivalence.
- It does not isolate which hunk, setting, dependency, environmental change, or interaction caused the original failure.
- A passing test after an edit, without a failing baseline on the same reproducer, establishes compatibility with the observed pass, not a causal effect of the edit.
- A generic `run` receipt cannot establish causal attribution merely because “python” appeared in argv.

This conclusion is **almost certain (90-99%)** for the current code path. Evidence: direct pure-function probes, source control flow, and the absence of causal fields in the receipt class/map. The remaining uncertainty concerns provider envelopes not represented by the four probes; it does not alter the missing counterfactual structure.

### 2.3 Text matching has already failed the precision requirement

The operational defect report records four implementations with the same use-versus-mention flaw: a destructive-command guard read a markdown body instead of the tool verb; a stub detector matched prose and negation; a marker scanner matched discussion of a marker; and a tool sentinel matched `timedout` in a JSON key whose value was false, despite `ok:true` (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-82`). It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that a blocking regex over causal phrases would recreate this defect class. Evidence: 9+ diagnosed real fires across four detectors and three failed rewordings (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:24-41,52-87`). ## 3. What exactly counts as a causal claim?

The harness needs to distinguish the *language strength* because each strength licenses a different conclusion.

### 3.1 Atomic causal claim form

Normalize a causal assertion to:

```text
claimant asserts that intervention X=x versus comparator X=x0
changes outcome Y from y0 to y
for unit/population U, under context C, during validity window T,
with modality M and strength S.
```

Required semantic roles:

- **X / treatment:** the factor allegedly changed: a code hunk, setting, plugin set, command construction, prompt, policy, model, or environmental condition.
- **Comparator:** what X is compared with. “Without the patch,” “with the orphan directories removed,” or a named baseline commit is meaningful; an omitted comparator is not.
- **Y / outcome:** an operational measurement, not “it got better.” Examples: `Glob` launch returns error 206, false-warning rate per 100 Stop events, task success under a fixed evaluator.
- **Unit and scope:** this exact reproduction, this repository/configuration, this workload distribution, or a broader population.
- **Context:** relevant versions, input, workspace/tree hash, environment, concurrent processes, and random seed where applicable.
- **Modality:** `actual_contributor`, `necessary_in_context`, `sufficient_in_context`, `average_effect`, `prevented`, `enabled`, `sole_cause`, or `root_cause`.
- **Validity window:** causal conclusions can expire when model, hook, tool, dependency, or environment versions change.
- **Evidence and assumptions:** bound receipts and explicit identification assumptions.

Atomicity is essential. “The orphan directories inflated argv, caused error 206, slowed Glob, and made Codex unsafe” is at least four claims with different outcomes and potentially different evidence. A single supported link must not launder the rest. FActScore’s atomic-fact approach provides relevant precedent, while VeriScore demonstrates that automated decomposition itself needs abstention and domain calibration.

### 3.2 Assertion ownership and polarity

The extractor must preserve who is asserting what:

| Text | Harness interpretation |
|---|---|
| `X caused Y.` | Direct causal assertion by the agent. |
| `The owner states that X caused Y.` | Attributed claim; evidence proves the attribution, not X→Y. |
| `X did not cause Y.` | Direct causal *refutation*; still needs evidence if load-bearing. |
| `X may have caused Y.` | Causal hypothesis, not an established effect. |
| `Did X cause Y?` | Question, no assertion. |
| `If X caused Y, Z would follow.` | Conditional antecedent, no assertion that X caused Y. |
| `Y followed X.` | Temporal observation, not cause. |
| `X is correlated with Y.` | Association claim, not cause. |
| `Removing X made Y disappear in three randomized A/B/A runs.` | Reports interventional evidence; causal strength still depends on controls and scope. |
| ``The detector matched `X caused Y`.`` | Mention of text, not an asserted relation. |

The local false-fire record makes these roles mandatory, not decorative (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:20-22,52-82`).

### 3.3 Words that imply cause without saying “cause”

Causal claims include “because,” “due to,” “led to,” “resulted in,” “explains,” “responsible for,” “drove,” “prevented,” “enabled,” “made,” “fixed by,” “root cause,” and some uses of “therefore.” They can also be implicit: “After deleting the cache, the failures stopped” is merely temporal unless the surrounding answer treats deletion as explanatory.

Therefore causal vocabulary is useful only as a high-recall *candidate generator*. It is not a truth detector, not an adequate assertion detector, and not a safe blocker.

## 4. Evidence ladder and licensed wording

The harness should validate the claimed strength, not collapse everything to verified/unverified.

### C0 — Observation only

**Evidence:** one or more direct measurements with provenance.  
**Licensed wording:** “X and Y co-occurred,” “Y began after X,” “the trace contains X before Y,” “X predicts Y in this sample.”  
**Not licensed:** “X caused Y,” “X explains Y,” “X was responsible for Y.”

Temporal order is necessary for many causal stories but not sufficient. A trace can establish that data/control flowed along a path, yet still fail to show that changing X would change Y. A correlation can prioritize experiments; it cannot silently become their conclusion. NIST explicitly distinguishes correlations from causal relationships and recommends designed experiments for the latter.

### C1 — Causal hypothesis

**Evidence:** C0 plus a plausible mechanism or contrast and named alternatives.  
**Licensed wording:** “X may explain Y,” “X is a causal candidate,” “the leading hypothesis is X, because …; experiment E would settle it.”  
**Required inline marker:** `UNVERIFIED` and the experiment that would settle it.  
**Not licensed:** an unqualified cause claim.

This rung is deliberately cheap. It lets an agent reason causally without pretending diagnosis is complete.

### C2 — Context-specific causal contribution

**Minimum evidence for deterministic/local software:**

1. Define one operational outcome Y and one intervention X.
2. Record a baseline/control state fingerprint and reproduce Y with X at the comparator level.
3. Apply a bounded intervention on X.
4. Record an intervention-state fingerprint and re-run the *same outcome oracle* under equivalent relevant state.
5. Observe the prespecified outcome change.
6. Show that the tested state difference is exactly the declared intervention or disclose residual differences.
7. Bind both outcomes to one claim ID and preserve raw artifacts.

**Preferred strengthening:** A/B/A or B/A/B reversal (rechallenge), minimization/delta debugging, an independent outcome signal, and a mechanism trace consistent with the intervention.

**Licensed wording:** “In reproduction R, changing X while holding recorded context C fixed changed Y; X was a causal contributor in that reproduced case.”  
**Not licensed:** “X always causes Y,” “X is the sole/root cause,” or a population-level effect.

For a patch, `pre-patch fail → post-patch pass → regression pass` supports that the patch bundle causally changed the specified reproducer in the tested state. It does not, without ablation, establish that a particular explanation inside the patch was the cause. Causal Testing is direct research precedent for generating counterfactual executions in defect diagnosis.

### C3 — Replicated or population-level causal effect

**Minimum evidence:** prespecified treatment/comparator/outcome; representative units or explicitly narrow population; randomized assignment when feasible; replication; raw group counts; effect size and uncertainty; exclusions and failures; interference/contamination assessment; and state/version provenance.

**Observational exception:** a causal claim from nonrandomized data must name the identification design and its assumptions—at minimum consistency, positivity, exchangeability/no unmeasured confounding as applicable, plus method-specific assumptions such as parallel trends or exclusion restrictions. It must report sensitivity/robustness checks and retain an `UNVERIFIED` tag for assumptions not empirically testable. Hernán and Robins show why the causal interpretation of standardized or weighted observational means depends on these assumptions.

**Licensed wording:** “Under design D and assumptions A, X changed/was estimated to change Y by E [interval] in population U.”  
**Not licensed:** universal or sole-cause language outside the study scope.

For harness changes, a paired randomized task-suite experiment is the usual target: same tasks/evaluator/budgets, treatment on/off, order randomized or blocked by task, enough independent repetitions to characterize stochasticity, and outcomes joined by run ID. Raw before/after production trends are C0 unless a credible design identifies the effect.

### C4 — Necessary, sufficient, sole, or “root” cause

These are different claims and should not share a badge:

- **Necessary in context:** Y does not occur without X across the tested relevant states.
- **Sufficient in context:** introducing X produces Y across the tested relevant states.
- **Sole cause:** no other causal contributor is required in the defined model and scope.
- **Root cause:** an organizational label for a chosen upstream actionable contributor; it is not automatically a unique scientific cause.

**Minimum evidence:** C2/C3 as appropriate, explicit modality, factorial or systematic alternative-cause testing, interaction assessment, negative controls, and a bounded causal model. Reversal alone does not prove sole cause; two factors can be jointly necessary or interact.

**Licensed wording:** must include the scope, e.g. “X was necessary for Y in tested state set S.” Avoid bare “the root cause” unless the report defines the stopping rule and alternatives considered.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that reserving bare “root cause” for C4 will reduce overclaiming in this workspace. Evidence: the known defects include several wrong single-signal diagnoses (`single-signal-verification.md:8-37`), while software failures commonly involve environment and interactions. **UNVERIFIED:** the magnitude and any effect on useful diagnosis speed; the settling experiment is an A/B annotation of 100 historical diagnoses for correctness, time-to-action, and owner-rated clarity.

## 5. Evidence that does *not* establish cause

The following are useful signals but are insufficient alone:

1. **Post hoc timing:** Y stopped after X changed.
2. **Correlation:** X and Y vary together in logs.
3. **Model attention, feature importance, or explanation prose:** predictive attribution is not an intervention.
4. **One green test:** compatible with success; no failing comparator.
5. **A different test pre/post:** outcome oracle changed, so the contrast is uninterpretable.
6. **A/B with uncontrolled state drift:** treatment is confounded with the drift.
7. **A mechanism story:** plausibility is not counterfactual evidence.
8. **A stack/dataflow trace:** shows a path, not necessarily causal necessity or sufficiency.
9. **Many agents repeating one claim:** shared prompt, model, and evidence are correlated signals. `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/rules/common/single-signal-verification.md:34-37` explicitly treats repeated subagent assertions from one stale input as one lens.
10. **A citation that merely mentions X and Y:** the cited source must support the same direction, modality, population, comparator, and outcome.
11. **A receipt whose command name looks relevant:** current source and direct probes show why lexical command classification is gameable and accidental.
12. **A successful intervention without isolation:** it establishes the changed bundle as a candidate cause of the measured difference, not the claimed internal component.

The correct downgrade is informative, not punitive: “observed,” “associated,” “consistent with,” or “causal hypothesis; experiment E would settle it.” The detector should never tell the agent to discard sound evidence merely because it cannot establish causality.

## 6. Proposed mechanism

### Proposal 1 — Causal wording contract

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Add one concise shared rule: any direct causal assertion in a load-bearing conclusion must either (a) cite a causal-claim ID whose completed rung licenses the wording, or (b) be downgraded inline to C0/C1 with `UNVERIFIED` and the settling experiment. “Root/sole/necessary/sufficient” additionally requires explicit modality and C4 evidence. Quoted, conditional, interrogative, and attributed claims preserve their roles and are not treated as the agent’s direct assertion.

This rule should include the evidence ladder, a ten-line wording table, and examples from this workspace. It must not require a rigid structured output on every response: StructuredOutput has a pinned 22.9% error rate, including 294 schema mismatches, and over-strict fan-out schemas are already a measured defect. The card is required only when the agent chooses causal wording for a load-bearing conclusion.

**Expected detector coverage/cost:** the rule itself has no mechanically guaranteed catch fraction and near-zero runtime cost. **UNVERIFIED:** compliance and behavioral effect; settle with 100 blinded historical causal-claim prompts split between rule-on/rule-off, scored for unsupported direct causal claims and unnecessary hedging.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that the rule improves author self-calibration but is insufficient alone. Evidence: the existing single-signal rule clearly states a comparable discipline yet the brief asks whether it can be made mechanical (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`); rules are useful guidance but can be omitted after compaction or fan-out.

### Proposal 3 — Add a `causal_receipt` that binds paired interventions

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

A causal receipt is a composite over existing execution artifacts, never a new “pass” inferred from a phrase. Minimum fields:

```json
{
  "kind": "causal_receipt",
  "claim_id": "cc-...",
  "design": "paired|aba_reversal|randomized_blocked|observational_identification",
  "oracle_id": "reproducer hash + parser/version",
  "baseline": {"state_hash": "...", "x": "x0", "y": "...", "artifact": "..."},
  "intervention": {"state_hash": "...", "x": "x", "y": "...", "artifact": "..."},
  "rechallenge": {"state_hash": "...", "x": "x0", "y": "...", "artifact": "..."},
  "declared_diff": ["..."],
  "residual_diff": ["..."],
  "repetitions": {"baseline": 0, "intervention": 0, "order": []},
  "effect": {"estimate": null, "interval": null, "raw_counts": {}},
  "negative_controls": [],
  "assumptions": [],
  "completed_rung": "C0|C1|C2|C3|C4"
}
```

Validation rules:

- Same `oracle_id` in comparator and treatment arms.
- Evidence artifacts exist and hashes match.
- State hashes differ only by `declared_diff`, or `residual_diff` is nonempty and caps the result at C1.
- Outcome order matches the prespecified design; a later generic green receipt cannot be paired accidentally.
- C2 requires a failing/affected comparator and changed treatment outcome. If the outcome is stochastic, one pair caps at C1.
- C3 requires raw replicated groups, declared assignment/blocking, effect estimate/uncertainty, and population scope.
- C4 requires explicit modality and alternative/interaction evidence.
- Any subsequent relevant mutation invalidates the receipt until state fingerprints are re-established.
- A receipt with contradictory transport/application status is invalid, not pass. The current exit-zero-first behavior must not authorize causal claims.

This replaces the *causal interpretation* of generic receipts; it does not replace generic receipts for their legitimate “command ran” purpose.

**Expected detector coverage/cost:** for submitted claims, the validator deterministically catches missing paired arms, mismatched oracle IDs, stale state, missing artifacts, and rung/wording mismatch. It cannot detect a bad oracle, an omitted confounder, fabricated but internally consistent external data, or an omitted claim. **UNVERIFIED:** the fraction of real false causal claims falling into the mechanically detectable classes; settle by error taxonomy on the labeled benchmark. Cost is two or more executions per C2 claim plus hashing/validation; the experimental executions dominate.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that paired claim-bound receipts materially improve causal groundedness over generic fresh-green receipts. Evidence: causal effects are defined by contrasts/interventions; NIST recommends designed experiments; Causal Testing uses counterfactual executions; and local generic receipts accept search/echo controls. The residual uncertainty is the frequency with which equivalent state can be cheaply reconstructed in this owner’s diverse tasks.

### Proposal 4 — Two-stage candidate extraction; never let extraction equal verdict

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Stage A is a candidate extractor over the final assistant message. It outputs atomic spans, assertion owner, polarity, modality, and `direct|attributed|conditional|question|mention`. It uses causal vocabulary and semantic context, but initially writes only shadow records. Stage B is deterministic: for direct load-bearing assertions, find a claim card and compute whether evidence licenses its rung. Only Stage B may advise; neither stage may say the claim is false.

Precision controls:

1. Parse and exclude fenced code, inline code, quoted evidence, blockquotes, citations, tables of examples, detector logs, and explicit “the phrase …” mentions before candidate scoring.
2. Preserve negation, questions, conditions, epistemic modals, and attribution rather than stripping them during atomic decomposition.
3. Treat implicit causal inference as `abstain` unless a semantic extractor is calibrated on this workspace.
4. Emit at most one compact nudge per Stop, with exact span and missing evidence field.
5. The nudge offers two valid repairs: attach/complete evidence, or downgrade wording. It never instructs the agent to discard a successful tool result.
6. Do not block while any extraction or adequacy result is model-dependent.
7. Keep extractor version/prompt/model in every record; model upgrades invalidate calibration.

Example nudge:

```text
Causal evidence gap for cc-17: “X caused Y” claims C2, but no comparator
outcome is bound to oracle r-82. Either run the paired comparator/intervention
check, or write “X is a causal hypothesis; paired experiment r-82 would settle it.”
```

**Expected detector coverage/cost:** Stage B has high structural precision because it evaluates explicit cards. Stage A recall and precision are **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the minimal-pair and historical-answer suite below. It is **likely (55-80%)** that a bounded deterministic candidate pass will be millisecond-scale; evidence is a single final-message parse with no required model/network call. A semantic pass adds one inference call per audited Stop; exact latency and token cost are **UNVERIFIED** and must be measured. Sampling only load-bearing final reports reduces cost at the price of unknown recall.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that this split can achieve a useful precision/coverage point. Evidence: atomic claim extraction has research precedent, but VeriScore documents over-extraction of subjective, trivial, and vague claims and domain-dependent performance; local detectors show the harm of role-blind matching. The wide band reflects transfer risk from scientific/biography text to agent-harness prose.

### Proposal 5 — Make association-to-cause upgrading a deterministic lint

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

When an agent cites evidence, record the evidence design class: `observation`, `temporal_trace`, `mechanism`, `paired_intervention`, `reversal`, `randomized_experiment`, or `observational_identification`. Compute a maximum licensed rung:

```text
observation/correlation/temporal trace alone       -> C0
plausible mechanism + named settling experiment   -> C1
paired isolated intervention                       -> C2
replicated randomized/identified effect            -> C3
explicit necessity/sufficiency alternatives tested -> C4 candidate
```

If claimed rung exceeds evidence rung, the system reports a *strength mismatch*, not “false.” This is cheaper and more reliable than asking a judge whether the prose “sounds causal.”

**Expected detector coverage/cost:** 100% of typed rung mismatches are caught; implicit/omitted claims remain outside that denominator. False positives arise from bad evidence classification or incomplete ledger capture, not from prose tone. **UNVERIFIED:** real-world precision; settle with adjudicated cards and forced detector-failure controls.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that a monotone rung check will be more stable than free-form model verification. Evidence: required fields and artifact hashes are mechanically decidable; current closed schemas and lexical receipts lack them. The remaining uncertainty is whether authors select the correct evidence-design class, so validator-generated classification from receipt structure is preferable to a self-declared label.

### Proposal 6 — Risk-tiered escalation, not universal causal review

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Route claims by consequence:

- **Routine/reversible:** C1 downgrade or C2 card; one deterministic nudge.
- **Multi-file/shared harness/config:** require C2 and a second mechanically independent outcome signal before action.
- **Destructive, security, medical, legal, financial, deployment, or broad policy:** require C3/C4 as applicable, fresh raw-evidence review, and user authority where already required by policy.
- **Observational production telemetry:** default to C0/C1 unless the report supplies an identification design and assumptions.

Do not translate legalistic labels such as `preponderance` or `beyond_reasonable_doubt` into evidence automatically. The existing `STAKE_TIERS` are strings (`ledger.py:123-128`), and `ClaimWarningModule` always selects the multifile label in the shown path (`modules.py:2216-2225`); a label without an operational evidence bundle is not a standard of proof.

**Expected detector coverage/cost:** fewer claims incur expensive review, but the exact distribution and savings are **UNVERIFIED**; settle by replaying a stratified month of final reports under all-claims versus risk-tier routing and measuring claims audited, false negatives, model calls, latency, and owner interventions.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that risk-tiering offers a better cost/coverage tradeoff than reviewing every causal phrase. Evidence: deterministic checks are cheaper than semantic judges, while high-stakes errors justify more evidence. The wide band reflects missing local causal-claim prevalence and loss data.

### Proposal 7 — Calibration and sunset are part of the feature

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Extend the existing `GATE_CONTRACTS`/shadow ladder rather than deploying a new observability service. The current architecture already defines OFF→SHADOW→ADVISE→ENFORCE and defaults heuristic gates to shadow (`modules.py:120-143`), with gate contracts intended to declare metrics/deadlines (`:145-260`). Add a causal-claim contract whose metrics are measured from adjudicated samples, not raw fires.

Recommended promotion rules are policy choices, not measured facts:

- Candidate extractor remains shadow until a versioned workspace test set exists.
- Advice requires owner-adjudicated precision of at least 98%, with a 95% confidence interval reported; use/mention, negation, successful-result, and attributed-claim strata each need their own result.
- Any instruction that would discard evidence, undo a successful result, or force destructive action requires deterministic evidence and zero such errors in the adversarial set; a model-dependent result can never authorize it.
- Blocking is disabled for causal claims. Reconsider only after sustained production precision, detector known-positive controls, independent outcome correlation, and a demonstrated harm that advice cannot mitigate.
- Sunset or revert to shadow if precision falls below threshold, any severe false action occurs, calibration expires after model/prompt/parser change, or cost per caught false claim exceeds an owner-set budget.

The 98% threshold is deliberately precision-first because the pinned operational report documents 9+ false fires across the use/mention defect class (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-87`). It is **UNVERIFIED** as the owner’s optimal utility threshold; settle by presenting the precision/recall/cost frontier from the benchmark rather than optimizing an undifferentiated F1 score.

**Expected detector coverage/cost:** this proposal measures rather than predicts them. The scorer must report catch fraction, precision, false-positive rate, severe-false-action count, abstention, wall time, model calls, tokens, and dollars per adjudicated/caught claim.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that shadow-first calibration prevents more harm than immediate enforcement. Evidence: the current harness explicitly supplies this ladder, local text guards have caused correctness regressions, and no reliable causal labels exist in the corpus. The residual uncertainty is whether shadow telemetry is durably observable; the pinned corpus hook detector is broken, so promotion must use a redirected benchmark sink until telemetry is independently verified.

## 7. Evaluation and promotion gates

### 7.1 Gold-set construction

Build a versioned, owner-adjudicated set with two components.

**A. Semantic minimal pairs (at least 200):** balanced direct cause, causal refutation, hypothesis, association, temporal sequence, question, conditional, attribution, quotation, code/log mention, heading, table, blockquote, implicit explanation, multi-cause, and necessity/sufficiency. Every causal base sentence gets role/polarity variants so lexical overlap is intentionally high.

**B. Historical final answers (at least 200):** stratify diagnoses, performance claims, post-fix summaries, MCP/tool failures, multi-agent synthesis, science work, and harness audits. Do not use the corpus’s owner-correction candidates as labels without re-adjudication because its own warning reports 41/41 false positives in a smoke sample.

Each item needs:

- atomic spans and claimant/role;
- direct causal assertion yes/no;
- X, comparator, Y, scope, modality;
- evidence design and maximum licensed rung;
- supported/refuted/not-enough-evidence;
- harm if acted on;
- valid rewrite at the licensed rung;
- adjudicator and disagreement record.

At least 20% should be dual-adjudicated blind. Disagreement becomes a distinct `ambiguous` label, not forced truth. This is especially important for implicit causal language.

### 7.2 Mechanically independent controls

Every eval run must prove each detector component can find a known present item before interpreting absence:

- Parser known-positive: a direct `X caused Y` assertion outside quotes.
- Role known-positive: an attributed claim whose claimant is the cited source.
- Ledger known-positive: a valid paired receipt with both artifact hashes.
- Freshness known-positive: a receipt after a known mutation.
- Negative controls: the exact local defect-report passages that mention destructive commands, markers, `timedout:false`, negated extension points, and detector phrases.
- Contradictory envelope: exit zero plus explicit application failure must be invalid/contradictory, never a passing causal arm.

If a known-positive control fails, that lane’s zero detections are no data—the same rule applied to `corpus.json:/hooks`.

### 7.3 Metrics that answer the owner’s actual question

Report a confusion matrix at each pipeline stage; an aggregate score alone hides the failure mode.

1. **Causal-assertion extraction precision:** correct direct causal spans / all nominated direct spans.
2. **Extraction recall:** correct direct spans found / all direct spans.
3. **Unsupported-claim precision:** truly under-evidenced direct claims / all warnings.
4. **False-claim catch fraction:** false direct causal claims warned / all false direct causal claims. This requires adjudicated truth, not merely missing receipts.
5. **Evidence-gap catch fraction:** claims above their licensed rung warned / all such claims.
6. **False-positive rate:** non-warning cases incorrectly warned / all non-warning cases.
7. **Severe false action rate:** warnings that instruct discard, undo, destructive action, or suppression of correct evidence. Target zero.
8. **Abstention rate:** ambiguous cases deferred / all cases.
9. **Cost per claim:** deterministic CPU milliseconds, semantic model calls/tokens/dollars, and experimental runs.
10. **Cost per caught false claim:** total audit cost / number of adjudicated false causal claims caught.
11. **Behavioral utility:** fraction repaired by evidence attachment, downgraded correctly, ignored, or made worse.
12. **Latency distribution:** median/p95/p99 at Stop, not mean alone.
13. **Slice metrics:** direct/implicit, negation, mention, attribution, code/log, domain, provider, model version, and claim rung.

Precision must be primary. Recall can be increased by shadow sampling and offline audit without injecting noise into live work. A detector that catches 90% of false claims but wrongly warns on 10% of correct/nonclaims is unacceptable here; the local false-fire history shows that downstream behavioral harm dominates raw catch count.

### 7.4 Causal evaluation of the causal guard

Do not infer that the guard helps because warnings correlate with later success. Difficult tasks generate both more warnings and more failures. Evaluate the guard itself with a randomized or blocked A/B experiment:

- Unit: versioned diagnosis tasks with fixed state and evaluator.
- Treatment: deterministic evidence-gap advice on versus shadow-only.
- Block by task and generator model/version; randomize order/assignment.
- Outcomes: false causal assertions in final answer, correct causal claims retained, task success, turns, tokens, latency, owner corrections, and false-action incidents.
- Preserve every run manifest and raw output; report attrition/timeouts.
- Do not let the same judge that generated advice be the sole outcome oracle.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that advice will reduce unsupported direct wording on the benchmark. Evidence: it offers a concrete evidence-or-downgrade repair and is scoped to detected gaps. **UNVERIFIED:** task correctness, token cost, and whether agents game the wording; this A/B experiment settles them.

## 8. Example adjudications

### Example A — Orphan directories and Windows error 206

Pinned measured ground truth states that about 1,293 orphaned plugin directories inflate command length past the Windows 32,767-character CreateProcess limit and that the same cap produces Codex `os error 206`. A report must still split claims:

1. `~1,293 orphan directories existed` — inventory observation (C0).
2. `The constructed command line exceeded 32,767 characters because those paths were included` — mechanism/measurement; C0/C1 unless contrasted.
3. `The orphan paths caused this launch failure` — C2 only if the same launch state fails with the paths and succeeds when only their inclusion is removed (or an equivalent safe command-construction intervention), ideally rechallenged.
4. `Deleting all orphan directories will fix the harness` — not licensed: the pinned input says 110/169 plugins lacked a live sibling and one orphan directory was being read by a live skill, so deletion changes more than the alleged factor and is unsafe.

The correct experiment need not delete files: construct equivalent argv manifests in a disposable state or change only inclusion in the launcher, record lengths, execute both, and reverse the inclusion. This isolates the claimed mechanism without risking live-only plugins.

### Example B — “The patch fixed the bug”

- Post-change `pytest` pass only: C0 (“tests passed on candidate”).
- Same reproducer failed on baseline and passed on candidate, with state hashes: C2 for “the patch bundle changed this reproducer’s outcome.”
- Removing one hunk reintroduces failure and restoring it removes failure, repeated: stronger C2 for that hunk in the reproduced context.
- One pass of an unrelated unit suite: does not support the fix claim.
- Search command `rg -n pytest modules.py` minted as `test/pass`: invalid evidence, as the local probe demonstrates.

### Example C — “The false guard caused a 13.6% regression”

The operational report says a false-positive class drove a here-string workaround whose failure rate was 13.6%, versus 0.0% for Write (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:35-41,100-102`). This supports a documented causal chain only to the extent that routing decisions and comparable attempts are recorded. Counts alone can be confounded by task differences. A strong claim would bind: guard fire → selected workaround, comparable write task, outcome; then compare randomized or matched executions with guard advice present/absent. Until then, “the recorded workaround had 13.6% failures and was adopted in response to false guard fires” is safer than a general quantitative effect claim.

### Example D — “Hooks did not fire”

Forbidden. `corpus.json:/hooks/DETECTOR_STATUS` says the detector failed a known-positive control. The only supported statement is “the corpus hook detector produced no usable data.” This is detector failure, not evidence about hook behavior.

### Example E — Observational telemetry

“Sessions with more retries fail more often” is C0 association. “Retries cause failure” reverses a plausible direction—hard failures may cause retries—and may be confounded by task difficulty. A causal study would randomize a bounded retry policy or use a defensible quasi-experimental threshold, define success/cost outcomes, check interference and attrition, and report assumptions. Production logs by themselves do not license the causal upgrade.

## 9. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

Every recommended component rides the current rules/hook/ledger/skill bus. This unit does **not** propose:

- a second orchestration runtime;
- a vector database or knowledge graph memory;
- a durable-execution engine;
- a trained critic;
- an observability SaaS;
- a guardrail framework;
- an always-on multi-agent judge panel.

The causal card is a small typed record, the causal receipt is a composite of ordinary artifacts, and the validator is deterministic. Semantic extraction is optional, shadow-first, and bounded to candidate nomination. This preserves the deliberately minimal architecture and does not compete with the model’s reasoning loop.

Full replacement *is* justified at one narrow boundary: generic lexical execution receipts must be fully replaced as **evidence for causal attribution**. They remain useful telemetry for “a command ran/passed,” but no amount of freshness turns them into a counterfactual. This is a semantic replacement, not a runtime replacement.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that a new causal daemon, graph service, or trained critic would fail the anti-overfit/value gate here. Evidence: all required state already fits bounded ledger events and artifact references; learned verifiers have nonzero, domain-dependent error; and the owner explicitly rejects those component classes absent exceptional justification. No exceptional need was found.

## 10. Failure modes the design deliberately leaves visible

1. **Omitted card:** the agent can make an implicit/unmarked causal claim. Shadow extraction and sampling mitigate but cannot eliminate this.
2. **Bad oracle:** a perfectly bound A/B test can measure the wrong outcome.
3. **Hidden state drift:** hashes cover only recorded state; external services, clock, network, scheduler, and caches can differ.
4. **Interference:** one experiment can affect another agent/session or shared service.
5. **Flakiness:** one pair can invert by chance; stochastic claims need repetition.
6. **Compound intervention:** a large patch can change multiple factors, supporting only a bundle-level claim.
7. **Unmeasured confounding:** observational identification assumptions may be false and sometimes cannot be empirically verified.
8. **Invalid generalization:** C2 evidence for one state can be overstated as C3 across tasks or machines.
9. **Semantic extractor drift:** model/prompt changes can alter extraction precision.
10. **Verifier self-interest:** the same agent chooses claim wording and experiments; immutable artifacts and independent reruns help, but cannot make deception or error impossible.
11. **Citation entailment error:** a real URL may not support the exact causal modality/scope.
12. **Selection effects:** only easy-to-test claims may receive cards, making audited claims look better than all claims.

The honest status for any of these is `NOT_ENOUGH_EVIDENCE`, not forced support/refutation.

## 12. Rejected alternatives and dead ends

### Reject: regex block on `because|caused|due to|root cause`

It repeats the diagnosed use-versus-mention defect, cannot preserve attribution/polarity/modality, and will punish bug reports documenting its own trigger. It has low implementation cost but an unacceptable false-positive mechanism.

### Reject: every causal claim gets an LLM judge

A judge produces another claim, not an intervention. SAFE/VeriScore show useful automated verification but also imperfect/domain-dependent behavior and meaningful inference cost. A judge can nominate missing assumptions or review raw evidence at high stakes; it cannot mint causal proof.

### Reject: majority vote across agents

Repeated agents often share model, prompt, and evidence. Convergence is not an intervention and correlated votes are not independent evidence. The local single-signal rule already warns that N agents reading the same stale input remain one lens (`single-signal-verification.md:34-37`).

### Reject: generic “two signals” automatically means cause

Two independent signals protect against detector failure and strengthen an observation. They do not necessarily form a treatment/control contrast. CPU growth plus log growth can establish that a process is active; they do not establish why it is active.

### Reject: before/after dashboard as causal proof

Concurrent changes, task mix, model versions, learning, seasonality, and regression to the mean confound raw trends. Dashboards are C0 screening evidence until a design identifies an effect.

### Reject: causal knowledge graph / persistent causal world model

This reintroduces a rejected memory/runtime class, creates stale edges that look authoritative, and does not solve identification. The bounded claim/receipt records provide provenance without pretending the graph is true.

### Reject: force strict StructuredOutput for every answer

The pinned corpus measures StructuredOutput at 22.9% errors, with schema mismatch a major fingerprint. Requiring a large closed causal schema on every response would tax noncausal work and create a new completion failure mode. Cards should be sparse, additive, and generated only for load-bearing causal assertions.

## 13. Gaps and settling experiments

1. **UNVERIFIED causal-claim prevalence:** no valid corpus label exists. Experiment: stratified owner adjudication of at least 200 historical final answers.
2. **UNVERIFIED false causal-claim prevalence:** missing evidence is not the same as falsity. Experiment: raw-evidence review and safe reproduction for the adjudicated causal subset.
3. **UNVERIFIED semantic extractor precision/recall:** scientific-text results do not transfer automatically. Experiment: minimal pairs plus historical answers, with slice metrics.
4. **UNVERIFIED live hook visibility:** corpus hook detector is broken. Experiment: send a synthetic known-ID hook event, independently confirm hook output and ledger append, then verify a no-fire control.
5. **UNVERIFIED provider payload sufficiency:** the normalized event has tool input/result but no causal fields. Experiment: capture sanitized Claude and Codex payloads for a paired test, validate that state/artifact IDs can be carried as additive events without changing strict result schemas.
6. **UNVERIFIED state-fingerprint adequacy:** external state may escape git/tree hashes. Experiment: enumerate outcome-specific state dependencies on 20 representative tasks; inject controlled drift and measure detection.
7. **UNVERIFIED cost per causal claim:** paired experiments may be cheap for unit tests and expensive for cloud/science runs. Experiment: report deterministic CPU, model calls/tokens, execution wall time, and external spend by domain.
8. **UNVERIFIED owner utility threshold:** 98% advice precision is a conservative policy choice. Experiment: present precision/recall/cost/severity frontiers and select a threshold based on observed harm, not F1 alone.
9. **UNVERIFIED causal-card compliance:** global rules may be forgotten. Experiment: compare global rule, task-template reminder, and no reminder on matched prompts.
10. **UNVERIFIED whether the existing fresh-context verifier is present in this unpacked bundle:** audits cite a Windows path, but `rg --files /local_disk0/codexwork/bundle` did not expose that skill. Experiment on Windows: prove the file detector with a known-present skill, then inspect the exact referenced path before routing any proposal through it.

## 14. Final decision

Adopt the causal evidence ladder and build the explicit causal-claim/paired-receipt lane on the current bus. Keep all causal prose extraction shadow-only until it earns precision on this workspace. Never equate candidate extraction, correlation, temporal order, a plausible mechanism, agent consensus, or a fresh generic pass with causation. License wording according to the strongest completed evidence rung, and make “not enough evidence; here is the settling experiment” a first-class successful outcome.

The design succeeds if it makes unsupported causal claims cheap to find and cheap to repair while remaining silent on mentions, questions, negations, attributed claims, and adequately evidenced conclusions. Its success must be measured by false-claim catch fraction, precision, false-positive rate, severe false actions, and cost per claim—not by number of warnings fired.
