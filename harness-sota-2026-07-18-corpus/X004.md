# X004 — Cross-domain borrow: clinical medicine → agent memory

**Unit:** X004  
**Lens:** differential diagnosis, iatrogenics, contraindication registries, and number-needed-to-treat  
**Target:** what an agent should remember across sessions, when it should forget, and how it should detect stale memory  
**Research date:** 2026-07-18  
**Scope discipline:** external clinical/medical-informatics research only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness, repository, corpus, audit, or subject tree.

## Bottom line

One clinical mechanism transfers well, but only as a bundle: **medication reconciliation at transitions, medication review/deprescribing over time, and a narrowly tiered contraindication register at the moment of action**. The bundle treats durable memory not as an ever-growing notebook but as an active regimen: every retained item needs an indication, scope, provenance, review trigger, and an explicit decision to continue, revise, suspend, or retire it.

The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve the *detectability* of stale, contradictory, or unjustified durable memories, because both domains have (a) state assembled from multiple fallible sources, (b) dangerous omissions and commissions, (c) transitions at which context changes, and (d) a need to distinguish an intentional change from accidental drift; the evidence is the WHO three-stage reconciliation process and AHRQ discrepancy taxonomy described below. The claim is **UNVERIFIED in an agent harness; the settling experiment is the shadow-mode, blinded replay and prospective A/B evaluation specified under “Evaluation and kill criteria.”**

It is only **likely (55-80%)** to improve end-task success, because clinical evidence itself is cautionary: electronic medication reconciliation reduced the proportion of medications with an unintended discrepancy in one meta-analysis, but did not consistently reduce patient-level process outcomes, while an overview of systematic reviews found little evidence of clinical-outcome benefit. Those are direct warnings against equating “more discrepancies found” with “better outcomes” ([Mekonnen et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27549581/); [Redmond et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31789354/)). The end-task-success claim is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a randomized prospective comparison measuring owner corrections and task rework, not just memory conflicts found.**

Differential diagnosis transfers only as a representation rule—store uncertain inferences as revisable hypotheses, not settled facts. It does **not** transfer as the governing memory architecture. Most memories are not mutually competing causal explanations of one syndrome, there is usually no diagnostic test with known operating characteristics, and “rare but lethal” diagnostic search would encourage speculative memory accumulation. A harness-wide “differential of memories” should therefore not be built.

## 1. What clinical medicine has actually solved

### 1.1 Medication reconciliation: compare actual prior state with intended new state

The World Health Organization describes medication reconciliation at a transition of care as three linked operations:

1. Build the **best possible medication history (BPMH)** using a systematic interview and verification against at least one other reliable information source.
2. Compare that history with prescribed medication, identify and resolve discrepancies with/by the prescriber, and document the changes.
3. Communicate the accurate current list and the reasons for changes to the patient and the next care provider.

WHO also distinguishes **intentional**, **undocumented intentional**, and **unintentional** discrepancies. That distinction matters: a changed list is not automatically an error; an unexplained change is the unsafe state ([WHO, *Medication Safety in Transitions of Care*, pp. 12–13](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/who-uhc-sds-2019-9-eng.pdf)).

NICE defines medicines reconciliation similarly—identify the accurate current list, compare it with the list now in use, recognize and resolve discrepancies, document changes, and communicate the complete result. It requires reconciliation at transitions and recognizes that it can be needed repeatedly during a stay, such as admission, ward transfer, and discharge ([NICE NG5, §1.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng5/chapter/Recommendations)).

AHRQ makes the discrepancy taxonomy operational. It identifies omission, commission, wrong dose/route/frequency, and substitution; asks whether each difference is appropriate and intentional; and calls for supporting documentation. It also recommends a “One Source of Truth” for the current list, while obtaining and verifying that list from the patient and other sources ([AHRQ MATCH, Chapter 5](https://www.ahrq.gov/patient-safety/settings/hospital/match/chapter-5.html)). “One current list” therefore does not mean “one unquestioned source.” It is the reconciled output of multiple inputs.

This is a mature process standard, not proof of large downstream benefit. A 2016 meta-analysis of ten studies (only one randomized trial) reported a 45% relative reduction in the proportion of medications with unintended discrepancies for electronic reconciliation, RR 0.55 (95% CI 0.51–0.58), but no significant reduction in the proportion of patients with discrepancies or mean discrepancies per patient; most assessed errors were minor ([Mekonnen et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27549581/)). A 2020 overview of nine high-quality systematic reviews found that conclusions supporting a measurable effect were a minority, generally based on very-low-quality evidence, and no included review found an effect on clinical outcomes ([Redmond et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31789354/)).

**The solved mechanism is therefore discrepancy discovery and accountable state transfer—not guaranteed outcome improvement.** That narrower claim is what should be borrowed.

### 1.2 Medication review and deprescribing: retention is an active treatment decision

Medication reconciliation answers “what changed?” Medication review answers “does each item still belong?” NICE describes medicines optimisation as seeking the greatest benefit through reconciliation, medication review, and shared decisions ([NICE NG5 overview](https://www.nice.org.uk/Guidance/NG5)). In condition-specific guidance, NICE explicitly calls for reassessing needs and circumstances at review and considering stopping medicines that are ineffective, while accounting for adverse effects and benefits outside the original target outcome ([NICE type 2 diabetes prescribing guide, “Reviewing medicines”](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng28/resources/prescribing-guide-individualising-medicines-use-for-people-with-type-2-diabetes-15609009709/chapter/Reviewing-medicines)).

The useful structure is not “delete old things.” It is:

- record the indication—the benefit the item is meant to produce;
- review benefit, harm, interactions, burden, and current circumstances;
- stop or taper when the indication has ended or harm exceeds benefit;
- supervise the change and preserve an accountable history.

Clinical medicine has not established that generic medication review always improves meaningful outcomes. A 2023 Cochrane review found low-certainty evidence and no consistent effect on medication-related problems, quality of life, or adherence across heterogeneous polypharmacy interventions ([Cochrane, *Interventions to improve the appropriate use of polypharmacy for older people*](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD008165_review-ways-healthcare-professionals-can-make-sure-older-people-are-given-suitable-medicines)). This is a second warning: “review everything periodically” can consume effort without producing benefit.

### 1.3 Contraindication and drug-allergy registries: structured negative knowledge checked at use time

Clinical systems do not merely remember treatments; they preserve high-value negative knowledge. NICE requires drug-allergy status to use an explicit state—“drug allergy,” “none known,” or “unable to ascertain”—rather than treating missing data as “no allergy.” At minimum, the record includes drug, reaction signs/symptoms and severity, and date. A new suspected reaction adds formulation, indication, timing, exposure, route, and drugs/classes to avoid. Allergy status is kept visibly separate from non-allergic adverse reactions, checked and confirmed before prescribing/dispensing/administration, updated when it changes, and carried across transitions ([NICE CG183, §§1.2.1–1.2.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/CG183/chapter/recommendations)).

This mechanism supplies four important ideas for memory:

1. **Negative knowledge is first-class.** “Do not use X in context Y because Z occurred” should not be buried in prose.
2. **Unknown is not negative.** “No known objection” and “not checked” are different states.
3. **The check occurs at the decision point.** A relevant contraindication is retrieved before the risky action, not dumped into every session prompt.
4. **Specificity controls interruption.** Exact target, scope, severity, evidence, and prior outcomes determine whether to block, warn, or merely annotate.

The last point is essential because medicine has also demonstrated the iatrogenic harm of its own safety machinery. A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of 16 studies estimated that clinicians overrode 90% of drug–drug interaction alerts (95% CI 85–95%; heterogeneity I²=100%) ([Felisberto et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38899788/)). AHRQ explains the mechanism: overly sensitive, context-free alerts generate excessive nonmeaningful warnings, which can cause important alerts to be ignored ([AHRQ, “Creating Meaningful Decision Support to Reduce Drug-Drug Interactions”](https://digital.ahrq.gov/program-overview/research-stories/creating-meaningful-decision-support-reduce-drug-drug)). A systematic review of drug-allergy decision support likewise found low specificity and override rates up to 90%, and called for better specificity ([Légat et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30194058/)).

Thus a “registry of everything ever disliked” plus an interruptive hook would copy a known clinical failure mode, not a clinical solution.

### 1.4 NNT/NNH: demand absolute, contextual value—not a universal score

Number needed to treat (NNT) is the expected number of people who must receive an intervention rather than a comparator for one additional person to experience or avoid a specified event over a specified time. Number needed to harm (NNH) expresses the corresponding additional harmful outcome. NICE defines them as reciprocals of absolute risk reduction/increase ([NICE glossary](https://www.nice.org.uk/Glossary?letter=N)).

The Cochrane Handbook adds the crucial constraints: NNT depends on the comparator/baseline risk, population or risk group, defined outcome, and time frame. Absolute risk differences are often unstable across baseline risks, so a single pooled NNT can mislead; benefits should be expressed separately for clinically identifiable risk groups and time periods ([Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 15, §§15.4.1–15.4.4](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15)).

The transferable principle is **marginal absolute utility in a defined context**. It is not the literal clinical statistic. A harness lacks randomized patient cohorts and stable outcome incidence, so calling a memory score “NNT” would imply evidential strength it does not have.

### 1.5 Differential diagnosis: useful uncertainty discipline, poor universal ontology

The National Academies describes diagnosis as an iterative cycle of information gathering, integration, interpretation, and revision of a working diagnosis. A working diagnosis may be a differential—a list of hypotheses—whose probabilities are updated as new information arrives; revisions and their uncertainty should be communicated ([National Academies, *Improving Diagnosis in Health Care*, Chapter 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK338593/)).

The transferable fragment is precise: **an inference remains a hypothesis with disconfirming conditions until verified.** For example, “the owner may prefer concise reports” must not become a permanent preference merely because one terse response was accepted once.

The rest does not transfer. Diseases are causal hypotheses about observations; durable memories include explicit instructions, environment facts, episodic outcomes, procedures, and permissions. Those items do not generally compete to explain a common syndrome. Medical differentials also rely on prevalence, pathophysiology, and tests whose sensitivity/specificity can be studied; agent memories usually lack those. Therefore:

- do not generate a broad differential for every memory;
- do not preserve low-probability speculative memories merely because their hypothetical harm is high;
- do represent genuinely inferred memories as provisional and store the evidence that would confirm or refute them.

## 2. Why the main analogy transfers—and where it breaks

### Structural match

| Clinical medicine | Agent memory |
|---|---|
| Patient’s actual medication use is distributed across interview, containers, pharmacy records, and prior charts | Current truth is distributed across explicit owner instructions, current workspace/configuration, tool observations, and prior-session records |
| A transition can introduce omissions, commissions, substitutions, or undocumented intentional changes | A new session/task/branch can omit a still-valid constraint, carry an irrelevant fact forward, mutate a preference, or silently replace a prior rule |
| A drug has an indication, dose/scope, adverse effects, interactions, and review timing | A memory has a purpose, applicability scope, cost of retrieval, conflicts, evidence, and a review trigger |
| Allergy/contraindication knowledge matters only when a relevant action is considered | A negative memory matters most before the matching tool, path, external action, or decision |
| Continuing a medicine is an active decision, not the absence of a decision | Retaining a durable memory should be an active, evidenced decision, not append-only default |

This match is strong because both systems manage **action-relevant, longitudinal, fallible state under transitions**. It does not depend on pretending an agent is a patient.

### Disanalogies that constrain the design

1. **No equivalent physiological ground truth.** A medication list can sometimes be checked against containers, dispensing records, laboratory results, or a patient interview. Many preferences are normative and only the owner can settle them. Therefore source authority must be typed; majority vote across sources is invalid.
2. **Agent state can often be cheaply re-observed.** Repository branch, installed tool, or file existence should usually be checked live rather than remembered. Clinical measurements can be invasive or expensive. The harness should preferentially forget/revalidate volatile environmental facts.
3. **Memory retrieval itself changes behavior.** Injected memory consumes context and anchors the model. A medication sitting on a list does not directly alter a clinician’s cognition to the same degree. Memory has an “iatrogenic” prompt cost even when factually true.
4. **The owner can issue superseding instructions instantly.** Some clinical changes require tapering or monitoring; most memory retirement does not. The safe borrowed part is accountable status change, not literal gradual withdrawal.
5. **Clinical taxonomies are domain-specific.** Dose, route, and formulation do not map one-to-one to memory. The correct abstractions are content, scope, strength, timing, and action class.
6. **Clinical outcome evidence is not harness evidence.** Medical reconciliation evidence motivates a mechanism but does not estimate agent benefit. All effect-size claims for the proposed harness remain experimental.

## 3. Concrete harness translation

### Recommendation (UNVERIFIED — settled by the evaluation in §5): a reconciled active-memory regimen

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement a small structured record plus transition-time reconciliation functions on the existing session-start/session-end or equivalent hook bus. Do not add a daemon, second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The likely implementation can remain under roughly 200 lines if it stores line-oriented JSON or a small JSON document and delegates semantic extraction to the already-running model; this size estimate is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the deliberately narrow schema and four deterministic operations below, but it is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a time-boxed spike that counts executable non-test lines and rejects the proposal if it requires a new service or exceeds the BUILD-SMALL boundary.**

#### 3.1 Record shape: every durable memory has an indication

Minimum fields:

```yaml
id: stable identifier
claim: concise action-relevant content
class: explicit_instruction | preference | environment_fact | procedure | episode | hypothesis | contraindication
status: active | provisional | suspended | superseded | retired | unable_to_ascertain
scope:
  owner: optional
  repository_or_project: optional
  task_or_action_class: optional
  version_or_branch: optional
indication: the future decision this memory is expected to improve
evidence:
  - source_type: owner_explicit | observed_state | task_outcome | inference
    locator: session/message/path/command reference
    observed_at: timestamp
confidence: required for inferred claims; absent for explicit owner directives
review:
  triggers: [owner_correction, contradiction, scope_change, version_change, failed_live_check, repeated_override]
  review_after: optional timestamp, never an automatic truth-expiry rule
interaction_keys: tools/paths/actions/topics against which this item should be checked
severity: info | caution | hard_stop
supersedes: prior memory IDs
decision_log: why added, changed, suspended, or retired
```

Design rules:

- An item without an `indication` is not eligible for durable active memory; it may remain in session history or an archive.
- Explicit owner instructions outrank inferred preferences within the same scope. A newer instruction is not automatically universal; its scope must overlap before it supersedes.
- `unable_to_ascertain` is distinct from “false” or “none,” borrowing NICE’s allergy-state discipline.
- Volatile environment facts default to re-observation at use time. Their stored form is a re-check hint, not truth.
- Hypotheses require confidence plus confirming/disconfirming evidence. They cannot cause a hard stop.
- Retired/superseded items remain as tombstones with their decision log so an old fact cannot be silently relearned from an older session.

#### 3.2 Reconcile only at meaningful transitions

Trigger reconciliation at:

- start of a new task/session when durable memory will be loaded;
- repository/project/branch change;
- owner correction or explicit “remember/forget” instruction;
- handoff between agents or execution environments;
- end of a task only when there is a candidate durable lesson.

At a transition:

1. Build a **best possible memory history** for only the upcoming task’s scopes. Inputs are the current owner instruction, relevant active records, live-observable state, and any explicit candidate from the just-finished task.
2. Compare the old active set, the proposed retrieved set, and newly observed state.
3. Classify differences:
   - **omission:** relevant active constraint was not selected;
   - **commission:** selected memory has no current indication or applicable scope;
   - **mutation:** content/strength/scope changed without a documented decision;
   - **duplication:** two records express the same constraint and risk double-weighting;
   - **interaction:** two individually plausible records conflict in this action context;
   - **status ambiguity:** missing evidence is being treated as “none.”
4. Mark the discrepancy intentional with a reason, or route it for resolution. Never silently rewrite the canonical item.
5. Inject only the reconciled, task-relevant active set; append a compact change note to the decision log.

The two-source clinical rule cannot be copied blindly. For an explicit owner instruction, the owner’s current statement is authoritative and does not require corroboration. For an inferred preference or a volatile fact, require either two mechanically independent signals or one live authoritative observation before promotion. This avoids turning “consensus” into a veto over owner intent.

#### 3.3 Deprescribe memory instead of using TTL deletion

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add an event-driven review function to the same record handler; do not add a periodic daemon.

Review an item when its trigger fires, not merely because it is old. At review, ask:

1. Is the original indication still present?
2. Does the scope still match?
3. Is the evidence still authoritative and observable?
4. Has retrieval demonstrably helped, been ignored, or caused correction/rework?
5. Does it conflict with a newer explicit instruction or live state?
6. What happens if it is withheld for a shadow run?

Then choose:

- **continue** with no semantic change;
- **narrow** scope;
- **reverify** live before use;
- **suspend** while uncertain;
- **supersede** with an explicit link and reason;
- **retire** from active retrieval while retaining a tombstone.

A time-to-live may trigger review, but must not itself assert falsehood. A six-month-old stable owner preference may still be valid; a five-minute-old branch name may already be stale. Age is a prior on volatility, not a verdict.

This mechanism would catch currently generic classes of failure that append-only memory misses:

- a tool or environment fact carried forward after the environment changed;
- a one-off task instruction generalized into a permanent owner preference;
- an old correction resurfacing after a newer instruction superseded it;
- a useful memory kept so broadly that it fires in unrelated repositories;
- a true but low-value memory repeatedly injected despite no observed benefit;
- “nothing is configured” inferred from a failed detector rather than recorded as unable to ascertain.

Whether those classes occur at material rates in this owner’s harness is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is corpus replay with a manually labeled stratified sample of owner corrections and stale-memory incidents, as specified in §5.**

### Recommendation (UNVERIFIED — settled by alert precision/override experiment): tiered contraindication memory

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Use deterministic matching on structured `interaction_keys` at the existing pre-tool or pre-action hook; do not perform an all-memory semantic search before every action.

Store a contraindication only when it includes:

- exact prohibited or cautionary action/tool/path/class;
- applicable scope and exceptions;
- observed failure/harm or explicit owner prohibition;
- severity;
- evidence locator and date;
- who/what can clear it;
- prior override outcomes.

Response tiers:

- **Hard stop:** current explicit owner prohibition, destructive boundary, or mechanically demonstrated severe failure with exact scope match. Clearing requires an explicit superseding owner instruction or verified remediation.
- **Interruptive caution:** strong evidence and likely material harm, but context may justify override. Require a short reason; repeated valid overrides trigger review rather than repeated interruption.
- **Non-interruptive annotation:** inferred preference, lower-severity issue, class-level match, or uncertain scope. Present only alongside the relevant plan/tool decision.
- **Silent telemetry:** weak or exploratory candidates; measure without influencing behavior until validated.

This would catch attempts to repeat a specifically corrected action, use a known-incompatible method in the same environment, or violate a scoped owner prohibition immediately before action. It would also avoid making every historical failure a global ban.

The alert-fatigue disanalogy is not optional: an agent may comply with alerts more mechanically than a clinician, so a false hard stop can deadlock work rather than merely be overridden. Therefore hypotheses cannot block; class-level matches cannot block; and the kill criterion must include blocked-correct-action rate.

### Recommendation (UNVERIFIED — settled by outcome-linked retrieval experiment): NNT-inspired memory economics

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add counters to each record and use them in selection; no new runtime component.

Do not calculate or display a pseudo-clinical “NNT.” Instead record context-specific absolute counts:

- `eligible_uses`: tasks where the memory’s scope and indication matched;
- `retrieved_uses`: eligible tasks where it was injected;
- `prevented_or_corrected_failures`: only where a counterfactual can be supported by a paired replay, explicit owner confirmation, or blinded adjudication;
- `memory_attributed_harms`: owner corrections, rework, blocked valid actions, or context displacement attributable to retrieval;
- `tokens_and_latency`: direct retrieval burden;
- `uncertain_outcomes`: kept separate, never forced into benefit or harm.

Report, per memory class and scope, absolute differences between retrieval and no-retrieval arms with confidence intervals. Always name the outcome and evaluation horizon. For example: “over 200 repository-edit tasks in project class P, retrieval was associated with 6 fewer repeated owner corrections (95% interval …) and 3 additional false constraints,” not “this memory has NNT 33.”

Prioritize a memory when its observed absolute benefit in the relevant baseline-risk group exceeds its observed harm and context cost. If evidence is insufficient, keep it provisional or run shadow evaluation rather than promoting it by intuition.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that per-record outcome counters will be statistically informative at this owner’s task volume, because individual memories may have sparse, confounded exposure while clinical NNT assumes defined comparator data; the evidence for this uncertainty is Cochrane’s warning that absolute effects vary with baseline risk. The premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by a pre-registered power analysis after a four-week shadow period, and drop per-record scoring in favor of class-level estimates if effective sample sizes are inadequate.**

### Limited recommendation (UNVERIFIED — settle by precision audit): hypothesis status for inferred memories

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add `provisional`/`hypothesis` status and confirming/disconfirming evidence fields. Require two independent observations before promoting an inferred cross-session preference, unless the owner explicitly confirms it. On contradiction, lower confidence or suspend; do not average an explicit instruction with behavioral inference.

This catches accidental “preference crystallization”: the agent interprets one accepted behavior, one task-specific request, or one absence of correction as a permanent rule. It does not warrant a differential-diagnosis engine.

## 4. Staleness detection: a trigger matrix

Staleness is not synonymous with age. It is a mismatch among claim, scope, evidence, and present action.

| Signal | Independent comparison | Default response | Example caught |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit owner correction | current instruction vs active record | supersede or narrow immediately; log reason | old style preference conflicts with current request |
| Live authoritative contradiction | observed tool/file/config state vs stored environment fact | mark “reverify,” withhold old fact, then update | remembered branch/tool availability changed |
| Scope transition | current repo/project/version vs memory scope | do not retrieve; review only if a broader scope is proposed | project-local workaround leaking globally |
| Evidence-source invalidation | source no longer exists or detector fails its positive control | set `unable_to_ascertain`; never infer absence | broken detector produces “feature absent” memory |
| Repeated justified override | override log vs expected constraint utility | reduce interruption tier and review specificity | stale warning repeatedly dismissed for valid exceptions |
| Repeated retrieval without attributable benefit | eligible/retrieved uses vs outcomes and token cost | shadow-withhold or retire from active set | true but useless boilerplate memory |
| Negative outcome after retrieval | correction/rework vs retrieved item and action | suspend and adjudicate | memory anchors the agent to obsolete solution |
| Superseding evidence | newer same-authority source with overlapping scope | create explicit supersession edge | changed owner constraint or remediated defect |
| Review date reached | clock vs `review_after` | request review/reverification, not deletion | potentially stale preference awaiting confirmation |

Two mechanically independent signals are required before recording an **absence** or declaring a memory **stale because its factual premise is false**, unless the current owner explicitly states the change. One signal may be the live observation; the other must be a different source type or field, not a rerun of the same failing detector. Before an absence detector is trusted, it must find a known-present control. If the control fails, store `unable_to_ascertain` and the failed detector, not “absent.”

This is a direct safety improvement over naive TTLs: it makes the reason for staleness inspectable and protects stable old facts while quickly invalidating new volatile ones.

## 5. Evaluation and kill criteria

### Phase A — retrospective, read-only replay

1. Sample sessions stratified by owner correction, environment transition, tool failure, and ordinary successful task. Keep adjudicators blind to whether a candidate came from the proposed reconciler.
2. Have two human adjudicators label candidate durable memories and discrepancy classes: omission, commission, mutation, duplication, interaction, or no issue. Resolve disagreements explicitly.
3. Compare current/naive retrieval against the proposed schema and rules in shadow mode.
4. Primary metrics:
   - precision and recall for memories adjudicated relevant at the next session;
   - stale/contradictory memory precision and recall;
   - false hard-stop and false caution rates;
   - unsupported inference promotion rate;
   - prompt tokens and latency.
5. Run positive controls: seeded known-present relevant memory, seeded contradiction, seeded unable-to-ascertain detector failure, and seeded supersession. The evaluator is invalid if it cannot find these.

### Phase B — prospective shadow mode

For four weeks or a pre-registered minimum task count, compute recommendations without changing prompts or blocking actions. Ask:

- Which proposed memories would have been injected?
- Which warnings would have fired?
- How often did the owner later correct the exact issue?
- How often would the proposal have blocked a successful action?
- Which records never had an eligible use?

No claim that the component is “done,” “absent,” or “broken” may rest on a single metric. Use at least an outcome signal (correction/rework/task success) and a mechanically different process signal (retrieval match, action trace, or adjudicated discrepancy).

### Phase C — randomized prospective trial

Randomize eligible tasks, or matched task blocks if contamination is a concern, between current memory and reconciled active memory. Pre-register:

- primary outcome: repeated owner corrections attributable to stale/omitted durable information per eligible task;
- co-primary harm: incorrect constraints or blocks attributable to memory per eligible task;
- secondary outcomes: task success, rework, tokens, latency, explicit override rate, and adjudicated omission/commission discrepancies;
- analysis strata: memory class, project scope, task type, and baseline correction risk;
- horizon: task completion plus the next relevant task.

The reconciler is worth enabling only if it improves the correction/rework outcome without a material rise in memory-attributed harm or context cost. Finding more discrepancies is insufficient.

### Explicit kill criteria

Reject or redesign the mechanism if any of these occur:

- it requires a second runtime, vector DB/KG, daemon, or other BUILD-LARGE component;
- interruptive alerts have low precision or valid-action blocking exceeds the pre-registered threshold;
- the system cannot distinguish `unable_to_ascertain` from “none” under detector failure;
- inferred hypotheses are promoted despite failing the two-independent-observation rule;
- reconciliation improves discrepancy counts but not owner correction/rework outcomes;
- prompt/context cost erases measured benefit;
- per-record benefit estimates remain too sparse or confounded—then retain simple class-level rules and discard scoring.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and minimal architecture

All proposals above ride the existing hook/skill/MCP bus and use a compact structured file. None reintroduces a second orchestration runtime, vector-DB/knowledge-graph memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. Retrieval is scoped deterministic filtering plus the model already performing the task; reconciliation occurs only at meaningful transitions, not continuously.

The design deliberately borrows a **workflow invariant** rather than clinical surface complexity:

> Gather from appropriate sources → compare old and intended state → classify discrepancies → document intentional changes → retrieve only action-relevant active items → review benefit/harm when evidence or context changes.

The smallest useful version is schema validation, scope matching, discrepancy classification, tombstoned supersession, and tiered exact-key checks. Outcome scoring is optional and should be deleted if Phase B shows sparse data. Automated semantic consolidation, embeddings, graph propagation, and autonomous global preference inference are outside the proposal.

## 7. What this catches that ordinary cross-session memory misses

The following are design-level predictions, not claims about the present harness:

1. **Silent commission:** a true memory is retrieved in a task where it has no indication or scope.
2. **Silent omission:** a relevant explicit constraint exists but is not in the task’s reconciled active set.
3. **Undocumented intentional change:** the agent behaves differently because context changed but does not record why, allowing the old version to return later.
4. **Accidental mutation:** a scoped instruction becomes global, or a caution becomes a prohibition.
5. **Preference crystallization:** an inference from one interaction is stored as an owner fact.
6. **False negative memory:** a broken detector is converted into “capability absent” rather than “unable to ascertain.”
7. **Post-remediation contraindication:** a previously valid warning remains interruptive after its cause is fixed.
8. **Iatrogenic retrieval:** a true low-value memory consumes context, anchors planning, or blocks a valid action.
9. **Historical resurrection:** a superseded item is relearned from an older session because no tombstone links old and new state.
10. **Alert fatigue:** broad low-specificity warnings train the agent/workflow to ignore or mechanically route around all warnings.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the mechanism can detect seeded examples of these ten classes, because each maps directly to a deterministic schema or comparison rule; this is **UNVERIFIED in implementation, and the settling experiment is Phase A’s seeded positive-control suite.** It is only **likely (55-80%)** that naturally occurring cases will be frequent enough to justify all four pieces, because no local corpus was examined by design; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is Phase A’s stratified prevalence estimate with confidence intervals.**

## 8. Rejected borrows and dead ends

### Rejected: “memory differential diagnosis” as a full engine

Reason: memories are heterogeneous records, not competing causal hypotheses. Generating alternatives for every stored fact would increase speculation and context load. Retain only provisional status and explicit disconfirmation conditions for inferred claims.

### Rejected: literal NNT as a per-memory score

Reason: NNT requires a defined outcome, comparator, baseline risk, population, and time horizon. Ordinary agent traces are observational, selected, and confounded. Use named absolute outcome counts in randomized or paired evaluation; do not borrow clinical authority through terminology.

### Rejected: TTL equals forgetting

Reason: age alone neither proves irrelevance nor falsity. TTL should trigger review. Scope mismatch, source invalidation, contradiction, and observed harm are stronger signals.

### Rejected: append-only “contraindications” with global blocking

Reason: medicine’s high alert-override rates show what happens when specificity and context are poor. In an agent harness, false blocks may be even more disruptive. Require exact scope, evidence, severity, and tiering.

### Rejected: periodic review of the entire memory store

Reason: generic medication-review evidence does not show consistent downstream benefit, and full-store review adds cost unrelated to current decisions. Use transition- and evidence-triggered review, with only a low-frequency audit sample for calibration.

### Rejected: automatic deletion after supersession

Reason: without a tombstone and reason, older session evidence can resurrect the retired claim. Remove from active retrieval, retain minimal lineage.
