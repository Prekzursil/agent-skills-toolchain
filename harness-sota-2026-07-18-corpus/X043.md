# X043 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → synthesis

## Scope and verdict

This unit used an ideation lens only. It did not inspect the local harness, corpus, audit findings, or subject repositories, because the assignment explicitly reserves that grounding for other units. The evidence base here is primary guidance from the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), and International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). Page references below use the page labels in the cited PDFs.

**Verdict:** the analogy transfers, but only in a bounded form. Nuclear operations has not solved “how to compress 12 MB into the correct decision.” It has solved a structurally adjacent problem: how to avoid trusting one fallible cognitive path at a consequential step. The useful borrow is **risk-graded independent verification of a decision case**, supported by self-checking, conservative stop criteria, and defence in depth. It is not “ask two agents and take the majority.”

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this is a productive transfer because DOE explicitly applies its human-performance tools not only to physical operators but also to scientists, engineers, procedure writers, and other knowledge workers whose document errors can later cause events; it separately distinguishes self-checking, peer-checking, concurrent verification, independent verification, and peer review ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, foreword pp. i–ii and contents pp. iii–iv](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The remaining uncertainty is whether the additional verification cost improves this owner's actual synthesis decisions; that is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E1, “Blind-IV synthesis bake-off,” defined below**.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that a naive “two agents see the same summary and agree” does not implement the nuclear mechanism. DOE defines independence as freedom of thought promoted by separation in time and distance, with the verifier checking the as-found condition rather than relying on the performer's observation or oral confirmation ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 46–47](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). Agreement after shared framing lacks that separation.

## What nuclear operations has actually solved

### 1. STAR self-checking: a local action loop, not a second opinion

In its own field, STAR means **Stop, Think, Act, Review**. DOE describes the operator pausing before a critical action, understanding the intended action and expected result, acting under the governing guidance, and then verifying that the observed result matches the expected result. DOE expressly includes calculations, data entry, experiments, and revision of drawings or procedures among appropriate uses ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The important mechanics are:

1. **Stop:** interrupt automatic continuation and remove distractions.
2. **Think:** state the intended action, governing document, expected output, and contingency before acting.
3. **Act:** perform the particular planned action against the particular identified component or artifact.
4. **Review:** compare actual output with the prediction and invoke the contingency if they differ.

This is more than a final proofreading pass. Prediction occurs before action, so the review has a precommitted expectation rather than a post-hoc rationalization. DOE also warns against taking several actions rapidly, acting under uncertainty, and failing to verify the result ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

**Translation:** before a synthesizer emits or promotes a recommendation, it should record: the decision it is about to make, the evidence classes it expects to support it, the strongest foreseeable defeater, and what result would cause it to abstain. After drafting, it compares the actual citations, coverage, and contradictions against that prediction. STAR is useful at the final decision boundary; applying it mechanically to every token or every low-consequence summary would be cargo cult.

### 2. A verification taxonomy: “two people” is not one mechanism

DOE's handbook separates four practices ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 41–49](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)):

| Nuclear mechanism | Own-field purpose | Degree of independence | Synthesis analogue |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Self-checking | Performer catches an error around their own act | None | Synthesizer validates its own predicted evidence and output |
| Peer-checking | Two people work together to prevent an incorrect act | Low | Reviewer watches or critiques the draft as it forms |
| Concurrent verification | Two people separately confirm a key condition during the act | Medium | Two agents separately confirm a critical claim before promotion |
| Independent verification (IV) | A person separated from production confirms the as-found state/document against governing requirements | High | Blind verifier reconstructs the decision-support result from the evidence ledger and requirements, before seeing the syntheses |
| Peer review | Fresh eyes inspect a risk-important work product | Variable | Post-draft critique for clarity, defects, or omissions |

DOE calls peer-checking the least rigorous of these checking and verification tools, says that it augments rather than replaces self-checking, and warns that either participant may become less attentive because they assume the other will catch the problem ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 42–43](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). DOE likewise warns against superficial verification, junior verifiers reluctant to challenge, and shared verbal or observed cues replacing personal confirmation ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 44–48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

This taxonomy matters because most agent “review” patterns collapse all five rows into “another completion.” The nuclear lesson is that the error-catching claim must be no stronger than the actual independence of the check.

### 3. Independent verification: reconstruct the state, do not bless the story

In its own field, DOE's independent verifier is separated from the performer in time and distance, independently checks the component, calculation, or document, compares the as-found condition against the governing document, reports disagreement, and signs only on agreement. The verifier does not alter the state while checking it. DOE states that IV tends to catch more errors than peer or concurrent checks because the verifier did not produce the work and is not cued by the performer ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 46–47](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The transferable invariant is not physical distance. It is **causal separation of the evidence path**:

- do not show the verifier the first synthesizer's prose or ranking before the verifier commits its own result;
- give both roles the same governing decision requirements but independently generated work packets or independent access paths into the evidence ledger;
- require claim IDs and source coordinates, not “I agree”;
- reconcile only after both outputs are frozen;
- record disagreement rather than coercing consensus.

The classic “two-person rule” is therefore too loose a label for this design. Two signatures can be ceremonial; DOE's more precise mechanism is independent verification. The handbook's cautions explicitly identify correlated attention, deference, proximity, and diffusion of responsibility as failure modes ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 45, 47–48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

### 4. Conservative decision-making: uncertainty changes the action

Nuclear conservative decision-making does not mean “always choose the most pessimistic prose.” It means that uncertainty, unexpected conditions, conflicting evidence, or departure from governing bounds produces a pause, a safer interim state, escalation, and help from a more knowledgeable person. DOE's “Pause When Unsure” practice lists inconsistent procedures, unexpected results, missing knowledge, and another person's concern as triggers; its prescribed response is to pause, put the job in a safe condition, notify supervision, and obtain help. It warns against dismissing contrary views and proceeding without clear abort criteria ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 16–17](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The NRC's human-factors coding guidance separately treats failure to stop or establish controls under unfavorable or uncertain conditions as non-conservative decision-making, and separately records inadequate independent verification and inadequate STAR self-checks ([NRC, Definition List for Human Factors, items 190, 196, and 197](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)).

IAEA security-culture guidance makes the organizational part explicit: formal and inclusive decision processes, explanation of decisions, solicitation of dissent and diverse perspectives, no bypassing of the process, qualified authority, and reinforcement of conservative decisions ([IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 28-T, p. 56 of the printed publication / PDF p. 65](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/PUB1761_web.pdf)).

**Translation:** a synthesis system must have an abstention state. “Insufficient coverage,” “unresolved high-severity contradiction,” “only derivative evidence,” and “verifier disagreement on an irreversible decision” should not silently become a lower-confidence recommendation. They should change workflow state to `HOLD`, preserve the last safe output, and request targeted evidence or owner judgment.

### 5. Defence in depth: layers with different failure modes

The NRC defines defence in depth as multiple independent and redundant layers that compensate for human and mechanical failure, so that no single layer is exclusively relied on; it specifically includes diverse as well as redundant safety functions ([NRC glossary, “Defense in depth”](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/basic-ref/glossary/defense-in-depth)). In a concrete fire-protection application, the layers have different purposes: prevent ignition; rapidly detect, control, and extinguish fires that occur; and preserve safe shutdown if suppression fails ([NRC, “Defense-In-Depth” fire protection](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/fire-protection/defense-in-depth)).

The transferable feature is **functional diversity**, not merely duplication. Three agents using the same model, prompt, summary, and ranking heuristic are redundant but not meaningfully diverse. A deterministic coverage check, an independent semantic reconstruction, and a conservative release gate fail differently.

## Proposed harness design — UNVERIFIED: risk-graded Independent Decision Case pipeline

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Build this as a small evidence-ledger reducer plus a synthesis skill and a release hook. Do not add a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The premise that the existing bus can expose a pre-synthesis and pre-release hook is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E0, “bus-fit spike”: implement a no-op ledger reducer and release veto in a disposable fixture and prove invocation through both a hook event record and an output-state transition**. Because this premise is unverified, the proposal heading carries the required `UNVERIFIED` tag.

### Architecture

```text
upstream findings
      |
      v
[1 deterministic evidence ledger + coverage manifest]
      |                         |
      |                         +--> deterministic integrity gates
      v
[2 risk/topic packets] --------+-------------------+
      |                                             |
      v                                             v
[3A synthesizer: decision case]       [3B blind verifier: own case]
      |                                             |
      +-------------------+-------------------------+
                          v
              [4 disagreement/reconciliation]
                          |
                          v
              [5 conservative release gate]
                 PASS | HOLD | OWNER-DECIDES
```

The architecture is intentionally asymmetric. Deterministic machinery proves structural properties such as identity, coverage, and source reachability. Agents judge meaning. The release gate does not ask an LLM to certify that another LLM “considered everything.”

### [1] Deterministic evidence ledger and coverage manifest

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Normalize upstream findings into append-only JSONL records without semantically rewriting them. A minimal record is:

```json
{
  "finding_id": "stable-id",
  "source_unit": "unit-or-document",
  "source_locator": "path-or-url#page/line",
  "topic": "controlled-label",
  "claim_text": "verbatim-or-lossless-extract",
  "evidence_type": "measured|observed|static-claim|external",
  "severity": "critical|high|medium|low|unknown",
  "confidence": "declared-band-or-unknown",
  "decision_ids": ["D-..."],
  "supports": ["finding-id"],
  "contradicts": ["finding-id"],
  "duplicate_of": null
}
```

The reducer emits counts and stable IDs by topic, severity, evidence type, source unit, decision, unresolved contradiction, and unassigned finding. It fails closed on duplicate IDs, missing locators, truncated records, and count mismatch between input and output. It does **not** deduplicate semantically by model judgment; it may record candidate duplicates while retaining every original.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this layer would catch silent record loss, accidental double counting by ID, unassigned high-severity items, and citation targets that cannot be resolved, because those are deterministic invariants rather than semantic judgments. This is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E2, “mutation test”: delete, duplicate, truncate, misroute, and break the locator of seeded records and require detection of every seeded mutation**.

I am **unlikely (20-45%)** that the ledger alone would detect a materially misleading but syntactically valid finding, because structure does not establish truth. Evidence: the NRC's defence-in-depth definition requires multiple layers precisely because a single layer is not exclusively reliable ([NRC glossary](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/basic-ref/glossary/defense-in-depth)).

### [2] Risk-graded packets instead of universal reading

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Construct bounded packets deterministically:

- **Decision packet:** every finding explicitly linked to the decision.
- **Must-read packet:** all critical/high findings, all contradictions, all owner corrections, all direct measurements, and all records marked `unknown` for evidence or confidence.
- **Coverage packet:** manifest counts plus one stratified sample from every remaining `(topic, evidence_type, source_unit)` cell; sampling seed is recorded and reproducible.
- **Challenge packet:** strongest counterevidence for each candidate recommendation, plus findings excluded by scope with exclusion reasons.

Packets contain source locators and lossless claim extracts. They are indexes into the evidence, not recursively summarized summaries. A role can open full source context when a packet record is ambiguous.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that this packet design can keep any one agent below the full 12 MB while preserving materially better coverage than naive top-k summarization, because it combines exhaustive inclusion of risk-significant classes with reproducible stratification across the remainder. This is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E1 using planted minority and cross-topic findings**. The wide confidence band reflects an important unknown: the upstream labels may themselves be wrong or incomplete.

This is deliberately not a claim that “no agent ever reads all evidence.” For a decision whose must-read set itself exceeds the context budget, the conservative response is decomposition or `HOLD`, not arbitrary truncation.

### [3A] Synthesizer decision case with STAR

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Define a structured but permissive decision-case contract in the synthesis skill prompt; validate required top-level fields without an over-constrained fan-out schema.

Before drafting, the synthesizer writes a STAR precommitment:

- `STOP`: decision ID, scope, authority, reversibility, and release consequence;
- `THINK`: expected supporting evidence classes, strongest plausible defeater, required coverage, and abort criteria;
- `ACT`: recommendation, alternatives, evidence IDs, counterevidence IDs, and explicit inference steps;
- `REVIEW`: compare actual packet/manifest coverage and contradictions with the precommitment; explain deviations.

The resulting decision case contains exactly one accountable recommendation or `ABSTAIN`, alternatives considered, evidence for and against, uncertainty, reversibility, verification status, and the smallest next experiment that could change the decision.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that precommitting expected results and abort criteria will expose some post-hoc rationalization and evidence cherry-picking, because STAR separates prediction from outcome review and DOE uses it for calculations and document work as well as physical actions ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). This is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E3, “STAR ablation”: compare hidden-defeater recovery with and without the precommitment while holding model and packets constant**.

### [3B] Blind independent verifier

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Assign a verifier a fresh context containing the governing decision question, acceptance criteria, ledger manifest, independently generated packet order, and source access. Withhold 3A's recommendation, rationale, ranking, and prose until 3B freezes:

- its own recommended disposition or `ABSTAIN`;
- decisive evidence IDs;
- strongest counterevidence;
- coverage gaps;
- predicted failure modes;
- confidence band.

Only then reveal 3A and compute agreements and disagreements field by field. Where practical, use a different model family or at least a different prompt, evidence order, and role specification. Different model families may reduce correlated errors, but that premise is **UNVERIFIED — settle within Experiment E1 by comparing same-model blind IV, cross-model blind IV, and non-blind review**.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that blinding reduces anchoring to the first synthesis relative to a reviewer who sees it, because DOE's IV mechanism explicitly isolates the verifier from the performer's cues and work production ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 46–47](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). Whether that reduction is large enough to justify cost in this harness is **UNVERIFIED — Experiment E1 settles it**.

I am only **roughly even (45-55%)** that two blind agents using the same foundation model provide enough semantic diversity for the highest-consequence decisions. Shared pretraining, shared tool outputs, shared upstream labels, and shared missing evidence are common-cause channels. This is **UNVERIFIED — Experiment E1 must stratify disagreement and joint-miss rates by model pairing**.

### [4] Disagreement is an output, not a defect to smooth away

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** A deterministic reconciler aligns both cases by decision ID and produces:

```text
AGREE: recommendation, evidence, confidence, exclusions
DISAGREE: recommendation | evidence interpretation | coverage | severity | reversibility
UNIQUE: decisive finding cited by only one role
COMMON GAP: evidence neither role could establish
```

Do not ask one agent to silently merge both cases. A short reconciliation pass may resolve a locator mistake or factual misunderstanding, but any changed disposition must preserve the before/after cases and reason.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that preserving dissent catches premature consensus and makes minority evidence inspectable, because IAEA guidance explicitly calls for dissenting views, diverse perspectives, and processes against groupthink ([IAEA No. 28-T, pp. 56–58 of the printed publication / PDF pp. 65–67](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/PUB1761_web.pdf)). This is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E4, “dissent-retention audit”: seed a correct minority case and measure whether it survives to the owner-facing artifact**.

### [5] Risk-graded conservative release gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Apply two-person concurrence only where consequence warrants it:

| Decision class | Release rule |
|---|---|
| Reversible, low blast radius | One decision case + deterministic integrity PASS |
| Material but reversible | One case + blind verifier; disagreement may release only with recorded experiment and rollback |
| Irreversible, destructive, security-sensitive, or owner-governance change | Deterministic PASS + independent concurrence; otherwise `OWNER-DECIDES` |
| Missing evidence, broken locator, unresolved critical contradiction, packet overflow, or verifier abstention | `HOLD` |

`HOLD` preserves the prior accepted state and emits a bounded request for the missing evidence or decision. It must not fabricate “conservative” certainty.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that risk grading gives a better reliability/cost trade-off than universal two-agent review, because DOE selects tools based on task risk and reserves more rigorous verification for adverse-consequence conditions rather than requiring every tool everywhere ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. ii, 41–47](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). This is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E5, “gate economics”: measure severe joint misses prevented per added token-minute across decision classes**.

## What this would catch that a single compressed synthesis misses

The following are expected catches, not measured facts about the present harness:

1. **Silent omission of a finding cluster.** The manifest count and stratified cells disagree with the packets. **Almost certain (90-99%)** for seeded structural omissions, provided ingestion itself saw the record; evidence is the deterministic count invariant. **UNVERIFIED — E2.**
2. **One source repeated many times and mistaken for independent corroboration.** Provenance and `duplicate_of` candidates expose shared ancestry. **Likely (55-80%)**, because semantic duplication remains judgment-dependent. **UNVERIFIED — E2 plus a paraphrase-duplicate challenge set.**
3. **A high-severity minority finding averaged away by bulk consensus.** Must-read routing and dissent retention keep it visible. **Very likely (80-90%)** when severity and linkage labels are correct. **UNVERIFIED — E1/E4.**
4. **Citation laundering.** A polished recommendation cites a summary that does not support the proposition. The verifier must independently follow source locators, and the structural gate rejects broken locators. **Likely (55-80%)** for unsupported or unreachable citations; entailment errors remain semantic. **UNVERIFIED — E1 with adversarial near-match citations.**
5. **Anchoring on the first plausible architecture.** Blind IV can produce a different candidate before exposure. **Likely (55-80%)** relative to non-blind review. **UNVERIFIED — E1.**
6. **Proceeding despite uncertainty.** Explicit abort criteria turn missing evidence or unresolved contradiction into `HOLD`. **Very likely (80-90%)** for encoded triggers, but only **unlikely (20-45%)** for uncertainty that neither role recognizes. **UNVERIFIED — E3/E5.**
7. **Diffused responsibility.** Separate signed cases identify who asserted what; DOE warns that excessive reviewers and reliance on others can reduce attention ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 43, 45, 47–49](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). **Likely (55-80%)** that explicit role outputs improve auditability. **UNVERIFIED — E4.**

## What it would not catch

This proposal must not be sold as a correctness proof.

- If all upstream units omit the same fact, the ledger and both agents inherit the omission. Defence in depth cannot recover evidence that never enters any layer.
- If source locators point to authoritative-looking but false material, structural checks pass. Independent source-quality evaluation is still required.
- If the topic/severity/decision labels are systematically wrong, packet routing can systematically hide important evidence. Random audit samples reduce but do not eliminate this common cause.
- If both agents share the same model, prompts, tools, and evidence order, correlated reasoning errors remain. Mere role names do not create diversity.
- If the owner wants a value judgment rather than an evidentiary conclusion, two-agent concurrence must not displace the owner's authority.
- If `HOLD` has no practical escalation path, conservative decision-making becomes paralysis or gets routinely bypassed.

I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that the design cannot establish completeness of the world, only completeness relative to its ingested ledger, because no cited nuclear mechanism claims otherwise and the boundary follows directly from the system input. This is a scope statement, not an absence claim about the local harness.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Structural similarities that make the borrow legitimate

1. **A consequential boundary exists.** Nuclear verification concentrates on critical or adverse-consequence states; synthesis has an analogous promotion boundary where thousands of claims become one owner-facing decision.
2. **Human/agent attention is fallible.** DOE explicitly extends the practices to knowledge workers and document production, not solely valve manipulation ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, foreword pp. i–ii](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
3. **The work product has a checkable as-found state.** A decision case, evidence ledger, source locators, and coverage manifest can be independently inspected without relying on the author's narrative.
4. **Failure can be prevented, detected, and mitigated by different mechanisms.** That matches NRC defence in depth: prevention, detection/control, and safe response rather than repeated copies of one barrier ([NRC fire-protection defence in depth](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/fire-protection/defense-in-depth)).
5. **Uncertainty can change workflow state.** Both domains can pause before an irreversible act and seek additional expertise or authority.

### Disanalogies that constrain the borrow

1. **Physical plant state is more externally observable than semantic truth.** A valve position can often be checked locally against a procedure; “best harness architecture” is open-ended, partially normative, and changes as models change. Therefore, the verifier should certify evidence coverage and the defensibility of a decision case, not claim objective truth.
2. **Nuclear procedures usually have a known governing basis.** Frontier harness design has incomplete requirements and moving technology. The decision question and owner values must be explicit inputs; IV cannot manufacture them.
3. **Human independence is not model independence.** Two LLM instances may share weights and systematic blind spots even when contexts are separated. Time/distance is a metaphor; causal diversity must be measured.
4. **Error consequences differ by orders of magnitude.** Universal nuclear-grade ceremony would swamp ordinary agent work. Risk grading is essential.
5. **Synthesis inputs are adversarially compressible.** A summary can erase provenance while remaining fluent. That is why the proposal uses a lossless ledger and source pointers before semantic compression.
6. **Concurrence can suppress novelty.** Frontier research benefits from speculative minority hypotheses. A two-person gate belongs at implementation/release decisions, not at ideation generation.

Because of these disanalogies, I am **very unlikely (5-20%)** to endorse a universal two-agent approval rule. Evidence: DOE itself says its handbook is a menu, no organization is expected to use every tool, and tool selection should follow local error-reduction needs ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, p. ii](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### UNVERIFIED — Majority vote among three or more synthesizers: reject

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Despite its small implementation footprint, majority vote confuses correlation with independence, can outvote a correct minority finding, and adds cost without a deterministic coverage layer. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to outperform blind IV per token on this task, because all voters can inherit the same framing and evidence omissions. This is **UNVERIFIED — E1 could include majority vote as a comparator**, but there is insufficient cross-domain justification to recommend it now.

### UNVERIFIED — One giant hierarchical summary tree: reject as the primary control

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** A tree is useful for navigation but creates serial semantic bottlenecks: once a leaf fact is dropped or distorted, every ancestor inherits the loss. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce reading load but **unlikely (20-45%)** to provide a trustworthy completeness claim without the ledger and sampling controls. **UNVERIFIED — E1 can compare it.**

### UNVERIFIED — Full duplicate read of all 12 MB by two agents: reject as the default

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** This may be warranted for a small number of irreversible owner decisions, but it does not solve the assigned scale problem and may reproduce common-context errors. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to cost substantially more context than risk packets by construction; whether it improves correctness is **UNVERIFIED — E1 should retain it as an upper-cost comparator**.

### New orchestration service, vector database, or knowledge graph — reject

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification).** This would violate the anti-overfit gate and reintroduce explicitly rejected component classes. The proposed invariants need stable IDs, JSONL, deterministic grouping, source pointers, two isolated agent calls, and a release hook—not a new runtime or retrieval substrate. I am **almost certain (90-99%)** that BUILD-LARGE is not justified for testing this borrow, because every proposed experiment can run on files and the existing bus.

## Anti-overfit gate

The proposal passes the gate conditionally:

- It rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus.
- The deterministic reducer and release gate are each constrained to **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.
- Agent role separation and decision-case contracts are **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**.
- It introduces none of the rejected second runtime, vector-DB/KG memory, durable execution, trained critic, SaaS observability, or guardrail framework classes.
- It is risk-graded; ordinary reversible work stays single-agent.
- Its value is measurable by seeded joint misses and owner decision quality, so it can be removed if it fails the bake-off.

The conditional part is bus fit: **UNVERIFIED — E0 must show the design can be implemented without a new orchestration layer**. If E0 fails, stop; do not escalate this proposal to BUILD-LARGE merely to preserve the analogy.

## Experiments that settle the uncertain premises

### E0 — Bus-fit spike

Create a disposable fixture with ten ledger records, a deterministic manifest, two role invocations, and a release veto. Success requires two mechanically independent signals for each stage: (a) an event/trace record showing invocation and (b) the expected artifact or state transition. Pass only if no daemon, database, or second runtime is introduced.

### E1 — Blind-IV synthesis bake-off

Build a gold challenge set from 200–500 findings with planted:

- high-severity minority evidence;
- duplicated claims with shared provenance;
- near-match but non-supporting citations;
- contradictory measurements;
- one decisive finding in a low-volume topic;
- missing governing requirement;
- a tempting but inferior first architecture.

Randomize across at least these arms: single synthesis; non-blind reviewer; same-model blind IV; cross-model blind IV; three-agent majority; hierarchical summary tree; full-read duplicate. Score severe joint misses, unsupported promoted claims, minority-evidence retention, abstention calibration, locator accuracy, token-minutes, latency, and owner preference under blinded presentation. Use frozen packets and seeds. A useful result requires bootstrap confidence intervals, not one anecdotal run.

### E2 — Deterministic mutation test

Programmatically seed deletions, duplicate IDs, truncations, broken locators, unassigned high-severity records, malformed contradictions, and count mismatches. The ledger layer should catch 100% of structural mutations and produce no semantic “truth” claims. Also prove the detector can find known-valid controls before interpreting zero detections.

### E3 — STAR ablation

Hold model, packet, and decision constant. Compare ordinary drafting with precommitted expected evidence, strongest defeater, and abort criteria. Measure hidden-defeater recovery, post-hoc citation substitution, correct abstention, and verbosity. Reject STAR if it adds ritual text without improving these outcomes.

### E4 — Dissent-retention audit
