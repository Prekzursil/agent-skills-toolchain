# X051 — Cross-domain borrow: control theory → agent-claim verification

## Executive result

The control-theory mechanism that transfers is **model-based fault detection and isolation (FDI) by analytical redundancy**, augmented by an **observability test** before verification and **bounded active excitation** when passive observations cannot distinguish competing explanations. The transfer is real but narrow. In control, a residual compares a model-predicted relationship among inputs and outputs with independent measurements; a structured bank of residuals is designed to respond differently to different faults. In an agent harness, the analogous residual compares an atomic claim with consequences measured directly by tools that did not originate from the agent's prose. A claim is verified only when the expected residual pattern is observed and the relevant claim state is observable.

This is not a general truth machine. It applies to claims whose truth has operational consequences that a harness can independently sense: file state, process exit, test behavior, compiler output, service response, database state, or version-control state. It does not establish the truth of an aesthetic judgment, an underspecified requirement, a claim about an inaccessible external system, or prose whose key concepts have no executable semantics. That disanalogy is decisive.

**Bottom line:** it is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the direct structural match between tool-observable software claims and analytical-redundancy residuals plus the long-established FDI literature below, that a small residual-based verification gate would catch unsupported “done,” stale-output, wrong-target, partial-success, and single-detector failures in machine-checkable work. It is **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on the absence of a stable plant model and independent sensors for open-ended semantic work, that the same mechanism can verify arbitrary research or design conclusions without human review. Those bands deliberately describe different scopes rather than averaging into a vague middle.

## 1. The source-field mechanism, in its own terms

### 1.1 Observability: can the hidden state be inferred at all?

For a dynamical system

\[
x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k, \qquad y_k=Cx_k+Du_k,
\]

the state \(x\) is hidden, the input \(u\) is known, and the output \(y\) is measured. Observability asks whether the state can be uniquely recovered from a history of known inputs and measured outputs. Åström and Murray define a linear system as observable when, for every finite horizon, the state can be determined from measurements of \(u(t)\) and \(y(t)\); they emphasize that an observable system has no hidden dynamics indistinguishable at its outputs ([Caltech, *Feedback Systems*, chapter 8](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM08/pdf/fbs-outputfbk_24Jul2020.pdf)). MIT's notes state the negative form even more usefully: an unobservable initial state produces the same output as the zero state for every input, so it cannot be distinguished; unique recovery is possible if and only if the system is observable ([MIT 6.241J, chapter 24](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/resources/mit6_241js11_chap24/)).

For linear systems, observability is not a feeling about “enough logs.” It is a structural condition: the observability matrix assembled from \(C,CA,\ldots,CA^{n-1}\) must have full rank. Rank failure identifies an entire subspace in which different internal states have identical measured behavior ([Caltech observability rank condition](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM05/pdf/fbs-outputfbk_12Aug2019.pdf)). This matters because no estimator, however sophisticated, can reconstruct a component in that unobservable subspace from those measurements.

The first transferable lesson is therefore a refusal rule: **test whether a claim is observable before spending effort “verifying” it**. If two truth states produce the same available evidence, no verifier using that evidence can distinguish them.

### 1.2 Analytical redundancy: predict relations, then inspect residuals

Physical redundancy compares duplicate hardware sensors. Analytical redundancy replaces some duplicate hardware with model-implied relationships among measured variables. Chow and Willsky formalized robust FDI as two stages: residual generation and decision making. The model defines parity relations that should hold under nominal operation; evaluating their violation produces residuals; decision rules inspect those residuals for fault signatures. They explicitly treat model uncertainty and formulate robust residual design as minimax optimization ([Chow & Willsky, 1984, IEEE TAC author-hosted paper](https://willsky.lids.mit.edu/publ_pdfs/43_pub_IEEE.pdf); independent bibliographic record and abstract at [NASA NTRS 19840056958](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19840056958)).

The simplest idealized form is:

\[
r_k = y_k-\hat y_k(u_{0:k},y_{0:k-1};M),
\]

where \(M\) is the nominal model, \(\hat y\) is the model's prediction, and \(r\) is the residual. Under a correct model and healthy system, \(r\) is zero or lies inside a noise/uncertainty envelope. A fault drives it outside that envelope. A NASA satellite-health example describes exactly this substitution: hardware redundancy directly compares identical components, whereas analytical redundancy uses the modeled dynamic relationship between commands and measurements to form a residual; nominally the residual is zero except for modeled uncertainty and becomes nonzero under fault ([NASA, *Health Monitoring of a Satellite System*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050157886/downloads/20050157886.pdf)).

The important sophistication is not merely “run a check.” A **structured residual bank** makes different residuals sensitive to different hypothesized faults and insensitive, as far as possible, to nuisance disturbances. The FDI literature calls this detectability and isolability. Staroswiecki and colleagues develop analytical redundancy relations for sensor, actuator, and component faults, with conditions for detectability and isolability and robust residuals intended to be sensitive to faults but insensitive to perturbations ([Automatica 37(5), 2001](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000510980100005X)). The NASA Possible Conflicts work likewise reports that decomposing a global model into residual-generating submodels can improve diagnosability of multiple faults ([NASA DASHlink, “Improving Multiple Fault Diagnosability using Possible Conflicts”](https://c3.ndc.nasa.gov/dashlink/resources/889/)).

Thus, a signature such as \((r_1,r_2,r_3)=(1,0,1)\) can implicate one fault while \((1,1,0)\) implicates another. A global red light detects inconsistency; a structured pattern can isolate whether the likely source is the plant, a sensor, an actuator, or the model itself.

### 1.3 Robust thresholds and dead bands: uncertainty is not failure

Real residuals are not exactly zero. Measurement noise, unmodeled dynamics, delay, numerical precision, and changing operating conditions all create nonzero nominal residuals. Robust FDI therefore separates residual generation from decision logic and uses thresholds or statistical tests. Chow and Willsky describe instantaneous thresholds, moving averages, and sequential statistical decisions ([1984 paper](https://willsky.lids.mit.edu/publ_pdfs/43_pub_IEEE.pdf)). Later robust designs explicitly trade sensitivity to faults against attenuation of disturbances and unknown inputs, and adaptive thresholds are used to reduce false alarms ([Automatica 41(8), 2005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0005109805001147)).

A dead band or hysteresis is a decision mechanism, not additional evidence. It prevents a noisy residual near a boundary from making an alarm flap on and off. The correct conceptual translation is a three-state decision—verified, contradicted, or inconclusive—with different enter and exit criteria, not a soft score that magically turns weak evidence into truth.

### 1.4 Controllability and active diagnosis: choose an input that reveals the state

Controllability is the ability to move a system from one state to another with admissible input over finite time; Caltech's control notes connect that property to the controllability matrix test ([Caltech CDS 101 lecture 5.1](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/courses/cds101/fa02/L5.1_controllability.shtml)). For verification, the more relevant descendant is **active fault diagnosis**: nominal input/output data can be uninformative, so the diagnostic system deliberately injects an input designed to make candidate fault states produce separable outputs. A 2019 control review states the central idea directly: design input signals that increase diagnostically relevant information, because passive data may not support reliable conclusions ([Punčochář et al., *Input design for active fault diagnosis*, Annual Reviews in Control 47](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1367578819300070)). System-identification theory names the related condition persistent excitation: the input must contain enough richness to identify what is being estimated ([MIT 6.435 input-design notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-435-system-identification-spring-2005/resources/lec4_6_435/)).

The transferable lesson is that “look harder at the same trace” is sometimes mathematically incapable of resolving ambiguity. A deliberately chosen, safe challenge can create the missing information.

### 1.5 Anti-windup: feedback must respect actuator limits

Integral control accumulates error to eliminate steady-state offset. If the demanded control exceeds an actuator's physical limit, the actuator saturates while the integrator can continue accumulating error. The stored error then causes overshoot and slow recovery after saturation ends. Åström and Murray describe anti-windup as feeding the difference caused by input saturation back to reset the integrator; in normal unsaturated operation the modification is inactive ([Caltech, *Feedback Systems*, PID chapter](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/books/AM05/pdf/am08-pid_19Jul11.pdf)).

This is secondary to truth verification but useful for verifier stability. A harness often has a retry/escalation accumulator: every failed check prompts more tests, more agents, or more remediation. Once the tool, time, or context budget saturates, continuing to integrate “verification error” cannot produce more actuation; it produces retry storms and delayed recovery. Conditional integration (stop accumulating while saturated) or back-calculation (track the action actually taken, not the action demanded) is the appropriate borrow.

## 2. Concrete translation: Claim-Residual Verification

I will call the translated design **Claim-Residual Verification (CRV)**. This name is descriptive, not a claim of a new control-theory result.

### 2.1 Map the variables without pretending software is an LTI plant

| Control-theory object | Agent-harness counterpart |
|---|---|
| hidden plant state \(x\) | actual post-task world state relevant to an atomic claim |
| control input \(u\) | agent tool actions plus verifier challenge probes |
| sensor output \(y\) | fresh tool measurements obtained independently of agent prose |
| nominal model \(M\) | executable claim contract: expected relationships if the claim is true |
| observer | deterministic adapter that derives claim-relevant state from measurements |
| residual \(r\) | typed discrepancy between expected and observed consequence |
| structured residual signature | pattern across independent checks that distinguishes likely failure modes |
| uncertainty envelope/dead band | explicit inconclusive region for flaky, nondeterministic, or partially observed checks |
| active excitation | safe probe chosen to distinguish live hypotheses, such as a negative control or reproducer |
| actuator saturation | verifier's time, tool-call, context, and retry limits |
| anti-windup | stop/back-calculate escalation when no additional verification action can be executed |

Unlike a numeric plant, most software claims are hybrid and discrete. CRV should therefore use **typed residuals**, not force everything into a scalar score:

```text
MATCH            expected consequence observed
MISMATCH         contradictory consequence observed
NO_OBSERVATION   required sensor unavailable or not run
STALE            observation predates the causal action being verified
WRONG_TARGET     observation applies to another path/ref/service/environment
MODEL_GAP        contract does not cover a material part of the claim
FLAKY            repeated admissible probes disagree
SENSOR_FAULT     verifier control proves the measurement path is defective
```

The “verified” state is a conjunction over required residuals, not an average. One decisive `MISMATCH`, `STALE`, `WRONG_TARGET`, or `SENSOR_FAULT` prevents “verified.” `NO_OBSERVATION`, `MODEL_GAP`, and unresolved `FLAKY` yield “inconclusive,” not success.

### 2.2 Atomic claim contracts

An agent's final answer is decomposed into atomic, externally decidable claims. Each contract records:

```yaml
claim_id: C7
claim: "the regression is fixed"
scope:
  workspace: <resolved identity>
  revision: <resolved identity>
  target: <test or behavior identity>
required_observations:
  - reproducer_failed_before_change
  - reproducer_passed_after_change
  - negative_control_detected_known_bad_variant
independence_domains:
  - process_exit
  - structured_test_artifact
freshness_after: <last relevant mutation event>
decision:
  verified: all required residuals MATCH
  contradicted: any authoritative residual MISMATCH
  otherwise: inconclusive
```

The contract must not accept “the agent said the command passed” as a sensor. The sensor is the harness-owned process result, artifact parser, filesystem stat, protocol response, or equivalent measurement. The expected result may not be supplied solely by the same free-form agent output being judged.

Examples of structured residual banks:

| Atomic claim | Independent observations | Failure signature caught |
|---|---|---|
| “File X was created with content H” | canonical path identity; fresh file stat; independently computed content hash | wrong directory, stale file, claimed-but-never-written, content drift |
| “Tests pass” | fresh process exit; parsed test report with nonzero discovered count; target/revision identity | stale transcript, zero-test false pass, wrong suite, wrong checkout |
| “Bug B is fixed” | reproducer fails on pre-change/baseline; passes post-change; nearby negative control still fails | irrelevant change, always-green reproducer, overbroad masking |
| “Service accepted the change” | request response; subsequent independent read-back; resource/version identity | accepted response without persistence, wrong tenant/resource, cached response |
| “Nothing matching P exists” | detector proves a known-present control; two mechanically independent searches; explicit scope | broken detector, ignored hidden/remote state, incomplete scope |
| “Task is complete” | each required claim contract resolves `MATCH`; no required contract remains absent or inconclusive | partial completion hidden by a fluent aggregate statement |

This pattern directly addresses the epistemic failure in trusting a narrator: the narrator proposes claims and may propose tests, but the gate owns measurement, freshness, scope identity, controls, and the decision rule.

## 3. Proposals

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: require an observability declaration for completion claims

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Require every completion-bearing atomic claim to name: (1) its truth states, (2) the fresh outputs that distinguish those states, (3) the scope identity, and (4) the authoritative sensor. If two material truth states have the same listed evidence, label the claim `UNOBSERVABLE` and forbid “verified”; allow `reported`, `argued`, or `needs human judgment` instead.

The implementation premise that the existing harness can enforce a JSON claim contract at finalization is **UNVERIFIED; experiment:** register a minimal contract requiring one file-state observation, attempt to finalize with the observation omitted, stale, and present, and confirm that only the present/fresh case is admitted. Because that premise is unverified, this proposal's heading carries the required tag.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the exact indistinguishability criterion in MIT's observability definition and the deterministic nature of the listed software states, that this catches claims for which the harness has no possible evidentiary path before it wastes retries. It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, based on the need for agents or task schemas to enumerate claim atoms correctly, that declaration alone materially improves final-answer truth without the residual gate in Proposal B.

What it catches that ordinary post-hoc checking misses:

- a claim about an inaccessible deployment when the only evidence is a local build;
- “there are no matches” when the detector has no validated positive control;
- “requirements are satisfied” when one requirement has no executable or human-owned acceptance criterion;
- a claim scoped to “the repository” when the observation identifies neither worktree nor revision.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: add a harness-owned structured residual gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a small finalization hook that consumes claim contracts and existing tool-event records, computes typed residuals, and emits only `VERIFIED`, `CONTRADICTED`, or `INCONCLUSIVE` per atomic claim. Keep domain logic in adapters already riding the tool/hook bus; the core should only enforce conjunction, freshness, scope identity, independence-domain labels, and control results. It must never rerun an arbitrary command copied from agent prose without the normal tool policy.

The premise that a useful core plus one or two adapters fits below roughly 200 lines and that the current event bus exposes trustworthy timestamps, target identities, and raw exit/artifact data is **UNVERIFIED; experiment:** implement a throwaway read-only prototype over 100 recorded tool events, then report core line count, missing fields, and classification accuracy on 30 hand-labeled claims. The proposal therefore inherits `UNVERIFIED` in its heading.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on analytical redundancy's separation of residual generation from decision making and the direct correspondence of fresh process/file/protocol measurements, that this catches unsupported completion, stale evidence, wrong-target evidence, and zero-test false passes for covered claim types. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on structured residual isolation in the FDI literature but uncertainty about available event metadata, that the residual signature can distinguish agent-action failure from sensor/check failure often enough to reduce blind retries. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, based on the lack of an executable nominal model, that this gate can verify aesthetic quality or open-ended factual synthesis merely by adding more residual adapters.

The gate should preserve the residual vector in the artifact. “Inconclusive because `NO_OBSERVATION(scope)`” is actionable; “confidence 0.62” is not.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: active verification probes with safety envelopes

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

When passive evidence leaves two hypotheses observationally equivalent, select a bounded challenge whose predicted outputs differ. Examples:

1. Run a claimed regression reproducer against the pre-change baseline and the post-change state. A reproducer that passes in both states is not informative.
2. Feed a known-present sentinel through an absence detector before accepting a zero result. This validates the sensor path, not the target conclusion.
3. For a test-discovery claim, add or select a harmless known-failing fixture in an isolated temporary copy and confirm the runner detects it; do not mutate the owner's live tree merely to test the verifier.
4. For read-after-write state, use a fresh independent read keyed by resource identity and version instead of trusting the write response.

Every active probe needs an admissible-input envelope: read-only by default; mutations only in an isolated disposable environment; bounded time and cost; no external side effects; and a declared rollback/disposal action. If no safe separating input exists, report `UNOBSERVABLE_WITH_ADMISSIBLE_INPUTS`.

The premise that the harness can safely obtain baseline states and isolated disposable copies for these probes is **UNVERIFIED; experiment:** on ten historical bug-fix tasks, attempt pre/post reproducer execution in disposable worktrees and record setup success, side effects, time overhead, and whether the probe separates fixed from unchanged states. This heading therefore carries `UNVERIFIED`.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on active fault diagnosis's use of designed inputs when nominal trajectories are uninformative and on the concrete pre/post reproducer pattern, that active probes catch vacuous tests and checks that are insensitive to the change. It is **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on side-effect and environment constraints, that a safe separating probe exists for most production-only or externally governed claims.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: dead-band and anti-windup for verifier stability

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Use a state machine around verification:

```text
UNASSESSED -> VERIFIED       only on complete matching residual set
UNASSESSED -> CONTRADICTED   on authoritative mismatch
UNASSESSED -> INCONCLUSIVE   on missing/model-gap/flaky evidence
INCONCLUSIVE -> VERIFIED     only after new, causally relevant evidence
CONTRADICTED -> VERIFIED     only after a relevant mutation plus fresh recheck
```

Add hysteresis by requiring a genuinely new causal event before changing state; repeated parsing of the same log cannot clear a contradiction. Add anti-windup by maintaining a bounded escalation counter keyed to the unresolved residual. When tool/time/context actuation saturates, stop accumulating retries, back-calculate the achievable action to “request authority,” “request human judgment,” or “report inconclusive,” and preserve the residual. Reset only when the environment, evidence, or authorization changes.

The premise that retry storms in this harness can be keyed reliably to a stable claim/residual identity is **UNVERIFIED; experiment:** replay 50 multi-attempt traces, compare naive string keys with normalized claim/scope/residual keys, and measure false merges and false splits. This heading therefore carries `UNVERIFIED`.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the direct saturation/integrator analogy and the cited anti-windup mechanism, that bounded escalation prevents repeated verifier actions after the relevant actuation budget is exhausted. It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, based on the weaker mapping between continuous integral state and heterogeneous agent retries, that back-calculation adds material value beyond a simple retry cap. Therefore the minimal first implementation should be a cap plus causal-reset rule; back-calculation is retained only if replay shows better recovery.

## 4. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 4.1 Where the structural assumptions transfer

The analogy transfers when all of the following hold:

1. **A claim has explicit competing states.** “File hash is H” has true/false states; “the design is elegant” does not without a human-owned rubric.
2. **There is a stable relation from state and action to observation.** A compiler invocation over a resolved revision predicts an exit and diagnostics; a vague research judgment does not.
3. **The verifier controls or authenticates the measurement path.** Raw tool results can serve as sensors; a paraphrase written by the claimant cannot be treated as independent sensing.
4. **Uncertainty can be bounded or classified.** Nondeterminism, flakiness, missing access, and stale state must yield an explicit envelope or `INCONCLUSIVE`, not be silently absorbed.
5. **At least one residual is sensitive to each material failure mode.** If wrong-revision execution and correct-revision execution generate the same logged fields, revision is unobservable.
6. **For isolation, residual signatures differ.** Two checks that both scrape the same cached output are numerically two results but structurally one sensor and cannot isolate a cache fault.

For file/process/test/API state, those assumptions are **very likely (80-90%)** to hold after explicit scoping, based on deterministic interfaces and the analytical-redundancy requirement for model-implied input/output relations. For broad semantic claims, they are **unlikely (20-45%)** to hold, based on the lack of unique state definitions, stable transition models, and independent sensors.

### 4.2 Where it fails

**No fixed plant.** An LTI plant has equations whose validity can be tested and bounded. An agent task changes domain, tools, requirements, and environment from turn to turn. A universal observer matrix is fiction. CRV must build a small claim-local model, not a global “world model.”

**The claimant can influence the model.** In classical FDI, a sensor fault usually does not rewrite the engineer's parity equations. An agent can propose both the claim and a weak test that passes. Therefore the verifier must own contract classes and negative controls; agent-supplied checks are hypotheses, not authorities.

**Common-mode failure is dominant.** Analytical redundancy can fail when model and sensor share the same bad assumption. In software, “independent” tools may share a filesystem cache, checkout, credentials, generated artifact, or parser. Independence is causal, not a count of command names. Two wrappers around the same compiler result are one signal.

**Adversarial behavior differs from stochastic noise.** Dead bands assume small nominal variation; a strategic agent can place evidence just inside a threshold or optimize to the verifier. Numeric evidence scores invite gaming. Typed, decisive predicates and held-out challenge probes are safer, though still not proof against a compromised toolchain.

**Truth may not be observable without changing the subject.** Active diagnosis perturbs a plant. A verification probe against production could send mail, charge money, delete state, or violate policy. Controllability does not confer authorization. The admissible input set can be empty, and “cannot verify safely” is then the correct result.

**Temporal semantics differ.** A tool result can be true at time \(t\) and stale after a later mutation. Control theory naturally models time evolution; a harness must explicitly attach causal ordering and scope identity or it will treat an old green check as a current measurement.

**A residual detects inconsistency, not which side is true.** If expected and measured consequences disagree, the agent may be wrong, the contract may be wrong, the sensor may be wrong, or the environment may have changed. Structured residuals and controls can isolate some of these, but a single mismatch is contradiction of the verification model—not metaphysical proof of claimant fault.

### 4.3 Why not use Kalman filtering, a learned critic, or a scalar confidence score?

A Kalman filter is powerful when transition and observation models plus noise distributions are meaningful. Arbitrary agent claims do not satisfy that premise. A learned critic adds another fallible semantic claimant and does not create an independent observation channel. A scalar confidence score collapses decisive mismatches and missing observations into an average, obscuring why the claim failed.

It is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, based on these model and independence failures, that a universal probabilistic “truth observer” would preserve the guarantees that make observability and analytical redundancy useful. CRV should borrow the structural discipline—observability, residuals, fault signatures, excitation, saturation handling—without importing continuous-state mathematics where its assumptions do not hold.

## 5. What this would catch, and what it would not

### Covered misses

1. **Unsupported completion:** the final claim has no matching tool-owned observation → `NO_OBSERVATION`.
2. **Stale green evidence:** the check predates a relevant edit → `STALE`.
3. **Wrong target:** the check ran in another worktree, revision, tenant, or resource → `WRONG_TARGET`.
4. **Vacuous test:** exit zero but no tests discovered, or the reproducer also passes on baseline → residual signature contradicts “bug fixed.”
5. **Broken absence detector:** known-present sentinel is missed → `SENSOR_FAULT`; the target absence claim remains inconclusive.
6. **Partial success:** aggregate “done” decomposes into claims and at least one required residual is absent or contradictory.
7. **Write-response optimism:** write endpoint reports success but independent read-back disagrees.
8. **Retry windup:** repeated attempts cannot add a new observation after budget or authority saturation, so escalation stops with the unresolved residual preserved.

For these covered, machine-observable patterns, detection is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on each pattern having a direct, deterministic residual and on FDI's established use of residual generation plus decisions. Exact recall and false-positive rates are **UNVERIFIED; experiment:** create a labeled suite containing at least ten examples of each pattern plus clean controls, replay the gate, and report the confusion matrix by residual type.

### Explicitly not covered

- correctness of an inaccessible external claim;
- whether prose is insightful, tasteful, persuasive, or complete under an unstated rubric;
- malicious compromise below every observation channel, such as a shared toolchain or host fabric;
- requirement correctness when the requirement itself is ambiguous;
- proof that no unknown failure mode exists;
- semantic factuality of research synthesis without authenticated sources and claim-specific entailment checks.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the observability indistinguishability result, that no harness using only the same available evidence can resolve these cases merely by adding more reasoning tokens. The remaining 1-10% reflects the possibility that a task-specific new sensor, formal specification, or safe active probe changes the evidence set; it does not license claiming verification from the old evidence.

## 6. Evaluation plan and falsification criteria

The borrow should be rejected, not merely tuned, if it fails controlled evaluation.

### Experiment 1: observability-gate validity

Construct 60 atomic claims: 20 fully observable, 20 deliberately unobservable, and 20 observable only with an active probe. Have independent human labelers specify the distinguishing evidence before the gate runs. Measure whether the declaration correctly separates the three classes. Include known-present controls to establish that each detector can observe something.

**Success criterion (UNVERIFIED until run):** at least 90% agreement on the deliberately unobservable cases and zero cases promoted from unobservable to verified without a new sensor. The 90% is a proposed engineering threshold, not a control-theory theorem.

### Experiment 2: residual fault injection

Inject one fault at a time into recorded, otherwise-correct traces: stale timestamp, wrong revision, zero discovered tests, altered exit code, cached read, omitted observation, and broken sentinel detector. Then inject pairs to test isolability. Compare a simple “last command succeeded” baseline with CRV.

**Success criterion (UNVERIFIED until run):** CRV improves detection of injected faults by at least 25 percentage points without increasing false contradiction on clean traces by more than 5 points. These thresholds are design choices and must not be reported as expected results.

### Experiment 3: active-probe information gain

For historical bug-fix tasks, measure whether baseline/post-change reproducer pairs distinguish “relevant fix” from “irrelevant edit” more often than post-change tests alone. Record cases where no admissible probe exists and all side effects.

**Success criterion (UNVERIFIED until run):** active probes resolve at least 30% of cases previously labeled inconclusive, with zero mutations outside disposable environments. Again, this is a go/no-go threshold, not a forecast.

### Experiment 4: verifier stability replay

Replay retry loops under: no cap; simple cap; cap plus causal reset; and cap plus back-calculation. Measure tool calls, time to stable terminal state, premature abandonment, and repeated use of identical evidence.

**Success criterion (UNVERIFIED until run):** cap plus causal reset reduces duplicate verifier actions by at least 50% without lowering fault-detection recall by more than 2 points. Retain back-calculation only if it improves recovery beyond that simpler policy.

### Falsifiers

Abandon or sharply narrow CRV if any of the following occurs:

- event metadata cannot establish freshness and scope identity;
- supposed independent sensors share common-mode state so often that signatures do not isolate faults;
- claim decomposition produces more unreviewable overhead than the human reading it replaces;
- active probes cannot be contained safely;
- clean nondeterministic tasks are routinely contradicted rather than marked inconclusive;
- the core requires a second orchestration runtime or large general world model to function.

## 7. Anti-overfit gate

All four proposals ride existing settings, hook, skill, MCP, or tool-event buses. They introduce no second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The core idea is a small deterministic predicate engine over evidence already produced by the harness. Domain adapters are added only for high-frequency claim types with crisp semantics.

Proposal A is **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**. Proposals B-D are each **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. No proposal is `BUILD-LARGE`. If replay shows that the small core cannot work without durable cross-run state or a generalized semantic model, the correct conclusion is to reject the expansion rather than justify a new runtime by momentum.

This passes the anti-overfit gate because the mechanism constrains claims at the boundary and remains useful even as base models improve: stronger agents may generate fewer false claims, but observability and independent measurement remain properties of the task and evidence channel, not model intelligence. That durability is **likely (55-80%)**, based on observability being an information constraint independent of estimator sophistication; the lower bound reflects that future native harnesses may subsume the small gate and make a custom implementation unnecessary.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Majority vote among agents

Rejected. Multiple agents can share training priors, prompt context, tools, and the same stale or wrong-target evidence. Voting adds narrators, not sensors. It can help search but does not satisfy analytical redundancy unless voters receive genuinely independent observations and fixed decision rules.

### “Always run more tests”

Rejected. Repeating an insensitive input does not improve observability. Active diagnosis calls for a separating input, and persistent excitation calls for informative input, not volume. Re-running the same probe also cannot validate a broken detector.

### One global verifier score

Rejected. It destroys fault isolation, lets numerous weak matches outweigh a decisive mismatch, and makes missing evidence hard to distinguish from contradictory evidence.

### Full formal verification of every task

Rejected as a general harness mechanism. Formal proof is excellent where a formal specification and semantics exist, but most agent tasks do not provide them. CRV can invoke formal tools as authoritative sensors for claim-local properties without pretending the entire task is formalized.

### Human reads everything

Not rejected as a safety fallback, but it fails the target constraint as the default. CRV's purpose is to automatically close machine-observable claims and route only unobservable, model-gap, ambiguous, or high-impact residuals to a human with the exact unresolved evidence.

## 9. Final recommendation

Adopt the borrow only as a **claim-local verification discipline**:

1. refuse verification of unobservable claims;
2. decompose observable claims into executable contracts;
3. generate harness-owned typed residuals from fresh, scope-bound measurements;
4. use structured residual patterns and known-present controls to distinguish claimant failure from sensor/model failure;
5. apply safe active probes only when they separate live hypotheses;
6. use an inconclusive dead band and bounded, causally reset escalation;
7. preserve human review for semantic claims and unresolved model gaps.

The strongest contribution from control theory is not “feedback makes agents better.” It is the harder epistemic rule that **a hidden state cannot be recovered from outputs that do not distinguish it**, followed by an engineering method—analytical redundancy and active diagnosis—for deliberately creating and evaluating those distinctions. That transfers. A universal autonomous truth observer does not.
