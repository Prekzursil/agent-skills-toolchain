# X093 — Cross-domain borrow: mechanism design → cost control

## Executive result

The useful borrow is **Chen, Kash, Ruberry, and Shnayder's decision-market mechanism**, specifically its combination of (1) conditional forecasts for every available action, (2) a decision rule with full support, and (3) a decision scoring rule that divides the realized action's proper score by that action's selection probability. The market and monetary-transfer story does **not** transfer. The causal-identification skeleton does.

My conclusion is **likely (55-80%)** that a small, randomized, properly scored compute-allocation pilot would discover materially better routing rules than uniform spend or a deterministic confidence threshold. Evidence: the source mechanism proves why deterministic selection destroys truthful evaluation of unchosen actions; contextual-bandit work independently identifies the same partial-label problem and shows that randomized logs support unbiased replay evaluation; and rational-metareasoning work independently frames computations as actions whose expected decision-quality gain must be traded against their cost. The wide band reflects the decisive untested premise: harness-visible outcomes may be too sparse, delayed, or gameable to train an instance-level router.

This is **not** a recommendation to build a prediction market, token economy, agent auction, second orchestrator, or self-reward loop. Those additions would add strategic theater without satisfying the assumptions that make the original mechanism truthful.

## The source mechanism in its own field

### 1. Proper scoring rules: elicit a probability, not confidence-shaped prose

A scoring rule assigns a numerical reward from a reported predictive distribution and the event that materializes. A rule is *proper* when reporting the forecaster's actual belief maximizes expected score, and *strictly proper* when that maximizer is unique. Gneiting and Raftery's review gives this definition on general probability spaces and relates proper rules to convex functions, entropy, and Bregman divergences ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007, PDF](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf); [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). Savage's foundational treatment explicitly studies proper scoring rules as devices for eliciting personal probabilities and expectations ([Savage 1971](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1971.10482346)). Brier's probability-verification paper supplies the classic quadratic score's origin ([Brier 1950](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%281950%29078%3C0001%3AVOFEIT%3E2.0.CO%3B2)).

For a binary event `y ∈ {0,1}`, one convenient reward convention is

```text
s_y(p) = -(p - y)^2
```

where the expected reward is uniquely maximized by reporting the forecaster's believed probability. The essential property is not that a single forecast becomes correct; it is that repeated reports cannot improve expected score by systematic exaggeration or hedging under the theorem's assumptions.

### 2. Why an ordinary proper score fails when forecasts choose the action

Decision markets ask a harder question: what outcome would occur under each possible action, when the decision maker will choose an action using those forecasts? Only the selected action's outcome becomes observable; all other action-conditioned outcomes are counterfactual. Consequently, an expert may distort forecasts to make the decision maker select the branch on which the expert expects the largest scoring gain.

Chen et al. state this failure directly: a standard strictly proper market is not generally myopically incentive-compatible when its forecast affects the decision, because only the prediction for the chosen action can be evaluated ([Chen et al. 2011, Harvard record](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-a5f7-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b); [Microsoft full paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/DM_full_version.pdf)). This is the exact structural problem in compute routing: after the harness chooses “stop,” “browse,” “run tests,” “ask another model,” or “reason longer,” it normally observes only the chosen path.

### 3. The specific fix: full support plus inverse-probability decision scoring

Let actions be `a ∈ A`. Let the forecaster report `P_a`, a distribution over outcomes conditional on action `a`. Let the decision rule choose action `a` with probability `d_a`, with **full support**: `d_a > 0` for every eligible action. Given any strictly proper base score `s`, Chen et al.'s construction scores the observed action and outcome as

```text
S_(a,o)(d,P) = (1 / d_a) * s_o(P_a)
```

The expected score cancels the selection probability:

```text
Σ_a d_a Σ_o Q_(a,o) [(1/d_a) s_o(P_a)]
= Σ_a Σ_o Q_(a,o) s_o(P_a).
```

Thus the report for every action is evaluated in expectation as if each conditional market were observed. Under the paper's assumptions, truthful conditional reporting uniquely maximizes expected score. Their Theorem 1 provides this construction; Theorem 2 says a strictly proper differentiable decision-scoring mechanism exists if and only if the decision rule has full support ([Chen et al. full paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/DM_full_version.pdf)). The paper also exposes the price: actual scores scale as `1/d_a`, so making exploration probabilities tiny increases worst-case loss/variance.

This is not merely analogous to later causal evaluation. It is mechanically consonant with contextual-bandit logging, where only the chosen action's reward is observed. Li et al. call this the “partial-label” setting and provide a randomized replay method with provably unbiased evaluation ([Li et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.5956)). Dudík, Langford, and Li show how doubly robust evaluation combines a reward model with the logged action policy and remains accurate when either component is good, addressing the bias/variance tradeoff of simpler estimators ([Dudík et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601); [ICML paper](https://icml.cc/2011/papers/554_icmlpaper.pdf)). These are mechanically independent literatures supporting the same requirement: randomize and record propensities if later evaluation must say what a different decision rule would have done.

### 4. Relation to value of computation

Mechanism design supplies truthful elicitation and evaluation under action-dependent observation; it does not supply the objective. Rational metareasoning supplies that objective. Russell describes object-level computations as actions with costs and benefits and says a rational metalevel selects computations by expected utility; he also notes that myopic value-of-computation approximations can fail when several computations are jointly required to change the current preferred action ([Russell, “Rationality and Intelligence,” pp. 7-9](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/ptai13-intelligence.pdf)). This yields the harness objective:

```text
choose a to maximize E[task utility | x, do(a)] - compute_cost(a)
```

not “spend more when confidence is low,” “keep all tools equally busy,” or “minimize tokens.” Confidence is merely one contextual feature; task utility net of cost is the target.

## Translation to an agent harness

## Recommendation — UNVERIFIED until the paired-routing experiment below — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Build a **compute decision logger and randomized audit gate**, not a new orchestrator. The recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because no cited result establishes that a harness can obtain a sufficiently reliable task-utility label from its existing tests, verifiers, or owner feedback. The settling experiment is the paired-routing pilot specified under “Validation and kill criteria.”

### Eligible decisions

Instrument a few internal, reversible compute choices with comparable outcome semantics:

```text
a0 = stop with the current answer
a1 = perform one additional targeted verification step
a2 = obtain one independent candidate/review, then synthesize
```

Do not initially mix qualitatively different actions such as external writes, destructive shell commands, or contacting people. Full support is safe only over actions whose occasional random selection has bounded downside. It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that mandatory exploration over destructive or externally consequential actions would be unacceptable; evidence is structural rather than empirical: full support assigns nonzero probability to every eligible action, including the bad action, so eligibility must exclude actions whose random execution is impermissible.

### Forecast before allocation

At a decision point with context `x`, require the cheap router/forecaster to emit, for each eligible action:

```json
{
  "action": "targeted_verify",
  "p_utility_pass": 0.74,
  "p_material_answer_change": 0.31,
  "predicted_incremental_tokens": 4200,
  "predicted_incremental_seconds": 35
}
```

`p_utility_pass` is the allocative forecast. `p_material_answer_change` is diagnostic only: changing an answer is not inherently valuable and would be a Goodhart-prone target. Cost fields are measured after the action and may initially use empirical medians rather than model guesses.

The router selects the action with greatest predicted net value most of the time, but an audit probability `ε` samples another **eligible** action. It logs the complete probability vector, chosen action, forecast vector, task fingerprint, actual token/time cost, and later outcome. The production action probabilities must never be reconstructed after the fact; the logged propensity is part of the evidence.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that an epsilon range of roughly 0.02-0.10 per eligible alternative is a tolerable pilot starting point. Evidence: the decision-market theorem requires positive support, while its `1/d_a` score exposes exploding variance as support approaches zero; however, no cited source chooses an epsilon for agent harnesses. **UNVERIFIED**: a sweep over `ε ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.10}` measuring regret, inverse-propensity effective sample size, and quota overhead would settle the local choice.

### Outcome, score, and allocation target

For benchmarkable coding/research tasks, define a blinded outcome `y` from task-level evidence available after completion: held-out tests, a deterministic verifier, or a blinded rubric applied to both outputs. Score the router's conditional outcome forecast with a bounded quadratic score and use the logged action probability for inverse-propensity evaluation. Prefer a clipped or doubly robust estimator for policy comparison; do not turn the high-variance `1/d_a` number into a live per-turn reinforcement signal.

The budget rule estimates, by task stratum,

```text
incremental value(a) = E[U | do(a), x] - E[U | do(a0), x] - normalized_cost(a)
```

and routes extra compute only where the lower confidence bound on incremental value is positive, subject to an exploration reserve. “Lower confidence bound” here is a safety/conservatism choice, not a mechanism-design theorem.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that bounded Brier scoring is preferable to log scoring in the pilot. Evidence: both are strictly proper under the standard assumptions, while the decision-market multiplier magnifies realized scores by `1/d_a`; bounded scores cap a source of variance that log scores leave unbounded near zero. **UNVERIFIED**: compare calibration error, policy value, and estimator variance under predeclared Brier and clipped-log variants on the pilot logs.

### Minimal data flow

```text
current candidate + cheap context features
                  |
                  v
conditional outcome/cost forecasts for a0, a1, a2
                  |
                  v
net-value policy + epsilon full-support audit over safe actions
                  |
                  v
chosen computation -> final candidate -> external/task outcome
                  |
                  v
propensity-aware score and offline policy comparison
```

The hook should append JSONL and make one local randomized choice. Offline analysis can be a separate script over those logs. This rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus and introduces no daemon, durable workflow engine, vector database, trained critic, or observability service.

### What it would catch that simple cost controls miss

1. **Selective-allocation bias.** Expensive verification is naturally chosen on hard tasks. Its raw pass rate can therefore be lower even when it helps. Propensity-aware randomized data separates task difficulty from the action's causal contribution.

2. **The “never tried, therefore useless” trap.** A deterministic router that never browses on a task class can never learn whether browsing helps that class. Full support over safe actions preserves discoverability.

3. **False confidence as a routing proxy.** A model can be confident and wrong or uncertain yet already correct. Proper scoring evaluates numerical forecasts against outcomes across repetitions; it does not reward confident language by itself.

4. **Compute that changes wording but not utility.** Tracking material answer change as a diagnostic while allocating on task utility catches escalations that produce churn without correctness gain.

5. **Quota saved on easy tasks but wasted on the wrong hard tasks.** Per-action conditional outcome forecasts distinguish “difficult but verification-responsive” from “difficult and not helped by this escalation.”

6. **Self-confirming historical policies.** If the harness always escalates the same signatures, historical correlations will endorse that policy because counterfactual outcomes are absent. Randomized action support and logged propensities make off-policy comparison possible.

7. **Goodharting on token count.** Minimizing tokens can select terse wrong answers; maximizing pass rate can spend without bound. Net task utility explicitly charges compute cost while leaving correctness in the objective.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that the logger would reveal selection bias in any existing deterministic routing data. Evidence: only selected-action outcomes are observed by construction, the same partial-label condition identified independently by Chen et al. and Li et al. Whether correcting that bias changes the chosen policy is **roughly even (45-55%)**; **UNVERIFIED**, and settled by comparing deterministic-policy estimates against randomized replay estimates on the same task strata.

## Adversarial transfer audit

### What transfers

The following structural correspondence is strong:

| Decision-market object | Harness object | Structural match |
|---|---|---|
| action | compute mode: stop, verify, second view | exact finite choice |
| conditional forecast | predicted task outcome under each mode | same action-conditioned report |
| only selected outcome resolves | only executed compute path is observed | exact missing-counterfactual problem |
| full-support decision rule | randomized audit among safe compute modes | same positivity requirement |
| inverse decision probability in score | propensity-weighted policy evaluation | same cancellation identity |
| market-maker loss rises as probabilities shrink | estimator variance rises as epsilon shrinks | same `1/d_a` mechanism |

The partial-label and positivity assumptions therefore transfer. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that omitting action propensities would invalidate causal comparisons between compute policies; evidence is the algebra above and the independent contextual-bandit replay literature.

### What does not transfer

1. **There is no genuinely self-interested, risk-neutral expert.** An LLM call has no persistent utility from its score and does not choose reports to maximize expected monetary payment. Proper scoring remains a useful calibration loss, but “truthful revelation” is not literally guaranteed. This breaks the economic interpretation.

2. **Copies of one model are not independent information holders.** Shared training, prompt context, and failure modes make a crowd of agents a correlated ensemble, not a market of privately informed experts. The decision-market paper's information-aggregation results cannot be imported merely by naming calls “traders.”

3. **Outcome truth is frequently missing.** A test result can resolve a coding claim; subjective usefulness, long-term maintainability, or owner satisfaction may be delayed or never observed. A proper score cannot repair a bad or absent outcome variable. Proxy scoring rules exist, but require an “appropriate proxy”; Witkowski et al. explicitly condition immediate proper scoring on that access ([Witkowski et al. 2017](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10590)). That condition is precisely what is uncertain here.

4. **The action can change the outcome and the evaluator.** Extra compute may rewrite tests, alter the rubric, or persuade a model judge. The clean mechanism assumes a verifiable realized outcome for the selected condition, not a participant-controlled scoreboard.

5. **Per-instance treatment effect is not observed.** Randomization identifies average conditional policy value over repeated comparable tasks. It does not reveal whether extra compute helped the exact task that received it. Even paired outputs share only evaluable artifacts, not two realizations of all downstream real-world consequences.

6. **Myopic incentive compatibility is not global robustness.** Chen et al. explicitly distinguish myopic incentives from manipulation across multiple rounds. A self-modifying or adaptive router could optimize against the evaluator over time.

7. **Full support is ethically and operationally bounded.** It is appropriate for extra internal reasoning; it is not permission to randomly execute every tool or external side effect.

These failures mean the analogy supports an **experimental-design and evaluation mechanism**, not a market runtime. It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** the case that token credits or synthetic payments would add truthful-revelation guarantees by themselves. Evidence: the required economic agent utility, risk neutrality, and persistent payoff maximization are absent; a numeric “payment” consumed only as prompt text does not create those assumptions.

## Goodhart analysis

Three candidate targets are especially dangerous:

* **“Did the answer change?”** Extra compute can maximize change by rewriting correct answers.
* **“Did a model judge prefer it?”** Generator and judge may share stylistic biases, and optimization can select judge-pleasing rather than task-correct output.
* **“Did tests pass?”** If the compute action can alter tests or select the evaluated subset, pass rate becomes endogenous.

The design mitigates, but cannot eliminate, Goodhart pressure by separating diagnostics from the allocative objective, keeping evaluation blinded where possible, using held-out task outcomes, logging several outcome channels, and reserving randomized audits. It is **likely (55-80%)** that a composite of held-out tests plus blinded pairwise review will be harder to game than a single self-judge score. Evidence: the signals depend on different mechanisms; **UNVERIFIED**: a red-team experiment where the router is explicitly prompted to maximize the composite without improving the task outcome must measure proxy/outcome divergence before deployment.

No metric should automatically become a permanent routing target. Freeze the evaluation definition during an experiment, keep a holdout not visible to the router, and rotate only between evaluation epochs. This is a direct response to metric optimization, not a claim that Goodhart effects disappear.

## Validation and kill criteria

### Phase 0: label audit — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Before adaptive routing, choose 100-300 replayable tasks with an outcome that neither candidate can modify. Run baseline and one escalation path with fixed seeds where supported, blind their identities, and evaluate both. Record utility difference and incremental cost.

Kill the project if two mechanically independent outcome signals (for example, held-out tests and blinded owner/rubric judgment) disagree so often that the sign of incremental value is unstable, or if fewer than a predeclared fraction of tasks obtain any trustworthy outcome. The threshold must be declared before reading results. **UNVERIFIED**: this phase is the experiment that determines label adequacy.

### Phase 1: randomized pilot — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

On safe, replayable tasks, log forecasts and choose between baseline and one escalation with a predeclared epsilon. Do not train online. At the end, report:

* calibration and Brier score by task stratum;
* incremental utility and cost with uncertainty intervals;
* effective sample size and weight distribution under inverse propensity;
* a doubly robust estimate as a mechanically independent analysis;
* disagreement between naive observed pass rates and randomized estimates;
* worst-regret audit examples.

The claim “routing improved” requires two mechanically independent signals: (1) a held-out randomized estimate of utility minus cost, and (2) a paired shadow-run comparison on a disjoint sample. Each detector must first recover a known-present synthetic effect (for example, an action whose evaluator-visible success is deliberately improved on a planted task subset) before it is trusted to report “no effect.” This control follows the unit's mandatory detector-validity standard.

### Phase 2: shadow policy — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Fit or hand-tune a shallow policy on pilot logs, then run it in shadow mode while the existing allocator remains authoritative. Promote only if the new policy's lower-bound net utility beats uniform allocation and a simple deterministic baseline on held-out randomized data. If a shallow rule performs as well as a learned router, keep the shallow rule.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a stratified lookup table or small logistic model will be sufficient for an initial router. Evidence: the action set and initial features are deliberately small; **UNVERIFIED**: nested cross-validation comparing the simple policy to a higher-capacity model settles this. This is explicitly an anti-overfit choice, not a claim of universal optimality.

## Anti-overfit gate

The proposal passes the stated gate:

* it rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus;
* it adds an append-only logger, a randomized chooser, and offline scoring;
* it does not add a second orchestration runtime;
* it does not add vector/KG memory;
* it does not add durable execution;
* it does not train a critic;
* it does not add observability SaaS or a guardrail framework.

The most important restraint is to **stop at the experiment** unless the randomized evidence beats simple baselines. Russell notes both that value-of-computation control can yield large gains in structured search settings and that accurate general metalevel control remains difficult, myopic policies can fail, and optimal metareasoning creates a regress because metareasoning itself costs compute ([Russell, pp. 7-9](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/ptai13-intelligence.pdf)). A cheap lookup table with randomized audits is therefore more faithful to the evidence than a large learned metacontroller.

## Rejected borrows and dead ends

### VCG auction for token allocation — rejected

VCG solves allocation among strategic agents with private valuations and quasilinear transfers by making truthful reports a dominant strategy under its assumptions. Harness subagents do not own token budgets, experience transfer utility, or possess reliably independent valuations. Simulated bids would be ungrounded model outputs, and payments would be prompt theater. VCG therefore does not transfer to this owner-level cost-control problem.

### Prediction market among subagents — rejected

The target is not consensus for its own sake; it is causal value of additional computation. Multiple correlated model samples can all bid confidently while sharing the same error. Without a resolved external outcome and real utility from scoring, the central mechanism is absent.

### Optimize self-reported confidence — rejected

Confidence thresholds are cheap and may be useful features, but rewarding the confidence report without external resolution creates exactly the proxy-target collapse the source field warns about. Properness is an expected-score relationship to realized outcomes, not a format instruction such as “be calibrated.”

### Always run paired shadows — rejected as production policy

Paired runs are valuable for a bounded audit but defeat cost control if applied uniformly. Use them on a randomized validation slice, not every task.

### Full contextual-bandit service — rejected

A continuously learning daemon would be a BUILD-LARGE component and reintroduce an orchestration/learning runtime before label validity is known. The small logger plus offline analysis tests the premise first.

## Final judgment

This cross-domain borrow **partially transfers**. The transferable result is precise: when the forecast influences which compute action runs, ordinary outcome scoring is selection-biased; safe actions need nonzero randomized support, logged propensities, and action-probability-adjusted offline evaluation. The nontransferable result is equally important: an LLM ensemble does not become an incentive-compatible market merely because the harness invents bids and scores.

The recommended artifact is therefore a small causal audit gate, not an economic simulation. It would spend a controlled exploration reserve to learn where compute changes task utility, then reclaim quota from strata where measured incremental value does not cover cost. Whether that produces net savings and equal-or-better answer quality is **roughly even (45-55%)** before local experimentation. Evidence for the mechanism's identification logic is strong; evidence that this harness has enough repeated, externally resolvable outcomes is absent by instruction because this ideation unit did not inspect local data. **UNVERIFIED**: the paired-routing experiment and held-out randomized pilot above are the named experiments that settle the recommendation.
