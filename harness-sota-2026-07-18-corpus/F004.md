# F004 — Frontier research: agent-as-a-judge and LLM-judge calibration failures

**Unit:** F004  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19 UTC  
**Scope:** What is actually established about LLM judges and agent-as-a-judge; what remains a proof of concept; calibration failures; and the minimum harness changes justified by evidence.  
**Subject mutation:** none. The unpacked harness was read-only. This report is the only written artifact.

## Executive judgment

LLM judges are useful measurement instruments, not authorities. The established result is narrow: on some tasks, with a sufficiently capable model and a carefully specified rubric, an LLM judge can approximate a particular set of human labels more cheaply than fresh human annotation. The equally established qualification is that the same instrument changes its answer under irrelevant changes in order, length, style, model identity, and adversarial text, and that performance varies substantially by task and rubric. MT-Bench's original result was over 80% agreement with human preferences, but its own study also documented position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning limitations ([Zheng et al., 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). LLMBar, JudgeBench, FairEval, length-controlled AlpacaEval, and prompt-injection work independently expose different failure surfaces ([Zeng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07641); [Tan et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784); [Wang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926); [Dubois et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475); [Shi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17710)).

“Calibration” is routinely overloaded in this literature. Agreement, rank correlation, position balancing, prompt refinement, and confidence calibration are different things. In statistical calibration, forecasts made with probability *p* should be correct about a *p* fraction of the time; correlation or Cohen's kappa does not establish that property ([Guo et al., 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html); [Cohen, 1960](https://doi.org/10.1177/001316446002000104)). Therefore a judge with high kappa can still be dangerously overconfident, and a judge with a well-calibrated base rate can still discriminate poorly.

Agent-as-a-Judge is a valuable proof of concept, not an established general solution. Its primary paper evaluates 55 AI-development tasks with 365 hierarchical requirements, reports better agreement with its human baseline than a plain LLM judge, and shows that giving the judge tools for reading, locating, and retrieving evidence improves agreement on that testbed ([Zhuge et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934); [reference implementation](https://github.com/metauto-ai/agent-as-a-judge)). The experiment is small, code-development-specific, uses the same benchmark and human process for design and evaluation, and does not establish calibrated confidence, adversarial robustness, or generalization across domains and model generations. Its paper itself calls the self-improvement “flywheel” an outlook/hypothesis, not a demonstrated result ([paper §6](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)).

The harness's current high-level posture is substantially right: its judge is advisory, runs after deterministic verification, cannot grant “done,” and labels its caught-error corpus as structurally blind to escaped defects ([local subject, `claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md`, lines 15–41 and 101–121](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)). The important correction is to stop treating kappa ≥ 0.6 as a calibration license. Retain kappa as one descriptive agreement statistic; promotion must instead require prospective, held-out, category-stratified estimates of false negatives, false positives, selective risk/coverage, perturbation stability, and—only if the system emits probabilities—proper probability calibration.

# 1. Established

## 1.1 Human agreement is achievable on some distributions; it is not universal validity

The strongest early positive result is MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena: GPT-4 judge agreement exceeded 80% on the studied comparisons, comparable to the reported human-human agreement. That establishes feasibility for approximate preference labeling on those distributions. The same paper explicitly reports position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and limited-reasoning biases, so it does not establish an unbiased general evaluator ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); [FastChat judge code and prompts](https://github.com/lm-sys/FastChat/tree/main/fastchat/llm_judge)).

G-Eval found a Spearman correlation of 0.514 with human judgments for one summarization setup and also flagged bias toward LLM-generated text. This is evidence that structured criteria and reasoning prompts can improve correlation in a task, not evidence that generated rationales are faithful or that the judge is calibrated ([Liu et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634); [G-Eval repository](https://github.com/nlpyang/geval)).

A broader empirical study across 20 NLP evaluation datasets and 11 LLMs found substantial variance by model, dataset, judged property, annotator expertise, and whether text was human- or model-generated. Its conclusion is that an LLM evaluator must be validated against human judgments for the intended task, rather than inherited as a generic capability ([Bavaresco et al., 2024 preprint / ACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18403); [ACL publication](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.20/)). This is a second, mechanically distinct signal from the single-benchmark studies: many datasets and models rather than one prompt/model pair.

**What is established:** local criterion- and distribution-specific agreement can be good.  
**What is not implied:** correctness, cross-domain transport, adversarial robustness, calibrated confidence, or future-model stability.

## 1.2 Position bias is real, measurable, and not fixed by asking for fairness

FairEval showed that merely swapping response order could reverse rankings; in its striking example, Vicuna-13B beat ChatGPT on 66 of 80 prompts when ChatGPT judged a manipulated ordering. Balanced position calibration—judge both A/B and B/A and aggregate—reduced the bias toward human labels ([Wang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926); [FairEval code/data](https://github.com/i-Eval/FairEval)).

The independent MT-Bench study also measured position bias and adopted order-swapping consistency logic ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). A later 150,000-evaluation study over 15 judges, MTBench and DevBench reported that position effects vary by judge, candidate, task, and quality gap ([Shi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)). These are independent datasets/experimental matrices, satisfying the two-signal threshold for the existence of the failure class.

Operational consequence: one pairwise ordering is not a trustworthy measurement. A disagreement under swap is evidence that the instrument is unstable on that case; the tagged abstention proposal is in §4.4.

## 1.3 Length, style, and superficial quality cues confound judgment

Length-controlled AlpacaEval documented that a judge favored longer answers even when standard automatic-evaluation correlation appeared high. A generalized linear adjustment for length increased reported Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena from 0.94 to 0.98 and improved robustness to verbosity manipulation ([Dubois et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475); [AlpacaEval implementation](https://github.com/tatsu-lab/alpaca_eval)). The important lesson is not that a GLM universally solves verbosity bias; it is that aggregate correlation can conceal a manipulable mediator.

LLMBar manually curated 419 pairs in which one answer follows the instruction and the other is distractingly attractive (for example, engaging or superficially polished). Evaluator performance varied substantially and even the best tested evaluators retained room for improvement ([Zeng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07641); [LLMBar repository](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LLMBar)). This is an independent signal that “sounds better” can beat “obeys the request.”

For this owner, the result has direct relevance: the harness's live defect D1 is precisely a use-versus-mention failure in deterministic prose scanners—quoted words are treated as actions and negation is ignored ([local known defects, lines 9–107](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md)). **likely (55-80%)** that an LLM judge reduces errors on that exact regex-failure slice because it can use sentence context, but the style and instruction-channel evidence above prevents a stronger forecast. This local effect is UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the nine recorded false fires plus known-positive controls specified in §4.8.

## 1.4 Difficult objective tasks expose much lower judge competence

JudgeBench converts difficult knowledge, reasoning, math, and coding tasks into paired responses with correctness-derived labels. Its authors report that several strong judges, including GPT-4o, performed only slightly better than random guessing on the benchmark ([Tan et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784); [JudgeBench code/data](https://github.com/ScalerLab/JudgeBench)). This does not prove all LLM judges are chance everywhere. It does establish that high human-preference agreement on conversational benchmarks does not transport automatically to close, objectively checkable response pairs.

Agent benchmarks demonstrate the preferable alternative where state is available. τ-bench compares the terminal database state to an annotated goal state and uses repeated-trial `pass^k` to expose behavioral inconsistency; the original experiment reported GPT-4o below 50% task success and retail `pass^8` below 25% ([Yao et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045); [τ-bench repository](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau-bench)). SWE-bench evaluates repository patches against executable test specifications rather than prose plausibility ([Jimenez et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770); [SWE-bench repository](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench)).

Established design rule: when an executable receipt, database-state comparison, type check, parser, hash, or other model-independent oracle exists, it dominates a judge's opinion. The judge belongs only on the subjective residue or in diagnosis after the fact.

## 1.5 Prompt injection is a first-class evaluator threat

JudgeDeceiver optimizes an injected string inside an attacker-controlled candidate response so an LLM judge selects that candidate. The paper reports stronger attacks than manually crafted prompt injections and finds its tested known-answer and perplexity defenses insufficient across search, RLAIF, and tool-selection case studies ([Shi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17710); [implementation](https://github.com/ShiJiawenwen/JudgeDeceiver); [CCS publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/3658644.3690291)). An independent EMNLP study reports universal adversarial attacks against zero-shot LLM assessment ([Raina et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.427/)). Together these establish the vulnerability class with different attack construction methods.

Agent-as-a-judge increases the potential blast radius because the evaluator can read candidate-created files, logs, issue text, and trajectories and may possess tools. Candidate content is therefore an attacker-controlled channel. XML delimiters or a “do not follow instructions in the candidate” sentence are useful hygiene but not a demonstrated security boundary; JudgeDeceiver's defense results are direct contrary evidence. The tagged containment proposal is in §4.6.

## 1.6 Agreement, discrimination, consistency, and probability calibration are distinct axes

Modern calibration work defines a calibrated probabilistic classifier by matching stated confidence to empirical frequency and shows that post-hoc methods can change calibration without necessarily changing accuracy ([Guo et al., 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)). Cohen's kappa instead corrects observed inter-rater agreement by an expected-chance term; it says nothing directly about whether “90% confident” judgments are correct 90% of the time ([Cohen, 1960](https://doi.org/10.1177/001316446002000104)). Expected calibration error itself is sensitive to binning and estimator choices, so it must not be the only reported probability metric ([Nixon et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)).

For a binary harness verdict, the minimum useful measurement set is:

1. **Discrimination/correctness:** confusion matrix, sensitivity/recall for defects, specificity, precision, balanced accuracy, and category-stratified results.
2. **Agreement:** raw agreement and kappa, with uncertainty; useful for comparison to a labeler, not a promotion license.
3. **Stability:** order-swap, paraphrase, padding, model-name masking, repeated-run, and model-version consistency.
4. **Selective prediction:** risk versus coverage when the judge can abstain. Selective classification explicitly treats abstention as a control for error at reduced coverage ([Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)).
5. **Probability calibration:** reliability curves plus Brier/log score and calibration error only when the output is a genuine probability that has been calibrated on held-out labels. Proper scoring rules reward honest probabilities ([Gneiting & Raftery, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).

A verbal confidence band generated inside the same prompt is not automatically a probability. It can be evaluated as an ordinal signal, but it must earn probabilistic interpretation prospectively.

## 1.7 Agent-as-a-Judge establishes richer evidence access on one testbed

The Agent-as-a-Judge paper's concrete contribution is to let a judging agent ask questions, construct a requirement graph, read/locate/search/retrieve evidence, and inspect workspace/trajectory state. On 55 DevAI tasks and 365 requirements, its reported alignment rates were generally higher than a plain LLM judge. The paper's ablation table increases alignment from 65.03% with the initial “ask” capability to 90.44% after graph/read/locate, while additional search/retrieval/planning/memory did not monotonically improve it ([paper tables 3, 5–7](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)). That non-monotonicity is important evidence against “more agent machinery is always better.”

The paper reports three individual human evaluator disagreement rates of about 10–30%, majority-vote human alignment around 94–95% on the three agents, agent-judge alignment roughly 89–94% depending on configuration/agent, and a cost of $30.58/118.43 minutes versus a modeled $1,297.50/86.5 hours for human evaluation ([paper §§3–4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)). These figures establish a promising efficiency/measurement result within DevAI, not human-equivalent correctness in general.

The current harness has already adopted the safest interpretation: advisory-only, after deterministic checks, evidence citations per item, execute-to-confirm for would-be blocking findings, and no authority to grant completion ([local judge lines 15–82](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)). Those constraints are evidence-aligned; the tagged preservation proposal is in §4.1.

# 2. Promising but unproven

Every claim in this section is forward-looking and carries the required confidence band plus evidence.

## 2.1 Tool-grounded judges can outperform transcript-only judges on complex agent work

**very likely (80-90%)** for this harness's subjective review workload, because the DevAI ablation improves sharply when the judge can graph requirements and locate/read actual artifacts, while executable-agent benchmarks show that final state contains evidence the dialogue alone does not ([Agent-as-a-Judge ablations](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934); [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)). The remaining uncertainty is cross-domain transport and whether this harness's artifact types resemble DevAI closely enough.

UNVERIFIED for this owner's workload: whether a read-only, artifact-grounded judge beats the existing transcript-centered review on prospective owner labels. The settling experiment is a preregistered shadow A/B over at least 100 new, category-stratified completions: blind human labels, identical cases, transcript-only judge versus receipt/artifact-grounded judge, with paired bootstrap intervals for defect recall and false-alert rate.

## 2.2 Invariance probes can serve as a cheap uncertainty detector

**very likely (80-90%)** that order-swap and irrelevant-padding disagreement will identify some unsafe verdicts, because position and verbosity failures have been reproduced in multiple studies and balanced ordering reduced observed position bias ([FairEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926); [MT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); [length-controlled AlpacaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475)).

**likely (55-80%)** that abstaining on any metamorphic inconsistency materially improves precision on this harness, because abstention removes unstable cases but its coverage cost depends on local case mix. UNVERIFIED locally; settle it by replaying a frozen labeled set under A/B, B/A, identifier masking, semantically irrelevant padding, and a candidate-embedded instruction canary, then plot error risk against retained coverage.

## 2.3 Diverse panels can reduce family-specific bias, but are not a default architecture

**likely (55-80%)** that a small cross-family panel improves robustness on contested, high-cost subjective cases. PoLL reports that panels of smaller, diverse models outperformed one large judge across six datasets and three settings, reduced intra-model bias, and cost over seven times less in that experiment ([Verga et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)). The evidence is multi-dataset but remains one study and does not cover tool-using agent trajectories or coordinated prompt injection.

**unlikely (20-45%)** that always-on panels are worth their complexity for this owner. The corpus already shows expensive fan-out and StructuredOutput failure, correlated models can share errors, and deterministic receipts cover most load-bearing facts. The justified use is escalation only: a contested, irreversible, subjective residual where an independent family adds information.

## 2.4 Rubric decomposition improves judge performance but can overfit the rubric

**very likely (80-90%)** that splitting compound acceptance criteria into falsifiable items improves traceability and reduces some holistic “vibe” errors; G-Eval and Agent-as-a-Judge both gain structure from explicit criteria, and the current harness already does this ([G-Eval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634); [Agent-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934); [local judge lines 43–67](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)).

**likely (55-80%)** that model-generated rubrics silently omit owner intent unless checked against explicit acceptance criteria, because a judge can optimize internal consistency while missing an unlisted requirement. UNVERIFIED locally; settle it by having humans label both omitted-requirement rate and item verdicts on a held-out sample, with the rubric generated before the judge sees candidate output.

## 2.5 Process inspection helps diagnosis more than it helps terminal scoring

**likely (55-80%)** that trajectory inspection improves error localization, because it exposes failed tool calls and intermediate state that final text hides, and the DevAI agent judge used trajectory/workspace access successfully ([Agent-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934)).

**roughly even (45-55%)** that trajectory access improves final correctness after all executable end-state receipts are already available. A plausible benefit is locating an untested requirement; a plausible harm is being persuaded by a fluent rationale or stale intermediate state. UNVERIFIED; settle it with a three-arm trial—final artifacts/receipts only, transcript only, and both—on independently labeled harness cases.

## 2.6 Prospective local calibration can make a judge useful as a bounded alert

**very likely (80-90%)** that prospective local evaluation can identify categories where the judge is useful versus unsafe, because the large cross-task study found strong task/property dependence and selective prediction provides a principled abstention trade-off ([Bavaresco et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18403); [Geifman & El-Yaniv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)).

**unlikely (20-45%)** that one global kappa threshold is sufficient for promotion. Kappa is agreement rather than calibration, category base rates differ, and the local label stream explicitly over-samples caught errors while missing escaped defects ([Cohen](https://doi.org/10.1177/001316446002000104); [local judge lines 101–121](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)).

# 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

## 3.1 “Human-level evaluator”

**The tell:** a paper reports agreement with a small or noisy human baseline on one benchmark, then changes the noun from “agreement” to “reliability” or “human-equivalent evaluation.” In DevAI, individual humans disagreed roughly 10–30%, the dataset had only 55 tasks, and the headline comparison is alignment with those labels—not an external correctness oracle ([Agent-as-a-Judge §3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)). MT-Bench's >80% agreement is real but distribution-specific and accompanied by documented biases ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)).

Counter-test (**CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**): disclose sample size, label protocol, adjudication, prevalence, confidence intervals, category results, and a held-out external oracle. If those are missing, “human-level” is marketing compression. **very likely (80-90%)** that this disclosure distinguishes a benchmark-agreement claim from general evaluator validity, because each field corresponds to a transport or uncertainty limitation observed in the cited studies.

## 3.2 “Calibrated judge” meaning “we improved the prompt”

**The tell:** the method reports higher correlation, kappa, or agreement but no reliability curve, proper scoring rule, confidence bins, or held-out probability mapping. FairEval's “calibration” balances evidence/order; AutoCalibrate refines scoring criteria toward human labels. Both can be useful, but neither term alone proves probabilistic calibration ([FairEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926); [AutoCalibrate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13308); [formal calibration definition](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)).

Counter-test (**BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**): ask, “When this judge says 0.8, are approximately 80% correct on untouched future cases?” If the system never emits a probability or the paper never tests that frequency, it is not confidence-calibrated. This is a direct application of the formal calibration definition, not a forward performance claim.

## 3.3 “Chain-of-thought makes the verdict explainable”

**The tell:** the generated rationale is treated as a causal trace or evidence rather than another model output. G-Eval shows a structured reasoning prompt can improve correlation, but that does not validate rationale faithfulness ([G-Eval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634)). A convincing paragraph can be post-hoc and can amplify style bias.

Counter-test (**NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**): use Claude Code's existing read/execute capabilities to require citations to externally inspectable artifacts and executable receipts. Delete the rationale and ask whether another process can reproduce the verdict from those citations. If not, the explanation is prose, not evidence. This rides the existing tool bus.

## 3.4 “More judge agents/modules always improve evaluation”

**The tell:** architecture diagrams grow while ablations are absent or non-monotonic. Agent-as-a-Judge's own ablations show a large early gain from graph/read/locate, but adding search/retrieval/planning/memory is not monotonically beneficial ([tables 3, 5–7](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)).

Counter-test (**CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**): require marginal lift, latency, cost, and failure rate per module on held-out cases. A module with no independent lift is scaffolding, not capability. **very likely (80-90%)** that this prevents adoption of at least some non-contributing modules because the cited ablations are already non-monotonic.

## 3.5 “A panel eliminates bias”

**The tell:** majority vote is treated as independence. Models trained on overlapping web corpora, post-training recipes, or vendor families can share blind spots. PoLL supports diverse panels as promising and cheaper in its settings, but does not establish immunity to shared attacks ([Verga et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)).

Counter-test (**BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**): report error correlation by model family and adversarial condition, not just panel accuracy. If all judges ingest the same injected candidate and prompt, three votes can be one failure repeated. **likely (55-80%)** that error-correlation reporting exposes some false independence, based on shared-condition failures; local effect size is UNVERIFIED and is settled by the prospective panel replay in §4.10.

## 3.6 “Self-improving judge-agent flywheel”

**The tell:** evaluator feedback trains or steers the evaluated agent, the improved agent is then measured by the same evaluator, and no frozen external oracle interrupts the loop. The Agent-as-a-Judge paper explicitly labels the flywheel an outlook and says it is hypothesized ([paper §6](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)). JudgeDeceiver shows why evaluator optimization pressure invites reward hacking ([Shi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17710)).

**very likely (80-90%)** that an unanchored self-judge loop will eventually optimize judge preferences rather than owner utility, because the evaluator becomes the reward surface and known attacks can manipulate that surface. This is a forward-looking inference from Goodhart-style optimization pressure plus demonstrated judge injection, not a direct long-duration harness experiment.

Counter-test (**CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**): freeze a held-out, human/adjudicated and executable oracle before optimization; never let judge-generated feedback alter that oracle; measure regression and exploit discovery out of sample. This is an experimental-separation rule on the existing evaluation bus, not a new runtime.

## 3.7 Decimal scores without decision-theoretic meaning

**The tell:** an answer gets 7.3/10 with no rubric anchors, repeated-run distribution, prevalence, or consequence model. LLM score scales can create precision without calibration.

Counter-test (**CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**): use anchored pass/fail/abstain per atomic criterion, plus evidence. If a numeric score does not change a prespecified decision and has no empirical interpretation, remove it. **likely (55-80%)** to reduce false precision because it removes an uninterpreted scale; the local readability effect is UNVERIFIED and is settled by the paired-report experiment in §4.11.

# 4. Specific things this harness should adopt

## Architecture decision — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**: evolve the existing bus; do not replace it with a judge runtime

The minimal target is:

```text
explicit DoD
    -> deterministic receipts/state checks (blocking authority)
    -> unresolved subjective items only
    -> one read-only shadow judge (advisory; pass/fail/abstain + evidence)
    -> cheap metamorphic rechecks on would-fire items
    -> bounded JSONL + prospective human labels
    -> offline calibration report by category/version
```

No second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is proposed. Every adopted item rides the existing agent/hook/skill/JSONL bus. This passes the anti-overfit gate and preserves the lesson that useful agent loops can be small.

## 4.1 Preserve “deterministic first, judge only the residue” — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Keep the current invariants that the judge runs after deterministic verification, never grants done, and cannot turn bare opinion into a block ([local judge lines 15–41](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)). Make dispatch mechanical: the parent passes only DoD items not already resolved by a typed receipt. Do not ask the judge to re-score test exit codes, hashes, parser results, database state, or exact file existence.

**Anti-overfit gate:** pass. It removes work and uses existing dispatch/config; no new runtime.  
**Expected value:** **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce judge exposure, cost, and false authority because model-independent state is the stronger oracle on exactly those items ([τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045); [SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770)).

## 4.2 Replace “kappa licenses promotion” with a calibration card — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Retain kappa, but only as one descriptive field. For every judge prompt/model version and DoD category, compute:

- case count and positive/negative prevalence;
- TP/FP/TN/FN and defect recall, specificity, precision, balanced accuracy;
- raw agreement and kappa with bootstrap intervals;
- abstention rate and risk at retained coverage;
- order/padding/name-mask/repeat instability;
- if probabilities are emitted: Brier score, log loss, and a reliability table; and
- latency/cost and schema/tool failure rate.

Promotion policy: **no opinion-based blocking in this wave**. A future owner-authored policy could consider only a receipt-mediated alert after prospective evidence; no global kappa threshold can self-promote the judge. The existing “never self-promote” rule remains ([local judge lines 36–39](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)).

**Anti-overfit gate:** pass. A small offline reducer over bounded JSONL, no service or database.  
**Expected value:** **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent false calibration claims, because agreement and probability calibration are formally different and local performance is category-dependent ([Guo et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html); [Bavaresco et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18403)).

## 4.3 Build a prospective, stratified shadow set — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** — **UNVERIFIED premise: label capture availability**

The recommendation inherits UNVERIFIED because I did not determine whether the current `claude-reflect` queue and ledger contain enough independent, prospective final labels with stable joins. The settling experiment is: select 50 known queue entries and 50 random recent completions, resolve each to a stable task/artifact hash using both the queue join and ledger supersession chain, and have the owner blind-label a 20-case pilot; report join rate and label ambiguity before implementation.

If the pilot succeeds, sample both:

- every caught owner correction (enriched defect cases), and
- a random stream of apparently successful completions (needed to find escaped defects and estimate base rates).

Freeze task text, artifact hashes, deterministic receipts, judge configuration, and the label before updating prompts. Split chronologically into development and untouched prospective evaluation. **Anti-overfit gate:** pass if implemented as bounded JSONL sampling plus an offline script. Reject any proposal to add a vector store, labeling service, or durable workflow engine.

## 4.4 Add four metamorphic tripwires before a would-fire alert — **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

**Expected value:** **very likely (80-90%)** to detect some known failures, based on independently demonstrated position, verbosity, identity/self-preference, and injection effects ([FairEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926); [AlpacaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475); [MT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); [JudgeDeceiver](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17710)). ## 4.5 Use an anchored three-way verdict and tiny tolerant record — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Per atomic criterion, require exactly `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `ABSTAIN`, plus:

- `criterion_id`
- `verdict`
- `evidence_ref[]` (artifact/receipt references, not prose as proof)
- `failure_class[]` from a short extensible list
- `judge_version`
- `metamorphic_flags[]`
- optional short rationale

Do **not** require a nine-section nested schema on every internal call. Unknown fields are ignored; missing optional fields do not fail the parent. Validate the small terminal record once and keep raw judge text in the shadow sink when parsing fails. This directly respects the measured 22.9% StructuredOutput error and over-strict-schema failure mode supplied in the pinned ground truth.

Do not solicit a percentage confidence in the online verdict until prospective data shows it has monotonic predictive value. Use perturbation stability and abstention first.

**Anti-overfit gate:** pass. Config/schema simplification.  
**Expected value:** **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce schema-induced evaluator failures because the recommendation removes required structure from the measured failure surface; probability withholding avoids false precision ([calibration definition](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)).

## 4.6 Treat candidate artifacts and trajectories as hostile evidence — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

The local judge already has read-only-on-source and unbacked/advisory rules ([lines 40–41, 54–82, 165–174](file:///local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md)). Preserve them. **Anti-overfit gate:** pass as prompt/config hardening. It is defense in depth, not claimed containment.  
**Expected value:** **likely (55-80%)** to stop naive injections and accidental command execution, but **almost certainly not (1-5%)** to provide a complete prompt-injection boundary by itself, because optimized attacks bypassed several content-level defenses ([JudgeDeceiver](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17710)). ## 4.11 Remove decorative analytic scoring from the terminal verdict — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

**Anti-overfit gate:** pass. It simplifies output.  
**Expected value:** **likely (55-80%)** to reduce schema failures and false precision, based on the measured StructuredOutput failure and the distinction between rationale/self-report and external calibration. ## 4.12 Do not build a trained critic, judge daemon, memory graph, or self-play flywheel — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

This is an explicit non-adoption decision. The available evidence does not justify the previously rejected components. Richer tool access gives the proven agent-judge benefit; a new runtime does not. The local corpus already has substantial orchestration/tool reliability defects, and Agent-as-a-Judge's ablation is non-monotonic ([paper ablations](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10934)).

**Anti-overfit gate:** pass by exclusion.  
**Forward judgment:** **very likely (80-90%)** that adding a second judge runtime now would cost more reliability than it adds, because the unvalidated judge would become another tool/schema/orchestration surface while deterministic receipts and a small shadow adapter cover the supported use case. Replacement should remain on the table only if a radically simpler deterministic-receipt harness replaces—not duplicates—the current orchestration.

## Recommended acceptance gate for any future authority — **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

This is a research recommendation, not a claim that the current data meets it.

1. Keep the judge advisory for at least one untouched prospective window.
2. Include random apparently-green completions, not only caught corrections.
3. Predeclare the target category, consequence, positive class, and maximum tolerated false-negative/false-positive rate.
4. Require known-positive and known-negative detector controls.
5. Require stable performance under order, padding, identity mask, rerun, and injection-canary conditions.
6. Report confidence intervals; do not promote on a point estimate or “≥30 cases” alone.
7. If a probability is used, fit calibration only on development data and report Brier/log loss plus reliability on untouched data.
8. Permit abstention and report risk/coverage.
9. Re-evaluate after every model, prompt, rubric, tool, or evidence-selection change.
10. Even after success, permit only receipt-mediated escalation; never let judge opinion grant completion.

**likely (55-80%)** that 100 cases are enough to reveal gross instability but **unlikely (20-45%)** that 100 total cases are enough to license low-frequency, high-consequence blocking by category. Evidence: error bounds widen rapidly after stratification and rare positives; the exact required sample depends on prevalence and the prespecified risk tolerance. UNVERIFIED sample size: settle it with a power/precision calculation after the 20-case pilot establishes observed prevalence and disagreement—do not use a universal 30–50 threshold.

## Bottom line for the next design unit

Keep the judge small, read-only, late, selective, and advisory. Give it evidence-location tools, not authority. Measure it as a versioned instrument on prospective local labels. Treat order/padding/injection disagreement as abstention evidence. Replace a single kappa gate with category-stratified error, stability, coverage, and—only where genuine probabilities exist—calibration metrics. Continue to let executable receipts and terminal state carry the blocking weight.

The radical option supported by evidence is not a larger judge society. It is a **smaller harness in which deterministic receipts resolve facts and one bounded judge touches only irreducibly subjective residue**. That is evolution of the current sound invariants and partial replacement of its decorative scoring/calibration machinery. It rides the existing bus, avoids every rejected heavyweight component, and remains falsifiable before authority is granted.
