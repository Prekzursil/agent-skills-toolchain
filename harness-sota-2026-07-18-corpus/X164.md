# X164 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → finite-horizon context control

## Scope and method

This is an ideation-lens unit. Per the task, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness. I used public military and government sources, prioritizing doctrine or service-hosted material. The proposal below is therefore a design hypothesis, not a finding that the subject harness has or lacks any named feature.

I tested four candidate borrows—commander's intent, Commander's Critical Information Requirements (CCIRs), OODA, After-Action Review (AAR), and red-team practices—against one target question: **under a finite token budget, what information must remain immediately available, what can be reduced to a pointer, and what can be evicted without corrupting action over a long horizon?**

## Executive verdict

The military analogy **partly transfers**, and the transferable unit is not “run an OODA loop” or “appoint a red team.” It is the paired doctrine of:

1. **commander's intent**: a short, durable statement of purpose, key tasks, and desired end state that enables action when the plan stops matching events; and
2. **CCIRs**: a deliberately small, situation-dependent, time-sensitive set of unknowns tied to decisions, continuously revised as the operation changes.

Army doctrine makes the pairing unusually explicit. Commander’s intent is supposed to let subordinates act without further orders when events diverge from the plan, and is typically three to five sentences containing purpose, key tasks, and end state ([FM 6-0, paras. 9-73–9-74, pp. 111–112](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). CCIRs are information requirements critical to timely decisions; they are mission-specific, situation-dependent, time-sensitive, continuously reviewed, and typically ten or fewer so limited collection resources remain prioritized ([FM 6-0, paras. 9-46–9-50, p. 107](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). The same manual says the goal of information systems is not to process vast quantities, but to produce accurate situational understanding quickly ([FM 6-0, paras. 3-39–3-42, pp. 69–70](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). This is substantially the same resource-allocation shape as finite-context selection.

My design recommendation is an **Intent-and-Decision Context Control (IDCC)** layer: keep a tiny intent card pinned; maintain at most ten live decision-linked information requirements; preserve evidence that answers, threatens, or invalidates those requirements; treat surprising decision-changing information as an exception; and demote everything else by horizon and latest time of value. At compaction, run a very small assumption/premortem challenge before eviction. Do not build a second orchestrator, memory service, vector database, or critic.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this decision-linked policy will preserve long-horizon task correctness better than pure recency or undifferentiated summarization, because doctrine independently links CCIRs to scarce collection/analysis resources and current/future decision points, while intent protects purpose under plan failure ([Joint Staff CCIR Insights, pp. 3–9](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/fp/ccir_fp4th_ed.pdf); [FM 6-0, paras. 9-47–9-50 and 9-73–9-74](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the corpus-replay and ablation experiment specified below**.

## What the source field has actually solved

### 1. Commander’s intent: retain the invariant, not the whole plan

#### In its own field

Mission command exists because a remote commander cannot specify every action under uncertainty. Army doctrine defines intent as a clear, concise expression of purpose and desired end state that helps subordinate commanders act without further orders when events do not unfold as planned. The doctrinal form adds key tasks, and the statement should be memorable two echelons down; shorter is better, normally three to five sentences ([FM 6-0, paras. 9-73–9-74](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). Army professional writing describes the associated operating model as empowering others to choose ways and means within parameters rather than centralizing every decision ([Army, “Mission command requires sharp commander's intent”](https://www.army.mil/article-amp/215297/mission_command_requires_sharp_commanders_intent)).

The mechanism is lossy by design. It does not preserve every order. It preserves the minimum semantic invariant needed to regenerate locally appropriate action:

- **purpose** — why this operation matters;
- **key tasks** — what must be achieved or preserved regardless of route; and
- **end state** — the conditions that count as success.

This solves a coordination problem under degraded communications and changed conditions, not an archival problem.

#### Translation to context

The direct translation is a pinned **Intent Card**, regenerated only on a genuine mission change:

```yaml
intent:
  purpose: why the owner wants this outcome
  end_state: observable conditions that mean done
  key_tasks: [non-substitutable outcomes]
  constraints: [must, must-not, scope, authority]
  risk_posture: what uncertainty may be resolved autonomously
  provenance: exact owner messages or governing files
  supersedes: prior intent-card version, if any
```

The card is not a transcript summary. It excludes implementation chronology, exploratory dead ends, and current tactics unless one is a true constraint. It is pinned because a stale implementation detail is replaceable; a lost “why,” prohibited action, or success condition is not.

**Proposal — Intent Card — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add a rule and structured state field requiring the agent to restate purpose, end state, key tasks, and constraints at task start and after a material owner correction. Context compaction must copy this field verbatim or produce an explicit supersession record.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this catches the class of compaction failure in which an agent remembers the current tactic but loses the owner’s reason, negative constraint, or definition of done. Evidence: military intent is expressly designed to preserve coordinated action when the original plan no longer fits, and doctrine prioritizes brevity and memorability ([FM 6-0, paras. 9-73–9-74](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). The agent-context effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting plan changes and negative constraints before compaction, then measuring post-compaction constraint violations against a baseline**.

### 2. CCIR: allocate attention by pending decision, not by document importance

#### In its own field

CCIRs are not “important facts.” They are unknown, needed information elements that are critical to timely decisions. Doctrine divides them into:

- **Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIRs):** gaps about an adversary or operating environment, normally tied to opportunities, threats, and decision points; and
- **Friendly Force Information Requirements (FFIRs):** gaps about the status and capability of one’s own force.

The National Defense University account states that both categories represent knowledge gaps and require continuous evaluation ([NDU Press, “Commander’s Critical Information Requirements”](https://ndupress.ndu.edu/Media/News/News-Article-View/Article/3843920/commanders-critical-information-requirements-crucial-for-decisionmaking-and-joi/)). FM 6-0 makes four properties explicit: a CCIR is specified for the operation, applicable to the commander specifying it, tied directly to a present or future mission, and time-sensitive. It also gives the strong practical limit: typically ten or fewer, because minimizing their number helps allocate limited resources ([FM 6-0, paras. 9-47–9-50](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)).

The Joint Staff’s CCIR study adds three important refinements:

1. CCIRs focus collection, analysis, and information flow, not just briefing content.
2. They span current operations, future branches, and later sequels rather than starving the future to feed the present.
3. A purely decision-point-centric implementation can be too narrow at operational level; broader assessment questions may be necessary for long-horizon planning ([Joint Staff, *Commander’s Critical Information Requirements*, pp. 3–9](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/fp/ccir_fp4th_ed.pdf)).

This doctrine has a striking “exception” concept as well. FM 6-0 directs staff to immediately surface information that answers a CCIR **or is exceptional information**—that is, information important enough that it would have answered a critical requirement had the requirement been foreseen ([FM 6-0, command-post information duties at p. 25 and CCIR treatment at pp. 68–70](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). That is a built-in defense against overfitting the filter to yesterday’s questions.

#### Translation to context

Replace generic “importance” ranking with a live **Decision Requirements Register**, capped at ten entries:

```yaml
decision_requirements:
  - id: DR-3
    question: "Does the existing interface permit interception before compaction?"
    decision_supported: "CONFIG-only vs BUILD-SMALL implementation"
    horizon: current | next | later
    class: external_state | own_state
    trigger_or_answer: "documented callback or observed event payload"
    latest_time_of_value: "before selecting implementation"
    status: open | answered | invalidated | expired
    evidence: [source pointers]
    disconfirmers: [facts that would overturn the working answer]
```

The military PIR/FFIR split should be renamed, not copied cosmetically:

- **external-state requirement:** what must be learned about the repository, environment, API, user domain, or other systems before a decision;
- **own-state requirement:** what must be known about current edits, tests, permissions, promises, resource budget, and failures.

Every candidate context item is classified against the register:

1. **Pin** if it defines intent, authority, a live owner correction, or a still-binding constraint.
2. **Keep hot** if it answers, partially answers, contradicts, or changes confidence in a live decision requirement.
3. **Keep as a short sentinel** if it is a key assumption or disconfirming indicator.
4. **Escalate as exception** if it changes a consequential decision even though no requirement predicted it.
5. **Demote to evidence pointer** if its exact detail may be retrieved and its conclusion plus provenance are sufficient now.
6. **Evict** if it is duplicated, superseded, expired past its latest time of value, or tied only to a completed decision with a preserved result and provenance.

Do not equate “answered” with “delete.” Preserve the decision, answer, confidence, and source pointer; discard bulky supporting detail only when retrievable. Owner corrections and exact legal/technical constraints remain pinned even after the immediate decision because they govern later decisions.

**Proposal — Decision Requirements Register and exception lane — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement a compact structured register plus a compaction-time selector. It must not retrieve or index a global memory store. It only labels state already present, retains compact decision records, and emits provenance pointers to ordinary files/tool results. A strict implementation budget is approximately 120–180 lines plus tests.

This proposal assumes a current hook or skill can run before context compaction or at an equivalent explicit checkpoint. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an interface spike that logs a known-present sentinel through one forced compaction and proves both event visibility and state reinjection**. If no such bus point exists, use the CONFIG Intent Card plus an agent-invoked checkpoint skill; do not create a daemon. The recommendation remains **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)** because the fallback rides the skill bus.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this catches stale-but-decision-critical information that recency policies evict, especially an old user correction, an unresolved test failure, or evidence needed only at a future branch. Evidence: CCIR doctrine explicitly spans multiple horizons and prioritizes limited collection and analysis resources by decision need ([Joint Staff CCIR Insights, pp. 3–9](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/fp/ccir_fp4th_ed.pdf); [FM 6-0, paras. 9-47–9-50](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). The effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with long-session replay containing delayed decision points and score whether the decisive evidence survives until use**.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the exception lane catches novel facts the fixed requirements did not anticipate without forcing every low-value observation into the hot context. Evidence: Army command-post doctrine separately elevates both CCIR answers and exceptional information, and the 2025 Army case study says refining FFIRs was intended to catch problems “bubbling before they erupted” ([FM 6-0, p. 25](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525); [Army, “Critical Information for Informed Decision Making”](https://www.army.mil/article/286869/critical_information_for_informed_decision_making)). This effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with adversarial replay in which a decisive observation matches no declared requirement and measure escalation precision/recall**.

### 3. OODA: use reorientation, reject the four-box checklist

#### In its own field

Boyd’s full loop is not merely Observe → Orient → Decide → Act. The Air University reproduction shows multiple feedback and feed-forward paths; orientation is shaped by new information, previous experience, cultural traditions, analysis/synthesis, and unfolding circumstances, while decisions are hypotheses and actions are tests ([Air University, corrected OODA diagram from *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf)). Air University scholarship likewise describes orientation as the focal point that shapes observation, decision, and action—not a neutral sorting stage ([Air University, “Finding the Right Model”](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/JIPA/Display/Article/3371164/finding-the-right-model-the-joint-force-the-peoples-liberation-army-and-informa/)).

The transferable mechanism is therefore **reorientation on feedback**: action changes the world; observations must update the mental model; the mental model changes which observations matter next.

#### Translation to context

After any material action—edit, test, tool failure, user correction, new external result—the harness should ask only:

- Which live decision requirement did this answer or invalidate?
- Did this create a new decision?
- Did a key assumption become false?
- Did the intent or constraints change?
- Which information just expired because its decision window closed?

**Proposal — Reorientation trigger — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add those five update questions to the post-action/checkpoint rule. Do not emit prose if nothing changed; update only the register fields.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this reduces accumulation of stale state by making expiration event-driven rather than dependent on token pressure. Evidence: Boyd’s model treats decisions as hypotheses, actions as tests, and feedback as an input to orientation ([Air University corrected OODA diagram](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf)). The harness effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying tool-result reversals and measuring time-to-invalidation and stale-fact reuse**.

OODA alone does **not** solve context eviction. This conclusion has two mechanically independent signals: the primary diagram specifies cognitive feedback structure but no capacity limit, retention rule, or eviction criterion; independently, the CCIR doctrine supplies explicit decision linkage, time sensitivity, continuous review, and a typical numerical cap ([Air University corrected OODA diagram](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf); [FM 6-0, paras. 9-47–9-50](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)). Turning “OODA” into a four-step context checklist would add ceremony without a selection function.

### 4. Red teaming: challenge what the filter is about to forget

#### In its own field

The Army’s Red Team Handbook is explicit that it is not itself official doctrine, though its practices support Joint and Army doctrine ([TRADOC G-2/UFMCS, *Red Team Handbook* v9, preface](https://home.army.mil/hawaii/7517/5978/4767/Red_Team_Handbook.pdf)). Its useful mechanisms here are narrow:

- **Key Assumptions Check:** enumerate stated and unstated premises, challenge whether each must remain true, identify circumstances that undermine it, and ask how the analytic line changes if it fails (pp. 163–165).
- **Premortem Analysis:** mentally assume plan failure, find vulnerabilities, and counter overconfidence, consensus, and groupthink (pp. 173–175).
- **observable signposts:** identify evidence that an unlikely but high-impact path is emerging and monitor those indicators (pp. 158–163, 209–210).

The handbook warns that indicators themselves are subject to bias, faulty analogy, and weak theory, so they must be checked before use (p. 163). That caution is directly relevant: a context filter can become confidently blind to anything outside its schema.

#### Translation to context

At a compaction boundary, not on every turn, run a bounded **eviction premortem**:

> “Assume the next consequential decision is wrong because this compaction discarded something. What discarded fact, correction, unresolved contradiction, or assumption would most plausibly cause that failure?”

The output is limited to three one-line **sentinels**, each with a falsifier or trigger. Sentinels remain hot for one decision horizon, then require explicit renewal. This is not another agent and not a trained critic; it is a short structured prompt in the same checkpoint.

**Proposal — Eviction premortem and assumption sentinels — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add one bounded prompt and three sentinel slots to the compaction rule. Run only when compaction will remove material tied to an open decision or when a decision requirement is being closed.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this catches hidden dependencies and disconfirming evidence omitted by the positive decision-requirement filter, because the source practice explicitly externalizes unstated assumptions and imagines failure before commitment ([Red Team Handbook v9, pp. 163–165 and 173–175](https://home.army.mil/hawaii/7517/5978/4767/Red_Team_Handbook.pdf)). The agent-context effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an ablation that plants a low-salience dependency contradicting the leading plan and measures both recovery and false-positive retention**.

Do not create a permanent “red-team agent.” That would spend context and orchestration capacity to police context, and it would risk becoming the second orchestration runtime rejected by the anti-overfit gate.

### 5. AAR: learn the eviction policy after action, not during every action

#### In its own field

The Army AAR is a standards-focused professional discussion intended to let participants discover what happened, why it happened, and how to sustain strengths and improve weaknesses. The original guide distinguishes that participatory discovery from a one-way critique and uses results to improve later training ([Department of the Army, TC 25-20, *A Leader’s Guide to After-Action Reviews*](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=039d8394b616db33ad146a3d37e4c7d93ba02858&repid=rep1&type=pdf)).

The mechanism is feedback against intended performance after an event, using participant observations rather than hindsight from one authority.

#### Translation to context

Do not retain every session lesson in the live context. Instead, after task completion or a diagnosed context failure, record one small policy-learning tuple:

```yaml
context_aar:
  intended: "which information the selector meant to preserve"
  actual: "what was available when the decision occurred"
  delta: "critical omission or wasteful retention"
  cause: "bad requirement, missed exception, stale horizon, provenance failure"
  one_change: "single selector/rule/test change"
  evidence: [replay pointer]
```

**Proposal — Context AAR tuple — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend the existing session-end or failure-review path with the tuple above and aggregate only counts plus reproducible examples. Promote a rule change only after repeated examples or a high-severity single failure; never append free-form lessons automatically to the permanent prompt.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this improves the selector without turning live context into a lesson archive, because AAR separates execution from standards-based learning and centers the gap between intended and actual performance ([TC 25-20](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=039d8394b616db33ad146a3d37e4c7d93ba02858&repid=rep1&type=pdf)). The software effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by running at least 30 replayed sessions, applying only changes supported by repeated AAR causes, and testing on a held-out session set for regression**.

## Proposed IDCC mechanism end to end

### State kept in hot context

1. **Intent Card** — one compact block; pinned.
2. **Decision Requirements Register** — at most ten open requirements, balanced across current, next, and later horizons.
3. **Decision ledger** — completed consequential decisions as one-line result + rationale + confidence + provenance, not full deliberation.
4. **Exception inbox** — surprising information awaiting classification; bounded to three entries and one checkpoint.
5. **Assumption sentinels** — at most three; each has a falsifier and expiry horizon.
6. **Working state** — current edits/actions, validation state, unresolved errors, and explicit promises to the owner.

### Compaction algorithm

```text
INPUT: candidate context items, Intent Card, open decision requirements,
       current/next/later horizons, evidence retrievability

1. Copy the current Intent Card and binding owner constraints verbatim.
2. Revalidate each open decision requirement:
   close answered/expired ones; preserve decision result and provenance.
3. Match each candidate item to:
   a. intent/authority/constraint,
   b. live requirement,
   c. decision ledger,
   d. assumption/disconfirmer,
   e. exception, or
   f. none.
4. Preserve exact text only when exactness is consequential or retrieval is unreliable.
5. Otherwise retain a claim + confidence + provenance pointer.
6. Evict duplicates, superseded tactics, raw successful tool output, and detail tied only
   to completed decisions.
7. Run the bounded eviction premortem; add at most three expiring sentinels.
8. Verify horizon coverage: at least one slot for each nonempty current/next/later horizon.
9. Emit token counts per class and a reason code for every retained item.
```

The selector must use deterministic precedence for pinned material and requirement matches. An LLM may propose requirements or summarize evidence, but it must not be the only enforcement layer for caps, expiries, provenance, or verbatim constraints.

**Proposal — Deterministic precedence and reason codes — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement class caps, expiry, exact-copy fields, and retention reason codes as ordinary validation around the structured state. This can be part of the same 120–180-line selector, not a separate component.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that reason codes make over-retention and accidental eviction more diagnosable than an opaque summary prompt, because every item becomes traceable to intent, a decision requirement, a sentinel, an exception, or no qualifying reason. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by having two independent reviewers diagnose seeded retention errors with and without reason codes and compare correct-cause identification and time**.

## Adversarial examination of the analogy

### Structural correspondences that do transfer

1. **Finite attention and collection capacity.** A commander and staff cannot collect or analyze everything; a model cannot attend to unlimited history. CCIR doctrine explicitly exists to prioritize limited collection and analysis resources ([FM 6-0, paras. 3-39–3-42 and 9-47–9-50](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)).
2. **Action-linked value.** Information matters because it changes a decision before a deadline, not because it is globally interesting. That is the CCIR definition and maps directly to token allocation.
3. **Changing relevance.** Requirements change as operations progress; context value also changes after edits, tests, discoveries, and owner corrections. Doctrine requires continuous CCIR review ([FM 6-0, para. 9-48](https://home.army.mil/wood/download_file/view/6638/1525)).
4. **Multiple horizons.** Immediate execution can consume all attention unless future branch and sequel questions reserve capacity. Joint Staff observations explicitly span all three horizons ([Joint Staff CCIR Insights, pp. 3–9](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/fp/ccir_fp4th_ed.pdf)).
5. **Plans fail while purpose persists.** The intent mechanism was designed for changed conditions and degraded direction, closely matching compaction or handoff where detail disappears but autonomous action must continue.

### Disanalogies and failure modes

1. **Humans have rich latent memory; an LLM context boundary is harder.** A subordinate may internalize doctrine, culture, and experience outside the written order. A stateless model cannot reliably recover evicted content without an external source. Therefore “intent only” is insufficient; provenance pointers and exact preservation of binding constraints are mandatory.
2. **Military hierarchy supplies an accountable commander.** In an agent harness, intent may be inferred from ambiguous dialogue, and the model can silently misstate it. The owner’s exact language must outrank synthesized intent, and material reinterpretation requires confirmation rather than doctrinal confidence.
3. **Military information can be recollected by staff and sensors.** Some tool output or transient UI state may be irretrievable. Eviction must consider **retrievability**, not merely current decision value. Doctrine’s collection analogy underprices one-shot evidence.
4. **The military setting is adversarial; much agent work is not.** Calling repositories “enemy” or every surprise “threat” would bias the model toward conflict and urgency. Rename categories to external state and own state; keep adversarial framing only for security or genuinely competitive work.
5. **CCIRs can create tunnel vision.** The Joint Staff study itself says narrow, predictable decision-point CCIRs can under-support assessment and future planning ([Joint Staff CCIR Insights, pp. 6–9](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/fp/ccir_fp4th_ed.pdf)). The exception lane, horizon balance, and periodic revalidation are not optional decorations; they are safeguards against the doctrine’s own failure mode.
6. **Latest time of value can be misestimated.** A fact apparently tied to a closed decision may constrain a later one. Preserve authoritative constraints and a compact decision ledger beyond local expiry; expire bulky evidence, not the existence of the decision.
7. **Premortems can preserve too much.** A language model can invent plausible failure stories for nearly any discarded item. The three-sentinel cap, falsifier, expiry, and measured false-positive rate are necessary.
8. **Military doctrine optimizes organizations, not token sequences.** The analogy specifies priority semantics but not the best compression codec, token allocation, or retrieval threshold. Those require empirical harness experiments.

The net assessment is **very likely (80-90%)** that the structural correspondence is strong enough to justify a small experiment, because limited attention, decision-linked value, changing relevance, and multiple horizons exist in both domains and are explicit in the cited doctrine. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that importing military terminology or the full staff process would improve the harness; the hierarchy, adversarial framing, and human latent-memory assumptions do not transfer. Both judgments are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with the named replay experiment plus qualitative review for needless ceremony and threat-biased behavior**.

## Anti-overfit gate

The proposal passes the stated gate:

- It rides the existing settings, hook, skill, and ordinary evidence-pointer bus.
- It adds one small structured state object, one bounded selection/checkpoint routine, and configuration rules.
- It does **not** introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.
- It keeps deterministic machinery to caps, expiry, reason codes, and verbatim fields; it does not attempt to out-model the model with an elaborate planner.
- It can be deleted cleanly if replay does not show benefit.

The aggregate code estimate is **likely (55-80%)** to remain under roughly 200 lines excluding tests if the existing bus exposes structured checkpoint state. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the interface spike before implementation**. If it requires a daemon or new runtime, stop: that version fails the anti-overfit gate and should not be built.

## Verification plan and falsification criteria

### Experiment 1 — interface spike

Purpose: establish whether BUILD-SMALL is technically honest.

1. Insert a known-present sentinel into a controlled long session.
2. Force the existing compaction/checkpoint path.
3. Signal A: event log proves the selector saw the pre-compaction structured state.
4. Signal B: post-compaction model output reproduces the sentinel and provenance from reinjected state.
5. Repeat with a deliberately absent sentinel to verify the detector does not hallucinate presence.

Falsifier: either signal fails, or the only solution is a daemon/new orchestration runtime. Fall back to CONFIG plus explicit checkpoint skill.

### Experiment 2 — corpus replay, blinded

Sample at least 100 long sessions stratified by correction count, tool-error/retry density, and horizon length. Replay identical prefixes under:

- baseline context behavior;
- Intent Card only;
- Decision Requirements Register only;
- full IDCC;
- full IDCC without premortem (red-team ablation).

Seed or identify decision points where an evaluator can score:

- owner-constraint violations;
- forgotten unresolved failures;
- stale-fact reuse;
- decisive-evidence survival;
- successful recovery of provenance;
- tokens retained per completed decision;
- false-positive retention;
- number of register updates and added latency.

Use a held-out evaluation set and blind evaluators to condition. Do not train a critic. Prefer executable outcomes—tests, exact constraints, expected file state—over model-graded prose.

Success threshold proposed for a pilot: at least 25% relative reduction in high-severity forgotten-context failures, no more than 5% regression in task success, and at least 10% fewer retained tokens at matched success. These thresholds are **roughly even (45-55%)** to be simultaneously achievable because no target-harness replay has tested this mechanism. This is **UNVERIFIED; the corpus replay itself settles it**.

### Experiment 3 — adversarial context cases

Construct five synthetic but realistic cases:

1. a negative owner constraint appears early and matters after two compactions;
2. a future-branch dependency is old but becomes decisive late;
3. a novel tool result invalidates the plan but matches no requirement;
4. an unresolved failure is buried under repeated identical retries;
5. a conclusion depends on a low-salience assumption that later becomes false.

Require exact provenance and action correctness, not mere recall. Run ablations to attribute each catch to intent, horizon reservation, exception lane, retry compression, or sentinels.

### Stop conditions

Reject IDCC rather than rationalizing it if any of these holds:

- requirements become a second task plan rather than a small set of unknowns tied to decisions;
- maintenance consumes more tokens than it saves on held-out long sessions;
- exception precision is too low to keep the inbox bounded;
- synthesized intent overrides exact owner language;
- the premortem increases retained tokens without reducing planted hidden-dependency failures;
- meaningful integration requires any forbidden large component.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just use OODA”

Rejected. OODA explains adaptive cognition and feedback but supplies no finite-capacity allocation or eviction rule. It is an update trigger, not a context manager.

### Preserve a complete running situation report

Rejected. Military staffs have people, systems, and reporting channels outside a commander’s immediate attention; a complete report inside the model’s hot context defeats the finite-window objective. Preserve compact state and retrievable provenance instead.

### Run a permanent adversarial agent against every context decision

Rejected. This reintroduces orchestration, consumes the resource being protected, and mistakes a bounded red-team technique for a standing runtime.

### Store all evicted information in a vector database or knowledge graph

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate and by the actual borrow. CCIR is a prioritization discipline, not a claim that semantic retrieval solves authority, time, contradiction, or exact provenance.

### Learn a trained retention critic

Rejected. The data and stable target labels are not established, model improvements could obsolete it, and a trained critic is expressly excluded. Deterministic pins/caps plus model-proposed requirements are smaller and falsifiable.

### Apply an AAR after every tool call

Rejected. AAR is an episodic learning mechanism. Per-action AAR would duplicate OODA feedback and flood context. Use it only at completion or diagnosed failure.

## Final recommendation

Proceed only with a staged, reversible pilot:

1. **Intent Card — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Pin purpose, end state, key tasks, authority, and constraints with exact provenance.
2. **Decision Requirements Register + exception lane — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Cap open questions at ten, link every one to a decision and latest time of value, split external state from own state, and preserve cross-horizon coverage.
3. **Reorientation trigger — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Update requirements and expiries after consequential actions or feedback.
4. **Eviction premortem + three sentinels — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Challenge hidden dependencies only at compaction boundaries.
5. **Context AAR tuple — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Learn selector changes from evidence after tasks, with held-out validation and no automatic prompt accretion.

The central design rule is: **retain purpose and decision leverage, not narrative completeness**. Military doctrine contributes a concrete answer to “important for what, and until when?” Its answer—important for a named decision, before its window closes, under a durable intent—is a strong structural match. The analogy stops before hierarchy, war language, standing red teams, or full staff machinery.
