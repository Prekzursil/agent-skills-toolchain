# F006 — Frontier research: when multi-agent coordination beats a single agent

**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Unit:** F006  
**Scope:** LLM-based agent coordination for an owner-operated coding/research harness. This is not a survey of classical distributed AI, human organizations, or multi-robot control.

## Executive finding

There is no defensible general rule that “multi-agent beats single-agent.” The best-supported rule is narrower:

> Use multiple agents when the task contains genuinely independent, information-heavy branches whose results can be compressed and centrally checked. Keep one agent when the task is sequential, shares mutable state, requires dense tool interaction, or already has a strong single-agent success rate. Spend extra inference on independent samples before spending it on elaborate dialogue.

That conclusion is supported by four different kinds of evidence:

1. A peer-reviewed decoding literature shows that multiple independent reasoning samples plus aggregation improve accuracy on many verifiable reasoning tasks. Self-consistency reported gains of +17.9 points on GSM8K, +11.0 on SVAMP, +12.2 on AQuA, +6.4 on StrategyQA, and +3.9 on ARC-Challenge; this is test-time ensembling, not evidence that agents need personas or conversation ([Wang et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)). Agent Forest later reproduced the basic scaling pattern over arithmetic, knowledge, chess, and code benchmarks with simple sampling and voting, and was published in TMLR ([Li et al., TMLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120); [code](https://github.com/MoreAgentsIsAllYouNeed/AgentForest)).
2. A production research system reports a 90.2% improvement over a single Opus 4 agent on Anthropic's internal research evaluation, specifically for breadth-first queries. Anthropic also reports that token count alone explained 80% of BrowseComp variance, that its multi-agent system used roughly 15 times the tokens of chat, and that coding/shared-context/dependency-heavy work was a poor fit. This is valuable production evidence but is not an independently reproducible benchmark result ([Anthropic, “How we built our multi-agent research system,” 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).
3. A recent controlled study matched prompts, tools, and compute across 260 configurations, six agentic benchmarks, five architectures, three model families, and found results ranging from +80.8% on decomposable financial reasoning to -70.0% on sequential planning. It also found all multi-agent variants degraded PlanCraft by 39–70% and centralized coordination propagated fewer errors than independent aggregation. This is the most directly relevant evidence, but it is a recent arXiv preprint rather than a mature replicated result ([Kim et al., “Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems,” arXiv:2512.08296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296); [code](https://github.com/ybkim95/agent-scaling); [Google Research summary](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)).
4. Failure analysis of more than 1,600 traces from seven multi-agent frameworks found 14 recurring failure modes in system design, inter-agent alignment, and verification; the taxonomy was grounded in 150 human-annotated traces with Cohen's kappa 0.88. Explicit verification helped, but superficial verifiers still accepted broken programs. This establishes that adding agents adds characteristic failure surfaces rather than automatically adding reliability ([Cemri et al., arXiv:2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657); [MAST repository and data](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST)).

For this harness, native fan-out is not the reliability problem: the supplied corpus records 1,745 `Agent` calls with 20 errors (1.15%), while `StructuredOutput` fails 22.9% of the time. The corpus also records 1,158 `general-purpose` invocations but only 29 `code-reviewer`, 28 `planner`, 18 each for `architect` and `security-reviewer`, and 13 `python-reviewer` invocations (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:119-124`, `:103-108`, `:13346-13368`). The direction should therefore be **selective native coordination with simpler handoffs**, not a new orchestration runtime.

## Method and evidence standard

I treated “established” as one of:

- a result in a peer-reviewed paper with meaningful baselines and an inspectable method;
- the same directional finding appearing through mechanically different signals, such as a controlled paper and a production report; or
- a native capability documented by its vendor and corroborated by the unpacked harness configuration.

I did **not** treat publication, a benchmark win, a framework README, or an arXiv upload as establishment. Recent 2025–2026 preprints are explicitly identified as lower-confidence even where their experiments are unusually broad. Exact effect sizes are not carried across tasks, models, or budgets.

The supplied corpus's `hooks` detector is marked broken, so I used no “hooks did not fire” inference. Harness-specific presence claims use two controls where absence matters: the unpacked tree proves that agent definitions exist, and the independent corpus field `agents_invoked` proves that many were actually invoked. I make no claim that an unlisted agent was never used.

## 1. What is established

### 1.1 Independent sampling and aggregation often beat one greedy answer on verifiable, single-answer tasks

The strongest replicated result commonly described as “multi-agent” is actually inference-time ensembling. Self-consistency samples multiple reasoning paths from one model and marginalizes their answers; it beat greedy chain-of-thought across arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks ([Wang et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)). Agent Forest uses multiple instances plus voting and reports that accuracy usually increases with ensemble size over GSM8K, MATH, MMLU, chess state tracking, and HumanEval; it explicitly records the proportional token cost and includes failed cases where debate noise harmed code generation ([Li et al., TMLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120); [code](https://github.com/MoreAgentsIsAllYouNeed/AgentForest)).

What this establishes is **diverse attempts plus a usable aggregator**, not peer conversation. The practical causal ingredients are more chances to sample a correct path and a verifier/majority signal that correlates with correctness. When outputs are subjective, correlated, or not cheaply verifiable, majority agreement is not evidence of truth.

### 1.2 Task decomposability, not agent count, is the key boundary condition

Anthropic's deployed Research architecture delegates breadth-first searches to independent context windows and reports its largest benefits on queries with multiple independent directions; it warns that coding tasks have fewer parallel branches and that shared-context or dependency-heavy work is a poor fit ([Anthropic engineering report](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The controlled 260-configuration study independently reports +80.8% for decomposable finance, +9.2% for decentralized dynamic web navigation, and large losses for sequential planning under a fixed budget ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). Claude Code's current official documentation makes the same operational distinction: teams are recommended for parallel research/review, separable modules, competing debugging hypotheses, and cross-layer ownership; it recommends a single session or subagents for sequential tasks, same-file edits, and dependency-heavy work ([Claude Code agent teams documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)).

These are mechanically different signals: a production system report, a controlled cross-benchmark preprint, and the native product's operating guidance. They establish the direction of the boundary, not a universal numeric threshold.

### 1.3 More agents buy test-time compute and context capacity; they do not create free intelligence

Anthropic reports that token use alone explained 80% of the performance variance in its BrowseComp analysis, with tool calls and model choice explaining most of the remainder; its multi-agent system consumed about 15 times the tokens of normal chat ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Agent Forest's cost grows proportionally with ensemble size ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120)). Claude Code states that each teammate has its own context window and token consumption scales with active teammate count ([official agent teams documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)).

Therefore an unmatched comparison of one call against five agents demonstrates that more inference can help, but does not isolate coordination. Cost-, call-, token-, and wall-clock-matched baselines are necessary before attributing a gain to architecture.

### 1.4 Diversity helps only when it produces meaningfully different, assessable errors

ReConcile used heterogeneous ChatGPT, Bard, and Claude 2 agents, confidence-weighted voting, and discussion. Across seven reasoning benchmarks it beat single- and multi-agent baselines by as much as 11.4 points; its self-consistency comparison matched average LLM calls, and ablations associated model diversity with gains. However, reported main comparisons used random subsets of 100 examples, so exact sizes are fragile ([Chen et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007); [code](https://github.com/dinobby/ReConcile)).

A 2025 controlled logic-puzzle study found intrinsic reasoning strength and group composition dominated debate order, confidence visibility, and depth; heterogeneous strong teams gained modestly, whereas structural changes did almost nothing for all-weak teams. It also observed that weak agents often followed an incorrect majority ([Wu et al., arXiv:2511.07784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)). This recent preprint is lower-confidence because it studies one synthetic puzzle family and did not calculate token-matched costs, but its process measurements explain the limitation: diversity helps only if agents can recognize valid counterarguments.

### 1.5 Central synthesis and external verification are safer than unconstrained peer propagation

The recent scaling study reports trace-level error amplification of 17.2 times for its independent architecture and 4.4 times for centralized coordination, attributing the difference to a validation bottleneck ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). MAST independently found incomplete or incorrect verification among common failure modes, found fewer failures in systems with explicit checks, and showed in a small intervention that adding high-level objective verification improved ChatDev task success by 15.6%; it also warns that a named “verifier” is not enough when checks are superficial ([Cemri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657); [data/code](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST)).

For code, an executable test, compiler, static analyzer, or reproduced failure is a more independent signal than another model's stylistic approval. The harness already encodes this sound hierarchy: its agent-as-judge is explicitly advisory and cannot grant completion (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/agent-as-judge.md:2-5`), while `AGENTS.md` requires a fresh-context check and evidence (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:51-62`, `:72-83`).

### 1.6 Coordination itself has stable failure modes

MAST's 14 modes cover agents disobeying specifications or roles, repeated steps, failure to request clarification, task derailment, ignored input, premature termination, and missing or incorrect verification. Its 1,600-plus traces span seven popular frameworks and multiple model families ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657); [repository](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST)). The controlled debate study separately shows majority pressure suppressing correction, especially among weaker models ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)). Anthropic's production retrospective reports early systems spawning 50 agents for simple queries, searching endlessly for nonexistent sources, and distracting one another with excessive updates ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

The common pattern is not merely “models make mistakes.” Delegation loses context, communication can propagate a wrong premise, termination gets harder, and additional messages create more opportunities for correlated error.

### 1.7 The native substrate already covers the useful architectures

Claude Code subagents have separate context windows, custom prompts/tool access, and return a summary to the parent; the official docs recommend them when side work would flood the main context ([subagent documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)). Native agent teams add a lead, shared task list, direct teammate messaging, and independent contexts; they are documented as experimental and carry known resumption, coordination, and shutdown limitations ([agent teams documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)).

The unpacked harness already describes independent Codex fan-out over native MCP at `claude/CLAUDE.md:19-42`, structured subagent handoffs at `claude/AGENTS.md:59-62`, and a separate local peer-mail daemon at `claude/CLAUDE.md:44-67`. The corpus confirms native `Agent` usage rather than mere installation: 1,745 calls with a 1.15% recorded error rate (`inputs/corpus.json:119-124`, `:9561-9589`). Thus the minimal path can stay on the existing native Agent/subagent/MCP/hook buses.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 UNVERIFIED — a lightweight task router can capture most of the upside

**very likely (80-90%)**, because the production report, official native guidance, and controlled preprint all identify the same task features—parallel branches, low shared state, and central synthesis—while the exact policy has not been tested on this owner's workload ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams); [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

The promising policy is not a learned router. It is a short preflight decision:

- Are there at least two branches that can finish without seeing each other's intermediate state?
- Can each branch own a disjoint evidence set or file set?
- Is the synthesis cheaper than doing all branches sequentially?
- Is there an independent acceptance test or source check?
- Is the task valuable enough to pay several times the tokens?

The **UNVERIFIED** premise is that these five answers predict owner-valued outcomes on this corpus; the settling experiment is a preregistered A/B replay of 30–50 representative tasks with equal token budgets and blinded outcome grading.

### 2.2 UNVERIFIED — conditional escalation from one agent to a small team

**likely (55-80%)**, because agent count raises cost linearly, coordination exhibits diminishing returns as base capability rises, and early disagreement can reveal useful diversity; no source establishes the ideal escalation threshold for this harness ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296); [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)).

A single lead could begin by decomposing the task, spawn two or three workers only for independent branches, then add a worker only when a branch is blocked or the first results expose a genuinely new hypothesis. This is more plausible than fixed swarms, but **UNVERIFIED** until a factorial experiment compares 1, 2, 3, and adaptive agents under matched tokens, recording success, elapsed time, redundant work, and owner corrections.

### 2.3 UNVERIFIED — cross-model diversity for high-stakes review

**likely (55-80%)**, because ReConcile's matched-call experiments and diversity ablations support heterogeneous teams, while the controlled 2025 debate study finds only modest gains and shows weak agents may not evaluate strong arguments correctly ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007); [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)).

Claude plus Codex is most promising as independent generation followed by evidence-based adjudication, not free-form conversation. It is **UNVERIFIED** that cross-vendor errors are sufficiently decorrelated on this owner's coding and research tasks; settle it by sampling at least 100 paired verdicts, measuring raw agreement, both-wrong rate against executable/source ground truth, and incremental catch rate over the stronger model alone.

### 2.4 UNVERIFIED — debate only when disagreement is evidence-bearing

**roughly even (45-55%)**, because debate improves some reasoning benchmarks but is bounded by the strongest participant, sensitive to diversity, and vulnerable to incorrect-majority pressure ([Du et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325); [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007); [Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)).

The promising form is one challenge round in which each reviewer must cite a failing test, source line, counterexample, or falsifiable premise. Repeated prose-only rounds are **unlikely (20-45%)** to outperform spending the same budget on fresh independent attempts, because Agent Forest found its simple aggregation ranked above more complicated collaboration methods and recorded debate-induced code degradation ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120)). The experiment is a token-matched comparison of independent-review-plus-adjudication against one- and three-round debate on real patches.

### 2.5 UNVERIFIED — Mixture-of-Agents for open-ended answer synthesis

**likely (55-80%)** to improve subjective answer quality at a fixed choice of older/open models, because layered proposal-and-synthesis reached 65.1% on AlpacaEval 2.0 versus 57.5% for GPT-4o and beat a ranker ablation ([Wang et al., “Mixture-of-Agents,” arXiv:2406.04692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692); [code](https://github.com/togethercomputer/MoA)).

It is **UNVERIFIED** whether this remains cost-effective against the current strongest single model, whether judge preference transfers to owner satisfaction, and whether it helps tool-using tasks. The settling experiment is a current-model, blind pairwise evaluation with total dollars, generated tokens, critical latency, and executable factual checks held or reported—not adoption from the 2024 leaderboard alone.

### 2.6 UNVERIFIED — learned or dynamically searched topologies

**unlikely (20-45%)** to repay their complexity for a personal harness now. MultiAgentBench exposes star, tree, chain, and graph structures and reports graph doing best in its research scenario, but its principal comparison is between multi-agent protocols rather than a strongest matched-budget single agent; “cognitive planning” improves milestone achievement only 3% ([MultiAgentBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01935); [MARBLE repository](https://github.com/MultiagentBench/MARBLE)). The newer scaling study predicts architecture choice in held-out configurations, but its cross-validated absolute model fit is only R² 0.373 (0.413 with a task-grounded capability metric) and awaits independent replication ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

The **UNVERIFIED** settling experiment is whether a learned router beats the five-question static gate by a practically meaningful margin on this owner's tasks after including development and inference cost. Until then, a learned topology would violate the anti-overfit spirit even if implemented on the existing bus.

## 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

### 3.1 “More agents” is presented as a coordination law

**Tell:** the agents never exchange task-relevant information; they are independent samples followed by majority vote. That can be a useful ensemble, but it does not establish delegation, specialization, social reasoning, or coordination. Agent Forest is unusually honest about this—it calls its method sampling and voting and reports proportional token use ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120)).

### 3.2 The single-agent baseline gets one call while the team gets N calls and N contexts

**Tell:** no equal-token, equal-call, equal-tool, or equal-wall-time baseline. Anthropic explicitly says tokens explain most variance in its research analysis ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). A result may still be economically useful, but “coordination caused the gain” is unsupported.

### 3.3 Personas substitute for capability or diversity

**Tell:** “architect,” “critic,” “scientist,” or “CEO” role names are added without an ablation against repeated samples from the same strong model, and without measuring whether their errors differ. The controlled debate study finds base reasoning strength and team composition dominate order, confidence visibility, and debate depth ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)). Anthropomorphic names are interface design, not evidence.

### 3.4 “Emergence” is inferred from transcripts

**Tell:** polished negotiation excerpts or an “aha moment” appear without a preregistered behavioral metric, held-out outcome, or counterfactual baseline. MultiAgentBench itself describes “emergent social behaviors” while its quantitative planning intervention is a 3% milestone gain ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01935)). A transcript can illustrate a mechanism but cannot establish it.

### 3.5 Majority agreement is treated as verification

**Tell:** correctness is inferred from consensus among instances of the same model. Correlated agents can confidently share the same bad premise; the controlled debate study directly observes majority pressure suppressing correction ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784)). A test, source, environment response, or independently trained verifier is qualitatively different from another vote.

### 3.6 A verifier exists, therefore the system is reliable

**Tell:** the verifier checks formatting, compilation, or TODO markers but not the user's behavioral objective. MAST includes examples of code passing superficial checks while failing game rules and finds missing/incorrect verification across frameworks ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657); [repository](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST)). “Reviewer agent ran” is a process receipt, not an outcome receipt.

### 3.7 Static benchmarks stand in for long-horizon agent work

**Tell:** the headline uses GSM8K, MMLU, HumanEval, or AlpacaEval but recommends an autonomous coding/research organization. Static answer aggregation does not model tool-state divergence, edit collisions, environment changes, or cascading execution errors. The recent controlled agentic study finds the sign of the effect reverses across decomposable finance and sequential planning ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

### 3.8 Cost is hidden behind parallel latency

**Tell:** only critical-path latency is reported while total model tokens, dollars, tool calls, retries, and human adjudication are absent. Claude Code's documentation explicitly says team token use scales with active teammates ([official docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)). Parallel execution can reduce elapsed time and simultaneously worsen cost and reliability.

### 3.9 Framework complexity is its own evidence

**Tell:** a graph, marketplace, roles, memory service, or coordination language is evaluated against another elaborate framework, not against one current strong agent with the same tools and budget. This is precisely where a personal harness should apply the anti-overfit gate: architecture earns its place only through measured owner-valued outcomes.

## 4. Specific things this harness should adopt

### 4.1 UNVERIFIED — default-single routing gate with explicit fan-out criteria

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Add a short routing rule to the global guidance: remain single-agent unless there are at least two independent branches, disjoint ownership/evidence, a central synthesis point, and enough task value to justify extra tokens. Route isolated branches to subagents; route tasks requiring workers to exchange findings or challenge hypotheses to native agent teams. Official docs already distinguish these modes ([subagents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents); [teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)).

**very likely (80-90%)** to reduce waste without losing most multi-agent wins, based on three aligned signals: Anthropic production experience, native product guidance, and the matched-compute scaling study. **UNVERIFIED** for this owner; settle with the 30–50 task A/B described in §2.1.

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS.** It is a prompt/routing rule on the native bus, with no runtime, database, or learned controller.

### 4.2 UNVERIFIED — start with 2–3 workers and expand only on demonstrated branch value

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Do not encode “maximum parallelism” as a virtue. Start with the smallest team that covers the task's independent branches. Require an additional spawn to name the unowned branch, conflicting hypothesis, or context-capacity reason. Anthropic documents pathological early spawning of 50 agents and 15-times-chat token use ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)); Claude Code says token use scales with teammate count ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)).

**likely (55-80%)** to improve cost-adjusted outcomes; evidence establishes rising cost and coordination overhead but not the optimal cap. **UNVERIFIED** threshold; settle with matched-token 1/2/3/adaptive arms.

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS.** Native spawn policy only.

### 4.3 Make independent fan-out the default multi-agent topology

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

For research sweeps, competing bug hypotheses, and separable repo/file work, give each worker a non-overlapping question and have it return a compact evidence-bearing handoff to the lead. Do not let workers debate unless initial results conflict on a falsifiable point. The harness already requires structured handoffs rather than raw-output prefixing (`claude/AGENTS.md:59-62`) and already documents Codex fan-out (`claude/CLAUDE.md:19-42`). Preserve these mechanisms.

**very likely (80-90%)** to be the best general multi-agent default, because independent sampling has stronger replicated evidence than elaborate dialogue and native subagents protect the lead context ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171); [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05120); [Claude subagent docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)).

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS.** Existing subagent/MCP bus; no new component.

### 4.4 UNVERIFIED — one lead owns synthesis; executable evidence owns completion

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Keep a centralized lead that re-anchors the original goal, deduplicates findings, resolves conflicts against sources/tests, and alone authors the final integrated answer. Review agents remain advisory. For code, completion requires executable receipts; for research, source-level claim checks. This matches centralized verification's lower error amplification in the recent controlled study and MAST's verification findings ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296); [Cemri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)).

**very likely (80-90%)** to outperform peer consensus for reliability, based on the independent controlled and trace-analysis signals. Exact improvement is **UNVERIFIED** for this harness; settle by logging whether centralized adjudication catches defects missed by worker consensus.

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS.** Existing lead, tests, and structured handoff bus.

### 4.5 Ban multi-writer shared-state coordination by default

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Parallel coding workers should own disjoint files/modules or isolated native worktrees. If two workers need repeated edits to the same file or depend on one another's uncommitted intermediate state, serialize the work under one agent. Claude Code explicitly advises against teams for same-file and dependency-heavy tasks ([agent team docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)); the matched-compute paper reports 39–70% degradation on sequential planning ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)).

**almost certain (90-99%)** to reduce edit collisions and stale-state reasoning, based on the direct state-ownership constraint and two independent sources. The claim does not imply isolated parallel modules always improve end quality.

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS.** Uses native serialization/worktree facilities, not durable execution.

### 4.6 UNVERIFIED — use cross-vendor review only where correlated-error reduction is worth its cost

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Retain Claude→Codex independent review for security-sensitive changes, irreversible decisions, uncertain architecture, and disputed findings. Do not require it for every edit. Require each reviewer to cite a test, source, or counterexample; the lead adjudicates. The current harness already exposes Codex by native MCP (`claude/CLAUDE.md:19-42`) and calls its own peer-mail layer interim (`claude/CLAUDE.md:65-67`).

**likely (55-80%)** to add value on selected high-risk work, based on heterogeneous-model gains in ReConcile but limited direct owner evidence ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007)). **UNVERIFIED** decorrelation premise; settle with the paired-verdict experiment in §2.3.

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS.** Reuses native MCP; no trained critic or new service.

### 4.7 UNVERIFIED — replace the live peer-mail daemon with native agent teams after a parity trial

**Tag: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Full replacement is now plausibly better than evolution. The harness's Layer 2 is a local always-on `mcp_agent_mail` daemon with supervisor and mailbox, explicitly described as interim pending native Agent Teams (`claude/CLAUDE.md:44-67`). Current Claude Code Agent Teams provide a lead, direct teammate messaging, shared tasks, independent contexts, and an in-process display mode; they are still experimental and have documented limitations around resumption, task coordination, and shutdown ([official docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)).

Run a parity trial first: on Windows, exercise (1) lead-to-peer and peer-to-peer messages, (2) long-running task completion, (3) session resume, (4) failure/shutdown cleanup, (5) permissions, and (6) transcript/receipt capture. Only after all required cases pass should the daemon, scheduled supervisor, and mingle skill be retired.

**likely (55-80%)** that native teams can replace the daemon for ordinary interactive collaboration, because the documented native feature now covers its central functions and avoids a second runtime. The premise is **UNVERIFIED** on this Windows installation; the six-case parity trial settles it. Resumption may remain a blocker because the vendor documents limitations.

**Anti-overfit gate: PASS only after parity.** The proposal removes a previously rejected second orchestration/runtime component rather than adding one. If parity fails, retain the daemon narrowly for the failing capability and do not build a third system.

## Recommended operating policy, condensed

1. Start single-agent.
2. Spawn a subagent for an isolated branch that would consume context but does not need peer communication.
3. Spawn a native team only when at least two workers need to share findings, challenge competing hypotheses, or own separable layers.
4. Start with 2–3 workers; every additional worker must name a new branch or hypothesis.
5. Give every worker a disjoint output/evidence scope and a typed handoff.
6. Keep one lead as synthesizer. Require sources or executable checks; never use vote count as completion proof.
7. Serialize same-file, sequential, and shared-state work.
8. Use cross-vendor review selectively for high-risk or disputed claims.
9. Measure total inference, elapsed time, success, rework, and owner correction.
10. Retire the peer-mail daemon only after the native-team parity test passes; add no replacement runtime.
