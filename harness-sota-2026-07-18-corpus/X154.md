## Executive result

The borrow **does transfer, but only partially**. The transferable mechanism is not “turn the chat log into an archive,” and it is not “preserve everything in original order.” It is a four-part control discipline:

1. **function-level appraisal before item-level selection** — decide which classes of evidence a task must preserve before judging individual messages;
2. **explicit, event-driven disposition schedules** — every context class has a cutoff, retention rule, and final action rather than an indefinite “keep if useful” instruction;
3. **provenance-preserving hierarchical description** — compact records without severing creator, activity, sequence, and source relationships;
4. **documented transformations plus disposition holds** — a summary is a derivative with a custody/transform record, and contested or not-yet-verified evidence cannot be evicted on schedule.

Call the translated mechanism a **Provenance-Aware Context Schedule (PACS)**.

The central design rule is: **eviction from the prompt is not deletion from the record**. Raw turns and tool results remain in the provider/session’s existing transcript or artifact store; PACS decides which representation is projected into the finite live window. This distinction makes the archival analogy much more viable for agents than literal archival destruction.

I am **very likely (80-90%)** that this control-plane borrow is structurally sound, because archival schedules already separate appraisal, cutoff, retention, and disposition at series level, while archival provenance already separates intellectual relationships from physical arrangement; NARA and SAA explicitly document both separations ([NARA scheduling guidance](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records), [SAA CUSTARD principles](https://www2.archivists.org/news/2002/statement-of-principles-for-the-custard-project)). I am **not** claiming it improves this owner’s harness without measurement. That effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with Experiment E1**, the replay ablation specified below.

The radical version—a new archival memory service—is rejected. PACS can ride the existing transcript, hook, skill, and artifact buses, using a small manifest and deterministic projector. A second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework is neither required nor proposed.

## 1. What archival science actually solved

### 1.1 Appraisal: select evidence by function and enduring value, not by isolated item salience

In archival practice, appraisal determines value and therefore final disposition. NARA defines appraisal as determining whether federal records become temporary or permanent, and defines archival value as value warranting preservation beyond the originating agency’s business need ([NARA Appraisal Policy, definitions](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)). Crucially, appraisal is not simply “read each document and score how interesting it sounds.” Library and Archives Canada’s macroappraisal method studies the creator’s role, mandate, activities, relationships, and context, emphasizing the context of creation over document content to identify the best available source documenting activities of long-term interest ([LAC macroappraisal and functional analysis](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/government/information-disposition/records/methodology.html)). NARA likewise requires functional/business-process analysis and an inventory before a schedule is drafted ([NARA, How to Schedule Records](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)).

This solves a volume problem by moving the decision boundary upward. An archivist can decide that the authoritative decision file for a function has enduring evidential value while routine duplicate/transitory copies do not, without independently predicting every item’s future research question. NARA states that, where different parts of an agency use the same information, it generally designates only the most complete series as permanent ([NARA, Proposing Disposition](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). NARA also recognizes sampling as selecting items so that the selection is representative of the whole ([NARA Appraisal Policy](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/appraisal)).

There is an important warning inside the field: top-down provenance-based appraisal can omit “powerless transactions” that reveal broader social context. The SAA Dictionary’s provenance entry records this criticism directly ([SAA Dictionary, provenance](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/provenance.html)). Thus archival appraisal is a governed, revisable judgment, not a neutral oracle.

### 1.2 Retention schedules: make disposition explicit, class-based, and event-driven

A records schedule is executable policy for recurring record series. NARA requires clear series/system descriptions, cutoff instructions, disposition instructions, and retention/transfer periods ([NARA schedule requirements](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). A cutoff marks when records leave active use and starts the retention clock. Temporary-record instructions can use fixed time or predictable events such as completion, publication, supersession, acceptance/rejection, audit, or settlement ([NARA, Preparing Disposition Instructions](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/instructions)).

The critical mechanism is not merely a time-to-live. It is the tuple:

`record series → scope → cutoff event → retention period → disposition → authority/version`

NARA warns against “destroy when no longer needed”: it delegates uncontrolled judgment to individual users, produces both over-retention and premature destruction, and is difficult to automate ([NARA, Analyzing Disposition and Retention Needs](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/sch-records)). That critique maps directly to vague agent instructions such as “keep important context.”

Schedules also admit exceptions. A records freeze suspends scheduled disposition when circumstances alter administrative, legal, or fiscal value; the freeze is explicitly applied and later lifted ([NARA Federal Records Centers freeze process](https://www.archives.gov/frc/arcis/freeze-faq)). In NARA policy, holds suspend disposition for legal, audit, investigative, or related needs ([NARA 825](https://www.archives.gov/files/about/policies/nara0825.pdf)).

NARA’s Capstone method shows another useful scaling technique: retention can be assigned by the **role** of a record creator instead of classifying every message’s content ([NARA Capstone forms](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/capstone-forms)). This reduces per-item classification burden but deliberately trades precision for systematic capture.

### 1.3 Provenance, original order, and hierarchical description: preserve relationships separately from physical placement

The principle of provenance says that significance depends heavily on creation context and that records from different origins should not be intermingled; its two conventional components are respect des fonds and original order ([SAA Dictionary, provenance](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/provenance.html)). Original order is the organization and sequence established by the creator ([SAA Dictionary, original order](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/original-order.html)). The reason is evidential: order can reveal relationships and how work was actually conducted.

The subtle but essential point is that provenance need not dictate physical co-location. SAA’s CUSTARD principles state that records of one creator need not be physically separate, provided provenance is reflected in description, retrieval by provenance is possible, and the system can represent the creator’s records together ([SAA CUSTARD principles](https://www2.archivists.org/news/2002/statement-of-principles-for-the-custard-project)). Archives describe from aggregate to specific levels, establishing intellectual control without item-level rewriting. Library and Archives Canada describes fonds/collection, series, file, and item as a hierarchy that preserves original context ([LAC, How archives are organized](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/basics/research-archives.html)).

Modern archival description also recognizes that provenance is not always a single tree. ICA’s Records in Contexts conceptual model says provenance is more complex than the traditional single hierarchy and models records alongside agents, activities, functions, and their relations ([ICA RiC-CM 1.0](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2023/12/RiC-CM-1.0.pdf)). That is relevant to agent work, where a statement may derive from a user message, a tool result, a file, and an inference rather than one creator.

### 1.4 Authenticity: preserve identity, integrity, custody, and transformations

Archival authenticity is not “the claim is true.” It is confidence that a record is what it purports to be and has not been altered without account. Chain of custody records the succession of persons or offices holding materials from creation onward; archival tradition treats documented, unbroken custody and fixity as important conditions for authenticity ([SAA Dictionary, chain of custody](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/chain-of-custody.html)). ICA’s electronic-records guidance identifies authenticity, reliability, integrity, and usability as core record characteristics and calls for metadata such as format name/version to support preservation and interpretation ([ICA, Electronic Records: A Workbook for Archivists](https://www.ica.org/app/uploads/2023/12/ICA_Study-16-Electronic-records_EN.pdf)).

For context management, this means a compacted summary should not silently impersonate the source. It is a derivative representation with its own creator (model/version or deterministic transform), creation time, covered source IDs, and integrity references. Authenticity protects against transformation drift; it does not prove semantic correctness or relevance.

## 2. Concrete translation: Provenance-Aware Context Schedules

### 2.1 Proposal A — Context series registry **[UNVERIFIED — settle with E1]**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Define a small, versioned schedule for context classes. Appraise by task function before looking at individual token salience. A starter schedule could be:

| Context series | Archival analogue | Live-window rule | Cutoff event | After cutoff | Hold triggers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner mandate and hard constraints | charter / policy series | preserve verbatim, high placement priority | explicit supersession by owner | retain prior version as cold provenance; project current version | ambiguity or conflict |
| Acceptance criteria and promised deliverables | case-file controlling documents | preserve verbatim until verified | criterion independently verified or explicitly waived | compact to result + evidence pointer | failed verification, owner correction |
| Authoritative observations | evidential record | preserve exact values plus source identity | source superseded or task closes | compact to typed fact + source pointer | conflicting observation, stale source under active use |
| Decisions and rejected alternatives | decision record | preserve decision, rationale, alternatives, authority | decision superseded | retain current decision; cold-link predecessors | reversal, unresolved objection |
| Open hypotheses and uncertainties | pending case material | keep explicitly marked as hypothesis | confirmed, falsified, or abandoned | move to outcome ledger | any dependent action remains open |
| Tool outputs used as evidence | exhibits / supporting records | retain relevant excerpt plus raw-result pointer | claim verified or abandoned | summary with exact source span/hash where available | parse failure, partial output, contradiction |
| Routine progress narration | transitory record | low priority; aggregate by phase | phase/task transition | one-line phase synopsis or evict | none unless it carries a commitment |
| Duplicate/reproducible output | convenience copy | keep canonical instance only | canonical instance identified | evict projection; retain pointer | reproduction is nondeterministic |
| Untrusted retrieved content | externally supplied accession | isolate from instructions; retain source/trust label | task no longer depends on it | compact as evidence, never as authority | prompt-injection/security review |

This is intentionally a schedule, not a learned importance scorer. Each row must include scope, cutoff, disposition, schedule version, and who/what may place or lift a hold. “Keep while useful” is prohibited for the same operational reason NARA discourages “destroy when no longer needed.”

It is **likely (55-80%)** that series-first rules reduce omission of low-salience but controlling evidence—exact numeric constraints, negative requirements, rejected approaches, and pending verification—because classification uses function and lifecycle rather than surface salience; the evidence is the archival move from content-by-content judgment to function/series appraisal and NARA’s requirement to define implementable cutoffs. The harness benefit is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1** must compare it against current compaction on hidden recall and compliance probes.

What it can catch that recency/truncation/flat summary misses:

- an old owner prohibition displaced by newer conversational material;
- an unclosed acceptance criterion accidentally treated as completed;
- a rejected approach reintroduced after its rationale was summarized away;
- a hypothesis promoted to fact because its epistemic state disappeared;
- a duplicate tool result consuming live context while the authoritative instance is evicted;
- a stale value retained after an explicit superseding event;
- evidence eligible for normal eviction but protected by an active contradiction/verification hold.

### 2.2 Proposal B — Provenance envelope and hierarchical finding aid **[UNVERIFIED — settle with E1 and E2]**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Attach a compact sidecar envelope to every context record or derived summary:

```json
{
  "id": "ctx-...",
  "series": "authoritative_observation",
  "creator": {"kind": "tool|user|agent|file", "id": "..."},
  "activity": "verify-build",
  "task": "...",
  "source_ids": ["turn-...", "tool-..."],
  "sequence": {"session": "...", "ordinal": 37},
  "trust": "owner|local-authoritative|external-untrusted|inference",
  "epistemic": "observed|inferred|hypothesis|contradicted",
  "schedule": {"id": "pacs-v1/authoritative-observation", "cutoff": null},
  "hold": {"reason": null, "set_by": null},
  "transform": {"kind": "raw|extract|summary", "producer": "...", "time": "..."},
  "integrity": {"source_hashes": ["..."]}
}
```

Maintain a lightweight hierarchical finding aid:

`owner/workspace → goal → task/phase → activity → record bundle → item`

Also allow cross-links for `supports`, `contradicts`, `supersedes`, `derived-from`, and `verifies`. This is a small adjacency list in the manifest, **not** a knowledge graph service or vector database. The live context projector assembles a task-specific view; it does not rewrite the underlying order. Original sequence remains metadata and can be reconstructed, while prompt placement is optimized for current use.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that separating intellectual provenance from physical prompt order is the correct translation, because SAA explicitly allows provenance to be represented descriptively without physical separation, while long-context research reports that models do not use all positions equally and often perform worse when relevant information is in the middle ([SAA CUSTARD principles](https://www2.archivists.org/news/2002/statement-of-principles-for-the-custard-project), [Liu et al., “Lost in the Middle,” TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)). Whether this particular envelope earns its token and implementation cost is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E2** measures provenance reconstruction, contradiction detection, and token overhead.

What it can catch:

- a summary whose claim has no retraceable source;
- evidence copied from an untrusted retrieval but later presented as an owner instruction;
- two statements that look inconsistent but apply to different task phases or source versions;
- a derived claim whose only source was itself a prior summary (summary-of-summary drift);
- a value superseded by a later observation but still retrieved without its succession relation;
- a context fragment detached from the activity that gave it meaning.

### 2.3 Proposal C — Scheduled context projector with holds **[UNVERIFIED — settle with E1]**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

At existing compaction or pre-model-call boundaries, deterministically compute the live projection:

1. identify the active goal/task/phase;
2. load all records in permanent-for-task series and all active holds;
3. apply cutoff events and supersession links;
4. represent each eligible bundle at the least lossy level that fits: verbatim item → excerpt → bundle summary → finding-aid pointer;
5. reserve explicit token budgets by series, not merely by recency;
6. place controlling mandate/constraints and immediate-action evidence where the model can use them; retain original sequence in metadata rather than forcing chronological prompt order;
7. emit a disposition receipt listing projected, compacted, cold-linked, and omitted record IDs plus reasons.

No item on hold may be omitted solely because its scheduled cutoff fired. Suggested automatic holds are: unresolved owner correction, contradictory authoritative observations, failed verification, open security concern, and a source supporting an unclosed acceptance criterion. Holds need a reason and an explicit release event; otherwise they become permanent leaks.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that deterministic receipts make premature eviction diagnosable, because every omission obtains a schedule/rule trace rather than an opaque salience score. This is an inference from the accountability function of archival disposition authorities and documented freezes, not direct agent evidence. The effect is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1** must measure whether receipts localize failures faster and whether holds inflate context without bound.

What it can catch:

- silent omission (record ID appears in neither projection nor disposition receipt);
- premature eviction (cutoff fired while a dependent criterion remains open);
- uncontrolled over-retention (expired, unheld records repeatedly projected);
- hold leaks (release condition satisfied but the hold remains);
- schedule drift (a record classified under an obsolete schedule version);
- context allocation dominated by one verbose series.

### 2.4 Proposal D — Authenticity and compaction audit **[UNVERIFIED — settle with E2]**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For every extract or summary, record source IDs, transform producer/version, timestamp, and source digest where the platform exposes stable bytes. On a sample of compactions—or always for controlling constraints—check:

- **identity:** the referenced source exists and is the intended record;
- **integrity:** the source has not changed without a new version;
- **coverage:** every atomic summary claim maps to at least one source span/record;
- **authority preservation:** trust and epistemic labels never upgrade silently;
- **lineage depth:** refuse repeated summary-of-summary compaction past a configured depth; recompact from raw records instead;
- **contradiction:** preserve both sides and place a hold instead of resolving by recency alone.

Hashes establish byte-level identity, not truth. Source pointers establish traceability, not entailment. A model-generated coverage check can itself be wrong, so controlling facts require deterministic extraction where possible or replay against raw evidence.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that lineage limits and raw-source recompaction reduce semantic drift, based on the archival distinction between original and derivative records and the general information loss inherent in repeated lossy summaries. The effect and optimal lineage limit are **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E2** must inject summary mutations and measure detection and end-task correctness.

What it can catch:

- unsupported detail introduced during compaction;
- dropped negation, unit, boundary, or exception;
- authority laundering from external content into system/owner state;
- a hash/source mismatch after a mutable file changed;
- circular provenance;
- a fifth-generation summary no longer grounded in raw evidence.

### 2.5 Proposal E — Periodic reappraisal, not permanent policy ossification **[UNVERIFIED — settle with E3]**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Version every schedule and require reappraisal after a model/tool change, repeated correction cluster, or material task-family change. Reappraisal reviews false-retain and false-evict examples by series, including a deliberate “powerless transaction” audit: were small user caveats, dissenting evidence, minority branches, or negative results systematically erased by top-down task summaries?

It is **likely (55-80%)** that scheduled reappraisal is necessary because both NARA’s scheduling process and LAC macroappraisal treat appraisal as documented institutional judgment, and SAA records a known top-down selection bias. The right cadence is **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E3** compares fixed versus periodically revised schedules over sequential task families.

What it can catch:

- a once-useful class rule made obsolete by a stronger model;
- systematic loss of user caveats that appear infrequently;
- schedules optimized for coding work misapplied to research or operations;
- a “permanent” class whose token cost no longer buys measurable correctness;
- a new tool/source type falling through every disposition rule.

## 3. Adversarial transfer test

### 3.1 Where the structures match

| Archival condition | Agent-context counterpart | Strength of transfer |
|---|---|---|
| More records exist than can receive item-level attention | More history exists than fits/usefully functions in the live window | Strong: both require selective representation |
| Future use cannot be predicted item by item | Later agent steps may depend on seemingly minor earlier evidence | Strong: class/function policy can hedge item uncertainty |
| Records derive meaning from creator, function, and sequence | statements derive meaning from speaker/tool, task phase, trust boundary, and causal history | Strong: provenance prevents decontextualization |
| Recurring series permit stable disposition policy | recurring agent record classes permit schedule templates | Moderate: agent tasks are less stable and demand reappraisal |
| Derivatives coexist with originals | summaries/extracts coexist with raw transcripts/tool results | Strong if raw sources remain retrievable |
| Authorized holds override ordinary destruction | unresolved corrections/conflicts must override eviction | Strong at the policy level |

### 3.2 Where the structures do **not** match

1. **Enduring cultural/evidential value is not next-action utility.** Archives select across decades and constituencies; a context window supports one imminent inference. A historically important record may be useless to the next step, while a transient stack trace may be essential for 30 seconds. Therefore archival appraisal supplies policy shape, not the value function.

2. **Archival disposal can be irreversible; prompt eviction should be reversible.** Deleting a record and omitting it from one model call have radically different stakes. PACS transfers the schedule metaphor only because cold provenance remains accessible. If the implementation destroys raw evidence at cutoff, the analogy becomes unsafe.

3. **Original order is evidence, not an optimal prompt layout.** Chronology can preserve causal relationships, but long-context models exhibit positional bias; literal original-order prompting can bury decisive evidence. Preserve original order as metadata and generate a current-use projection.

4. **Authenticity is not truth.** An authentic owner statement can be mistaken; an authentic tool result can be stale; a perfectly hashed retrieved page can contain prompt injection. Authenticity answers identity/integrity questions only.

5. **Creator identity is messier for agents.** An output may combine model inference, tool bytes, system instructions, and retrieved text. A single-fonds tree is inadequate. Multi-provenance links are required, borrowing the broader relational direction of ICA Records in Contexts without implementing its ontology.

6. **Agent functions mutate quickly.** Government mandates and recurring record series can remain stable for years; task phases can change within minutes. Schedules need narrow scope, explicit versioning, and cheap reappraisal.

7. **Top-down appraisal encodes power.** If “owner goal” dominates every selection, caveats, dissent, anomalous evidence, and security warnings may vanish. The SAA provenance entry’s critique of excluding powerless transactions is a direct warning, not a historical footnote.

8. **Runtime latency differs.** Human archival appraisal can involve review and public authority. Per-turn context projection must be deterministic and cheap. This rules out elaborate semantic appraisal on every call.

9. **Latent future dependencies defeat perfect schedules.** No finite taxonomy can know every fact a later branch will need. The system must preserve cold retrievability and evaluate false eviction; it cannot promise lossless finite context.

### 3.3 Verdict

The analogy transfers as a **governance and metadata discipline**, not as a storage architecture or literal arrangement rule. I am **very likely (80-90%)** in this bounded verdict because each successful mapping preserves the same invariant—controlled selection without severing evidential relationships—while each rejected mapping depends on a non-shared objective such as permanence or chronological presentation. Evidence comes from NARA’s explicit schedules/holds, SAA’s separation of intellectual and physical arrangement, ICA’s relational provenance, and empirical long-context positional bias cited above.

The overall performance recommendation remains **UNVERIFIED; Experiment E1** is required before adoption. If E1 shows no material reduction in constraint loss, provenance errors, or recovery time after accounting for added tokens and latency, stop: the analogy is intellectually attractive but operationally non-transferable.

## 4. Experiments that settle the unverified premises

### E1 — Replay ablation: schedule vs recency vs flat summary

Construct at least 100 long-horizon replay cases from task transcripts, stratified by coding, research, multi-step operations, and owner-correction episodes. Seed each with hidden probes covering:

- exact constraints and negative requirements;
- superseded facts;
- rejected alternatives and rationale;
- unresolved acceptance criteria;
- conflicting tool observations;
- untrusted text resembling instructions;
- a low-salience caveat early in the session.

Replay identical next-step decisions under three context policies: current/baseline, recency plus flat compaction, and PACS. Keep model, decoding, available raw store, and token budget fixed. Score end-task success, atomic constraint recall, stale-fact use, authority-boundary violations, false completion, recovery calls, projected tokens, latency, and disposition-receipt usefulness during diagnosis. Predeclare a minimum adoption threshold, for example: statistically reliable reduction in critical context failures with no material end-task regression and bounded overhead. The threshold itself is a product choice, not an archival fact.

### E2 — Authenticity and provenance mutation test

Create record bundles with known lineage. Inject dropped negations, changed numbers/units, swapped source IDs, stale hashes, circular derivation, trust-label upgrades, contradictory successors, and summaries compacted through one to five generations. Compare raw-source recompaction and ordinary recursive summarization. Measure mutation detection, false alarms, source reconstruction, final-answer correctness, added tokens, and compute. This settles whether the provenance envelope produces operational value rather than decorative metadata.

### E3 — Appraisal drift and minority-evidence test

Train/configure a schedule on one tranche of task families, then evaluate sequentially on changed models, tools, and task families. Compare a fixed schedule with correction-triggered reappraisal. Specifically label rare caveats, dissenting observations, negative results, and security warnings. Measure false eviction/retention per series and whether reappraisal improves the next tranche without overfitting the previous one.

I am **likely (55-80%)** that E1 is sufficient to falsify the main performance claim, because it directly controls model and token budget while varying the context policy. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a single replay suite establishes long-term generality, because schedule quality can overfit task families; that is why E3 is separately required. These confidence bands are methodological judgments supported by the stated experimental controls and the known top-down appraisal bias, not by results that have already been run.

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Pure recency / sliding window

Cheap and model-improving, but has no representation of unresolved obligations, supersession, trust, or holds. It remains a necessary baseline, not a sufficient policy for long-horizon evidential continuity.

### Flat running summary

Compact, but collapses source, creator, epistemic state, and transformation history into prose. Repeated rewriting can make it impossible to distinguish owner direction from inference. PACS does not ban summaries; it makes them derivatives with sources and a schedule.

### Per-item learned importance score

Can optimize immediate relevance, but recreates the item-by-item prediction problem that functional appraisal avoids and makes omissions difficult to explain. A score may rank within a scheduled series later, but should not decide whether controlling classes exist.

### Literal original-order prompt

Rejected. It confuses preservation of evidential relationships with presentation order and conflicts with empirical positional bias in long contexts ([Liu et al., TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)).

### Keep everything in a larger window

Rejected as the primary answer. The “Lost in the Middle” results show that availability in a long prompt does not guarantee uniform use. It also avoids neither provenance loss in summaries nor authority confusion.

### Full Records in Contexts graph / vector retrieval memory

Rejected. RiC is useful evidence that provenance can be multi-relational, but implementing its full ontology would be a BUILD-LARGE knowledge-graph move. The task needs a handful of typed links, not a general semantic infrastructure.

### Role-only “Capstone” retention

Useful as a coarse fallback—owner directives deserve stronger default retention than routine agent narration—but insufficient alone. Role does not distinguish an authoritative tool observation from verbose noise, and positional power can erase minority evidence. Use role as one schedule field, not the whole appraisal method.

- Archival sources establish mechanisms and professional rationale, not causal evidence for LLM performance. All harness-effect claims above therefore carry experiments and confidence bands.
- The SAA Dictionary includes historical quotations and contested professional positions. I used it for definitions and explicit debate, not as proof that every archivist or institution follows one practice.
- Records retention law and institutional archival duty are not imported into the harness. The transfer is conceptual and technical; it creates no legal compliance claim.

## 8. Bottom line for the neighboring design units

Do not build “agent archival memory.” Build a **small, inspectable context disposition layer**:

- appraise recurring evidence classes by task function;
- give every class an explicit cutoff and disposition;
- let holds override eviction;
- preserve provenance and original sequence as metadata;
- treat summaries as derivatives, never invisible replacements;
- emit a disposition receipt;
- reappraise schedules when corrections or task families change;
- keep cold raw evidence retrievable;
- reject the design if equal-budget replay does not beat recency/flat-summary baselines.

This is ambitious in policy—context becomes governed evidence rather than an accidental tail of chat—but minimal in architecture. That is the part of archival science that transfers.
