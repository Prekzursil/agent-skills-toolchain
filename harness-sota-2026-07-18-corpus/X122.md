# X122 — Cross-domain borrow: forensic science → long-horizon context

## Executive judgment

The useful forensic-science borrow is a **context-control protocol**, not a generic analogy to keeping a crime scene clean. Its specific pieces are: (1) classify information by task relevance; (2) expose the decision-maker first to the primary evidence and only later to potentially biasing context, a procedure called Linear Sequential Unmasking–Expanded (LSU-E); (3) record the decision and confidence at every disclosure boundary; (4) use a context manager to separate access from analysis; and (5) test the resulting process with blind, case-like samples whose ground truth is known.

The transfer is **likely (55-80%) to improve the faithfulness and auditability of agent context selection**, because an agent, like a forensic examiner, makes path-dependent interpretations of ambiguous material, and both settings permit control over information order. The evidence is (a) forensic experts have reversed judgments on the same evidence under changed contextual information ([Dror, Charlton, and Péron, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2005.10.017)); (b) LSU-E explicitly controls order, relevance, objectivity, and biasing power ([Dror and Kukucka, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsisyn.2021.100161)); and (c) an OSAC bloodstain-pattern methodology operationalizes ordered access and documentation when order is violated ([OSAC 2022-S-0030, version 2.1](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/09/17/OSAC%202022-S-0030%20Standard%20Methodology%20in%20Bloodstain%20Pattern%20Analysis%20Version%202.1.pdf)). The range is deliberately broad because no cited study evaluates LLM context windows.

The transfer is **unlikely (20-45%) to identify the globally optimal tokens to evict by itself**, because forensic context management assumes evidence can remain available outside the examiner's immediate view, whereas context-window eviction may destroy or compress information and token utility changes as the task evolves. The evidence is the mechanism's own scope: LSU-E optimizes *sequence of exposure* using biasing power, objectivity, and relevance; it does not estimate future information value or lossy-summary error ([Dror and Kukucka, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsisyn.2021.100161)). Therefore this unit recommends LSU-E as the **control plane around retrieval, disclosure, summarization, and eviction**, not as a replacement for relevance retrieval or token budgeting.

The proposed harness change is an **Epistemic Context Gate** implemented as a small policy hook plus an append-only disclosure ledger, with blind replay tests in the existing evaluation bus. It does not require a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

## 1. What forensic science has actually solved

### 1.1 Cognitive contamination is managed by restricting *when*, not merely *whether*, information is seen

Forensic interpretation often combines ambiguous primary material (a latent print, questioned handwriting, a scene) with reference material and case narrative. The risk is not limited to intentional prejudice. Earlier information can anchor perception, generate hypotheses, and steer attention toward confirming features. In a field study, fingerprint experts were unknowingly shown prints on which they had previously made identification decisions, but were given a context suggesting exclusion; most made a different judgment ([Dror, Charlton, and Péron, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2005.10.017)). A related re-examination study reports six changed decisions across 48 trials, with inconsistency concentrated in difficult decisions and trials containing corroborating or conflicting case information ([OJP abstract of Dror and Charlton, 2006](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/why-experts-make-errors)). These studies are small and discipline-specific; they demonstrate susceptibility, not a universal forensic error rate.

Original LSU addressed comparative examinations: inspect and document the questioned trace **before** seeing the known reference. This prevents the target from determining which features the examiner notices in the trace. LSU-E generalizes that rule beyond pairwise comparison: start with the raw evidence, form and record an initial impression, then disclose additional task-relevant information in an explicit order ([Dror and Kukucka, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsisyn.2021.100161)). LSU-E orders later information with three interacting criteria:

1. **Biasing power:** expose less biasing information before more biasing information.
2. **Objectivity:** expose more objective information before more subjective information.
3. **Relevance:** expose core task-relevant information before peripheral information, and omit task-irrelevant information.

At each stage the examiner documents what was disclosed and any resulting change. The older LSU proposal additionally ties scrutiny to confidence: changing a tentative initial judgment is less concerning than reversing a high-confidence judgment; a high-confidence reversal can trigger blind review ([OJP abstract of Dror et al., 2015](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/letter-editor-context-management-toolbox-linear-sequential)). This is important: the protocol does not pretend later evidence must never change a conclusion. It makes the *dependency* of the conclusion on that evidence visible.

This is not merely a laboratory suggestion. OSAC's proposed bloodstain-pattern methodology defines task-irrelevant information, requires the available source materials to be listed before analysis, prescribes an order of access, and requires out-of-order access to be documented and explained ([OSAC 2022-S-0030, version 2.1, §§3.12-4](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/09/17/OSAC%202022-S-0030%20Standard%20Methodology%20in%20Bloodstain%20Pattern%20Analysis%20Version%202.1.pdf)). The same document acknowledges a practical limit: some crime-scene context is unavoidable. That qualification matters to the agent translation; context control is a gradient, not a fantasy of a perfectly blank model.

### 1.2 The context-manager model separates the right to know from the need to know

The National Institute of Justice–funded context-management project describes a **case manager model**: the manager may see the full case, while the examiner receives only information needed for the current analytic task. It evaluates this alongside sequential unmasking and blind re-examination ([Thompson, 2018 final project report](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/252784.pdf); [OJP report record](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/developing-effective-methods-addressing-contextual-bias-forensic-science)). This is a capability-separation mechanism. It recognizes that information may be operationally necessary to the organization while being epistemically harmful or unnecessary to the person making a particular judgment.

The manager is not automatically unbiased. A human manager can misclassify relevance, and a context gate can encode its designer's assumptions. Forensic practice handles this incompletely through written protocols, disclosure logs, review, and testing; it does not prove that the gate is neutral. That limitation transfers directly.

### 1.3 Blind re-examination and blind proficiency testing test the *pipeline*, not the declared best behavior

A blind verifier repeats a key judgment without seeing the first examiner's conclusion or other biasing context. A blind proficiency test goes further: quality staff introduce a realistic, known-ground-truth case into ordinary workflow without telling the examiner it is a test. Declared tests can elicit special test-taking behavior. Blind tests can exercise evidence receipt, routing, analysis, and report release, and can reveal process errors or misconduct that isolated component tests cannot. These characteristics and practical implementation barriers are documented in an open-access review ([Mejia, Cuellar, and Salyards, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/)). Houston's forensic laboratory reports implementing blind quality-control cases across the laboratory pipeline and describes the difficulty of making test items indistinguishable from real submissions ([Hundl et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7317955/)).

Blind testing is not magic ground truth. Synthetic cases may be easier, cleaner, or recognizable; concealment can fail; a finite test set can itself be overfit. One latent-print blind-QC study examined 376 prints in 144 fictitious cases, found no false positives but other errors, and reported correct conclusions for 51.1% of submitted prints; the authors also treat print quality as a material covariate ([Neumann et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.forsciint.2021.110823)). That result is not an agent benchmark and should not be numerically transferred. Its transferable lesson is that a realistic hidden test can reveal failure modes that an aggregate pass/fail score obscures.

## 2. Concrete translation to finite-window agent context

### 2.1 Correspondence, with boundaries

| Forensic element | Agent-harness analogue | What does **not** transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Primary trace/evidence | User's current request; authoritative raw tool output; exact source excerpt; observed test failure | A tool result can be stale, malicious, or generated; “raw” does not mean true |
| Known reference | Existing implementation, candidate patch, expected answer, previous agent conclusion | Reference code may be essential immediately for a code-edit task |
| Case narrative | Earlier plans, summaries, issue labels, audit claims, owner hypotheses | Prior context can contain binding requirements, not merely bias |
| Task relevance | Information causally needed for the current decision | Relevance evolves during exploration and is not fully knowable ex ante |
| Sequential unmasking | Stage retrieval and disclose evidence before hypotheses and prior conclusions | A language model cannot unsee tokens already supplied in the same call |
| Examination notes | Immutable stage snapshots: claim, evidence IDs, confidence, unresolved alternatives | A model's stated confidence is not calibrated probability |
| Context manager | Deterministic hook/policy that assembles stage-specific context from typed items | The selector can introduce its own selection bias |
| Blind verifier | Independent replay without the original answer/summary | Model outputs are stochastic and independence may be only partial |
| Blind proficiency case | Hidden, naturalistic task with seeded facts and known scoring | Synthetic tasks may not reproduce long-horizon production work |

The deepest structural match is **path dependence under ambiguity**. An agent that sees “the bug is probably in authentication” before reading logs may search and compress around that hypothesis, just as an examiner can attend to features consistent with a target. Once the agent writes a summary, the hypothesis can become part of the apparent evidence available to future turns. This creates an information feedback loop: prior interpretation influences selection; selection influences summary; summary becomes future context.

It is **very likely (80-90%) that this feedback loop can occur in principle**, because autoregressive models condition every later token on earlier supplied tokens and harness summaries are later supplied as input. The evidence is the model architecture's conditioning relation plus the forensic empirical evidence that order changes expert judgments; the exact frequency and damage in this owner's workload are **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E1, the counterbalanced disclosure replay defined below**.

The key disanalogy is **conservation of evidence**. A laboratory can mask a case narrative while preserving it in the case file. A finite context system may evict an item, replace it with a lossy summary, or lose the retrieval handle. Thus, “mask now” is safe only if “retrieve later” remains true. It is **almost certain (90-99%) that permanent deletion cannot be justified merely by LSU-E**, because none of LSU-E's criteria estimates future need and deletion removes the possibility of later disclosure; the evidence is the mechanism's published definition and the logical irreversibility of deletion.

### 2.2 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: Epistemic Context Gate**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

The recommendation inherits **UNVERIFIED** because its benefit for LLM work has not been established; settle it with Experiments E1-E3 below.

Build a stateless context-assembly hook that consumes a small manifest for each candidate context item:

```text
item_id, source_uri, observed_at, content_hash
kind = primary_evidence | requirement | reference | hypothesis | prior_conclusion
authority = primary | secondary | generated
task_relevance = required | conditional | peripheral | excluded
biasing_power = low | medium | high
loss_policy = exact_pointer | exact_inline | summary_allowed
depends_on = [item_id...]
```

The hook does not store a new memory system. It orders and labels items already available through the harness and emits an append-only JSONL disclosure ledger. Exact evidence remains in its existing file/tool/session store with a stable pointer and hash. The policy has four stages:

1. **Evidence-first packet.** Supply the current user request, binding requirements, and the most objective primary evidence. Do not supply prior conclusions or speculative summaries unless required to locate evidence.
2. **Snapshot.** Before hypothesis-rich material is disclosed, record the agent's candidate explanations, evidence IDs, discriminating tests, uncertainty, and missing information. This is an observation checkpoint, not a commitment.
3. **Sequential disclosure.** Add reference implementation, earlier summaries, issue narratives, and other agent conclusions from lower-bias/higher-objectivity to higher-bias/lower-objectivity. At each boundary record which claims changed and which disclosed item caused the change.
4. **Eviction by epistemic role.** Under token pressure, evict duplicated prose and prior conclusions first; retain binding requirements and exact pointers/hashes to primary evidence. Summaries may replace narrative, but may not replace the only retrievable copy of a requirement, counterexample, or raw result. An evicted item remains addressable outside the window.

This policy treats **requirements separately from evidence**. A user instruction may be subjective but must not be delayed merely because it is “biasing.” In forensic work, task instructions delimit the examination; in agent work, owner instructions delimit authorized action. Conflating the two would make the borrow unsafe.

It is **likely (55-80%) that the gate can be implemented under roughly 200 lines if candidate items already have stable IDs and storage pointers**, because the required work is manifest validation, sorting, budget truncation, and JSONL logging. This premise is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E0: implement a throwaway hook against three representative session formats and count non-generated source lines plus adapters**. If stable item IDs do not exist, the recommendation remains BUILD-SMALL only if a thin adapter suffices; otherwise this unit does not justify BUILD-LARGE.

It is **likely (55-80%) that preserving exact pointers while evicting prose will reduce “summary became evidence” errors without materially increasing the active token budget**, because pointers are small and enable reinspection, while prose duplication consumes tokens. This is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E2, measuring unsupported-claim rate, retrieval success, and tokens per solved task with and without pointer retention**.

### 2.3 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: stage-boundary reversal alarm**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a schema for each stage snapshot with `claim`, `evidence_ids`, `confidence_band`, `alternatives`, and `changed_since_stage`. Configure the existing structured-output mechanism to request this compact record only at disclosure boundaries, not on every reasoning step. A deterministic comparison flags:

- a high-confidence conclusion that reverses immediately after exposure to a prior agent conclusion;
- a claim whose cited evidence IDs did not change while its polarity did;
- a new claim supported only by a summary or generated hypothesis;
- an eviction of the sole exact pointer supporting a live claim;
- a requirement silently disappearing between compactions.

The alarm does not block the reversal. Later evidence may correctly change the answer. It requests reinspection or blind replay when the dependency is epistemically suspicious, mirroring LSU's extra scrutiny for high-confidence revisions.

It is **likely (55-80%) that a boundary-only schema is less brittle than forcing structured output across every model response**, because it reduces schema frequency and constrains only a small audit record; this is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E3 by comparing schema-failure rate and task success for boundary-only, every-turn, and no-schema conditions**. This recommendation is intentionally CONFIG and not a new critic service.

### 2.4 Proposal — **UNVERIFIED: blind context proficiency suite**

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Create a replay evaluator that injects hidden, case-like context challenges into ordinary benchmark tasks. The runner knows the ground truth; the acting agent does not know which tasks are proficiency tests. Each item should vary one context hazard while keeping the underlying evidence constant:

- a plausible but false prior summary;
- an authoritative-sounding audit claim contradicted by raw output;
- a late-arriving binding requirement;
- two duplicated narratives competing with one terse counterexample;
- an obsolete tool result with a newer exact result;
- a compaction boundary across which a negation, numeric constraint, or exception must survive;
- a poisoned “expected fix” that points away from the diagnostic evidence.

Score the entire path: whether primary evidence was retrieved, whether the wrong item was promoted into a summary, whether the contradiction survived eviction, whether the final answer is correct, and whether the disclosure ledger identifies the influence. Do not reveal which cases are blind until after the run. Rotate templates and retain a sealed holdout so the harness cannot optimize to a fixed canary set.

It is **very likely (80-90%) that declared context tests alone overestimate production discipline**, based on forensic blind-testing literature showing that known tests invite special behavior and blind tests more closely exercise normal workflow ([Mejia, Cuellar, and Salyards, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552087/)). Whether hidden agent tests produce a meaningfully different estimate is **UNVERIFIED — settle with Experiment E4 by randomly assigning matched tasks to declared versus hidden labels and comparing error rates and tool/context behavior**.

## 3. What this would catch that ordinary relevance ranking misses

The following are target failure classes, not claims that this specific harness currently lacks defenses. Their current incidence is **UNVERIFIED — E1-E4 settle incidence using recorded sessions and blinded replays**.

1. **Hypothesis laundering.** A speculative earlier conclusion is compacted into declarative prose, later retrieved as if it were observed fact. A provenance type and evidence dependency expose that it is `prior_conclusion`, not `primary_evidence`.
2. **Reference-first anchoring.** The agent reads a proposed patch or expected answer before independently characterizing the failure, then notices only supporting facts. Evidence-first staging produces a counterfactual pre-reference snapshot.
3. **Context-induced reversal without new evidence.** The answer flips after seeing another agent's opinion even though no primary evidence changes. The stage diff makes this visible and can trigger blind replay.
4. **Semantic eviction of the minority fact.** A verbose consensus survives compaction while one terse contradiction disappears. Role-aware retention protects counterexamples and their exact pointers rather than scoring only lexical/query similarity.
5. **Requirement decay.** A long-horizon summary drops an exception, prohibition, numeric limit, or authorization boundary. Requirements receive a non-lossy retention policy independent of apparent topical relevance.
6. **Stale evidence supersession failure.** An old result remains salient after a new result arrives. `observed_at`, authority, hashes, and explicit supersession allow the assembler to disclose the newer primary observation first while retaining the audit trail.
7. **Evaluator theater.** The harness behaves carefully only on recognizable tests. Hidden naturalistic cases evaluate the normal routing and compaction pipeline.
8. **Selector contamination.** The context-selection process preferentially admits items that support its own prior summary. Counterbalanced replay tests the same evidence under different narrative orders; divergence is a measurable warning even when neither answer is known in advance.

Ordinary relevance retrieval can return highly relevant but biasing material. The forensic contribution is to ask a different question: not only “will this help?” but “what must be observed and frozen before this is allowed to influence interpretation?”

## 4. Experiments and decision thresholds

### E0 — feasibility spike

Run the proposed manifest/order/ledger logic on three representative long sessions. Pass if the core is under 200 non-generated lines, adapters do not require a daemon, and every in-window item can be traced to a stable source pointer. Fail the BUILD-SMALL proposal if source identity requires a new storage service.

### E1 — counterbalanced disclosure replay

Sample at least 100 completed tasks stratified by task type and context length. Construct two replays with identical evidence and requirements: evidence-first versus narrative/prior-answer-first. Randomize order and blind the scorer to condition. Measure final correctness, unsupported claims, tests selected, evidence opened, and claim reversals. Include a known-present order-sensitive synthetic control to prove the detector can detect an effect before interpreting a null result. This control plus condition-level output differences are mechanically independent signals; a single final-score comparison is insufficient.

Adopt staged disclosure only if it improves a preregistered primary outcome without worsening completion rate or token cost beyond an owner-selected bound. The magnitude is **roughly even (45-55%) to be large enough to matter**, because order effects are documented in humans but agent-specific magnitude is unknown; E1 settles it.

### E2 — lossy-eviction challenge

Seed tasks with one decisive counterexample, one exception-bearing requirement, duplicated narrative, and later distractors. Compare baseline compaction with role-aware retention plus exact pointers. Pass only if two independent signals agree: (1) the final output respects the seeded fact, and (2) the context ledger shows that its exact pointer remained retrievable. First verify the ledger detector on a known-present pointer.

### E3 — schema burden test

Compare boundary-only snapshots, every-turn structured snapshots, and no snapshots on matched tasks. Measure schema errors, retries, token overhead, task success, and reversal detection. Use raw parser outcomes and session-level behavior as independent signals.

### E4 — declared versus blind proficiency

Randomize matched context-contamination tasks to declared and hidden presentation. Use correctness and path-level evidence/provenance behavior as independent signals. Validate that the concealment detector can recognize a deliberately obvious test before claiming hidden tasks were indistinguishable. If agents infer hidden tests above chance, redesign cases; do not report the resulting score as blind performance.

## 5. Adversarial assessment of the analogy

### Where it holds

- **Controlled exposure exists in both systems.** A lab manager can stage a case file; a harness can stage retrieval and prompt assembly.
- **Judgments are path-dependent.** Forensic empirical studies show contextual effects, and an agent's later generation is formally conditioned on earlier context.
- **Primary evidence and interpretation are distinguishable.** Raw logs and user requirements can be provenance-typed separately from summaries and hypotheses, even though raw data are not infallible.
- **Normal-work testing matters.** Both systems can change behavior when a test is declared, so blind path-level evaluation is structurally appropriate.

### Where it fails or becomes dangerous

- **Forensic evidence is usually conserved; context may be destroyed.** Masking cannot justify deleting. The harness must retain stable external pointers before eviction.
- **Task relevance is less stable.** A fingerprint examiner has a comparatively fixed question; an exploratory agent may discover that yesterday's peripheral fact is tomorrow's root cause. Classification must permit promotion and retrieval.
- **The gate sees the information it filters.** A model-based context manager can itself be anchored. The minimal proposal therefore uses deterministic labels/order where possible and audits the selector with counterbalanced tests. It does not claim neutrality.
- **“Raw” tool output can be adversarial or wrong.** Prompt injection, stale state, partial logs, and flaky tools mean evidence-first is not evidence-naive. Authority, time, source, and corroboration still matter.
- **Independence is weaker for model replicas.** Two calls to the same model share training and may share systematic errors. Blind replay detects context sensitivity, not truth, unless ground truth or external verification exists.
- **Confidence reports are not calibrated.** They are change markers, not probabilities. The alarm should use them for triage, never as proof.
- **Sequential disclosure costs tokens and latency.** The protocol should trigger at material decision boundaries, not every turn. E3 measures the tradeoff.

These disanalogies rule out the strongest version of the borrow: a universal forensic-style quarantine that withholds all context until an agent independently analyzes “raw evidence.” Many tasks require prior requirements, schemas, or reference code to know what evidence to collect. The viable borrow is narrower: explicitly type context, preserve provenance, stage *biasing interpretations* after necessary evidence and requirements, record changes, and test the actual pipeline blindly.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

The proposals pass the stated anti-overfit gate conditionally:

- They ride existing hook/skill/MCP and evaluation buses.
- They add a sorter/validator/ledger and replay fixtures, not a second scheduler.
- They use existing artifact storage and stable pointers, not vector-DB or knowledge-graph memory.
- They compare snapshots deterministically, not with a trained critic.
- They emit local JSONL, not observability SaaS.
- They can be removed without migrating durable state.

It is **very likely (80-90%) that a deterministic gate plus blind fixtures is less vulnerable to model-generation overfit than a new learned context curator**, because its rules are inspectable, model-independent, and tested on sealed permutations; the evidence is architectural rather than an agent trial, so **UNVERIFIED — E1 and E4 must compare it against both the baseline and any model-based curator before adoption**.

Do **not** build a context daemon, universal memory service, or second agent runtime from this unit's evidence. If E0 shows stable pointers and item typing are unavailable, the result is a gap for another design unit, not permission to escalate to BUILD-LARGE. If E1 shows no material order effect while its positive control succeeds, retire staged disclosure and keep only any independently beneficial provenance/requirement-retention behavior demonstrated by E2.

## 7. Gaps and evidence limits

- No cited source evaluates LSU-E in an LLM agent or finite context window. Every effectiveness claim for the translation is therefore marked **UNVERIFIED** with a settling experiment inline.
- The cited fingerprint context studies are small and do not yield a general forensic effect size. This unit uses them as existence proofs, not rate estimates.
- LSU-E is a proposed general approach with standards uptake in at least the cited OSAC methodology; that does not establish universal adoption or outcome improvement across forensic disciplines.
- Blind proficiency evidence supports ecological validity and whole-pipeline testing, but concealment and representativeness remain difficult. The agent suite must demonstrate concealment rather than assume it.
- This ideation unit intentionally did not inspect the local repository or subject tree. Consequently it makes no claim that stable context item IDs, a disclosure hook, exact external pointers, or a replay facility are present or absent. A repository-grounding unit must establish each status with a validated known-present control and two mechanically independent signals before implementation planning.
- The appropriate token/latency threshold is an owner value judgment and is not inferred here.

## 8. Bottom line

The forensic borrow transfers if it is stated precisely: **control the provenance and order of epistemically different information, preserve an immutable pre-influence snapshot, and test the real pipeline with hidden known-ground-truth cases**. It does not transfer as a standalone eviction algorithm. The smallest defensible build is a typed, evidence-first context gate with exact-pointer retention, stage-boundary diffs, and blind replay fixtures. Its benefit is **likely (55-80%) but UNVERIFIED — the preregistered E1-E4 experiments above determine adoption**, with the probability grounded in the structural match, human forensic context-effect evidence, operational context-management standards, and blind-testing practice.
