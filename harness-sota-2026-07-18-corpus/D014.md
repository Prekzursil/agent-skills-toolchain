# D014 — HARNESS-BENCH metric: groundedness

## Executive decision

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** This is the tag for the metric's proposed deterministic resolver/validator/aggregator; native agents and configuration supply the semantic grading and task definitions, as decomposed in §3.2.

Define **groundedness** at the atomic-claim level:

> Of the externally verifiable claims the agent itself asserts in the scored deliverable, what fraction has at least one citation that (a) resolves to the cited evidence as it existed during the run and (b) actually supports the whole atomic claim?

The primary per-artifact score is

\[
G(a)=\frac{\sum_{c\in C_a} y_c}{|C_a|},\qquad
y_c=\mathbf 1[\operatorname{eligible}(c)\land \operatorname{resolvable}(c)\land \operatorname{supported}(c)]
\]

where `C_a` is the frozen set of eligible atomic claims extracted from artifact `a`. If `|C_a| = 0`, `G(a) = N/A`, **not 1.0**. An uncited, unresolvable, contradicted, only partially supported, or grader-error claim has `y_c = 0`. For the special claim families `absent`, `broken`, and `done`, `supported(c)` additionally requires two mechanically independent signals and a successful known-positive control for any detector used to infer absence. This imports the owner's mandatory evidence rule into the numerator rather than leaving it as prose.

This is deliberately a **ratio, never a quantity reward**. The leaderboard aggregate is the unweighted macro-mean of per-task `G(a)` over the same benchmark tasks and trial count; it is not pooled by claims, bytes, words, tokens, citations, tool calls, or KB. A concise verification agent with two supported claims scores `2/2 = 1.0`, exactly as a verbose agent with twenty supported claims scores `20/20 = 1.0`. The concise agent is not pushed downward merely because it emits less. The denominator and raw counts remain visible for uncertainty and audit, but never become score bonuses.

Groundedness does **not** measure whether the answer contains all important facts, whether the sources are authoritative, whether a citation is needlessly attached, whether the task was completed, or whether prose is good. Those are separate diagnostics. Anthropic's current research-agent guidance likewise separates groundedness, coverage, and source quality rather than collapsing them into one metric ([Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). ALCE also separates answer correctness and citation quality ([Gao et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)).

**Recommendation confidence:** very likely (80-90%) that this definition will rank genuinely evidence-backed research outputs better than citation-count or KB/token proxies, because FActScore and SAFE both score decomposed factual units rather than output volume, ALCE evaluates citation support, and the local harness already exposes final output plus evidence receipts. This remains **UNVERIFIED for this owner's task distribution; the settling experiment is the 40-artifact blinded calibration described below**. The claim does not extend to task correctness outside factual/research assertions.

## 2. Operational specification

### 2.1 Scoring unit: an eligible atomic claim

An **eligible claim** is the smallest proposition in the scored, agent-authored deliverable that an external observer could in principle mark true, false, or insufficient from evidence. Include:

- numeric, date, version, identity, existence, state, comparison, causal, capability, and performance assertions;
- claims about current files, code behavior, command results, tool availability, and produced artifacts;
- factual premises used to justify a recommendation;
- asserted `absent`, `broken`, `fixed`, `complete`, `done`, `green`, or equivalent claims;
- forward-looking feasibility claims when stated as factual predictions. Their cited evidence must support the premise and the prediction must carry the required confidence band; confidence language does not itself make the claim supported.

Exclude only:

- the user's statements when clearly attributed as requirements rather than adopted as facts;
- direct quotations, provided the surrounding text does not assert the quote is true;
- pure preferences, commands, questions, section labels, and normative recommendations such as “adopt X”;
- code and data literals presented as examples rather than claims about execution;
- explicitly labeled hypotheses or `UNVERIFIED` gaps whose sentence makes no positive factual assertion beyond “this remains unverified.” Any factual reason attached to the gap remains eligible.

Atomization is semantic, not sentence counting. “The hook exists, is enabled, and fired” becomes three claims. “Tests passed on Python 3.12” becomes at least the outcome and environment claims if the environment matters. A citation must support the **whole atom**; partial entailment is zero.

FActScore established the useful core abstraction: decompose long-form output into atomic facts and compute the percentage supported by a reliable source ([Min et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)). SAFE similarly decomposes answers into individual facts before search-based verification ([Wei et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/937ae0e83eb08d2cb8627fe1def8c751-Abstract-Conference.html)). ALiiCE shows that sentence-only attachment can be too coarse and evaluates citation recall/precision over finer claim positions ([Xu et al., NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.23/)). These are method precedents, not evidence that a particular local atomizer is accurate.

### 2.2 Citation types and resolvability

A citation is **resolvable** only if the evaluator can recover the exact evidence object used for scoring and bind it to the claim. Accepted types:

1. **Repository/local source:** absolute or workspace-relative path plus line, symbol, or bounded range; record file SHA-256 and, when available, git revision. Resolution requires that the locator exists in the frozen snapshot and the selected passage contains the cited evidence.
2. **Web source:** canonical URL plus an evaluation-time snapshot or archived response, access timestamp, content hash, and selected passage. A URL returning 200 without the claimed passage is not resolved. A search-result snippet is not a source.
3. **Tool/command evidence:** provider-neutral event or typed receipt ID plus tool name, normalized arguments hash, result hash, status, timestamp, cwd/revision, and selected output passage. Exit zero alone supports only successful process termination, not the claimed postcondition.
4. **Dataset/API record:** endpoint or dataset identifier, query/record key, version or retrieval timestamp, payload hash, and selected fields.
5. **User-provided pinned input:** artifact ID/hash and exact passage. “The user said so” may resolve the requirement, but does not prove an external-world fact unless the benchmark explicitly treats the user as the authoritative source.

Bare source names, home pages, dead links without a run snapshot, mutable files without a hash/revision, unlocatable “command output,” and citations whose targets are outside the scorer's authorized scope are unresolved. Resolver status is one of `resolved`, `missing`, `ambiguous`, `unauthorized`, `content_changed`, `parse_error`, or `timeout`; only `resolved` proceeds to support judging.

### 2.3 Support verdict

For each claim `c`, collect the passages from its resolved citations and assign exactly one verdict:

- `supported`: the cited evidence entails the entire claim in its stated scope, time, and modality;
- `contradicted`: evidence directly conflicts;
- `insufficient`: relevant evidence exists but does not establish the whole claim;
- `unresolved`: no citation resolves;
- `grader_error`: atomization, retrieval, parsing, or judging did not complete.

Only `supported` maps to `y_c = 1`; every other verdict maps to zero. This conservative failure semantics prevents infrastructure failure from inflating the score. Reports must retain error rates so a scorer outage cannot masquerade as an agent regression.

Use deterministic checks first: exact file/hash/field/value, test receipt, schema validation, and timestamp/revision comparisons. Use a fresh, blinded model grader only for semantic entailment that deterministic logic cannot decide. The grader receives the atom, selected passages, task time, and no producer identity or proposed answer score. It must return a structured verdict plus passage spans and rationale. The producer never self-scores.

For `absent`, `broken`, and `done`, one citation is never enough even if semantically persuasive:

- require two mechanically independent signals (different mechanisms or data fields, not a repeated command);
- for absence, require the detector to find a known-present control in the same run;
- for done, require both a direct artifact/state postcondition and an independent behavioral/verification receipt appropriate to the task;
- retain both evidence classes in the receipt so independence can be audited.

This is stricter than open-domain FActScore by design because the owner's live defect log shows repeated false absence conclusions from unvalidated detectors (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`).

### 2.4 Exact aggregation

For artifact `a` with eligible claims `C_a`:

```text
claim_pass(c) = 1 if:
  at least one attached citation resolves,
  the resolved evidence supports the whole atomic claim,
  and special two-signal/control rules pass when applicable;
else 0

groundedness(a) = sum(claim_pass(c) for c in C_a) / len(C_a)
```

For agent/harness variant `v`, evaluated on the same tasks `T` and `k` trials:

\[
G(v)=\frac{1}{|T'|}\sum_{t\in T'}\left(\frac{1}{k_t}\sum_{r=1}^{k_t}G(a_{t,r})\right)
\]

where `T'` contains tasks with at least one eligible claim in every compared variant/trial. A zero-claim artifact is `N/A` and triggers a coverage diagnostic; it is not silently dropped when comparing systems. Report task exclusions explicitly.

Always report:

```text
groundedness = 0.875
supported_claims = 14
eligible_claims = 16
zero_claim_artifacts = 0
unresolved = 1
insufficient = 1
contradicted = 0
grader_error = 0
95% CI = task-cluster bootstrap interval
```

Do not report KB/token, claims/token, citation count, longest report, total verified facts, or evidence bytes as quality scores. Do not multiply groundedness by output length. Raw counts establish sample size only.

### 2.5 Separate companion diagnostics — never blended into `G`

The following close obvious loopholes without changing what groundedness means:

- **Required-fact coverage:** importance-weighted fraction of a task-specific reference checklist covered. This detects strategic silence. The recent importance-aware recall proposal makes the same precision/recall distinction, but it is a 2026 preprint and not local validation ([Jafari et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03141)).
- **Citation hygiene:** fraction of all attached citations that both resolve and support some nearby atom. This detects citation dumping. It is not the groundedness numerator.
- **Source quality:** rubric over authority, primary-vs-secondary status, currency, and conflict of interest. A perfectly supported claim from a weak source may have `G=1` and poor source quality; that is useful diagnostic separation.
- **Outcome/task success:** executable end state, not prose. Local typed receipt logic already maps completion families to fresh verification evidence (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:2092-2118`).
- **Calibration:** supported/unsupported accuracy of the scorer against human labels, plus claim-boundary agreement. This measures the metric, not the agent.

An agent is benchmark-eligible only if it attempts the same required task and does not return `N/A`; however, coverage is displayed beside groundedness rather than multiplied into it. Thus the owner's terse verification agent is not penalized for low KB/token, while an empty answer cannot win.

## 4. Failure modes and required behavior

| Failure mode | How it biases `G` | Required handling |
|---|---|---|
| Atomizer merges several propositions | One partially supported sentence may pass or fail as a block | Split to minimal truth-conditional units; retain source spans; audit boundary agreement |
| Atomizer omits difficult claims | Inflates score | Human-labeled claim inventory on calibration set; report extraction recall |
| Atomizer treats quoted/user text as agent assertion | Deflates score and recreates use-vs-mention defect | Role/quote-aware exclusions; adversarial prose fixtures based on the documented defect (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-50`) |
| Citation parser accepts a URL/path syntactically | Inflates resolution | Require content snapshot/hash and selected passage, not syntax or HTTP status |
| Mutable web page or local file changes | Non-reproducible verdict | Score frozen snapshot; retain access time/hash/revision |
| Search snippet supports claim but source does not | Inflates score | Snippets cannot resolve; open and snapshot underlying source |
| One citation supports only half a compound claim | Inflates score | Atomic split; whole-atom entailment required |
| Citation is authoritative but irrelevant | Inflates score | Relevance/entailment verdict independent of source reputation |
| Citation is relevant but low quality | `G` can be high while research is weak | Separate source-quality diagnostic; do not rewrite groundedness definition |
| Contradictory sources | Arbitrary pass | Preserve contradictions; mark unsupported unless the claim accurately represents uncertainty or resolves conflict |
| Model grader agrees with producer | Correlated error inflates score | Blind producer identity; use fresh context; calibrate against humans; deterministic checks outrank model verdict |
| Resolver/grader outage | Mass false regression | `grader_error=0` in numerator, but block baseline comparison and surface scorer-health failure |
| Agent cites enormous documents | Grader finds support agent never located | Require bounded passage/span associated with citation; cap context |
| Agent outputs no claims | Vacuous 100% | `N/A`, zero-claim diagnostic, benchmark ineligible |
| Agent omits hard but required facts | High `G`, incomplete answer | Separate required-fact coverage; never reward volume |
| Agent emits many trivial claims | Easy high ratio and apparent diligence | Equal task macro-average; coverage checklist emphasizes task-important facts; show claim inventory but no claim-count reward |
| Unsupported extra citations | At least one good citation can still pass a claim | Separate citation-hygiene precision; show all citation verdicts |
| `done` supported only by exit zero | False completion | Require independent postcondition plus behavioral/verification receipt |
| Absence inferred from detector returning zero | False absence | Require known-positive control and second independent signal |
| Scorer prompt/model changes | Baseline drift | Version/prompt hashes; no cross-version regression comparison without bridge calibration |
| User interrupt/API failure omits Stop | Selection bias | Eval runner owns expected-trial manifest; missing artifacts are missing trials, not N/A |

## 6. Goodhart analysis

Optimizing raw groundedness alone perversely encourages:

1. **Strategic silence:** omit uncertain but important facts, producing a perfect ratio. Countermeasure: `N/A` for no claims and separate required-fact coverage. Never fold word count into `G`.
2. **Triviality:** state only easy, low-value claims. Countermeasure: task-specific required facts and difficulty strata, reported beside `G`.
3. **Citation carpeting:** attach many sources to every sentence, hoping one entails it. Countermeasure: bounded passage selection and separate citation-hygiene precision.
4. **Claim fragmentation:** split one idea into many tiny, easy atoms to dilute one unsupported core claim. Countermeasure: frozen atomizer, source spans, human boundary calibration, and task macro-averaging; do not reward atom count.
5. **Claim fusion:** merge a risky claim with a supported one to evade detection. Countermeasure: minimal truth-conditional atomization and whole-atom support.
6. **Safe-source monoculture:** cite an authoritative source even when stale or not primary. Countermeasure: separate source-quality/currentness rubric and task-time snapshots.
7. **Evaluator targeting:** write in forms the atomizer ignores or cite passages likely to fool the support grader. Countermeasure: held-out adversarial fixtures, version rotation only with bridge calibration, and periodic human audits.
8. **Evidence laundering:** cite another agent's claim rather than the underlying source. Countermeasure: citations resolve to primary evidence or explicitly record the intermediate claim as insufficient.
9. **Over-verification:** spend excessive latency/tokens proving low-stakes facts. Countermeasure: groundedness is evaluated on fixed outputs/tasks and never divided by or rewarded for tool use; cost/latency remain separate Pareto axes.
10. **Suppression of useful uncertainty:** avoid saying what is unknown because unsupported claims score zero. Countermeasure: correctly scoped `UNVERIFIED` gaps are excluded when they assert no positive fact; unsupported factual premises inside them still count.

The most important anti-Goodhart decision is **not combining groundedness with volume**. A score such as supported claims per KB/token would rank verbosity and compression strategy, not evidential reliability. A terse verifier that returns one decisive, fully supported claim must retain `G=1.0`; a long report earns no bonus for producing more scoreable surface area. Coverage answers “did it say enough?” Groundedness answers “was what it said supported?” Keeping those axes separate makes the trade-off visible instead of hiding it in an arbitrary scalar.

The semantic verifier is an ordinary fresh-context native agent used only in evaluation; deterministic receipts remain authoritative.

Do **not** turn groundedness into a live universal Stop blocker.
