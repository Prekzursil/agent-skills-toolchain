# I06 — Initiative / anticipation calibration for RE and security work

## Executive answer

The harness should not interrupt because work is “security,” “reverse engineering,” or dual-use. Those labels describe a domain, not a decision. It should interrupt only when the **next concrete action** is about to cross a consequential boundary and the boundary is supported by external evidence. Everything else should be made safe automatically, escrowed as a durable thread, or batched to the next natural breakpoint.

The operational rule I recommend is:

> **Interrupt now only if delay until the next task breakpoint can materially worsen an authorization, external-impact, irreversibility, secret-exposure, or load-bearing-evidence conflict; otherwise take a reversible safe step, record the risk with evidence and expiry, and surface it once at the breakpoint. Never warn from dual-use semantics alone.**

This is **very likely (80-90%)** better calibrated than generic proactive warnings because (a) the workspace's correction detector is so recall-biased that its unfiltered control produced 41/41 false positives, (b) the owner repeatedly asks the agent to continue rather than stop, (c) prior local regex guards were disabled after blocking legitimate research, and (d) attention-sensitive alerting research explicitly treats interruption as a cost to balance against the cost of delay. The exact precision and owner-focus effect are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with the shadow-replay and prospective A/B experiment in Finding 8**.

The eight findings below lead with inferred-surprise items. All proposals obey the anti-overfit gate: they ride the existing dispatcher/hook/ledger/AskUserQuestion buses; none introduces another orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. None adds a seventh hook.

## Decision policy: what warrants interruption and what is noise

Before the findings, this is the concrete policy they implement.

| Class | Required evidence | Timing | Agent behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interrupt now** | Two mechanically independent external signals, or one terminal tool result plus an explicit owner-authored scope rule, show that the **next action** crosses authorization/target identity, creates an externally visible effect, exposes a secret, is hard to recover, or applies a disputed load-bearing RE anchor. Waiting to the next breakpoint would materially increase harm. | Immediately before the boundary-crossing tool call. | Preserve the pending action as a risk thread; ask one discriminating question with a safe default. Never execute the crossing action until answered. |
| **Surface at breakpoint** | External evidence shows a costly but recoverable risk, a contradiction that does not affect the next action, an expiring assumption, or a cheaper alternative. | After current atomic step/test/tool batch; once per dedupe key. | Finish the safe atomic step, include one compact evidence packet, and default to the reversible path if the owner does not answer. |
| **Just act safely; report in digest if useful** | A reversible containment exists with equal goal progress: snapshot, read-only inspection, dry-run, offline copy, narrower target, or independent verifier. | No interruption. | Take the containment action, verify its result externally, append disposition to the ledger, continue. |
| **Noise; suppress** | Domain label alone; generic “this could be dual-use”; a single heuristic match; ordinary tool errors; risks already mitigated; speculative future harm with no imminent action; repeated alert with no new evidence; style/best-practice advice unrelated to the current decision. | Never as an alert. | Optionally count in shadow telemetry; do not consume owner attention. |

This policy makes the owner the authority on real boundary choices without making them the harness's routine verifier. NIST defines risk as likelihood combined with impact and emphasizes that risk tolerance is context-specific; highest contextual risks receive the most urgent treatment ([NIST AI RMF, “Framing Risk”](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/1-sec-risk/)). Horvitz's mixed-initiative work likewise calls for reasoning over uncertain goals, attention, costs and benefits instead of merely making proactive guesses ([Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/)).

## Finding 1 — Risk escrow: defer the warning, never defer the thread — UNVERIFIED (settle with 30-session prospective replay plus forced compact/resume tests)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Add a typed `risk` event and `open_risks` state projection to the existing event ledger, with decision-boundary scheduling. Fold it into the existing shared dispatcher and SessionStart replay; add no hook.

**What it is.** A detected risk becomes a durable record before the agent decides whether to speak. Minimal schema:

```json
{
  "kind": "risk",
  "risk_id": "sha256(project|target|boundary|evidence)",
  "claim": "live write target may not be the inspected process",
  "boundary": "before next memory-write call",
  "severity": 4,
  "imminence": 5,
  "reversibility": 1,
  "evidence": [
    {"type": "process-list", "ref": "tool_result_hash"},
    {"type": "module-identity", "ref": "artifact_hash"}
  ],
  "status": "open",
  "surface_after": "current_atomic_step",
  "expires_after": "target/process change",
  "safe_default": "re-enumerate and remain read-only"
}
```

`risk_id` deduplicates repeats. Status is one of `open`, `mitigated`, `accepted`, `dismissed_noise`, `expired`, `superseded`. Every disposition is append-only. At Stop, an existing dispatcher module may emit at most one due risk; on compact/resume, the existing replay shows open risks before recent notes. A critical risk due **before the next tool call** is rendered by the already-running PreToolUse dispatcher, so the implementation consumes no new hook slot.

**Why inferred.** The owner’s repeated “why did you stop” signal says unnecessary pauses are costly ([corpus.json:10421-10426](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10421-L10426)); “ask the questions again since it got interrupted” shows that interrupting can itself lose a thread ([corpus.json:10620-10622](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10620-L10622)). The existing ledger was deliberately built to replay ground truth across compaction ([sessionstart-replay.py:2-7](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/sessionstart-replay.py#L2-L7)), but its typed kinds do not include risk ([ledger.py:61-65](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py#L61-L65)). The inferred surprise is that **the best proactive warning system begins as a persistence system, not a notification system**.

**How to build.** Extend `KINDS`, state initialization and append projection in `event-ledger/ledger.py`; add `--kind risk`, `--boundary`, `--expires-after`, `--safe-default`, `--evidence-ref` to `note.py`; render only due open risks in `sessionstart-replay.py`; implement `risk_due(event)` as a shared-dispatcher module. Wrap all parsing, persistence and rendering in `try/except` and return allow/exit 0 on error, matching the ledger's current fail-open style ([ledger.py:27-32](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py#L27-L32)). Cap replay at three open risks and collapse the rest to a count to protect prompt focus. Do not write risk prose from the model alone: the event must contain one external evidence reference to exist and two to become interrupt-eligible.

**External signal that verifies it.** Unit tests use a temporary `EVENT_LEDGER_ROOT`, append a risk, kill/reload the process, and assert exact replay after `compact`, `clear`, and `resume`; a Git HEAD change plus a terminal tool-result hash provide independent signals. A forced detector-known-positive test appends a known `risk` and a known `failure`, proving the renderer finds both before testing absence. Prospective verification is owner behavior: no risk is considered successfully surfaced until the ledger records an owner selection or a later external tool result proves the safe default was taken.

**SOTA citation.** Attention-sensitive alerting explicitly balances the cost of delaying information against the cost of interrupting the user ([Horvitz, Jacobs & Hovel, UAI 1999](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/attention-sensitive-alerting/)). Oasis reports that aligning notifications with the perceptual structure of tasks reduces interruption costs ([Iqbal & Bailey, TOCHI 2010](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=161181)). Microsoft’s human-AI guidelines call for remembering recent interactions and supporting efficient dismissal and correction ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)).

**Expected effect.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent discussed risks from disappearing across compaction because the existing ledger already replays persisted failures and validates live HEAD; the remaining uncertainty is implementation correctness under concurrent sessions. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce disruptive repeats because dedupe and status are deterministic, but the prospective experiment must measure whether the chosen boundaries match the owner's felt focus.

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it for recording/deferral; confirm-first only when the risk reaches an interrupt-now boundary.

## Finding 2 — Operational-envelope crossing, not “dual use,” is the confirm trigger — UNVERIFIED (settle with labeled replay of 200 RE/security tool transitions)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Add a project/session `operational_envelope` object to the existing goal/ledger configuration and a narrow policy rule to the current dispatcher and shared behavior contract. This extends the existing lifecycle bus; it is not a new guard framework.

**What it is.** The envelope describes what the owner has already authorized along five observable axes:

```json
{
  "targets": ["local process: game.exe", "workspace binaries"],
  "modes": ["static-read", "dynamic-read", "offline-write"],
  "external_effects": ["none"],
  "data_classes": ["owner-provided binaries", "public docs"],
  "destructive_ceiling": "reflog/file-checkpoint recoverable",
  "expires_on": ["process identity change", "host change", "credential context change"]
}
```

The agent infers this from the explicit task and tool state without asking if the envelope is unambiguous. It interrupts only when the **next proposed tool call** exceeds an axis: local to third-party host, static read to runtime write, offline artifact to live service, public/owner data to credentials or unrelated personal data, recoverable edit to non-recoverable effect. Merely mentioning malware, bypasses, memory manipulation, exploit techniques, disassembly, or HackTricks never crosses the envelope.

**Why inferred.** The corpus shows the owner explicitly wants thorough RE and multiple disassemblers ([corpus.json:10375-10382](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10375-L10382)), while a local guard comment records that credential-read blocking interfered with legitimate inspection of the owner's own config and research workflows ([modules.py:50-56](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py#L50-L56)). Another comment records false positives from commands that merely contained destructive tokens and therefore made the lifecycle regex opt-in ([modules.py:65-74](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py#L65-L74)). These independent signals imply that lexical suspicion is noise; observable scope transition is the valuable event.

**How to build.** Add an optional envelope to the ledger goal state. On UserPromptSubmit, populate only fields explicitly entailed by the owner prompt or externally observed environment; unknown fields remain `unknown`, never guessed permissively. On PreToolUse, the current dispatcher maps tool name and arguments to a transition tuple. An envelope crossing is not enough to interrupt: require (1) a parsed next action, (2) a target/identity observation from a different tool or environment field, and (3) no existing owner authorization covering that tuple. Ask one choice: “Stay inside envelope (recommended)” versus “Authorize this one transition”; an optional durable widening must state its consequence. The current rule “never ask mid-task” should receive one explicit exception: “except immediately before an externally evidenced operational-envelope crossing.”

No content classifier decides whether the request is good or bad. This avoids trying to encode genuine dual-use judgment as keywords. The envelope controls actions, not knowledge.

**External signal that verifies it.** Replay 200 real tool transitions with owner labels `within`, `crossing-confirm`, `crossing-deny`. Require two sources for target identity—for example process enumeration plus module hash, repository remote plus authenticated account, hostname plus tool endpoint. A transition is a true positive only if the next tool arguments exceed the labeled envelope; a keyword hit without a transition is a mandatory true negative. Then run prospective shadow mode for 30 sessions and compare shadow decisions with actual owner stops/redirects.

**SOTA citation.** NIST states that risk tolerance is contextual and use-case-specific rather than universal ([NIST AI RMF, risk tolerance](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/1-sec-risk/)). The 18 human-AI guidelines include scoping services when in doubt, making correction easy, and adapting cautiously over time ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)).

**Expected effect.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to suppress domain-label false alarms because the trigger contains no domain-label predicate; that follows mechanically from the design. It is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve genuine dual-use autonomy while catching real scope changes; evidence is the workspace's false-positive history and NIST's context-specific risk framing, but the transition mapping remains UNVERIFIED until replay-labeled.

**Value × effort:** transformative × low.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it inside the envelope; confirm-first on a verified crossing.

## Finding 3 — Automatic containment before conversation — UNVERIFIED (settle with paired live/offline task trials and artifact equality checks)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Add a “contain first” action table to the current dispatcher policy: if a reversible, scope-preserving action makes equal progress, take it and do not interrupt.

**What it is.** Before raising a non-imminent risk, choose the strongest available containment that preserves the goal:

1. read-only inspection before write;
2. snapshot/checkpoint before mutation;
3. dry-run or query before external change;
4. offline copy/replay harness before live target;
5. narrower target set before broad enumeration;
6. disposable credential-free/sandbox context before authenticated context;
7. two independent evidence tools before acting on a load-bearing anchor.

Containment must produce a terminal external signal. “I will be careful” is not containment. Examples are a checkpoint ID, artifact hash, dry-run diff, process/module identity pair, test result, or server acknowledgement. If containment succeeds, continue and mention it only in the next normal progress update. If it fails and the boundary is imminent, escrow then interrupt.

**Why inferred.** The owner asks for offline-first/live-like verification rather than mock-only work in a raw sample ([corpus.json:12256-12260](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L12256-L12260)) and repeatedly asks autonomous work to continue ([corpus.json:12251-12254](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L12251-L12254)). The existing behavior contract already reserves blocking for dangerous/irreversible actions and injection for everything else ([AGENTS.md:64-70](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md#L64-L70)). Containment operationalizes that preference without re-proposing edit checkpoints or rollback: those are existing mechanisms to invoke, not new components.

**How to build.** Add an ordered `safe_alternatives` table keyed by tool/action class to shared configuration. The dispatcher injects at most one internal instruction, not a user-facing message: `TAKE_SAFE_DEFAULT:<action>; VERIFY_WITH:<signal>`. The model calls the existing checkpoint/read/dry-run/inspection mechanism. Only a terminal tool result can mark the risk `mitigated`. If no safe alternative exists, normal envelope logic applies. Fail-open means a lookup/parser failure returns no policy action and exit 0; it never invents a successful containment.

**External signal that verifies it.** Compare the contained artifact against the intended live artifact using a different tool: hash/diff, replay result versus live read-only state, or checkpoint restoration test. For every automatically mitigated risk, require a stored tool-result hash and later state observation. In evaluation, paired tasks must measure goal completion, owner interruptions and unintended external effects.

**SOTA citation.** Horvitz's mixed-initiative principles emphasize significant value-added automation and explicit cost-benefit reasoning under uncertainty ([Horvitz, CHI 1999](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/chi99horvitz.pdf)). A 2025 preregistered proactive-AI study reports that anticipatory help can feel threatening and reduce adoption, especially when unsolicited; it distinguishes offering help from acting automatically ([Harari & Amir, 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09309)). The latter is a recent preprint, so its generalization to this expert owner is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the paired task trial above**.

**Expected effect.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce unnecessary confirmations while maintaining safety because it advances only through externally verified reversible actions; the evidence is the owner's continuation preference and mixed-initiative cost-benefit principle. Whether it improves elapsed task time is **roughly even (45-55%)** because extra snapshots and cross-checks can cost time; paired trials must settle that.

**Value × effort:** high × low.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it.

## Finding 4 — Disagreement, not mere tool use, should trip RE-anchor escalation — UNVERIFIED (settle with seeded wrong-anchor and cross-tool-disagreement tests)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Fold evidence attestations and a disagreement path into the existing `truth-verify` hook. This **replaces its current “any matching RE tool ran” verifier condition**; it does not add a hook.

**What it is.** Current `truth-verify` detects an unhedged address/offset and considers the turn verified when any matching RE tool, binary read, or shell inspection ran ([truth-verify/check.py:130-152](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/truth-verify/check.py#L130-L152)). That proves activity, not that the claimed anchor came from the tool or that independent tools agree.

Replace the Boolean with an evidence tuple:

```text
(artifact_hash, image_base, anchor_kind, normalized_value, producer, result_hash, timestamp)
```

- One attestation allows a hedged report and continued read-only analysis.
- Two mechanically independent attestations that agree permit the anchor to drive a write/patch/runtime mutation.
- Two attestations that disagree create an immediate risk only when the next action consumes the anchor; otherwise escrow it to the breakpoint.
- Re-running the same command, same backend through a different MCP alias, or same decompiler database is one signal, not two.

**Why inferred.** A raw owner message explicitly doubts trainer behavior despite accumulated decompilation and asks that specific functions be verified ([corpus.json:11794-11798](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L11794-L11798)). The common stop-feedback asks for Ghidra, Binary Ninja and IDA coverage ([corpus.json:10375-10378](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10375-L10378)). The current hook itself names “wrong-but-consistent anchor” as the classic RE sunk-cost trap ([truth-verify/check.py:187-194](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/truth-verify/check.py#L187-L194)), yet its code accepts any matching tool call. Tightening provenance is beyond re-proposing the verifier.

**How to build.** Normalize address claims from the final response and imminent mutation inputs. Extract attestations only from structured tool results or files read this turn; never synthesize them from assistant prose. Define backend independence by executable/database identity, not MCP tool name. On a mutation, require matching `artifact_hash` and image base before comparing normalized anchors. If agreement is absent, inject a read-only alternative. If disagreement is present, use AskUserQuestion once with both values, both evidence references and “stay read-only and re-derive” as default. Every exception exits 0 and emits no block, preserving fail-open behavior; actual mutation authority remains with the agent/permission system.

**External signal that verifies it.** Seed a fixture where IDA and Ghidra agree, one where image bases differ but normalized RVAs agree, one where values truly conflict, and one where the same IDB is exposed through two aliases. The expected result is respectively allow, allow, confirm-before-consumption, and “one signal only.” A binary hash from the filesystem plus each tool's database/image-base result are mechanically independent. A second-model review may inspect the evidence packet, but it cannot substitute for the binary/tool signals.

**SOTA citation.** A preregistered CHI 2025 study found that explanations increased reliance on both correct and incorrect LLM answers, while sources and inconsistent explanations reduced reliance on incorrect answers ([Kim et al., CHI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fostering-appropriate-reliance-on-large-language-models-the-role-of-explanations-sources-and-inconsistencies/)). Microsoft’s appropriate-reliance synthesis defines success as accepting correct outputs and rejecting incorrect ones, not maximizing trust ([Passi, Dhanorkar & Vorvoreanu, 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/appropriate-reliance-on-generative-ai-research-synthesis/)).

**Expected effect.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch seeded “a tool ran but did not support this anchor” cases because provenance is the direct predicate. It is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent wrong-anchor compounding in real RE sessions; the local owner signal and hook rationale support the failure mode, but real backend independence and parser coverage remain UNVERIFIED.

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it for gathering/reconciling evidence; confirm-first only if a disputed anchor is about to drive a mutation.

## Finding 5 — The four-line evidence packet: make interruption cheap to judge — UNVERIFIED (settle with owner-rated packet comparison and decision-time telemetry)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Standardize every owner-facing risk interruption as one compact evidence packet rendered through native AskUserQuestion, using the current dispatcher.

**What it is.** The packet has exactly four semantic lines and two or three choices:

```text
BOUNDARY — Next action would write to PID 4128; current authorization is dynamic-read.
EVIDENCE — process list: game.exe PID 4128; module hash differs from inspected binary hash.
CONSEQUENCE — writing now may apply the patch to a different build; checkpoint cannot undo process memory.
DEFAULT — stay read-only, re-identify module, then continue.
[Stay read-only (recommended)] [Authorize this write once] [Stop and inspect]
```

No generic safety lecture, no chain-of-thought, no repeated context, no unexplained confidence score. The packet names what changed, cites external evidence, states the concrete consequence and offers a reversible default. If there are more than three risks, show the highest due risk and retain the rest in escrow.

**Why inferred.** AskUserQuestion is already used 1,329 times ([corpus.json:135-140](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L135-L140)), so the owner has an existing control vocabulary. The corpus also contains a request for more progress visibility rather than iteration counts ([corpus.json:10581-10586](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10581-L10586)). At the same time, NIST's security-fatigue study found that too many or too-complex security decisions can lead to resignation and decision avoidance ([Stanton et al., 2016](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2016/09/security-fatigue/final)). The inferred need is not “explain more”; it is **compress the discriminating evidence**.

**How to build.** Add a renderer template to the existing dispatcher configuration. Reject rendering unless `boundary`, at least two independent `evidence` refs for interrupt-now, `consequence`, and `safe_default` are populated. Use the existing confidence-band lexicon only in the consequence when probability is uncertain. Deduplicate by `risk_id`; record the selected option to the event ledger. An unanswered noncritical packet defaults safely after the product's normal interaction lifecycle; a critical authorization crossing remains pending and is never auto-authorized.

**External signal that verifies it.** Compare packet versus ordinary warning in a within-owner test: time to decision, correct identification of the boundary, dismissal rate, “noise/useful/too late” rating, and whether the next tool state matches the choice. Decision correctness is validated by subsequent tool output, not by the model restating the packet. A second model can check that the packet faithfully cites the tool results, but owner choice or execution result closes the loop.

**SOTA citation.** Microsoft’s guidelines require efficient dismissal/correction and clarity about why the system acted ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)). Research on appropriate reliance warns that explanations themselves may increase reliance in wrong answers ([Kim et al., CHI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fostering-appropriate-reliance-on-large-language-models-the-role-of-explanations-sources-and-inconsistencies/)). NIST reports security fatigue when security decisions become too numerous or complex ([Stanton et al., 2016](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2016/09/security-fatigue/final)).

**Expected effect.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce decision time and fatigue relative to free-form warnings because it caps volume and exposes only discriminating evidence; the evidence is the HAI guidance and security-fatigue research. Owner-specific benefit is UNVERIFIED until packet comparison.

**Value × effort:** high × low.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** confirm-first (the packet is used only after the interrupt gate has already passed).

## Finding 6 — Breakpoint delivery with a severity clock — UNVERIFIED (settle with timestamped breakpoint-versus-immediate A/B trials)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Add a deterministic breakpoint/urgency scheduler inside the shared dispatcher, folding into existing UserPromptSubmit, PreToolUse, PostToolUse and Stop events. No new hook.

**What it is.** Every escrowed risk gets two times: `next_breakpoint` and `latest_safe_surface`. Recognized breakpoints are external events, not model introspection:

- a terminal `SUCCESS:<id>` or `FAILED:<id>` tool result;
- a test/build/analysis process exit;
- a task completion/update event;
- a checkpoint creation;
- transition from read-only analysis to mutation;
- a Stop event after an atomic step.

The severity clock overrides breakpoint deferral only if waiting would cross the action boundary or destroy the evidence/option to recover. Focus is never inferred from prose such as “I am deeply focused”; it is inferred only from observable task phase and terminal events. Repeated alerts are suppressed until evidence, consequence or boundary changes.

**Why inferred.** The owner's quality-of-focus constraint makes timing part of correctness. The corpus records both a wish for work to continue ([corpus.json:12251-12254](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L12251-L12254)) and a wish to stop and obtain a report when explicitly requested ([corpus.json:10589-10594](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10589-L10594)). The behavior contract already prefers terminal-state outputs specifically to prevent loops ([AGENTS.md:36-39](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md#L36-L39)), giving the scheduler a reliable existing breakpoint signal.

**How to build.** Implement a pure `schedule(risk, event) -> {hold, surface, expire}` function in the dispatcher. Default `latest_safe_surface` is the earlier of the next boundary-crossing tool call or one atomic-step completion. Severity 5/authorization risks cannot pass their boundary; severity 3-4 surface at the next breakpoint; severity 1-2 remain digest-only unless repeated external evidence raises them. Persist scheduler decisions to the risk event so compaction cannot reset the clock. On parser error, hold nothing and exit 0; existing permission and safety behavior remains authoritative.

**External signal that verifies it.** Timestamp event stream, risk detection, breakpoint and display. Seed known-positive terminal tool results and a known nonterminal progress event to prove the scheduler discriminates them. In A/B trials, externally measure task completion time, owner response latency, interrupted-command count and post-warning error rate. The owner rates timing as `too early`, `right`, or `too late`; that rating updates calibration only after the session, not inside the same context.

**SOTA citation.** Attention-sensitive alerting models the cost of deferral against interruption cost ([Horvitz, Jacobs & Hovel, UAI 1999](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/attention-sensitive-alerting/)). Oasis aligns delivery with task structure ([Iqbal & Bailey, TOCHI 2010](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=161181)). A three-week field study reported models for task transition/break timing with user-rated timing accuracy, though its workplace population and break recommendations are not RE/security tasks ([Kaur et al., CHI 2020](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/optimizing-for-happiness-and-productivity-modeling-opportune-moments-for-transitions-and-breaks-at-work/)). Generalization to this owner is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the proposed A/B trial**.

**Expected effect.** It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce mid-step interruption without making high-severity warnings late because explicit boundaries and clocks constrain deferral; evidence is attention-management research and existing terminal signals. Exact timing accuracy is **roughly even (45-55%)** before owner-specific trial data.

**Value × effort:** high × medium.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it for scheduling; confirm-first when a due risk is interrupt-eligible.

## Finding 7 — Granular owner feedback becomes calibration data, not a new prompt — UNVERIFIED (settle with 50 adjudicated alerts and held-out precision/recall)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Extend the existing selection-event/continuous-learning path with risk dispositions and conservative per-boundary thresholds. Do not train a critic and do not create a new memory system.

**What it is.** Each shown packet already produces an owner choice. Treat that choice as granular feedback:

- `stay_safe` = warning actionable;
- `authorize_once` = boundary real, owner accepts it;
- `dismiss_noise` = false-positive/context mismatch;
- `stop_inspect` = high value;
- no response is **not** a negative label.

Later external outcomes add `prevented_failure`, `no_effect`, or `missed_risk`. Calibration is per boundary class (`target`, `authorization`, `external_effect`, `irreversibility`, `secret`, `anchor_conflict`), not per scary word. Thresholds update only after a minimum sample and only between sessions. Changes are capped and logged; global controls can reset or freeze learning. A candidate never learns from the model rating its own warning.

**Why inferred.** The corpus's correction detector demonstrates exactly why implicit semantic learning is dangerous: 41/41 unfiltered samples were false positives ([corpus.json:10371-10374](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10371-L10374)). The shared goal module already excludes task notifications and system reminders from user-goal learning because they can hijack the goal ([modules.py:1279-1321](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py#L1279-L1321)). The owner also explicitly changes course—e.g., asking to stop and find a more reliable alternative to Frida ([corpus.json:10701-10706](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10701-L10706)). The surprising inference is to learn from **dispositions attached to verified boundaries**, not from conversational tone or correction-like text.

**How to build.** Add `risk_disposition` to the current selection-event schema and append it through the existing ledger. Use a simple Beta-binomial or count threshold per boundary—standard library only, no model training. Example conservative rule: remain shadow-only until 10 owner-adjudicated cases; promote only with lower confidence bound above the chosen precision floor; demote immediately after two false interrupts of the same class; never lower the hard authorization/secret boundary based on convenience feedback. Surface threshold changes only in a periodic harness report, not during work.

**External signal that verifies it.** Owner button choice is one external signal; subsequent process/filesystem/network/tool state is the second. A held-out labeled set computes precision, recall, interrupt rate per hour, missed-boundary rate and calibration by class. Detector validation must include known-present owner-authored messages and known-present internal task notifications before estimating false-positive rate. Same-context assistant judgments are excluded by construction.

**SOTA citation.** The HAI guidelines recommend learning from user behavior, adapting cautiously, encouraging granular feedback and providing global controls ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)). A recent proactive-agent prototype emphasizes controllability, uncertainty flagging and simulation before live use, but its deployment sample is only two users, so generalization is weak ([Long et al., 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12626)). Its suitability here is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the held-out evaluation, not simulated-user ratings**.

**Expected effect.** It is **very likely (80-90%)** to avoid contamination by agent-authored pseudo-user messages because only native owner selections and later tool outcomes are labels. It is **likely (55-80%)** to improve owner-specific alert precision after enough adjudicated cases; this is supported by granular-feedback guidance but remains UNVERIFIED at the proposed sample size.

**Value × effort:** high × medium.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it for recording and conservative between-session calibration; confirm-first before any permanent relaxation of a hard boundary.

## Finding 8 — Shadow replay sets the interruption budget before deployment — UNVERIFIED (this finding is itself the settling experiment)

**Improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus):** Add an offline evaluator beside the existing event-ledger/selection evaluation scripts. It reads corpus/ledger data and proposed decisions; it never runs as an interactive hook and never blocks work.

**What it is.** Define a budget before enabling owner-facing interrupts:

- **primary:** precision among owner-adjudicated `interrupt-now` events;
- **safety:** recall on labeled authorization/external-effect/irreversibility/secret/anchor-conflict crossings;
- **focus:** interrupts per active hour and per completed atomic task;
- **timing:** `too early/right/too late` owner ratings;
- **continuity:** open risks lost across compact/resume (target zero);
- **utility:** prevented failure or materially changed action;
- **anti-fatigue:** repeat-without-new-evidence rate (target zero).

Do not optimize a single accuracy score. A system can get high accuracy by never alerting or high recall by nagging constantly. Promotion ladder: offline replay → live shadow log → breakpoint-only digest → interrupt-now for one boundary class → broader classes. Every stage has rollback to the prior configuration. Use recent sessions as holdout; never tune and score on the same sessions.

**Why inferred.** The corpus supplies exact broad counts but capped samples ([corpus.json:2-11](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L2-L11)); its correction candidates are recall-biased ([corpus.json:10371-10374](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L10371-L10374)); its hooks detector is broken ([corpus.json:13260-13263](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L13260-L13263)). Therefore, static confidence about alert quality would be false precision. The workspace also has enormous ordinary error volume, including 6,461 Bash errors and 2,031 PowerShell errors ([corpus.json:13-20](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L13-L20), [corpus.json:39-44](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json#L39-L44)); an evaluator must prove it is not simply converting tool noise into owner noise.

**How to build.** Reuse redacted event hashes and selection records. Sample across RE/security and nonsecurity sessions, tool classes and severity classes. Two independent human/model-assisted passes may propose labels, but disagreements go to the owner or remain unlabeled; a second model is independent context but not ground truth. Include adversarial negatives: documentation that mentions destructive commands, owner-owned credential filenames, static malware research, MCP aliases to the same backend, repeated tool errors, and quoted prompt injections. Include positives: live target changes, external network effects, secret literals in actual outgoing fields, destructive commands with resolved broad targets, and conflicting anchors consumed by a mutation. Publish confusion matrices and examples, not only aggregate scores.

The initial precision floor is an owner choice; absent that choice, use shadow mode only. I recommend no more than one owner-facing risk packet per atomic task and no more than one repeat per risk without new evidence. Whether those numerical caps fit this owner is **UNVERIFIED; settle them by comparing focus ratings and missed-risk counts across the first 30 shadow sessions**.

**External signal that verifies it.** Held-out owner labels plus actual subsequent execution state. For absence claims, first prove each detector finds a seeded known-present event, then use two different fields—event type plus tool/target state—to establish absence. Replay output must be reproducible from a frozen input hash. Prospective success requires both a precision floor and no increase in dropped-thread count; self-reported model confidence is never a success signal.

**SOTA citation.** Microsoft’s 2025 appropriate-reliance report says mitigations can backfire and therefore require user research; it also notes that verification effort and volume can lower task performance ([Vorvoreanu et al., 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fostering-appropriate-reliance-on-genai-lessons-learned-from-early-research/)). A 2025 survey of 40 LLM uncertainty methods argues that benchmark calibration and epistemic uncertainty do not necessarily predict downstream human utility ([Devic et al., 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07461)). The proactive-programming literature reports benefits and important design nuances in randomized evaluation, supporting experimental rather than intuition-only rollout ([Chen et al., 2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04596)).

**Expected effect.** It is **almost certain (90-99%)** to expose deterministic parser/dedupe failures before owner-facing deployment because seeded cases directly exercise those paths. It is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent a high-recall/low-precision alert system from shipping because precision and interruption budget are explicit promotion gates. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that offline replay alone will predict owner focus accurately; evidence is the ecological-validity critique of uncertainty benchmarks, hence live shadow mode is mandatory.

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium.

**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.

**Autonomy:** just-do-it for offline and shadow evaluation; confirm-first before turning on owner-facing interruption classes or choosing the precision/attention budget.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Warn whenever the task is dual-use” — rejected

It has no action boundary, no contextual risk tolerance and no external evidence. It would turn the owner's core domain into a permanent warning state. Local false-positive history in credential/destructive-token guards independently demonstrates the danger of lexical triggers ([modules.py:50-74](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py#L50-L74)).

### “Ask the owner whenever confidence is low” — rejected

Model uncertainty is not an external signal and low confidence can concern trivia rather than consequence. NIST risk is likelihood **and impact**, while recent uncertainty research warns that benchmark uncertainty may not correspond to downstream utility ([NIST AI RMF](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/1-sec-risk/), [Devic et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07461)). Ask only at an evidenced boundary.

### “Have the same agent critique whether its warning is useful” — rejected by the unit law

That closes the loop inside the same context. A second pass by the same context can format a candidate but cannot validate it. The accepted validators here are tool results, different backends/artifacts, execution state, a fresh second model, or the owner.

### “Add a seventh proactive-risk hook” — rejected

The settings already route the relevant native events through shared and local dispatchers ([settings.json:117-145](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json#L117-L145), [settings.json:310-354](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json#L310-L354), [settings.json:484-504](/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json#L484-L504)). All proposed logic folds into those buses or replaces the current truth-verify predicate.

### “Build a learned proactive-agent daemon / trained risk critic” — rejected by anti-overfit gate

It would be BUILD-LARGE, require a new runtime and rely on learned self-evaluation where deterministic action boundaries suffice. A small policy table, append-only risk projection and offline evaluator are easier to falsify and remove.

### “Put every risk in OPEN-THREADS.md” — rejected as duplication

OPEN-THREADS is already named as an existing continuity surface in the unit brief. Risk escrow belongs as a typed projection in the existing event ledger and can render into existing thread views; a second manually synchronized file would create two sources of truth.
