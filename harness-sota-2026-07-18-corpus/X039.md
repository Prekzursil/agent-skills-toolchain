# X039 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → agent-claim verification

## Executive judgment

The analogy transfers, but only in a constrained form. The transferable mechanism is **independent verification of an as-found condition against a governing criterion**, surrounded by risk-graded self-checking, conservative stop rules, and genuinely independent layers. The non-transferable shortcut is “ask a second agent whether the first agent sounds right.” DOE explicitly distinguishes peer checking from independent verification: peer checking is the least rigorous checking method, while independent verification separates performer and verifier in time and distance, with the verifier examining the condition without relying on the performer's account ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 41–47](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

My central design recommendation is therefore not a generic critic agent. It is a small **claim-evidence gate** that (1) turns important assertions into typed, testable postconditions; (2) requires the producing agent to perform a STAR self-check; (3) independently re-observes the relevant state through an evidence channel selected by the harness rather than by the producer; and (4) refuses to certify a claim whose truth cannot be established at the requested assurance level. This is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce false “absent,” “broken,” “fixed,” and “done” claims in tool-using work, because those claims usually have externally observable postconditions and DOE's independent-verification mechanism is designed precisely to catch a performer's incorrect condition without depending on the performer's narrative; the exact effect size is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the stratified shadow-replay experiment specified below**.

This is not a proposal for a second orchestration runtime, vector database, durable workflow engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. It rides the existing hook/tool/skill bus. It passes the anti-overfit gate if kept to typed claim records, a small policy table, and existing tool invocations. A daemon or universal multi-agent review system would not pass that gate.

## Scope and evidence discipline

This is the requested ideation lens. I did not inspect the local harness, subject tree, corpus, audit findings, or neighboring artifacts. Statements about the existing system below are limited to the measured ground truth and mandatory instrumentation supplied in the unit brief; I do not independently re-claim them. All nuclear-domain claims are grounded in public DOE, NRC, or IAEA sources. Claims about model behavior use primary research papers where they matter to the analogy.

The DOE handbook describes itself as good-practice guidance rather than a requirements document and tells organizations to select tools appropriate to their needs, not to deploy every tool ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, foreword and introduction](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). That warning is important: copying every nuclear ceremony into an agent harness would be cargo culting, not engineering.

## What nuclear operations actually solved

### 1. STAR: local error interception, not independent proof

**Mechanism in its own field.** DOE defines STAR as Stop, Think, Act, Review. The operator pauses and removes distractions; determines the intended action, expected result, controlling document, and contingency; performs the action on the identified component; then verifies that the observed result matches the expected result ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The handbook applies self-checking to equipment manipulations but also to calculations, computer data entry, readouts, experiments, and editing drawings or procedures. It also lists predictable degradations: acting despite discrepancies, batching several actions, omitting the governing document, distraction, and failing to verify the result.

This mechanism solves an attentional and sequencing problem. It forces the performer to state an expected observable before acting and to compare the post-action observation with that expectation. It does **not** make the performer independent of their own mistaken premise. The handbook itself places self-checking below checking and verification practices and says that use of human-performance tools does not guarantee perfect performance ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 5, 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

**Concrete translation.** Before an agent emits or relies on a consequential claim, the harness should force a compact STAR record:

1. **Stop:** identify the exact assertion and its scope, including target, revision/state, and time.
2. **Think:** declare the governing criterion, expected observable, acceptable evidence type, and what result would falsify the assertion.
3. **Act:** run the relevant observation or operation.
4. **Review:** compare the actual output to the predeclared expectation; record mismatch rather than narratively repairing it.

Example: instead of “the hook is broken,” the record says: target `hook H`; criterion “event E produces record R”; positive-control detector “the same query finds known-present record K”; test observation “E produced no R”; alternate observation “the hook process log contains no receipt for E”; freshness and command outputs attached. The brief already mandates two mechanically independent signals for absence/broken/done claims; STAR makes the expected result and falsifier explicit before the agent sees the output.

**What it catches.** STAR is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch wrong-target operations, failure to inspect an action's result, and narrative claims that do not match the immediately returned tool output, because those are direct counterparts of the DOE failure modes and the comparison is local and mechanical. Its benefit for a reasoning error in which the agent chose the wrong governing criterion is only **unlikely (20-45%)**, because the same model supplies both premise and review; research on intrinsic LLM self-correction finds that performance can fail to improve or even degrade without external feedback ([Huang et al., “Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). Exact harness rates are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with the replay and mutation experiments below**.

### 2. Two-person concept: control of action, not a truth oracle

**Mechanism in its own field.** DOE's nuclear-explosive two-person concept exists to ensure that no lone individual has unrestricted access to a nuclear explosive. Each team member must be reliability-certified, authorized, technically knowledgeable, knowledgeable about safety and security requirements, and positioned to detect incorrect or unauthorized acts and respond. DOE distinguishes zone coverage from the more stringent person-to-person coverage for configurations vulnerable to inadvertent or deliberate acts ([DOE Order 452.2F, attachment 2](https://www.energy.gov/documents/nuclear-explosive-safety)).

The key point is often lost in cross-domain retellings: this is primarily an access, observation, and intervention control. It reduces the chance that one person can perform an undetected dangerous or unauthorized act. Mere presence of two people is not proof that a proposition is true.

**Concrete translation.** For irreversible or externally consequential agent actions, require two distinct roles:

* a performer proposes the exact action, target, and predicted postcondition;
* an authorizer independently resolves the target and policy preconditions and can veto before execution;
* after execution, a verifier evaluates the resulting state rather than merely confirming that the performer executed the proposal.

The first two bullets are analogous to person-to-person coverage; the third comes from independent verification. For ordinary factual output with no consequential action, the two-person concept adds little beyond independent verification and should not be invoked.

**What it catches.** A two-role authorization check is **likely (55-80%)** to catch target confusion and unauthorized or irreversible action when target identity and policy are machine-checkable, because the second role can block before the effect occurs and DOE applies the stronger coverage exactly where inadvertent or deliberate actions are consequential. It is only **unlikely (20-45%)** to improve the truth of open-ended prose by itself, because two model calls can share training priors, prompt framing, tools, and source errors. Exact rates are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with a blinded same-model/different-model/mechanical-verifier comparison below**.

### 3. Independent verification: the strongest direct borrow

**Mechanism in its own field.** DOE separates four practices:

* peer checking prevents an incorrect action and is explicitly the least rigorous;
* concurrent verification has two people separately confirm a condition while co-located, but DOE says true independence cannot be achieved side by side;
* independent verification confirms an equipment condition, document, or calculation after the performer's work, separated in time and distance;
* peer review checks a work product before completion.

For independent verification, the verifier is not involved in producing the state or document, receives no observable cues from the performer, determines the **as-found** condition without changing it, compares that condition with the governing document, reports disagreement, and signs only if it agrees. DOE says this tends to catch errors better than peer or concurrent checking because the verifier's knowledge is not influenced by the performer ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 41–48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). DOE also warns against co-location, walking to the component together, relying only on process indicators, junior verifiers reluctant to challenge, and superficial verification based on an assumption that someone else will catch the problem.

This is more specific than “independent reviewer.” Independence has operational content:

1. separation from production;
2. no leakage of the performer's conclusion-forming narrative;
3. direct examination of the as-found object or state;
4. comparison to a governing source external to the claim;
5. a method competent for that particular object;
6. explicit disagreement handling;
7. an auditable attestation.

**Concrete translation.** The harness should treat a claim as an object to verify, not prose to critique. The producer emits a minimal claim contract:

```text
claim_id
claim_type: presence | absence | state | mutation | test | citation | calculation | semantic
subject: exact object and scope
state_anchor: path/revision/URL/version/time as applicable
governing_criterion: the requirement against which truth is judged
expected_observable
producer_evidence_handles
risk: low | consequential | irreversible
freshness_limit
```

The harness—not the producer—selects a verifier recipe by `claim_type`. It gives the verifier the claim, state anchor, and governing criterion, but withholds the producer's chain of thought, persuasive summary, and conclusion-shaped interpretation. The verifier obtains a fresh as-found observation. For absence, brokenness, and completion claims, it first demonstrates that the detector finds a known-present control and then uses a mechanically independent signal. For mutations, it examines the target diff/state plus a behavior check. For test claims, it checks exit status, requested scope, and artifact freshness rather than accepting a sentence saying tests passed. For citations, it opens the cited source and checks entailment at the exact location. For calculations, it recomputes through a different implementation or invariant. It returns `verified`, `contradicted`, or `not established`; “not established” is not silently coerced to success.

The verifier may be an LLM for semantic matching, but an LLM verdict alone is not independent evidence. This limitation is empirically important: a broad review reports that self-correction works reliably when tasks provide dependable external feedback, not merely prompted self-evaluation ([Kamoi et al., “When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297)); a 15-judge, 150,000-instance study finds systematic position bias in LLM judges ([Shi et al., “Judging the Judges”](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18/)).

**What it catches.** This gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch claims whose asserted postcondition is contradicted by a fresh, correctly scoped, mechanically readable state—examples include “file exists,” “edit landed,” “command succeeded,” “test suite X passed,” “service accepted the request,” and “detector found zero”—because direct re-observation does not depend on trusting the producer. It is **likely (55-80%)** to catch citation non-entailment when source text is accessible and the assertion is narrow, because the verifier receives both a governing source and a bounded claim, but semantic judgment remains fallible. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** to resolve open-world synthesis claims such as “this is the best architecture,” because there may be no authoritative as-found state or complete governing criterion. All effect sizes are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with the experiments below**.

### 4. Conservative decision-making: change the burden of proof at high consequence

**Mechanism in its own field.** NRC describes conservative decision-making as using conservative assumptions and rejecting an option unless it is demonstrated safe, rather than requiring proof that it is unsafe. NRC also calls for systematic decisions under uncertainty, contingencies, interdisciplinary review of safety-significant decisions, and later effectiveness reviews of assumptions and unintended consequences ([NRC, Safety Culture Components](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/enforcement/sc-componets011306)). IAEA guidance similarly connects conservative decision-making with stopping to review, seeking more information when uncertain, assuming adverse outcomes are possible, and visibly supporting justified stop-work decisions ([INSAG-15, section 3.3](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2014/04/f14/INSAG-15.pdf)).

This is not “always say no.” It is a burden-of-proof rule selected because the consequence asymmetry is extreme. Nuclear guidance still uses risk information and graded controls.

**Concrete translation.** For claims that authorize irreversible changes, release to users, security decisions, deletion, payment, or assertions that suppress further investigation (“nothing is wrong,” “no data exists,” “done”), the harness should require affirmative evidence sufficient for the claim type. Failure to establish the claim yields `not established` and either a reversible fallback or human escalation. Low-consequence narrative and exploratory tasks should remain permissive.

**What it catches.** A risk-gated burden reversal is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent consequential action based on ambiguous or missing evidence, because an unverified claim no longer defaults to permission. Applying the same rule to every claim is **very likely (80-90%)** to cause waste and false blocking, because many agent outputs are open-ended and the DOE handbook itself recommends selecting tools by task and risk rather than using all tools everywhere. The net benefit and the correct risk threshold are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with the latency/false-block portion of the shadow replay**.

### 5. Defence in depth: independence and common-cause analysis, not “more checks”

**Mechanism in its own field.** IAEA defines defence in depth as successive prevention, monitoring, control, mitigation, and emergency-response levels. If one level fails, a subsequent level acts; hazards such as fire, flooding, and earthquakes receive special attention because they can defeat several levels at once. IAEA also says the levels should be functionally independent as far as practicable and that the existence of several levels never justifies continued operation with one unavailable ([INSAG-12, paras. 47–54 and table I](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/P082_scr.pdf)). Its training material is equally explicit that defence in depth is a reasoning framework, not a technique that dictates one technical solution, and that mitigation must not justify weakening prevention ([IAEA Basic Principles of Nuclear Safety, chapter 4](https://gnssn.iaea.org/main/bptc/BPTC%20Module%20Documents/Module03%20Basic%20principles%20of%20nuclear%20safety.pdf)).

The important transferable idea is **failure diversity**. Five prompts to the same model against the same stale source are not five defences. They are one defence repeated five times, vulnerable to a common cause.

**Concrete translation.** Use a small, risk-graded claim assurance stack:

* **Level 1 — prevent:** typed claim scope, explicit governing criterion, safe/reversible defaults;
* **Level 2 — detect locally:** STAR plus immediate tool-result/postcondition comparison;
* **Level 3 — verify independently:** fresh as-found observation through an evidence channel not controlled by the producer;
* **Level 4 — contain:** do not promote `contradicted` or `not established` into consequential action; preserve rollback/reversibility where the existing bus supports it;
* **Level 5 — learn:** sample signed claims, inject detector mutations, and update the small recipe/policy table from observed misses.

Each layer must list its common-cause dependencies: same model family, same prompt, same tool server, same parser, same cache, same source, same credentials, and same state snapshot. Where two claimed signals share the dependency capable of causing the target failure, they count as one signal.

**What it catches.** Explicit common-cause accounting is **likely (55-80%)** to expose sham redundancy such as two probes through the same broken detector or two agents consuming the same stale summary, because the dependency inventory directly reveals the shared failure path. It is only **unlikely (20-45%)** to discover an unmodeled common cause automatically; independence claims themselves must be tested. Exact rates are **UNVERIFIED — settle them with the common-cause fault-injection experiment below**.

It fails with `not established` when the recipe cannot obtain competent evidence.

The blind verifier is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce anchoring compared with showing the verifier the full producer narrative, because DOE obtains independence by separating cues and contemporary LLM judges show prompt-position and preference biases. ## What the design would catch that the current process can miss

The following are target failure classes, not claims that every class is present in the subject harness:

1. **Detector blindness masquerading as absence.** A query returns zero because the detector, parser, scope, or hook is broken. Positive-control validation plus a second data plane prevents “zero” from becoming “absent.” Catch likelihood is **very likely (80-90%)** when a known-present control and truly independent data plane exist; **UNVERIFIED — test by disabling the primary detector while leaving its target present**.
2. **Narrated success after tool failure.** An agent proceeds as if a write, command, API request, or test succeeded despite nonzero status or contradictory output. STAR Review plus a postcondition verifier makes the contradiction explicit. Catch likelihood is **very likely (80-90%)** for structured outputs; **UNVERIFIED — inject failures and misleading partial output**.
3. **Right action, wrong object.** A valid command runs against the wrong file, branch, service, account, or revision. Exact state anchors, independent target resolution, and direct as-found inspection address this. Catch likelihood is **likely (55-80%)**, reduced where aliases or state changes make identity ambiguous; **UNVERIFIED — use near-name and stale-revision mutations**.
4. **Stale evidence.** A prior passing test, cached fetch, or old file read is cited for a later state. Freshness limits and state anchors prevent old evidence from certifying a new claim. Catch likelihood is **very likely (80-90%)** when the state exposes timestamps, hashes, revisions, or request IDs; **UNVERIFIED — replay evidence after controlled mutation**.
5. **Scope laundering.** A narrow test passes and is described as “all tests pass,” or one directory search becomes a repository-wide absence claim. Typed subject/scope fields make the mismatch machine-visible. Catch likelihood is **likely (55-80%)** for tools that expose invocation scope; **UNVERIFIED — label a replay set of scope-overclaim examples**.
6. **Citation presence without support.** A URL exists but does not entail the attached sentence. Bounded claim-to-passage verification catches some cases. Catch likelihood is **likely (55-80%)** for explicit factual propositions and **unlikely (20-45%)** for synthesis or disputed interpretation; **UNVERIFIED — evaluate on a human-labeled entailment set with adversarial near-supports**.
7. **Correlated reviewers.** Multiple agents repeat the same error because they share prompt framing, source, model prior, or tool path. The independence ledger refuses to count correlated replicas as separate signals. Detection of declared shared dependencies is **very likely (80-90%)**; discovery of undeclared dependencies is **unlikely (20-45%)**. Both are **UNVERIFIED — run held-out common-cause injections**.
8. **Premature completion.** The plan says done while required artifacts or postconditions are missing. A completion claim is verified against the original acceptance criteria and fresh state, not the agent's completion summary. Catch likelihood is **likely (55-80%)** when acceptance criteria are testable and only **roughly even (45-55%)** when the request is aesthetic or open-ended; **UNVERIFIED — replay owner-corrected completion cases**.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Structural correspondences that make the borrow valid

1. **Both domains have performers who can confidently misperceive state.** DOE's practices are explicitly designed to anticipate, prevent, and catch active human errors and latent organizational weaknesses ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, foreword](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). Agents likewise produce claims after actions and observations; on externally observable tasks, the postcondition can be checked without trusting the narration.
2. **Both have a distinction between action correctness and resulting-state correctness.** DOE distinguishes checking an action from verifying equipment/document condition. Agent harnesses need the same distinction: approving a command is not evidence that its intended state resulted.
3. **Both suffer common-cause failure.** IAEA explicitly designs for hazards capable of defeating multiple layers. In an agent harness, shared model, context, cache, parser, tool, or source can defeat multiple apparent reviewers.
4. **Both benefit from consequence grading.** Nuclear controls become stronger as consequences and irreversibility increase. Agent work ranges from harmless drafting to deletion, publication, financial action, or security-sensitive change; uniform ceremony would be irrational.

These correspondences make transfer of independent as-found verification **very likely (80-90%)** valid for bounded, externally observable claims; the evidence is the near-isomorphism between “component/document condition versus governing document” and “workspace/external state versus acceptance criterion.” The breadth of that class in this owner's workload is **UNVERIFIED — settle it by classifying a random sample of historical consequential claims by whether they have an independently observable postcondition**.

### Disanalogies that forbid a literal transplant

1. **Physical plant state is often more determinate than open-world truth.** A valve position can be physically inspected against a procedure. “Best architecture,” causal explanations, forecasts, and contested facts may have no single sensor or governing document. The gate must be allowed to return `not established`; it cannot manufacture proof.
2. **Nuclear procedures operate within engineered boundaries.** Agent tasks traverse mutable repositories, remote services, permissions, caches, web sources, and ambiguous natural language. State can change between production and verification; anchors and freshness rules are therefore necessary, but cannot eliminate races.
3. **Two humans are not equivalent to two model samples.** Separate people have distinct perception and experience; two calls to one model can share the same learned error and framing. Even different models may share training sources. Mechanical evidence diversity matters more than agent count.
4. **The consequence asymmetry differs.** Nuclear operations justify expensive fail-closed controls because low-probability failures can be catastrophic. Most agent claims are cheap and reversible. Conservative refusal everywhere would overfit the harness to rare high-risk work.
5. **Verification can perturb the target.** Some commands, reads, API calls, and tests have side effects; a verifier that repeats the producer's operation can change state or duplicate an external action. DOE's “determine as-found condition without changing it” translates into read-only verification recipes and idempotency/request identifiers, not blind replay.
6. **Organizational rank maps imperfectly.** DOE warns that junior verifiers may hesitate to challenge senior performers. Agents do not experience rank identically, but prompt authority, prior conclusions, and routing policy can create an analogous anchoring effect. Blinding helps but does not establish full independence.
7. **More layers can create complacency.** DOE warns that performers may pay less attention because they expect verifiers to catch errors and that overused peer checks lead to complacency ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 43, 45, 47–48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). A harness can similarly spend tokens on ritual attestations that all share one failure mode.

Because of these disanalogies, a universal “two-agent rule” is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be the best verification architecture; it provides neither competent evidence nor independence by itself, and empirical LLM-judge bias undermines the premise that another generated verdict is a truth oracle. This conclusion does not require a future experiment for the logical point, but the magnitude of any benefit from a second agent is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the comparative experiment below**.

## Falsifiable evaluation plan

### Experiment 1 — Stratified historical shadow replay

Sample at least 300 past consequential claims, stratified across presence, absence, mutation, test, command/API success, citation, calculation, and semantic synthesis. Hide later owner corrections from the systems being tested. Have humans label only the claim contract and final truth/postcondition, not read every trace. Compare:

* baseline harness;
* STAR only;
* unblinded second-agent review;
* blind as-found verifier;
* full risk-graded gate.

Measure false certification rate, contradiction recall, `not established` precision, human escalations, tokens, tool calls, wall time, and false blocks. Pre-register the primary outcome as false certification on consequential claims; otherwise a system could look better merely by refusing everything.

The full gate is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform unblinded second-agent review on false certification because it uses fresh external evidence and reduces conclusion leakage; **UNVERIFIED — this experiment settles the claim**. It is **likely (55-80%)** to cost more tool calls but **unlikely (20-45%)** to justify that cost on low-risk claims; **UNVERIFIED — the same experiment measures the trade-off**.

### Experiment 2 — Detector and state mutation suite

Create controlled tasks with ground truth, then inject one fault at a time: empty output from a blind detector, stale cache, truncated tool output, nonzero exit with plausible text, wrong near-name target, stale passing-test artifact, citation near-match, and uncommitted/unstaged state mismatch. Include a known-present control for each absence detector. Hold out some mutations when designing recipes.

Success criterion: the gate must not certify the target claim when the competent evidence path is faulty; it should say `contradicted` when alternate evidence disproves it and `not established` when assurance is unavailable. The claim that this will separate detector failure from genuine absence is **very likely (80-90%)** for injected faults with a working alternate channel; **UNVERIFIED — this mutation suite settles it**.

### Experiment 3 — Independence/common-cause matrix

Compare verifier pairs along independently varied axes:

* same model versus different model;
* full trace versus blind claim contract;
* same tool/data plane versus different data plane;
* same parser/cache versus independent parser/fresh read;
* model verdict versus deterministic invariant.

Inject failures at the source, cache, parser, tool server, and prompt-framing levels. Compute joint miss probability and compare it with the product of individual miss probabilities; large positive deviation reveals correlated failure. The claim that tool/data-plane diversity matters more than merely changing the model is **likely (55-80%)**, because the relevant target classes are externally observable and a shared faulty observation defeats either model; **UNVERIFIED — this matrix settles it**.

### Experiment 4 — Burden-of-proof and stopping threshold

Randomize risk thresholds in shadow mode. For each, measure prevented harmful actions, false stops, reversible fallbacks, escalation load, and completion latency. Do not infer success from a lower action count. The recommended three-level risk table is **roughly even (45-55%)** to be the optimal granularity; there is no evidence here that three bins dominate a continuous or two-bin policy. This is **UNVERIFIED — the threshold experiment settles it**.

### Adoption gate

Adopt only if the blind as-found verifier materially reduces false certification on consequential claims, retains acceptable `not established` precision, and does not impose its cost on low-risk work. Keep the static recipe/policy layer small. If deterministic checks cover most benefit, omit a second agent. If the full gate fails to outperform a simple postcondition check, choose the simple check.

## Rejected alternatives
