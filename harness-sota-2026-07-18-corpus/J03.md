# J03 — Pre-computing likely-next needs: index refreshes, health checks, and research pre-fetch

## Executive verdict

The ambitious answer is **not** a free-running “dreaming agent.” It is a small, read-only **anticipatory compiler**: turn durable open threads and objective workspace deltas into expiring, provenance-bearing *prepared packets*; learn which packet classes are useful from the owner’s later behavior; and surface only the few packets whose measured expected value exceeds their attention and compute cost. This rides the existing event-ledger, open-loops, calibrated-silence, scheduled-task, and background-digest buses. It creates no second orchestrator, daemon, vector database, trained critic, observability service, or new raw hook.

The most important result is negative but actionable: **there is no honest measured hit-rate answer in the supplied corpus yet.** Signal 1: the corpus marks its entire hook section `DETECTOR_STATUS: BROKEN` and says to treat it as no data (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:13260-13263`). Signal 2, mechanically independent: the event-ledger’s OATS implementation says no `selection` outcomes are logged and that the logging is not wired (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/oats.py:34-38`). The live-observation defect report independently says hook behavior across 1,964 sessions is presently unmeasurable and gives the two-probe experiment needed to settle where telemetry lives (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-147`). Therefore, all hit-rate rankings below are priors, explicitly confidence-banded, and must be replaced by observed rates after a shadow trial.

My ranked prior is:

1. **Need-scoped health/warm-path checks** are **very likely (80-90%)** to have the best near-term hit rate, because they target a tool already implied by a durable next action and the corpus records large, repeated avoidable failure classes: 275 “Tavily not connected,” 232 `ECONNREFUSED`, 709 `ENAMETOOLONG`, and 1,631 shell unmatched-quote failures (`corpus.json:4280-4296,4316-4332,4388-4408`).
2. **Resume packets for explicit open threads** are **very likely (80-90%)** to be used, because they predict from an already recorded intention rather than guessing a new intention; the owner explicitly corrected forgotten clipboard/research requests (`corpus.json:11447-11454`) and explicitly said not to forget memory persistence (`corpus.json:11579-11587`).
3. **Change-triggered incremental index refresh** is **likely (55-80%)** to be useful when restricted to an active project and an actually changed source fingerprint. Basic-memory search/read/write are already heavily used—357 searches, 353 reads, and 408 writes (`corpus.json:13476-13485`)—but no outcome join currently proves that a refresh prevented a miss.
4. **Active-question research pre-fetch** is **likely (55-80%)** to be useful when it fetches metadata/deltas for a pinned open research question, because `deep-research` is the most-used named skill at 70 invocations (`corpus.json:13265-13291`) and the owner explicitly requests SOTA research (`corpus.json:11297-11305`). Broad topic dreaming is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat its distraction and staleness cost; this is supported by notification research and by the absence of any local outcome evidence for broad prefetch.

Those bands are priors, not measured owner-specific rates. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles them:** run Findings 1 and 7 in silent shadow mode for 30 active owner sessions or 100 eligible predictions, whichever comes later; join each prediction to a later external use, dismissal, supersession, or expiry; then report Wilson intervals by action class and context.

## Grounding and constraints

### What the local evidence can and cannot support

The corpus covers 11,633 files and 10.62 GB with zero files failed, one bad line, and capped samples (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). Its tool counts are therefore strong evidence of frequency and failure, but not of causal usefulness. For example, WebSearch has 8,316 calls and a 0.65% tool error rate, while WebFetch has 8,042 calls and a 5.57% error rate (`corpus.json:62-76`); that supports preferring metadata search before full fetch, but it does not prove that a prefetched result answered a later need.

The `owner_correction_candidates` section is explicitly recall-biased: its own smoke sample found 41/41 false positives (`corpus.json:10371-10374`). I use only samples whose quoted text itself unambiguously expresses a preference or correction, and I do not use the 2,268 candidate count as a behavioral rate.

The workspace already has the correct skeleton:

* The event-ledger is an append-only, failure-aware per-project log (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:1-13`) and already defines a `selection` event kind (`ledger.py:61-65`).
* Open-loops already stores durable “when X, do Y” intentions and derives unresolved failures/dangling goals from the ledger (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/_common.py:2-26`).
* Background-digest is already a FIFO, archive-before-consume, fail-open SessionStart surface (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/background-digest/reader.py:2-43,138-181,205-224`). It is folded into the shared dispatcher, so extending its producers adds no raw hook (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:1-3,53-69`).
* Calibrated-silence already estimates acted/ignored rates, raises its threshold using correction, terseness, late hour, and interjection density, and fails open (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:2-53,94-124,342-387`). Its reaction logger explicitly uses the owner’s next prompt or an actual later tool invocation—an external signal, not same-context self-review (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/log_reaction.py:2-28`).
* A weekly scheduled harness benchmark already demonstrates the desired pattern: compute outside the session, append only a regression delta, and surface it through the existing digest without a new runtime or hook (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/harness-bench/README.md:1-9`).

This unit therefore rejects a duplicate `dreamer-v2`, a new agent daemon, and a new notification UI. The quality bar is met by extending proven seams and testing the full producer → spool → surface → reaction join.

## Finding 1 — Inferred surprise: shadow “likely-next” counterfactual ledger before enabling any proactive action

**Improvement:** Add a silent, counterfactual prediction ledger that measures what *would* have been prepared and whether the owner later used it. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

**What it is:** A scheduled `anticipatory-score.py` reads only (a) unresolved event-ledger/open-loop records, (b) current project fingerprint, and (c) recent successful tool sequences. It emits at most three typed predictions such as `health:mcp:search-hub`, `index:basic-memory:<project>`, or `research:<question-id>`, each with `prediction_id`, `evidence_refs`, `created_at`, `expires_at`, `estimated_cost`, and `mode=shadow`. Initially it performs no prefetch and shows nothing to the owner. A tiny joiner later labels a prediction from external events: `used` if the owner/agent invokes the predicted capability or consumes a packet; `dismissed` only from an actual owner dismissal; `superseded` if the open thread is closed/replaced; `expired` otherwise. Do **not** infer success from the predicting model’s prose.

**Why inferred:** The owner wants proactive work, but their correction “now? why would I want it later” also demands timeliness (`corpus.json:11284-11288`), while “don’t need something pretentious or long” rejects elaborate overhead in the same sentence. The local system already attempts Wilson-smoothed hit-rate learning (`calibrated_silence.py:11-26`), but the outcome-producing selection event is unwired (`oats.py:34-38`). The missing product is measurement, not another predictor.

**How to build:**

1. Extend the existing event schema with `kind="anticipation"`, not a new store. Required fields: `prediction_id`, `class`, `context_key`, `evidence_refs[]`, `candidate_action`, `cost_ms_est`, `expiry_ts`, `state=predicted|prepared|used|dismissed|superseded|expired`.
2. Run the scorer as a Windows Scheduled Task alongside `ClaudeHarnessBench`; it is not a hook and does not run an LLM in v1. Use transparent rules: open thread names a tool → health candidate; source fingerprint changed → index candidate; an active research question has a stale source TTL → metadata candidate.
3. Fold reaction joining into the already-existing calibrated-silence reaction path. `used` requires a later event with matching `context_key`; `dismissed` requires owner text; no observed response remains `unknown` until expiry.
4. After 100 resolved predictions, promote only classes whose one-sided Wilson lower bound beats a cost-sensitive threshold. Keep exploration at 5-10% among reversible, read-only candidates; never explore a mutation.
5. Provide one diagnostic command that prints the confusion matrix, coverage, compute spent, time saved proxy, and Wilson interval. No button and no always-on display.

**External signal that verifies it:** A later owner turn or tool execution in a different context, joined by `context_key` and time window. For latency value, compare the later task’s time-to-first-successful-tool-result against matched no-prefetch sessions. For correctness, the predicted artifact must pass a fresh execution/query when consumed.

**SOTA citation:** Horvitz’s continual-computation work frames precomputation as allocation under expected future value and real-time refinement, rather than “use idle compute because it exists” ([IJCAI 1999 paper](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/99-2/Papers/087.pdf)). Contextual bandits learn from later user feedback; Li et al. reported a 12.5% click lift over a context-free bandit in personalized news, evidence for contextual outcome learning but not a guaranteed transfer to this owner ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.0146)). Current proactive-agent benchmarks also separate proactivity from task completion, warning that completing work is not proof that anticipation was good ([π-Bench preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14678)).

**Expected result:** A reliable owner-specific ranking is **very likely (80-90%)** after the stated minimum sample because the labels come from external actions and the implementation already has the necessary typed ledger and reaction seams. This does not mean prediction quality will be high; it means the estimate will be auditable. Evidence: ledger event types exist (`ledger.py:61-65`), the reaction logger already parses later tools/owner text (`log_reaction.py:8-23`), and the current missing selection logging is explicit (`oats.py:34-38`).

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium.  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it for shadow logging; confirm-first before any class is promoted to performing paid network prefetch or a mutation.

## Finding 2 — Inferred surprise: compile every live thread into a verified resume packet, not another reminder

**Improvement:** Turn “never drop a thread” into precomputed continuation state. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

**What it is:** For each durable open loop, prepare an expiring *resume packet*: objective, last externally verified state, exact next safe command/query, required capability, known blocker, evidence timestamps, and invalidation fingerprint. OPEN-THREADS/open-loops remains canonical; the packet is derived cache, never a competing task list. The digest says only “2 resume packets ready” plus the highest-value line, not a long recap.

**Why inferred:** The owner’s pain is not merely failure to remember a label; it is re-paying the reconstruction cost and discovering omitted requirements. In one explicit correction, they said both the requested SOTA research and clipboard upgrade were ignored (`corpus.json:11447-11454`). In another, they instructed the agent to search memory first because five of ten original columns were never built (`corpus.json:11567-11570`). Open-loops already calls forgotten threads the owner’s “#1 felt pain” (`open-loops/_common.py:5-16`), so the next step is to prepare resumable state rather than duplicate resurfacing.

**How to build:**

1. Add `packet_path`, `source_event_ids`, `source_fingerprint`, `verified_at`, and `invalid_after` as derived metadata on existing loop records or a sibling cache directory.
2. A scheduled producer selects loops with an explicit next action. It runs only read-only probes: `git status --porcelain=v2`, `git rev-parse HEAD`, workflow/task status, file existence/hash, or MCP initialize handshake. It writes a short markdown/JSON packet atomically.
3. Before surfacing, compare current HEAD/config/source hashes to the packet fingerprint. If stale, mark `needs_refresh`; never present the command as current.
4. Surface through `background-digest`; do not add a hook. Archive-before-consume already prevents silent loss (`background-digest/reader.py:35-43,161-181`).
5. When the owner resumes the thread, inject the packet only into that matching project/context. The first command remains a probe, not a mutation.

**External signal that verifies it:** In the later resumed session, independently query the repo/workflow/tool and compare it with packet fields. Count a hit only when the matching thread resumes before expiry and at least one packet artifact/command is used. A stale mismatch is a miss even if the prose sounded plausible.

**SOTA citation:** Kamar and Horvitz model reminders using three distinct predictions—forgetting, relevance, and interruption cost—rather than firing every remembered item ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/investigation-of-principles-of-context-sensitive-reminding/)). The CHI-validated Human-AI Interaction guidelines recommend remembering recent interactions, learning from behavior, timing services by context, easy dismissal, and scoping uncertain services ([Amershi et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)).

**Expected result:** Explicit-thread packets are **very likely (80-90%)** to outrank speculative topic ideas in hit rate because the future task is already owner-committed; evidence is the durable loop plus the owner’s direct forgotten-request corrections. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles the magnitude:** A/B alternate eligible resumed threads between label-only resurfacing and verified resume packets for 30 resumptions; measure reconstruction tool calls and time-to-first-progress.

**Value × effort:** transformative × medium.  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it for read-only packet compilation; confirm-first for any packet step that mutates a repo, service, or external system.

## Finding 3 — Inferred surprise: replace fleet-wide health polling with plan-conditioned warm paths

**Improvement:** Probe only capabilities implicated by a live next action, and prepare a known-good fallback route. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** This extends B1 `mcp-liveness`; it does not duplicate it.

**What it is:** A health candidate is generated only when an open thread/resume packet names a capability likely to be used soon. The producer performs the cheapest semantically meaningful probe: executable resolution using PowerShell `Get-Command` on Windows, MCP `initialize` rather than port-open, a small known-present query for search/index services, and a read-only API identity/status call for remote tools. It records `ready`, `degraded`, or `unknown`, plus a verified alternate route already present in the semantic router. Healthy results remain silent. Failures become one digest delta; only basic-memory retains its already-approved self-heal.

**Why inferred:** The owner specifically demanded that a consolidated search-hub not be silently weaker than standalone Exa and asked for similar MCP downgrades to be caught (`corpus.json:11303-11306`). The corpus shows the concrete cost of unconditioned bad routes: 275 Tavily-not-connected failures and 232 `ECONNREFUSED` fingerprints (`corpus.json:4388-4408`). Tool-preflight currently checks existence only and explicitly does no ping/auth (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/tool-preflight/preflight.py:1-10`); its `shutil.which` implementation (`preflight.py:51-55,108-114`) is also inappropriate as the sole Windows `.cmd` detector given the observed `which` false absence in the defect report (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-118`). B1 liveness already demonstrates the stronger handshake and throttling pattern (`mcp-liveness/liveness.py:9-28,352-375`).

**How to build:**

1. Add a `probe(capability, expected_use_before)` library callable from scheduled producers. Reuse `mcp-liveness` handshake code; do not create a health daemon.
2. Require two independent signals before `down`: e.g., MCP initialize failure plus process/port state; command resolution via `Get-Command` plus an actual `--version`/identity execution. Each detector ships a known-present positive fixture and proves it before reporting absence.
3. Cache healthy probes with capability-specific TTL. Probe on demand from predicted use, not every installed MCP. The existing B1 60-second throttle is evidence for this style (`mcp-liveness/liveness.py:345-358`).
4. For failure, resolve the semantic router’s next working provider and run its tiny control. Store the fallback only after that execution succeeds.
5. Append one delta to background-digest. No new hook, no per-turn prompt, no fleet dashboard.

**External signal that verifies it:** The actual protocol handshake/identity response and a separate known-positive control. Ultimate hit = the later task invokes that capability before TTL and avoids a first-attempt failure; compare against prior unprepared sessions. The same-context agent’s belief is never a label.

**SOTA citation:** Attention-sensitive alerting balances expected delayed-review cost against interruption rather than alerting on all state changes ([Horvitz, Jacobs & Hovel](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/attention-sensitive-alerting/)). HAX explicitly says proactive systems should time action based on the current task and attention ([Microsoft HAX Guideline 3](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/guideline/time-services-based-on-context/)).

**Expected result:** Plan-conditioned health checks are **very likely (80-90%)** to be the highest-hit operational precompute class because eligibility already predicts imminent tool use and the local failure base is large. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles it:** shadow-predict capability use, then enable probes for a randomized half of eligible predictions; compare first-call success and latency across 100 uses.

**Value × effort:** high × low.  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it for read-only probes and already-approved basic-memory restart; confirm-first for starting GUI/licensed tools, changing config, killing processes, or repairing other services.

## Finding 4 — Inferred surprise: refresh indexes only on source deltas, and require a retrieval canary

**Improvement:** Make index freshness a verified derived-cache invariant rather than a timer. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

**What it is:** For each existing index (basic-memory, recall-inject, semantic skill router), store a manifest of source roots, source fingerprint, index fingerprint, builder version, last successful refresh, and two canaries: one known-present and one known-absent query. Refresh only if (a) a source fingerprint changed, (b) that project has an active/open thread or recent use, and (c) resource budget permits. Build to a temporary destination, execute both canaries through the *real consumer*, and atomically swap only on pass. On failure, retain the last-good index and append one digest delta.

**Why inferred:** The corpus records 357 basic-memory searches and 353 reads (`corpus.json:13476-13485`), making retrieval a frequent dependency. The existing recall index itself contains an “Anticipatory layer … BUILT” entry with its source path and keywords (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/recall-inject/index/recall_index.json:6283-6310`), which is a known-positive canary candidate. The defect report demonstrates why a zero-result detector without a positive control cannot establish absence (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). Timer-only rebuilds waste work when sources have not changed; unverified incremental rebuilds risk silently serving partial indexes.

**How to build:**

1. Add `index-manifest.json` beside each existing index; do not centralize indexes in a new database.
2. Fingerprint Git-backed sources with `HEAD + dirty path list + relevant config hash`; fingerprint note stores using file count, max mtime, and a rolling path/size hash. Avoid Glob, whose corpus error rate is 13.32% (`corpus.json:78-84`); use bounded `rg --files`/native directory walking and explicit roots.
3. Debounce changes for 2-5 minutes, rebuild off the interaction path, cap CPU/RAM, and skip if a foreground agent is active on the same resource.
4. Query the built index with a versioned known-present fixture and a nonsense known-absent fixture through the production search API. A file existing is not enough.
5. Swap atomically; retain one rollback generation. Emit only `refresh-failed` or a material capability delta, never routine success.

**External signal that verifies it:** Independent source fingerprint change plus real consumer query results on known-positive and known-negative controls; later usefulness is a matching search that returns the newly indexed item before expiry. This satisfies the two-signal absence law.

**SOTA citation:** Continual-computation policy explicitly weighs offline precomputation against later real-time refinement ([Horvitz](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/thinking-ahead-continual-computation-policies-allocating-idle-real-time-resources-solve-future-challenges/)). The Human-AI guidelines recommend cautious adaptation and notifying users about capability changes, which supports atomic last-good retention and delta-only notification ([Amershi et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=564561)).

**Expected result:** Change-triggered active-project refresh is **likely (55-80%)** to beat periodic rebuilds on compute efficiency and freshness, based on frequent local memory use and objective source deltas. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles it:** for four weeks log eligible deltas, refresh duration, canary pass rate, and whether newly indexed items are retrieved within seven days; compare with a simulated daily timer baseline.

**Value × effort:** high × medium.  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it because it writes only replaceable derived indexes with last-good rollback; confirm-first if any indexer would transmit private source content externally.

## Finding 5 — Active-question research pre-fetch: metadata first, evidence capsule second

**Improvement:** Pre-fetch research only for a pinned unresolved question, in two cost tiers. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

**What it is:** A `research-watch` producer consumes explicit research questions from open-loops/event-ledger. Tier 0 periodically checks cheap primary-source metadata (release feed, DOI/Crossref/OpenAlex/arXiv record, official docs changelog, GitHub release/commit) using stable identifiers. Only when metadata changed *and* the question remains open does Tier 1 fetch and cache the primary artifact, extract a short evidence capsule (claim, source URL/ID, publication/update date, retrieved-at, content hash, quoted span locator, expiry), and append a one-line delta to background-digest. It never runs broad autonomous web search from vague interests.

**Why inferred:** `deep-research` is invoked 70 times, more than any other named skill (`corpus.json:13265-13283`), and the owner explicitly asks for frontier/SOTA research (`corpus.json:11297-11305`). Search is a safer first tier than fetch in this corpus: WebSearch is 0.65% errors versus WebFetch 5.57% (`corpus.json:62-76`). Research poll loops already appear (`deep_researcher_check` has three retry-loop runs, `corpus.json:10332-10339`), so prefetch can remove waiting only when attached to an active question.

**How to build:**

1. Add an open-loop predicate `research_watch` with required stable IDs/domains, TTL, and stop condition. Creating a new watch requires owner confirmation unless it is explicitly requested in the current task.
2. Schedule Tier 0 with jitter and exponential backoff. Use primary APIs/feeds and validators (`ETag`, `Last-Modified`, release/commit ID). A dead provider is skipped using Finding 3’s verified fallback.
3. If changed, Tier 1 downloads into a content-addressed cache with license/access metadata. Extract no conclusion that a parser cannot anchor to source text; a later model may interpret it, but the capsule itself is provenance, not truth.
4. Cross-check publication identity using a second independent registry when the conclusion depends on novelty/date (e.g., DOI registry plus publisher page). A single search result does not establish “latest.”
5. Expire capsules on source change or question closure. Surface one delta line, gated by calibrated-silence; full detail stays on demand.

**External signal that verifies it:** Source-server metadata/content hash plus a second identity/date registry; hit = a later owner task cites/opens the cached primary artifact or uses its stable ID before expiry. Correctness is verified again by opening the source at consumption time. Same-context summary approval is not a signal.

**SOTA citation:** ProTOD reports a two-stage proactive planner and adaptive exploratory retrieval, improving goal completion by 10% in its evaluated task-oriented dialogue setting; that supports staged retrieval, not an expectation of 10% here ([COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.614/)). π-Bench reports that prior interaction helps later hidden-intent resolution while proactive assistance remains challenging ([2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14678)). ProAgentBench reports that long-term history and real interaction data improve prediction, while synthetic data underrepresents bursty behavior; it is a 2026 preprint and should not be treated as settled deployment evidence ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04482)).

**Expected result:** Pinned-question metadata polling is **likely (55-80%)** to have a useful hit rate; full broad-paper prefetch is **unlikely (20-45%)** to do so. Evidence: frequent local research use, lower WebSearch failure, and strong task scoping. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles it:** 60-day shadow trial comparing (a) metadata changed, (b) full capsule prepared, (c) artifact later used, (d) bytes/tokens spent, and (e) stale-at-use rate by question class.

**Value × effort:** high × medium.  
**Novelty:** user-asked (research pre-fetch), with inferred-surprise two-tier restriction.  
**Autonomy:** confirm-first to create a standing topic watch or incur paid/high-volume access; just-do-it for a watch explicitly recorded by the owner within its budget.

## Finding 6 — Deliver a single opportunity digest at a breakpoint; never stream alerts

**Improvement:** Batch anticipatory output into one ranked, glanceable SessionStart/breakpoint digest with bounded deferral. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)** for thresholds and output caps after producers emit the existing format; no seventh hook is added.

**What it is:** Prepared packets accumulate silently. At main-session start—or a later natural task boundary if a session is already active—the existing background-digest surfaces at most three ranked items plus counts (`ready`, `stale`, `attention-needed`). Each item has one evidence phrase, age/expiry, and a direct action token; details are pull-only. Routine healthy/no-change work stays silent. Urgent means externally demonstrated risk of data loss or an imminent deadline, not model-assigned enthusiasm.

**Why inferred:** Prompt fatigue is a hard constraint, and the local reader currently allows 12 surfaced entries (`background-digest/reader.py:67-68,138-158`), which is too high as an owner-facing default for anticipatory work. The owner asked for speed and rejected work dragging for weeks (`corpus.json:11284-11288`). Existing calibrated-silence already raises the bar for terse prompts, late hour, correction rate, and recent interjection density (`calibrated_silence.py:28-43,109-124,342-378`); anticipatory producers should use this common gate rather than invent notification rules.

**How to build:**

1. Set anticipatory `MAX_SURFACE=3`; group overflow by class, not FIFO prose. Preserve FIFO within equal priority so nothing starves.
2. Rank by externally grounded expected utility: `P(use before expiry) × saved_latency_or_risk − compute_cost − attention_cost`. Use observed rates from Finding 1; before maturity, deterministic order is data-loss risk, explicit open thread, imminent active-tool failure, changed research source, optional idea.
3. Surface only in main sessions; the existing subagent guard already prevents theft/consumption (`background-digest/reader.py:28-33,109-121,205-220`).
4. Add `snooze`, `dismiss`, and `always/never for this class` as terse text/CLI actions, not UI buttons. Feed actual reactions to calibrated-silence.
5. Defer nonurgent items until a breakpoint with a maximum age, then quietly expire or archive them; do not nag.

**External signal that verifies it:** Owner’s later action/dismissal and observed task switching. Compare digest variants on action rate, dismissals, time-to-action, and owner corrections; the owner’s response is the label.

**SOTA citation:** Field research found users value notifications for passive awareness while acknowledging disruption ([Iqbal & Horvitz](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/notifications-and-awareness-a-field-study-of-alert-usage-and-preferences/?lang=ja)). Bounded deferral was designed to trade limited delay for lower disruption ([Horvitz, Apacible & Subramani](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=316112)). Experiments also found messaging interruptions generally disruptive and especially harmful during fast search tasks ([Cutrell, Czerwinski & Horvitz](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/notification-disruption-and-memory-effects-of-messaging-interruptions-on-memory-and-performance/?lang=ja)).

**Expected result:** One bounded digest is **very likely (80-90%)** to create less prompt fatigue than separate producer alerts, because it reduces interruption count by construction and uses the existing archive/surface-once bus. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles owner-specific benefit:** alternate three-item batched delivery with immediate per-item delivery for 30 eligible sessions, with owner opt-in, and compare dismissals, corrections, and resumed-thread completion.

**Value × effort:** high × low.  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.  
**Autonomy:** just-do-it for batching and silent expiry; confirm-first before changing an existing owner-selected global notification policy.

## Finding 7 — A hard autonomy ladder: prepare freely, verify before presenting, ask before consequential action

**Improvement:** Encode reversibility and external verification into every anticipatory candidate. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

**What it is:** Every candidate declares one of four execution levels:

* `L0 observe` — read metadata/status; autonomous.
* `L1 prepare` — write replaceable derived cache/resume packet; autonomous with quota and rollback.
* `L2 propose` — surface a command or choice; no execution.
* `L3 act` — external mutation, paid job, process kill/start beyond an existing approved self-heal, repo/config change, or message; owner confirmation required.

The level is computed from the actual tool verb and target, never from prose mentioning a command. Every “done/down/absent” result carries an external verifier type and evidence reference. A prediction may automatically move L0→L1, but never L2→L3 based on its own confidence.

**Why inferred:** The live defect report shows detectors repeatedly confused mention with use and that a false `FAILED` verdict could make the agent discard a successful tool result (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:1-83`). It also documents seven wrong absence conclusions from unvalidated probes (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`) and a fabricated orchestrator constraint propagated into about 15 prompts (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-161`). Proactivity magnifies exactly these risks unless preparation and action are structurally separated.

**How to build:**

1. Add required candidate fields `autonomy_level`, `tool_verb`, `target`, `reversible`, `rollback_ref`, `verifier`, `known_positive_control`, and `budget`.
2. Reject candidates missing a level or verifier. Parse structured tool intent, not markdown/prose payload.
3. L0/L1 code wraps its entire main path in `try/except`, exits 0, and preserves last-good output. Failure records an `unknown` state; it never turns an error into “absent.”
4. L2 output is gated and batched through Findings 1 and 6. L3 routes to the existing AskUserQuestion/Workflow permission mechanisms, not a new approval system.
5. Regression suite includes all recorded false fires plus known-present/known-absent controls and injected failures. Test producer → packet → digest → external reaction end to end; do not ship unwired levels.

**External signal that verifies it:** A different tool/protocol result or owner approval. “Prepared” requires filesystem hash/canary; “healthy” requires handshake plus control; “used” requires later invocation; “accepted action” requires owner response. The originating model’s same-context review is rejected by construction.

**SOTA citation:** The Human-AI guidelines call for scoping services under uncertainty, making rationale visible, supporting dismissal/correction, and providing global controls ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=564561)). PROBE decomposes proactivity into searching, identifying a bottleneck, and executing a resolution, and reports only 40% best end-to-end performance in its evaluated frontier-agent setting—strong evidence against collapsing detection directly into autonomous mutation ([PROBE preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19771)).

**Expected result:** This ladder is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent anticipatory false positives from becoming consequential mutations because L3 is structurally permission-gated; evidence is the explicit tool-verb discriminator and external verifier requirement. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to preserve all useful autonomy, because overclassification into L3 could create approval fatigue. **UNVERIFIED — experiment that settles calibration:** replay 200 historical tool intents, including the recorded false fires, and have an independent ruleset plus owner adjudication label the correct level; require zero unsafe underclassifications and measure overclassification rate before rollout.

**Value × effort:** transformative × low.  
**Novelty:** inferred-surprise.  
**Autonomy:** confirm-first to adopt the policy globally; after adoption, L0/L1 are just-do-it and L3 remains confirm-first.

## Architecture, hook budget, and anti-overfit gate

```text
Windows Scheduled Tasks (existing background pattern)
  ├─ likely-next shadow scorer
  ├─ resume-packet compiler
  ├─ need-scoped health probes
  ├─ delta index refreshers
  └─ active-question research watch
           │
           ├─ objective execution / source / known-positive controls
           ▼
event-ledger + existing open-loops + replaceable caches
           │
           ├─ existing calibrated-silence reaction join
           ▼
existing BACKGROUND-DIGEST queue
           │
           ▼
existing shared SessionStart dispatcher (one batched surface)
           │
           ▼
owner action/dismissal or later tool execution = external outcome
```

**Hook count:** zero new raw hooks. All producers are scheduled/background jobs. Delivery folds into the existing `background-digest/reader` entry already inside the shared SessionStart dispatcher (`_dispatch/manifest.json:53-69`). Reaction joining extends the existing calibrated-silence path. Therefore this design does not add a seventh hook and does not need to name a replacement.

**Fail-open:** every producer preserves last-good derived state and records `unknown` on its own error; every surfaced check exits 0. This follows the existing background reader (`background-digest/reader.py:205-224`), mcp-liveness contract (`mcp-liveness/liveness.py:27-28`), and tool-preflight contract (`tool-preflight/preflight.py:159-165`).

**Anti-overfit:** every proposal rides the existing scheduled-task/hook/ledger/digest bus. None introduces a second orchestration runtime, vector DB/KG, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The scorer starts as transparent rules plus empirical Wilson rates. A more elaborate model is not licensed unless the simple policy’s logged errors show a stable, material opportunity and an offline replay beats it without increasing false interventions.

**Prompt fatigue:** healthy/no-change background work is silent; details are pull-only; delivery is capped and batched. The owner is never asked to label routine examples—their natural later behavior provides the primary label.

**Never drop a thread:** predictions expire, but canonical open loops do not. A prediction’s expiry removes only a derived preparation; archive-before-consume preserves surfaced history (`background-digest/reader.py:35-43`). An open thread closes only on an external completion/owner signal, never because its reminder was displayed.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### Rejected: a permanent “dreaming” LLM daemon

It is a previously rejected second orchestration runtime, has no current outcome labels, consumes resources continuously, and would self-evaluate in the same context. PROBE’s reported 40% best end-to-end result is additional evidence that unconstrained proactive execution is not ready to be trusted ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19771)). A daemon is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to beat the proposed transparent scheduled producers on trust-adjusted value in this workspace until shadow evidence says otherwise; evidence is the hard law against same-context correction, existing hook false fires, and no measured anticipatory outcomes.

### Rejected: refresh every index nightly

This ignores whether sources changed, whether a project remains active, and whether the rebuilt index works. It spends compute without expected-value gating. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat delta-triggered active-project refresh on compute efficiency; evidence is Horvitz’s expected-value framing and the availability of objective fingerprints. **UNVERIFIED — settling experiment:** four-week shadow comparison specified in Finding 4.

### Rejected: ping all 93 touched MCP servers

Capability-use breadth does not imply current need; the corpus records 93 servers touched across history, while many are highly specialized (`corpus.json:13370-13463`). Fleet polling creates noise and can start expensive/licensed backends. Need-scoped probes are more defensible. The claim that fleet-wide polling has a lower owner-value hit rate is **likely (55-80%)**, based on sparse per-server use and notification-cost research; **UNVERIFIED — settling experiment:** compare predicted-use precision for all-fleet alerts against plan-conditioned alerts across 100 later capability uses.

### Rejected: broad autonomous web “gem hunting” from inferred interests

The owner likes research, but broad interest is not a live question. Results stale, consume attention, and can amplify the dead-MCP/fetch failure classes. Broad prefetch is **unlikely (20-45%)** to have acceptable trust-adjusted hit rate until pinned-question metadata watch proves otherwise. The experiment is Finding 5’s 60-day shadow trial.

### Rejected: a second OPEN-THREADS database or notification dashboard

Open-loops, event-ledger, basic-memory, and background-digest already exist. A new store creates split-brain and a new UI risks dead buttons/built-but-not-wired. Derived resume packets are caches tied to canonical event IDs, not a competing truth source.

## Rollout and definition of done

1. **Instrumentation first:** implement only the anticipation event schema, silent scorer, and external join. Run corruption/concurrency/fail-open tests. No proactive surface.
2. **Shadow baseline:** collect at least 100 resolved eligible predictions over at least 30 active sessions. Report class-specific coverage, acted/expired/dismissed/superseded counts, Wilson intervals, compute, and potential latency saved.
3. **Pilot the safest class:** enable L0 need-scoped health for a randomized half of predicted uses. Healthy stays silent. Require two independent detector signals and known-positive controls.
4. **Enable derived caches:** resume packets and index refresh only after end-to-end consumer canaries pass. Retain last-good rollback.
5. **Enable research metadata only:** create watches only for explicit questions. Tier 1 full fetch remains budgeted and gated.
6. **Turn on batched surface:** maximum three items, main session only, easy dismissal, calibrated-silence logging.
7. **Monthly prune:** remove any producer whose upper confidence bound remains below its cost threshold or whose output is never consumed. This is how the harness avoids accumulating impressive but dead mechanisms.

“Done” requires all of the following external evidence:

* every candidate has provenance, expiry, autonomy level, and external verifier;
* known-positive and known-negative controls pass through the actual consumer;
* producer exceptions exit 0 and preserve last-good state;
* no new raw hook appears in settings;
* no canonical thread disappears when a packet expires or is surfaced;
* at least one randomized or counterfactual comparison establishes owner-specific value;
* the owner can globally disable the anticipatory layer and suppress any class;
* no mutation occurs without existing authority/owner confirmation.
