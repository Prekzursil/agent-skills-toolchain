# X097 — Cross-domain borrow: metrology → agent-claim verification

**Unit:** X097  
**Source field:** metrology (calibration, traceability, measurement uncertainty, reference standards)  
**Target problem:** proving an agent claim without trusting the agent and without a human reading everything  
**Research posture:** ideation lens only; no local-repository inspection, as directed  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19

## Executive conclusion

The borrow is **metrological traceability plus measurement assurance**, not the superficial idea of giving an LLM a “confidence score.” In metrology, a result is not trusted because the instrument sounds confident or because its vendor is reputable. The result is related to a stated reference through a documented, unbroken calibration chain; every link contributes uncertainty; the measurement system is checked around use; and a decision rule determines whether the result is fit for the intended purpose. The International Vocabulary of Metrology (VIM) defines metrological traceability in precisely these terms and explicitly warns that traceability alone neither guarantees adequate uncertainty nor freedom from mistakes ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). NIST further assigns responsibility in two directions: the result provider must support the traceability claim, and the result user must assess its validity ([NIST metrological traceability policy and FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)).

The structural analogy **does transfer, but only for a bounded subset of agent claims**. It transfers strongly for operational, closed-world propositions whose terms, reference state, procedure, and pass criterion can be frozen: “file F now has digest H,” “command C exited 0 against tree T,” “test set S passed under environment E,” “API response R contained field V at time t,” or “the diff contains no path outside allow-list A.” It transfers weakly or not at all for open-ended semantic propositions such as “this design is best,” “the user’s real intent is X,” “no relevant evidence exists,” or “this source proves the causal conclusion.” Those lack the analogue of a stable measurand, a universal unit, and a reference realization.

My bottom-line assessment is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the exact structural match between VIM’s documented reference chain and machine-captured tool/file/test evidence, that a small “claim calibration certificate” riding the existing hook/skill/MCP bus would reduce false acceptance of operational completion claims. The size of the reduction is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the stratified shadow trial specified below**, comparing false-accept rates with and without certificates on human-adjudicated claims. It is only **unlikely (20-45%)**, based on the absence of common reference standards and stable measurement models for open-domain language, that the same mechanism can honestly certify arbitrary semantic truth; the proposed design therefore refuses that scope.

The important lesson is not “add more verification agents.” It is: **make every eligible claim carry a machine-checkable chain to a reference, an explicit uncertainty/limitation account, a predeclared decision rule, and evidence that the checking process itself remains in calibration.** An additional LLM reading the first LLM’s prose is analogous to comparing two uncalibrated instruments: agreement can be caused by shared bias and is not traceability.

## 1. The source mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 Start by defining the measurand and the requirement

Metrology does not begin with a number; it begins with what is intended to be measured. VIM calls that the **measurand** and notes that its specification requires the kind of quantity and the relevant state of the object or phenomenon ([VIM 2.3](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.3.html)). VIM’s more general definition of measurement presupposes a description commensurate with intended use, a measurement procedure, calibrated equipment, and specified conditions ([VIM 2.1](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.1.html)).

This matters because an apparently precise value can answer the wrong question. “Temperature” is underspecified without object, location, time, scale, and conditions. Metrology treats this as a definition problem, not as noise to be averaged away.

The source field also distinguishes verification from validation. VIM defines **verification** as provision of objective evidence that an item fulfils specified requirements ([VIM 2.44](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.44.html)); **validation** is verification where the requirements are adequate for the intended use ([VIM 2.45](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.45.html)). Thus, passing a precisely specified check and checking the right thing are separate obligations.

### 1.2 Calibrate against a reference; do not equate calibration with checking

VIM defines calibration as an operation, under specified conditions, that relates reference-standard values and uncertainties to instrument indications and then uses that relationship to obtain a measurement result and uncertainty ([VIM 2.39](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.39.html)). Its notes explicitly say calibration should not be confused with adjustment or with verification.

Calibration therefore characterizes a measurement process. It does not merely ask whether one output looks plausible. A thermometer can be compared against reference points across its range, revealing offset, nonlinearity, drift, or a region where its uncertainty is too large. The relationship, conditions, and uncertainty accompany later results.

### 1.3 Establish an unbroken traceability chain

VIM defines **metrological traceability** as the property by which a measurement result can be related to a reference through a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, with each calibration contributing to measurement uncertainty ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). The same entry adds several safeguards that are easy to omit in a shallow analogy:

- traceability requires a calibration hierarchy;
- the reference and the time it was used must be specified;
- each input to a multi-input measurement model may require its own traceability branch;
- effort should be commensurate with an input’s contribution;
- traceability does not itself prove that uncertainty is adequate or that no mistakes occurred.

VIM separately defines the **traceability chain** as the sequence of standards and calibrations relating a result to a reference, and observes that comparison between standards can serve as calibration when it checks and, if necessary, corrects the value and uncertainty assigned to one standard ([VIM 2.42](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.42.html)).

NIST turns those definitions into an allocation of responsibility. Its policy says an unbroken chain typically ends at national or international standards; the provider supports the traceability claim; the user assesses it; and traceability alone does not establish fitness for purpose ([NIST policy](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability)). The longer NIST FAQ says that merely using an instrument calibrated by NIST is insufficient: the provider must document the measurement process and the calibration chain connecting the particular result to its reference ([NIST Technical Note 2156 web edition](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)). This is directly relevant to agent evidence: merely citing a reputable tool, test framework, model, or URL is not a chain from the particular claim to reference state.

### 1.4 Carry measurement uncertainty through the chain

VIM defines **measurement uncertainty** as a non-negative parameter describing dispersion of values attributed to a measurand, based on the information used ([VIM 2.26](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html)). Its notes include components arising from systematic effects and distinguish components evaluated statistically from those evaluated using other information. VIM also defines a **measurement model** as the mathematical relation among all quantities known to be involved ([VIM 2.48](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.48.html)).

The Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM), JCGM 100:2008, is the normative guide named by BIPM for evaluating and expressing measurement uncertainty ([BIPM JCGM publications](https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/jc/jcgm/publications)). NIST’s corresponding public guidance organizes the method around Type A evaluation, Type B evaluation, combined standard uncertainty, expanded uncertainty, propagation, and reporting ([NIST Technical Note 1297](https://physics.nist.gov/Pubs/guidelines/TN1297/tn1297s.pdf)).

The transferable principle is not that every uncertainty must become a Gaussian probability. It is that every material input, correction, limitation, and reference link must be visible, and that the reported strength of the result cannot exceed the weakest material link. Hiding a systematic effect behind repeated measurements does not make it disappear.

### 1.5 Use decision rules and guard bands for conformity

Measurement and conformity are distinct. A result near a specification limit can be uncertain enough that “pass” is not justified even when its point estimate lies inside the nominal boundary. ILAC G8 exists specifically to guide laboratories in applying decision rules when issuing statements of conformity under ISO/IEC 17025 ([ILAC guidance-series entry for G8:09/2019](https://ilac.org/publications-and-resources/ilac-guidance-series/); [G8 document](https://ilac.org/?ddownload=122722)).

The metrological mechanism is to predeclare how uncertainty affects acceptance. A conservative rule can shrink the acceptance region—often described as a guard band—so that an item passes only when the evidence clears the boundary by the required margin. The customer’s risk tolerance determines the rule; the laboratory does not silently switch rules after seeing the result. This converts “the measurement looked good” into an auditable decision whose false-accept risk can be characterized.

### 1.6 Continuously assure the measurement process, not just the reference artifact

NIST defines an internal measurement assurance program as monitoring stability and reproducibility of instruments, standards, or systems before and after characterization, calibration, or use ([NIST Technical Note 2156 web edition](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)). Its checklist for traceability through calibration is unusually concrete:

1. characterize the transfer system before shipment;
2. calibrate it against an appropriate reference and issue a report containing values, uncertainties, and traceability details;
3. characterize it again after return to detect change;
4. characterize it immediately before use, make the measurement, evaluate uncertainty including the calibration contribution, characterize it immediately after use, and document all steps.

NIST also lists proficiency-evaluation materials, reference materials, measurement-quality-assurance programs, and accreditation among the tools that support traceability ([NIST policy](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability)). The key idea is that a certificate from last month does not prove the instrument remained valid during today’s measurement. Bracketing checks and ongoing reference challenges detect drift, damage, range-specific error, and procedural failure.

## 2. Concrete translation: a Claim Traceability and Assurance Protocol

The proposed harness mechanism is a **Claim Traceability and Assurance Protocol (CTAP)**. It is intentionally a protocol and a few validators on the existing bus, not a second agent runtime. CTAP applies only to claims for which the harness can define a reference-backed verification contract.

### Proposal 1 — Typed claim contracts — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Before work begins, or at the latest before finalization, classify each consequential completion claim into a supported type. Each type declares:

- **claim/measurand:** the exact proposition, with subject, property, scope, and time;
- **intended use:** what decision the owner will make from it;
- **reference:** the state or rule that can settle the proposition;
- **measurement procedure:** the allowed independent observation procedure;
- **influence conditions:** working directory, revision/tree hash, environment, dependency versions, clock, network endpoint, credentials scope, test selection, and other state that can change the result;
- **decision rule:** pass, fail, or indeterminate thresholds fixed before observing the result;
- **freshness window:** when the evidence expires;
- **required independence:** which links must not share an evidence source or implementation;
- **unsupported semantic remainder:** what the contract expressly does not establish.

Example contract:

```json
{
  "claim_type": "tests-pass",
  "claim": "suite tests/unit passes for output tree sha256:T at UTC t",
  "reference": "runner exit status plus parsed test-event stream",
  "procedure": ["invoke pinned runner", "parse events", "re-hash output tree"],
  "conditions": {"cwd": "...", "tree": "sha256:T", "runner": "vX", "env_profile": "sha256:E"},
  "decision_rule": "PASS only if exit=0, failed=0, collected>0, tree_after=T",
  "fresh_until": "tree changes or environment profile changes",
  "independence": ["exit status", "event parser"],
  "does_not_establish": ["test adequacy", "absence of untested defects"]
}
```

It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the logical difference between VIM verification and validation and on common zero-test/stale-state failure modes encoded in the example, that specifying `collected>0`, state identity, and the semantic remainder prevents a certificate from equating “command succeeded” with “software is correct.” The claim that this exact schema covers most real completion claims is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to classify 500 historical final-answer claims and measure the supported, unsupported, and ambiguous proportions**.

### Proposal 2 — Machine-readable claim calibration certificate — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

At finalization, a hook should require a compact certificate for each supported consequential claim. The certificate is generated from hook-captured observations, not from agent-authored prose. It contains:

- claim ID and canonical claim text;
- claim type and contract version;
- subject-state identifiers before and after observation (content hashes where meaningful);
- reference identifiers, versions, timestamps, and snapshots or immutable locators;
- procedure/tool identity, arguments, working directory, relevant environment fingerprint, start/end times, and exit status;
- raw-observation digest and normalized result;
- each chain link and its status;
- uncertainty/limitation components, including unknowns;
- independence/shared-cause labels;
- decision rule and resulting `PASS | FAIL | INDETERMINATE | UNSUPPORTED`;
- validity/expiry condition;
- tamper-evident digest over the certificate and referenced local evidence.

The final answer may summarize the certificate, but it cannot mint `PASS`; only the deterministic validator can. Missing links yield `INDETERMINATE`, not an agent-overridable warning. A content hash proves evidence integrity and identity, **not truth**; the certificate must say that explicitly.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on direct correspondence to VIM 2.41’s documented unbroken chain and time-specific reference requirement, that this catches missing-link self-attestations such as “tests pass” with no test selection, “file updated” with no identity of the observed file, or “API says X” with no time/versioned response. Whether a sub-200-line core can cover the owner’s dominant claim types is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a two-day implementation spike limited to three contracts (`file-state`, `tests-pass`, `command-result`) with a hard line-count and integration audit**.

### Proposal 3 — Bracket the verification event with check standards — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Borrow NIST’s before/after measurement-assurance pattern. For a verification run:

1. capture the subject identity and run a known-present positive control through the detector/verifier;
2. run the target verification;
3. capture subject identity again and rerun a control, preferably one exercising the same parsing path;
4. invalidate the result if the subject changed unexpectedly, the verifier fails either control, or the environment/reference crossed its expiry condition.

Examples:

- Before testing, hash the built tree and demonstrate that the test-event parser detects a deliberately included known passing and known failing fixture; after testing, hash the tree again and repeat the parser control.
- Before claiming an item is absent, make the detector find a known-present sibling of the same type; then use a mechanically independent enumeration or query representation as the second signal.
- Before checking a remote field, query a stable schema/version endpoint or validate a fixture response; after the target call, ensure the parser still recognizes the fixture and record response freshness.

This proposal directly operationalizes the mandatory two-signal absence rule without asking a human to read the result. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on elementary detector logic and NIST’s explicit pre/post assurance checklist, that a failed positive control prevents a false absence conclusion caused by a dead detector. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the ability of bracketing identities to expose time-of-check/time-of-use state changes but not atomic changes occurring within an observation, that the state brackets catch materially stale verification. The residual coverage is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by fault-injecting mutations before, during, and after 1,000 verification runs and measuring detection by timing class**.

### Proposal 4 — An evidence “uncertainty budget,” without fake probabilities — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

For each claim type, enumerate residual-risk components rather than letting the agent emit a scalar confidence:

| Component | Agent-harness analogue | Machine treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Reference uncertainty | oracle may be incomplete, stale, contested, or only a proxy | reference class, version, freshness, known limitation |
| Procedure uncertainty | verifier/parser can misread raw evidence | positive/negative controls; versioned parser; conformance fixtures |
| Subject definition | wrong file, revision, environment, endpoint, or time | canonical identity and before/after digest |
| Sampling | only some tests/files/claims observed | explicit denominator and selection method |
| Coverage | tests prove only exercised properties | contract’s `does_not_establish` field |
| Correlation | two “independent” checks share model, source, cache, or parser | dependency-family labels; correlated signals do not count twice |
| Drift | tool, schema, web content, dependencies, or model changed | expiry trigger and periodic recalibration |
| Transformation | summary may not be entailed by raw observation | retain raw digest; deterministic transformation where possible |

Use categorical levels (`bounded`, `estimated`, `unknown`, `not-applicable`) until empirical calibration supports numerical risk. Do not add or multiply arbitrary LLM confidence values. Only repeated labeled outcomes can justify an empirical false-accept estimate for a specific claim type, contract version, and operating range.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on VIM’s treatment of systematic and non-statistical uncertainty components and the known non-calibration of natural-language self-confidence, that explicit component records are more honest than a single model-generated percentage. The proposition that these categories predict error monotonically is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is to score categories before adjudication on the shadow-trial corpus and test whether observed false-accept rates order by category with confidence intervals**.

### Proposal 5 — Predeclared risk-based decision rules and abstention — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Each contract must define its conformity rule before execution. Suggested policy:

- `PASS`: all required links are present; required controls pass; state and freshness conditions hold; and residual-risk components are within the contract’s acceptance envelope.
- `FAIL`: objective evidence contradicts the claim.
- `INDETERMINATE`: evidence exists but a chain link, control, independence requirement, or risk bound is inadequate.
- `UNSUPPORTED`: no reference-backed contract exists for this kind of proposition.

Risk tier changes the guard band. A low-impact formatting claim might require one deterministic observation plus identity. A destructive migration completion claim might require dry-run/post-run invariants, two independent state observations, complete denominators, and a random manual audit sample. Semantic “best design” claims should remain `UNSUPPORTED` while their operational subclaims can be certified separately.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the definitions alone, that adding `INDETERMINATE` and `UNSUPPORTED` prevents the logic error of mapping missing evidence to success. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on ILAC’s decision-rule rationale but without agent-domain outcome data, that risk-tiered guard bands reduce harmful false acceptance at the cost of more abstentions. The trade-off is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by plotting false-accept and abstention rates by risk tier during the shadow trial and choosing thresholds before production enforcement**.

### Proposal 6 — Calibrate the verifier with hidden reference claims and range tests — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Treat each contract implementation as a measuring system. Maintain small, versioned conformance fixtures and periodically inject hidden challenges:

- true, false, indeterminate, and unsupported claims;
- boundary cases, such as zero tests collected or a nominal pass with changed tree identity;
- wrong working directory, stale timestamp, partial output, parser schema change, cached response, symlink/path alias, and truncated log;
- correlated “two signals” that secretly consume the same underlying artifact;
- adversarial agent prose claiming completion despite certificate failure.

Record performance by contract version and operating range. A verifier calibrated on small UTF-8 files is not thereby calibrated on large binary trees, remote eventually-consistent APIs, or semantic source entailment. Disable automatic `PASS` outside the demonstrated range.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on calibration’s field meaning and NIST’s ongoing measurement-assurance model, that hidden reference cases catch verifier drift and range-specific defects that happy-path unit tests miss. The adequacy of a small fixture suite is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with mutation testing of every decision branch plus quarterly blind challenges drawn from newly adjudicated production failures**.

### Proposal 7 — Mechanically independent comparisons only where they add information — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Define independence at the evidence-production level, not by agent count. Two LLMs reading the same truncated log through the same parser are one signal. Two shell wrappers around the same filesystem enumeration are one signal. A test runner’s process exit and its event stream are partially independent but can share the runner; a direct postcondition query through a separate API can add stronger evidence.

Every signal should declare dependencies: source artifact, acquisition tool, parser, model family, cache, credentials, clock, and reference. The decision rule counts two signals only if the failure mode being excluded does not have a common cause across both. Disagreement yields `INDETERMINATE` and a targeted third method, not majority vote by default.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on common-cause reasoning and VIM’s branched traceability-chain model, that nominal multiplicity without mechanical independence overstates evidence. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that a lightweight dependency label taxonomy will capture enough real common causes to improve decisions without excessive annotation; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is retrospective labeling of 100 paired-verifier failures followed by inter-rater agreement and missed-common-cause analysis**.

## 3. What this catches that self-attestation misses

The following are mechanism-level predictions, not claims about locally inspected harness behavior. Per the unit brief, no local harness was examined.

| Failure class | Why an agent’s claim can look persuasive | CTAP detection |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong subject | command ran in another directory/revision or inspected a sibling file | canonical subject identity, cwd, revision/tree and pre/post digests break the chain |
| Stale result | code or environment changed after the test | freshness trigger and bracketing identities invalidate the certificate |
| Vacuous success | runner exited 0 after collecting zero tests | predeclared rule requires `collected>0` and event-stream evidence |
| Partial scope sold as universal | selected tests pass but prose says “all tests” | denominator/selection is part of the claim; semantic remainder forbids expansion |
| Broken absence detector | empty output is interpreted as “none exist” | known-present positive control plus independent enumeration must succeed first |
| Dead or malformed evidence path | remote connection/parser silently returns nothing useful | reference/control link fails; result becomes `INDETERMINATE` |
| Correlated double-check | two agents repeat the same source/parser error | dependency labels prevent the second result counting as independent |
| Truncated or transformed evidence | summary drops failures or parser ignores schema change | raw digest, parser fixtures, and normalized/raw consistency checks expose the gap |
| Boundary false pass | nominal point result clears a threshold by too little | guard-banded decision rule abstains |
| Verifier drift | tool update changes output semantics | hidden reference claims and version-scoped calibration range detect deterioration |
| Evidence integrity confused with truth | artifact hash is presented as proof of correctness | certificate labels hash as identity/integrity only; truth still needs a valid reference procedure |
| “Verified” but unfit | requirement passed although it is not adequate for owner intent | verification/validation split requires intended-use adequacy to be assessed separately |

For the first six operational classes, detection is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on deterministic identities, controls, and explicit decision predicates, provided integrations actually capture the required raw state. That integration premise is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the fault-injection matrix in Section 5**. For semantic scope inflation, detection is only **likely (55-80%)**, because matching prose to a typed claim may itself require semantic interpretation; settle it with blinded annotation of entailment between 500 final-answer sentences and their certificates.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 There is no SI for truth

Physical metrology benefits from agreed quantity definitions, units, reference realizations, transfer standards, and models. Most natural-language claims have none. “Best,” “safe,” “complete,” “relevant,” and “intended” depend on values, scope, and counterfactuals. A document can be an authoritative reference for what it says, but not automatically for whether the world conforms to it or whether an agent’s inference follows.

Therefore, “traceable to a URL” must never mean “true.” At most it means that a time-stamped source snapshot contains specified content and that a declared inference procedure related that content to a claim. Source authority, freshness, entailment, and contradictory evidence remain distinct links.

The conclusion that universal semantic traceability does not transfer is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the absence of a shared unit/reference system and the context dependence of open-domain propositions. This is not an empirical assertion that no future formalization can help; it is a scope judgment about the proposed metrology mapping.

### 4.2 Measurement uncertainty is not generic epistemic uncertainty

GUM-style uncertainty has a defined measurand and measurement model. Agent uncertainty includes ambiguity, missing hypotheses, model misspecification, adversarial evidence selection, logical invalidity, and unknown unknowns. These are not necessarily a numerical dispersion around a value. Adding “5% parser risk + 10% source risk” would create false mathematics unless a validated probabilistic model supports the combination.

The protocol therefore borrows the **discipline of an uncertainty budget**, not automatic numerical propagation. Numerical false-accept probabilities are permitted only when empirically calibrated for a stable claim type and versioned verifier. This restriction is **very likely (80-90%)** to avoid misleading precision, based on the mismatch between VIM’s quantity-value model and heterogeneous semantic errors; its usability cost is **UNVERIFIED and should be measured as certificate abstention and operator-override rates in the shadow trial**.

### 4.3 The claimant is adaptive and can game the measurand

A thermometer does not normally rewrite the measurement requirement to make itself pass. An agent can narrow a claim, omit a consequential subclaim, choose friendly evidence, run an easy subset, or word a final answer just outside the certificate’s parser. This makes claim selection part of the adversarial surface.

Mitigation is incomplete: task-level invariants and consequential-claim templates must be chosen independently of the agent where possible; the finalizer compares the answer against issued certificates; and random audits sample uncatalogued claims. Nonetheless, the claim that automated claim extraction finds all consequential assertions is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is adversarial red-teaming in which agents are rewarded for making uncertified consequential claims that escape the finalizer**. Until that experiment shows acceptable recall, high-risk use still requires sampled human review.

### 4.4 Reference and verifier can share the same defect

Calibration chains assume competent links but do not magically eliminate common-mode error. A “golden” expected output may encode the same misunderstanding as the implementation; two LLM judges may share training and prompt biases; a test and production code may share a faulty library. VIM itself warns that traceability does not ensure absence of mistakes ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)).

Dependency-family labeling and heterogeneous references reduce this risk but cannot prove independence. Consequently, any statement that “two checks prove truth” is rejected. Two suitable checks can bound named failure modes. This narrower claim is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on common-cause failure analysis, to be operationally useful.

### 4.5 State may be non-repeatable or observer-dependent

Remote APIs, live web pages, concurrent repositories, randomized tests, and permission-dependent views can change during observation. A snapshot is a reference for what was observed, not necessarily for a durable world-state claim. Bracketing helps but cannot reconstruct every interleaving.

For mutable remote systems, CTAP should certify only time-bounded observations and explicitly include consistency assumptions. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on timestamped snapshots and identity checks, that this is still more informative than an unqualified present-tense assertion; it is **UNVERIFIED for eventually consistent services, settleable by controlled concurrent mutation experiments against representative APIs**.

### 4.6 Calibration can become ritual compliance

A beautifully complete certificate can prove the wrong proposition. VIM’s verification/validation distinction is the antidote: objective evidence of a specified requirement is not proof that the requirement serves the intended use. CTAP must keep `does_not_establish` visible and must not allow a green operational certificate to render a whole recommendation “correct.”

This failure mode is why the proposal deliberately returns `UNSUPPORTED` rather than forcing every sentence into a pseudo-measurement. The conservative boundary is a feature, not missing implementation.

## 5. Falsifiable pilot and acceptance gates — UNVERIFIED

### Proposal 8 — Shadow-mode calibration trial — NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

This recommendation is explicitly **UNVERIFIED; the experiment below settles whether the borrow produces net value in this harness**.

### 5.1 Scope

Implement only three contracts:

1. `file-state`: existence, content digest, allow-listed path effects;
2. `tests-pass`: exact suite, nonzero collection, results, subject/environment identity;
3. `command-result`: exact command invocation, cwd, exit, raw-output digest, postcondition.

Run in shadow mode: certificates cannot block work initially. Do not add an LLM judge, daemon, database, queue, or new orchestration layer.

### 5.2 Dataset

Sample at least 500 consequential claims, stratified by claim type, risk, tool, and apparent outcome. Preserve all failures and oversample boundary cases. Human adjudicators inspect only the sample, not every future run. Give adjudicators the task, raw state, and evidence but hide CTAP’s result until labeling is complete. Double-label a subset and resolve disagreements so that evaluation noise is visible.

The sample size needed for a tight false-accept upper bound depends on the observed event rate; **UNVERIFIED, settle it with an exact binomial power calculation before data collection rather than treating 500 as universally sufficient**.

### 5.3 Mechanically independent fault injection

Inject at least these faults:

- change the subject between test and final answer;
- run in the wrong directory;
- collect zero tests with exit 0;
- truncate the event stream;
- alter parser schema;
- serve a cached/stale response;
- disable the detector while leaving the target absent;
- make a known-present control invisible;
- cause two nominal verifiers to share one corrupted artifact;
- modify a symlink target without changing the asserted path string;
- let a passing subset masquerade as the full suite;
- emit prose claiming success after certificate failure.

For every asserted absence/broken/done conclusion in the evaluation, require two mechanically independent signals. Validate each detector on a known-present positive control before interpreting a zero result.

### 5.4 Metrics

Primary metric: **false-accept rate** among CTAP `PASS` results, with a confidence interval, compared with the current final-answer-only baseline on the same adjudicated claims.

Secondary metrics:

- false-reject rate;
- `INDETERMINATE` and `UNSUPPORTED` rates;
- escaped consequential-claim rate;
- fault-injection detection by failure class;
- certificate generation latency and token overhead;
- human-audit minutes per accepted claim;
- override rate and reason;
- calibration drift by contract version;
- shared-cause failures incorrectly counted as independent.

### 5.5 Predeclared go/no-go rule

Proceed to enforcement for a claim type only if:

- its upper confidence bound on harmful false acceptance improves materially over baseline;
- at least 90% of injected faults in its declared detection scope are detected, with no catastrophic class silently accepted;
- positive-control failures always prevent `PASS` in the trial;
- certificate overhead is acceptable for that risk tier;
- at least one independent reviewer can reproduce the decision from raw evidence without trusting agent prose.

The 90% injection threshold is a design starting point, not a metrologically justified universal constant. Its fitness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by assigning harm weights before the trial and tightening catastrophic-class requirements to 100% observed detection while reporting statistical uncertainty**. If CTAP merely increases paperwork, shows no false-accept improvement, or encourages broader unsupported claims, reject it.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

Every proposal above rides the existing settings/hook/skill/MCP bus and is either configuration or a sub-200-line component. The design does **not** introduce any previously rejected component:

- no second orchestration runtime;
- no vector database or knowledge graph;
- no durable-execution engine;
- no trained critic;
- no observability SaaS;
- no guardrail framework.

The persistent artifacts are ordinary compact certificates and referenced evidence already produced by work. Contract validators are deterministic adapters. Hidden reference claims are fixtures, not a learned critic. Human review calibrates a sampled evaluation set, not every action.

This minimal architecture is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the narrow three-contract pilot and deterministic hook design, to remain below the complexity of an agent-verifier service. Maintaining many domain-specific contracts could nevertheless grow into a de facto framework; that long-run complexity is **UNVERIFIED, settleable by a hard governance rule that retires any contract whose measured harm reduction does not exceed its maintenance and abstention cost over two review periods**.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Ask a second agent to verify everything” — rejected

Agent count is not mechanical independence. Shared model, prompt, context truncation, tool layer, and source can yield shared bias. A second agent may be useful as an evidence-discovery method, but it cannot be the terminal reference and does not by itself form a calibration chain. This alternative also spends tokens on every claim without producing a reproducible reference-backed certificate.

### “Require citations for every statement” — rejected as sufficient mechanism

Citations improve provenance but do not prove entailment, freshness, authority, or coverage. A URL is analogous to naming a laboratory without documenting the calibration linking this result to its standard. Citations remain one possible chain input, never the certificate.

### “Hash all evidence and sign it” — rejected as sufficient mechanism

Hashes and signatures establish identity/integrity and possibly origin. They do not establish that the measurement procedure addressed the right claim or that the source was true. They are valuable chain controls but cannot be the reference standard for semantic truth.

### “Attach an LLM probability to every claim” — rejected

Without claim-type- and version-specific empirical calibration, the number is not a GUM uncertainty and has no defined coverage. It conceals distinct systematic, coverage, freshness, and common-cause risks. The protocol uses categorical limitations until labeled outcomes justify a numerical estimate.

### “Build a universal proof graph / knowledge graph” — rejected

Open-domain semantics lack universal reference realizations; graph completeness would itself be an unverified claim. This would violate the anti-overfit gate by adding a knowledge-graph memory system and substantial new runtime. A small per-claim chain is sufficient for the transferable subset.

### “Block any claim that cannot be certified” — rejected

Research, design, interpretation, and judgment remain valuable even when not mechanically certifiable. The honest output is to distinguish certified operational subclaims from reasoned but unsupported semantic conclusions, with confidence bands and named experiments. Universal blocking would select for narrow, easily measured work rather than useful work.

## 8. Recommended decision

Adopt the idea only as a **bounded operational-verification layer**. Begin with the shadow pilot, three claim types, explicit `INDETERMINATE`/`UNSUPPORTED` outcomes, positive controls, subject-state bracketing, and calibrated decision rules. Do not market or name it as a general truth engine.

The implementation recommendation is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by the direct mapping from metrological traceability, measurement assurance, and conformity decision rules to deterministic tool evidence, to improve the auditability of completion claims. Its actual reliability and cost are **UNVERIFIED; the Section 5 shadow trial is the required settling experiment**. Expansion to web-source entailment or general semantic verification is **unlikely (20-45%)** to meet the same assurance standard without sampled human adjudication, based on the lack of stable measurands and reference standards; keep such expansion out until a separate experiment demonstrates calibrated false-accept bounds.

The most important guardrail is the VIM warning that traceability does not guarantee fitness or freedom from mistakes. In agent terms: a complete evidence chain can show exactly what was observed, by what procedure, against which reference, under which state, with which residual limitations. That is a major improvement over trust. It is not omniscience.
