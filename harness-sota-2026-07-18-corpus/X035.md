# X035 — Cross-domain borrow: aviation safety → compute and quota cost-control

**Unit:** X035  
**Source field:** aviation safety (fuel/energy planning, threat-and-error management, crew resource management, checklists, safety reporting, automation surprise)  
**Target:** spend compute and quota where it is likely to change the answer, rather than uniformly  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external ideation only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect the local harness, repository, or audit findings. Consequently, “what it catches” below names failure classes the proposed mechanism can detect; it does not claim those classes are present beyond the target problem stated in the brief.

## Executive verdict

The analogy **does transfer, but only in a narrow and useful form**. The best borrow is not “pilots use checklists, so agents need more checklists.” It is the architecture of commercial-aviation **fuel/energy planning and in-flight fuel management**, supplemented by **Threat and Error Management (TEM)** and **automation-mode annunciation**:

1. decompose one total resource into purpose-labelled buckets;
2. size normal consumption from task-specific empirical history;
3. keep contingency separate from a protected final reserve;
4. re-estimate at explicit decision points using actual progress;
5. spend discretionary margin only under named conditions and visible authority;
6. declare a changed operating state when the reserve is threatened;
7. learn from normal operations and near misses, not only catastrophic overruns.

In aviation, this system reconciles efficiency with uncertainty without pretending that every flight needs the same fuel. EASA explicitly describes fuel schemes as performance-based and says the newer rules permit operational efficiency, cost, and environmental benefits while retaining safety requirements ([EASA Opinion 02/2020](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/opinions/opinion-022020)). Its operative rules require aircraft-specific consumption data, account for planned and unplanned components separately, and require in-flight replanning ([EASA Easy Access Rules for Air Operations, CAT.OP.MPA.181](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/online-publications/easy-access-rules-air-operations)).

Translated to an agent harness, this becomes a **Compute Flight Plan (CFP)**: a small policy and accounting layer over existing model/tool/agent buses. It does not choose a uniformly cheap or expensive mode. It starts with a task-class baseline, preserves a small “answer assurance reserve,” and releases extra compute only when evidence says another action has a plausible decision-changing role. It also makes escalation and reserve use conspicuous to the agent and owner.

The central prospective claim is **UNVERIFIED**: a CFP will improve answer quality per unit compute in this owner’s workload. The settling experiment is a blinded replay A/B test on a stratified set of historical tasks, comparing status quo against CFP on owner preference, objective completion tests, total tokens/tool calls/wall time, unnecessary-action rate, and false-stop rate. I assess improvement as **likely (55-80%)**, based on the strong structural match described below, EASA’s use of empirical consumption monitoring and in-flight replanning, and the fact that the mechanism is a thin policy layer; uncertainty remains because agent compute has weaker predictability and different failure costs than aircraft fuel.

I reject four tempting but structurally unsound transfers:

- Do **not** make every agent step challenge-response. Cockpit checklists cover selected critical configurations; turning all reasoning into a checklist taxes routine work and encourages box-ticking.
- Do **not** treat “final reserve” as permission to hide unused budget until the end. Compute cannot recover time already lost, and some tasks need early exploration.
- Do **not** equate model confidence with a fuel gauge. Token counts are observable; answer correctness is latent.
- Do **not** create a second orchestration runtime, durable workflow engine, vector database, trained critic, or observability service. None is needed for this borrow.

## 1. The source mechanism in its own field

### 1.1 Fuel is planned as labelled components, not one undifferentiated allowance

EASA CAT.OP.MPA.181 requires an operator to establish a fuel/energy planning and in-flight replanning policy, carry enough usable fuel/energy to complete the planned flight and tolerate deviations, and base planning on current aircraft-specific consumption-monitoring data where available. The plan distinguishes taxi, trip, contingency, destination-alternate, final-reserve, additional, extra, and commander-discretionary fuel. It also requires replanning when the flight proceeds along a route or to a destination other than originally planned ([EASA CAT.OP.MPA.181 consolidated rules](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/online-publications/easy-access-rules-air-operations)).

Those categories have different meanings:

| Aviation component | Function in aviation | Why separation matters |
|---|---|---|
| Taxi | Predictable ground movement before takeoff | Keeps non-mission overhead visible |
| Trip | Expected consumption for the planned route | The base plan is inspectable and comparable with actual burn |
| Contingency | Variance between planned and actual trip consumption | Ordinary uncertainty is budgeted without consuming emergency margin |
| Alternate | Resource to reach a different safe endpoint | The plan funds a fallback, not just the preferred result |
| Final reserve | Protected minimum at the endpoint | Crossing it changes the operational state; it is not ordinary padding |
| Additional | Specific critical-scenario requirement | Known high-impact threats receive explicit coverage |
| Extra | Anticipated delay or operational constraint | Foreseeable task-specific conditions are not disguised as generic contingency |
| Discretionary | Commander-added amount | Judgment exists, but authority and amount are visible |

This is more than bookkeeping. Categories prevent an operator from counting the same safety margin twice, reveal why excess is carried, and support different rules for using each component. The FAA likewise describes preflight fuel as destination consumption plus separately considered alternate, reserve, en-route reserve, other required, and contingency fuel, with aircraft-specific rates and forecast conditions ([FAA fuel-planning guidance](https://www.faa.gov/media/32801)).

### 1.2 Contingency can be empirical and route-specific

EASA’s acceptable means of compliance allows a statistical contingency-fuel method based on deviation between planned and actual trip fuel. It requires a continuous two-year data period before use and enough data for the particular city-pair/aircraft combination to be statistically significant; operators monitor each combination and calculate the needed contingency from its own history ([EASA Easy Access Rules, AMC6 CAT.OP.MPA.181](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/online-publications/easy-access-rules-air-operations)).

The safety insight is not the literal “two years.” It is that margin is **conditional on an operational class and calibrated from observed forecast error**, rather than being uniform. A transatlantic winter flight and a short familiar route need not receive the same uncertainty allowance, but a reduced allowance must be supported by data and management-system maturity. EASA’s 2020 Opinion describes three performance levels and ties increased efficiency to the robustness of the operator’s management system ([EASA Opinion 02/2020](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/opinions/opinion-022020)).

### 1.3 In-flight checks convert a static plan into a closed loop

The policy is not “calculate once and hope.” EASA’s rules distinguish planning from in-flight management and require checks and replanning. A planned route may include a decision point, with enough fuel computed for either continuing to the commercial destination or proceeding to an optional refuelling destination; the applicable quantities are recomputed from that decision point ([EASA Easy Access Rules, AMC6 CAT.OP.MPA.181 reclearance/decision-point procedure](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/online-publications/easy-access-rules-air-operations)). The FAA Aeronautical Information Manual instructs pilots to declare a low-fuel emergency when remaining usable fuel indicates traffic priority is needed for a safe landing ([FAA AIM 5-5-15](https://www.faa.gov/Air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap5_section_5.html)).

The deeper mechanism is **forecast → measure → compare → replan → change state**. Reserve is meaningful because the system measures remaining resource against remaining route and has named responses before exhaustion.

### 1.4 TEM focuses countermeasures where threats and errors can propagate

Threat and Error Management models normal operations as a progression: threats can induce crew errors; mismanaged errors can produce an undesired aircraft state; that state can produce an unsafe outcome. Countermeasures are grouped into planning, execution, and review. They include system-provided resources such as SOPs and checklists plus crew actions such as briefings, monitoring, cross-checking, and decision-making ([ICAO-sourced TEM overview hosted by SKYbrary](https://skybrary.aero/index.php/articles/threat-and-error-management-tem-flight-operations); [ICAO TEM paper by Dan Maurino](https://skybrary.aero/sites/default/files/bookshelf/515.pdf)).

TEM does **not** assume errors can be eliminated. It seeks early detection and recovery while a return to the normal state remains possible. That matters for cost-control: an individual expensive action is not necessarily waste; the target is a chain in which uncertainty, retrying, or route drift continues without restoring answer progress.

The FAA’s CRM guidance gives the team-level complement. CRM is “effective use of all available resources”—people, hardware, and information—and centers situation awareness, communication, task allocation, decision-making, monitoring, and cross-checking within SOPs ([FAA AC 120-51E](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-51E.pdf), especially pp. 1–2). The same circular says briefings should address the most threatening situations, emphasizes the monitoring role, and recommends recurrent practice and specific behavioral feedback rather than a one-time lecture (pp. 1, 8–10).

### 1.5 Automation surprise shows why invisible auto-escalation is dangerous

NASA researchers Sarter and Woods define a central pilot-automation problem as lack of mode awareness: operators do not know the automation’s current and future status or behavior, so an unexpected action or failure to act becomes an “automation surprise.” Their A320 study attributes such surprise to mismatches between human and machine properties and capabilities, not simply to inattentive pilots ([NASA Technical Reports Server record 20040112190](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040112190)).

NASA’s current human-systems guidance turns this into explicit design requirements: notify operators of safety-critical mode changes; record and expose automation state and performance data; delineate whether the human or automation is responsible for an operation; and provide override/shutdown capability ([NASA Human Integration Design Handbook, §10.6](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/10-0-crew-interfaces-vol-2/)).

For cost-control, an automatic switch from “single pass” to “multi-agent,” “cheap model” to “premium model,” or “targeted search” to “broad crawl” is a mode change. If it is invisible, the owner cannot calibrate trust or intervene, and the agent may reason as though it remains in the cheaper mode.

### 1.6 Reporting closes the calibration loop, but “blameless” is imprecise

Aviation’s useful concept is **just culture**, not consequence-free “blamelessness.” ICAO states that reporting depends on trust and that a positive culture supports reporting organizational or personal errors. Its roadmap says a no-blame culture is neither desirable nor feasible: acceptable and unacceptable behavior must be distinguished; protection encourages reports that reveal underlying risk, and feedback returns learning to the workforce ([ICAO Safety Management Manual resources, Chapter 3](https://www.icao.int/safety-management/SMI/SMM/Chapter%203); [ICAO Global Aviation Safety Roadmap, Focus Area 6](https://www.icao.int/safety/fsix/Library/Global_Aviation_Safety_Roadmap_Part_2.pdf)).

The transfer is a non-punitive record of “compute near misses”: a task that consumed contingency, an escalation that did not change the answer, an early stop that missed decisive evidence, or a mode transition that surprised the owner. The purpose is calibration, not scoring an agent’s moral worth.

## 2. Structural mapping: what transfers and what does not

### 2.1 The mapping

| Aviation safety structure | Agent-harness analogue | Transfer quality |
|---|---|---|
| Route/flight objective | Explicit acceptance criteria and evidence obligations | Strong: both define a terminal condition |
| Aircraft/city-pair consumption history | Task-family/model/tool cost and completion history | Moderate: agent tasks are more heterogeneous |
| Trip fuel | Minimum credible execution plan | Strong: both represent expected resource for a chosen route |
| Contingency fuel | Budget for ordinary uncertainty, tool failure, and one recovery path | Strong if separated from retries with no new information |
| Alternate fuel | Budget for a distinct fallback method/model/source | Strong: a fallback must be funded to be real |
| Final reserve | Protected answer-assurance actions (verification, contradiction check, deliverable integrity) | Moderate: unlike fuel, assurance actions vary by task |
| Additional/extra fuel | Explicit allowance for known hazards or anticipated constraints | Strong: e.g. unstable API, unfamiliar domain, large artifact |
| Commander discretion | Owner or lead-agent authorized extra compute | Moderate: authority can be automated only within bounds |
| Fuel gauge and predicted landing fuel | Actual spend plus forecast-to-complete | Weak-to-moderate: spend is measurable, completion is not |
| Decision point/reclearance | Evidence checkpoint: continue, divert, escalate, or land | Strong if checkpoints are semantic rather than every N tokens |
| TEM threat→error→undesired state | uncertainty/tool fault→unproductive action→loop or degraded answer | Strong as a diagnostic taxonomy, not a causal law |
| Mode annunciation | Visible budget/model/parallelism state with reason and authority | Strong: both prevent hidden automation transitions |
| Safety report/debrief | Structured cost/quality near-miss event and offline policy update | Moderate: requires enough repeated task classes |

### 2.2 Why this is not just a metaphor

Five structural properties genuinely align:

1. **Finite, observable resource.** Fuel mass and compute consumption can be measured continuously.
2. **Uncertain route cost.** Weather/traffic and tool/model behavior make exact completion cost uncertain.
3. **Choice among routes.** Flights can continue/divert; agents can continue a method, switch methods, reduce scope with permission, or stop and ask.
4. **Asymmetric endpoint failure.** Running out before a safe endpoint is worse than arriving with some unused resource. The magnitude differs drastically, but the direction is shared.
5. **Historical calibration opportunity.** Repeated aircraft-route combinations and repeated task-tool-model families both generate forecast-versus-actual residuals.

The transfer of **layered budgets plus replanning** is therefore **very likely (80-90%)** to be conceptually sound, with evidence from the five shared structures and EASA’s operational use of exactly those controls. “Conceptually sound” does not mean empirically beneficial in this harness; that benefit remains **UNVERIFIED** pending the replay A/B experiment described above.

### 2.3 The disanalogies that constrain the design

#### A. Fuel has a physical gauge; answer quality does not

Tokens, dollars, tool calls, elapsed time, and remaining quota are directly observable. “Distance to correct answer” is not. A model’s self-reported confidence is not an equivalent gauge and may be miscalibrated. Therefore the harness must forecast remaining **planned actions**, not claim to forecast correctness precisely. It can say “two required citations and one test remain,” not “the answer is 87% complete.”

#### B. Fuel is largely fungible; compute actions have path-dependent information value

One kilogram of fuel is much like another. One thousand tokens spent reading a decisive specification may dominate ten thousand spent exploring irrelevant files. Compute consumed early can change the state of knowledge and the value of later actions. Therefore a reserve cannot be a rigid percentage blindly locked until the end. It must reserve **capabilities/actions**—for example, a test run and a contradiction search—while allowing early use when those actions are informative.

#### C. Aviation endpoints and minima are regulated; task quality thresholds are plural

A safe landing is clearer than “best answer.” Agent tasks span coding, research, explanation, design, and external action, with different loss functions. Any universal compute table will overfit. The unit of calibration must be a coarse task family plus risk tier, and owner preference must outrank a generic efficiency score.

#### D. Aviation has stable fleets/routes; frontier models and tools drift

Two years of data can be sensible for a city-pair/aircraft combination. It is far too stale for rapidly changing models, prompts, prices, quotas, and tool reliability. Agent calibration needs recency weighting and cold-start fallbacks, with invalidation on model/tool/policy changes.

#### E. Aviation’s cost of reserve violation is safety-critical; most compute overruns are economic

The harness must not import emergency rhetoric or rigid prohibitions where the owner may rationally buy a better answer. “Reserve threatened” should mean “change mode, expose tradeoff, and obtain authority where required,” not “pretend a token overrun is a mayday.”

#### F. Crew independence is real; subagents may share correlated failure

Two pilots have distinct perception and can challenge one another. Multiple agents using the same model, prompt lineage, and evidence may produce correlated errors. Thus “CRM” does not justify routine fan-out. A cross-check earns budget only when it is mechanically diverse: a different evidence source, executable test, alternative model family, or explicit disconfirming role.

These disanalogies rule out literal transplantation but do not defeat the narrower closed-loop budgeting mechanism.

## 3. Proposed harness translation

## 3.1 UNVERIFIED proposal — Compute Flight Plan ledger

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Before material execution, the lead agent emits a compact machine-readable plan into existing run state:

```json
{
  "task_family": "repo-change",
  "risk_tier": "medium",
  "acceptance": ["requested behavior implemented", "targeted tests pass", "diff reviewed"],
  "route": [
    {"action": "locate", "budget": {"tool_calls": 8}},
    {"action": "inspect", "budget": {"tokens": 12000}},
    {"action": "edit", "budget": {"tool_calls": 4}},
    {"action": "verify", "budget": {"tokens": 8000, "tool_calls": 4}}
  ],
  "contingency": {"tokens": 8000, "tool_calls": 5, "uses": ["one failed-tool recovery", "one alternative hypothesis"]},
  "alternate": {"tokens": 6000, "route": "manual targeted inspection if primary index/search fails"},
  "assurance_reserve": ["run relevant test", "inspect final diff", "check acceptance list"],
  "known_threats": ["unfamiliar build system"],
  "escalation_authority": {"automatic_up_to": "contingency", "owner_required_for": ["second fan-out", "premium-model escalation above cap"]}
}
```

Budgets are ceilings and forecasts, not quotas to consume. Omitted dimensions are not synthesized into false precision. A short, routine request may use a one-line profile rather than JSON.

This proposal depends on an **UNVERIFIED** premise: coarse task families predict enough cost variance to outperform a single default. The settling experiment is to train no model at all: bucket 200–500 historical runs by task family/risk/tool route, calculate forecast residuals under (a) one global median and (b) per-family recency-weighted quantiles, and compare pinball loss and false-reserve-breach rate on a held-out temporal slice. The premise is **likely (55-80%)**, because EASA’s statistical contingency method benefits from route/aircraft conditioning and agent tasks visibly differ in required action types; the wide band reflects much greater task heterogeneity.

**What it catches that uniform budgeting misses:**

- predictable overhead being hidden inside “reasoning”;
- contingency silently spent on routine work before any failure occurs;
- a fallback claimed in prose but left unfunded;
- verification crowded out by exploration;
- a large budget assigned without a named threat or route;
- a small budget assigned to a task with multiple mandatory acceptance criteria.

## 3.2 UNVERIFIED proposal — semantic decision points and evidence-gain checks

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

At natural phase boundaries—not every token interval—the harness performs a short “fuel check”:

1. What acceptance item was retired since the last checkpoint?
2. What new evidence changed the hypothesis, plan, or deliverable?
3. What remains, and what is the forecast cost to the next safe endpoint?
4. Which bucket funds the next action?
5. Continue, divert to an alternate method, escalate mode, ask the owner, or finish?

Suggested decision points are: after initial reconnaissance; after the first failed primary method; before spawning or widening fan-out; before premium-model escalation; after a test or external source invalidates the plan; when 50% and 80% of contingency are consumed; and before touching assurance reserve.

The key gate is **prospective marginal value**, expressed qualitatively to avoid fake precision:

```text
NEXT ACTION: run focused integration test
DECISION IT CAN CHANGE: whether implementation is complete
NOVEL SIGNAL: executable behavior, independent of code inspection
COST CLASS: medium
IF NEGATIVE: return to edit route
IF POSITIVE: land after final diff review
BUCKET: assurance reserve
```

An action with no answer-changing decision, no novel signal, and no required deliverable contribution is cancelled. Repeated actions are permitted when the failure changes the conditions (for example, repaired credentials) or the second action is mechanically independent; identical retries without changed state consume a retry allowance and then trigger diversion.

This is **UNVERIFIED** because agents may produce plausible but post-hoc “decision it can change” rationales. The settling experiment is a blinded annotation study: humans label whether proposed actions could change the final answer, compare labels with the agent’s declaration, and then A/B enforce the gate on replayable tasks. I assess the gate as **likely (55-80%)** to reduce obvious low-value calls, supported by TEM’s planning/execution/review cycle and the semantic clarity of the fields; it is **roughly even (45-55%)** to improve total owner preference before tuning, because false cancellation could suppress useful exploration.

**What it catches:** retry loops with no changed precondition; broadening searches after decisive evidence is already available; fan-out whose outputs cannot alter a decision; continuing the original route after its premise has failed; and stopping when a funded acceptance item remains.

## 3.3 UNVERIFIED proposal — explicit compute modes and annunciation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Define a small, visible state machine using existing settings and run metadata:

```text
ECONOMY → NORMAL → CONTINGENCY → ASSURANCE-RESERVE
             ↘ DIVERTED (alternate method)
             ↘ OWNER-AUTHORIZED EXTRA
```

Every mode transition records and displays:

- old mode → new mode;
- trigger/evidence;
- expected decision it can change;
- added ceiling and current remaining quota;
- who authorized it (policy, lead agent, or owner);
- rollback/stop condition.

The harness should never silently move into parallel agents, a higher-priced model, wider web acquisition, or recursive investigation. Conversely, it should visibly announce a forced downgrade caused by quota pressure, because degraded capability can also surprise the owner.

This proposal is **UNVERIFIED** as a cost-control intervention; the settling experiment is an instrumented usability test comparing owners’ ability to predict current cost mode, stop an unwanted escalation, and identify why a downgrade occurred, with and without annunciation. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to improve mode awareness, based directly on NASA’s mode-change notification, state-data availability, responsibility delineation, and override requirements; it is only **likely (55-80%)** to reduce spend, because visibility does not guarantee intervention.

**What it catches:** hidden auto-escalation, nested fan-out, premium-model selection inherited from a subtask, silent quota-driven quality degradation, and disagreement between the lead agent’s believed mode and actual runtime mode.

## 3.4 UNVERIFIED proposal — protect assurance actions, not a token percentage

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

The aviation “final reserve” should translate into a list of protected answer-assurance actions selected by task type:

- code change: targeted test, diff inspection, acceptance-criteria check;
- factual research: primary-source check, date/currentness check, contradiction search, citation-to-claim check;
- destructive/external action: target resolution, authority check, preview/dry run where available;
- design: explicit alternative, failure-mode review, and decision rationale;
- simple explanation: answer the user directly and check that every asked part is covered—no forced heavyweight ritual.

Exploration cannot consume these capabilities without a visible reserve transition. If the budget cannot fund them, the harness must either simplify the route, request more authority/quota, or state the verification gap inline. This is a semantic protection rule; it does not hold back an arbitrary 10% of tokens.

The proposal rests on an **UNVERIFIED** premise that precommitting assurance actions reduces the common pattern of spending heavily on exploration and then truncating verification. The settling experiment is to compare matched runs near a hard cap, measuring completion of predeclared assurance actions, objective defect rate, and owner preference. It is **likely (55-80%)** to improve assurance completion, because category separation prevents fungible exploration from invisibly consuming the allocation; impact on overall answer quality is **roughly even (45-55%)** until task-specific reserve templates are calibrated.

**What it catches:** “done” declarations without the planned independent signal, citations gathered but not matched to claims, code edited but not tested, and budget exhaustion immediately before deliverable integrity checks.

## 3.5 UNVERIFIED proposal — TEM-shaped compute near-miss events

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Record a compact event only on meaningful triggers, not every call:

```json
{
  "event": "contingency_exhausted",
  "task_family": "external-research",
  "threat": "primary source inaccessible",
  "error": "three retries without state change",
  "undesired_state": "assurance reserve threatened",
  "recovery": "diverted to alternate authoritative source",
  "answer_changed": true,
  "cost_delta": {"tokens": 4100, "tool_calls": 4},
  "policy_candidate": "retry once, then alternate"
}
```

Other triggers: mode surprise reported by owner; escalation that did not change the answer; false stop found during verification; alternate route used; reserve entered; and high-cost success that reveals an underestimated task class. Store bounded structured events with the existing run record and aggregate simple counts/quantiles offline. Do not add a vector store or observability platform.

Just-culture translation means the record describes conditions, decisions, and recovery, not “bad agent.” It also records successful recoveries and useful discretionary spend, preventing a cost-only metric from punishing expensive actions that changed the outcome.

This is **UNVERIFIED** because event labels such as `answer_changed` may be unreliable when self-reported. The settling experiment is dual annotation of a sample by the agent and an independent replay/evaluator, measuring agreement and whether policies derived from events reduce recurrence on held-out cases. It is **likely (55-80%)** that trigger-based events will reveal reusable cost patterns, supported by ICAO’s reporting-and-feedback logic and TEM taxonomy; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** that self-report alone will be accurate enough for automatic policy changes, because the acting model is not mechanically independent.

**What it catches:** ordinary runs that nearly exhausted quota but technically “succeeded”; recurring threat/error chains hidden by aggregate cost; beneficial expensive actions that a blunt cost cap would remove; and policies whose failure mode is false economy rather than overrun.

## 3.6 UNVERIFIED proposal — CRM-style challenge for expensive irreversible branches

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it).**

Use existing agent/delegation capability only at a few expensive branch points. Before second-order fan-out, broad crawl, or consuming owner-authorized extra, require one short challenger response:

```text
Claimed need: spawn three investigators.
Challenge: What distinct decision can each change? Are their signals independent?
Cheapest adequate alternative: one investigator plus executable test.
Disposition: revised to one investigator.
```

This is closer to CRM monitoring and appropriate questioning than routine “multi-agent debate.” FAA CRM guidance explicitly emphasizes monitoring/cross-checking, appropriate assertiveness and advocacy, and no negative repercussions for appropriate questioning ([FAA AC 120-51E](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-51E.pdf), pp. 1, 6, 9–10).

The proposal is **UNVERIFIED** because same-model challengers may be correlated or may ritualistically approve escalation. The settling experiment is to compare (a) no challenge, (b) same-model challenge, and (c) mechanically diverse check such as a cheap model plus deterministic cost calculation, measuring cancellation of non-answer-changing branches and false vetoes. Same-model challenge is **roughly even (45-55%)** to create net value; a mechanically diverse challenge is **likely (55-80%)**, based on independence of its cost signal but still subject to poor value estimation.

**What it catches:** fan-out justified only by “thoroughness,” duplicated roles, no synthesis budget, and escalation whose cheapest adequate alternative was never considered.

## 4. The integrated operating procedure

The minimal end-to-end procedure is deliberately small:

### Preflight

1. State acceptance criteria and task family.
2. Select a base route and coarse expected budget from recent comparable runs; use conservative defaults on cold start.
3. Name threats that materially change cost or verification needs.
4. Allocate contingency, alternate route, and protected assurance actions.
5. State automatic versus owner-required escalation authority.

### Execution

6. Account actual spend by route action and bucket.
7. At semantic decision points, state progress, novel evidence, forecast-to-next-endpoint, and next action’s decision-changing role.
8. Divert after unchanged-state retry limits or invalidated route assumptions.
9. Announce every compute-mode transition.

### Landing

10. Complete protected assurance actions or disclose which could not be funded/performed.
11. Stop when acceptance criteria are met and the next action has no plausible answer-changing decision.
12. Emit a near-miss event only when a trigger fired; update statistical profiles offline after sufficient observations.

This procedure deliberately uses aviation’s **planning, execution, review** rhythm without importing a cockpit checklist at every step.

## 5. Concrete examples

### Example A: research question with unstable current facts

**Base route:** identify authoritative primary sources, fetch them, synthesize.  
**Threat:** current information may have changed; source may be inaccessible.  
**Contingency:** one alternate query and one alternate authoritative source.  
**Assurance reserve:** publication-date check, claim-to-source audit, contradiction search.  
**Decision point:** after two primary sources answer the factual core.

If both sources converge and the contradiction search is clean, another ten broad searches have no named decision to change and are cancelled. If sources disagree, contingency funds targeted resolution because it can change the answer. If the official site fails twice with unchanged network state, the route diverts rather than repeatedly burning quota.

Expected catch is **UNVERIFIED**: fewer broad searches after evidentiary saturation without increasing unsupported claims. A stratified replay of current-fact questions, scored for citation entailment and total fetches, would settle it. This is **likely (55-80%)**, because the gate directly targets actions lacking novel evidence, but research serendipity can make apparent redundancy useful.

### Example B: code change with a failing test

**Base route:** inspect relevant code, edit, targeted test, diff review.  
**Threat:** unfamiliar test harness.  
**Contingency:** diagnose one environmental/test failure.  
**Alternate:** inspect CI configuration or run the narrow underlying command.  
**Assurance reserve:** one executable behavior check plus diff review.

When the same command fails twice with the same precondition, the TEM chain is recorded: threat (unfamiliar harness) → error (unchanged retry) → undesired state (verification reserve at risk). The harness diverts to the alternate. It does not solve the test by consuming the protected diff review.

Expected catch is **UNVERIFIED**: identical retry loops and unverified completion near a cap. Replay on tasks with deterministic tool transcripts and seeded environment failures would settle it. This is **very likely (80-90%)** for exact unchanged-state loops, because equality of command/fingerprint/precondition is mechanically detectable; improvement on messy non-identical failures is only **likely (55-80%)**.

### Example C: “be exhaustive” design request

**Base route:** requirements, three architectures, evidence comparison, recommendation.  
**Threat:** open-ended exploration has no natural stopping point.  
**Contingency:** investigate one surprising alternative.  
**Assurance reserve:** adversarial disanalogy and acceptance-criteria coverage.  
**Decision point:** once each architecture has evidence, cost, failure mode, and owner-fit.

The harness does not equate “exhaustive” with uniform fan-out. Additional branches must add a distinct decision-relevant alternative or evidence source. A challenger tests whether proposed agents are independent and whether synthesis is funded.

Expected catch is **UNVERIFIED**: breadth for its own sake and fan-out that leaves no synthesis budget. A blind owner-preference evaluation of matched design outputs at equal and unequal budgets would settle it. Net benefit is **roughly even (45-55%)** because owners may value exploratory breadth beyond immediately measurable decision change.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and implementation boundary

All six proposals pass the stated anti-overfit gate:

- They ride existing configuration, hook, run-state, model, and agent buses.
- The only state is a small per-run ledger plus bounded structured events.
- There is no second orchestration runtime.
- There is no vector database or knowledge graph.
- There is no durable-execution engine.
- There is no trained critic.
- There is no observability SaaS.
- There is no guardrail framework.

The combined first experiment should remain **BUILD-SMALL**, even if the individual snippets together later exceed 200 lines: implement only the ledger schema, transition annunciation, unchanged-retry detector, and checkpoint prompt in an existing hook/skill. Do not start with statistical automation. Export events and analyze them with a one-off script. Promotion to any persistent service would be **UNVERIFIED** and unjustified until the small experiment shows sustained owner-preference improvement; the settling experiment is the replay/live trial in §7.

The anti-overfit rationale is substantive. Frontier models may learn better native stopping and tool-use behavior, making elaborate routing policy obsolete. A transparent ledger and a few semantic gates can be removed cheaply, while a second runtime creates coordination failure modes and maintenance cost. I assess a small bus-riding implementation as **very likely (80-90%)** to be technically feasible, because each required datum—declared task class, counters, mode, trigger, and action list—is simple run metadata; this remains **UNVERIFIED** until a spike confirms the actual hook payloads and mutability available in the subject harness. The settling experiment is a read-only payload capture followed by a throwaway hook that round-trips a ledger on ten synthetic runs.

## 7. Evaluation plan: the experiment that should decide adoption

### 7.1 Offline replay, not immediate fleet-wide policy

Build a stratified evaluation set containing at least these task families: simple Q&A, current-fact research, repository diagnosis, repository change, large artifact creation, flaky-tool recovery, and explicitly exhaustive design. Include both cheap successes and expensive successes; sampling only overruns would bias the policy toward premature stopping.

For each task, replay or approximate two conditions:

- **Control:** current harness behavior.
- **CFP:** ledger + semantic checkpoints + explicit modes + assurance actions; no statistical auto-tuning initially.

Where tool/environment replay is impossible, use matched live tasks and mark them separately. Randomize presentation to owner/evaluators.

### 7.2 Primary metrics

1. Owner pairwise preference, with ties allowed.
2. Objective task completion: tests, acceptance criteria, citation entailment/currentness, artifact integrity.
3. Total paid tokens/model calls/tool calls and wall time.
4. Quality-adjusted cost frontier: cost among outputs non-inferior on quality, not a single blended score.
5. False-stop rate: control succeeds or contains decisive evidence that CFP cancels.
6. Waste-catch precision: cancelled actions that independent reviewers judge unable to change the answer.
7. Assurance completion rate.
8. Mode-awareness score: can the owner correctly identify mode, reason, and authority?

Do not optimize raw cost alone. A policy that saves 30% by failing 10% more tasks is not automatically better; the owner’s loss function decides.

### 7.3 Mechanically independent verification signals

Claims of “no quality regression” or “waste removed” require two independent signals:

- **Signal A:** objective or external outcome—tests, source entailment, acceptance checklist, or owner blind preference.
- **Signal B:** transcript/action analysis by an evaluator not responsible for the original action, ideally a different model family or human.

Before trusting the waste detector, validate it on known-present controls: inject an exact unchanged retry loop and a known answer-changing contradiction search. The detector must identify the first as redundant and preserve the second. Cost counters are independently checked against provider billing/usage records where available, rather than only the harness ledger.

### 7.4 Adoption thresholds

Pre-register thresholds rather than declaring victory after inspection. A reasonable starting rule, explicitly **UNVERIFIED**, is:

- at least 15% median cost reduction on the high-cost half of tasks;
- no more than 2 percentage points absolute loss in objective completion;
- non-inferior owner preference with a confidence interval chosen before the trial;
- waste-catch precision above 80%;
- false-stop rate below 5%;
- no reduction in assurance-action completion.

These numeric thresholds are **UNVERIFIED** and normative, not sourced from aviation. The settling experiment is owner review of the loss tradeoff followed by pre-registration; their appropriateness is **roughly even (45-55%)** until the baseline distributions and owner utility are known.

### 7.5 Calibration after proof

Only after the static CFP wins should the harness estimate per-family base and contingency budgets. Use recency-weighted empirical quantiles, minimum sample sizes, cold-start conservative defaults, and invalidation when model/tool/version changes. Never auto-update a policy from agent self-reports alone. Require objective spend data plus outcome/evaluator signal.

Adaptive calibration is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is temporal holdout evaluation against a global-budget baseline, with explicit monitoring of false stops by task family. It is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform a global allowance once a family has enough stable observations, based on EASA’s route/aircraft-specific statistical method; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** to remain calibrated across major model/tool changes without reset, because the data-generating process changes.

## 8. Alternatives considered and rejected

### 8.1 Generic checklist before every tool call — reject

FAA checklist research found that checklist design and cockpit operating conditions can themselves impede effective use; checklists could be difficult to locate, poorly designed, interfere with operations, or be hard to use under real conditions ([FAA report DOT/FAA/AM-91/7](https://www.faa.gov/data_research/research/med_humanfacs/oamtechreports/1990s/1991/9107)). This warns against adding uniform friction. Use short checklists at expensive/irreversible phase transitions, not everywhere.

### 8.2 Fixed “always use 20% for verification” reserve — reject

It copies the surface form of final reserve but ignores task heterogeneity and compute path dependence. Protect named assurance actions instead.

### 8.3 Confidence-triggered escalation — reject as sole mechanism

Self-confidence is not a calibrated fuel gauge. Escalation needs observable triggers: conflicting primary sources, failed test, unmet acceptance criterion, tool-error fingerprint, or missing independent signal.

### 8.4 Routine multi-agent CRM crew — reject

CRM uses all resources effectively; it does not say maximum crew size is always safer. Same-model agents can share blind spots and impose synthesis cost. Use a challenger only at expensive branch points and demand a distinct decision or independent signal.

### 8.5 Full cost-optimization daemon — reject

A daemon would violate the anti-overfit gate and create a second locus of orchestration authority. The source mechanism requires visible categories and replanning, not a new runtime.

### 8.6 “Blameless postmortem” with no accountability boundary — reject

ICAO’s just-culture formulation explicitly rejects pure no-blame. For the harness, protect honest error/near-miss reporting while distinguishing ordinary error from deliberate bypass of owner-set caps or fabricated evidence.

### 8.7 Optimize average token cost — reject

Averages hide tails, task mix, and false economy. Evaluate the quality-cost frontier, high-cost strata, and task-family false-stop rates.

## 9. Failure modes of the proposal itself

1. **Budget anchoring:** the agent expands work to consume the allowance. Mitigation: ceilings are not targets; landing condition is acceptance complete plus no answer-changing next action.
2. **Bureaucratic narration:** checkpoint text costs more than it saves. Mitigation: only semantic phase boundaries and terse structured fields; disable on simple requests.
3. **Goodharting `answer_changed`:** agents claim every action mattered. Mitigation: independent sampled review and objective signals; never auto-tune from self-report alone.
4. **Premature diversion:** a retry detector treats a transient failure as permanent. Mitigation: one bounded retry and precondition/fingerprint comparison; known recovery signals reset the allowance.
5. **Reserve starvation early:** decisive verification is needed before implementation completes. Mitigation: reserve actions are capabilities, not chronological locks; early use is allowed with visible replan.
6. **Task taxonomy explosion:** each task becomes its own class. Mitigation: coarse families, minimum sample sizes, and hierarchical fallback to global defaults—no learned ontology required.
7. **Mode alert fatigue:** every small variance triggers an announcement. Mitigation: announce only changes in model tier, parallelism, acquisition scope, owner authority, contingency/reserve state, or material quota forecast.
8. **Cost-only local optimum:** the harness stops valuable exploration. Mitigation: include expensive successes in calibration and track owner preference/false stops.
9. **Correlated challenger:** the reviewer repeats the actor’s assumptions. Mitigation: require mechanically distinct evidence where stakes justify it; otherwise skip the ritual.
10. **Stale calibration:** a new model inherits old budgets. Mitigation: version-keyed profiles, recency weighting, and cold-start reset.

I assess bureaucratic overhead as the largest adoption risk: **likely (55-80%)** if the procedure is applied to every request, based on FAA’s evidence that checklist usability can interfere with operations; **unlikely (20-45%)** if it activates only on material-cost thresholds and transitions. The latter remains **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is to measure ledger/checkpoint overhead as a percentage of total cost by task family and disable it where median overhead exceeds savings.

This is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is completion of Phases 0–1 and the pre-registered replay trial.

## 11. Bottom line

Aviation has not solved “how many tokens make an answer correct.” It has solved a more transferable problem: how to operate efficiently under uncertain consumption without allowing ordinary variance, operational pressure, or automation opacity to erase the margin needed for a safe endpoint.

The useful borrow is a thin **Compute Flight Plan**:

- purpose-labelled resource buckets;
- empirical, task-conditional contingency;
- funded alternate routes;
- semantic in-run replanning;
- protected assurance actions;
- explicit mode annunciation and authority;
- TEM-shaped, just-culture near-miss learning.

The analogy survives adversarial testing because both domains have finite observable resource, uncertain route cost, alternate routes, asymmetric endpoint failure, and learnable forecast error. It is constrained by latent answer quality, non-fungible information value, task heterogeneity, model drift, lower stakes, and correlated agents. Those disanalogies argue for a small transparent policy—not a literal cockpit, universal checklist, or new orchestration runtime.

The final adoption recommendation is **UNVERIFIED**: run the controlled trial; do not deploy adaptive budgeting on analogy alone. I am **likely (55-80%)** to expect a static CFP to reduce low-value high-cost actions without material quality loss, based on the mechanism-to-problem structural match and aviation’s operationally mature closed-loop resource planning. The replay A/B, known-present detector controls, independent outcome signals, and pre-registered false-stop thresholds are what can turn that forecast into evidence.
