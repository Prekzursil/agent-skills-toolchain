# X165 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → agent orchestration

## Executive judgment

The borrow is worth making, but only in a narrow form. Military doctrine has not solved an abstract optimization problem called “how many parallel workers are optimal.” It has solved the operational control problem around it: how to distribute finite capability across several simultaneous efforts without overloading command, how to preserve unity of effort when subordinates act independently, how to shift resources as the situation changes, and how to learn after execution.

The most useful mechanism is a synthesis I call **bounded mission-command task organization**. This is my label, not an official doctrinal term. Its doctrinal components are:

1. a clear purpose and end state (commander’s intent);
2. decentralized execution within explicit boundaries (mission orders and disciplined initiative);
3. a bounded direct span of control;
4. one dynamically designated main effort, minimally resourced supporting efforts, and retained capacity for contingencies;
5. reassessment and shifting of the main effort as the situation develops; and
6. an after-action review that compares intent with events and converts experience into the next allocation policy.

The structural analogy is **likely (55-80%)** to improve agent orchestration for heterogeneous, decomposable work, because both domains have scarce supervisory attention, locally informed executors, uncertain task evolution, finite resources, and coordination costs; the evidence is the Army’s explicit two-to-five direct-subordinate guidance, its main/supporting-effort resource rules, and its mission-command treatment of decentralized execution [Army ATP 3-21.8 supplement](https://www.benning.army.mil/Infantry/DoctrineSupplement/ATP3-21.8/appendix_a/StepsofTroopLeadingProcedures/Step3/index.html), [ATP 3-90.99/MCTP 12-10D](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCTP%2012-10D.pdf), and [Army explanation of ADP 6-0](https://www.army.mil/article/225414/combined_arms_center_launches_new_mission_command_doctrine). This confidence is deliberately below “very likely”: soldiers, units, and headquarters are not language-model calls, military success is not a clean reward signal, and doctrine supplies judgment rules rather than a marginal-productivity estimator.

The literal numerical transplant—“always run two to five workers”—is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be valid, because the Army source describes a leader’s manageable subordinate elements, not independent inference replicas, and another Army case describes effective delegation through subordinate headquarters when seventeen battalions exceeded a direct C2 span [ATP 3-21.8 supplement](https://www.benning.army.mil/Infantry/DoctrineSupplement/ATP3-21.8/appendix_a/StepsofTroopLeadingProcedures/Step3/index.html), [1ID DIVARTY case](https://www.lineofdeparture.army.mil/Journals/Field-Artillery/FA-2024-Issue-1/Looking-to-the-Past-for-LSCO/). The usable lesson is “bound and measure direct coordination load,” not “five is magic.”

The build recommendation is therefore a small admission-and-reallocation policy on the existing agent/hook bus, not another orchestration runtime. **NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** It starts only workers with distinct effects, keeps direct reports bounded by measured supervisor load, designates one main effort, treats unused concurrency as a reserve option rather than an idle agent, and admits another worker only when its expected decision-changing contribution exceeds coordination and opportunity cost. This is **UNVERIFIED; the experiment that settles it is the preregistered paired-task replay in §9**.

## 1. What the source field actually does

### 1.1 Commander’s intent and mission orders: preserve purpose under decentralization

The Army’s 2019 mission-command formulation is “the Army’s approach to command and control that empowers subordinate decision making and decentralizes execution appropriate to the situation.” The same official Army account says commanders use C2 to provide purpose and direction and integrate activity toward mission accomplishment [Combined Arms Center release on ADP 6-0](https://www.army.mil/article/225414/combined_arms_center_launches_new_mission_command_doctrine).

Marine Corps doctrine explains the mechanism more precisely. A mission contains a task and the reason or intent behind it; the task may become obsolete as conditions change, while the more durable intent continues to guide initiative. The intent exists so subordinates may depart from the original plan when the unforeseen occurs while remaining consistent with higher aims [MCDP 1, *Warfighting*, “Commander’s Intent”](https://www.marines.mil/portals/1/publications/mcdp%201%20warfighting.pdf). The Army’s exposition adds that mission orders describe situation, intent, desired results, and required subordinate tasks without prescribing exactly how to accomplish them, thereby preserving freedom of action [Army University Press, “Mission Command”](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/NCO-Journal/Archives/2020/May/Mission-Command/).

In its own field this is not “send everyone the same prompt.” It is a compact control contract: why the operation matters, what outcome counts, which key tasks and constraints bind, and where local judgment is authorized. The doctrine also contains a brake on autonomy. Before taking disciplined initiative, a subordinate considers whether benefit outweighs the risk of desynchronizing the larger operation and whether the action advances the commander’s intent [Army University Press, “Mission Command,” discussion of ADP 6-0](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/NCO-Journal/Archives/2020/May/Mission-Command/).

That final test matters for parallelism: local productivity is insufficient if it desynchronizes the whole.

### 1.2 Span of control: limit direct coordination relationships

In troop-leading procedures, Army guidance says leaders assign each task to a subordinate, avoid fracturing unit integrity where possible, ensure every unit and asset is employed under adequate mission command, avoid unnecessarily complicated command structures, and try to keep span of control between two and five subordinate elements [ATP 3-21.8 doctrine supplement, paragraph A-119](https://www.benning.army.mil/Infantry/DoctrineSupplement/ATP3-21.8/appendix_a/StepsofTroopLeadingProcedures/Step3/index.html).

This is not merely an aesthetic preference for small teams. It bounds the number of direct relationships a leader must brief, monitor, reconcile, redirect, and support. An operational Army case makes the scaling mechanism visible: a division artillery headquarters could not directly control seventeen supporting battalions, so it delegated control authorities to subordinate brigade headquarters while keeping centralized command; the design kept each echelon at no more than five subordinate units [1ID DIVARTY case study](https://www.lineofdeparture.army.mil/Journals/Field-Artillery/FA-2024-Issue-1/Looking-to-the-Past-for-LSCO/).

The two sources are mechanically different signals for the source-field claim: one is prescriptive small-unit guidance, the other an operational organization case. Together they support “military organizations explicitly manage direct C2 fan-out.” They do **not** establish a universal causal optimum of two to five.

### 1.3 Main effort, supporting efforts, and reserve: parallel does not mean equal

Joint Army/Marine Corps guidance defines the main effort as the subordinate unit whose mission is most critical to overall success at that time. It receives priority of support and resources. Commanders commonly shift the main effort during execution. Supporting efforts directly contribute to the main effort and receive the minimum assets necessary to accomplish their missions [ATP 3-90.99/MCTP 12-10D, paragraphs 2-12 through 2-14](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCTP%2012-10D.pdf).

Current infantry guidance describes the reserve as uncommitted capacity used to exploit success, defeat counterattacks, or restore momentum; it is not a follow-and-support force already committed to the plan [ATP 3-21.8, paragraphs 4-98 through 4-100](https://home.army.mil/benning/8117/7919/3106/20260316_ATP_3-21.8.pdf).

This solves a subtle allocation problem in its own field. Simultaneous efforts are intentionally asymmetric. The commander identifies the presently decisive contribution, weights it, gives other efforts only what their enabling purposes need, and preserves an option against uncertainty. “Use every available slot immediately” is not the general rule. There are domain exceptions: artillery doctrine prizes keeping delivery systems firing, illustrating that reserve logic depends on whether idle capacity preserves option value or merely wastes throughput [1ID DIVARTY case study](https://www.lineofdeparture.army.mil/Journals/Field-Artillery/FA-2024-Issue-1/Looking-to-the-Past-for-LSCO/).

### 1.4 OODA: a reassessment rhythm, not a worker-count formula

Boyd’s observe-orient-decide-act model concerns competitive adaptation and disruption of an opponent’s decision process. Air University’s account emphasizes operating inside an opponent’s OODA loop, while another Air University discussion emphasizes orientation and preventing an adversary from adapting [Air University Press, Fadok on Boyd and Warden](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/AUPress/Display/Article/4135381/john-boyd-and-john-warden/), [Air University, “Decision Advantage in Competition”](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Wild-Blue-Yonder/Article-Display/Article/3118712/decision-advantage-in-competition/).

OODA supplies no direct rule for how many subordinates to employ. Using it as “cycle faster by spawning more agents” would force the analogy: additional workers may increase observations while simultaneously slowing orientation, decision, and integration. Its useful role here is only the repeated control point at which the orchestrator updates task state and reallocates effort.

### 1.5 After-action review: calibrate the organization from observed execution

The Army’s 2025 AAR circular defines an AAR as a candid professional discussion of performance against standards, not a critique or a success/failure grade. Its agenda reviews objectives and commander’s intent, reconstructs what happened, discusses key issues, and identifies performance to sustain or improve. It explicitly asks for participation and differing observations [TC 7-0.1, *After Action Reviews*, chapter 3](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A6C09408-2436-47A4-93A3-6684A1B59042-1739993594606/TC7_0x1.pdf). An earlier official field-manual appendix gives the compact four-part sequence: what was supposed to happen, what happened, what was right or wrong, and how to perform to standard next time [FM 7-0, Appendix K](https://www.first.army.mil/Portals/102/FM%207-0%20Appendix%20K.pdf).

In its own field, the AAR turns multiple local perspectives into a shared account and a concrete next-performance adjustment. It is not merely a postmortem document and not a generic “lessons learned” memory dump.

### 1.6 Red teaming: challenge allocation assumptions selectively

The Army University of Foreign Military and Cultural Studies handbook says red teams challenge explicit and implicit assumptions, problem frames, biases, and groupthink and develop alternative perspectives. It also says the approach must be tailored: time, expertise, team makeup, and leader behavior matter, and different units need different “doses” [UFMCS, *Applied Critical Thinking Handbook*, chapter I](https://www.benning.army.mil/CFDP_INST_HW/content/2E%20Applied%20Critical%20Thinking%20Handbook%20v8%201_Sep%2716.pdf).

Crucially, the handbook says its contents are **not doctrine or a school solution**. Treating “always spawn a red-team agent” as military doctrine would therefore be false. The transferable idea is a selectively commissioned contrary perspective when the cost of an unchallenged assumption justifies the worker and integration cost.

## 2. The concrete translation

The harness analogue is a **worker task organization**, not a simulated military hierarchy.

| Source-field element | Harness translation | Operational test |
|---|---|---|
| Commander’s intent | A shared intent envelope: purpose, desired end state, acceptance tests, key constraints, prohibited actions, and authority boundaries | Can a worker explain why its output matters and adapt without silently changing the goal? |
| Mission order | A distinct task-effect contract, not merely a topic: “produce evidence that changes decision X,” “implement component Y,” or “falsify assumption Z” | Does the task have an independently useful deliverable and a named consumer? |
| Span of control | A cap on **active direct reports**, adjusted by measured integration load rather than copied from the military number | Can the orchestrator ingest updates, resolve conflicts, and redirect without a growing ready-to-integrate queue? |
| Main effort | The worker/output on the current critical path; it gets priority context, tool budget, and reviewer attention | If delayed, does final completion move? |
| Supporting effort | A worker whose deliverable removes a named risk or dependency for the main effort | Is there an explicit handoff edge to the main effort? |
| Reserve | Unspent concurrency, token, and time budget; normally **not** an idle running agent | Can capacity be committed when a new branch, blocker, or promising lead appears? |
| OODA checkpoint | An event-driven reassessment after material evidence, blocker, conflict, or phase completion | Has the task graph or value of another worker changed? |
| AAR | A structured per-run allocation record comparing planned and actual contribution | Did worker *n* change a decision, unblock work, catch a defect, or merely duplicate? |
| Red team | A time-boxed counter-hypothesis task at a consequential decision gate | Is there a costly assumption and an evidence-access path capable of falsifying it? |

The admission rule should be expressed as a falsifiable inequality, not as role folklore:

`admit worker i only if E(decision-changing contribution_i) > E(coordination + duplication + integration + opportunity cost_i)`

The quantities need not begin as a trained predictive model. A harness can start with observable proxies: whether the proposed output has a unique named consumer; whether its read/write scope overlaps another worker; whether it depends on unfinished output; whether the orchestrator has unprocessed handoffs; whether previous workers of this task class changed the final result; and whether reserving the slot has material option value.

## 3. Proposed changes

### 3.1 UNVERIFIED — Intent-and-effect envelope — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Every delegated task carries these fields:

- `purpose`: why this task contributes to the owner’s objective;
- `end_state`: observable completion condition;
- `effect`: the decision, artifact, dependency, or risk the result changes;
- `key_tasks`: mandatory outcomes, not a step-by-step script;
- `constraints`: safety, ownership, file, source, time, and tool boundaries;
- `authority`: what the worker may decide or mutate without escalation;
- `interfaces`: inputs it may rely on and the consumer of its output;
- `abort_or_report`: conditions requiring stop, handoff, or escalation;
- `evidence_standard`: what must accompany the conclusion.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce locally plausible but globally irrelevant outputs, because mission-command sources consistently bind initiative to purpose and warn about desynchronizing the whole; the direct harness effect remains **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is to randomize otherwise identical delegated tasks to current versus intent-and-effect envelopes and score goal drift, clarification turns, and accepted output**.

This is configuration, not a new planner. It rides the existing delegation message.

### 3.2 UNVERIFIED — Fan-out admission gate and direct-report budget — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Before spawning, the orchestrator evaluates five gates:

1. **Effect independence:** Is the deliverable independently executable now, or does it await another worker?
2. **Distinctness:** Does it have a different effect, evidence source, mutation scope, or falsification target from active work?
3. **Integration capacity:** Is there capacity to consume the result promptly? A growing completed-but-unintegrated queue closes admission.
4. **Criticality/support:** Is it the main effort, a named enabling effort, or a justified red-team effort? “Might be useful” is insufficient.
5. **Marginal value:** Based on same-class AAR records, is another worker still changing outcomes often enough to cover its cost?

Start with a conservative direct-report ceiling of three, not because doctrine proves three optimal, but because it creates measurable headroom. Permit expansion one worker at a time up to the platform limit when all gates pass. Reduce the ceiling when outputs collide or integration latency grows. If the existing agent bus permits nested delegation, nesting is allowed only for a genuinely separable subproblem with its own intent; it must not be introduced merely to evade the direct-report cap.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that queue-sensitive admission catches supervisor saturation earlier than a raw concurrency cap, because the doctrinal span-of-control mechanism regulates C2 relationships and the relevant harness symptom is unconsumed coordination work, not worker execution time; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is to sweep concurrency while measuring coordinator integration latency and accepted-output throughput**.

### 3.3 UNVERIFIED — Main/supporting-effort ledger and dynamic reweighting — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

Maintain a tiny in-memory ledger for the current run:

```text
worker | effect | relation(main/support/red) | consumer | dependency | state
```

Exactly one active deliverable is the main effort at a time unless the task demonstrably has multiple independent final artifacts. Supporting work must name the main-effort edge it enables. At each material event—new evidence, failure, blocker, phase completion, conflict, or owner correction—the orchestrator re-orients and may shift the main effort. Reweighting changes context priority, tool/time budget, and review order; it does not need process preemption.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that explicit main/support edges expose “parallelism theater,” because doctrine allocates support by contribution to a temporarily decisive effort rather than giving every activity equal standing; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is to have a blind evaluator label each historical worker output’s actual consumer and compare that graph with the declared ledger**.

### 3.4 UNVERIFIED — Reserve as an empty slot, not a reserve agent — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

For uncertain tasks, leave at least one available concurrency slot and some token/time budget uncommitted until the first reassessment. Commit it only to exploit a high-value lead, attack a newly discovered blocker, replace a failed worker, or commission a consequential counter-hypothesis.

It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that reserve capacity beats full initial saturation on mixed research/build workloads, because military doctrine supports option value under uncertainty but inference jobs may instead benefit from immediate latency hiding; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is a randomized comparison of full fan-out at time zero versus one reserved slot, stratified by task entropy and dependency density**.

### 3.5 UNVERIFIED — Event-driven AAR with ordinal-worker contribution — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)

At integration, append a compact local record:

- intended effect;
- actual effect;
- unique evidence or mutation contributed;
- whether it changed a decision, caught a defect, unblocked the critical path, or was discarded;
- wall time and token/tool cost;
- overlap/collision count;
- coordinator wait-to-ingest and ingest-to-accept time;
- owner correction attributable to the output;
- what allocation rule to sustain or change next time.

Aggregate by coarse task class and by worker ordinal (first, second, third, and so on). Use rolling counts and medians, not embeddings, a knowledge graph, or a trained critic. The stopping signal is empirical saturation: recent extra workers cease changing accepted outputs while integration cost continues.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this record will reveal duplicated or unused work if those outcomes occur, because direct consumer, acceptance, overlap, and timing fields make them observable; the evidence is the AAR’s intent-versus-event comparison and multi-perspective reconstruction [TC 7-0.1](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/A6C09408-2436-47A4-93A3-6684A1B59042-1739993594606/TC7_0x1.pdf). It is only **likely (55-80%)** that the resulting policy will improve future allocation, because observational task mix can confound ordinal contribution; that claim is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is the held-out replay described in §9**.

### 3.6 UNVERIFIED — Selective red-team dispatch — CONFIG (settings/JSON only)

Dispatch a red-team worker only when all three conditions hold: a consequential decision is pending; the main plan rests on a falsifiable assumption; and an independent evidence path exists. Its mission is to produce the strongest evidenced counter-case or a precise failure mode, not a stylistic second opinion. Time-box it and give its output directly to the decision owner.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that this catches correlated agreement around a bad assumption better than an additional same-role worker, because the UFMCS mechanism explicitly changes frame and challenges assumptions rather than merely adding votes [UFMCS handbook](https://www.benning.army.mil/CFDP_INST_HW/content/2E%20Applied%20Critical%20Thinking%20Handbook%20v8%201_Sep%2716.pdf). The harness benefit is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is to seed falsifiable but plausible assumptions into benchmark tasks and compare same-role replication with chartered counter-hypothesis review**.

## 4. What this would catch that worker-count rules miss

These are prospective failure classes, not claims that the present harness lacks detectors. Present coverage is addressed under GAPS.

1. **False decomposition.** Two workers appear parallel but one cannot act until the other defines an interface. The effect-independence gate rejects the premature spawn. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect explicit dependency edges because those edges are required fields; hidden dependencies remain possible, evidenced by the difference between declared and actual interfaces.

2. **Duplicate reconnaissance.** Several workers search the same sources and return paraphrases. The distinctness gate and AAR consumer/unique-evidence fields expose the duplication. Detection is **likely (55-80%)**, because URL, query-family, and accepted-evidence overlap are measurable while semantically equivalent differently worded findings are harder; the evidence is the proposed instrumentation, whose effectiveness remains **UNVERIFIED by the overlap-labeling experiment in §9**.

3. **Coordinator saturation.** Workers finish, but the orchestrator cannot read, reconcile, and use their outputs. Completed-but-unintegrated queue depth and ingest latency close admission. Detection is **very likely (80-90%)** if events are timestamped, because backlog and latency are direct signals; whether throttling improves end-to-end results is **UNVERIFIED and is settled by the concurrency sweep in §9**.

4. **Equal funding of unequal work.** Low-value side investigations consume the same budget as the critical implementation or decision. Main/support designation exposes missing contribution edges and reallocates review/tool budget. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce this failure where a critical path is identifiable, evidenced by doctrine’s explicit priority-of-support mechanism; it is **unlikely (20-45%)** to help genuinely independent batch work, where the evidence is the absence of a single critical-path consumer by construction.

5. **No capacity for surprise.** Full initial fan-out prevents response to a newly discovered blocker or promising lead. A reserved empty slot retains the option. Benefit is **roughly even (45-55%)** until tested because immediate full utilization may dominate on predictable tasks; the evidence required is the stratified reserve experiment.

6. **Correlated consensus.** More same-role agents amplify a shared framing error. Selective red-team dispatch changes the question from “do you agree?” to “what evidence would make this wrong?” It is **likely (55-80%)** to improve detection of seeded assumption errors, based on the UFMCS counter-framing mechanism; generalization to natural errors is **UNVERIFIED by the seeded-assumption experiment**.

7. **Late discovery that worker *n* added nothing.** Ordinal contribution records estimate where marginal accepted value flattened for each coarse task class. It is **likely (55-80%)** to identify historical saturation, because contribution and integration costs are recorded; prospective prediction remains **UNVERIFIED by held-out replay**.

8. **Local initiative that damages global work.** A worker makes a locally reasonable mutation outside its interface or changes the goal. Authority, constraints, consumer, and desynchronization checks make the violation explicit. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect declared file/interface violations where tool events reveal targets; semantic goal drift is only **likely (55-80%)** to be caught because acceptance tests may be incomplete.

## 5. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 5.1 Where the structures match

| Shared structure | Why it supports transfer |
|---|---|
| Finite supervisory attention | A commander cannot directly control arbitrary subordinate elements; an orchestrator cannot deeply ingest arbitrary outputs. |
| Local information | Subordinates encounter facts the commander did not foresee; workers discover evidence, errors, and interfaces during execution. |
| Uncertain evolution | Both plans face contingencies, making static allocation brittle. |
| Need for unity of effort | Individually competent actions can conflict or fail to advance the common outcome. |
| Heterogeneous roles | Main and supporting efforts contribute differently; research, implementation, verification, and adversarial review are not interchangeable votes. |
| Resource trade-offs | More executors consume support, communication, time, and integration capacity. |

These commonalities make the control-logic transfer **likely (55-80%)**, with the cited mission-command, span, and resource-priority sources as evidence. They justify an experiment, not automatic deployment.

### 5.2 Where the structures do not match

1. **Human trust and competence are persistent; model instances may be ephemeral and correlated.** Mission command assumes trained subordinates with accountability, shared doctrine, and accumulated trust. Agent instances may share the same model failure modes and have no durable relationship. Therefore importing “trust” as permission without verification is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be safe; the evidence is the doctrine’s explicit competence, mutual trust, and shared-understanding prerequisites [Army University Press on ADP 6-0 principles](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/NCO-Journal/Archives/2020/May/Mission-Command/).

2. **Military subordinate units are composite capabilities, not identical samples.** A battalion brings internal command, logistics, and specialization. One agent call may bring little differentiated capability. Thus the Army’s span number cannot be copied.

3. **Military command layers add capability; agent hierarchy may add lossy summarization.** The DIVARTY example delegates through real headquarters with staffs and authorities. Nested agents may merely compress messages and increase latency. It is **roughly even (45-55%)** whether nested delegation improves large task performance, because the structural benefit of bounded direct reports competes with context loss; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment compares flat versus one-level-nested organizations at equal model/token budget**.

4. **Adversarial tempo differs from task throughput.** OODA aims at adaptive advantage relative to an opponent. Many coding and research tasks have no active opponent; faster cycling can create churn. Therefore “more agents make a faster OODA loop” is **unlikely (20-45%)**, because more observations also increase orientation and integration work; evidence must come from the concurrency sweep, not the military source.

5. **Lives, terrain, and irreversible commitment make military reserve semantics different.** Compute slots can often be created or released cheaply, although rate limits and deadlines restore some scarcity. Reserve should therefore mean unspent option capacity, not an agent waiting idly.

6. **Military outcomes are noisy and delayed; harness artifacts can often be tested.** The harness should prefer executable tests, source checks, and diff review over commander judgment whenever available. Borrowing doctrine must not weaken deterministic verification.

7. **The enemy can deceive; peer workers usually do not.** Red teaming maps to cognitive challenge, but most agent workers are not hostile. Permanent adversarial duplication can waste budget and manufacture objections.

8. **A military main effort may be physically decisive; knowledge work may have several independent outputs.** For batch transformations, independent audits, or embarrassingly parallel searches, enforcing one main effort adds ceremony. The policy must allow a declared independent-batch mode.

### 5.3 Verdict on each fashionable borrow

- **Commander’s intent:** transfers as an intent-and-authority envelope. **Likely (55-80%)**, based on the shared need to adapt without goal drift and the doctrinal task-versus-purpose distinction.
- **Span of control:** transfers as a measured coordinator-load constraint; the number does not transfer. The qualitative mechanism is **likely (55-80%)**; a universal two-to-five limit is **very unlikely (5-20%)**, based on the difference between units and inference calls.
- **Main/support/reserve:** transfers as dynamic budget priority and option capacity. **Likely (55-80%)** on dependency-rich tasks and **unlikely (20-45%)** to help embarrassingly parallel batches; evidence is the presence or absence of contribution edges.
- **OODA:** transfers only as event-driven reassessment. “Faster loop wins” does not establish the right worker count. The narrow transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to keep allocation responsive; the broad speed claim is **unlikely (20-45%)** because orientation cost rises with outputs.
- **AAR:** transfers as structured allocation telemetry and policy calibration. Observability benefit is **very likely (80-90%)**; causal policy improvement is **likely (55-80%)** and UNVERIFIED by held-out replay.
- **Red teaming:** transfers selectively as counter-hypothesis work, not as a standing mandatory worker. **Likely (55-80%)** for consequential falsifiable assumptions; **unlikely (20-45%)** to repay cost on routine deterministic tasks, based on the handbook’s own insistence on tailored dosage.

## 6. Stop conditions: when more stops helping

Do not infer saturation merely because a worker is slow or disagrees. Stop admitting workers when any of these observable conditions holds:

1. no candidate task has a distinct effect and named consumer;
2. the next task depends on unfinished active work;
3. completed-but-unintegrated outputs reach the direct-report budget;
4. median ingest-to-accept time rises for two successive worker additions while accepted-output throughput does not;
5. recent same-class ordinal workers produce no unique accepted evidence, mutation, defect, or decision change;
6. read/write or evidence scope substantially overlaps active work without an explicit independent-verification purpose;
7. the remaining uncertainty is not reducible by another worker with available evidence/tools;
8. the acceptance test is already satisfied and additional work has no named risk to retire.

Conditions 3 through 5 are proposed thresholds, not validated constants. It is **likely (55-80%)** that combining direct backlog with marginal accepted contribution is more informative than raw concurrency, because it observes both coordination cost and useful yield; this is **UNVERIFIED, and the concurrency-sweep experiment settles it**.

Conversely, admit one more worker when a slot exists and a distinct executable task can: unblock the critical path, access an independent evidence channel, falsify a consequential assumption, isolate a mutation scope, or exploit a newly discovered lead whose expected value exceeds integration cost.

## 7. Anti-overfit gate

All recommendations ride the existing delegation/configuration/hook bus. None requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The state is deliberately small:

- one intent envelope per task;
- one in-memory worker/effect ledger per run;
- a compact append-only local AAR record;
- rolling counts/medians by coarse task class and worker ordinal.

No proposal needs BUILD-LARGE. If a sub-200-line admission hook cannot express the policy because the current bus lacks spawn, completion, and integration events, the correct next step is to expose those events on the existing bus, not to build a daemon. This fallback is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve the harness’s minimal architecture because it adds data at existing boundaries; actual bus sufficiency is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is a read-only interface audit followed by a disposable prototype**.

## 8. Alternatives rejected

### Fixed “five-agent squad”

Rejected. Doctrine’s two-to-five figure is about direct subordinate elements and is explicitly contextual. A fixed squad ignores task independence, model latency, context size, tool contention, and integration load.

### Spawn all specialists, then vote

Rejected. Main/support doctrine does not treat simultaneous efforts as equal votes. Same-model specialists may be correlated, voting discards evidence quality, and the coordinator still pays ingestion cost.

### Permanent red-team agent

Rejected. The UFMCS handbook says red-team method and dose depend on need, time, expertise, and team makeup, and explicitly says it is not doctrine. A permanent seat is ritual, not justified allocation.

### OODA-speed maximization

Rejected. Boyd’s model describes adaptive competition, not marginal worker productivity. More observers can slow orientation. Measure the whole loop, especially integration.

### Learned worker-count predictor

## 9. Preregistered validation plan

### Experiment A — Find the saturation curve

Sample at least four task classes: tightly coupled implementation, broad evidence research, independent batch work, and adversarial verification. Replay matched tasks at direct concurrency 1, 2, 3, 4, and platform maximum. Equalize model family and total token/tool budget where feasible.

Measure:

- acceptance-test success;
- owner/blind-evaluator correction count;
- end-to-end wall time;
- accepted unique contributions;
- discarded or duplicate outputs;
- completed-to-ingested queue depth;
- ingest-to-accept time;
- mutation conflicts and rework;
- contribution of each ordinal worker.

Primary falsification: if accepted-output throughput keeps increasing without rising integration latency or corrections through the maximum, a bounded admission gate adds no value for that class.

### Experiment B — Intent envelope ablation

Randomize matched delegated tasks between the current message and the proposed envelope. Blind-score end-state satisfaction, unauthorized scope changes, clarification turns, and usable handoffs. Primary falsification: no improvement in end-state satisfaction or scope control after accounting for prompt length.

### Experiment C — Full fan-out versus reserve

Randomize initial allocation between all slots committed and one empty reserve slot. Stratify by estimated dependency density and uncertainty. Allow the reserve arm to commit only after a logged material event. Primary falsification: reserve increases completion time without improving correctness, recovery, or decision-changing contribution.

### Experiment D — Same-role replica versus red-team charter

Seed plausible, falsifiable assumptions into consequential benchmark decisions. At equal budget, compare another ordinary worker with a counter-hypothesis worker. Primary falsification: the chartered red team does not find seeded errors more often or creates enough false objections to erase the gain.

### Experiment E — Flat versus one-level nested delegation

Use tasks with at least six genuinely separable work packages. Equalize model and total budget. Compare flat direct reports with two coordinators each owning coherent packages. Measure lost facts across handoffs, integration latency, correctness, and coordinator workload. Primary falsification: nesting reduces direct backlog but worsens final correctness or total latency.

Adopt a rule for a task class only if it improves the preregistered primary quality measure without a practically unacceptable wall-time/cost increase across held-out tasks. Do not pool classes until interaction tests show similar effects.
