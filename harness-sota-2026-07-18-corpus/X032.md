# X032 — aviation-safety → failure-recovery

## Scope and verdict

This unit is an ideation lens. Per the task, I did **not** inspect or make claims about the local harness implementation. I used the pinned problem statement only to define the target: resuming useful work after a worker, session, or machine dies mid-task.

**Verdict:** a narrow aviation borrow transfers well: combine air-traffic-control **position relief**, maintenance **work-status markers/walkthroughs**, and flight-deck **restart-from-the-beginning when position is uncertain**. The transferable principle is not “write a better summary.” It is: preserve an externally inspectable status surface continuously; make the incoming operator independently preview the real position; mark incomplete work unmistakably; resolve discrepancies before explicitly assuming control; and restart from a known safe boundary when sequence position cannot be established.

I assess it as **very likely (80-90%)** that this composite can reduce incorrect continuation after agent/session loss. Evidence: the FAA mechanisms address the same causal structure—work crossing an operator boundary, incomplete/forgotten information, uncertain sequence position, and configuration that can differ from memory—and they deliberately use multiple barriers rather than a narrative handoff alone ([FAA ATC Appendix A, especially §§2–6](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/ATpubs/ATC/AppdxD.html); [FAA Human Factors Guide, pp. 101–104](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/about/initiatives/maintenance_hf/training_tools/HF_Guide.pdf); [FAA AC 120-71B, §§5.2.3 and 5.6.2](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-71B.pdf)). This is still **UNVERIFIED for this harness; the settling experiment is the crash-injection evaluation specified below**, because aviation evidence does not measure agent-harness outcomes.

The full human procedure does **not** transfer unchanged. Sudden process or machine death removes the outgoing specialist, so verbal briefing, readback, and outgoing-worker monitoring cannot be prerequisites. The viable translation substitutes durable intent/outcome markers and independent reconciliation against authoritative state. Without that substitution, the analogy fails.

## What aviation has actually solved

### 1. ATC position relief is receiver-led reconciliation, not sender-authored summarization

The FAA's Standard Operating Practice for Transfer of Position Responsibility states the failure mode plainly: pertinent information is forgotten or incompletely covered when relief practices vary and memory lacks systematic reminders. Its solution has four stages ([FAA ATC Appendix A](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/ATpubs/ATC/AppdxD.html)):

1. **Preview the position.** The relieving specialist follows a checklist, reads the persistent Status Information Area (SIA), directly observes equipment and the operational situation, listens to communications, and correlates current and pending traffic with movement information.
2. **Verbal briefing.** The outgoing specialist explains abnormal or special-interest items not captured in the SIA; the incoming specialist asks questions until the situation is understood.
3. **Explicit assumption of responsibility.** The incoming specialist affirmatively indicates acceptance; the outgoing specialist releases the position and notes the time. Responsibility does not drift implicitly.
4. **Review after transfer.** The outgoing specialist checks the SIA, written notes, equipment, and operation for omissions or inaccuracies, then assists if necessary.

Two design details matter. First, the outgoing and incoming specialists share responsibility: the sender must provide an accurate briefing and the receiver must not accept until unresolved questions are resolved. Second, status is recorded as soon as operationally feasible rather than reconstructed at shift end. The FAA additionally requires tailored relief checklists, defines concrete status categories, makes the SIA the first checklist item, makes live traffic the last, assigns responsibility for SIA accuracy, and calls for recording relief briefings where practicable ([FAA Facility Operation and Administration, §2-2-4](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/foa_html/chap2_section_2.html)).

In its own field, this mechanism protects continuous control of live traffic when one controller replaces another. It externalizes stable status, preserves a verbal channel for fast-changing or anomalous state, forces the receiver to look at the actual position, identifies a precise ownership transition, and retains a short overlap in which the former controller detects omissions.

### 2. Maintenance turnover makes incomplete physical work visible

The FAA Human Factors Guide treats information transfer as a prime opportunity for errors and defines effective turnover as a formal exchange owned by both sides. Its minimum system has four components: turnover meetings, a walkthrough at the actual worksite, turnover checklists, and work-status markers ([FAA Human Factors Guide, pp. 97, 101–104](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/about/initiatives/maintenance_hf/training_tools/HF_Guide.pdf)).

The walkthrough topics are unusually relevant to recovery: jobs/tasks in process, the workcard being followed, last steps completed, problems and unusual occurrences, needed resources, locations of removed parts and fasteners, ordered parts, proposed next steps, and communications with support personnel. The status-marker mechanism addresses a specific dangerous inference: an incoming worker may assume a job was completed when it was not. “Work complete” and “work in progress” markers provide an additional barrier at the component itself; the guide explicitly says this redundancy is meant to prevent human error propagating through the system.

In its own field, the marker is not a claim hidden in a meeting transcript. It is colocated with the object whose state matters. The incoming maintainer also walks the physical work with the outgoing maintainer rather than accepting prose as ground truth.

### 3. Flight-deck checklist interruption has an uncertainty rule

FAA AC 120-71B requires operators to establish procedures for completing the correct sequence after interruption. If sequence cannot be re-established or the crew is unclear where to resume, the relevant checklist section is reaccomplished from the beginning. The same AC recommends doing so “without hesitation” whenever progress is unclear ([FAA AC 120-71B, §§5.2.3 and 5.6.2](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_120-71B.pdf)). A NASA simulator study explains why a mere remembered pointer is weak: an interruption displaces sequence position in limited working memory; explicit holds provide a return marker ([NASA CR-177460, *Checklist Interruption and Resumption: A Linguistic Study*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19880016964/downloads/19880016964.pdf)).

In its own field, the key is epistemic: the system does not reward a confident guess about the next item. Uncertain progress changes the procedure. It trades a bounded amount of repeated work for restored sequence integrity.

### 4. Automation-surprise research supplies the warning against blindly trusting recovery metadata

The NTSB's Asiana 214 report identifies poor mental models, low system observability, and dynamic/nonroutine situations as contributors to mode-awareness failure and automation surprise. It also notes that increased integration can increase complexity and confusion ([NTSB AAR-14/01, §2.4.2](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/accidentreports/reports/aar1401.pdf)).

For recovery, this is a warning rather than a separate borrowed ritual: a polished checkpoint can create “recovery surprise” if its declared mode differs from what the filesystem, remote service, subprocess, or user-visible world actually contains. Therefore the checkpoint must lead the receiver to observation; it must not masquerade as authoritative state.

## Concrete harness translation

## Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: crash-tolerant position-relief card

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build one small recovery-card writer on the existing lifecycle/tool-hook bus, plus a recovery skill/prompt that consumes the card. Do not build a second orchestrator, scheduler, database, or durable-execution engine.

### Continuous card, not an end-of-session summary

Keep one compact, machine-readable, human-readable card per active task in storage that survives the worker process. Update it atomically around material side effects:

- task identity, objective, scope, and current owner/generation;
- last **verified safe boundary**, with the command/test/evidence that established it;
- current phase/mode and the procedure or plan item being followed;
- each in-flight side effect as an intent record: target, operation, idempotency key if available, start time, and outcome `not-started | in-flight | confirmed | ambiguous`;
- dirty or modified resources and their last observed revisions/hashes;
- tests/checks run and their results;
- open hazards, blockers, assumptions, user commitments, and external dependencies;
- proposed next action, explicitly labelled as a proposal rather than fact;
- pointers to authoritative evidence (files, VCS state, job IDs, API object IDs, logs), not copied claims alone.

The writer records intent before a material mutation and records the observed outcome afterward. A crash between the two leaves a conspicuous `in-flight` marker, analogous to maintenance's “work in progress” tag. Use write-to-temporary-file plus atomic replacement and include schema version and checksum so a torn or incompatible card is detectable rather than silently trusted.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that pre-intent/post-outcome markers will distinguish “the model planned it” from “the tool conclusively returned it” after a worker crash. Evidence: the two writes bracket the only event that can turn intention into an effect, and the maintenance marker specifically targets the mistaken-completion inference. It is **UNVERIFIED under the harness's actual hook ordering and termination semantics; settle it by killing workers at every hook boundary in the crash-injection experiment**.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that the complete core can remain under roughly 200 lines if it only serializes a fixed schema, performs atomic replacement, and invokes already-existing probes; evidence is the deliberately narrow function list. This estimate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a counted prototype including error paths on both Git Bash and PowerShell hosts**. If cross-machine durable storage, leases, or per-service reconciliation must be invented rather than reused, the classification must be revisited; concealing a daemon inside “BUILD-SMALL” would violate the anti-overfit gate.

### Cold-recovery procedure

On session or worker replacement, the incoming agent runs this bounded procedure before continuing material work:

1. **Preview.** Read the recovery card and referenced plan/checklist. Display its age, schema validity, checksum status, last safe boundary, and all `in-flight`/`ambiguous` operations.
2. **Walk the position.** Inspect authoritative current state: VCS status and revision, relevant file hashes/content, process/job state, test artifacts, and remote object state where scoped credentials and APIs exist. The exact probes are task-specific; absence may not be concluded until the detector proves it can find a known-present control and a second mechanically independent signal agrees.
3. **Reconcile.** Produce a short discrepancy table: `card claim | observed state | confidence | action`. Never promote “tool call was issued” to “effect occurred.” For external mutations, query by object ID/idempotency key before retrying.
4. **Restore sequence integrity.** If the next step and effects are established, continue. If sequence position is unclear, return to the last verified safe boundary and reaccomplish the bounded section. If replay is non-idempotent or destructive, stop for user authority rather than guessing.
5. **Assume responsibility explicitly.** Write a new owner/generation and `RECOVERY-ACCEPTED` only after unresolved discrepancies are either resolved or surfaced as blockers. A stale worker with an older generation must be prevented from writing a newer card if the existing bus exposes an atomic compare-and-set or lock primitive.
6. **Review.** Because a dead outgoing worker cannot monitor the takeover, the incoming worker performs an immediate second check after its first material action. For higher-risk tasks, a separate existing verifier hook or a human reviews the discrepancy table; this is verification, not a new critic runtime.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this procedure will expose stale checkpoints, partial mutations, and false-completion assumptions when authoritative state is observable. Evidence: it uses two mechanically different channels—the persisted claim and direct observation—and the FAA relief process likewise combines status areas, observation, briefing, and review. It is **UNVERIFIED for opaque external side effects; settle per connector by crash injection plus reconciliation queries using stable object IDs**.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** that a generic recovery procedure can safely auto-resolve ambiguous non-idempotent external actions such as sending a message or submitting a transaction when the remote system offers neither idempotency keys nor searchable receipts. Evidence: neither a local intent marker nor replay reveals whether the remote side committed. The harness must surface this ambiguity, not invent completion.

### What it would catch

The design targets these failure classes:

- a mutation began but its tool result never reached the transcript/card;
- an agent described work as done although authoritative state does not contain it;
- a session summary points at the wrong next plan item;
- a new worker retries an already-committed external side effect;
- filesystem/VCS/remote state changed after the checkpoint;
- a stale worker resumes and overwrites the successor's state;
- checkpoint corruption or schema drift makes fields silently unreadable;
- a recovery detector reports “absent” because its own environment or credentials are broken;
- the first post-recovery action reveals that the recovered mental model was wrong.

These are **candidate blind spots, not claims that the present harness lacks protections**. Existing coverage is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a capability inventory plus injected failures showing whether current recovery detects each class through two independent signals**. Consequently, the heading “what it would catch” means “what this mechanism is designed to catch if not already covered,” not “defects observed in the local harness.”

## Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: near-miss capsule after successful recovery

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** This is a schema/output extension to Proposal A, not a second service; both must fit the same small-build budget.

After recovery reaches a verified safe boundary, emit a structured, local incident capsule from fields already present in the card: failure point, ambiguous effects, detector/reconciliation results, repeated work, time to safe resumption, and the countermeasure that would have prevented ambiguity. Aggregate by failure signature. Keep this separate from performance scoring, and omit sensitive content by default.

This borrows the incentive structure, not the regulatory details, of NASA's Aviation Safety Reporting System (ASRS): voluntary reports are confidential and de-identified; FAA enforcement protections in qualifying cases encourage reporting; NASA analyzes reports for system deficiencies and publishes safety outputs ([ASRS confidentiality and incentives](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/confidentiality.html); [ASRS program briefing](https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/overview/summary.html)).

It is **likely (55-80%)** that separating near-miss learning from worker evaluation will produce more candid and diagnostically useful reports than attaching blame to the failed worker. Evidence: ASRS deliberately combines confidentiality/de-identification with incentives and uses reports to identify system deficiencies. Applicability to autonomous workers is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by an A/B trial comparing report completeness, actionable-field rate, and recurrence under blame-linked versus system-learning-only collection**. Do not claim “blameless culture” from a JSON flag alone; organizational incentives belong to the owner/operator.

This proposal does not directly resume work and should not block recovery. If it adds latency or becomes a mandatory essay, remove it.

## Adversarial transfer test

| Aviation assumption | Agent-harness reality | Transfer judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Incoming and outgoing humans can overlap and converse. | On process/machine death, the sender is unavailable and its last internal beliefs may be lost. | **Does not transfer.** Replace briefing/readback with pre-crash durable markers and receiver-led reconciliation. |
| A trained controller can visually/audibly inspect a bounded live position. | Agent tasks are open-ended; relevant state may span files, subprocesses, remote APIs, user intent, and secrets. | **Partial transfer.** Require task-specific authoritative pointers and probes; a universal state snapshot is implausible. |
| Checklists describe standardized aircraft/ATC procedures. | Software and research work often has no fixed sequence, and plans legitimately change. | **Partial transfer.** Checklist only recovery invariants; do not encode the whole task workflow. |
| Repeating a short checklist section is normally safe. | Repeating a deploy, payment, deletion, email, or issue creation may duplicate irreversible effects. | **Does not transfer by default.** Restart only from an idempotent safe boundary; otherwise reconcile or ask. |
| Physical configuration changes relatively continuously and is directly observable. | Remote and distributed state can change concurrently, be eventually consistent, or be inaccessible after credential loss. | **Partial transfer.** Timestamp observations, retain object IDs/revisions, and represent ambiguity explicitly. |
| The former controller can monitor after handoff. | A dead worker cannot provide overlap. | **Does not transfer.** Substitute a second post-assumption probe or existing independent verifier, acknowledging that this is weaker than knowledgeable human overlap. |
| High ceremony is affordable because safety stakes are extreme and position relief is episodic. | Agent crashes may be frequent and tasks may be cheap; ceremony can cost more than replay. | **Partial transfer.** Use tiny automatic cards continuously and scale reconciliation to side-effect risk. |
| Confidential reporting protections affect licensed humans' incentives. | An agent has no career, shame, or legal exposure; operators and vendors do. | **Weak transfer.** Use system-learning structure for owner/operator behavior, not anthropomorphic “agent psychology.” |

The analogy passes only because the target is **lossy transfer of operational responsibility over partially completed work**, not because cockpits and coding sessions are generally alike. The maintenance work-in-progress marker has a particularly strong structural match to a pre-intent record. The live verbal handoff and post-transfer outgoing monitor have no direct match and must not be cargo-culted.

## Anti-overfit gate

Both proposals ride existing surfaces: lifecycle/tool hooks, a skill/prompt, ordinary files or already-available task storage, and existing VCS/API probes. They do not require a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

The deliberately rejected radical alternative is an event-sourced workflow service that durably schedules every tool call and guarantees exactly-once continuation. It would be **BUILD-LARGE (new daemon/service/runtime -- needs its own justification)** and would reintroduce the specifically rejected durable-execution/runtime class. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat the small recovery card across the owner's mixed workload without substantial operational cost; evidence is that exactly-once semantics across arbitrary external systems still require connector-specific idempotency/reconciliation, while the prohibited architecture adds a second control plane. That comparative judgment is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment would compare crash-recovery correctness, latency, maintenance burden, and connector coverage against the BUILD-SMALL prototype across the same fault matrix**. Do not build it from this unit.

## UNVERIFIED settling experiment: crash-injection recovery trial

The recommended mechanism inherits an **UNVERIFIED** premise until this experiment establishes transfer to the harness.

Construct 30–50 short tasks spanning local edits, test runs, subprocess jobs, VCS operations, and sandboxed fake external APIs. Inject termination at mechanically distinct boundaries:

- before intent marker;
- after intent marker/before tool invocation;
- during tool execution;
- after external commit/before tool response;
- after response/before checkpoint update;
- during atomic card replacement;
- immediately after recovery acceptance;
- concurrent stale-worker wake-up;
- state changed independently after checkpoint;
- credential/connector failure that makes a present object look absent.

For each run, capture ground truth from an independent fault injector/fake service ledger plus the workspace/VCS state. Compare baseline transcript/summary recovery with the recovery-card procedure. Primary outcomes: unsafe continuation rate, duplicate side-effect rate, false-done rate, false-absence rate, time and tool calls to verified useful work, repeated-work cost, and corrupt-card detection. A success criterion should be chosen before running; a reasonable starting gate is zero unsafe or duplicate side effects in the finite test and a material reduction in false-done/false-absence errors without more than a small fixed recovery overhead. The numerical gate is **UNVERIFIED; settle its practical threshold using owner-valued loss weights before the trial**, rather than pretending all errors and delays cost the same.

Detector validity is part of the trial: every “not present,” “not running,” or “not committed” conclusion must first demonstrate the probe on a known-present control and then obtain a mechanically independent second signal. A test is not passed merely because the recovery script exits zero.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this experiment can falsify the central mechanism if it is ineffective, because injected crash timing supplies known ground truth and the outcome metrics directly measure continuation correctness. Evidence: the fake service ledger and workspace state are independent of the recovery card. Generalization to all real connectors remains **UNVERIFIED; settle that only with connector-specific shadow tests or non-destructive staging environments**.

## Failure modes and safeguards

1. **Checkpoint becomes a stale second truth.** Safeguard: timestamps/revisions, explicit provenance, reconciliation before acceptance, and no assertion that the card outranks authoritative state.
2. **Hook storms create overhead or recursive failure.** Safeguard: only bracket material side effects, debounce pure/read operations, prohibit the writer from invoking model/tool loops, and fail visibly without blocking harmless work.
3. **Card captures secrets.** Safeguard: allowlisted fields, references rather than raw outputs, local restrictive permissions, and redaction tests. It is **likely (55-80%)** that allowlisting is safer than attempting to redact arbitrary transcripts after capture; evidence is that unrecorded fields cannot leak through this artifact, whereas post-hoc pattern redaction must recognize every secret form. This remains **UNVERIFIED; settle it with seeded-secret canary tests across every field and error path**.
4. **Atomic file replacement is mistaken for distributed ownership.** Safeguard: treat atomic replacement only as torn-write protection. Multi-worker exclusion requires an existing compare-and-set/lease primitive; otherwise recovery must stop concurrent workers rather than claiming fencing.
5. **Restart-from-boundary repeats damage.** Safeguard: label boundaries `replay-safe`, `requires-reconciliation`, or `requires-approval`; never infer replay safety from a missing response.
6. **Status schema grows into orchestration.** Safeguard: fixed recovery-only fields, no scheduler, no dependency graph, no autonomous retry policy, and a hard size/line budget reviewed after the prototype.
7. **False precision from hashes.** A matching file hash says nothing about an unobserved email, deployment, or remote mutation. Require evidence scoped to each side-effect domain.
8. **Postmortem becomes blame telemetry.** Safeguard: separate incident capsules from per-worker ranking and publish aggregation rules. This safeguard's behavioral benefit is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the reporting A/B trial above**.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **“Just save the full transcript.”** Rejected: a transcript records attempted conversation, not necessarily committed world state; it also makes the receiver reconstruct sequence and hazards under high context load. The aviation procedure specifically avoids unsupported reliance on memory and asks the receiver to inspect the position.
- **“Have the dying worker write a perfect final summary.”** Rejected: abrupt death is precisely when the finalizer does not run. Continuous status markers survive this failure timing.
- **“Resume from the last tool call automatically.”** Rejected: issuance, return, and side-effect commit are different facts; automatic replay is dangerous for non-idempotent actions.
- **“Restart the whole task from scratch.”** Rejected as a universal policy: it discards verified useful work and can repeat external effects. Bounded restart from a verified replay-safe boundary is the transferable FAA rule.
- **“Use another model as CRM copilot for every step.”** Rejected: a model reading the same transcript is not mechanically independent and does not recreate the outgoing controller's direct observation. A second model is useful only if it performs independent state probes, in which case the probes—not model plurality—supply the safety signal.
- **“Centralize every event in a durable workflow engine.”** Rejected by the anti-overfit gate and because connector-specific ambiguity survives the extra runtime.
- **“Copy ASRS and make every crash report mandatory.”** Rejected: mandatory reporting contradicts the voluntary incentive mechanism and can obstruct the actual recovery path. A small post-recovery capsule is enough to test whether learning value exists.

## Gaps

- Current harness recovery behavior and existing protections were intentionally not inspected; this unit cannot say which candidate blind spots are actually uncovered.
- No empirical study located here directly tests FAA position-relief procedures on autonomous software agents. Transfer remains UNVERIFIED pending the crash-injection trial.
- The cited FAA Human Factors Guide incorporates maintenance research and some Department of Energy turnover standards; this unit did not independently reproduce every underlying study. The guide is used as an FAA primary operational source, not proof of effect size.
- No evidence here establishes that the existing hook bus guarantees pre-tool and post-tool callbacks on all failure modes, or that its storage survives machine loss. Those premises are UNVERIFIED; settle them with hook-boundary kill tests and a storage-failure matrix before implementation classification is finalized.
- Cross-machine continuation needs a pre-existing durable shared location. If none exists, adding one may exceed BUILD-SMALL and needs a separate architecture decision; this unit does not smuggle it in.
