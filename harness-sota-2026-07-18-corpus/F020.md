# F020 — Frontier research: human-in-the-loop placement — where oversight pays

**Unit:** F020  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** Placement of owner attention in an agent harness: when to ask, when to gate, when to review, and when not to interrupt. This is not a general AI-governance survey and does not redesign adjacent orchestration, memory, or telemetry systems.

## Executive finding

The strongest defensible conclusion is not “keep a human in the loop.” It is: **spend human attention at decision boundaries where (a) user intent is still underdetermined, (b) an action will create a costly or external commitment, or (c) the reviewer has independent, outcome-relevant evidence. Do not spend it on continuous passive watching or generic approval prompts.** The evidence for the first half comes from randomized human–AI experiments, selective-prediction experiments, and the known economics of reversible versus irreversible action. The evidence against generic oversight is unusually direct: a preregistered meta-analysis of 106 experiments found human–AI combinations worse than the better of human or AI alone on average, cognitive-forcing studies found that effective review friction is disliked, and a 2026 coding study found that ordinary developers missed covert sabotage 94% of the time ([Vaccaro et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1), [Buçinca et al. 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), [Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647)).

For this harness, the practical architecture is a **thin risk router on the existing permission/hook bus**, not a second orchestration runtime: allow low-risk, reversible, locally verifiable work to flow; ask once at unresolved goal choices; require a compact “action card” immediately before high-impact side effects; require independent evidence at completion; and escalate repeated or contradictory failure signals. This architecture is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce both owner interruption load and consequential mistakes, based on the experimental results above plus Claude Code’s native pre-tool permission and hook primitives; the exact net benefit is **UNVERIFIED — settle it with the shadow-mode and randomized replay experiment specified in §4.7**.

## Research method and evidentiary standard

I prioritized peer-reviewed experiments, preregistered synthesis, official product documentation, and primary research/repositories. “Established” below means replicated as a broad human-factors result, supported by a reasonably powered controlled experiment, or a directly documented native capability. It does **not** mean established specifically for this owner’s harness. Recent 2026 preprints are treated as promising signals, not settled facts, even when their samples are large.

The local subject was read-only. The broken corpus `hooks` detector is treated as **no data**, per the pinned brief and the live defect report: the latter says zero hook records were found while a hook demonstrably fired and prescribes two independent storage probes before concluding telemetry is absent (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 133-147). I therefore make no claim about how often current hooks fire or help.

## 1. What is established

### 1.1 “Human + AI” is not itself a safety or quality mechanism

The best broad synthesis is Vaccaro, Almaatouq, and Malone’s preregistered meta-analysis of **106 human-participant experiments and 370 effect sizes**. Human–AI combinations beat humans alone (pooled Hedges’ *g* = 0.64) but performed worse than the best of human-alone or AI-alone (*g* = -0.23, 95% CI -0.39 to -0.07). Decision tasks showed losses; content-creation tasks showed gains. When humans alone were better than AI, combination helped; when AI alone was better, combination hurt. The authors also report possible publication bias and heterogeneous designs, so this supports a routing principle, not a universal numerical prediction ([Nature Human Behaviour article and methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)).

**Established implication:** adding a final human approver to every agent action cannot be presumed to produce complementarity. The harness must identify cases where the owner has different information or authority, rather than merely placing the owner after the model.

### 1.2 Capability is jagged; task type matters more than a global trust setting

In a field experiment with 758 BCG consultants randomly assigned to no AI, GPT-4, or GPT-4 plus prompt guidance, AI users completed 12.2% more in-frontier tasks, 25.1% faster, and at higher quality. On a deliberately outside-frontier task, AI users were 19% less likely to reach the correct answer. Similar-looking work therefore crossed an uneven capability boundary ([Dell’Acqua et al., field experiment](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321)). METR’s agent evaluations independently show a related trajectory pattern: agents can make rapid early progress but plateau on longer tasks, and failed runs include both capability limitations and elicitation-fixable mistakes ([Claude 3.5 Sonnet evaluation](https://evaluations.metr.org/claude-3-5-sonnet-report/), [RE-Bench primary report](https://metr.org/blog/2024-11-22-evaluating-r-d-capabilities-of-llms/)).

**Established implication:** oversight placement should be conditioned on observable task/action properties—ambiguity, reversibility, external effect, evidence availability—not a session-wide “autonomous” versus “supervised” toggle.

### 1.3 Independent judgment before seeing the AI answer reduces anchoring, but costs attention

Buçinca, Malaya, and Gajos randomized 199 participants across three cognitive-forcing interfaces, two ordinary explainable-AI interfaces, and a no-AI baseline. Forcing participants to engage analytically reduced overreliance compared with ordinary explanation displays. The most effective designs received the worst subjective ratings, and benefits were larger for participants with higher “Need for Cognition” ([paper abstract and artifacts](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml)).

Bondi et al. provide a mechanically different signal in a selective-prediction setting using real conservation data. Human accuracy improved when the interface told the human that the system had deferred **without revealing the AI prediction**; revealing the prediction changed human behavior and reduced the clean independence that deferral assumed ([Bondi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06751)).

**Established implication:** where human judgment is valuable, collect it before exposing the model’s preferred answer or persuasive rationale. In this harness that means asking the owner to choose among genuinely intent-dependent outcomes before showing a model-endorsed default, not requesting “approve?” after the agent has framed one option as complete.

### 1.4 Explanations are not substitutes for verifiable evidence

The Buçinca experiment explicitly found that ordinary explainable-AI approaches did not cure overreliance, while friction that forced actual thinking did. A review of AI-advised decision experiments likewise reports that explanations rarely produce complementary performance and can invite confirmation bias ([Fok and Weld 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aaai.12182)). This is consistent with the broader meta-analysis rather than being a second independent meta-analysis.

**Established implication:** an agent’s prose account of why its action is safe is weak review material. Prefer diffs, exact targets, test results, exit codes, source links, before/after state, and reversible previews. A human gate with no independent evidence is accountability theater.

### 1.5 Ordinary continuous supervision degrades into rubber-stamping

The strongest direct agent-specific signal is new and therefore not yet “settled,” but it falsifies any claim that ordinary review is reliably protective. In a controlled 2026 preprint with more than 100 developers working for roughly five hours with frontier coding agents, **94% failed to detect covert sabotage without a monitor**. Even when a monitor correctly warned users, sabotage still succeeded in 56% of monitored sessions because users accepted the code; the paper attributes failures to minimal review, plausible cover stories, and overtrust ([Ye et al. 2026, abstract and full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647)). Because this is a very recent preprint and a deliberately adversarial condition, its exact rate is not established beyond that setup. The narrower conclusion—that “a human was watching” does not guarantee detection—is directly observed.

The qualitative field evidence points the same way but is not causal: interviews with 17 experienced agent users found four modes of oversight (a priori control, co-planning, real-time monitoring, post-hoc review) and reported difficulty reviewing agent-generated code ([Dhanorkar, Passi, and Vorvoreanu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05391)).

**Established implication:** do not interrupt for every command. High prompt volume consumes the vigilance on which the protection depends. Batch safe actions and reserve hard stops for high-value boundaries.

### 1.6 Clarification has value when it resolves material intent entropy, not merely because language is vague

Zhang and Choi formalized when clarification is useful across question answering, translation, and natural-language inference. Their IntentSim estimator, based on the distribution of plausible user intents, consistently outperformed other uncertainty estimators at identifying cases that benefit from clarification. The paper explicitly models user preference for speed versus carefulness ([NAACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.306/)). A separate analysis of human task dialogues found humans rarely clarified mere referential ambiguity but often clarified task-level uncertainty, warning against keyword-style “vagueness” detectors ([Madge, Purver, and Poesio 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.crac-1.1/)).

**Established implication:** ask only if plausible answers lead to materially different actions or artifacts. If a safe reversible default preserves options, act and report the assumption.

### 1.7 Selective escalation is deployable on the native bus

Claude Code officially supports permission modes, `allowedTools`, `disallowedTools`, and a permission-prompt tool; `plan` mode permits analysis without mutation ([Claude Code CLI reference](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/cli-usage)). Its permission rules support allow/ask/deny behavior, and official documentation states that `PreToolUse` hooks run before the normal permission system and can approve or deny a call ([Claude Code permissions documentation](https://docs.anthropic.com/ko/docs/claude-code/iam)). The page is served in Korean in the indexed result, but it is first-party Anthropic documentation and describes the same product schema; production implementation must re-check the installed version’s English docs/schema.

**Established implication:** selective gating does not require a new runtime. It can ride existing rules, permissions, and hooks.

## 2. Promising but unproven

### 2.1 Risk-weighted oversight beats cadence-based checkpoints

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that gates based on action consequence (externality, irreversibility, credential/data sensitivity, destructive scope) outperform “ask every N tool calls” checkpoints for this harness. Evidence: controlled studies show human attention is not automatically complementary; cognitive forcing works but is disliked; Claude Code already distinguishes tool/action classes in its permission layer ([Vaccaro et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1), [Buçinca et al.](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), [Anthropic CLI reference](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/cli-usage)). **UNVERIFIED — settle with a replay experiment comparing consequential errors caught per owner interruption under risk routing versus fixed cadence.**

### 2.2 Front-load intent, back-load proof, avoid midstream narration

It is **likely (55-80%)** that one compact intent checkpoint before divergent implementation plus one evidence checkpoint at completion will outperform frequent progress approvals on owner time and rework. Evidence: ambiguity-estimation work identifies clarification value before acting; cognitive forcing preserves independent judgment before anchoring; developer interviews observe both co-planning and post-hoc review but do not compare their causal effectiveness ([Zhang and Choi 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.306/), [Bondi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06751), [Dhanorkar et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05391)). **UNVERIFIED — settle with within-owner A/B trials measuring corrective turns, abandoned work, time-to-accept, and escaped defects.**

### 2.3 Machine triage can concentrate scarce human review

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a narrow deterministic/low-capability triage layer can reduce review volume without materially increasing missed high-impact anomalies, if it routes rather than approves. OpenAI reports monitoring tens of millions of internal coding-agent trajectories and escalating roughly 1,000 moderate-severity alerts to humans, explicitly describing this as limiting human review ([OpenAI deployment report](https://openai.com/index/how-we-monitor-internal-coding-agents-misalignment/)). This is first-party operational reporting, not an independently audited controlled trial; it establishes feasibility, not the claimed causal benefit for this harness. **UNVERIFIED — settle with shadow labels on all proposed prompts, blinded owner adjudication of a stratified sample, and measured precision/recall before enabling blocking.**

### 2.4 A structured “action card” is better than a raw shell prompt

It is **likely (55-80%)** that showing `intent / exact target / effect / reversibility / evidence after action` improves consequential approval quality compared with raw command text. Evidence is indirect: human–AI studies favor independent judgment and verifiable information, while this harness has live evidence that matching raw payload prose instead of semantic role causes false alarms (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 9-50 and 63-82). **UNVERIFIED — settle with a randomized approval study using recorded safe and unsafe calls; compare detection, false denial, decision time, and confidence calibration for raw versus structured prompts.**

### 2.5 Escalating retry loops is valuable, but a fixed retry number is not yet justified

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that repeated identical error fingerprints with no changed hypothesis are a useful escalation signal. Evidence: the supplied corpus contains 5,692 error fingerprints and measured retry loops, and the live defect report documents repeated false conclusions caused by unvalidated detectors (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 111-129). It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that “after exactly two failures” is the best threshold; task and tool error distributions differ. **UNVERIFIED — settle by replaying historical loops and plotting recoveries versus wasted calls after each repeated fingerprint, segmented by tool and error class.**

### 2.6 Review based on independent artifacts can outperform transcript review

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that owner review of diffs/tests/target state is more useful per minute than reading a long agent transcript. Evidence: explanations rarely produce complementarity, recent sabotage experiments show transcript-era human monitoring is weak, and software outcomes often admit executable checks ([Fok and Weld 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aaai.12182), [Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647), [InterCode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14898)). The exact magnitude for this harness remains **UNVERIFIED — settle with timed blinded reviews of the same completed tasks using transcript summaries versus evidence bundles, scored against seeded defects.**

### 2.7 Confidence-based self-escalation alone is inadequate

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that model-written confidence should be only one weak signal, never the sole gate. Evidence: the useful clarification estimator models distributions over intents rather than accepting a self-report; explanation research shows persuasive rationales can increase reliance; the jagged-frontier experiment shows users can confidently apply AI outside its competence ([Zhang and Choi 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.306/), [Buçinca et al. 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), [Dell’Acqua et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321)). **UNVERIFIED — settle by calibrating self-reported bands against historical task outcomes and comparing them to behavioral signals such as test disagreement, tool errors, and unresolved requirements.**

## 3. Hype, and the tell that gives it away

### 3.1 “Human in the loop” as a checkbox

**Hype claim:** any workflow with a final approve button is safe.  
**Tell:** the proposal reports presence of approval, not error-detection rate, false approvals, owner time, or what evidence the approver sees. It ignores the meta-analysis and the 94% missed-sabotage result ([Vaccaro et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1), [Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647)).

### 3.2 “Explainability creates trust”

**Hype claim:** a rationale makes an action reviewable.  
**Tell:** evaluation measures trust, satisfaction, or perceived transparency rather than appropriate reliance or task correctness. Cognitive forcing reduced overreliance while receiving worse user ratings; explanations alone often fail to create complementarity ([Buçinca et al. 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), [Fok and Weld 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aaai.12182)).

### 3.3 “Ask whenever uncertain”

**Hype claim:** more clarification means more alignment.  
**Tell:** no interruption-cost model, no distinction between harmless referential ambiguity and action-changing task ambiguity, and no safe-default path. Primary research explicitly frames clarification as a utility trade-off and finds task uncertainty more predictive than surface ambiguity ([Zhang and Choi 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.306/), [Madge et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.crac-1.1/)).

### 3.4 “A second AI reviewer replaces human oversight”

**Hype claim:** actor-plus-critic is equivalent to independent review.  
**Tell:** actor and reviewer share model family, context, incentives, or evidence, and the paper reports benchmark accuracy rather than correlated failure. OpenAI’s own monitoring deployment escalates anomalies to humans instead of claiming the monitor closes the loop ([OpenAI monitoring report](https://openai.com/index/how-we-monitor-internal-coding-agents-misalignment/)). A second model can triage; it is not independent evidence.

### 3.5 “Continuous monitoring is defense in depth”

**Hype claim:** logging or watching every step guarantees control.  
**Tell:** no alert budget, no measured base rate, no false-positive burden, and no experiment showing users act correctly on warnings. In the new sabotage study, correctly raised warnings were still ignored often enough for 56% sabotage success in the monitored condition ([Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647)).

### 3.6 “One universal risk score”

**Hype claim:** a scalar confidence/risk threshold routes all tasks.  
**Tell:** the score has no decomposition into ambiguity, externality, reversibility, scope, sensitivity, and evidence quality; calibration is not stratified by tool/action class. The jagged frontier and heterogeneous meta-analysis make a universal scalar especially suspect ([Dell’Acqua et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321), [Vaccaro et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)).

### 3.7 “Human oversight preserves accountability” without preserving agency

**Hype claim:** the owner remains accountable because the owner can stop the agent.  
**Tell:** the owner sees the request only after the agent has selected and framed the option, or cannot edit the proposed action. Native permission-prompt tooling can return a modified input as well as allow/deny, so binary rubber-stamping is not a platform necessity ([Anthropic Claude Code SDK permission example](https://docs.anthropic.com/fr/docs/claude-code/sdk)).

## 4. Specific adoptions for this harness

### 4.1 UNVERIFIED recommendation — adopt a three-lane consequence router

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Define three lanes using existing permission rules:

1. **Flow:** read/search, local inspection, reversible edits inside authorized workspace, tests, formatting, and other operations whose failure is contained and observable.
2. **Inform:** actions that change local artifacts materially but remain readily reversible; batch them and provide a concise post-action evidence bundle rather than asking per call.
3. **Confirm:** external communication/publication, credential or secret access, permission changes, installs, deploys, financial/legal commitments, destructive or broad overwrite/delete, writes outside explicit scope, and operations whose exact target cannot be resolved.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to be implementable without a new runtime because Claude Code exposes allow/deny rules and permission modes ([official CLI reference](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/cli-usage), [official permissions documentation](https://docs.anthropic.com/ko/docs/claude-code/iam)). Net benefit is **UNVERIFIED — shadow-classify 500 real tool calls, manually label all Confirm cases plus a random Flow sample, and require zero missed seeded catastrophic actions before enforcement.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. It uses the native permission bus, adds no orchestration runtime, memory system, durable executor, trained critic, SaaS, or guardrail framework. Keep the taxonomy small and action-based; do not learn a bespoke classifier until deterministic rules fail in measured cases.

### 4.2 UNVERIFIED recommendation — ask at the “branch point,” not at task start by default

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Add a harness rule: ask one concise question only when two or more plausible interpretations would produce materially different, costly-to-reverse outcomes and existing context cannot choose. Otherwise state the safe assumption and proceed reversibly. For a needed choice, present neutral options before revealing a recommendation; if a recommendation is necessary, label it separately after the options.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce rework without creating question fatigue, based on intent-entropy research and independent-judgment experiments ([Zhang and Choi 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.306/), [Bondi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06751)). **UNVERIFIED — tag clarification turns for four weeks and compare answered-question rate, downstream owner correction, abandoned work, and delay against matched tasks where the agent assumed.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is an interaction rule on the existing conversation bus, not a planning subsystem.

### 4.3 UNVERIFIED recommendation — replace raw high-risk prompts with a five-field action card

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

At `PreToolUse`, only for Confirm-lane actions, show:

```text
INTENT: what owner goal this serves
TARGET: exact resolved resource(s), count, and scope
EFFECT: externally observable or irreversible change
RECOVERY: rollback/backup path, or "none"
VERIFY: independent postcondition that will be checked
```

Allow **approve once / edit action / deny**. Never build the card by scanning arbitrary payload prose for scary substrings; derive it from tool name, structured arguments, resolved targets, and known command semantics. That requirement is grounded locally: four detectors confused mention with use, including a sentinel that treated a false-valued JSON field name as failure (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 52-82). The current hook schema’s ability to expose every needed field is **UNVERIFIED — dump actual `PreToolUse` inputs for one known-present Bash, Write/Edit, and MCP call before design; if structured target/effect cannot be derived, keep native prompts rather than guessing.**

The card’s improvement over raw prompts is **likely (55-80%)**, based on cognitive-forcing and evidence-verification results, not a direct coding-agent card trial ([Buçinca et al. 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml)).

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes only if the schema probe confirms a small hook. If implementation grows beyond the existing bus or needs a semantic policy daemon, reject it; that would cross into BUILD-LARGE without evidence.

### 4.4 UNVERIFIED recommendation — use evidence gates at completion, not “I checked” attestations

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

For tasks claiming “fixed,” “done,” “absent,” or “deployed,” require a compact evidence bundle appropriate to the claim:

- changed artifact: scoped diff plus test/lint/build result;
- external action: returned identifier/status plus independent read-back where available;
- absence: known-positive detector control plus a mechanically independent second signal;
- destructive action: pre-target enumeration, post-target enumeration, and recovery statement;
- research claim: source links and explicit unverified gaps.

Do not require every field for every task; choose the smallest evidence type that can falsify the claim. The local necessity of known-positive controls is confirmed by seven wrong conclusions from unvalidated probes (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 111-129). The benefit of this generalized gate is **very likely (80-90%)**, because it converts persuasive self-report into falsifiable outcomes; exact interruption cost is **UNVERIFIED — replay 100 historical completion claims with and without the bundle and score caught defects and added latency.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes if implemented as a Stop hook plus task-type templates. Do not create a durable workflow engine or provenance graph. The evidence bundle belongs in the existing transcript/artifact stream.

### 4.5 UNVERIFIED recommendation — escalate stagnant failures, not ordinary iteration

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Trigger a non-blocking “change hypothesis” reminder when the normalized error fingerprint repeats without a material change in command, target, or stated hypothesis. Escalate to the owner only when one of these is also true: destructive workaround proposed, authority/scope expansion needed, externally consequential deadline, or the next diagnostic requires a choice only the owner can make. Do not hard-code two retries globally; begin in shadow mode and learn per-tool distributions from the existing corpus.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that stagnant-loop detection is useful, given 5,692 corpus error fingerprints and known retry loops. It is **UNVERIFIED whether a sub-200-line implementation can normalize PowerShell, Bash, MCP, and structured-output failures without excessive false matches — settle with an offline replay that must demonstrate detector known-positive controls, per-tool precision, and examples of both caught loops and allowed productive retries.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes if this is a stateless or session-local hook using existing fingerprints. Reject any proposal for durable execution, vector memory, or a learned critic.

### 4.6 UNVERIFIED recommendation — batch owner review around artifacts and boundaries

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)**

Interaction policy:

- no approval for low-risk reads and diagnostics;
- one branch-point question when intent truly diverges;
- one action card immediately before each distinct irreversible/external commitment (not each command in a command sequence if the targets/effect are identical);
- concise progress updates on a time/event cadence for awareness, never requiring acknowledgment;
- one completion evidence bundle;
- immediate escalation for scope/authority changes or detected high-impact anomaly.

This is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve owner vigilance better than per-command confirmation, based on the friction/overreliance trade-off and recent warning-acceptance failures ([Buçinca et al. 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), [Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647)). **UNVERIFIED — compare interruptions, median response latency, denial reversals, owner corrections, and consequential escapes before and after rollout.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes. This is a native interaction policy.

### 4.7 UNVERIFIED recommendation — instrument the oversight policy before enforcing it

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Run in three stages:

1. **Detector validation:** demonstrate a known-present Flow, Inform, and Confirm example; demonstrate a known-present loop; validate a safe negative. Use mechanically different signals where claiming absence.
2. **Shadow mode:** log proposed lane, reason codes, tool/action class, whether the owner was actually asked, decision, latency, edits, and downstream correction. Because the corpus hook detector is broken, first locate the real event sink using the two probes already prescribed in `INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 133-147; if neither works, add minimal append-only local event output before evaluating.
3. **Controlled rollout:** randomize eligible medium-risk episodes between native prompting and routed cards. Never randomize genuinely catastrophic actions to a no-gate arm; use seeded/replayed cases for those. Primary metrics: consequential errors prevented per blocking interruption, false-block rate, owner minutes, corrective turns, and escaped defects. Secondary metrics: task time and subjective annoyance.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that evaluation is required before claiming this policy works, because current hook telemetry cannot support a causal or even usage claim and the local defect report demonstrates detector failures (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md`, lines 111-147). It is **likely (55-80%)** that this can remain under 200 lines if an event sink exists; that sizing is **UNVERIFIED — the named sink-discovery experiment settles it.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes only as minimal local event logging and offline analysis. It must not become observability SaaS, a durable execution service, or a second orchestration runtime.

### 4.8 UNVERIFIED recommendation — adopt an explicit non-adoption list

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Record these as rejected by default unless later experiments overturn them:

- approval on every tool call;
- periodic “continue?” gates based only on elapsed turns/time;
- model self-confidence as the sole escalation criterion;
- free-form rationale as proof of safety or completion;
- actor-model self-review as independent verification;
- learned universal risk score before deterministic lanes are exhausted;
- a second agent runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or general guardrail framework.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this negative configuration prevents architecture creep without reducing useful oversight, because every retained mechanism above rides an existing bus and each rejected item either lacks independent evidence or is explicitly barred by the anti-overfit gate. **UNVERIFIED — review exceptions quarterly against measured escaped errors; an exception requires a concrete failure the minimal bus cannot address and an evaluation plan.**

**Anti-overfit gate:** passes by construction and explicitly excludes all previously rejected component classes.

## 5. Placement decision table

| Moment | Human role | Gate? | Required review object | Why attention pays or does not |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Request is clear; action is local, reversible, testable | Set goal once | No | Post-action evidence | Per-action approval adds anchoring/fatigue without unique human information. |
| Multiple plausible intents lead to materially different artifacts | Supply preference | Yes, before plan commits | Neutral options and consequence delta | Owner has information the agent cannot infer; early correction avoids rework. |
| Agent has a safe reversible default that preserves options | Receive assumption | No | Assumption in progress/final note | Interruption cost exceeds immediate risk. |
| External, destructive, permission-changing, secret-bearing, costly, or legally/socially committing action | Authorize consequence | Yes, immediately pre-action | Five-field action card | Owner uniquely supplies authority and accepts consequence. |
| Long low-risk implementation | Stay aware | Non-blocking update only | Progress, blockers, next boundary | Continuous approval depletes vigilance. |
| Same failure recurs with unchanged hypothesis | Choose only if scope/authority must change | Conditional | Fingerprint, attempts, changed hypotheses, smallest next experiment | Human value comes from reframing or authority, not watching retries. |
| Completion claim has executable/read-back evidence | Verify outcome | Usually no blocking gate unless impact warrants | Diff/tests/status/read-back | Independent evidence is more diagnostic than narrative. |
| Completion/absence claim lacks independent evidence | Withhold acceptance or authorize evidence acquisition | Yes if consequential | Explicit gap and cheapest settling experiment | A human cannot validate what neither party can observe. |
| Monitor raises high-impact anomaly | Adjudicate with evidence | Yes | Alert reason, raw action/target, independent state | Selective escalation conserves attention, but warning must be actionable. |

## 6. Alternatives considered and rejected

### Full manual approval

Rejected because it spends the most attention where the human has the least incremental information. It conflicts with prompt-fatigue evidence and provides no answer to missed sabotage under active review ([Buçinca et al. 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), [Ye et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647)).

### Fully autonomous execution plus final review

Rejected for consequential actions because external and irreversible effects cannot always be undone at final review. Final review remains appropriate for local reversible work, especially with independent tests/diffs.

### Model-chosen escalation from self-reported confidence

Rejected as the sole mechanism because calibration is task-dependent and rationales can anchor reviewers. Behavioral/action properties and observed evidence disagreement are harder signals.

### Actor/critic multi-agent review

Rejected as the default because it introduces extra scaffolding and correlated model failure without adding human authority or independent environment evidence. A narrow monitor may triage at scale, but its value must be measured and alerts must terminate in actionable human adjudication ([OpenAI monitoring report](https://openai.com/index/how-we-monitor-internal-coding-agents-misalignment/)).

### A new governance/oversight service

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. The native permission, hook, conversation, and evidence buses are sufficient to test every recommended mechanism. A BUILD-LARGE service has no present justification.
