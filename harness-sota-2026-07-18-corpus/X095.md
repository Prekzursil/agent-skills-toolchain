# X095 — cross-domain borrow: metrology → reliable agent conclusions

## Executive result

**Verdict: transfer, but only partially and with a hard boundary.** The metrology mechanism worth borrowing is **check-standard measurement assurance inside a traceable measurement process**: run a stable, known reference through the same measurement process as ordinary work; retain the result and method identity; compare current behavior with an empirical baseline/control limit; and reject the run when the check is out of control. NIST describes check-standard methodology as a way to expose errors that affect a measurement process over time, with regularly spaced measurements acting as surrogates for otherwise-unseen measurements ([NIST, “What is a check standard?”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)). NIST further says the current calibration is rejected when the check-standard result exceeds the control limits ([NIST, “Control of bias and long-term variability”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc352.htm)).

The concrete harness translation is a **probe-assurance gate** around high-consequence negative or terminal conclusions (`ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, `DONE`):

1. state exactly what property is being probed and what result would count;
2. send a known-present control through the same observation and interpretation path;
3. where feasible, send a known-absent control too;
4. require two mechanically independent signals about the target;
5. preserve raw outputs, method identity, scope, time, and known limitations as a short traceability record;
6. emit `INCONCLUSIVE`—never the negative/terminal conclusion—if a control fails, the signals disagree, scope is incomplete, or independence cannot be demonstrated.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch the specific class “a detector reports zero/absence because the detector itself is blind,” because a known-present control traversing that same path will also disappear; the evidence is the identical structural role of NIST’s check standard, which exposes changes in the measurement process rather than assuming every workload item can be independently known ([NIST check-standard definition](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)). The benefit in this owner’s workload is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the replay-plus-mutation trial specified below**, so this is not a claim that the six-plus supplied incidents have already been prevented.

The full quantitative apparatus of physical metrology does **not** transfer. Agent conclusions are often categorical, open-world, context-sensitive propositions rather than scalar estimates of a stable measurand. A numeric “± uncertainty” attached to “there are no skills” would be pseudo-metrology unless a calibrated empirical error model exists for that exact claim class. The International Vocabulary of Metrology itself warns that traceability neither ensures fitness for purpose nor absence of mistakes ([BIPM/JCGM VIM 2.41, Note 5](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). The useful borrow is therefore the **reference/control discipline and rejection semantics**, not SI-style numerical uncertainty.

## What metrology solved in its own field

### 1. Calibration is a relationship, not a self-declaration

The VIM defines calibration as a two-step operation: under specified conditions, establish a relation between reference-standard values (with their uncertainties) and instrument indications (with their uncertainties), then use that information to obtain a measurement result from an indication ([BIPM/JCGM VIM 2.39](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.39.html)). Its notes explicitly distinguish calibration from adjustment and verification. The important idea is epistemic: an instrument does not establish its own correctness merely by returning a plausible reading.

In ordinary calibration work, that prevents circular assurance. “The scale says the reference mass is 1 kg” is evidence only because the reference value is established outside that reading and because the comparison occurs under specified conditions. Calibration can reveal a correction and its uncertainty; it is not synonymous with tweaking the instrument until it agrees.

### 2. Traceability records an unbroken comparison chain and its uncertainty contributions

VIM defines metrological traceability as the property by which a measurement result relates to a reference through a **documented, unbroken chain of calibrations**, each contributing to measurement uncertainty ([BIPM/JCGM VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)). It defines the traceability chain as the sequence of standards and calibrations used to relate the result to that reference ([BIPM/JCGM VIM 2.42](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.42.html)). NIST’s policy uses the same definition, assigns the result provider responsibility for supporting the traceability claim, assigns the result user responsibility for assessing its validity, and warns that traceability alone does not guarantee fitness for purpose ([NIST Policy on Metrological Traceability](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability)).

This solves two recurrent metrology problems:

- a result cannot gain credibility merely by saying “traceable to NIST”; the provider must identify the chain and support the claim;
- every link contributes uncertainty, so provenance is not a decorative audit log—it is part of deciding whether the result is adequate for its intended use.

NIST’s uncertainty guidance also combines individual uncertainty components **and covariances where appropriate**, rather than pretending correlated components are independent ([NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)). That is an important constraint on the proposed “two signals” rule: two commands that consume the same stale index and parser are not two independent signals.

### 3. Check standards continuously test the measurement process

Calibration at commissioning time does not prove that a process remains sound. NIST’s check-standard method addresses drift and latent process failure:

- A check standard is an artifact or reproducible process characteristic measured repeatedly. Historical results establish bias and long-term variability; measurements spaced over time serve as surrogates for measurements that cannot economically be made on all test items ([NIST, “What is a check standard?”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)).
- NIST says a persistent shift can indicate damaged reference/check standards or a procedure change; changed variability can indicate environmental-control loss, handling change, or instrument degradation ([NIST, “How are bias and variability controlled?”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section2/mpc22.htm)).
- The check standard should resemble workload items, be stable, and remain continuously available. NIST calls for check standards at each critical measurement level rather than one universal artifact ([NIST, “Control of artifact calibration”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc35.htm)).
- Once an empirical baseline and limits exist, the check is evaluated in real time. An out-of-limit value makes the run out of control and the current calibration is rejected; recurring signals require investigation ([NIST, “Control of bias and long-term variability”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc352.htm)).

This is stronger than “run a smoke test sometimes.” The reference is workload-shaped, the observation is accumulated over time, the acceptance criterion is established before the current result, and failure invalidates the current run rather than becoming a footnote after publication.

### 4. Verification and validation remain distinct

VIM defines verification as objective evidence that specified requirements are fulfilled, and validation as verification where those requirements are adequate for intended use ([BIPM/JCGM VIM 2.44](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.44.html), [VIM 2.45](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.45.html)). A detector can therefore pass a narrow unit check yet be unfit for the claim the agent makes. For example, proving that a file enumerator returns one fixture verifies that behavior; proving that the method covers ignored files, remote-only paths, inaccessible directories, and the relevant time boundary would be part of validating it for a broad “absent everywhere” conclusion.

## Concrete translation to an agent harness

### Structural mapping

| Metrology term | Harness analogue | What must be recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Measurand | Precisely scoped proposition, e.g. “no matching file exists in roots R at revision T” | claim type, quantifier, roots, revision/time, exclusions |
| Measuring system | Tool + transport + permissions + enumerator/parser + agent interpretation | tool/method versions, authority, exit status, truncation/pagination state |
| Indication | Raw command/API response | immutable raw output or digest plus retrievable location |
| Reference standard | Fixture or state whose truth is established independently of the current probe | fixture identifier, expected truth, origin, expiry/revalidation condition |
| Check standard | Known-present/known-absent canary shaped like the current workload | control query, expected result, observed result, same-path proof |
| Calibration/verification | Compare the probe path with the controls; confirm stated requirements | pass/fail and acceptance rule fixed before target interpretation |
| Traceability chain | Links from final claim through interpretation, raw observations, controls, and externally established references | ordered evidence links and transformations |
| Influence quantity | Permissions, cwd/root, revision, pagination, cache age, quoting, locale, timeout, network state | explicit context fields and anomaly flags |
| Out-of-control run | Failed canary, signal conflict, truncation, stale scope, or unexplained change in detector behavior | `INCONCLUSIVE`, rejected claim, diagnostic reason |

The mapping is conceptual, not a claim of numerical equivalence. The table’s target-side design is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a schema-instrumented replay showing whether each field predicts or explains injected and historical misses**.

### [UNVERIFIED — settle with replay-plus-mutation trial] Proposal A — Probe Assurance Gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add one pre-publication hook or small validation helper for only three high-risk claim families: `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, and `DONE`. It accepts a compact claim envelope and returns `ACCEPT`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, or `ESCALATE`. It does not orchestrate work, choose tools, store general memory, or judge arbitrary prose.

Minimal envelope:

```json
{
  "claim_class": "ABSENT|BROKEN|DONE",
  "proposition": "precisely scoped predicate",
  "scope": {"roots": [], "revision_or_time": "", "exclusions": []},
  "method": {"observer": "", "parser": "", "authority": "", "version": ""},
  "controls": [
    {"kind": "known-present", "expected": true, "observed": true, "same_path": true},
    {"kind": "known-absent", "expected": false, "observed": false, "same_path": true}
  ],
  "signals": [
    {"raw_evidence": "", "failure_domains": []},
    {"raw_evidence": "", "failure_domains": []}
  ],
  "coverage_limits": [],
  "decision": "ACCEPT|INCONCLUSIVE|ESCALATE"
}
```

Acceptance rule:

```text
if claim_class not in {ABSENT, BROKEN, DONE}: pass through
if proposition or scope is underspecified: INCONCLUSIVE
if no known-present control traversed the same relevant path: INCONCLUSIVE
if any control differs from its externally established expectation: ESCALATE
if fewer than two target signals: INCONCLUSIVE
if signals share a material failure domain: INCONCLUSIVE
if raw results disagree or any result is truncated, paginated, stale, or unauthorized: ESCALATE
otherwise: ACCEPT, retaining the envelope with the claim
```

The gate should require a negative control only when one can be stable and cheap. A positive control is mandatory because it proves that the detector can see at least one known-present item. The negative control catches inverted predicates, filters that match everything, and parsers that collapse all responses to “present.”

Expected effect: the gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to convert detector-blindness false negatives into `INCONCLUSIVE`, not because a confidence estimate says so, but because the same-path known-positive check directly challenges the detector’s ability to observe a present item; the supporting source-field mechanism is NIST’s workload-shaped check standard ([NIST check-standard definition](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)). This expected effect is **UNVERIFIED; replay the supplied wrong-conclusion cases and inject filter, permission, truncation, pagination, stale-cache, wrong-root, and parser mutations to settle it**.

The gate is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce confidently wrong `DONE` claims, conditional on completion criteria being machine-observable; evidence is VIM’s distinction between verification and validation for intended use ([VIM 2.44](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.44.html), [VIM 2.45](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.45.html)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying completed and incomplete tasks with mutated test selection, stale artifacts, and unmet acceptance criteria, then compare false-accept rates**.

It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to prevent wrong open-ended semantic conclusions, because no finite canary validates a model’s interpretation of arbitrary evidence and the propositions lack stable reference standards; this follows from the disanalogy analyzed below. That limit is a reason to keep the gate narrow.

### [UNVERIFIED — settle with independence-ablation trial] Proposal B — Failure-domain declarations, not “two commands”

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Require each signal to declare its material failure domains. The second signal qualifies only when it changes at least one decisive domain and does not retain a known common-mode cause. Useful dimensions are:

- observation substrate: filesystem walk vs version-control object database vs service API;
- execution path: shell process vs direct API/MCP call;
- enumerator/parser: native structured response vs text parsing;
- authority: current credentials/host vs separately checked authority boundary;
- state source: live state vs immutable revision/snapshot;
- interpretation: direct invariant/assertion vs free-form model reading.

Examples:

- `find` followed by `rg` over the same directory walk is not independent for an unreadable directory or wrong root.
- Two API calls through the same cached index are not independent for stale-index failure.
- Rephrasing a question to the same model over the same truncated output is not independent.
- A live filesystem enumeration and an object-database query can be independent for an enumeration/parser defect, but not for a wrongly scoped repository.
- A functional known-good request and a separately sourced transport/registration status can be independent for diagnosing a tool as broken, but they are not independent if both are produced by the same gateway health cache.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to prevent false compliance with the two-signal rule because explicit failure-domain overlap makes common-mode dependence reviewable; evidence is NIST TN 1297’s requirement to account for covariance when combining uncertainty components ([NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)). It is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by giving evaluators paired probes with hidden shared faults, comparing overlap-detection and wrong-acceptance rates with and without the declarations**.

This is not a claim that qualitative failure-domain labels are mathematically equivalent to covariance. They are a conservative design-time screen. No numeric independence score should be emitted without empirical joint-failure data.

### [UNVERIFIED — settle with a shadow-mode time series] Proposal C — A small family of workload-shaped check standards

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Maintain canaries per critical probe class, not one global “hello world”:

- enumeration: one known-present object at each relevant scope/representation boundary and one known-absent name;
- structured output: a valid boundary-shape fixture plus a deliberately invalid fixture whose rejection is expected;
- MCP/tool connectivity: one read-only functional request with a stable expected invariant;
- web retrieval: one controlled reachable endpoint and one controlled failure response;
- completion: a deliberately failing acceptance fixture and a known-passing fixture;
- pagination/truncation: a fixture guaranteed to exceed one page or response threshold.

NIST explicitly says the check standard should match workload type and geometry and that critical measurement levels need their own check standards ([NIST, “Control of artifact calibration”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc35.htm)). The transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to be more diagnostic than one universal canary because class-specific controls exercise different failure paths; the evidence is that source-field requirement plus the target’s heterogeneous probe mechanisms. Its real operational value is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a four-week shadow run measuring injected-fault detection by canary family, false alarms, latency, and stale-canary incidents**.

Canary truth must be established outside the probe under test. A detector that creates a fixture and then uses its own enumeration result to certify the fixture exists is circular. Prefer immutable packaged fixtures with independent digests, service-owned test resources with documented invariants, or target-state setup confirmed by a separate authority. Each canary needs an owner, expiry/revalidation condition, and scope identity.

### [UNVERIFIED — settle after sufficient baseline observations] Proposal D — Control-chart drift detection, shadow mode first

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Record canary outcomes and latency by method version and environment. After enough stable observations, use simple predeclared run rules or a Shewhart-style chart to detect step changes; consider EWMA only for a demonstrated need to detect smaller persistent shifts. NIST describes Shewhart charts as simple and better for large changes, while EWMA uses weighted prior observations and is more sensitive to small changes ([NIST, “How are bias and variability controlled?”](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section2/mpc22.htm)). NIST also establishes baselines and limits from historical check-standard values before real-time rejection ([NIST control procedure](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc352.htm)).

Do not manufacture “3σ” limits from a tiny, autocorrelated, version-mixed sample. Start in shadow mode, segment at method/configuration changes, and graduate to rejection only after false-alarm and miss costs are measured. Drift detection is **likely (55-80%)** to reveal persistent degradation that per-call controls miss, because it aggregates check behavior over time; the premise is **UNVERIFIED, and the settling experiment is a shadow-mode series with seeded gradual and abrupt degradations plus predeclared detection-delay/false-alarm thresholds**.

This is the lowest-priority element. The per-claim rejection gate should ship first if the replay experiment succeeds.

## What the mechanism would catch that a single probe misses

The following are predicted catches, not observations of the local harness:

1. **Blind zero-result detector.** A filter, parser, permission boundary, wrong root, or disabled capability returns an empty target result. The same-path known-present check also returns empty; the run becomes `ESCALATE`. Catch probability is **very likely (80-90%)** when the control truly crosses the same failing component; evidence is the NIST check-standard role ([NIST check-standard definition](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle with one mutation per named fault**.
2. **All-positive/inverted detector.** The known-absent control appears present, so the run is rejected. Catch probability is **very likely (80-90%)** for deterministic inversion or match-all defects; evidence is direct logical discrimination by opposite controls. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle with predicate-inversion and parser-default mutations**.
3. **Common-mode “independent” confirmations.** Two surface-different probes share one stale index, wrong scope, parser, or credential. Failure-domain declarations prevent them from satisfying the gate. Benefit is **likely (55-80%)** because declared dependencies expose many—not all—shared causes; evidence is the source-field need to account for correlated inputs ([NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle with hidden-dependency ablation**.
4. **Partial enumeration mistaken for universal absence.** Pagination, truncation, timeout, exclusions, or an unvisited root violates the scoped measurement procedure, so the conclusion becomes `INCONCLUSIVE`. Benefit is **very likely (80-90%)** if completion metadata is machine-visible, but only **likely (55-80%)** if the agent must infer it from text; evidence is VIM’s requirement that calibration operate under specified conditions and establish the indication-to-result relationship ([VIM 2.39](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.39.html)). Both estimates are **UNVERIFIED; settle with response-boundary mutations**.
5. **Detector drift after a version/configuration change.** Class-specific canary history changes level or variability and triggers investigation. Benefit is **likely (55-80%)** after a valid baseline exists; evidence is NIST’s control-chart use for persistent shifts and variability changes ([NIST bias/variability control](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section2/mpc22.htm)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle with shadow-mode seeded drift**.
6. **“Done” based only on artifact existence.** A negative completion canary demonstrates whether the verifier can reject an artifact that exists but violates acceptance criteria; an independent outcome signal tests the intended effect. Benefit is **likely (55-80%)** only for tasks with explicit, observable criteria; evidence is the verification/validation distinction ([VIM 2.44](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.44.html), [VIM 2.45](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.45.html)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle with incomplete-but-plausible artifacts in replay**.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Why the analogy does transfer

The structural commonality is not “agents are instruments.” It is narrower:

1. A process converts an inaccessible target state into an observable indication.
2. The observation path can fail while returning a syntactically valid result.
3. The operator may not know the truth of ordinary workload items.
4. A stable known item can traverse the same path and reveal some process faults.
5. A failed process control should invalidate the current result.

Those five properties are present in both physical measurement and tool-mediated agent probing. The transfer of same-path controls and rejection semantics is therefore **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally sound; evidence is NIST’s explicit use of check measurements as surrogates for unavailable workload measurements and its rejection of out-of-control runs ([NIST check standard](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm), [NIST control procedure](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc352.htm)). Operational effectiveness here remains **UNVERIFIED; the replay-plus-mutation trial settles it**.

### Where the analogy breaks

1. **No stable scalar measurand.** “Does object X exist?” can be sharply defined, but “is the harness broken?” or “is the task done?” can depend on intent, time, and an open set of criteria. There is no SI unit, smooth error function, or natural standard uncertainty.
2. **Truth can change during the probe.** Files, services, permissions, and branches can change between observations. Two disagreeing probes may both have been correct at their respective times. The envelope must pin revision/time when possible; otherwise it can only report the temporal limitation.
3. **Controls have incomplete path coverage.** A tiny canary may bypass filename length, pagination, repository size, authentication, or encoding behavior that affects the target. NIST’s “same type and geometry” requirement maps to this exact problem ([NIST check standard](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)). A passing canary is evidence against only the failure modes it exercises.
4. **Reference truth can be wrong or stale.** Fixtures can drift, test accounts can expire, and a digest can prove identity without proving semantics. Controls need independent establishment and revalidation.
5. **Agent interpretation adds a non-instrument layer.** A tool may produce perfect evidence and the model may still overgeneralize. The traceability envelope makes that transformation inspectable but does not calibrate semantics.
6. **Independence is causal, not cosmetic.** Different command names do not imply independent failure. Without a failure-domain model, redundancy can create unjustified confidence.
7. **Metrological traceability is not truth.** VIM directly says traceability does not ensure adequate uncertainty or absence of mistakes ([VIM 2.41, Note 5](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)); NIST says traceability alone does not guarantee fitness for purpose ([NIST traceability policy](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability)). A harness must not rename provenance “proof.”

Therefore this borrow is accepted only for **process assurance around bounded claim types**. It is rejected for numeric confidence manufacture, universal truth certification, or arbitrary-answer grading.

## Uncertainty discipline for agent conclusions

Metrology’s uncertainty budget should translate into a **qualitative limitation budget**, not made-up probabilities. For each gated conclusion, record:

- scope uncertainty: what places, revisions, and time window were not observed;
- observation uncertainty: permissions, timeouts, pagination, truncation, cache age, and transport health;
- interpretation uncertainty: parser assumptions and semantic transformations;
- control relevance: which failure modes the canary exercises and which it does not;
- dependence: common failure domains between signals;
- reference uncertainty: how and when control truth was established.

The published state should be one of `SUPPORTED`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, or `CONTRADICTED`. A calibrated probability is allowed only after repeated labeled outcomes for the same claim/method/context class and an out-of-sample calibration check. It is **very unlikely (5-20%)** that generic model token probabilities or self-reported confidence alone would supply valid metrological-style uncertainty for these propositions, because they do not enumerate scope gaps, common-mode failures, or reference uncertainty; the evidence is the mismatch with VIM’s measurement-result/reference chain and NIST’s component-plus-covariance treatment ([VIM 2.41](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html), [NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)). That probability estimate itself is an analogical judgment, not a measured model-calibration result.

## Settling experiment: replay, mutate, ablate

This experiment is required before enforcement because all owner-specific benefit claims above are `UNVERIFIED`.

### Dataset

- Freeze the supplied six-plus wrong-conclusion incidents as positive cases, preserving the original observation paths and final claims.
- Add an independently adjudicated sample of correct `ABSENT`, `BROKEN`, and `DONE` conclusions to measure needless blocking.
- Create workload-shaped fixtures for each probe family.
- Seed one fault at a time: empty-parser default, predicate inversion, wrong root, unreadable subtree, stale cache/index, pagination omission, truncation, timeout treated as empty, stale revision, shared backend, invalid completion criterion, and changed canary truth.

### Arms

1. baseline single probe;
2. two probes with no independence declaration;
3. positive control plus two probes;
4. positive and negative controls plus two probes;
5. full gate with failure-domain declarations and scope metadata.

### Predeclared metrics

- **Primary:** confidently wrong terminal conclusions per eligible case.
- **Safety:** correct conclusions incorrectly blocked or escalated.
- **Diagnosticity:** fraction of faults assigned to the correct failure domain.
- **Coverage:** fraction of eligible claims that can supply a workload-shaped control and genuinely independent second signal.
- **Cost:** added tool calls, latency, tokens, and human escalations.
- **Calibration:** among accepted conclusions, empirical error rate by claim family; do not pool heterogeneous families.

### Decision rule

Adopt Proposal A for a claim family only if it materially reduces confidently wrong conclusions on held-out and mutated cases without exceeding a pre-agreed escalation/cost budget. Adopt Proposal B only if it reduces acceptance of hidden common-mode pairs. Keep Proposal D in shadow mode until its false-alarm rate and detection delay are characterized. Numeric thresholds must be chosen by the owner before labels are revealed; this unit does not invent owner utility weights.

The gate succeeding on all historical incidents but failing mutations would indicate overfit. Succeeding on mutations but blocking most correct conclusions would indicate an unusable reference/control design. A failed experiment should lead to rejecting the proposal for that claim family, not weakening the acceptance test after seeing results.

## Anti-overfit gate and architecture decision

All proposals ride the existing hook/skill/MCP bus. They add a bounded claim envelope, fixtures/configuration, and at most a small validator/time-series checker. They do **not** add a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The design keeps ordinary claims on the direct path and activates only for high-consequence universal-negative/terminal statements.

The minimal version is Proposal A with one positive control and explicit second-signal independence. Proposal C is configuration that supplies better controls. Proposal D is optional and subordinate. No BUILD-LARGE component is justified: the state and decision logic are small, the data volume is tiny, and nothing in the cited metrology mechanism requires a daemon.

The risk of over-instrumentation is **likely (55-80%)** if the gate is applied to every factual sentence, because open-ended claims cannot supply stable workload-shaped standards and escalation cost would dominate; evidence is the disanalogy above and NIST’s requirement that check standards resemble the workload ([NIST check standard](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section1/mpc12.htm)). This is **UNVERIFIED; settle by reporting eligible-claim coverage and cost separately for the three gated families versus unrestricted prose in the replay study**.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Always run the same probe twice”

Rejected. Repetition estimates repeatability under the same failure conditions; it does not reveal systematic blindness, wrong scope, or a shared stale source. NIST’s uncertainty treatment explicitly accounts for covariances rather than treating multiple inputs as independent by count ([NIST TN 1297 §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-5-combined-standard-uncertainty)).

### “Ask a second model to critique the conclusion”

Rejected as the primary control. A second model reading the same missing/truncated evidence shares the decisive observation failure. It may help interpret conflicting evidence, but it is not a reference standard and is not mechanically independent of the evidence path.

### “Attach a confidence percentage to every answer”

Rejected. Without labeled, claim-class-specific calibration data and defined scope, the number lacks the reference chain and component accounting that give metrological uncertainty meaning. VIM’s warning that traceability does not eliminate mistakes is directly relevant ([VIM 2.41, Note 5](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html)).

### “Build a universal truth/critic service”

Rejected by both the analogy and the anti-overfit gate. There is no universal reference standard for open-ended semantic claims, and such a service would be a second runtime or trained critic—the expressly rejected architecture classes—without evidence that it solves the observed detector-validation failure.

### “A passing canary proves the target result”

Rejected. A control verifies parts of the measurement process, not target truth. Passing controls plus independent target evidence can support a conclusion; controls alone cannot. This follows from the distinct VIM concepts of calibration, verification, and validation ([VIM 2.39](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.39.html), [VIM 2.44](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.44.html), [VIM 2.45](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.45.html)).
