# X070 — Cross-domain borrow: epidemiology → compute and quota cost-control

**Unit:** X070  
**Lens:** epidemiologic surveillance, case definitions, sensitivity/specificity, base-rate reasoning  
**Target:** spend agent compute and quota where added work changes the answer, rather than uniformly  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Local-grounding boundary:** This is deliberately an external ideation study. I did not inspect the harness repository, subject trees, corpus, audit findings, or local configuration. Assertions about what the present harness does or lacks are therefore not made as facts.

## Executive verdict

The borrow **does transfer, but only at the allocation-and-measurement layer**. The specific transferable mechanism is **base-rate-aware two-phase surveillance with stratified reference verification**:

1. apply a cheap, deliberately sensitive case definition to every member of a population;
2. apply an expensive, more specific reference procedure to selected provisional positives;
3. also apply that expensive procedure to a known probability sample of provisional negatives;
4. retain each item's selection probability and estimate sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, and subgroup miss rates from the verified sample; and
5. choose escalation thresholds from expected consequence/net benefit, not from classifier accuracy alone.

In an agent harness, “population member” becomes a task or decision point, “suspected case” becomes a cheap indication that extra compute might change the delivered answer, and “reference procedure” becomes an expensive paired run, independent verification, deeper retrieval, or stronger test regime. The purpose is not to label answers medically. It is to learn, by task stratum, where an extra token/tool/model dollar has positive decision value.

**Transfer confidence: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: the mathematical resource problem is shared—an inexpensive imperfect classifier, a costly reference action, heterogeneous base rates, false-positive and false-negative costs, and a limited verification budget—and epidemiologic two-phase sampling explicitly estimates cheap-classifier error from a reference-classified subset ([Lyles et al., two-phase sampling definition](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278465/)). The confidence is not higher because agent-answer quality lacks a stable, independent gold standard; tasks and models drift; and escalation changes the artifact rather than merely observing a latent state. The harness transfer is **UNVERIFIED; a randomized paired shadow experiment described below would settle whether it improves decision-changing escalations per unit compute without increasing consequential misses**.

This is not a claim that epidemiology has solved agent cost-control. It has solved a narrower and highly relevant design problem: how to allocate scarce definitive measurement without making the unmeasured region invisible.

## 1. What epidemiology has already solved

### 1.1 The core mechanism: two-phase (double) sampling

In epidemiology, a broad screen is often cheap enough to apply to an entire study sample, while a definitive measurement is expensive, invasive, or operationally scarce. Two-phase sampling classifies everyone with the imperfect mechanism and reclassifies a subset with the gold standard. The overlap provides estimates of the screen's sensitivity and specificity ([Lyles et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278465/), especially the definition in the “Two-phase sampling” section).

This is not merely “screen, then confirm every positive.” The sampling fractions for screen-positive and screen-negative groups may differ. Lyles et al. explicitly discuss conditional reclassification with separate rates for initially diseased and initially non-diseased classifications. Their simulations vary prevalence, sensitivity, specificity, sample size, reclassification rate, and the cost ratio between ordinary and gold-standard classification; they report that the benefit changes materially with those quantities ([same paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278465/)). That is precisely the discipline cost-control needs: the fraction escalated is an estimated operating point, not a universal constant.

The critical negative-side sample is easy to omit and is not optional if the system claims to know its miss rate. Epidemiologic diagnostic-method literature calls the resulting failure **partial verification (work-up) bias**: when reference verification depends on an initial positive result, false negatives become systematically unobserved and sensitivity can be inflated. A methodological review describes partial verification as the case where index-positive patients are more likely to receive the gold standard and explains the bias toward increased sensitivity ([Kohn et al., “Recognising bias in studies of diagnostic tests”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6693499/)). A separate analysis defines verification bias as estimating accuracy only from participants receiving the gold standard and studies designs that reference-test fractions of both screen positives and negatives ([de Groot et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3115446/)).

**Field-level conclusion: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: the cited methodological papers define the design and the bias directly, and the mechanism is standard probability sampling rather than an analogy-dependent interpretation. The remaining uncertainty is ordinary scientific uncertainty about performance in any particular population, not about what the design is.

### 1.2 Tiered case definitions separate cheap detection from costly confirmation

Public-health surveillance commonly distinguishes suspected, probable, and confirmed cases. A WHO outbreak manual describes suspected cases as generally based on signs and symptoms, probable cases as adding an epidemiologic link, preliminary result, imaging, or fatal outcome, and confirmed cases as laboratory-confirmed. It also says early investigation definitions should be sensitive enough to capture most cases and may be refined as evidence accumulates ([WHO, *Managing epidemics*, case-definition guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/275657/WHO-WHE-IHM-GIP-2018.2-eng.pdf)). WHO's yellow-fever surveillance definition provides a concrete suspected → probable → confirmed ladder and specifies the additional evidence required at each stage ([WHO yellow-fever case definitions](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/case-definitions-for-public-health-surveillance)).

The point is functional separation. A surveillance definition is not an individual clinical diagnosis. CDC explicitly says surveillance case definitions are uniform criteria for classification and counting and are not intended for clinical diagnosis or individual care ([CDC surveillance case definitions](https://ndc.services.cdc.gov/)). That warning matters to this borrow: an agent “suspected escalation case” is a routing category, not a declaration that the answer is wrong.

**Field-level conclusion: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: WHO and CDC explicitly define both the tiers and their intended scope.

### 1.3 Base rates determine whether a positive screen deserves expensive work

Sensitivity and specificity do not by themselves answer “given a positive screen, how likely is a true case?” CDC's surveillance evaluation guidance states that positive predictive value depends on sensitivity, specificity, and prevalence; it also notes that low positive predictive value produces non-case investigations, false epidemic signals, costly investigation, and wasted resources ([CDC, *Guidelines for Evaluating Surveillance Systems*](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/00001769.htm)).

A numerical illustration shows why this is central to quota allocation. If an escalation screen has 90% sensitivity and 90% specificity but only 1% of low-risk tasks would actually benefit from escalation, then among 10,000 such tasks the expected counts are 90 true positives and 990 false positives, for a positive predictive value of about 8.3%. The arithmetic is illustrative, not an empirical claim about this harness. Uniformly escalating every flagged task would spend most of its budget on cases that do not benefit.

Conversely, a high negative predictive value in a common low-risk stratum says little about rare catastrophic misses. Epidemiology therefore evaluates representativeness and subgroups, not only pooled accuracy. CDC says representative surveillance must characterize occurrence over time and distribution by person and place, and highlights the need to identify systematically excluded subgroups ([CDC surveillance-system guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/00001769.htm)). In a real sentinel-genomic program, Washington State's post-implementation data became more representative and timely overall, yet hospitalized cases remained underrepresented—an instructive example that overall improvement does not erase stratum-specific gaps ([CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases, Washington sentinel surveillance](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/29/2/22-1482_article)).

**Field-level conclusion: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: the base-rate relation follows Bayes' theorem, CDC states the surveillance consequence, and the numerical example is fully specified arithmetic.

### 1.4 Test only in the “decision-changing” zone

Clinical epidemiology adds a sharper cost-control rule. A test has value only if its result can move the preferred action across a decision threshold. The National Academies/NCBI treatment of probabilistic diagnostic testing says that when pretest probability is sufficiently far above or below the treatment threshold, the test result does not alter management; its test and test-treatment thresholds depend on true- and false-positive rates, consequences, and test cost ([NCBI Bookshelf, *The Use of Diagnostic Tests: A Probabilistic Approach*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK235178/)).

Decision-curve analysis makes the same point in outcome terms: compare a prediction-driven strategy with “act on all” and “act on none,” weighting true-positive benefit against false-positive harm at relevant thresholds. A model can have respectable discrimination yet no net benefit at a particular operating threshold ([Vickers et al., BMJ, net-benefit evaluation](https://www.bmj.com/content/352/bmj.i6)).

This is the final bridge from surveillance to cost-control. Do not buy a second answer merely because the first answer is uncertain. Buy it when the additional evidence has a meaningful chance of changing a consequential action.

**Field-level conclusion: very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: the sources explicitly derive decision thresholds and net benefit. Confidence is below “almost certain” only because utilities and acceptable thresholds are contextual rather than discovered solely from data.

## 2. Concrete translation to an agent harness

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: escalation case definitions on the existing dispatch bus

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add one deterministic pre-dispatch/post-draft policy hook that emits an **escalation case class** and a reason code. It does not orchestrate tasks or run a second scheduler.

Suggested ladder:

| Epidemiology | Harness translation | Evidence available cheaply | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not a case | routine | reversible/read-only work; ordinary task stratum; no failing check; output far from a decision boundary | normal route |
| Suspected | escalation candidate | one broad signal: irreversible external effect, high-stakes domain, novel/unseen task stratum, failed check, unresolved source conflict, output at a policy threshold, or budget overrun pattern | targeted additional evidence or place in escalation lottery |
| Probable | likely decision-changing escalation | two mechanically distinct signals, e.g. high consequence plus failing test; source conflict plus missing primary evidence; schema/check failure plus repeated repair | escalate now |
| Confirmed benefit | escalation changed the preferred answer/action or caught a reference-verified defect | paired run and outcome/reference adjudication | update the stratum's benefit estimate; do not treat it as a permanent global rule |

The “confirmed” label is deliberately retrospective. It prevents the routing classifier from declaring its own success.

Candidate features should be observable and compact: operation reversibility, explicit domain/risk flag, tool/test exit state, source count/type, task family, model/route version, marginal token/tool cost, and whether escalation changed the final action. Free-form chain-of-thought is neither needed nor appropriate. Self-reported confidence may be retained as one weak feature, never as the only gate.

**Expected transfer: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: tiered definitions provide a low-cost common vocabulary and deterministic reason codes make selection auditable. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by running the hook in shadow mode and measuring its sensitivity, specificity, predictive value, and calibration against paired adjudicated outcomes by task stratum**.

Why BUILD-SMALL is plausible but not asserted as a measured estimate: the policy requires a feature extraction function, a rules table, a seeded sampler, and an append-only event emitter; it does not require durable workflow state, a model server, or a new database. Exact line count is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a minimal implementation spike against the actual hook API**.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: three-bucket quota allocation, including negative sentinels

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Partition the expensive-compute budget into three declared buckets:

1. **Mandatory protection:** escalation for predeclared high-consequence/irreversible cases. This is a safety policy, not learned thrift.
2. **Targeted verification:** probable cases and suspected cases whose estimated expected loss reduction exceeds marginal compute cost.
3. **Sentinel exploration:** seeded, stratified probability samples of screen-negative and low-score cases, with deliberate coverage of task strata and new model/tool versions.

The sentinel bucket is the epidemiologic heart of the design. If all expensive checks follow the cheap screen, the harness can measure only how often flagged tasks were useful. It cannot estimate misses among unflagged tasks. Every sampled item must log its inclusion probability; otherwise inverse-probability correction and honest population estimates are impossible.

Do **not** hard-code a universal split such as 70/20/10 in the design document. Initial fractions depend on unknown benefit prevalence, reference cost, acceptable miss rate, and stratum volume. Lyles et al.'s simulations demonstrate that the efficient reclassification fraction changes with prevalence, classifier performance, and gold-standard cost ([two-phase cost-effectiveness study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278465/)). Begin with a calibration pilot large enough to put useful uncertainty bounds on each consequential stratum, then revise fractions at declared intervals.

**Expected transfer: very likely (80-90%) for detecting selection-blind spots; likely (55-80%) for reducing total compute at equal outcome quality.** Evidence for the first is the mechanically direct verification-bias result: sampling apparent negatives reveals false negatives that positive-only verification cannot observe. The second depends on the unknown distribution of decision-changing escalations and is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a randomized budget-neutral comparison against the current allocation policy, reporting consequential miss rate and decision-changing escalations per million tokens/tool-cost unit**.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: base-rate table and decision-changing escalation rule

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Maintain a small, versioned table keyed by coarse task stratum, not a learned global router. Each row contains:

- count entering the stratum;
- count sampled for reference verification and their inclusion probabilities;
- estimated prevalence of “escalation changes the correct action”;
- estimated screen sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value with uncertainty intervals;
- mean marginal compute/tool cost;
- observed consequence weight or owner-declared severity class;
- model, policy, and toolchain version window.

The online policy is intentionally simple:

`escalate when estimated expected reduction in decision loss > marginal escalation cost`,

subject to mandatory safety escalation and a sentinel sampling floor.

Operationally, the expected reduction is estimated from paired verified examples in the same coarse stratum, not from the current model's confidence alone. If the interval is too wide or the stratum is new, classify it as data-poor and spend from the sentinel exploration bucket. If both “escalate” and “do not escalate” lead to the same final action in paired audits, reduce targeted spend there even when the second answer is stylistically better.

The primary efficiency metric should be **decision-changing verified improvements per marginal cost**, accompanied by consequential false-negative rate. Raw agreement, judge preference, token count, or average score alone is inadequate: a more polished answer that does not alter the action is not the target outcome.

**Expected transfer: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: diagnostic threshold theory says testing is useful in the intermediate zone where it can alter management, and decision-curve analysis compares net benefit with act-all/act-none defaults. This is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by preregistering consequence weights and thresholds, replaying a held-out paired sample, and then running an online randomized trial in the borderline zone**.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: drift reset and stratum sentinels

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Treat model changes, tool changes, policy changes, and materially new task families like changes in pathogen/test/population: they invalidate simple pooling. On a version boundary:

- retain historical rows for comparison but do not silently pool them;
- restore a higher sentinel sampling fraction for the changed strata;
- require minimum recent verified coverage before returning to exploitation-heavy allocation;
- report pooled and stratum-specific results side by side; and
- include a “no recent evidence” state rather than carrying forward stale precision.

This is not a claim that task behavior follows infectious-disease dynamics. It borrows only surveillance's insistence that test performance and prevalence are population- and time-dependent.

**Expected transfer: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: CDC surveillance guidance treats flexibility, representativeness, and timeliness as separate system attributes, while the Washington sentinel study found residual subgroup underrepresentation despite overall improvement. The appropriate reset magnitude and recertification window are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with change-point backtests across actual model/tool version transitions and prospective monitoring of post-change miss-rate intervals**.

## 3. What this could catch that a cost-only dashboard misses

Because I did not inspect the current harness, each item below is a **catch set**, not a claim of current absence.

1. **Silent false negatives from confidence-gated escalation.** A task looks routine to the cheap screen but an expensive paired run changes a consequential action. Negative sentinels make this observable.
2. **Base-rate traps.** A flag appears accurate in a curated/high-risk sample but has poor predictive value in the much larger low-risk task stream, consuming quota on false positives.
3. **Verification bias in “escalation success” metrics.** If only escalated cases receive strong tests or owner review, the apparent router sensitivity is inflated because unflagged failures have no reference label.
4. **Spend without action change.** Extra research, a larger model, or a critic improves prose or confidence but leaves the supported decision unchanged. Paired decision-impact logging distinguishes that from real benefit.
5. **Subgroup masking.** Aggregate savings look good while rare task strata—high-stakes edits, unfamiliar ecosystems, new tools, or low-volume languages—carry most consequential misses.
6. **Version drift.** A cost rule calibrated on one model/tool version continues after its sensitivity or base rate changes.
7. **Correlated verification.** The same model family, context, and prompt produces both answer and “independent” judgment, so agreement is mistaken for correctness. Reference-type reason codes expose how much evidence is actually mechanically independent.
8. **Exploration starvation.** The allocator becomes confident because it stopped buying labels in regions it thinks are safe, not because those regions remained safe.

**Detection confidence: very likely (80-90%) for making items 1-6 measurable if paired reference outcomes exist; likely (55-80%) for items 7-8.** Evidence: items 1-3 are direct analogues of false negatives, prevalence-dependent predictive value, and partial verification bias; items 4-6 follow from decision-threshold and stratified/timely-surveillance principles. Items 7-8 need a credible independent reference and adequate exploratory sample. Whether these failure modes materially occur in this harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by analyzing a probability sample of both escalated and non-escalated historical/prospective tasks with blinded reference adjudication**.

## 4. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 4.1 There is usually no gold standard for an agent answer

Disease studies can sometimes define a reference laboratory result or later clinical outcome. Many agent outputs are open-ended, multi-objective, and preference-dependent. A stronger model or majority vote is not truth. If the “gold standard” shares the same model, prompt, retrieved evidence, and blind spot, conditional errors are correlated and the apparent sensitivity/specificity can be badly misleading.

**Disanalogy strength: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: the harness target includes design, explanation, and code tasks whose correctness cannot universally be reduced to one laboratory-like binary state; diagnostic methodology itself warns that an imperfect or selectively applied reference introduces bias ([de Groot et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3115446/)).

Mitigation is to define reference outcomes narrowly: executable tests for behavioral claims, primary-source entailment checks for factual claims, reversible sandbox trials for operational claims, and blinded owner/expert adjudication for preference-laden decisions. Report “unadjudicable” rather than force a binary label. This mitigation is **likely (55-80%) to make calibration useful for well-specified code/retrieval tasks**, based on their externally checkable outcomes, but **UNVERIFIED; settle it by measuring inter-reference agreement and later owner-correction rates per task stratum**.

### 4.2 Escalation is an intervention, not merely a test

A lab test usually observes a latent condition. Extra agent compute can rewrite the answer, call tools, gather evidence, and alter downstream state. Therefore sensitivity/specificity of the escalation screen is not enough; the estimand must be the **causal incremental benefit of escalation** relative to the normal route.

**Disanalogy strength: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: this follows from the defined target action—spending additional compute changes the produced artifact—whereas the cited two-phase design reclassifies an existing state. The concrete consequence is that paired or randomized comparisons are required; observational “escalated tasks did better” data are confounded by routing difficulty.

### 4.3 Tasks do not transmit like infections

There is no justified analogue of an infection reproduction number, spatial contagion, incubation period, or compartmental disease dynamics for independent user tasks. Errors can share a root cause and propagate through copied artifacts, but that is dependency propagation, not biological transmission.

**Disanalogy strength: very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: task streams lack the contact-network and transmission process assumed by epidemic models. Therefore this unit rejects outbreak-growth models as quota allocators unless a specific artifact-dependency graph is independently demonstrated.

### 4.4 Prevalence is endogenous and drifts quickly

The prevalence of “would benefit from escalation” changes when the base model improves, prompts change, tools recover, owners change task mix, or the allocator itself changes which tasks reach later stages. Historical prevalence is not a fixed natural constant.

**Disanalogy strength: very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: model/policy versions are direct interventions on the event definition and routing population. This supports short versioned windows and sentinel renewal, not a permanent global PPV.

### 4.5 Rare catastrophic errors defeat ordinary calibration

A modest sentinel sample cannot bound an extremely rare but severe miss tightly. Zero observed events is not proof of zero risk. Mandatory rules for irreversible, security-sensitive, legal, medical, financial, privacy, or external-impact actions must remain outside a purely learned cost optimizer.

**Disanalogy/limitation strength: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: binomial sampling cannot tightly estimate event probabilities far below the reciprocal of the verified sample size; consequence asymmetry also makes average net benefit insufficient.

### 4.6 The final transfer boundary

The borrow is valid for **sampling design, base-rate correction, explicit case definitions, subgroup surveillance, and decision-threshold accounting**. It is invalid as a claim that answer quality is a disease, that a model judge is a lab test, or that epidemic-dynamics machinery should schedule agents.

## 5. Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

### Rejected — adaptive multi-armed-bandit allocation

Recent epidemiologic/operations research proposes nonstationary multi-armed-bandit allocation of scarce infectious-disease tests across communities, balancing high estimated risk with uncertain risk ([Zan et al., *IISE Transactions*, DOI 10.1080/24725854.2023.2266488](https://doi.org/10.1080/24725854.2023.2266488)). This is relevant frontier evidence, but not the recommended harness design.

**Rejection confidence: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: the published method adds a physics-informed transmission model and bandit regret machinery; this harness lens lacks validated transmission dynamics, reward stationarity, and ground-truth feedback. It risks becoming a second orchestration runtime and violates the brief's anti-overfit preference. A simple stratified sampler retains exploration and produces auditable inclusion probabilities.

### Rejected — verify only tasks the model says are uncertain

This is cheap but makes the index test determine reference verification, reproducing partial verification bias. Model uncertainty and model error may also share a cause, so confident errors become invisible.

**Rejection confidence: very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: positive-only work-up bias is established in diagnostic-method studies, and same-model confidence is not mechanically independent of the answer.

### Rejected — uniformly run the strongest route

This protects against routing misses but does not control cost and spends on tasks outside the decision-changing zone. It is still an essential experimental comparator (“escalate all”), not the default architecture.

**Rejection confidence: likely (55-80%) as an operating policy.** Evidence: diagnostic threshold theory explicitly identifies probability regions where testing cannot change management. Applicability to the harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with paired runs measuring the fraction of escalations that change a reference-supported action**.

### Rejected — sentinel sampling alone

Sentinels estimate population behavior but do not protect a specific high-consequence task. Mandatory risk rules and targeted verification remain necessary.

**Rejection confidence: very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: surveillance and individual clinical action have different purposes; CDC explicitly warns that surveillance definitions are not individual diagnostic criteria.

### Rejected — maximize sensitivity, specificity, or AUC without utilities

Those metrics describe classification, not whether spending compute is worthwhile. They omit prevalence, asymmetric consequences, and marginal cost. Decision-curve/net-benefit analysis exists precisely because discrimination alone may not imply useful action ([BMJ net-benefit review](https://www.bmj.com/content/352/bmj.i6)).

**Rejection confidence: almost certain (90-99%).** Evidence: the target is cost-control under consequence tradeoffs, which cannot be identified from unweighted accuracy alone.

### Architecture gate result

All retained proposals ride an existing dispatch/hook/config event bus. None requires a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. State is a small versioned table plus append-only sampled outcomes. The design therefore passes the stated anti-overfit gate **provided the actual harness exposes the assumed hook/event points**. That premise is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an API/config inspection by a local-grounding unit**.

## 6. Experiment that would settle the transfer

### Experiment — UNVERIFIED: randomized paired shadow calibration

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

The experiment must estimate incremental benefit, not merely judge difficult tasks.

#### Phase 0: preregister the estimand

Define per task stratum:

- normal route and escalation route;
- “decision-changing verified improvement”;
- consequential miss classes and weights;
- reference methods allowed and their independence grade;
- marginal token, latency, paid API, and scarce-quota cost;
- maximum acceptable consequential false-negative bound;
- task/version strata and planned sampling probabilities.

Do not tune these definitions after seeing which policy wins without labeling the analysis exploratory.

#### Phase 1: shadow screening

Run the cheap escalation definition on all eligible tasks without changing delivery. Store only compact features, reason codes, stratum, version, score/class, and seeded sample decision. Prove detector validity with a known-present synthetic suspected case and known-present routine case before interpreting zero counts.

#### Phase 2: stratified paired verification

Within each stratum:

- sample a high fraction of probable cases;
- sample a lower but nonzero fraction of routine/screen-negative cases;
- oversample new, low-volume, and high-consequence strata while retaining inclusion probabilities;
- create normal and escalated outputs without letting adjudicators know the route;
- use at least two mechanically independent outcome signals before declaring a consequential defect absent or an escalation beneficial (for example, executable acceptance tests plus blinded owner/expert adjudication; or primary-source entailment plus a later real-world correction signal).

Randomize the route in a safe borderline region if paired execution itself creates order/context effects. Never randomize away mandatory safeguards or permit experimental outputs to cause irreversible external action without normal authorization.

#### Phase 3: estimation

Report, with uncertainty intervals and by stratum/version:

- prevalence of decision-changing escalation benefit;
- screen sensitivity/specificity and PPV/NPV;
- consequential false-negative rate;
- incremental verified improvements per million marginal tokens and per paid-tool unit;
- latency cost;
- proportion of escalations that change only style, not action;
- reference disagreement/unadjudicable rate;
- weighted net benefit relative to escalate-all, escalate-none, and the existing policy;
- results both raw and corrected for unequal sampling probabilities.

Do not claim success from aggregate token savings alone. A policy passes only if it reduces cost at a preregistered non-inferior consequential outcome level, or improves outcomes at an acceptable incremental cost, and neither signal is contradicted by a consequential stratum.

#### Phase 4: prospective canary

Deploy only to reversible, low-consequence traffic first; retain mandatory escalation and sentinel sampling. Freeze thresholds for the canary window. Roll back on a preregistered miss boundary, reference disagreement spike, or cost regression. Version every result.

**Experiment adequacy: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: randomization addresses the intervention/selection problem, negative sampling addresses verification bias, stratification addresses base-rate heterogeneity, and decision-impact metrics address the target objective. It remains **UNVERIFIED; the experiment itself is the settling test, and its power/sample duration must be chosen from pilot event rates rather than invented here**.

## 7. Falsification criteria

Abandon or materially revise this borrow if any of the following occurs:

1. Reference outcomes are unadjudicable or mechanically dependent for most consequential task strata.
2. Cheap case definitions have no stable discrimination beyond mandatory risk rules across version windows.
3. Decision-changing escalation benefit is so common that escalate-all dominates at acceptable cost.
4. Benefit is so rare and reference verification so costly that calibration consumes more quota than it can plausibly save.
5. Sentinel sampling finds consequential misses without discoverable precursors at a rate that forces near-uniform escalation.
6. Stratum sizes are too small for useful bounds and cannot be responsibly pooled.
7. The policy creates gaming or feedback effects—such as terse outputs avoiding escalation—that outweigh savings.

**Falsifiability confidence: very likely (80-90%).** Evidence: each criterion corresponds to a required structural assumption—reference validity, screen discrimination, heterogeneous marginal value, affordable measurement, manageable residual risk, estimable base rates, and policy stability. If those assumptions fail, the epidemiologic mechanism does not solve the target problem.

## 8. Minimal recommended decision

Proceed only with a shadow pilot of **two-phase stratified verification plus negative sentinels**. Do not begin with a learned router, bandit, daemon, or global “uncertainty score.” The pilot's first deliverable is a versioned table showing where escalation actually changed a supported action and what was missed among screen negatives.

**Recommendation confidence: likely (55-80%).** Evidence: the proposed pilot directly tests the transfer's disputed assumptions while using the established controls for base rates and verification bias. It is reversible and small. The net saving and safety result are **UNVERIFIED; the randomized paired shadow experiment above is required before production routing**.

If that experiment cannot produce an acceptably independent reference outcome, stop. In that case the analogy does **not** transfer far enough to justify adaptive compute allocation; retain explicit high-consequence rules and measure cost descriptively rather than presenting a self-scored optimizer as epidemiologically calibrated.
