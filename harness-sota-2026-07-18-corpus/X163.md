# X163 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → agent memory

**Unit:** X163  
**Lens:** military doctrine (commander’s intent, OODA, after-action review, red teaming) applied to cross-session agent memory  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external research and design only. Per the task, this unit did not inspect or make claims about the local harness repository.  
**Bottom line:** the analogy transfers, but not as “give the agent a military staff” and not as a reason to retain more history. The useful borrow is a small epistemic control loop: preserve a short intent, maintain a decision-linked running estimate, label assumptions and their invalidators, admit AAR outputs only as candidates, and promote a candidate to an active lesson only after validation and observed improvement. OODA supplies a reason to audit this loop because stored orientation changes what the agent notices; it does not supply the storage design.

## Executive judgment

Military doctrine has not solved machine memory. It has, however, developed operational answers to a structurally similar problem: a distributed decision-maker must act from incomplete information; some guidance must endure; situational facts decay; assumptions can silently become false; observations are not yet lessons; and scarce attention must be directed toward information that changes a decision.

The closest doctrinal mechanism is therefore not a single named technique. It is a **four-part control system**:

1. **Commander’s intent** separates enduring purpose and end-state conditions from the disposable plan.
2. **Running estimates plus commander’s critical information requirements (CCIRs)** continually refresh facts, assumptions, conclusions, and questions tied to anticipated decisions.
3. **After-action review (AAR) plus the Army Lessons Learned Program (ALLP)** separates what was intended, what occurred, what explains the variance, what will change, and whether behavior/performance actually changed.
4. **Red-team key-assumptions checking** makes hidden premises explicit and records what would eliminate them.

This composite is a **likely (55-80%)** fit for an agent harness’s memory-control problem because both domains involve action under partial observability, changing conditions, costly stale beliefs, and bounded attention; the evidence is the Army’s explicit use of continuous estimates, revalidation of facts and assumptions, decision-specific information requirements, and validated lessons. Its effectiveness in this owner’s harness is **UNVERIFIED; the “Shadow Memory Ledger Experiment” specified below would settle it**. The confidence is not higher because military staff processes depend on trained humans, hierarchy, doctrine, and shared context, while an LLM may misread labels, fabricate provenance, or overweight any text placed in context.

The radical design implication is: **replace undifferentiated durable memory with a small, typed, evidence-bearing ledger whose active contents are regenerated from current intent and current decisions.** Archive is not memory; retrieval is not truth; an observation is not a lesson; and age is only a review cue, not proof of staleness.

## Research method and evidence standard

This is an ideation unit, so I used public military doctrine and official military publications rather than the local subject tree. Primary or institutional sources dominate:

- U.S. Army ADP/ADRP doctrine for commander’s intent, running estimates, assessment, and CCIRs.
- U.S. Army FM 7-0 Appendix K for AAR procedure.
- Center for Army Lessons Learned material for the distinction between captured observations and learned changes.
- The U.S. Army Red Team Handbook for explicit assumption challenge.
- U.S. Air University material for Boyd’s OODA model and, especially, orientation.

The linked `rdl.train.army.mil`, `first.army.mil`, `api.army.mil`, `army.mil`, and `airuniversity.af.edu` items are official U.S. military hosts. Some Army doctrine links expose older editions alongside newer doctrine. I use the older ADRP 5-0 where it gives unusually explicit assessment language, and cross-check its running-estimate formulation against the 2019 ADP 5-0. I do not infer that an older edition is current merely because it remains hosted.

## 1. Commander’s intent: preserve purpose, not the plan

### How it works in its own field

Army doctrine defines commander’s intent as a concise expression of purpose and desired military end state that lets subordinate commanders pursue the desired result without further orders even when events do not unfold as planned. It may include purpose, key tasks, and end-state conditions; it is meant to be brief, and subordinate intent nests within higher intent. See U.S. Army [ADP 6-0, Mission Command, paragraphs 1-45 through 1-49](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/1FE33715-CFD1-4614-A489-B3E0480C3F80-1428688882108/adp6_0.pdf).

The mechanism is not motivational prose. It is a compression boundary:

- The **purpose** explains why the operation matters in relation to the higher purpose.
- **Key tasks** name activities whose accomplishment is important to the whole force, without prescribing every subordinate action.
- The **end state** describes conditions constituting success.
- The plan can change when circumstances invalidate it, while action stays inside the intent’s limits.

This matters for memory because it separates what is intended to endure from procedures that were only locally useful.

### Translation to agent memory

An agent needs a very small **memory intent** above its session summaries:

```yaml
memory_intent:
  purpose: "What durable memory is for in this workspace"
  key_constraints:
    - "Owner corrections outrank inferred preferences"
    - "Claims about changing external state require refresh"
    - "A workaround is scoped to its observed conditions"
  desired_end_state:
    - "The next session can act correctly without replaying history"
    - "Every active mutable belief can be challenged or refreshed"
  exclusions:
    - "No secrets unless explicitly authorized"
    - "No transient plans, logs, or unvalidated postmortem guesses"
```

This is a relevance constitution, not a summary of everything the owner has done. A candidate memory must state which purpose, constraint, or future decision it supports. If it supports none, it remains in the archive or is discarded according to retention policy; it does not enter active memory.

The transfer catches a common class of error: preserving the old *means* after the owner’s *end* or constraints have changed. Examples include remembering a one-session workaround as a permanent rule, remembering a superseded task plan as an enduring objective, or optimizing a local metric while violating the owner’s higher-level intent.

### Adversarial assessment

The analogy transfers only partially.

- A military commander is an accountable human authority. An agent’s “intent” may be inferred from conversation and can therefore be wrong. The harness must never manufacture intent from repeated behavior and silently elevate it to owner policy.
- Military intent assumes competent subordinates and shared understanding. An LLM can treat concise language as vague or overgeneralize it.
- Operations have recognizable end states; open-ended personal workflows often do not.
- “Key tasks” in an operation are not synonymous with durable preferences. A task can expire while a preference persists, or vice versa.

Therefore the transferable invariant is **purpose/end-state/constraint separation**, not command hierarchy or martial vocabulary.

### UNVERIFIED Proposal 1 — owner-approved memory intent

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add one short, owner-readable memory-intent document with explicit scope and exclusions. Changes to this document require direct owner instruction or an explicit confirmation; an agent may propose but not self-ratify changes.

This proposal would improve retrieval precision **likely (55-80%)**, based on commander’s intent’s proven role as compact guidance under plan failure and on the simple mechanical reduction in eligible memories. The agent-harness effect is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a 50-task blinded replay comparing irrelevant-memory injection, owner corrections, and task success with and without the intent gate**.

The proposal passes the anti-overfit gate: it is configuration on the existing instruction/memory bus, adds no orchestration runtime, database, critic model, observability service, or guardrail framework.

## 2. Running estimates and CCIRs: memory is a maintained assessment, not a pile

### How running estimates work in their own field

Army operations doctrine calls a running estimate a continuous assessment of the current situation used to judge whether an operation is proceeding according to intent and whether future operations are supportable. Staff sections continuously update facts, assumptions, friendly and enemy status, civil considerations, conclusions, and recommendations. See [ADP 5-0, The Operations Process, paragraphs 1-54 through 1-57](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/E4166A5D-0FE7-4780-916A-A7E9B227147C-1337689957702/adp5_0.pdf). The earlier ADRP states the failure mode directly: inaccurate running estimates can yield omissions, flawed plans, or bad execution decisions; see [ADRP 5-0, paragraphs 1-69 through 1-72](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/ARI-MSCP/pdfs/adrp5_0.pdf).

Assessment doctrine adds the crucial freshness rule. Monitoring compares the current situation with the forecast situation, and during preparation/execution staff determine whether facts remain relevant, assumptions remain valid, and new conditions have emerged. See [ADRP 5-0, chapter 5, paragraphs 5-1 through 5-5](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/ARI-MSCP/pdfs/adrp5_0.pdf).

The mechanism is active maintenance, not passive recency sorting:

- facts and assumptions are distinct;
- the estimate is scoped by a staff function;
- new observations update conclusions and recommendations;
- assessment is against intent and forecast outcomes;
- the estimate anticipates decisions rather than attempting to preserve all available information.

### How CCIRs work in their own field

CCIRs further limit attention. Army planning doctrine defines a CCIR as information the commander identifies as critical to timely decision-making. A CCIR is operation-specific, commander-specific, situation-dependent, directly linked to a current or future mission, and time-sensitive. Commanders continually review it as conditions and decisions change; doctrine notes that keeping the set small helps allocate limited resources. See [ATP 5-0.2-1, paragraphs 2-75 through 2-78](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/C3369D5F-389D-4304-8ACA-8F8DAB5D4795-1607437986985/atp5_0x2_1.pdf). A separate training circular likewise defines CCIR as information critical to timely decisions and says staff refine it throughout operations; see [TC 6-0.2, “Recommend Commander’s Critical Information Requirements”](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC%2FA711F133-D3F1-4B5D-B0C6-94D722B58291-1563301440627/tc6_0x2.pdf).

The deep idea is **decision pull rather than collection push**. A datum becomes high-priority because a pending decision depends on it, not merely because it is new, frequently mentioned, or similar to the present prompt.

### Translation to agent memory

Replace the undifferentiated “memory list” with a **Memory Running Estimate (MRE)**. Each active record is typed and carries its own challenge conditions:

```yaml
id: mem-2026-000123
statement: "Tool X requires authentication method Y in environment Z"
kind: fact                # intent | fact | assumption | candidate_lesson | validated_lesson
scope:
  workspace: "example"
  environment: "Windows / PowerShell 7"
  task_class: "deployment"
supports_decision:
  - "whether to invoke Tool X"
source:
  - locator: "session/event/reference"
    observed_at: "2026-07-19T00:00:00Z"
confidence: "very likely (80-90%)"
invalidators:
  - "Tool X version changes"
  - "authentication configuration changes"
recheck_on:
  - "first Tool X use in a new environment"
last_validated_at: "2026-07-19T00:00:00Z"
status: active            # active | challenged | superseded | retired
supersedes: []
```

The active view is produced by deterministic filters before semantic retrieval:

1. Select the current intent and pending decision(s).
2. Restrict candidates by explicit scope.
3. Exclude `challenged`, `superseded`, and `retired` records.
4. Exclude `candidate_lesson` from instruction context; it may appear only in a clearly marked evidence panel.
5. Surface assumptions with their invalidators and confidence, never as facts.
6. If a recheck event fires, refresh or quarantine before use.
7. Retrieve semantically only within that eligible set.

The harness should maintain a tiny set of **Memory Critical Information Requirements (MCIRs)** for each consequential task. Examples:

- “Has the owner changed the governing objective or constraint?”
- “Is the remembered interface/version/environment still the one in use?”
- “Does this lesson’s scope match the current task?”
- “Is there a newer direct correction that supersedes this inferred preference?”
- “What observation would make the selected workaround wrong?”

These are not global checklists. Like CCIRs, they should be linked to a decision, time-sensitive, few in number, and retired once answered.

### How staleness is noticed

Staleness should be event-driven first and time-driven second.

**Strong staleness signals:**

- direct contradiction by a newer owner statement;
- a declared invalidator occurs (version, environment, branch, policy, objective, dependency, or authority change);
- an authoritative source now reports a different value;
- observed outcome falls outside the memory’s stated prediction or scope;
- two sources that were expected to agree diverge.

**Weak staleness signals:**

- age;
- long non-use;
- low retrieval frequency;
- lack of recent confirmation.

A weak signal should schedule review, not declare falsity. This is an important disanalogy: military operational facts often decay quickly, whereas an owner’s explicit stable preference can remain true for years. A universal TTL would forget durable intent and preserve recently repeated nonsense.

When challenged, a record should leave active retrieval immediately but retain provenance long enough for adjudication where retention policy permits. Supersession should be an edge, not an overwrite: `new_record supersedes old_record`, with the old record excluded from active context. That preserves why behavior changed without asking the model to reconcile two live instructions.

### What it would catch

The MRE/MCIR design is aimed at catching:

- a remembered capability or failure mode after its version/configuration changes;
- a preference inferred from behavior after a direct owner correction;
- environment-specific advice applied to another shell, OS, repository, or tool version;
- contradictory memories both retrieved because vector similarity cannot express authority or supersession;
- an assumption written as a fact and never revisited;
- a correct old fact that is irrelevant to the current decision;
- a recent but unvalidated anecdote outranking an older validated lesson;
- an archive summary treated as operational guidance merely because it was retrieved.

These are target cases, not claims that each presently occurs in the subject harness; this unit intentionally did not inspect the harness.

### Adversarial assessment

Running estimates transfer better than most military analogies because their essential structure is epistemic rather than kinetic. Still, important differences remain:

- A staff has domain owners who can maintain separate estimates. A single agent has correlated failure modes: the same model may extract, challenge, and retrieve the belief.
- Military reporting has designated sensors and reporting chains. Agent observations often come from untrusted web text, tool errors, or model-generated summaries.
- “Current situation” can often be externally observed in operations. Preferences and intentions may require the owner’s interpretation.
- The cost of maintaining every possible invalidator can exceed the value of the memory.
- An attacker can plant text designed to look like a fact, invalidator, or owner instruction; provenance and authority must be enforced outside the model.

The right transfer is a typed record and deterministic eligibility gate. The wrong transfer would be a simulated general staff of many agents producing more prose.

### UNVERIFIED Proposal 2 — Memory Running Estimate ledger and MCIR gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Implement an append-only JSONL or small-file ledger plus a pre-context hook that deterministically selects active records by status, scope, authority, and decision linkage before the existing retrieval mechanism runs. Use a post-tool/post-session hook to mark declared invalidators and queue refresh. No new daemon is required.

The deterministic gate would reduce injection of known-superseded or scope-mismatched memory **very likely (80-90%)**, because status/scope filtering directly excludes those records; evidence is the doctrinal separation of facts/assumptions and decision-specific CCIRs plus the mechanical semantics of the filter. Reduction of total owner corrections is only **likely (55-80%)** because many corrections are unrelated to memory. Both harness outcomes are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with the Shadow Memory Ledger Experiment and a seeded stale-memory benchmark described below**.

The anti-overfit gate passes: this is a thin adapter on the existing memory/context and hook buses. It explicitly rejects a vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, second orchestration runtime, trained critic, observability SaaS, and general guardrail framework. Semantic retrieval may remain where it already exists; the proposal narrows its eligible corpus.

## 3. AAR and ALLP: an observation is not yet a learned memory

### How AAR works in its own field

FM 7-0 defines an AAR as guided analysis during or after training/operations to improve future performance. It is immediate, direct, standards-based, participatory, and centered on self-discovery rather than blame. Its core agenda is:

1. What was supposed to happen?
2. What did happen?
3. What was right or wrong about what happened?
4. How should the task be performed to standard next time?

The doctrine calls for multiple perspectives, including opposing-force perspectives, accurate time-sequenced observations, assignment of responsibility for changes, and integration of results into later training. See [FM 7-0 Appendix K, especially K-1 through K-10 and K-22 through K-44](https://www.first.army.mil/Portals/102/FM%207-0%20Appendix%20K.pdf). The 2025 Army description of TC 7-0.1 reinforces that follow-up may include immediate retraining, SOP revision, and integration/sharing of lessons; see the Army Training Management Directorate’s [“TMD publishes training circular to augment FM and ADP 7-0”](https://www.army.mil/article/283214/tmd_publishes_training_circular_to_augment_fm_and_adp_7_0).

The Army lessons-learned mechanism goes further than merely writing an AAR. The CALL Services Handbook says units discover, validate, integrate, and assess lessons; recommended changes are monitored through implementation; and a “lesson learned” requires changed performance or observed changed behavior. See the [CALL Services Handbook, chapter 3](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2023/01/19/ab6f3bd5/15-11-call-services-handbook-jun-15-public.pdf). Army reporting on comprehensive lessons likewise states that accepted lessons must be fact-based and that active solicitation and validation reduce the risk of learning the wrong lesson; see [“Comprehensive Lessons Learned”](https://www.army.mil/article/28869/comprehensive_lessons_learned).

This yields a useful maturity ladder:

```text
event evidence → observation → candidate lesson → validated lesson
                                      ↓
                         change implemented and assessed
```

The pivotal doctrinal insight is that **capture is not learning**. A neatly written postmortem can still encode the wrong causal story.

### Translation to agent memory

At task/session end, capture only a structured **candidate lesson card**:

```yaml
expected:
  intent: "What outcome/constraint governed the attempt"
  standard: "How success was to be judged"
observed:
  timeline_refs: ["tool/event references"]
  outcome: "What actually happened"
variance:
  - "Specific difference between expected and observed"
explanation:
  hypothesis: "Why the variance occurred"
  confidence: "likely (55-80%)"
  alternatives:
    - "Competing explanation"
proposed_change:
  action: "What to do differently"
  scope: "Where it applies"
  owner: "Who/what validates it"
validation:
  test: "Future task or replay that can discriminate explanations"
  success_measure: "Observable change in result or behavior"
status: candidate_lesson
```

Promotion rules:

- A single failure can create a candidate, not a universal rule.
- A direct owner correction can immediately become authoritative intent/preference within its explicit scope, but any inferred causal explanation remains a candidate.
- A tool/interface fact can become active after authoritative confirmation; a workaround still needs scope and an invalidator.
- A candidate becomes a validated lesson only after the proposed change is applied and the predicted improvement is observed, or after independent authoritative evidence establishes it.
- If results are mixed, narrow scope or retain competing hypotheses; do not average them into vague advice.
- A validated lesson can later be challenged and superseded. “Validated” is not “eternal.”

The AAR should be triggered by **learning value**, not every session. Good triggers include owner correction, repeated tool failure, rollback, surprising success, large plan/actual divergence, or a consequential decision made from uncertain information. This avoids turning memory into a diary.

### What it would catch

- the agent “learning” from a coincidental success;
- a retry that succeeded for an unknown reason becoming a permanent recipe;
- a postmortem explanation unsupported by the event timeline;
- repeated capture of the same complaint without an implemented change;
- a lesson marked done because text was written, even though behavior did not change;
- blame-oriented summaries that name an actor but do not identify a correctable condition;
- lessons generalized across environments without validation.

### Adversarial assessment

AAR transfers because both domains compare intent, action, evidence, and future performance. It fails if automated into self-congratulatory model prose.

The most important disanalogy is independence. Military AARs can include participants, observers, evaluators, and OPFOR with genuinely different access and incentives. Multiple completions from the same model are not mechanically independent signals. Therefore an agent AAR should privilege tool traces, tests, owner corrections, authoritative sources, and later task outcomes over a second model-generated narrative.

Another disanalogy is consequence and repetition. Military training deliberately repeats standardized tasks. Many agent tasks are unique, so behavioral validation may be delayed or impossible. Such a candidate must remain explicitly unvalidated rather than being promoted for convenience.

### UNVERIFIED Proposal 3 — candidate-to-lesson promotion hook

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a post-task skill/hook that creates candidate cards only on high-value triggers, links claims to event references, and requires a later success measure before promotion. The hook should never write directly into authoritative instruction memory.

This would reduce false lesson promotion **very likely (80-90%)** on records governed by the promotion rule, because candidates are mechanically barred from authoritative retrieval; the evidence is FM 7-0’s expected/actual/standard/change structure and CALL’s requirement for implementation plus observed change. Whether it improves end-task quality is **UNVERIFIED and likely (55-80%); settle it by seeding 30 one-off successes/failures with confounded causes and measuring incorrect generalization over subsequent matched and mismatched tasks**.

The anti-overfit gate passes. It is a small lifecycle/state rule on the existing hook and memory buses, not a trained critic or durable workflow engine.

## 4. Red teaming: every consequential assumption needs a defeat condition

### How it works in its own field

The Army Red Team Handbook describes a Key Assumptions Check as an exercise that exposes stated and unstated premises, clarifies the logic depending on them, and identifies changes that would eliminate an assumption. It is useful early but can be applied before final judgments; its value is exposing faulty logic, surprising circumstances, and obscure relationships. See the U.S. Army TRADOC G-2 [Red Team Handbook, “Key Assumptions Check,” page 163](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/arimcpii/downloads/Red_Team_Handbook_v9.pdf).

The same handbook’s Assumption Sensitivity Analysis anonymously elicits confidence estimates so attention can be directed to low-confidence/high-danger premises rather than treating all assumptions equally. See the [Red Team Handbook, “Assumption Sensitivity Analysis,” page 105](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/arimcpii/downloads/Red_Team_Handbook_v9.pdf).

The mechanism is not generic skepticism. It requires the analytic line, its premises, confidence, dependency, and observable conditions that would defeat a premise.

### Translation to agent memory

Every consequential `assumption` and every causal `candidate_lesson` gets:

- the decision it supports;
- evidence for and against;
- a confidence band;
- consequences if false;
- one or more **invalidators/signposts** stated as observable events;
- the cheapest discriminating check;
- a review owner or trigger.

Before a high-impact remembered assumption drives action, a pre-action hook asks only:

1. Did a declared invalidator occur?
2. Has the scope changed?
3. Is there newer contradictory evidence from a higher-authority source?
4. Is the cost of checking lower than the cost of being wrong?

If yes, the record is challenged and refreshed. If no, execution proceeds. This is intentionally narrower than asking an LLM to “red team everything,” which would add latency and generate speculative objections.

For example, “the MCP is unavailable” is not stored as an eternal fact. It is an observation or scoped fact with invalidators such as “server configuration changes,” “connection health check succeeds,” or “a different environment is active.” “This owner prefers concise answers” is scoped to the evidence and challenged by a newer explicit instruction requesting exhaustive documentation.

### Adversarial assessment

This transfers because stale memory is often a once-reasonable premise whose supporting conditions changed. It also addresses confirmation bias: retrieval itself makes a belief salient, so a separate invalidator field counters the tendency to search only for confirming evidence.

But red teaming can become pathological:

- unlimited challenge creates analysis paralysis;
- model-generated counterarguments are correlated with the original model’s framing;
- an attacker can manufacture apparent contradiction;
- low-confidence trivia does not deserve review cost;
- an invalidator can itself become stale.

Therefore challenges must be decision-linked and risk-weighted. The harness should spend checks where `probability belief is wrong × consequence × action irreversibility` justifies them, using a coarse rule rather than false numerical precision.

### UNVERIFIED Proposal 4 — invalidator-first freshness check

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend the MRE record with invalidators and recheck triggers; evaluate deterministic triggers first and invoke a model or external source only when adjudication is necessary.

This would detect declared condition changes **very likely (80-90%)** when event metadata exposes those changes, because matching is mechanical. Detection of semantic contradictions in free text is only **roughly even (45-55%)**, because wording, scope, authority, and negation are hard; this estimate is based on the structural difficulty rather than a local benchmark. Both are **UNVERIFIED; settle them with a mutation test that changes owner intent, OS/shell, dependency version, branch, and tool health while measuring trigger recall, false challenges, and wrong-memory use**.

The anti-overfit gate passes. This extends the same ledger/hook; it does not introduce a critic service or guardrail framework.

## 5. OODA: useful warning, weak storage blueprint

### How it works in its own field

Boyd’s OODA cycle is observe, orient, decide, act, with feedback from action and unfolding interaction. Air University’s corrected rendering of Boyd’s diagram shows observation, orientation (analysis/synthesis, previous experience, culture/tradition, new information), decision as hypothesis, action as test, and feedback; see [Air University Press’s corrected OODA diagram for *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/Boyd_Errata_page384.pdf).

The important point for memory is orientation. Air University summarizes Boyd’s view that orientation is shaped by prior experience and unfolding circumstances and in turn shapes observation, decision, and action; see [“OODA Loop makes its mark on Maxwell”](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/News/Display/Article/420819/ooda-loop-makes-its-mark-on-maxwell/). Air University teaching material also frames deciding/acting as tests of assumptions and says the consequences of prior actions feed subsequent observation; see [“Cultural KSAs: Skill Development Using the OODA Loop”](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/AFCLC/News/Article-Display/Article/1777083/cultural-ksas-skill-development-using-the-ooda-loop/).

### Translation—and limit

Cross-session memory is part of an agent’s orientation: it affects what evidence appears relevant, which tools seem available, what risks are anticipated, and which action is chosen. A stale memory can therefore produce a closed loop:

```text
stale orientation
  → selective observation/retrieval
  → decision consistent with the stale belief
  → action interpreted through that belief
  → summary that reinforces the stale orientation
```

The useful design constraint is to feed **action outcomes back into the status of the memories that materially influenced the action**. When a decision records `memory_ids_used`, later evidence can challenge those records. Without attribution, an AAR may know the task failed but not which durable beliefs shaped the failure.

However, OODA does **not** specify what to store, how to establish provenance, when to delete, or how to arbitrate conflicting memories. Treating “loop faster” as the answer would be dangerous: a faster agent can reinforce stale orientation faster. OODA is therefore a rationale for feedback and attribution, not a standalone memory mechanism.

### UNVERIFIED Proposal 5 — lightweight memory-to-decision attribution

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** When the pre-context hook injects a durable record, attach its ID to the task trace; after an outcome or correction, mark the used records as supported, neutral, or challenged based on mechanically observed evidence. Do not ask the model to assign causal credit numerically.

Attribution would make implicated memories discoverable **very likely (80-90%)** because the injection IDs are mechanical. It would correctly identify causal memories only **unlikely (20-45%)** without controlled comparison, since use in context is not proof of causal influence. This limitation is decisive: the system may queue a review but must not auto-delete merely because an injected record preceded failure. The causal benefit is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by replaying matched tasks with one memory record toggled and comparing action changes**.

The anti-overfit gate passes. This is trace metadata on the existing bus, not observability SaaS or a causal-inference service.

## 6. Integrated design: the smallest useful “doctrinal memory”

The five proposals are best implemented as one bounded mechanism, not five subsystems.

### Active layers

| Layer | Contents | Admission | Exit / forgetting rule | Retrieval treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intent | Explicit owner purpose, constraints, end-state conditions | Direct instruction or owner approval | Explicit supersession/revocation | Always visible when in scope; highest authority |
| Current facts | Mutable environment/workspace/tool facts | Authoritative source or direct observation with provenance | Declared invalidator, contradiction, scope exit, supersession | Eligible only in matching scope |
| Assumptions | Necessary but unproved premises | Explicit label, confidence, decision link, invalidator | Confirmation to fact; falsification/retirement; decision closes | Shown with warning and cheapest check |
| Candidate lessons | AAR hypotheses and proposed changes | Triggered AAR with event references | Validation, rejection, merger, or expiry from review queue | Never injected as authoritative instruction |
| Validated lessons | Scoped changes shown to improve behavior/performance | Applied change plus observed result, or independent authoritative validation | Challenge, supersession, scope invalidation | Eligible in matching conditions |
| Archive | Raw event history and retired records, subject to retention policy | Existing capture policy | Retention/privacy deletion policy | Searchable evidence, not automatic guidance |

### Write path

1. Capture an evidence pointer, not a prose memory, at event time.
2. On a learning trigger, create an AAR candidate card.
3. Classify it as fact, assumption, preference/intent proposal, or candidate lesson.
4. Require scope, authority, decision link, invalidators, and provenance for active mutable records.
5. Promote only under the class-specific rule.
6. Append supersession; do not silently overwrite history.

### Read path

1. Parse current owner intent and consequential decisions.
2. Generate at most a few MCIRs.
3. Apply deterministic authority/status/scope filters.
4. Fire invalidator/recheck rules.
5. Retrieve semantically within the eligible set.
6. Present record type, provenance, confidence, and freshness alongside content.
7. Record IDs injected into the decision context.

### Forgetting policy

“Forget” has three distinct meanings and should not be one operation:

- **Remove from active orientation:** immediate when challenged, superseded, out of scope, or no longer decision-relevant.
- **Retire from routine retrieval:** when a candidate’s decision closes, its validation opportunity passes, or its value no longer justifies review cost.
- **Delete underlying evidence:** controlled by privacy, security, legal, and storage policy—not by epistemic confidence alone.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** safer to separate active retirement from physical deletion, because erroneous deletion is irreversible while exclusion from context is reversible; the evidence is the different operational purposes of active estimates versus retained AAR records. The actual privacy/storage tradeoff is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with an owner-approved retention threat model and recovery drill before choosing deletion windows**.

### Conflict resolution

Use authority and specificity before recency:

1. Current explicit owner instruction.
2. Governing policy/authoritative source in current scope.
3. Direct observation in the current environment.
4. Validated lesson with matching conditions.
5. Assumption/candidate, visibly labeled.
6. Inference from repeated behavior.

Within equal authority and scope, newer evidence may supersede older evidence. If two same-level records still conflict, quarantine both from automatic action and raise an MCIR. Do not ask embedding similarity or the language model to silently choose.

## 7. Verification program

### Shadow Memory Ledger Experiment

Run the proposed ledger in shadow mode first; it recommends context but does not control the live agent.

**Corpus:** at least 100 representative tasks with a deliberately balanced set of stable preferences, changed preferences, versioned facts, environment-specific facts, one-off failures, repeated failures, surprising successes, and direct owner corrections.

**Comparators:**

- existing memory/context behavior;
- existing behavior plus a simple recency/TTL baseline;
- MRE/MCIR eligibility and invalidator gate.

**Measures:**

- precision of injected durable memories;
- recall of truly useful memories;
- stale-memory injection rate;
- scope-mismatch rate;
- owner corrections attributable to remembered claims;
- task success and time/tool-call cost;
- candidate-to-validated promotion precision;
- number and age of unresolved challenges;
- context tokens consumed.

**Mechanical independence requirement:** do not declare a stale record detected, a lesson validated, or the mechanism done from a model judgment alone. Require two mechanically independent signals where the conclusion is “broken,” “absent,” or “done.” Examples:

- direct owner correction **plus** a changed action in a later replay;
- authoritative version/config source **plus** a live tool probe;
- deterministic status/scope filter output **plus** inspection of the final injected context;
- test outcome **plus** event trace showing the proposed change was actually used.

Before claiming the detector misses no stale memories, prove it finds seeded known-stale controls in each class. A second model paraphrase is not an independent signal.

### Seeded stale-memory benchmark

Create mutations with known answers:

- owner reverses a preference;
- dependency version changes;
- task moves from Windows/PowerShell to Linux/bash;
- a server transitions unavailable → healthy and healthy → unavailable;
- branch/workspace scope changes;
- a workaround succeeds once due to a hidden confounder;
- an older validated rule conflicts with a newer direct instruction;
- a malicious document asserts a false “owner preference.”

Score whether the system (a) retrieves, (b) labels, (c) checks, (d) quarantines, and (e) escalates correctly. Include false-positive controls: an old but still-valid owner preference, a durable safety constraint, and a stable interface fact.

### Promotion experiment

Seed candidate lessons with:

- genuine causal fixes;
- coincidental recoveries;
- environment-limited fixes;
- ambiguous causes;
- owner corrections whose *instruction* is authoritative but whose causal explanation is wrong.

Success means the system promotes genuine scoped lessons, leaves ambiguous items as candidates, and separates authoritative instruction from speculative explanation. Completion requires the independent signals above.

### Rollout gates

1. Shadow only.
2. Advisory display of type/scope/freshness.
3. Deterministic exclusion of explicitly superseded/challenged records.
4. Deterministic scope/authority filtering.
5. Event-driven refresh for a narrow set of version/config changes.
6. Only then consider automated lesson promotion—and only if its measured precision meets an owner-set threshold.

The staged rollout is **very likely (80-90%)** to limit harm relative to immediate replacement because each control is reversible and separately measurable; evidence is the shadow comparator and progressively narrow enforcement. Whether it is worth the operational complexity is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by requiring a material reduction in stale/scope-mismatched injection without a material drop in useful-memory recall or increase in task latency**.

## 8. Anti-overfit gate and rejected alternatives

The proposal deliberately remains a small typed ledger plus hooks. It rides existing instruction, hook, skill, MCP, and retrieval buses. It does not introduce any previously rejected component.

### Rejected: “store every AAR in a vector database”

This confuses archive with orientation, similarity with authority, and an observation with a lesson. It also makes supersession and invalidation implicit. A vector DB/KG memory is excluded by the brief’s anti-overfit gate and is unnecessary for the proposed control semantics.

### Rejected: a society of staff agents

Simulating commander, intelligence officer, red team, and lessons-learned officer creates a second orchestration runtime and correlated prose. It does not provide mechanically independent evidence. The doctrine’s roles should become record types and deterministic checks, not personas.

### Rejected: universal TTL deletion

Age is not falsity. TTL would delete old stable intent and retain newly repeated errors. Time may trigger review; declared invalidators and authoritative contradictions determine active status.

### Rejected: automatic “lesson learned” after every failure

FM 7-0 and CALL distinguish observation, analysis, change, and assessed improvement. Automatic promotion would institutionalize confounded explanations.

### Rejected: model-only red team on every retrieval

This adds latency and correlated speculation. Deterministic invalidators, scope checks, provenance, and authority resolve the easy cases. Model reasoning is reserved for ambiguous high-impact cases.

### Rejected: delete a memory whenever a task using it fails

Context inclusion does not establish causality. Failure can arise from execution, external state, or unrelated reasoning. A failed outcome challenges implicated records; controlled replay or independent evidence adjudicates them.

### Rejected: OODA-speed optimization as the goal

Boyd’s model emphasizes orientation, not mere cycle speed. Faster retrieval from a corrupted orientation accelerates error. Optimize decision quality and refresh latency subject to bounded context cost, not loops per minute.

## 9. Where the analogy genuinely breaks

1. **No real commander-subordinate relation.** The owner can state authority; the model cannot safely infer it from frequency or rhetorical confidence.
2. **No naturally independent staff.** Agent components may share a model, prompt, data, and failure mode. Role labels do not create epistemic independence.
3. **Different adversary model.** Military doctrine assumes active opponents, deception, fog, and physical constraints. Agent memory also faces benign drift, accidental contamination, prompt injection, and software change. Not every stale record is adversarial.
4. **Different cost of forgetting.** Units retain records for accountability and institutional learning; a personal harness may need aggressive deletion for privacy. Epistemic retention and physical retention must be governed separately.
5. **Different repeatability.** Training tasks can be repeated against standards. Creative or one-off agent work may never yield a clean validation trial.
6. **Different provenance.** Military information can have formal reporting chains. Agent context blends owner messages, generated summaries, tool output, documents, and external web content. Authority must be encoded outside free text.
7. **Language-model susceptibility.** Labels such as `assumption` help but do not guarantee calibrated use. The model may ignore them, over-weight them, or invent missing fields.
8. **Doctrine has institutional overhead.** Full military process would be absurd for routine agent tasks. Only consequential decisions and learning triggers warrant the loop.

These disanalogies rule out a literal transfer. They do not defeat the narrow transfer because typed epistemic status, decision linkage, invalidators, and evidence-gated promotion are useful independently of military hierarchy.

## 10. Final recommendation

### UNVERIFIED Recommendation — replace undifferentiated active memory with a Memory Running Estimate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Build one minimal MRE mechanism comprising:

- an owner-approved memory intent;
- typed records (`intent`, `fact`, `assumption`, `candidate_lesson`, `validated_lesson`);
- scope, authority, provenance, decision link, invalidators, and supersession;
- a deterministic pre-context eligibility filter;
- high-value-trigger AAR capture;
- evidence-gated lesson promotion;
- memory-ID attribution to later outcomes;
- shadow-mode verification before enforcement.

Do **not** build a new memory daemon, vector database, knowledge graph, staff-agent runtime, trained critic, durable workflow engine, guardrail suite, or observability service.

The overall recommendation is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce stale and wrongly generalized memory while retaining useful durable intent. Evidence: commander’s intent cleanly separates purpose from disposable plan; running estimates explicitly refresh facts and assumptions; CCIRs tie attention to decisions; AAR/ALLP separate observation from validated behavioral change; and red-team checks identify assumption defeat conditions. The harness-level result remains **UNVERIFIED; the Shadow Memory Ledger Experiment, seeded stale-memory benchmark, and promotion experiment above would settle it**.

The key principle worth borrowing from military doctrine is not “remember the battle.” It is:

> Preserve intent; maintain the estimate; expose assumptions; promote only demonstrated learning; and let changed conditions retire yesterday’s truth from today’s decision.
