# K03 — Predicting intent from history: the “I know what you mean” partner dimension

## Executive answer

The harness can reliably infer **defaults, not mandates**. Repeated, scoped, recent owner choices can select a reversible implementation default; explicit current-turn language always wins; ambiguous scope, contradictions, destructive/external effects, secrets, spend, publication, and irreversible state still require confirmation. The useful leap is not a bigger “persona prompt.” It is a small, testable intent contract layered onto the already-built basic-memory, event-ledger, `OPEN-THREADS.md`, AskUserQuestion, inquisitiveness guard, intent-drift sentinel, and continuous-learning bus.

That conclusion is **very likely (80-90%)**, supported by four mechanically different signals: (1) the corpus includes explicit reversals and exceptions—“stop the upload, and delete it,” “do not delete the installers,” “no resets,” and local-only media constraints (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11177-11180,11201-11210,11219-11222`); (2) the owner explicitly reports forgotten scope (“you ignored [the research request]” and “ignored clipboard upgrade”) (`corpus.json:11447-11456`); (3) the existing harness already treats memory as temporal and supersedable (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/CLAUDE.md:5-13`); and (4) PERSONAMEM reports that direct prompting of frontier models struggled with evolving profiles and achieved only about 50% overall accuracy in its simulated benchmark ([Jiang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14225)). The exact thresholds proposed below are **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is the historical replay benchmark in Finding 2 followed by a prospective shadow trial in Finding 1.

## Grounding and detector discipline

The available corpus is a 1.3 MB distillation of 10.62 GB / 1,564,084 transcript lines, with 11,633 files seen, zero failed, one bad line, 32 oversized lines, and capped samples (`corpus.json:2-11`). Its 2,268 “owner-correction candidates” are explicitly recall-biased, and an unfiltered smoke sample was 41/41 false positives (`corpus.json:10371-10374`). I therefore use candidate text only where its semantics are direct and corroborated by a different signal; I do **not** convert raw candidate frequency into an owner preference.

The hooks subsection is NO DATA, not evidence of no hook activity: its detector failed a known-positive control (`corpus.json:13260-13261`). Independently, the live-defect input reports both zero transcript hook fields and a demonstrably firing hook, and prescribes event-ledger plus system-reminder probes to settle persistence (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-147`). I make no hook-effectiveness claim.

The local behavioral signals used here are stronger than the candidate count:

* The owner asked to be re-asked after interruption (`corpus.json:11171-11174`) and separately asked that important work be persisted (`corpus.json:11237-11240`).
* The owner explicitly scoped deletion exceptions and prohibited resets (`corpus.json:11201-11210,11219-11222`).
* The owner approved continuous autonomous execution without inter-phase approval in one scoped workflow (`corpus.json:11189-11192`) but later stopped two background agents (`corpus.json:11464-11468`). This makes “likes autonomy” a **contextual** preference, not global permission.
* The owner called out two discussed-but-dropped threads in one correction (`corpus.json:11447-11456`). This is direct evidence for the never-drop-a-thread requirement.
* Existing memory guidance says recall must be proactive after compaction and records supersession/validity (`CLAUDE.md:7-11`); the event-ledger is already append-only, typed, failure-aware, and projected to state (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:2-13,61-65,250-285`).
* Existing inquisitiveness is already EVPI-gated, non-blocking, and fail-open (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/inquisitiveness/prompt-guard.py:2-20`), while the isolated sentinel already caps questions at three and says not to ask about safe defaults or style (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/agents/intent-drift-sentinel.md:14-33`). Proposals below extend these components; none adds a seventh hook.

## Inference boundary: act, state-and-act, or ask

| Historical proposition | Required evidence | Allowed behavior | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit current-turn instruction | Current owner message | Act within authorization | Highest-freshness owner signal. |
| Stable workflow/style default | At least two owner-authored examples in the same task class, no later contradiction, and compatible current context | State the assumption briefly and take a reversible step | Low downside; easy correction. |
| Project decision / open obligation | Explicit ledger/memory/thread record plus live repository/tool-state corroboration | Continue or reconcile; do not silently close | Preserves threads without trusting self-narrative. |
| One-off historical choice | One example only | Treat as a hypothesis, not preference | One event may be situational or accidental. |
| Contradicted, stale, cross-domain, or source-uncertain history | Any conflict, expired validity, or uncertain authorship | Ask one easy choice if it changes the path; otherwise use a safe default and disclose it | Dynamic profiles and source contamination make extrapolation unsafe. |
| Scope expansion, spend, publication, external messaging, deletion, credentials, security boundary, irreversible change | History alone is never sufficient authorization | Confirm first unless explicitly authorized now | Preference is not permission. |

This decision boundary is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce both needless questions and harmful presumption because it combines the workspace’s direct reversals with validated HAI guidance: support correction, scope services when uncertain, learn over time cautiously, expose global controls, and make consequences clear ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)). Its local effect size is **UNVERIFIED**; Finding 2’s replay experiment settles it.

## Findings (inferred-surprise first)

### 1. Inferred-surprise — Shadow intent futures: learn without acting on the guess

**improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a shadow-only `intent_prediction` event to the existing event-ledger writer, invoked from continuous-learning-v2 or `/self-reflect`, not a new hook. For each materially ambiguous turn, record up to three candidate interpretations, the chosen safe action class, evidence note IDs, and a probability band. Never show it to the owner and never let it authorize an action during calibration.

**what it is.** A prospective calibration loop. Later, a mechanically external label closes the loop: (a) explicit owner correction/approval in a future turn; (b) execution evidence that one interpretation satisfied the acceptance test; or (c) an isolated second-model adjudication over a redacted transcript slice when owner feedback is absent. Same-context self-agreement is forbidden.

**why-inferred.** The owner wants the partner to “know what I mean” but also has direct reversals and stopped agents (`corpus.json:11177-11180,11464-11468`). Shadow learning delivers personalization without spending the owner’s focus or risking premature action. It is **likely (55-80%)** to expose domain-specific calibration differences because NIST emphasizes that trust depends on task and risk, not a single global score ([NIST, 2021](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2021/05/nist-proposes-method-evaluating-user-trust-artificial-intelligence-systems)), and the corpus shows autonomy varying by workflow.

**how-to-build.** Extend `ledger.KINDS` with `intent_prediction` and `intent_outcome`; store only note permalinks/hashes plus a compact proposition, never raw transcript history. A scheduled or explicit evaluator joins predictions to the next owner-authored turn and tool/test outcome. Require minimum sample count per task class and report Brier score, abstention rate, false-autonomy rate, and owner-interruption count. Graduate only individual inference classes (for example, “run tests after edits”), never the whole predictor. This folds into continuous-learning/event-ledger and adds zero hooks.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** Held-out future owner turns and actual execution results; ambiguous cases are adjudicated by an isolated model that did not generate the prediction. Promotion additionally requires a small owner-reviewed disagreement sample. The claim that useful classes will graduate is **UNVERIFIED**; a 30-session shadow trial with pre-registered thresholds settles it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** Cooperative IRL explicitly models the human reward as initially unknown and derives active learning/communication behaviors ([Hadfield-Menell et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03137)); uncalibrated confidence can cause both misuse and disuse ([Li et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07632)).

**value x effort.** transformative x medium.

**novelty.** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy.** just-do-it for shadow logging and reversible evaluation; confirm-first before any class is promoted to autonomous mutation.

### 2. Inferred-surprise — Counterfactual “would you have asked?” replay benchmark

**improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add an offline intent benchmark beside the semantic-router/harness benchmark rather than another runtime component.

**what it is.** A versioned set of historical decision points with four labels: `infer-and-act`, `state-and-act`, `ask`, `refuse/require-authority`. It tests both under-questioning and prompt-fatigue: inference accuracy alone is insufficient.

**why-inferred.** The corpus’s correction detector is demonstrably contaminated (41/41 false-positive smoke sample; `corpus.json:10371-10374`), while direct samples include a missed two-thread request (`corpus.json:11447-11456`) and explicit after-action scope correction (`corpus.json:11201-11204`). The owner needs a detector that proves it recognizes known positives before conclusions. A benchmark is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent anecdotal preference rules from shipping because the known defects document seven wrong conclusions eliminated by known-positive controls (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`).

**how-to-build.** Source-filter records to direct owner-authored messages; exclude system reminders, task notifications, stop-hook boilerplate, and agent prompts. Stratify by task/risk. Each case contains only prior history available at that point, current request, candidate actions, and an independently observed later outcome. Use time-based train/test split to prevent future leakage. Score: intent choice, unsafe-action false positive, unnecessary-question rate, dropped-thread rate, and calibration error. Include known-positive controls (the quoted direct corrections) and known-negative controls (agent-authored prompt samples). Run it from the existing harness-bench schedule or explicit `/eval`; no hook.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** Frozen later owner messages, repository/test/tool outcomes, and blinded owner labels for a small contested set. The evaluator executes in a separate context. The attainable baseline is **UNVERIFIED**; labeling 100 stratified cases and comparing current sentinel versus the proposed contract settles it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** LaMP establishes multi-task evaluation for personalized text generation/classification and tests lexical, semantic, and time-aware retrieval ([Salemi et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11406)); PERSONAMEM specifically tests profile evolution across long histories ([Jiang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14225)).

**value x effort.** transformative x medium.

**novelty.** inferred-surprise.

### 3. Inferred-surprise — Evidence-weighted preference cards, not a monolithic persona

**improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend existing basic-memory notes with a tiny schema and resolver; do not add a vector DB, KG, trained user model, daemon, or second memory runtime.

**what it is.** Each preference card carries `proposition`, `scope`, `source_kind`, `evidence_refs`, `observations`, `counterexamples`, `valid_as_of`, `expires/review_after`, `superseded_by`, `risk_ceiling`, and `confidence_band`. Hard constraints and permissions are separate types from soft defaults. The resolver returns `current`, `contested`, `stale`, or `insufficient`, never a synthesized psychological persona.

**why-inferred.** Current guidance already requires `superseded_by` and `valid_as_of` (`CLAUDE.md:9-11`), and the corpus shows contextual exceptions: autonomous continuous workflow (`corpus.json:11189-11192`), later user-stopped agents (`corpus.json:11464-11468`), and a specific “no resets” instruction (`corpus.json:11219-11222`). It is **very likely (80-90%)** that scoped cards are safer than global rules because dynamic-user benchmarking finds profile evolution difficult and aggregate personalization papers show user preferences vary by context ([Jiang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14225); [Garbacea et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18943)).

**how-to-build.** Reuse markdown notes and existing search; add a schema template plus a resolver in the current memory skill. Only direct owner messages can create/strengthen a preference; agent summaries can nominate a hypothesis but cannot promote it. Two consistent, same-scope observations permit a soft default; contradictions immediately mark `contested`; absence of correction is not positive evidence. Explicit current-turn instructions supersede retrieval output. Show the owner only when a card is created/changed and behavior consequence matters, satisfying prompt-fatigue constraints.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** A deterministic resolver test suite with chronological contradictory cards plus held-out future owner choices. A different-context evaluator checks source identity. Whether two observations is the right promotion threshold is **UNVERIFIED**; replay benchmark calibration by task class settles it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** The CHI guidelines recommend remembering interactions, learning behavior, adapting cautiously, granular feedback, global controls, and notifying changes ([Amershi et al., 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=564561)). Persona-Plug also reports that retrieving isolated history can lose continuity, motivating structured whole-history modeling—but this design deliberately takes the smaller, inspectable path ([Liu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11901)).

**value x effort.** high x low.

**novelty.** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy.** just-do-it for recording explicit, low-risk scoped defaults; confirm-first for hard constraints, authorization, destructive/external scope, or resolving a contested card.

### 4. Inferred-surprise — Thread escrow with evidence-backed closure

**improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Fold structured thread reconciliation into the existing persist-nudge/event-ledger/`OPEN-THREADS.md` path; this explicitly replaces no hook and adds none.

**what it is.** Every discussed obligation gets an ID and status: `proposed`, `accepted`, `in_progress`, `deferred-until`, `blocked-on-owner`, `done-evidence`, or `cancelled-by-owner`. The agent may infer the next reversible step, but it cannot infer closure from silence or from its own prose.

**why-inferred.** The owner explicitly complained that SOTA feature research and a clipboard upgrade were ignored (`corpus.json:11447-11456`), asked to be re-asked after interruption (`corpus.json:11171-11174`), and asked to persist an important session (`corpus.json:11237-11240`). This makes thread preservation **almost certain (90-99%)** to match owner intent; the evidence is direct owner language across separate events.

**how-to-build.** Extend the existing open-thread line format with `id`, `source_ref`, `next_action`, `owner`, `status`, and `closure_evidence`. At plan changes and before final response, reconcile accepted thread IDs against tool/task results. Collapse user presentation to one compact line only when a thread is newly blocked, contradicted, or at risk; otherwise remain silent. The existing session-start replay already surfaces goals, open failures, decisions, and milestones (`event-ledger/sessionstart-replay.py:31-63`) and runs only on recovery paths to avoid normal-start token cost (`sessionstart-replay.py:66-84`). Reuse that path.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** Closure requires a test/build/tool result, repository state, artifact existence/content check, or explicit owner cancellation. At the next session, replay must recover a known seeded open thread and a known closed thread; a separate script compares the ledger projection to `OPEN-THREADS.md`. The exact false-nudge rate is **UNVERIFIED**; seed-and-replay tests plus 20-session prospective telemetry settle it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** Amershi et al. recommend recent-interaction memory and efficient correction ([CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)); mixed-initiative design treats timing, uncertainty, and user attention as core decision variables ([Horvitz, 1999](https://erichorvitz.com/uiact.htm)).

### 5. Inferred-surprise — Risk-indexed autonomy ladder, learned per task class

**improvement — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Add a declarative action matrix consumed by the existing sentinel/inquisitiveness workflow: `observe`, `draft`, `local-reversible`, `local-mutating`, `external-reversible`, `external-irreversible/destructive`.

**what it is.** History can raise or lower convenience within a risk class but cannot cross authorization boundaries. Examples: infer and run read-only diagnostics; state-and-run ordinary tests; infer a formatting default; ask before deleting installers, spending, publishing, messaging, changing credentials, or expanding the target set.

**why-inferred.** The corpus juxtaposes broad autonomous approval (`corpus.json:11189-11192`) with specific deletion exceptions and stopped agents (`corpus.json:11201-11204,11464-11468`). Thus a single “autonomy preference” is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** adequate; direct contradictory contexts are the evidence. Task-specific trust is also the premise of NIST’s user-trust model ([NIST, 2021](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2021/05/nist-proposes-method-evaluating-user-trust-artificial-intelligence-systems)).

**how-to-build.** Put action classes and required authority in one JSON file. The sentinel resolves current request + scoped preference card + action class. `observe/draft/local-reversible` may be just-do-it when intent confidence passes replay-calibrated thresholds. The last three classes require explicit current authorization according to configured policy. Never treat previous authorization as durable permission unless the owner explicitly made it standing and scoped. Provide one global “less/more proactive” control only for low-risk classes.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** Deterministic policy tests across known reversals plus actual tool side-effect classification; future owner overrides are counted per class. No same-context confidence decides promotion. Threshold performance is **UNVERIFIED**; the Finding 2 benchmark and Finding 1 shadow trial settle it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** Calibrated confidence matters because both overconfidence and underconfidence impair collaboration ([Li et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07632)); cognitive forcing can reduce overreliance but was liked less, showing why friction belongs only at consequential boundaries ([Buçinca et al., 2021](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml)).

**value x effort.** high x low.

**novelty.** inferred-surprise.

### 6. Inferred-surprise — Source-authenticity firewall for preference learning

**improvement — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Add a shared source classifier before continuous-learning/basic-memory may promote an “owner preference.” This is a library call, not a hook.

**what it is.** Classify evidence as direct owner, hook feedback, system reminder, teammate, task notification, tool output, agent-authored prompt, or unknown. Only direct owner evidence can authorize or promote preferences. All other classes may identify a candidate to verify.

**why-inferred.** The corpus detector’s 41/41 false-positive smoke sample consists of agent-authored prompts (`corpus.json:10371-10374`), and its samples visibly include task notifications and teammate messages (`corpus.json:11231-11234,11381-11402`). Separately, a fabricated orchestrator constraint propagated into about 15 prompts and induced a higher-failure write path (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`). It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that provenance filtering is necessary; two mechanically independent signals—corpus contamination and the diagnosed orchestrator incident—support that claim.

**how-to-build.** Parse message role plus known wrapper tags before text semantics. Known-positive test: a direct owner correction. Known-negative controls: `<task-notification>`, `<teammate-message>`, stop-hook boilerplate, and “Research task:” agent prompts. Store the original source type and reference with each card. Unknown source fails closed for learning but fail-open for session operation: it does not block work, it merely declines to learn a durable preference.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** A labeled provenance fixture suite and an independent parser over raw transcript envelopes; both must agree before promotion. The raw Windows transcripts are not present on this node, so end-to-end precision is **UNVERIFIED**; replaying the parser against 200 raw envelopes on the Windows box settles it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** Instruction hierarchy research separates privileged user instructions from lower-trust tool content and demonstrates why source role must precede semantics ([Wallace et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208)).

**value x effort.** high x low.

**novelty.** inferred-surprise.

**autonomy.** just-do-it for filtering and candidate recording; confirm-first if source identity is uncertain and the inferred preference would alter consequential action.

### 7. User-asked — Prompt-fatigue-aware active clarification

**improvement — CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Refine the existing EVPI/AskUserQuestion policy; do not create another question hook.

**what it is.** Ask only when plausible answers lead to materially different consequential paths. Batch at most three mutually exclusive questions, but prefer one easy choice. If uncertainty affects only style or a reversible local detail, state the inferred default and proceed.

**why-inferred.** The owner explicitly wants reduced cognitive load, and local code already says AskUserQuestion is unavailable inside the synchronous prompt hook, caps follow-up at 1-3, and forbids asking about codebase-inferable facts (`prompt-guard.py:147-159`). The sentinel separately says no questions for safe defaults or stylistic choices (`intent-drift-sentinel.md:14-33`). It is **very likely (80-90%)** that consolidating these existing rules will reduce interruptions without increasing unsafe action because it changes selection policy, not capabilities.

**how-to-build.** Compute a coarse decision table, not a model self-score: `(answer_changes_scope or irreversible_action or external_effect) AND evidence_conflict` → ask; `safe_reversible_default` → state-and-act; `insufficient authority` → confirm. Rank questions by expected avoided rework divided by owner effort. Offer concrete choices and the recommended default. Remember explicit answers as scoped cards so the same question is not repeated.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** Compare question count, owner response latency, post-action corrections, and costly pivots against the frozen replay set and prospective future sessions. Owner answers themselves are the label. The magnitude of fatigue reduction is **UNVERIFIED**; an A/B-by-session trial with a stop condition on correction rate settles it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** Active reward-learning research finds that optimizing both information gain and human answer difficulty produces easier questions and faster learning ([Bıyık et al., 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/b-iy-ik20a.html)); active preference learning selects labels based on predictive entropy and preference certainty ([Muldrew et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/muldrew24a.html)).

**value x effort.** high x low.

**novelty.** user-asked.

**autonomy.** just-do-it for safe defaults; confirm-first where the decision table says the answer changes consequential scope or authority.

### 8. User-asked — Correctability receipt: “why I inferred this” on demand, not every turn

**improvement — NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it).** Use brief assumption statements and existing AskUserQuestion/correction mechanisms; store the full evidence receipt in the ledger/memory rather than injecting it into every response.

**what it is.** When acting on history, emit at most one short line only if the inference is non-obvious: “Assuming X from [scoped prior decision]; I’m taking reversible step Y.” Provide an on-demand command/view that shows evidence refs, scope, age, contradictions, and what future behavior will change. A correction tombstones the old card and records the new valid-as-of card.

**why-inferred.** The owner wants “I know what you mean,” not invisible mind-reading, and current memory already supports supersession (`CLAUDE.md:9-11`). The defect record shows false inferred facts can propagate widely when recipients cannot challenge them (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`). An on-demand receipt is **likely (55-80%)** to improve calibrated trust with less fatigue than an explanation every turn; Amershi et al. recommend explaining behavior, efficient correction, conveying future consequences, and global controls ([Microsoft HAI Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=564561)).

**how-to-build.** Add a response convention to the intent/sentinel skill: silent for explicit current intent; one-line assumption for history-dependent reversible action; blocking confirmation for consequential ambiguity. Link the internal receipt to preference card and event IDs. Correction performs an actual state transition (`superseded_by`) and reports the changed future default once.

**the EXTERNAL signal that verifies it.** Owner correction creates a new memory record, and a fresh isolated session must retrieve the new card and reject the old known-positive stale card. Repository/tool state verifies any claimed action. Whether the receipt is optimally terse is **UNVERIFIED**; owner preference in a two-format trial settles it.

**SOTA citation with URL.** The validated HAI guidelines include efficient correction, explanation, cautious adaptation, granular feedback, and notification of changes ([Amershi et al., 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)).

**value x effort.** medium x low.

**novelty.** user-asked.

**autonomy.** just-do-it for receipts and reversible actions; confirm-first for any consequential inference.

## What is reliably inferable now

The following are **very likely (80-90%)** safe inference classes, supported by the direct corpus signals and bounded-action design above, but their exact coverage is **UNVERIFIED** until Finding 2’s replay benchmark:

1. **Continue explicitly accepted, still-open threads** when the ledger/thread record and live state agree. Never silently mark done.
2. **Recover context before asking for repetition** after compaction/interruption, because current guidance explicitly mandates proactive recall (`CLAUDE.md:7-11`) and the owner asked to be re-asked after interruption (`corpus.json:11171-11174`).
3. **Use established same-project style/workflow defaults** when repeated, recent, uncontested, and reversible.
4. **Run read-only diagnostics and ordinary verification** implied by a build/fix request, subject to current scope and tool reliability.
5. **Prefer comprehensive follow-through inside an explicitly autonomous scoped mission**, while preserving cancellation and exception controls; the corpus supports scoped thoroughness (`corpus.json:10377-10394,11189-11192`) but the candidate-pattern counts are not treated as verified owner frequency.
6. **Surface a forgotten accepted thread compactly** rather than making the owner reconstruct it.

## What must still be asked

These boundaries are **almost certain (90-99%)** given direct corpus reversals and standard authorization semantics:

1. A contradiction between current instruction and remembered preference—current instruction normally wins, but ask if the contradiction could cause irreversible harm.
2. Which of two materially different scopes/outcomes the owner wants when neither is a safe reversible default.
3. Deletion, destructive cleanup, resets, publication, messages to third parties, purchases/spend, credential/security changes, or other irreversible/external actions without explicit current or explicitly standing scoped authorization.
4. Whether a one-off historical behavior is a durable preference when applying it would materially change results.
5. Whether an old preference still applies across a new project/domain or after a recorded counterexample.
6. Whether an open thread should be cancelled; silence is not cancellation.

## Anti-overfit and hook-ceiling check

All proposals ride existing buses: basic-memory markdown/vector retrieval, event-ledger, `OPEN-THREADS.md`, the current inquisitiveness/sentinel paths, continuous-learning-v2, AskUserQuestion, and harness-bench. They add **zero hooks**. Finding 4 explicitly folds into persist-nudge/event-ledger rather than adding a seventh hook. No proposal introduces a second orchestration runtime, vector DB/KG, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The design is deliberately an evidence schema, a policy table, and an offline evaluator—not a new “user-model service.”

The radical alternative—a learned always-on user simulator trained over 10.62 GB—is **unlikely (20-45%)** to beat this minimal architecture safely at present: PERSONAMEM finds frontier direct-prompting systems struggle with evolving profiles ([Jiang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14225)); this corpus’s correction labels are contaminated (`corpus.json:10371-10374`); and source errors have already propagated a fabricated constraint (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`). A learned model could be reconsidered only if the shadow/replay dataset becomes large, source-clean, calibrated by risk class, and demonstrably superior out of sample.

## Build order and acceptance gates

1. **Source classifier + frozen replay set** (Findings 6 and 2). Acceptance: known positives found; known negatives rejected; time-split evaluation; no same-context labels.
2. **Preference-card schema + risk matrix** (Findings 3 and 5). Acceptance: chronological contradiction and supersession tests; current owner instruction wins; history cannot authorize destructive/external actions.
3. **Thread escrow reconciliation** (Finding 4). Acceptance: seeded open/deferred/done/cancelled threads survive replay and only externally evidenced closure is accepted.
4. **Shadow predictor** (Finding 1). Acceptance: 30-session prospective calibration, task-class metrics, no runtime authority.
5. **Clarification/receipt tuning** (Findings 7 and 8). Acceptance: fewer repeated/unnecessary questions without increased costly correction or dropped-thread rate.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this sequence gives useful value before the predictor itself is trusted because source hygiene, thread escrow, and explicit action boundaries are independently beneficial and use existing infrastructure. The exact implementation size and Windows behavior are **UNVERIFIED**; line-count spikes or raw-envelope incompatibility discovered in the first build gate would settle whether any item exceeds BUILD-SMALL.
