# I07 — Autonomy calibration by reversibility × stakes

## Executive verdict

The harness needs a **single action-level autonomy covenant**, not a more cautious personality and not another guard. The proposed default is:

| | Low stakes | High stakes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reversible, with an externally proven inverse** | **JUST-DO-IT.** Inspect, test, edit behind a checkpoint, restart a local service, retry by a different method, or produce a draft. | **JUST-DO-IT, staged.** Take the smallest bounded step whose rollback has already been exercised; require an external outcome receipt before expanding blast radius. |
| **Irreversible, or no externally proven inverse** | **JUST-DO-IT within a pre-authorized de minimis budget.** Examples are spending one ordinary search call or terminating a reproducibly rerunnable hung test. Log the spend/loss; do not interrupt the owner. | **CONFIRM-FIRST.** Examples are force-pushing history, bulk-deleting ambiguous orphaned plugin trees, publishing/merging, rotating credentials, spending a material quota, or taking an action with third-party, legal, privacy, production, or safety consequences. Escrow the thread and continue any independent safe work while waiting. |

This is not “three permissive boxes and one safety box” by assertion. It depends on strict definitions:

* **Reversible** means all four are present: a named inverse; a bounded recovery window; a captured before-state or reproducible source; and an **external restore receipt** (filesystem hash/diff, command exit plus state readback, service health probe, CI result, second model using raw evidence, or owner observation). A model saying “I can undo this” inside the same context is not evidence. If any element is missing, classify the action as irreversible.
* **High stakes** means a plausible failure crosses any of these boundaries: material data loss; shared/production state; secrets or identity; paid or scarce quota above a pre-authorized cap; public or third-party communication; merge/release/history mutation; legal, medical, financial, physical, or privacy consequence; or more than a bounded owner recovery burden. Stakes concern consequence, not implementation difficulty.
* **Authority is separate from confidence.** A technically obvious action can still need authority; a difficult action can still be autonomous when it is in scope, staged, and recoverable. Existing explicit instructions such as “no check-ins” authorize ordinary execution but do not silently authorize the high-stakes/irreversible quadrant.

The placement is **very likely (80-90%)** to fit this owner better than the current broad “clarify long-horizon work” posture because the corpus contains repeated direct instructions to continue without check-ins and to perform deployment/monitoring work without making the owner operate the tools (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:11837`, `:12221-12229`, `:12377-12384`), while the same owner explicitly requires “no risks” and confirmed working behavior (`corpus.json:12371-12374`). The remaining uncertainty is transfer from the sampled projects to every future domain; settle it with the replay-and-shadow evaluation in Finding 7, not with intuition.

## Grounding and method

The corpus is a high-coverage distillation, not a transcript-perfect preference dataset: it covers 11,633 files and 10.62 GB with zero failed files, one bad line, and capped samples (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). Its owner-correction detector is explicitly recall-biased; an unfiltered smoke sample was 41/41 false positives (`corpus.json:10371-10374`). I therefore use a candidate as owner evidence only when the retained record is plainly a first-person instruction rather than a task notification, hook injection, or agent-authored prompt. Counts from the candidate section are treated only as supporting prevalence, never as clean labels.

Two local signals establish the initiative preference independently:

1. Direct owner records say “No check-ins between items,” “restart the funnel yourself,” “i meant you should do that,” and “you monitor everything yourself, and tell me when i need to do manual input” (`corpus.json:11837-11840`, `:12221-12230`, `:12377-12384`).
2. Separately, the normalized pattern table records 12 instances of “why did you stop proceed until you finish everything,” plus 34 and 30 variants authorizing completion of all required/logical steps (`corpus.json:10389-10398`, `:10424-10427`). Because the detector is noisy, these counts corroborate but do not replace the direct records.

Two different signals establish the counterweight—proof and restraint:

1. The owner says to test existing features first, take “no risks,” and ensure everything is confirmed and working (`corpus.json:12371-12374`).
2. The live-defects input independently documents six confident wrong conclusions from single probes and a seventh caught by a known-positive control (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). It also records a fabricated constraint propagated into roughly 15 agent prompts, making same-context policy inference unsafe (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-160`).

The existing harness already contains the buses this design needs. The behavior contract limits questions to material ambiguity in the first one or two actions (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:7-20`), requires durable decision/failure/milestone notes (`claude/AGENTS.md:28-34`), and mandates fail-open hooks (`claude/AGENTS.md:64-70`). The shared dispatcher already orders checkpoint, ledger, recall, claim-warning, and subagent modules and records terminal results (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/README.md:1-29`). The event ledger replays on resume/compact/clear (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:933-983`), and `note.py` already records goals, failures, decisions, milestones, preconditions, and supersession (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/note.py:1-42`). The local dispatcher already folds six SessionStart checks behind one invocation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:1-70`). Accordingly, every proposal below uses an existing rule/skill/ledger/dispatcher bus. **No proposal adds a hook.**

The external research supports calibrated mixed initiative rather than maximum automation. Horvitz frames mixed initiative as allocating contribution to the human or computer at the appropriate time and explicitly grounds it in uncertainty and user control ([Microsoft Research, 1999](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/)). Amershi et al.'s validated human-AI guidelines call for efficient correction/recovery, disambiguation when goals are uncertain, cautious adaptation, global controls, and communicating consequences ([CHI 2019 paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)). PRISM models proactive intervention as a benefit-versus-burden decision with asymmetric false-alarm costs and reports 22.78% fewer false alarms on ProactiveBench ([arXiv:2602.01532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01532)). PACT explicitly evaluates ask-or-act behavior with a metric trading assistance accuracy against clarification frequency ([arXiv:2605.24350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24350)). ProACT treats forgotten constraints, loops, ambiguous goals, and underspecified plans as collaboration breakdowns and evaluates non-interruptiveness as a first-class outcome ([arXiv:2607.03730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03730)). These papers support the structure, but their exact empirical gains do not transfer automatically to this coding harness; all transfer claims below carry confidence bands and local experiments.

## Real-operation placement and defense

### Reversible × low stakes → just-do-it

* Read/search the corpus, repository, settings, and audit artifacts; run bounded diagnostics; query an already-authorized research source. These are observational and produce external tool results. Corpus reliability also favors direct `Grep`/search over `Glob`: Grep has 2.26% errors while Glob has 13.32% (`corpus.json:55-84`).
* Make a scoped working-tree edit after the existing edit checkpoint; run the smallest relevant test; revert on failure. This placement is valid only when checkpoint creation and a restore checksum are available. Write/Edit are heavily used and imperfect (~3.2–3.4% errors), so the post-edit readback/test is part of the action, not optional (`corpus.json:31-52`).
* Restart a dead local MCP or funnel with a health probe. The owner directly corrected the agent to restart the funnel itself (`corpus.json:12221-12230`), and the existing architecture describes a local self-healing health rail (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/CLAUDE.md:44-67`). The inverse is stop/restart plus configuration rollback, while health is externally observed.

### Reversible × high stakes → just-do-it, staged

* Repair a shared harness module behind the existing checkpoint, run its focused tests, then canary one provider/event before wider activation. Shared policy is high stakes because it affects future sessions; it remains reversible only if the exact installed baseline is captured and a restore drill succeeds. The installer already promises a timestamped rollback copy and atomic JSON updates (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/README.md:46-55`).
* Prepare a draft PR or release candidate without merging/publishing it. The artifact is reviewable and discardable; CI and diff are external signals. The Nature meta-analysis of 106 experiments found human-AI combinations were on average worse than the best member for decision tasks but better for content creation, supporting machine preparation plus retained human authority for consequential decisions ([Vaccaro et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)).
* Apply a reversible local game/trainer setting in a sandbox and automatically roll it back after a live probe. The owner asks for live probing without manual log ferrying (`corpus.json:12383-12384`) but also requires no-risk confirmed behavior (`:12371-12374`). A sandbox observation is the external signal; absence of that observation moves the operation to confirm-first.

### Irreversible × low stakes → just-do-it within a pre-authorized de minimis budget

* Spend an ordinary, non-sensitive web/search call from an already-authorized pool below a configured per-task cap. The spent call cannot be recovered, but its consequence is small. The current shared module already identifies metered tools for at-the-moment advisory (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/modules.py:644-657`), so this is a threshold policy on the existing bus, not a new meter.
* Terminate a demonstrably hung, reproducibly rerunnable local test process after collecting its command/log/elapsed-time receipt. The corpus includes a direct owner observation of a test running 26m59s with no output and a separate correction that an overlong test should be verified and the plan continued (`corpus.json:12161` and `:12203-12206`; the retained direct sample is at `:12203-12206`). Lost process progress is irreversible, but rerunning from captured inputs keeps stakes low. If the process controls production, external hardware, or an unreproducible experiment, stakes are high instead.
* Consume bounded scratch state that has no owner value and is independently proven disposable. “Regenerable” normally makes an item reversible under this report's definition; this quadrant is intentionally narrow. Ambiguous plugin directories do **not** belong here: the pinned evidence says 110/169 have no live sibling and one allegedly orphaned directory was read by a live skill, so bulk deletion is high stakes and confirm-first.

### Irreversible × high stakes → confirm-first

* Force-push or mirror history; merge/publish a release; send a public or third-party message; rotate/delete credentials; make a purchase or material quota spend; change production/shared identity or access. The behavior contract already requires explicit approval for force-push to main (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/AGENTS.md:93-102`).
* Bulk-delete the ~1,293 orphan-marked plugin directories. The operation is both data-destructive and classification-uncertain; the pinned two-signal investigation found live dependence plus many directories without a live sibling. Confirmation must present a manifest, disposition classes, recovery source, and dry-run receipts—not a generic “proceed?”
* Persist a newly inferred preference that materially broadens future authority. Ask Now, Use Later finds a large acquisition gap in long-lived agents, but that does not justify silently converting one contextual behavior into a global permission ([arXiv:2605.28108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28108)). Ask once only when a reusable, consequential preference is actually load-bearing; record its scope and expiry.

## Findings (inferred-surprise first)

### Finding 1 — Make “reversible” a proved capability, not an adjective

**Improvement.** Add a rollback-certificate requirement to the autonomy matrix. This is the most important surprise: many nominally reversible actions are operationally irreversible because the inverse was never captured or tested.

**What it is.** Before a high-stakes action can enter either reversible quadrant, require `before_subject`, `inverse`, `rollback_deadline`, and `restore_receipt`. A Git diff alone is insufficient for generated state, databases, live services, secrets, or cross-process configuration. If the certificate is absent or stale, the classifier deterministically treats the action as irreversible.

**Why inferred.** The owner wants the agent to execute and monitor autonomously (`corpus.json:12377-12384`) yet wants “no risks” and confirmed working results (`:12371-12374`). The harness already claims timestamped rollback copies (`agents/harness/README.md:53-55`) and has edit checkpoints, but the live-defects record shows that unvalidated detectors repeatedly produced false certainty (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`). The implied need is not another warning; it is executable proof that autonomy remains recoverable.

**How to build.** Extend the existing checkpoint/event-ledger record, not a new hook: (1) at action planning, attach a small `autonomy` object with the four fields; (2) reuse edit-checkpoints for files and register action-specific inverse commands only from an allowlisted schema; (3) on the existing PostToolUse path, execute a non-destructive readback or, in a disposable fixture, the actual restore; (4) store the external receipt hash and subject digest; (5) make the rule/skill treat missing or mismatched certificates as irreversible. Keep the record bounded and redacted. This folds into the shared dispatcher/checkpoint module and adds **zero hooks**, preserving the ~6-hook ceiling and fail-open behavior.

**External signal.** A disposable-fixture restore test must reproduce the pre-action subject hash/state, and a fresh-context verifier receives only the before-state, action, inverse, and receipts. Disagreement routes the action to confirm-first. The acting context's own review never closes the loop.

**SOTA.** Amershi et al. explicitly require efficient correction, recovery, and cautious adaptation ([CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)); Lee and See argue that trust should support appropriate reliance rather than maximum reliance ([Human Factors 46(1)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392)).

**Assessment.** value **transformative** × effort **medium**. Novelty **inferred-surprise**. Autonomy **just-do-it**. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce false “safe to proceed” classifications because certificate absence becomes a deterministic state rather than a same-context judgment; evidence is the existing checkpoint/ledger substrate and the documented single-signal failures. Actual reduction magnitude remains to be measured by Finding 7.

### Finding 2 — Use a one-box confirmation boundary, not universal clarify-first

**Improvement.** Replace the broad behavioral intuition “ambiguous/long work means ask” with the 2×2 action covenant above: confirm only high-stakes actions lacking an externally proven inverse.

**What it is.** Classification occurs per operation immediately before execution, not once per task. A large project may contain hundreds of autonomous reads, edits, tests, retries, and drafts plus one credential rotation that waits for approval. Confidence and ambiguity choose how much verification to do; stakes and reversibility choose who has authority.

**Why inferred.** The owner repeatedly rejects unnecessary pauses: “No check-ins between items” (`corpus.json:11837-11840`), “i meant you should do that” (`:12227-12230`), and “tell me when i need to do manual input” (`:12377-12380`). Yet the current contract says to clarify long-horizon/ambiguous work and never ask mid-task (`claude/AGENTS.md:7-20`), which cannot express a newly encountered consequential boundary. The owner signal implies selective interruption, not either blanket autonomy or blanket confirmation.

**How to build.** Update the shared behavior contract and the semantic task-routing skill with a 20–30-line decision table and definitions. Add `stakes={low,high}`, `reversibility={proved,unproved}`, `authority={act,ask}`, and evidence IDs to the existing ledger decision entry. Use the existing `AskUserQuestion` only for the one confirm-first cell. Do not add a classifier model, service, hook, or trained critic. The action remains fail-open at the hook level; the behavioral contract still forbids executing an unapproved high/irreversible operation.

**External signal.** Run a fixed scenario suite through a fresh-context second model that sees only raw operation facts and the matrix; compare its authority decision with a separately authored gold label, then review disagreements with the owner on a bounded sample. For runtime actions, the actual tool receipt and rollback receipt settle the declared axis values.

**SOTA.** Horvitz's mixed-initiative principles couple automation with direct control and uncertainty-aware allocation ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/)). PACT formalizes ask-or-act and measures clarification utility rather than accuracy alone ([arXiv:2605.24350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24350)). PRISM uses asymmetric missed-help/false-alarm costs and spends extra deliberation near ambiguous/high-stakes boundaries ([arXiv:2602.01532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01532)).

**Assessment.** value **transformative** × effort **low**. Novelty **inferred-surprise**. Autonomy **just-do-it** for policy drafting and shadow evaluation; **confirm-first** only before activating a material global behavior change. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce unnecessary check-ins because three cells no longer ask; evidence is the direct owner corrections and PACT/PRISM's explicit burden-aware gating. It is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve safety as well as the current contract because the high/irreversible boundary is sharper, but the lower confidence reflects possible axis misclassification; Findings 1 and 7 test that risk.

### Finding 3 — Keep working while a decision waits: confirmation is a branch, not a stop sign

**Improvement.** Add **decision escrow** to the existing event-ledger/open-thread machinery so a confirm-first action never causes the rest of the task to vanish or stall.

**What it is.** When the matrix reaches high/irreversible, write a durable pending decision with: exact blocked action, why authority is required, 2–3 bounded choices, default safe state, dependent thread IDs, independent runnable work, evidence gathered, and expiry/invalidators. Continue all independent reversible work. On owner response, resolve/supersede the escrow and resume dependents. Never infer approval from silence, a peer message, or progress on sibling tasks.

**Why inferred.** “Never drop a thread” is a hard requirement for this unit. The owner also says not to stop between tier items (`corpus.json:11837-11840`) and corrects stopped autonomous loops (`:12251-12254`). The event ledger already records decisions/failures/milestones and supports supersession (`claude/hooks/event-ledger/note.py:1-42`), while the local SessionStart dispatcher already resurfaces open loops (`claude/hooks/_dispatch/manifest.json:53-59`). The missing behavior is to connect confirmation to those existing rails while execution continues elsewhere.

**How to build.** Extend `note.py`/ledger fields with `status=pending_decision`, `choices`, `depends_on`, `safe_default`, `invalidated_by`, and `resume_action`; extend the existing open-loops resurfacer to render one compact grouped prompt when a decision becomes blocking. Reuse TaskCreate/TaskUpdate where native tasks exist—the corpus shows TaskUpdate has only 0.38% errors across 3,710 calls (`corpus.json:95-100`). Fold this into the current event-ledger/open-loops checks; **no seventh hook**. Wrap parsing/rendering in try/except and exit 0 on its own failure.

**External signal.** A state-machine integration test starts two independent threads plus one approval-dependent thread, restarts/compacts the session, and verifies from ledger JSON and task status that (a) independent work reached terminal receipts, (b) the blocked thread resurfaced exactly once, and (c) the recorded owner choice resumed only its declared dependents. The filesystem/ledger state, not the acting model, is the oracle.

**SOTA.** ProACT targets forgotten constraints, ambiguous goals, and discussion loops while optimizing non-interruptiveness ([arXiv:2607.03730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03730)). Amershi et al. require recent-interaction memory and efficient correction ([CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)).

**Assessment.** value **high** × effort **medium**. Novelty **inferred-surprise**. Autonomy **just-do-it**. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce discussed-then-forgotten decisions because pending state becomes externally durable and replay-tested; evidence is the existing ledger/resurfacer. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce owner stress without increasing prompts because decisions are deduplicated and grouped, but only the shadow prompt-burden metric in Finding 7 can establish that owner-specific effect.

### Finding 4 — Introduce an interruption budget measured in owner decisions, not messages

**Improvement.** Make prompt fatigue a scored cost: one owner interruption may settle several coupled high/irreversible choices, but unrelated decisions must not be bundled into coercive “approve all” prompts.

**What it is.** A confirmation prompt contains only: consequence, irreversibility reason, recommendation, 2–3 mutually exclusive choices, safe default, and what autonomous work continues. Deduplicate identical pending decisions by subject digest. Do not ask for status, permission to run ordinary tests, permission to inspect files, or permission to retry a reversible method. Do not narrate every matrix classification to the owner; store it in the ledger and surface only boundary crossings.

**Why inferred.** AskUserQuestion is already used 1,329 times (`corpus.json:135-140`), but the corpus repeatedly records owner requests for no check-ins and self-operation (`:11837`, `:12377-12384`). The current `/plan` command universally waits for confirmation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/commands/plan.md:1-14`, `:91-96`), while the shared behavior contract says questions should be targeted and EVPI-gated (`claude/AGENTS.md:7-12`). The owner signal implies that the scarce resource is focus, not merely turn count.

**How to build.** Configure the AskUserQuestion wrapper/skill template with a `decision_digest`, `blocking_scope`, and maximum three choices; write one ledger event per actual decision; batch only records sharing the same subject and rollback boundary. Add a per-session report computed offline from existing ledger/transcript records: `decision_interrupts`, `duplicate_prompts`, `questions_for_reversible_actions`, `owner_corrections_after_act`, and `owner_minutes_waited` where timestamps exist. Keep the metric out of prompt context except on explicit review.

**External signal.** Compare a fixed replay set under current policy versus the matrix using independent, blinded annotation of whether each owner interruption was necessary; then shadow-log live `AskUserQuestion` calls and direct owner corrections for two weeks. A lower unnecessary-interruption rate without a higher unauthorized-action correction rate is the acceptance condition.

**SOTA.** PACT's clarification utility explicitly balances assistance accuracy against question frequency ([arXiv:2605.24350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24350)); PRISM explicitly models benefit-versus-burden and false-alarm cost ([arXiv:2602.01532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01532)). Amershi et al. recommend scoping/disambiguation only when in doubt and global user controls over behavior ([Microsoft HAI Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)).

**Assessment.** value **high** × effort **low**. Novelty **inferred-surprise**. Autonomy **just-do-it**. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: **CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**. It is **likely (55-80%)** to lower prompt fatigue because it removes several documented prompt classes and deduplicates the rest; evidence is the owner’s no-check-in instructions and PACT/PRISM. The band is not higher because the corpus lacks clean cognitive-load labels and hook telemetry is explicitly broken (`corpus.json:13260-13264`).

### Finding 5 — Personalize authority from explicit scoped corrections, never from behavioral guesswork

**Improvement.** Add a small, expiry-aware autonomy preference record: “inspect/restart/test/deploy locally without asking; ask only when manual input or the high/irreversible boundary is reached.”

**What it is.** A preference is promotable only from (a) an explicit owner statement, or (b) two mechanically independent signals plus a later owner confirmation. Every record carries scope (project/action class), source transcript line/event ID, `valid_as_of`, expiry/review condition, and counterexamples. “No check-ins” remains scoped to its task unless the owner explicitly globalizes it. A peer, subagent, stop hook, or agent-authored prompt can never grant authority.

**Why inferred.** The direct owner instruction “you monitor everything yourself, and tell me when i need to do manual input” is reusable (`corpus.json:12377-12380`), while the corpus precision warning proves that naive mining misattributes agent-authored prompts (`:10371-10374`). The harness already instructs semantic memory to mark supersession and validity (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/CLAUDE.md:5-13`), so a scoped preference record builds on rather than duplicates basic-memory or continuous-learning-v2.

**How to build.** Define a markdown/JSON schema used by the existing basic-memory and semantic router: `preference`, `scope`, `source_kind=explicit_owner`, `source_receipt`, `valid_as_of`, `expires_on_condition`, `counterexamples`, `last_confirmed`. Seed only the direct local operations preference above after owner review of the wording. The runtime rule retrieves it when the action class matches; otherwise it falls back to the 2×2. Do not train a preference model and do not add vector/KG memory—the existing basic-memory bus remains canonical.

**External signal.** On retrieval, reopen the cited raw owner record through a different tool/context and compare its text and scope to the stored preference. On the first materially broader use, AskUserQuestion obtains owner confirmation; the stored answer becomes the external settlement. A nightly deterministic checker expires records whose source is missing or whose counterexample is newer.

**SOTA.** Ask Now, Use Later reports that eight frontier agents lag an oracle by at least 62 points on acquiring reusable preferences and identifies acquisition, not recall, as the bottleneck ([arXiv:2605.28108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28108)). Amershi et al. recommend learning from user behavior but adapting cautiously and notifying users about changes ([CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)).

**Assessment.** value **high** × effort **low**. Novelty **inferred-surprise**. Autonomy **confirm-first** for the initial global wording; subsequent in-scope uses are **just-do-it**. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: **NATIVE (Claude Code already does this; we just enable it)** through existing basic-memory retrieval plus the current behavior contract. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce repeated preference explanations without over-broad authority because source scope and expiry are mechanically checked; evidence is the explicit owner record and existing temporal-validity convention. The lower bound reflects that retrieval reliability has not been measured for this new schema.

### Finding 6 — Separate action authority from completion proof and harden the external verifier

**Improvement.** Let the matrix answer “may I act?” and the existing stake-tier receipt system answer “what proves it worked?”; never let one substitute for the other.

**What it is.** A just-do-it decision is not permission to declare success. Routine reversible work needs a claim-specific external receipt; high-stakes reversible work needs subject-bound complementary receipts plus rollback proof; irreversible low-stakes spend needs a quota/spend receipt; high/irreversible work needs approval plus independent outcome verification. Completion remains `SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, or `NOT_ENOUGH_EVIDENCE`—not a self-review paragraph.

**Why inferred.** The corpus records 53 Read retry loops, 14 Bash loops, and six TaskOutput loops (`corpus.json:10297-10331`), while StructuredOutput errors on 22.9% of 3,694 calls (`:103-108`). Tool execution and schema completion are therefore not reliable evidence of objective completion. Audit unit D30 further found that proof tiers exist but are not selected from actual stakes and that a claimant can manufacture an accepted “run/ok” receipt (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/audit-findings/D30.md:7-21`). This finding builds on D30 rather than re-proposing its receipt subsystem.

**How to build.** Add the matrix fields to the existing D30 stake-tier selection: `routine`, `high_reversible`, `low_irreversible`, `high_irreversible`. Bind receipts to the changed subject digest and actor identity; require a distinct verifier/context for the latter two where a truth claim follows. Reuse ClaimWarningModule and the event ledger; fold into the current dispatcher module. No new runtime, model service, or hook. All hook-side failures exit 0 and log `FAILED:<id>`; behavioral safety rules still prevent an unapproved high/irreversible action.

**External signal.** A test corpus includes counterfeit `Write-Output run; Write-Output ok`, stale pre-mutation success, wrong-worktree success, valid post-mutation test, rollback hash, and owner approval. The deterministic receipt evaluator must reject the first three and accept the properly bound cases; a fresh-context verifier independently reopens the exhibit for high-stakes claims.

**SOTA.** The 2024 preregistered meta-analysis found that human-AI combinations underperformed the better member on average and particularly lost on decision tasks, arguing against assuming that adding human confirmation or AI self-review inherently improves outcomes ([Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1)). Lee and See frame appropriate reliance as calibration to system capability and context ([Human Factors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15151155/)).

**Assessment.** value **high** × effort **medium**. Novelty **user-asked** (the external-signal law makes it mandatory). Autonomy **just-do-it** for routine/high-reversible actions with receipts; **confirm-first** for high/irreversible action authority. NATIVE-vs-BUILD: **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**. It is **very likely (80-90%)** to block the documented counterfeit/stale proof cases because they are deterministic fixtures from inspected code; evidence is D30's executable counterexample. Broader completion-accuracy improvement is **likely (55-80%)** because subject binding and independent exhibits address known failure modes, but live incidence is not yet measured.

### Finding 7 — Ship only after corpus replay plus shadow calibration, with owner burden in the score

**Improvement.** Build an offline autonomy-calibration evaluator before changing live behavior. This is the anti-overfit and honesty gate for Findings 1–6.

**What it is.** A bounded benchmark of real workspace operations labeled on `stakes`, `reversibility evidence`, `authority`, `external verifier`, `owner interruption necessary`, and `thread retained`. ## Concrete decision procedure

For every proposed side effect, apply this sequence. The sequence uses external state at every epistemic closure point:

1. **Resolve the subject and authority.** Identify exact target, environment, affected people/systems, and explicit user scope. Read actual state with a tool; do not infer it from the plan.
2. **Score stakes.** High if any high-stakes boundary listed in the executive verdict is plausibly crossed. When evidence conflicts, classify high until an external probe resolves it.
3. **Prove reversibility.** Locate/capture the source state, name the inverse, and obtain a restore receipt in a disposable fixture or from an existing independently verified checkpoint. Without the receipt, mark irreversible.
4. **Route.** Only high + irreversible asks. All other cells act, subject to the de minimis spend cap and existing user scope.
5. **Preserve the thread.** On ask, write decision escrow and continue independent safe work. On act, write the matrix decision and receipt IDs without narrating it to the owner.
6. **Verify outcome externally.** Use execution result, state readback, test/CI, health endpoint, different tool/context, second model on raw evidence, or owner observation. Same-context self-review is never terminal evidence.
7. **Expand only after receipt.** High-stakes reversible action moves from canary to wider scope only when the canary and rollback receipts agree. A failed/missing receipt stops expansion and either rolls back or creates decision escrow.

## Anti-overfit and hook-ceiling gate

All seven findings ride the existing behavior-contract, skill-router, AskUserQuestion, checkpoint, event-ledger, open-loop, Basic Memory, and dispatcher buses. They do **not** add a second orchestration runtime, vector database/knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. They add **zero hooks**: Findings 1, 3, and 6 fold into existing checkpoint/ledger/dispatcher modules; Findings 2 and 4 are configuration/skill policy; Finding 5 uses native Basic Memory; Finding 7 is an offline test/shadow evaluator. This satisfies the ~6-hook ceiling without naming a replaced hook because no seventh is proposed.

Minimality is deliberate. The policy is a 2×2 plus deterministic evidence fields, not a learned risk classifier. PRISM's reported gains make learned gating interesting research, but importing a trained intervention model would be BUILD-LARGE, would require a new evaluation and calibration lifecycle, and would reintroduce a rejected trained-critic/guardrail class. It is therefore rejected here. A small decision table is **very likely (80-90%)** to be easier to audit and less prone to framework overfit in this workspace because each route is recoverable from ledger facts and the harness already has the needed buses; evidence is the current shared dispatcher architecture (`agents/harness/README.md:1-29`) and the extensive failure history of heuristic detectors (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:9-87`).

## Alternatives considered and rejected

* **Ask on every high-stakes action, even reversible ones.** Rejected because it taxes owner focus even when a canary and exercised inverse bound harm. The staged high/reversible cell preserves owner authority over irreversible expansion while allowing productive preparation.
* **Ask on every irreversible action, even de minimis ones.** Rejected because every paid API call and killed rerunnable process would interrupt the owner. The low/irreversible cell is deliberately capped and logged. Its cap must be configured explicitly; silence never authorizes material spend.
* **Let confidence determine autonomy.** Rejected because model confidence does not confer authority and the local harness's confidence gate is not outcome-calibrated (audit claim at `/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/audit-findings/D30.md:71-77`). Confidence selects verification depth; stakes/reversibility select ask versus act.
* **Use the owner-correction miner to learn the policy automatically.** Rejected because its own precision warning reports 41/41 false positives on an unfiltered smoke sample (`corpus.json:10371-10374`). Explicit owner statements can seed scoped preferences; everything else requires independent labeling and external settlement.
* **Add an autonomy hook.** Rejected because it would consume scarce hook budget, duplicate the shared dispatcher, and risk the use-versus-mention false-positive class. Policy belongs in the behavior contract/skill, while receipts belong in existing modules.
* **Have the acting model run a second internal critique.** Rejected by construction under this unit's law. A different prompt in the same context is not an independent signal. Only a different context/tool, execution result, second model on raw evidence, or owner observation closes the loop.
