# D015 — HARNESS-BENCH metric: efficiency

**Unit:** D015  
**Metric:** efficiency — tokens and wall-clock per successful task  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** metric definition and instrumentation design only. The subject trees were read-only. No harness code, configuration, installation, or scheduler state was changed.

## Executive decision

Efficiency must be a **quality-gated, task-level resource metric**, not an agent verbosity metric and not a ratio of output bytes to tokens. The operational primary measures are:

1. **processed tokens per success (TPS)**: every model token consumed by all attempts, including failed attempts and all descendant agents, divided by independently graded successes; and
2. **autonomous wall-clock seconds per success (WPS)**: the end-to-end makespan of every attempt, including failures, retries, tools, queues, hooks, and descendant agents, divided by independently graded successes.

A candidate harness is eligible for an efficiency comparison only after passing a frozen success/evidence non-inferiority gate. Its single summary score is the worse of its token and wall-clock ratios against the reference harness, not an average that lets one resource hide a regression in the other. Raw TPS and WPS remain the authoritative values.

This design does **not** score agents on output volume. A verification agent that writes few kilobytes after reading and checking a large body of evidence receives no low “KB/token” score: there is no agent-level productivity leaderboard and no byte term anywhere in the formula. Its work is part of the enclosing task's evidence contract. It is compared only indirectly, through paired executions of the same task, and only after correctness has been held non-inferior.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that this design will distinguish material harness-efficiency regressions without penalising the corpus's low-KB/token verification agent. Evidence: the formula has no output-volume term; success requires verification appropriate to the task; failed attempts remain costly; and Anthropic's production report says token usage itself explains much of agent performance variance and that multi-agent systems can consume about 15 times chat tokens, making an unconditional “fewer tokens is better” objective unsafe ([Anthropic, “How we built our multi-agent research system”](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The remaining uncertainty is empirical sensitivity on this owner's task distribution; the settling experiment is the paired shadow benchmark in §11 across at least 30 frozen tasks and three seeds per harness variant.

## 1. Grounding: what the available evidence can and cannot measure

### 1.1 The corpus is strong evidence for retry waste, not latency

The corpus miner covered 11,633 files and 10.62 GB, failed on zero files, saw one bad line and 32 oversized lines, and explicitly limits absence claims to what the distilled corpus detects (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). That makes its call and error counts useful ground truth for avoidable attempts. It does **not** currently provide usable tool latency: for example Bash, Read, Edit, PowerShell, Write, WebFetch, Glob, StructuredOutput, and Agent all have `p50_ms: 0` and `p95_ms: 0` in the distilled records (`corpus.json:13-124`). Zero here is missing/non-captured duration, not evidence that tools took zero time.

The waste is large enough that an efficiency metric must charge failures and retries, not examine successful calls alone:

- Bash recorded 108,121 calls with 6,461 errors (`corpus.json:13-20`); the unmatched-quote EOF fingerprint alone occurred 1,631 times (`corpus.json:4279-4296`).
- PowerShell recorded 19,181 calls with 2,031 errors (`corpus.json:39-44`).
- Glob recorded 6,222 calls with 829 errors (`corpus.json:79-84`), while `ENAMETOOLONG: name too long, uv_spawn` occurred 709 times in the fingerprint table (`corpus.json:4316-4332`).
- StructuredOutput recorded 3,694 calls with 846 errors (`corpus.json:103-108`); one over-strict schema mismatch occurred 294 times (`corpus.json:4370-4386`).
- A read-before-write failure occurred 939 times (`corpus.json:4298-4314`), and `ECONNREFUSED` occurred 232 times (`corpus.json:4406-4422`).
- The Tavily extract tool recorded 532 calls, 137 errors, and 135 “not connected” failures (`corpus.json:9141-9148`).

Those failures consume tokens before and after the error and extend the task's elapsed time. Therefore, `sum(resources for all attempts) / successes`, rather than `resources among successful attempts / successes`, is the correct accounting boundary.

### 1.2 Hook timing is a known blind spot and must not be inferred from the corpus

The corpus's `hooks` section explicitly says its detector failed a known-positive control and that the entire section is no data (`corpus.json:13260-13264`). The live-defects input independently reports zero `hookName`/`hook_event_name` records despite a hook demonstrably firing and specifies two distinct searches needed to determine where hook telemetry lives (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-147`). I therefore make no claim from the corpus about hook incidence or cost.

Separate native traces and source inspection from audit unit A07 are claims from a prior unit, but they are directly relevant hypotheses: a SessionStart hook was measured at p50 50.970 seconds, a local Stop dispatcher at p50 10.422 seconds, and ordinary PreToolUse plus PostToolUse imports were estimated near a 0.42-second warm floor (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/audit-findings/A07.md:1-18,39-45`). A07 also reports that the existing dispatcher timer begins after imports and stops before terminal append (`A07.md:39-45`). The unpacked source confirms the boundary: `HarnessDispatcher` starts `perf_counter()` inside `dispatch` after construction (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/agents/harness/dispatcher.py:235-252`), records elapsed before returning (`dispatcher.py:331-338`), and constructs the dispatcher only later in the CLI entry point (`dispatcher.py:530-547`). This is two mechanically different signals—prior native timing plus current source boundary—for the narrower conclusion that internal `elapsed_ms` is not full host wall-clock.

**Confidence:** **almost certain (90-99%)** that measuring only the existing dispatcher's `elapsed_ms` would undercount user-visible harness time. Evidence: the timer is instantiated after module import and dispatcher construction and is written before process exit (`dispatcher.py:235-252,331-338,530-560`), while A07 separately measured import time (`A07.md:39-45`).

### 1.3 The installed “harness-bench” is not an efficiency bench and is statically false-green

The existing README defines the weekly bench as a semantic-router recall regression net, not an end-task harness benchmark (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/harness-bench/README.md:1-9`). These are independent static signals: incompatible real interface and fake-only integration seam.

The current scheduled wrapper's success must not be used as the efficiency metric's success signal. Fixing that existing contract is a prerequisite to reusing its low-frequency scheduling/digest bus.

## 2. Unit of measurement and immutable boundaries

### 2.1 Benchmark item

A **benchmark item** `j` is an immutable task manifest containing:

- `task_id` and manifest content hash;
- task stratum (for example local edit, multi-file bug, tool/MCP research, evidence-heavy verification, long-running external job);
- clean starting snapshot or fixture hash;
- prompt and allowed affordances;
- maximum autonomous wall time;
- predeclared success/evidence contract;
- criticality (`ordinary` or `critical`); and
- fixed suite weight `w_j`, chosen before candidate results are seen.

Task boundaries cannot be inferred afterward from conversational prose. A benchmark launcher creates `run_id`, starts the root process, supplies `HARNESS_BENCH_TASK_ID` and `HARNESS_BENCH_RUN_ID`, and owns the stopwatch. All root and descendant work carrying that run ID belongs to the attempt.

### 2.2 Attempt

An **attempt** `a=(h,j,s)` is one harness variant `h`, one item `j`, and one fixed seed/order replicate `s`. A retry after process failure is either part of the same attempt if the harness initiates it, or a new predeclared replicate if the benchmark runner initiates it. The harness may not silently reset the resource counters.

The attempt begins immediately before the agent process is spawned. It ends when the root process and every registered descendant have terminated, or at the item timeout. The independent grader runs afterward; grader time and grader tokens are reported separately and excluded from agent TPS/WPS so a change to the grading implementation cannot masquerade as a harness regression. Stop hooks and teardown before process termination are included.

Interactive human turns are forbidden in the scored offline lane. An attempt that asks for authority or clarification not supplied by its manifest fails as an autonomous attempt. Production telemetry may record interactive work, but it is reported separately and never mixed into the scored offline distribution.

### 2.3 Successful task

For attempt `a`, define:

`S_a = 1` only if **all** predeclared hard checks pass; otherwise `S_a = 0`.

Hard checks are task-class-specific and established before the run:

- **Code/change tasks:** isolated grader tests pass; scope/diff invariant passes; required fresh test/build receipt post-dates the last mutation; and no forbidden state change occurs.
- **Research/verification tasks:** required claims are supported by reachable cited evidence; a fixed blinded claim sample passes source-entailment checks; required known-positive and known-negative controls are present; and the artifact contract passes. There is no word, byte, finding-count, or citation-count reward.
- **Operational tasks:** the external target state and independent readback agree, with idempotency/rollback requirements where the manifest calls for them.
- **Exploratory tasks with no independently testable outcome:** telemetry is retained, but the item is **unscored**. It cannot contribute a “success” to the denominator.

The agent's terminal phrase, exit code, or self-assigned confidence is not success. The known harness defect in which contradictory envelopes can produce pass-shaped receipts (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/audit-findings/F08.md:15-21`) is why a receipt alone is insufficient.

Before a grader is trusted, it must demonstrate two mechanically independent signals and controls:

1. run a known-pass and known-fail fixture and produce opposite outcomes; and
2. for a scored run, reconcile the grader result against an independent state signal (for example test process exit **and** expected artifact/state invariant; citation reachability **and** source-entailment sample).

A critical item has zero tolerance: any critical regression makes the candidate ineligible regardless of aggregate score.

## 3. Exact token formula

For each model request `m` attributable to attempt `a`, define provider-normalised processed tokens `p_m`:

```text
Claude:
  p_m = input_tokens
      + cache_creation_input_tokens
      + cache_read_input_tokens
      + output_tokens

OpenAI/Codex:
  p_m = input_tokens + output_tokens
```

For OpenAI, `input_tokens` already includes cached input; cached tokens remain a detail rather than an added term. Reasoning tokens are included in `output_tokens` and retained as a detail, not added twice. The OpenAI Usage API documents input tokens as including cached tokens and exposes cached input separately ([OpenAI Usage API reference](https://platform.openai.com/docs/api-reference/usage/audio_transcriptions_object)); the OpenAI Agents SDK aggregates input/output usage across model calls, including tool calls and handoffs, and preserves request-level cached/reasoning details ([OpenAI Agents SDK usage](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/usage/)). Claude Code's native monitoring schema exposes input, output, cache-read, cache-creation, request attempt, success, and request duration fields ([Claude Code monitoring](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage)).

Then:

```text
P_a = sum(p_m for every model request by the root and all descendants in attempt a)
```

Properties:

- Replayed context is counted each time the provider processes it.
- Cached tokens are counted as tokens because this metric is capacity/processing efficiency, not current-dollar price efficiency.
- Root and subagent tokens are summed. Parallelism cannot make token work disappear.
- Failed requests count if the provider reports usage. Retries count separately.
- Grader-model tokens, if any, are recorded under `grader_tokens` and excluded from `P_a`.
- Tool output bytes and final artifact bytes are diagnostics only; they are not tokens unless subsequently placed in a model request and reported by the provider.
- A provider without exact usage makes the attempt telemetry-incomplete; token counts are never estimated from file size or a tokenizer from another model.

For a set of attempts `A`, define:

```text
K(A)   = sum(S_a for a in A)
TPS(A) = sum(P_a for a in A) / K(A), if K(A) > 0
         +infinity, otherwise
```

All failed-attempt tokens remain in the numerator. This is deliberately different from the conditional mean `E[P | success]`, which would make cheap repeated failure look efficient.

## 4. Exact wall-clock formula

The primary autonomous wall clock uses the outer benchmark runner's monotonic clock:

```text
W_a = min(t_all_processes_terminal, t_timeout) - t_root_spawn_start
```

where `t_all_processes_terminal` is reached only after the root and all run-ID descendants terminate. If the item times out, `W_a` is the full timeout and `S_a=0`. `W_a` includes:

- model latency and provider retries;
- tool and MCP execution;
- dead-server connection attempts;
- permission/harness checks in the scored noninteractive profile;
- hook processes, imports, lock contention, and Stop/teardown hooks;
- waits for descendant agents and coordination; and
- harness-initiated repair/retry loops.

It does not sum parallel branch durations. It is makespan: the user waits for the critical path once. Tokens separately charge every branch.

For attempts `A`:

```text
WPS(A) = sum(W_a for a in A) / K(A), if K(A) > 0
         +infinity, otherwise
```

Again, failed attempts consume wall time in the numerator. Also report successful-attempt p50/p95, timeout rate, time-to-first-tool, model-wait, tool-wait, and hook-wait as diagnostics; none replaces WPS.

METR explicitly distinguishes human task duration (a difficulty proxy) from the AI agent's own completion time and notes that the latter varies by provider and harness ([METR Time Horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). Therefore, human-duration estimates may stratify task difficulty but must not substitute for `W_a`.

## 5. Comparison across heterogeneous tasks

Raw suite-wide TPS and WPS are always published. For the headline comparison, use fixed strata so a long research task does not erase all signal from short local tasks and so candidates cannot alter task mix.

For stratum `q` and harness `h`:

```text
TPS_hq = sum(P_a for attempts in q under h) / sum(S_a for those attempts)
WPS_hq = sum(W_a for attempts in q under h) / sum(S_a for those attempts)
```

If a harness has zero successes in any required stratum, both ratios for that stratum are infinite and the summary efficiency score is zero.

Against a frozen reference harness `r`, with stratum weights `w_q > 0` and `sum(w_q)=1` fixed before execution:

```text
R_token = exp(sum_q w_q * ln(TPS_hq / TPS_rq))
R_wall  = exp(sum_q w_q * ln(WPS_hq / WPS_rq))

EfficiencyScore(h | r) = 100 / max(R_token, R_wall)
```

Interpretation:

- `100` means no change on the worse resource ratio.
- `>100` means both weighted token and wall burdens improved.
- `<100` means at least one burden regressed.
- The `max` is deliberate: a 50% wall improvement cannot buy permission for a 10x token increase, and vice versa.

Publish the two ratios beside the scalar. Do not rank candidates whose confidence intervals overlap materially; mark them tied/inconclusive.

### Quality eligibility gate

Efficiency is considered only if all of the following are true:

1. no critical task regresses;
2. the lower bound of a predeclared 95% paired bootstrap interval for `success_rate_candidate - success_rate_reference` is at least `-0.02` (two percentage points);
3. every scored success satisfies the same evidence contract;
4. telemetry completeness is at least 99.5% for both candidates; and
5. the candidate does not change task manifests, graders, timeouts, model identity, seed set, or allowed affordances.

The two-point margin is a starting policy, not a discovered constant. **UNVERIFIED — the experiment that settles whether two percentage points is suitably sensitive is a retrospective power analysis over at least 100 paired attempts drawn from the frozen owner-task suite.** Until that experiment, the recommendation heading is:

### UNVERIFIED recommendation: use a 2-point non-inferiority margin initially

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Store the margin in the benchmark manifest and version it; do not tune it after seeing candidate outcomes.

**Confidence:** **roughly even (45-55%)** that `2%` is the right operating margin. Evidence: it is strict enough to make success primary but the available corpus contains tool events, not repeated externally graded end tasks, so it cannot estimate task-level variance. The power-analysis experiment above settles it.

## 6. Why this does not penalise the low-KB/token verification agent

The owner's warning is satisfied structurally, not by an exception bolted onto the score:

1. **No output-volume numerator.** There is no KB, words, findings, diffs, lines, or citations-produced term.
2. **No per-agent leaderboard.** Root, verifier, researcher, and implementer resources roll up to the task. The harness is evaluated, not individual rhetorical output.
3. **Same-task pairing.** Evidence-heavy tasks are compared with the same manifests and evidence contracts under reference and candidate harnesses; they are not compared to short code edits.
4. **Verification is part of success.** Removing the verifier or shortening its work helps only if the external result remains equally correct and evidenced. If the verifier prevents false completion, its tokens buy denominator successes rather than count as “unproductive prose.”
5. **Failures are charged.** A terse agent that produces an unsupported answer has `S=0`; its few tokens do not create a good TPS.
6. **Unscorable exploration is excluded.** The benchmark does not pretend that output size is a surrogate for value.

Example:

```text
Verifier A: 80k tokens, 4 KB report, passes 9/10 tasks
Verbose A:  40k tokens, 40 KB report, passes 4/10 tasks

Across ten equal attempts:
Verifier A TPS = 800k / 9  = 88.9k tokens/success
Verbose A TPS  = 400k / 4 = 100k tokens/success
```

The verifier wins despite being ten times worse on KB/token. More importantly, production comparison is paired by task and reports uncertainty; the example is explanatory, not a universal weighting claim.

## 7. Instrumentation design and attachment points

### 7.1 Authoritative offline collector

### UNVERIFIED proposal: outer-runner task envelope

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).** Extend the existing low-frequency benchmark launcher rather than create a daemon. The launcher writes append-only JSONL outside the subject worktree and performs four actions:

## 8. Instrumentation validation and missing-data policy

Telemetry failure must never improve the score.

### Known-positive controls

Before any comparison:

1. **Token control:** one fixed model request with nonzero input/output. Detector A is transcript/rollout usage; detector B is native telemetry/provider usage. Both must find it and agree within documented field semantics.
2. **Wall control:** a fixture with a deterministic bounded tool wait. Detector A is outer monotonic process duration; detector B is native span/process timestamps. Both must show at least the injected duration within tolerance.
3. **Child control:** one root plus one child, each producing a unique usage marker. Root total must equal the sum of both without duplication.
4. **Failure control:** one deliberate tool failure followed by a successful repair and one terminal task failure. All consumed resources must remain present; only the correct attempt may contribute to `K`.
5. **Grader control:** known-pass and known-fail artifacts plus a tampered/fake receipt. The grader must reject the latter.

### Completeness rules

- Every model usage record has a stable request/usage ID; duplicate IDs are deduplicated exactly once, with conflicts failing reconciliation.
- Every run has one start, one terminal/timeout, one grade, a manifest hash, provider/model identity, and closed descendant set.
- Clock reversal, missing terminal, missing usage, unresolved child, schema error, or grader exception marks the run `telemetry_incomplete=true` and `S=0` for operational reporting.
- A harness variant below 99.5% complete runs is **ineligible**, not assigned an optimistic imputation.
- Raw incomplete-run count and reasons are published next to TPS/WPS.
- Provider aggregate reconciliation is an audit check, not a replacement for task attribution.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that this missing-data policy prevents the most direct “delete expensive telemetry” Goodhart attack. Evidence: missing records remove eligibility and do not lower either numerator. The residual risk is correlated loss that affects reference and candidate differently but passes completeness; the settling experiment is fault injection at each event-write boundary and forced process termination during start, usage, end, and grade append.

## 9. Failure modes

| Failure mode | Bias if ignored | Detection / treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Cached-token semantics differ by provider | double-count or omit cache reads | provider-specific formulas; retain raw fields; known-positive reconciliation |
| Usage record repeated after resume/compaction | token inflation | stable request/usage-ID dedupe; conflicting duplicate is incomplete |
| Descendant not linked to root | undercounts fan-out | inherited run ID plus native/transcript lineage; unresolved child fails completeness |
| Child continues after root reports done | wall undercount | outer process-tree/registered-child closure, not root Stop timestamp |
| Hook timer begins after imports | hides harness overhead | outer monotonic timer is authoritative; native hook spans diagnostic |
| Parallel branches | summing duration overstates user wait | wall uses makespan; tokens sum branches |
| Provider retry reports one final request only | token undercount | retain request `attempt`; reconcile provider/native totals; incomplete if attempts cannot be accounted |
| Human response delay | overwhelms harness effect | no interactive turns in scored lane; production interactive telemetry separate |
| Grader runtime changes | apparent wall regression | grader duration/tokens separate from agent WPS/TPS; version/hash grader |
| False-green receipt or exit 0 | inflates success denominator | independent grader plus state/content signal and pass/fail controls |
| Timeout/hang | disappears from successful mean | full timeout in numerator, `S=0` |
| Task batching/redefinition | makes tasks look cheaper | immutable manifest IDs/hashes and fixed boundaries |
| Warm cache/order effects | unfair candidate advantage | paired seeds, counterbalanced order, cold/warm strata reported separately |
| Machine contention | wall noise | randomized interleaving, host/load metadata, repeated seeds, confidence intervals |
| Tool output avoids model context | token metric misses external work | WPS catches latency; report calls, bytes, and dollars as diagnostics |
| Prices/models change | incomparable cost | pin model/provider; raw tokens primary; dollars secondary with price-table version |
| Telemetry sink blocks hot path | observer increases WPS | append-only bounded local writes; benchmark runner owns heavy aggregation |

## 10. Goodhart analysis

### G1. “Do less verification”

**Perverse behaviour:** remove test runs, evidence reads, negative controls, or verifier agents to save tokens/time.

**Countermeasure:** success is external and evidence-gated before efficiency. Critical regressions veto the candidate; no output volume is rewarded. A shortened verifier is a win only if the same independent evidence contract still passes.

**Residual risk:** **likely (55-80%)** that a finite frozen suite will miss some real verification failures. Evidence: all finite test suites have coverage limits, and the local receipt path has already exhibited contradictory success signals (`F08.md:15-21`). The settling experiment is delayed blinded regrading of a random 10% of “successful” artifacts plus rotating held-out tasks; any discovered false success retroactively sets `S=0` and triggers a grader review.

### G2. Cherry-pick easy tasks or redefine “task”

**Perverse behaviour:** split one job into many trivial successes, omit hard strata, or rerun only favourable seeds.

**Countermeasure:** immutable task manifests, fixed stratum weights, all scheduled attempts included, manifest hashes, and no exploratory item in the denominator.

### G3. Fail fast and often

**Perverse behaviour:** cheap failures can look good if only successful attempts are averaged.

**Countermeasure:** all attempt tokens and wall time are divided by the number of successes. Failed attempts increase burden and never increase the denominator.

### G4. Trade tokens for wall with uncontrolled fan-out

**Perverse behaviour:** spawn many agents to improve makespan while exploding tokens, or serialize everything to save tokens while making the owner wait.

**Countermeasure:** root-plus-descendant token sum, makespan wall time, and `max(R_token,R_wall)` summary. Both dimensions must improve for a score over 100.

### G5. Game caching

**Perverse behaviour:** stuff huge prefixes into caches, claim low billed tokens/cost, or run the candidate second to inherit warm state.

**Countermeasure:** processed tokens count cached input; cold and warm strata are counterbalanced; current-dollar cost is a secondary diagnostic. Cache creation/read fields remain visible.

### G6. Suppress telemetry

**Perverse behaviour:** lose child records or expensive requests.

**Countermeasure:** incomplete telemetry blocks eligibility; known-positive controls and two-source reconciliation precede absence claims.

### G7. Optimize to the grader

**Perverse behaviour:** hard-code fixture answers, produce pass-shaped receipts, or exploit a narrow rubric.

**Countermeasure:** held-out rotating tasks, grader hashes, independent state checks, tampered-receipt controls, and delayed audit. The benchmark never auto-promotes a harness from score alone.

### G8. Minimize answer usefulness because output tokens count

**Perverse behaviour:** terse, incomplete answers save output tokens.

**Countermeasure:** artifact/output size is not rewarded, but contract completeness is required for `S=1`. For user-facing tasks, a fixed rubric checks required decisions, evidence, and actionable handoff—not length.

### G9. Hide cost in tools or humans

**Perverse behaviour:** replace model reasoning with unmetered expensive APIs, GPU jobs, or human clarification.

**Countermeasure:** no humans in the scored lane; record tool calls, external billed dollars/credits, CPU/GPU time where exposed, and byte volume as diagnostic vetoes. WPS captures latency but not all monetary cost, so a separate cost ceiling is necessary for deployments even though dollars are not this unit's primary metric.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that the paired quality gate plus two non-substitutable resource axes materially reduces the Goodhart pressure of a naked “tokens/task” target. Evidence: every direct shortcut above either loses success eligibility or appears in one of the axes. It does not eliminate benchmark overfitting; held-out rotation is still required.

## 11. Evaluation and promotion protocol

### Proposal: paired shadow benchmark before any enforcement or pruning

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).** Use the existing benchmark bus to run reference and candidate harnesses in isolated, identical snapshots. Do not add a second orchestrator, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Protocol:

1. Freeze manifests, graders, variants, models, provider, timeout, seed list, and weights.
2. Include at minimum the major observed waste classes: quoting failure, read-before-write, long-path/plugin discovery, over-strict structured output, dead MCP/fallback routing, connection refusal, ordinary edit/test, multi-file fix, evidence-heavy verification, and child-agent research.
3. Include a known-positive and known-negative instrumentation control in every batch.
4. Run reference/candidate in randomized counterbalanced order on the same host class; use at least three seeds per item initially.
5. Grade blind to harness variant.
6. Apply critical-task and success non-inferiority gates.
7. Publish TPS, WPS, successful p50/p95 wall, timeout/error/retry rates, R_token, R_wall, score, confidence intervals, completeness, tool-call diagnostics, and grader audit results.
8. Promote only when quality is eligible, both resource ratios improve beyond noise, no diagnostic veto fires, and a held-out batch agrees.
9. Run a post-promotion canary and retain instant config rollback.

**Confidence:** **likely (55-80%)** that 30 tasks × 3 seeds will detect large regressions such as seconds-per-hook or repeated dead-tool loops, because those effects are much larger than millisecond noise in the cited local evidence. **UNVERIFIED — it may be underpowered for 2-5% changes; the settling experiment is sequential power analysis after the first paired batch, using observed paired variance without inspecting which variant won.**

## 12. Anti-overfit gate and architectural fit

All proposals above ride existing buses:

- the outer wrapper extends the existing scheduled benchmark lane;
- run correlation extends the normalized shared hook schema;
- append-only records reuse the event-ledger pattern;
- native telemetry is optional configuration and reconciliation;
- the digest remains the bounded human-facing notification path.

No rejected component is reintroduced. In particular, this design has no second orchestration runtime, vector database/KG memory, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or generic guardrail framework. Aggregation is an offline function over JSONL, not a daemon. The simple architecture is deliberate: resource measurement wraps the agent loop; it does not replace or elaborate the loop.

The metric also resists harness overgrowth: every proposed hook/module must demonstrate end-task TPS/WPS improvement under non-inferior success. A hook that adds 200 ms to every call but prevents costly retries can still win; one that merely emits prose cannot claim value from output volume.

## 13. What not to use

Reject these candidate metrics:

- **KB/token or findings/token:** directly penalises verification and rewards verbosity/output padding.
- **tokens only among successful runs:** hides retry/failure waste.
- **wall only among successful runs:** hides timeouts and encourages uncontrolled parallel token burn.
- **$/success as the primary metric:** price tables, cache discounts, subscriptions, and model routing change; retain it as a versioned diagnostic.
- **tool calls/success:** one call can range from a local read to a long external research job; useful only diagnostically.
- **agent turns/success:** turns do not equal wall time; the live-defects input records a prior false conclusion caused by using turn count instead of a 20m35s wall wait (`INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-118`).
- **self-reported completion:** vulnerable to false-green receipts and unsupported claims.
- **one blended additive score:** permits compensating a catastrophic token increase with a wall improvement or vice versa.
- **human task-duration horizon:** useful for difficulty stratification, not the agent's actual resource efficiency.

## 16. Final recommendation

Adopt `TPS = all processed root+descendant tokens / independently graded successes` and `WPS = all attempt makespan seconds / independently graded successes` as the authoritative efficiency pair. Gate comparison on frozen evidence-based success, charge failed attempts, forbid agent/output-volume rankings, and summarize only with `100 / max(R_token, R_wall)` against a frozen paired reference. Collect authoritative wall time at the outer benchmark process boundary, collect exact provider-native usage, use the existing hook/ledger buses only for correlation and diagnostics, and make missing telemetry ineligible rather than cheap.

**Confidence:** **very likely (80-90%)** that this is a materially safer efficiency objective than tokens/task, wall/task, KB/token, or a blended additive score. Evidence: it directly incorporates the corpus's retry waste, captures the measured hook-critical-path hypothesis, holds verification quality constant, charges fan-out on tokens and makespan on wall, and makes telemetry loss non-beneficial. The paired shadow experiment and held-out audit remain necessary before promotion.
