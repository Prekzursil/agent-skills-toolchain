# M05 — Trust / autonomy calibration: the audit trail

**Unit:** M05, partner-dimension / trust-autonomy calibration  
**Question:** What must be recorded for the owner to trust more autonomy, and what is bureaucracy?  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Mode:** research and design only. The unpacked subject was read-only; this artifact is the only write.

## Executive conclusion

The best audit trail here is not a larger transcript. It is a compact chain of **decision-and-outcome receipts**: what objective authorized a consequential action; which thread it served; what risk/reversibility class it occupied; what was actually changed; which mechanically independent result verified the outcome; and how the owner later corrected, accepted, reverted, or ignored it. Everything else should be available by reference, not pushed into the owner's attention.

I assess this direction as **very likely (80-90%)** to increase warranted autonomy while reducing audit fatigue. The evidence is (1) the corpus contains very high tool volume—108,121 Bash calls alone—so narration at tool-call granularity cannot be the owner's useful interface (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:13-20`); (2) completion/failure claims have repeatedly been wrong when based on one detector (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:111-129`); (3) the existing append-only event ledger already supplies a minimal durable bus (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:2-13`, `:230-287`); and (4) research on appropriate reliance treats calibrated use, not maximized trust, as the goal ([Lee & See, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392); [Schemmer et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3581641.3584066)).

The audit interface should answer five questions without requiring the owner to reconstruct a session:

1. **Why was this action authorized?** Objective/thread id, authority source, constraints, and autonomy tier.
2. **What happened in the world?** Target, mutation digest, external tool result, artifact/commit/test reference.
3. **Why is the result believed?** Independent verifier receipts, including known-positive controls for absence/broken/done claims.
4. **Can it be undone?** Checkpoint/rollback handle, scope, and expiry.
5. **What remains open?** Every discussed commitment has a state and can close only with an external receipt or explicit owner disposition.

The following are bureaucracy and should **not** be required: full chain-of-thought; a prose explanation for every tool call; duplicated raw tool output; full prompt bodies or secrets; owner confirmation for reversible in-scope actions; same-context “I checked my work” assertions; repeated unchanged status; cryptographic tamper-evidence without an adversarial-local-writer threat model; or a second orchestration/memory/observability runtime.

## Grounding and evidence discipline

The corpus is a strong count source: it distilled 10.62 GB, saw 11,633 files, failed on zero files, and reports one bad line, while warning that samples are capped (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:2-11`). It is not a ground-truth label set for owner corrections: its 2,268 candidates are explicitly recall-biased, and 41/41 in an unfiltered smoke sample were false positives (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10371-10374`). I therefore use correction candidates only as a reason to demand verified labels, never as proof of what the owner meant.

The corpus's `hooks` section is **NO DATA**, not evidence of no hook activity, because its own known-positive control failed (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:13260-13264`). Separately and mechanically independently, live-operations evidence says hooks demonstrably fired while the miner found no `hookName`/`hook_event_name` records (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-146`). These two signals establish that present audit coverage is not measurable from transcripts; they do not establish where all hook telemetry went.

The local design already contains the right primitives and must be evolved, not duplicated:

- The event ledger is append-only and typed, with `turn`, `decision`, `failure`, `goal`, `milestone`, `selection`, and `note` events (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:61-65`) and an authoritative JSONL before its derived state projection (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:258-284`).
- The ledger's Stop path currently records only HEAD, branch, dirty-file count, cwd, and session fragment (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/stop-hook.py:76-99`). That is useful recovery state, not yet an autonomy audit receipt.
- Recovery already replays goal, open failures, HEAD drift, notable decisions, and a turn timeline (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/sessionstart-replay.py:31-63`).
- Edit checkpoints already snapshot once per human turn and are silent/fail-open (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/edit-checkpoints/checkpoint.py:2-19`), with indexed restore records (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/edit-checkpoints/checkpoint.py:107-144`).
- Contextual open loops already surface user-authored intentions, one-shot or throttled, and never block (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/trigger_match.py:2-28`, `:90-136`).
- Calibrated silence already uses external next-turn behavior rather than agent self-judgment (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:39-53`), but its reaction classifier can label any unmatched next turn as “ignored” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/log_reaction.py:143-151`). That heuristic is not sufficient evidence for promotion of autonomy.

### Hook-budget and anti-overfit gate

All eight findings below use **zero new hook processes**. They fold into the existing shared dispatcher, local dispatcher, event-ledger, checkpoint, open-loop, and calibrated-silence code paths. The live configuration already consolidates local PreToolUse and PostToolUse work through `_dispatch/dispatch.py` (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json:250-259`, `:344-352`) and Stop work through two dispatchers (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/settings.json:484-503`). No proposal adds a seventh hook, daemon, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, guardrail framework, or observability SaaS. This passes the anti-overfit gate: the design is a few receipt fields and small projections riding the existing bus.

## Findings — inferred surprises first

### 1. Earn autonomy per action class, never as a global “trust level”

**Improvement:** Create an **Autonomy Promotion Matrix** whose unit is `(action_class, target_scope, risk_band, reversibility)`—for example `edit:tracked-source:low:checkpointed`, `deploy:staging:medium:rollback`, or `message:external:high:irreversible`. Global trust is too coarse: a system can be excellent at tracked edits and poor at MCP liveness or shell quoting in the same history. The corpus demonstrates this heterogeneity: ToolSearch is 0.08% error (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:87-92`), while StructuredOutput is 22.9% (`:102-108`), Glob is 13.32% (`:79-84`), and a Tavily endpoint is 25.75% (`:174-180`).

**What it is:** Each class has an autonomy tier: `observe`, `propose`, `act+report`, or `act+sample-audit`. Promotion requires a minimum number of **verified outcome receipts**, a bounded severe-failure count, and a working rollback path. Demotion is immediate on an owner correction, rollback, independent verifier failure, or authority violation. Success is never inferred from the agent's own final text.

**Why inferred:** The owner asks for more autonomy but also mandates two independent signals and same-context self-correction rejection. Those demands imply that trust must attach to demonstrated behavior, not persona or eloquence. The local corpus's wide reliability spread is the workspace-specific signal.

**How to build:** Extend the event-ledger event schema with `action_class`, `scope`, `risk`, `reversibility`, `authority_ref`, `outcome`, `severity`, and `verifier_receipt_ids`. Add a dependency-free projection, approximately 150 lines, that calculates per-class counts only from closed receipts. Store thresholds in one JSON config; suggested initial policy is 10 verified low-risk successes and zero severe failures before `act+report`, then random 10% external audits. Treat these as starting priors, not facts. **UNVERIFIED:** the exact 10/10% operating point; settle it with a four-week replay/shadow experiment measuring owner reversals, verifier failures, and confirmation count under several thresholds before enabling promotion.

**External signal that verifies it:** A receipt closes only on an execution result plus a mechanically independent postcondition—for example process exit plus test artifact, API receipt plus independent read-back, or git mutation plus test. Promotion/demotion consumes those external outcomes and explicit owner correction/rollback events. A second model may verify a semantic claim, but cannot substitute for an execution result when one exists.

**SOTA:** Trust calibration research frames the objective as appropriate reliance, and the classic levels-of-automation model separates information acquisition, analysis, decision selection, and action implementation instead of treating autonomy as one switch ([Lee & See, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392); [Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354)). Recent mixed-initiative work likewise centers dynamic contribution and appropriate reliance ([Gombolay, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i28.35220)).

**Assessment:** **very likely (80-90%)** to improve calibrated autonomy, because the corpus provides large, action-specific outcome samples and shows strongly different error rates; exact thresholds remain UNVERIFIED by the experiment above.  
**Value × effort:** **transformative × medium**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **confirm-first** for the initial policy and promotion thresholds; subsequent evidence-based low-risk promotions can be **just-do-it** under that approved policy.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 2. Replace narrative logs with causal “action receipts” joined to raw evidence

**Improvement:** Make the audit trail a causal receipt graph, not a transcript summary. One consequential action gets one compact record with references to raw evidence; routine reads/searches remain unrecorded unless they are the verifier for a load-bearing claim.

**What it is:** The minimum receipt is: `receipt_id`, time, actor/session, objective id, thread id, authority source, action class, target/scope, risk/reversibility, pre-state digest/checkpoint id, sanitized command/tool-input digest, raw tool-result reference and status, mutation/artifact digest, verifier receipt ids, confidence band, and terminal disposition (`verified`, `reverted`, `corrected`, `owner-accepted`, `expired`). It explicitly does **not** store hidden reasoning or duplicate full output. Sensitive payloads are redacted and content-addressed; the ledger retains a digest and local reference.

**Why inferred:** The event ledger intentionally preserves objective events rather than lossy self-summary (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:2-13`), but its current Stop snapshot cannot answer why an action was allowed or what verified it (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/stop-hook.py:76-99`). The owner needs inspectability without another prompt-taxing narrative.

**How to build:** Add `action` and `verification` kinds (or use typed `note` events only if schema compatibility demands it), a receipt builder inside the existing dispatcher, and a `ledger receipt <id>` view. Keep raw tool results where they already live; record offsets/digests, not copies. Bind checkpoints by their existing `turn` and `restore` values. Add fixture tests for redaction, missing raw references, cross-session ids, and corrupt JSONL. The whole change can remain below roughly 200 lines if rendering is a CLI projection rather than a new UI.

**External signal that verifies it:** A standalone verifier process must resolve every referenced artifact, recompute hashes, and compare pre/post state. For “done,” require both the original execution receipt and an independent postcondition receipt. For “absent/broken,” also require the detector to find a known-present control, exactly as the local rule requires (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/rules/common/single-signal-verification.md:20-37`).

**SOTA:** NIST's AI RMF makes accountability/transparency and traceable, documented risk management first-class rather than equating explanation with trust ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)). Microsoft’s 18 human-AI interaction guidelines were evaluated across 20 products and emphasize supporting efficient invocation, dismissal, correction, and scoped expectations over continuous explanation ([Amershi et al., 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)).

**Assessment:** **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce reconstruction effort, because it extends an existing authoritative append path and binds claims to independently resolvable evidence; the magnitude of owner time saved is **UNVERIFIED**, settled by timing five real incident reconstructions using receipts versus transcripts.  
**Value × effort:** **high × medium**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for schema/projection implementation; confirm before changing retention/redaction policy.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 3. Treat every discussed commitment as escrow: it cannot disappear or self-close

**Improvement:** Turn `OPEN-THREADS.md` and open-loops into a **Commitment Escrow** with a small state machine. The new element is not reminders; those exist. It is externally evidenced closure and provenance across session boundaries.

**What it is:** A thread record contains `thread_id`, exact originating user/message reference, normalized commitment, owner, scope, state (`proposed`, `accepted`, `active`, `blocked`, `deferred`, `done`, `rejected`), next trigger, last external receipt, and `close_authority`. `done` requires an independent postcondition receipt. `deferred`/`rejected` requires the owner's explicit disposition or a cited superseding instruction. Silence, compaction, or the agent's own “done” prose never closes it.

**Why inferred:** “Never drop a thread” is a hard requirement. The corpus's candidate patterns include repeated “why did you stop” and “proceed until you finish” forms (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:10424-10430`, `:10564-10570`), but because the candidate set is recall-biased these strings are supporting context, not verified owner labels. Independently, the ledger already retains goals and open failures (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/event-ledger/ledger.py:272-283`), and open-loops already maintains one-shot/recurring triggers (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/open-loops/trigger_match.py:9-20`). The missing join is commitment state plus evidence-gated closure.

**How to build:** Extend `open-loops/_common.py` records and `event-ledger` projections rather than creating a new store. Add `--accept`, `--defer`, `--block`, and `--close --receipt <id>` to the existing CLI. On SessionStart recovery, inject only active/deferred threads whose trigger matches; at Stop, show only state deltas or a single count (`3 open; 1 newly blocked`), never the full list. A reconciliation test should seed a known thread, compact/replay, and prove the same id survives; a second test must attempt self-closure without a receipt and prove rejection.

**External signal that verifies it:** Closure requires a referenced test/API/artifact receipt checked by a different process, or an owner message id for disposition. A daily independent reconciler compares the ledger projection with `OPEN-THREADS.md`; disagreement produces one anomaly, not two competing truths.

**SOTA:** Human-AI interaction guidance stresses remembering recent interactions and enabling efficient correction ([Amershi et al., 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)). Mixed-initiative systems depend on bi-directional communication and shared awareness of who contributes what ([Gombolay, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i28.35220)).

**Assessment:** **almost certain (90-99%)** to prevent silent thread loss if every closure path enforces receipt/owner disposition; the evidence is the explicit state-machine invariant plus independent projection reconciliation. Whether delta-only surfacing is the least fatiguing presentation is **UNVERIFIED**; settle it with owner comparison of three real weekly digests.  
**Value × effort:** **transformative × medium**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for capture and replay; **confirm-first** for deferral/rejection of an owner commitment.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 4. Put inherited “facts” and constraints into provenance escrow

**Improvement:** Every load-bearing fact passed into a fan-out prompt gets a **Grounding Receipt**. Untagged orchestrator prose is treated as a hypothesis, not authority.

**What it is:** A receipt carries `claim_id`, normalized claim, source type (`owner`, file+line, command result, URL, agent inference), observation time, validity/TTL, probe id, known-positive control where relevant, and downstream consumers. Fan-out prompts carry claim ids, not copied “ground truth.” A child either consumes the cited evidence or re-probes externally. Contradictions open a thread; they are not resolved by same-context rationalization.

**Why inferred:** A fabricated “Write tool is blocked” constraint was absent from skill/rule/setting sources, originated in a launcher, propagated into about 15 prompts, and routed work toward a 13.6%-failure workaround instead of a 0%-failure tool (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:151-161`). This is direct evidence that orchestrator-authored premises are an audit and trust boundary.

**How to build:** Add a `claim` subtype to the existing event ledger and a tiny prompt serializer that renders `[claim:C123 source=... probe=P7 valid_as_of=...]`. Require external citations for `constraint`, `absent`, `broken`, and `done`; inference may be passed only with `UNVERIFIED` and its settling experiment. At fan-out read-back, the child reports claim ids it relies on. Add regression fixtures for the fabricated Write constraint: an unreceipted version must be downgraded; a file+line or real probe must pass.

**External signal that verifies it:** The grounding receipt points to raw file lines, a real execution result, or owner message. Before propagating an absence, a separate probe must demonstrate a known-present control. A child agent's agreement is not independent if it only rereads the same claim; the local rule explicitly says subagent consensus from the same lens remains one signal (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/rules/common/single-signal-verification.md:34-37`).

**SOTA:** NIST's traceability/accountability framing supports documentation across the AI lifecycle, while cognitive-forcing research shows that explanations alone can leave or increase overreliance; interventions that force engagement with evidence reduce it, at a subjective-experience cost ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf); [Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287)). Claim receipts are the low-friction forcing function: inspect the source when load-bearing, not on every sentence.

**Assessment:** **almost certain (90-99%)** to prevent the exact unproven-premise propagation class when downstream prompts must carry resolvable claim ids; this follows from the observed incident and mechanical acceptance rule. Semantic paraphrases evading normalization are **likely (55-80%)** without testing; settle with a mutation suite over 100 paraphrases of the known fabricated constraint.  
**Value × effort:** **transformative × low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for attaching provenance; **confirm-first** only when contradictory owner authorities cannot be ordered.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 5. Run proactive autonomy in shadow mode before asking for—or taking—more control

**Improvement:** Add **Counterfactual Shadow Receipts** for candidate proactive actions. The harness learns whether an intervention would have helped without interrupting the owner or mutating live state.

**What it is:** For a candidate new autonomy class, record `would_act`, trigger, intended action, expected external postcondition, and expiry. Where safe, replay it in an existing worktree/sandbox or against a read-only API. Compare the predicted benefit with external outcome and with what actually happened. Only evidence-backed candidates enter the Autonomy Promotion Matrix; low-value candidates silently expire.

**Why inferred:** The owner explicitly wants anticipatory capability but treats prompt fatigue as regression. Existing calibrated silence attempts to learn usefulness from acted/ignored/annoyed reactions (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/calibrated_silence.py:2-25`), while its classifier can call an unrelated next turn “ignored” (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/calibrated-silence/log_reaction.py:143-151`). Shadowing avoids asking the owner to train every candidate and avoids treating mere conversational movement as a reliable label.

**How to build:** Extend the calibrated-silence ledger with `shadow_candidate`, `oracle`, `observed_outcome`, and `eligible` fields. Add a CLI evaluator invoked by the existing dispatcher only for configured action classes; it must never mutate live targets. Eligible oracle examples: tests in an existing worktree, liveness read-back from a separate client, static diff validation, or a later owner action that exactly matches the candidate. Start with one narrow class, such as retry/pivot suggestions after a repeated tool fingerprint. Do not generalize until the class has receipts.

**External signal that verifies it:** A worktree test, separate API read, later matching owner action, or explicit owner rating. The agent's statement that its intervention “would help” is never an outcome. Promotion requires the same two-signal rule as live actions.

**SOTA:** A 2025 experiment found proactive help can reduce competence-based self-esteem and system satisfaction, with effects varying by AI knowledge—direct support for treating unsolicited action as an attention/agency cost, not a free benefit ([Benner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12599-024-00918-y)). Recent human-centric proactive-agent guidance explicitly frames design and evaluation as a distinct problem ([BEHAVE AI, CHI 2025 workshop](https://doi.org/10.1145/3708557.3716155)).

**Assessment:** **likely (55-80%)** to find useful proactive actions without prompt fatigue, because it replaces owner interruption with sandbox/read-only outcomes; **UNVERIFIED** whether enough candidate classes have cheap external oracles. Settle with a 30-candidate pilot: proceed only if at least 20 have unambiguous external outcomes and the false-promotion rate is below the owner-approved threshold.  
**Value × effort:** **high × medium**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for read-only/shadow evaluation; **confirm-first** before any class graduates to live mutation.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 6. Make reversibility visible as an undo receipt, not a vague assurance

**Improvement:** Every autonomous mutation returns a compact **Undo Receipt**: what changed, the exact rollback handle, what rollback will and will not restore, an independent verification status, and retention expiry.

**What it is:** The owner sees one line only when material: `acted: 4 tracked files | verified: 27 tests | undo: U-184 until 2026-08-18 | open: 0`. Drill-down resolves `U-184` to the existing checkpoint record. This builds beyond edit-checkpoints: checkpoints already exist, but they are not joined to the action, its outcome, or a human-usable receipt.

**Why inferred:** More autonomy becomes safer when the owner retains fast recourse. The existing hook creates whole-tree git snapshots once per turn and GC-safe refs (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/edit-checkpoints/checkpoint.py:2-16`, `:124-144`) and records non-git copies (`:147-173`). The audit improvement is to expose that real mechanism at the moment trust is needed, without asking permission before each reversible edit.

**How to build:** When the action receipt opens, resolve the turn's checkpoint index entry and attach `undo_id`, `restore_type`, affected scope, and retention. After mutation, an independent verifier confirms the restore ref/copy exists and the postcondition passes. Add `ledger undo-show U-184`; keep actual rollback behind the existing `/rollback` flow. Never claim full reversibility for external messages, remote destructive APIs, credential exposure, or irreversible side effects.

**External signal that verifies it:** `git cat-file`/`rev-parse` confirms the checkpoint object while the existing index provides a second data source; for non-git files, independently hash the backup and original preimage recorded in the receipt. A throwaway-repo acceptance test must perform mutation → rollback → byte-for-byte comparison. This is execution evidence, not same-context review.

**SOTA:** Levels-of-automation research argues for choosing automation by function and consequence rather than maximum automation everywhere ([Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354)). Human-AI guidelines emphasize efficient dismissal and correction ([Amershi et al., 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)); an undo token is a concrete implementation of that recourse.

**Assessment:** **very likely (80-90%)** to support just-do autonomy for tracked, checkpointed edits because the rollback primitive already exists and can be externally exercised. It is **almost certainly not (1-5%)** sufficient for irreversible external actions, based on the absence of any restorable preimage for those actions; those remain confirm-first.  
**Value × effort:** **high × low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it** for reversible, in-scope edits; **confirm-first** for irreversible actions.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 7. Audit the instrumentation with known-positive canaries; never infer health from silence

**Improvement:** Add an **Audit Witness** to the existing event-ledger/dispatcher path: a synthetic known-positive canary plus a second independently read signal proves that each audit channel is alive.

**What it is:** On a bounded schedule (for example once per installation check, not every turn), an external test process feeds a fixture known to trigger each detector and verifies both native hook output and a ledger witness record. Production silence is interpretable only while the most recent canary is fresh. A stale/missing witness marks telemetry `UNKNOWN`, never `healthy` or `no fires`.

**Why inferred:** The corpus explicitly marks its hook detector broken because a known-positive control produced zero fires (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:13260-13264`). Live operations separately found zero hook-named records across 12 GB despite a demonstrable fire and prescribes grepping both the event ledger and transcript system reminders (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/INPUT-known-harness-defects.md:133-146`). This is the exact audit-of-audit gap.

**How to build:** Extend the existing golden hook tests—not settings—with fixtures for one known fire and one known silence per critical detector. Write a `witness` event through `ledger.append`, then have a separate test command read JSONL and parse native stdout. Store `detector`, code digest, fixture id, output digest, ledger seq, two probe results, and timestamp. A SessionStart projection may report only stale/bad witnesses. All runtime code stays try/except and exit 0; test failure fails CI/install-check, not the owner's live session.

**External signal that verifies it:** Signal one is the hook's native output under a known-positive fixture. Signal two is an independent process reading the durable ledger witness and checking code/fixture digest. The negative control proves discrimination. These are mechanically distinct from the agent context making the conclusion.

**SOTA:** NIST's measurement and monitoring orientation requires risk-management evidence to be documented and evaluated, not presumed from an interface ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)). The local single-signal rule independently codifies known-positive detector validation (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/rules/common/single-signal-verification.md:22-33`).

**Assessment:** **almost certain (90-99%)** to prevent “zero means no hooks fired” when witness freshness is enforced, because detector health becomes an explicit precondition. **UNVERIFIED:** whether native hook stdout and ledger witness can both be captured in the deployed Windows hook harness without altering payload contracts; settle by running the golden fixture through the real dispatcher and independently reading the ledger before implementation is considered done.  
**Value × effort:** **high × low**  
**Novelty:** **inferred-surprise**  
**Autonomy:** **just-do-it**; it is non-blocking diagnostic infrastructure.  
**NATIVE-vs-BUILD:** **BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**.

### 8. Show the owner an exception digest; keep routine evidence pull-only

**Improvement:** Build an **Attention-Budgeted Trust Digest** over the receipts. The audit log remains complete enough for reconstruction, but the owner is interrupted only for authority decisions, irreversible risk, failed verification, changed autonomy tier, contradiction, or a thread at risk of being dropped.

**What it is:** A session/weekly digest has four short sections: `acted autonomously`, `verification exceptions`, `autonomy promotions/demotions`, and `open-thread deltas`. Unchanged successes collapse by action class: “27 checkpointed edits, 27/27 verified.” No heartbeat chatter, repeated warning, full command output, or “still working.” A drill-down command resolves receipt ids.

**Why inferred:** Prompt fatigue is a hard constraint. The workspace already has 1,329 AskUserQuestion calls (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/inputs/corpus.json:134-140`) and a persist nudge specifically designed to fire once only under a strong conjunction (`/local_disk0/codexwork/bundle/claude/hooks/persist-nudge/nudge.py:2-15`). ## What to record, conditionally record, and discard

| Class | Policy | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Owner objective, scope, constraints, authority changes | Always, by reference | Establishes authorization and prevents invented constraints. |
| Accepted commitment/thread and explicit disposition | Always | Enforces never-drop-a-thread. |
| Consequential mutation or external side effect | Always as one receipt | Needed for accountability, verification, and undo. |
| Independent execution/postcondition evidence | Always for terminal claims | Same-context self-check is rejected by construction. |
| Checkpoint/rollback handle and limitations | Always when claiming reversibility | Converts reassurance into usable recourse. |
| Owner correction, rollback, rejection, explicit acceptance | Always as a label | Calibrates future autonomy using real authority. |
| Read/search/tool discovery | Only when it supports a load-bearing claim | Routine capture would swamp the useful trail. |
| Proactive suggestion | Only as aggregate reaction data; shadow first | Attention cost matters; “ignored” is not correctness. |
| Raw output | Store once at source, reference by digest/offset | Avoid duplication and secret spread. |
| Prompt or tool payload containing secrets | Redact; retain minimal metadata/digest | Auditability does not justify credential replication. |
| Internal chain-of-thought / self-evaluation | Never | It is neither required evidence nor an external signal. |
| Unchanged polling/heartbeat | Aggregate | No decision changed; narration is bureaucracy. |
| Owner confirmation for reversible in-scope action | Do not request once policy permits | Confirmation fatigue defeats useful autonomy. |

## Rollout order and acceptance gates

1. **Action receipts + Audit Witness first.** Without trustworthy telemetry, calibration statistics are decorative. Acceptance: native known-positive output, durable witness, negative control, and fail-open live behavior.
2. **Commitment Escrow + Grounding Receipts.** Acceptance: compaction/replay preserves a seeded thread; self-closure without external receipt fails; the fabricated Write constraint regression is downgraded unless sourced.
3. **Undo Receipt.** Acceptance: throwaway mutation restores byte-for-byte and receipt resolves the real checkpoint.
4. **Shadow-mode calibration.** Acceptance: owner approves thresholds after replay/shadow evidence; no live mutation during shadow.
5. **Autonomy Promotion Matrix.** Acceptance: promotions are reproducible from closed receipts, demotion occurs on seeded correction, and irreversible classes cannot auto-promote.
6. **Attention digest.** Acceptance: routine successes aggregate; exceptions and open commitments survive; owner chooses cadence based on a real crossover.

Each stage is independently useful and wired into the same ledger; no stage creates a button or view without its producer and acceptance test. Each runtime path is try/except, exit 0 on its own failure. Tests/install checks may fail closed because they are external validation, not session hooks.
