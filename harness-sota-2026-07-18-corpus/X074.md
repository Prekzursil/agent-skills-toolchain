# X074 — Cross-domain borrow: law of evidence → agent-claim verification

**Unit:** X074  
**Lens:** law of evidence (admissibility, authentication and chain of custody, hearsay, completeness, burden and standard of proof)  
**Target:** prove an agent's material claim without trusting the agent and without requiring a human to read the entire record  
**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope discipline:** external research and translation only. Per the unit brief, I did not inspect the local harness, corpus, audit, defects file, or subject tree. Consequently, this paper proposes an architecture and falsification experiments; it does not claim that a particular local hook or feature exists or is absent.

## Executive judgment

The borrow **partly transfers, and the transferable part is useful**. The mechanism worth borrowing is not “a court,” not legalistic prose, and not a giant rule engine. It is evidence law's layered allocation of questions:

1. **What proposition is actually being offered?**
2. **Is the offered item what the proponent says it is?** (authentication and custody)
3. **Is this a firsthand record, or a statement relayed through one or more declarants?** (personal knowledge and hearsay layers)
4. **When exact content matters, is the original or an accuracy-preserving duplicate available?** (original/duplicate rules)
5. **Has selective quotation created a false impression?** (completeness)
6. **Is an inference produced by a sufficiently grounded and reliably applied method?** (expert-method gatekeeping)
7. **Who bears the burden, what happens after rebuttal, and how much persuasion is required given the cost of error?**
8. **What passes a threshold gate versus what merely changes evidentiary weight?**

That decomposition is the central result. Authentication is not truth; an intact chain is not truth; admissibility is not truth; a reliable method is not necessarily a correct conclusion; and an unrebutted presumption does not erase the ultimate burden. The Federal Rules and Supreme Court doctrine repeatedly preserve these distinctions: Rule 901 sets only a foundation that an item is what it is claimed to be; Rule 902's electronic-record notes explicitly say authentication does not establish reliability or overcome hearsay; Rule 702 distinguishes reliability from ultimate correctness; and Rule 301 distinguishes the burden of producing rebuttal from the burden of persuasion ([FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901), [FRE 902](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902), [FRE 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702), [FRE 301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_301)).

The concrete harness translation is a **small evidentiary docket**: for each material claim, store a claim-specific proof packet pointing to harness-captured raw events; apply deterministic gates for identity, custody, exact content, completeness, and computation; explicitly trace every relay layer; then spend independent challenge work only on high-impact, disputed, sampled, or method-dependent claims. A verifier returns separate statuses—`authenticated`, `content-matched`, `method-checked`, `contested`, and `meets-burden`—rather than one misleading `verified: true` bit.

It is **very likely (80-90%)** that this separation will catch unsupported “done,” misquoted-source, stale-evidence, and broken-absence claims that a citation-presence check misses, because each of those failures violates a mechanically testable link (event existence, artifact identity, context, time/order, or detector validation), and the legal sources above show why identity, reliability, and persuasive sufficiency are distinct. The exact gain is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-1 (the seeded claim-mutation benchmark defined below) would settle it**.

It is only **likely (55-80%)** that the approach will improve semantic/factual verification of open-ended synthesis, because no provenance system proves that a source entails an agent's interpretation, and the strongest remaining checker may share the original model's errors. This is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-2 (blind adversarial entailment evaluation with mechanically independent ground truth) would settle it**.

The analogy fails if implemented as a courtroom simulator with model “lawyers” and a model “judge.” It is **very likely (80-90%)** that such a design would reproduce correlated testimony because the model roles lack the law's compulsory process, sanctions, institutional independence, public procedure, and genuinely adverse incentives; the remaining uncertainty concerns how much model/tool diversity reduces correlation. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; EV-2's diverse-judge arms would settle the correlation question**. I therefore do **not** recommend it.

## 1. What the source field has actually solved

This analysis uses the U.S. Federal Rules of Evidence and U.S. Supreme Court cases as a concrete, inspectable evidence-law system. It does not claim that all legal systems use identical rules.

### 1.1 Authentication is a threshold question, not a truth certificate

In its own field, Federal Rule of Evidence 901(a) requires the proponent to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item “is what the proponent claims it is.” Rule 901(b) supplies nonexclusive methods, including testimony from a knowledgeable witness, distinctive characteristics considered with the circumstances, and evidence that a process or system produces an accurate result. The Advisory Committee describes authentication as a form of conditional relevance and warns that satisfying authentication does not guarantee admission because other objections, including hearsay, can remain ([FRE 901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901)). Rule 104(b) similarly lets a gatekeeper ask whether enough foundation exists for a reasonable factfinder to find a conditional fact, while Rule 104(e) preserves later attacks on weight or credibility ([FRE 104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_104)).

This solves a recurring epistemic category error: “this artifact came from system X” and “the assertions inside the artifact are true” are different propositions. A genuine newspaper may contain an error; a bit-for-bit genuine command log may record a flawed command; an authentic model answer may hallucinate.

**Harness translation:** every proof packet needs typed predicates, not a single verified flag:

- `identity`: this is the exact artifact/event claimed;
- `custody`: it has not changed since the relevant observation;
- `source`: it originated from the represented process, endpoint, file, or tool;
- `content`: the cited bytes/fields say the represented thing;
- `entailment`: those contents actually support the claim;
- `sufficiency`: the total record meets the applicable proof standard.

**Targeted failure:** the logical leap “the URL exists, therefore the prose is supported,” as well as “the test output is authentic, therefore the implementation is correct.”

### 1.2 Electronic self-authentication uses certification plus inspectability—not a magic hash

In its own field, Rule 902 lets specified classes of evidence authenticate without a live foundation witness. Two 2017 provisions are especially relevant. Rule 902(13) covers a record generated by an electronic process or system that produces an accurate result, supported by a qualified person's certification. Rule 902(14) covers copied electronic data authenticated through a process of digital identification. Both incorporate advance notice and availability for inspection so an opponent has a fair chance to challenge ([FRE 902](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902)).

The Rule 902(14) committee note explains the normal hash comparison: different hash values establish nonidentity, while matching values make nonidentity highly improbable. Crucially, the Rule 902(13) note says a certification establishes only authenticity. A spreadsheet's output may be authentically from the computer while the information remains unreliable. It also says a webpage retrieval can be authentic without proving who placed the statement on the webpage ([FRE 902, committee notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902)).

**Harness translation:** the harness—not the claiming agent—acts as the custodian. At tool-call time it records an append-only event identifier, request and response hashes, timestamps, tool identity/version where available, exit/status fields, and references to raw retained content. A later proof packet points to those events. The agent may select relevant evidence but cannot mint the underlying receipt. A hash answers “same bytes?”; the verifier separately asks “correct source?”, “successful operation?”, “fresh enough?”, and “supports this proposition?”

**Targeted failures:**

- content modified after a check but before a “done” assertion;
- a quote whose stored bytes do not match the cited raw response;
- a purported successful command whose captured exit status was nonzero;
- an agent-created transcript fragment that has no harness event;
- a copied artifact that is intact but came from the wrong run, branch, endpoint, or time.

### 1.3 Chain of custody is a continuity argument whose gaps affect weight; it is not maximal ceremony

In its own field, chain of custody connects a physical or digital item offered later to the item collected earlier. It matters when substitution, contamination, or alteration is plausible. But the doctrine does not demand a live witness for every handoff. In *Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts*, the Supreme Court stated that the prosecution must establish chain of custody but need not call everyone who handled the evidence; gaps normally affect weight rather than admissibility, and the proponent decides which links are crucial enough to prove ([*Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts*, footnote 1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/07-591.ZO.html)).

This is an important restraint on the analogy. A harness does not need blockchain, signatures on every token, or a durable-execution system. It needs continuity at mutation boundaries that could change the truth of the claim: source retrieval, transformation, edit, test, and final assertion.

**Harness translation:** represent custody as a short directed chain of event IDs and content hashes:

```text
source retrieval E17/h1
  -> excerpt extraction E19 (input h1, output h2, byte offsets 440:821)
  -> analysis computation E22 (inputs h1+h2, method/version m7, output h3)
  -> material claim C4 (depends on E17, E19, E22)
```

Missing nonmaterial handoffs lower weight; a missing mutation boundary blocks the affected claim. **Targeted failures:** evidence from before the latest edit, unrecorded filtering, silent unit conversion, and claims whose “source” is another agent's paraphrase rather than the underlying event.

### 1.4 Hearsay rules force each relay layer to justify itself

In its own field, hearsay is generally an out-of-court assertion offered for its truth ([FRE 801](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801)). Its characteristic weakness is that the original declarant is not exposed to the usual credibility tests. Rule 805 handles nested hearsay directly: each layer of a combined statement must independently conform to an exception ([FRE 805](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805)).

Rule 803(6), the regularly conducted activity or “business records” exception, illustrates procedural trustworthiness. The record must be made at or near the time by someone with knowledge, kept in the regular course of activity, made as a regular practice, supported by a custodian/witness/certification, and not shown by the opponent to be untrustworthy in source or preparation ([FRE 803(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803)). The purpose matters. *Melendez-Diaz* explains that records created for an entity's ordinary administration differ from reports manufactured to prove a fact at trial; it relies on *Palmer v. Hoffman* for that distinction ([*Melendez-Diaz*, Part III-D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/07-591.ZO.html)).

The confrontation principle adds a procedural insight. Rejecting the claim that supposedly neutral scientific testing should simply be trusted, *Melendez-Diaz* quotes *Crawford*: reliability is assessed by a specified procedure—adversarial testing—not merely asserted as an intrinsic property. The opinion identifies fraud, incompetence, unknown methods, judgment calls, and error as proper targets of challenge ([*Melendez-Diaz*, Part III-C](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/07-591.ZO.html)).

**Harness translation:** trace declarants and transformations:

```text
claim: “Dependency Q fixed vulnerability V in version 4.2”
layer 1: agent says the claim
layer 2: search snippet says a blog says the claim
layer 3: blog says vendor advisory says the claim
layer 4: vendor advisory identifies package/version/CVE
layer 5: local lockfile identifies installed version
```

The harness does not reject all relayed information. It exposes the chain and requires every truth-bearing link needed for the proposition. A search result that merely quotes a blog is not independent support from the vendor. Two agents repeating the same snippet are two relays from one declarant, not corroboration.

The “ordinary-course versus prepared-for-proof” distinction transfers strongly. Contemporaneous, automatically captured tool logs deserve more initial weight than a retrospective explanation the agent generates after being challenged. **Targeted failures:** self-serving after-the-fact test narratives, circular citations among agents, summaries whose underlying source was never retrieved, and “independent” confirmations sharing one upstream assertion.

### 1.5 Original, duplicate, and completeness rules protect content fidelity without demanding originals everywhere

In its own field, Rule 1002 requires an original writing, recording, or photograph when a party seeks to prove its content, subject to other rules and statutes ([FRE 1002](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1002)). This is narrower than the misleading label “best evidence rule”: it is about proving content, not a universal demand for the best possible proof.

Rule 1003 admits an accuracy-preserving duplicate to the same extent as the original unless authenticity is genuinely disputed or admission would be unfair. Its committee note recognizes that a partial reproduction may be inadequate when omitted portions qualify the selected part ([FRE 1003](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1003)). Rule 1004 permits secondary evidence when originals were lost or destroyed without the proponent's bad faith, cannot be obtained through process, were controlled but not produced after notice, or are not closely related to a controlling issue. The committee calls this a rule of preference and rejects a needlessly elaborate hierarchy of secondary evidence ([FRE 1004](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1004)).

Rule 106 supplies the rule of completeness: when one party introduces all or part of a recorded statement, an adverse party may require contemporaneous consideration of another part necessary in fairness. The committee identifies the problems as misleading decontextualization and the inadequacy of delayed repair; the 2023 amendment permits necessary completion even over a hearsay objection ([FRE 106](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_106)).

**Harness translation:**

- For exact claims (“the file contains X,” “the API returned Y,” “the owner said Z”), require a raw event locator plus hash and byte/line/JSON-pointer range.
- Accept cached copies when hashes or another digital-identification method establish identity; do not refetch merely to perform ritual.
- If the original is unavailable, record a typed reason rather than silently upgrading a summary into primary evidence.
- Automatically attach a bounded context window and structural parent for every excerpt. For JSON, include the containing object and relevant sibling fields; for command output, include status, stderr, and truncation metadata; for prose, include adjacent qualification/caveat text.
- Let a challenger request “completion” from the retained raw event without rerunning the whole task.

**Targeted failures:** cherry-picked quotes, omitted “not” or “except” clauses, a success line paired with a later failure line, a JSON value detached from its key/version/units, and summaries standing in for an available original.

### 1.6 Burdens and proof standards allocate error rather than pretending certainty is free

In its own field, Rule 301 separates two burdens. A presumption can shift the burden of producing rebuttal evidence, but it does not shift the ultimate burden of persuasion from the party who originally carried it ([FRE 301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_301)).

Proof standards reflect asymmetric error costs. *In re Winship* requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt for every fact necessary to constitute a charged crime and describes that standard as a mechanism for reducing factual-error risk ([*In re Winship*](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/397/358)). *Addington v. Texas* requires clear and convincing evidence for involuntary civil commitment, reasoning that ordinary preponderance insufficiently protects the individual's grave liberty interest while criminal reasonable doubt would create an inappropriate barrier given diagnostic uncertainty and competing state/patient interests ([*Addington v. Texas*](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/441/418)). The lesson is not to attach invented percentages to legal phrases. It is to select a standard based on the consequences of false acceptance and false rejection.

**Harness translation:** assign the burden to the claiming agent/run. A low-risk, easily reversible claim can pass on one authenticated direct observation. A destructive, security-sensitive, financial, legal, medical, deployment, or “nothing remains” claim requires stronger and more independent proof. If a challenger produces a concrete contradiction, the claim returns to `contested`; the original claimant still bears persuasion. Silence by the challenger is not affirmative truth.

For absence, brokenness, and completion claims, require two mechanically independent signals and a positive control proving each detector can find a known-present analogue. That implements the brief's mandatory rule and maps to burdens: the claimant must establish the basic foundation before any presumption arises.

**Targeted failures:** “no occurrences” from a detector that cannot see the target class, “all tests pass” when only one suite was enumerated, “service is down” from a single network path, and “cleanup complete” from an empty result produced by the wrong scope.

### 1.7 Method gatekeeping asks whether the inference process is reliable and fitted to this case

In its own field, current Rule 702 admits expert opinion only when the proponent shows it is more likely than not that the testimony helps the factfinder, uses sufficient facts/data, is produced by reliable principles/methods, and reflects reliable application of those methods to the facts ([FRE 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)). Its committee notes list nonexclusive considerations such as testability, known/potential error rate, standards and controls, general acceptance, unjustified extrapolation, obvious alternative explanations, and whether the same care is used outside litigation. The notes also sharply separate admissibility reliability from merits correctness and say gatekeeping does not replace adversarial testing ([FRE 702, committee notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)).

**Harness translation:** inference-heavy claims carry a method card:

- inputs and their proof-packet IDs;
- method/tool/version and parameters;
- applicability assumptions;
- known error or validation evidence, if available;
- obvious alternatives considered;
- exact output and transformation into the stated claim.

A model's fluent explanation is not the method card. **Targeted failures:** a valid benchmark applied to the wrong platform, a parser run against truncated data, a numerical answer with an undocumented unit conversion, a causal conclusion drawn from correlation, and a model judge asked to evaluate a domain where it has no established reliability.

## 2. The concrete design: a minimal evidentiary docket

### 2.1 Claim types and proof packets

Only **material claims** enter the docket: claims that justify an action, constitute a requested result, describe completion/absence/brokenness, materially affect risk, or are explicitly challenged. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that requiring packets for every sentence would add substantially more cost and ceremony than material-claim packets because receipt production and checking scale with packet count; the actual overhead is **UNVERIFIED, and EV-4 would settle it**. Evidence law likewise avoids proof of every proposition through stipulation, self-authentication, and threshold gates.

A compact packet can be line-oriented JSON rather than a fragile, deeply nested fan-out schema:

```json
{
  "claim_id": "C4",
  "claim": "All migration tests pass against the edited tree",
  "type": "completion",
  "impact": "high",
  "as_of": "2026-07-19T12:34:56Z",
  "burden": "high",
  "evidence": ["E31", "E37"],
  "independence": "different_runner_and_artifact_scan",
  "positive_controls": ["PC-test-discovery", "PC-edited-file-visible"],
  "custody": ["tree-hash:h7", "test-input-hash:h7"],
  "status": {
    "authenticated": true,
    "content_matched": true,
    "method_checked": true,
    "contested": false,
    "meets_burden": true
  }
}
```

The packet is a receipt index, not a restatement of all evidence. Raw tool records remain on the existing transcript/artifact bus. The human-facing answer receives compact marks such as `C4: high-burden met (E31,E37)`; a human drills into only challenged, high-impact, or sampled packets.

### 2.2 Gate order

The verifier evaluates inexpensive, objective questions first:

1. **Materiality:** does the claim need verification?
2. **Event existence:** do all referenced harness receipts exist?
3. **Identity/custody:** do hashes, run IDs, timestamps, and input-tree/source identities connect evidence to the claim?
4. **Content fidelity:** do quoted bytes/fields and statuses match?
5. **Completeness:** do nearby text, structural parents, stderr, truncation, or later events materially qualify the excerpt?
6. **Relay trace:** does every truth-bearing layer lead to a direct source or explicitly typed exception/limitation?
7. **Method fit:** for inferred results, are inputs, method, assumptions, and application inspectable and suitable?
8. **Corroboration:** are multiple signals mechanically independent, or merely repeated readings of one upstream source?
9. **Burden:** given claim impact and contradiction status, is the evidence sufficient?
10. **Challenge/sampling:** route only unresolved high-risk claims and a calibrated random sample to additional checking or a human.

A gate failure does not always discard the evidence. It yields a typed outcome: `inadmissible-no-foundation`, `authentic-but-unreliable`, `secondary-source`, `context-missing`, `stale-after-mutation`, `correlated-signals`, `method-unvalidated`, `contested`, or `insufficient-for-impact`. That is more informative than pass/fail and mirrors the legal distinction between admissibility and weight.

### 2.3 What “independent” means

Independence is about failure mechanism, not process count. Examples:

- Strong: parse a file format directly **and** query the authoritative program's own inventory command, provided neither consumes the other's output.
- Strong: compare an API response with a signed/official source or with a local observable consequence produced through a different path.
- Strong: run a behavioral test and separately inspect the state transition/artifact it is supposed to cause.
- Weak: ask the same model twice.
- Weak: ask two models to read the same erroneous snippet.
- Weak: run the same detector twice in different shells.
- Not independent: one agent cites another agent's summary of the same source.

The docket records an `upstream_source_set`; two signals with overlapping decisive upstream sources do not count as two. This is the direct technical translation of hearsay-within-hearsay and confrontation, not a superficial “two witnesses” metaphor.

## 3. Proposals and build boundary

Every proposal below is intentionally small and rides the existing transcript/hook/skill/MCP bus. None introduces a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: harness-owned evidence receipts

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Add a hook-side receipt writer around existing tool events. It records event ID, run ID, tool and operation, time, request/response hashes, status/exit code, truncation indicator, artifact/source identity, and a raw-content locator. It must not depend on the agent volunteering accurate metadata. For mutable local state, capture the relevant revision/tree/content identity at observation and at claimed completion.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to detect fabricated execution, wrong-run evidence, stale-after-edit proof, and status/output mismatches because these are deterministic consistency checks over harness-owned fields. The effectiveness premise is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-1 would settle it by mutating each receipt relation and measuring detection and false-positive rates**.

Anti-overfit gate: pass. It is a thin receipt layer on the existing bus, not a new runtime. If the existing bus already provides immutable event IDs and raw-content hashes, this reduces to configuration/formatting rather than duplicate capture; that local premise was intentionally not investigated in this unit.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: claim ledger plus layered verifier registry

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Require material final claims to emit compact claim-ledger entries. A small verifier registry dispatches by claim type:

- exact quote/field → byte or JSON-pointer match plus context completion;
- file/state existence → authoritative read plus time/order check;
- absence/broken/done → two independent signals plus positive controls;
- computation → deterministic recomputation when possible;
- test/result → command status, discovery positive control, tested input identity, and observable artifact/state where impact warrants;
- relayed source → upstream-source trace and source-class label;
- inference → method card and counterexample/alternative checks.

Do not force every tool response into one universal nested schema. Verifiers accept minimal common fields plus claim-specific optional fields, and unknown claim types remain `unverified` rather than failing schema serialization.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that agents can identify most material claims without unacceptable omission, based on the tractability of the enumerated categories, but omission is the central attack surface. This premise is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-3 (human-labeled material-claim recall on representative sessions, including adversarially phrased implicit claims) would settle it**. Until EV-3 passes a predeclared recall target, high-impact final responses need a hook-side heuristic scan for completion, absence, brokenness, quantities, quotations, and action-justifying claims, with scan misses included in sampling.

Anti-overfit gate: pass. The verifier registry is a set of small adapters on the existing bus. It must remain deterministic where possible; an LLM entailment judge may add weight but cannot independently authenticate its own source or serve as the sole verifier for a high-impact claim.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: impact-weighted burden profile

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Define three operational profiles without pretending they are numerical translations of legal standards:

- `ordinary`: one authenticated direct observation, no material contradiction, freshness appropriate to the task;
- `heightened`: direct evidence plus an independent signal or deterministic reproduction; complete context and source chain; contradiction resolved;
- `critical`: heightened foundation plus explicit alternative/counterexample testing, immutable state identity, and human approval when the remaining judgment cannot be mechanically resolved.

Map claims to profiles by consequence and reversibility, not by agent confidence. Destructive operations, releases/deployments, security controls, high-stakes medical/legal/financial output, irreversible communication, and universal/absence/completion claims default upward. A concrete rebuttal reopens the claim and leaves the burden of persuasion on the claimant.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that impact-based profiles will reduce verification cost relative to uniform maximal checking while preserving more scrutiny where false acceptance is costly; *Addington* supports the allocation principle, not the harness performance claim. The performance premise is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-4 (risk-weighted error/cost comparison against uniform checking and no gate) would settle it**.

Anti-overfit gate: pass. This is configuration on the claim gate. There is no separate policy engine.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: notice, challenge, completion, and calibrated sampling

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus)**

Before a high-impact claim is relied upon, make its packet inspectable by the next decision point. A challenge operation can request raw evidence, surrounding context, an upstream declarant, method details, or a new independent test. The challenger may be a deterministic checker, a later agent step, or a human depending on claim type and impact. Randomly sample unchallenged claims and send detected misses back into verifier tests and burden configuration.

This borrows Rule 902's notice-and-inspection economy, Rule 106's immediate completion, and confrontation's adversarial test without pretending that a model is a constitutional adversary. It is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce the amount humans read because humans see compact packets and escalations rather than full transcripts. The premise is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-5 (timed human review with full transcript versus docket-and-escalation, measuring missed seeded defects) would settle it**.

Anti-overfit gate: pass only if challenge is an operation on the existing agent/tool loop. A standing panel of debate agents or a new judge service would fail the gate and is not proposed.

### Proposal E — UNVERIFIED: ordinary-course verifier calibration record

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only)**

Keep versioned verifier facts—positive-control cases, seeded mutation cases, last pass time, known blind spots, and observed false-positive/false-negative counts—in ordinary run records, created whether or not a specific disputed claim exists. Do not let a verifier certify itself solely through a report generated after it is accused of failing.

This is the business-record insight: contemporaneous routine records have stronger process guarantees than retrospective litigation-shaped narratives. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that such records will expose stale or never-validated detectors because dates, versions, and control outcomes are directly inspectable. The claimed operational benefit is **UNVERIFIED; experiment EV-6 (silently break/replace verifiers and measure time-to-detection with and without routine calibration records) would settle it**.

Anti-overfit gate: pass. Store compact JSON next to the verifier configuration or existing run artifacts; do not create a telemetry service or external observability product.

## 4. Adversarial transfer analysis

### 4.1 Structural correspondences that make the borrow real

| Evidence-law structure | Agent-verification structure | Why the mapping holds |
|---|---|---|
| A proponent advances a proposition and bears a foundation/burden | An agent advances a material claim used for a decision | In both, the claimant has better access to the asserted basis and should identify it rather than forcing every consumer to rediscover it. |
| Exhibits can be substituted, altered, decontextualized, or misidentified | Files, logs, web responses, screenshots, tool results, and excerpts have the same failure modes | Hashes, event IDs, time/order, raw retention, and context are directly applicable. |
| A statement may relay another declarant's statement | Agent answers commonly relay search snippets, other agents, documentation, and tool output | Upstream-source overlap and transformation layers are mechanically traceable. |
| Exact content and ultimate truth are different issues | A quote may be accurate while its inference is false | Authentication/content matching and semantic sufficiency need separate statuses. |
| Different decisions impose different costs for factual error | Agent claims range from casual/reversible to destructive/high-stakes | Risk-tiered burdens allocate verification effort rationally. |
| Admissibility screening and later weight are distinct | A proof packet can meet minimum foundation while retaining caveats/contradictions | Typed statuses preserve useful but imperfect evidence instead of binary discard. |

### 4.2 Disanalogies that limit or defeat parts of the borrow

1. **Courts have institutionally distinct actors; a harness often does not.** A judge, opposing counsel, witnesses, and jury have different roles and incentives. Two invocations of one model family are correlated components of one system. Therefore “cross-examination” cannot mean merely prompting another agent to disagree.

2. **Courts possess compulsory process and sanctions.** Subpoena power, disclosure obligations, oath, contempt, professional duties, appeal, and perjury consequences change behavior and evidence availability. An agent certificate has none of those deterrents. Therefore harness-owned contemporaneous receipts, not agent solemnity, must ground authenticity.

3. **Evidence rules optimize adjudicative legitimacy and policy, not pure truth.** Privilege, prejudice, constitutional rights, institutional competence, and finality can exclude probative material. The harness should borrow epistemic procedures only; it should not assume every exclusionary rule improves factual accuracy.

4. **Legal proof standards are qualitative social instructions.** “Beyond a reasonable doubt” is not a calibrated probability threshold that can be copied into JSON. The transfer is asymmetric-error budgeting and burden allocation, not made-up percentages.

5. **A trial has bounded parties, issues, and an endpoint.** Agent work can be open-ended, rapidly changing, and connected to live systems. Freshness and invalidation after mutation matter more in a harness than in a fixed trial record.

6. **Ordinary-course records can still encode systematic error.** Regularity supports process provenance, not substantive accuracy. A consistently broken detector can produce impeccable logs. This is why positive controls and mutation tests are necessary.

7. **Completeness is contextual, not mechanically solved by N adjacent lines.** A qualification may be elsewhere in a document or another source. Context windows catch local truncation but cannot prove global completeness.

8. **Admissible evidence can support competing conclusions.** The legal system does not promise automatic truth after gatekeeping. The harness must retain `contested` and `underdetermined` as legitimate terminal epistemic states.

### 4.3 Explicitly rejected borrows

- **A model court with prosecutor, defense, and judge agents:** rejected because shared training/data/tool sources undermine independence, while token use and rhetoric increase. It risks becoming a second orchestration runtime, explicitly barred by the anti-overfit gate.
- **Blockchain custody for every event:** rejected because append-only event IDs plus content hashes at material mutation boundaries provide the required identity checks without consensus infrastructure. Chain of custody is an argument about continuity, not a demand for a blockchain.
- **A universal hearsay ban:** rejected because most useful agent knowledge is necessarily relayed. The right translation is provenance and layer-specific weight, not exclusion of all secondary information.
- **Human review of every receipt:** rejected because self-authentication, stipulation, thresholds, challenge, and sampling exist precisely to avoid ceremonial proof where no genuine dispute exists.
- **One model judge as factfinder:** rejected because it can test semantic entailment but cannot supply independent provenance and may share the claimant's blind spots.
- **Numerical “preponderance = 51%, clear-and-convincing = 75%, reasonable doubt = 95%”:** rejected as false precision unsupported by the cited legal authorities or harness calibration.
- **“Two sources” as a universal rule:** rejected because source count ignores common upstream declarants. Independence must be represented as a failure-path property.

## 5. Failure modes this design is intended to expose

The following are design targets, not claims that these failures are present in the local harness:

| Missed by a superficial verifier | Evidentiary-docket catch |
|---|---|
| A citation URL resolves but does not support the sentence | Separate identity/content/entailment statuses; quote/field match; inference gate |
| Two agents agree because both saw the same erroneous snippet | Upstream-source-set intersection marks correlated hearsay |
| “No files matched” came from an invalid pattern or wrong scope | Absence burden requires positive control plus an independent detector/path |
| Tests passed before the final edit | Custody/time gate compares tested tree/content identity with final state |
| Output says “success” but exit status/stderr says failure | Harness receipt binds stdout, stderr, status, and truncation metadata |
| Correct excerpt omits a nearby exception | Rule-of-completeness context attachment and challenge operation |
| A cached copy is treated as suspect merely because it is not a refetch | Digital-identification check admits the identical duplicate efficiently |
| A hash match is treated as proof that the contents are true | Typed authentication status prevents promotion to reliability/sufficiency |
| Another agent's summary is treated as the source | Relay trace requires the underlying declarant/event or lowers weight explicitly |
| A benchmark/tool is valid in general but misapplied here | Method card checks inputs, applicability assumptions, and application |
| A detector produces immaculate logs while systematically missing targets | Routine positive controls and seeded mutation cases test the detector itself |
| The claimant responds to a contradiction with one more assertion | Rebuttal changes status to contested; burden of persuasion stays with claimant |
| A secondary summary replaces an available raw source | Original preference requests raw content; typed exception records true unavailability |
| Every harmless sentence receives expensive review | Materiality, risk tiers, deterministic gates, and sampling bound review cost |

## 6. Experiments that would settle the UNVERIFIED premises

### EV-1 — Seeded claim-mutation benchmark

Build a corpus of genuine claim packets, then seed one fault at a time: wrong event ID, wrong hash, wrong run, stale tree, nonzero status hidden by stdout, truncated response, quote altered by one token, omitted qualifying context, correlated “second” source, unvalidated absence detector, missing transformation, wrong unit, and fabricated tool execution. Predeclare expected status for each mutation. Measure per-class recall, false positives on untouched packets, runtime, and bytes retained. Compare layered gates with citation-presence checking and with no gate.

Success criterion must be chosen before running. A sensible initial research target is high recall on deterministic mutation classes with near-zero false positives, but the exact threshold is **UNVERIFIED; EV-1 pilot error distributions should determine a justified threshold rather than this paper inventing one**.

### EV-2 — Semantic support and alternative-explanation benchmark

Give a blinded reviewer or programmatically generated ground truth claims paired with sources that (a) entail, (b) contradict, (c) mention but do not entail, (d) depend on an omitted qualifier, or (e) support multiple live interpretations. Compare deterministic extraction, one model judge, diverse model judges, and human adjudication. Report results by domain and claim form; do not collapse into one accuracy number.

This experiment is crucial because provenance cannot solve entailment. A result showing strong deterministic provenance but weak semantic judgment would validate only the narrow transfer, not the entire verification objective.

### EV-3 — Material-claim recall

Human-label material claims in representative final answers and action rationales. Include implicit completion, universals, absence, risk, causal justification, quantities, quotations, and claims embedded in recommendations. Measure recall of agent-emitted ledger entries and a hook-side heuristic scanner. Red-team agents that strategically omit or linguistically soften claims.

If recall is inadequate, do not respond by forcing the whole answer through an over-strict universal schema. Prefer a small set of high-risk lexical/structural triggers, risk-based fail-closed behavior, and sampling while improving extraction.

### EV-4 — Risk-weighted error/cost trial

Replay tasks under (a) no formal verification, (b) uniform verification of every claim, and (c) impact-weighted burden profiles. Score false acceptance cost, false rejection cost, latency, tool calls, human minutes, and task completion. Blind scorers to condition. Vary the loss matrix because a deletion, a factual research answer, and a formatting edit have different costs.

### EV-5 — Human review compression

Seed known faults in full transcripts. Give reviewers either full transcripts or compact dockets with escalations and drill-down access. Measure defect detection, review time, confidence calibration, and unnecessary source opens. The docket succeeds only if it saves time without hiding consequential faults.

### EV-6 — Verifier-of-verifier resilience

Silently mutate verifier versions: break path scope, remove a file class, swap success/failure parsing, truncate inputs, or disable a check. Compare detection latency with and without routine positive controls and versioned calibration records. Require the health detector to demonstrate a known-present positive before accepting its “healthy” or “nothing found” report.

## 7. Recommended implementation sequence

This sequence is forward-looking and therefore confidence-banded:

1. Start with Proposal A receipts and only three deterministic claim adapters: exact quote/field, test/completion state identity, and absence with positive controls. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that these deliver the best early signal-to-complexity ratio because they rely on byte/status/order checks rather than model judgment. This prioritization is **UNVERIFIED; EV-1 per-class results would settle it**.
2. Add the separated status vocabulary and the ordinary/heightened/critical configuration. It is **likely (55-80%)** that separating authentication from sufficiency will improve downstream decision quality because it prevents valid hashes from being treated as truth, but this behavioral effect is **UNVERIFIED; a downstream-decision arm in EV-4 would settle it**.
3. Add relay/upstream-source tracing and completeness requests. It is **likely (55-80%)** that these will catch correlated citations and selective context at acceptable cost, based on Rules 805 and 106's direct structural fit. The harness cost/benefit is **UNVERIFIED; EV-1 and EV-5 would settle it**.
4. Add method cards only for high-impact inference-heavy claims. It is **likely (55-80%)** that universal method cards would create more ceremony than value, while targeted cards preserve the Rule 702 benefit. This scope judgment is **UNVERIFIED; EV-4 stratified by claim type would settle it**.
5. Add semantic model judging last and only as a nonexclusive weight signal. It is **very likely (80-90%)** that treating a same-family model judge as mechanically independent would overstate corroboration because it can share training, prompt, retrieval, and reasoning failure paths; EV-2 would quantify rather than merely assert the size of that correlation.

No BUILD-LARGE component is justified by this lens. If later experiments demonstrate that existing hooks cannot retain or address raw events, that would reopen the receipt-transport question, but it would not by itself justify a new orchestration runtime.

## 8. Bottom line

Law of evidence has not solved automatic truth. It has solved a more tractable and highly relevant problem: how to make proof inspectable, contestable, proportionate, and resistant to predictable category errors under bounded decision procedures.

The borrow transfers when expressed as:

- harness-owned contemporaneous receipts;
- typed claim/evidence relationships;
- authentication distinct from reliability and sufficiency;
- custody across truth-changing boundaries;
- per-layer provenance for relayed assertions;
- exact-content preference with accurate duplicates allowed;
- immediate contextual completion;
- method/application checks for inference;
- burdens scaled to asymmetric error costs;
- rebuttal that reopens rather than silently erases the claimant's burden;
- independent challenge and calibrated sampling rather than universal human review.

It does not transfer as theatrical adversarial multi-agent debate, legal proof percentages, universal exclusion rules, blockchain ceremony, or a monolithic “verified” bit.

The recommended minimal docket is **likely (55-80%)** to improve agent-claim verification without overfitting the harness because most of its strongest gates are deterministic and ride existing events rather than adding an orchestration layer. That overall performance claim is **UNVERIFIED; the combined EV-1/EV-4/EV-5 program would settle detection, cost, and human-review value**.
