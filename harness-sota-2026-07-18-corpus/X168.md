# X168 — Cross-domain borrow: military doctrine → observability without a baseline

**Unit:** X168  
**Source field:** military doctrine (commander's intent, continuous assessment, after-action review, red teaming, OODA)  
**Target problem:** determining whether an agent-harness change helped when no historical baseline exists  
**Lens boundary:** external research and design only. Per the task, I did not inspect or make claims about the local harness. The only write is this artifact.

## Executive verdict

The borrow **partially transfers**, but only if it is stated narrowly.

The useful military mechanism is not the slogan “run an AAR,” and it is not “cycle through OODA faster.” It is a linked discipline:

1. state the purpose, desired end state, and acceptable constraints before action (commander's intent);
2. define criteria of success and forecasted outcomes during planning;
3. instrument the action before it begins and continuously monitor facts, assumptions, and signposts;
4. compare actual events with forecasted events to identify variances;
5. reconstruct what happened from multiple perspectives and objective records in a facilitated AAR; and
6. use a red-team assumption check and disconfirming signposts to stop the organization from explaining every outcome as success.

This chain solves a real part of the “no baseline” problem: it creates a **prospective reference** when no retrospective reference exists. FM 6-0 defines assessment as continuous monitoring plus evaluation against success criteria; defines a variance as the difference between actual conditions and what the plan forecast at that time; and says initial success criteria are established during planning and later used during preparation and execution ([FM 6-0, preserved 2003 edition, chapter 6, pp. 6-4–6-6 and 6-11](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)). The Army Research Institute's history of AARs says the facilitator prepares data-collection instruments before an exercise, aggregates performance data afterward, begins by reviewing the mission and objectives, solicits multiple perspectives, establishes facts, and only then examines causes and improvements ([Morrison & Meliza, *Foundations of the After Action Review Process*, 1999, pp. 1–3](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA368651)). The UFMCS handbook adds explicit key-assumption checks, observable signposts, pre-mortems, and periodic rechecking ([UFMCS, *Red Team Handbook*, 2012, pp. 163–181](https://archive.org/details/ufmcs-red-team-handbook-2012)).

However, this chain does **not** by itself answer the causal question “did the harness change cause the improvement?” The Army's own 2011 literature review says empirical investigations of AAR effectiveness had been few and anecdotal and calls for transfer-of-training studies showing that variations in AAR design cause differences in task performance, rather than relying on subjective approval ([Keiser & Arthur, *Establishing an Intellectual and Theoretical Foundation for the After Action Review Process*, 2011, pp. 24–25](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA543054)). That is a decisive disanalogy, not a footnote. A forecast-to-actual comparison detects surprise; it is not a counterfactual. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the explicit Army research warning plus standard experimental-design logic, that importing AARs without a concurrent comparator would produce better narratives but would not establish whether the change helped.

The concrete design is therefore a **Mission Evidence Cycle**: military prospective assessment and adversarial review wrapped around a small, concurrent, randomized or blocked comparison of current versus candidate harness behavior. The military borrow supplies disciplined intent, evidence capture, variance analysis, and anti-rationalization. Experimental design supplies the missing counterfactual. NIST describes blocking as holding important nuisance factors homogeneous while varying the treatment of interest, and summarizes the rule as “block what you can, randomize what you cannot” ([NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, randomized block designs](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm)).

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the doctrinal forecast/variance mechanism, AAR instrumentation history, UFMCS signpost methods, and NIST blocking guidance, that a minimal Mission Evidence Cycle would distinguish more real improvements from regressions than post-change dashboard inspection alone. **UNVERIFIED: the “Harness Switchback Trial” experiment specified below would settle that likelihood for this owner and harness.**

## 1. What military doctrine solved in its own field

### 1.1 Commander's intent: preserve the outcome while allowing the method to adapt

In FM 6-0, commander's intent is a clear and concise statement of what the force must do and the conditions it must meet to succeed, focused on the desired end state. It sets the boundaries within which subordinates may exercise initiative, while commanders are told to impose only minimum constraints so they do not unnecessarily limit freedom of action. Mission orders emphasize the purpose (“why”), and mission command concentrates on the objective rather than prescribing how to achieve it ([FM 6-0, paras. 1-68–1-74](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)).

The mechanism is structurally important for measurement. A plan can change under pressure while its intended effect remains stable. That gives assessment a durable referent even when tactics change. In its own field, commander's intent is not a metric and not a test plan; it is the semantic anchor that says what “success” is supposed to mean despite local adaptation.

Translation to a harness: every material change gets a one-screen **intent-to-evidence card** written before deployment:

- purpose: which owner-visible failure or friction the change is intended to reduce;
- desired end state: the observable condition that counts as success;
- non-negotiable constraints: quality, safety, cost, latency, and autonomy boundaries that may not be traded away;
- delegated freedom: what implementation details may vary without invalidating the test;
- decision: ship, revise, roll back, or gather more evidence;
- expiry: when the decision must be revisited.

This catches a subtle failure that aggregate observability misses: a change can improve its local metric while violating the owner's actual purpose. For example, fewer tool errors can be bought by avoiding difficult tools, lower latency can be bought by doing less work, and fewer correction messages can be bought by ending sessions earlier. Those examples are logical failure modes, not claims that this harness currently exhibits them.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, because the intent construct separates “why/end state” from execution details in FM 6-0, that an intent-to-evidence card would reduce metric substitution during harness evaluation. **UNVERIFIED: the “Blind Intent Classification” experiment below would settle whether independent reviewers classify ship/rollback decisions more consistently with the card than without it.**

### 1.2 Continuous assessment: forecast first, then look for variance

FM 6-0's assessment mechanism is unusually concrete:

- assessment is continuous monitoring and evaluation against criteria of success;
- monitoring includes facts and assumptions underlying the plan and seeks opportunities, threats, and information gaps;
- evaluation compares relevant information against success criteria and identifies variances;
- a variance is the difference between the actual situation and what the plan forecast for that time or event;
- criteria of success are developed during planning and can include timelines, loss or consumption rates, effectiveness, actions, facts, and assumptions;
- success criteria can change during execution, but those changes are part of the decision process rather than silently rewritten history ([FM 6-0, paras. 6-6–6-18](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)); and
- execution assessment deliberately compares forecasted outcomes with actual events and asks whether the current plan remains valid ([FM 6-0, paras. 6-77–6-81](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)).

In its own field, this handles operations for which there is no identical previous battle. It does not manufacture a historical control. Instead, it makes the plan's expectations inspectable and turns surprise into an explicit variance that demands interpretation or adjustment.

Translation to a harness: before enabling a candidate change, the intent-to-evidence card must contain a **forecast ledger**. For every declared effect, record:

- metric and exact computation;
- eligible population and exclusions;
- expected direction and smallest owner-meaningful effect;
- observation window or sample requirement;
- supporting signpost (“if the mechanism is working, we expect X”);
- disconfirming signpost (“if X occurs without Y, the explanation is probably wrong”);
- guardrail and abort threshold;
- known nuisance factors and planned blocks; and
- the assumption whose failure invalidates interpretation.

The ledger catches hindsight bias and post-hoc metric selection: after seeing results, an evaluator cannot quietly elevate the one metric that moved favorably. It also catches mechanism failure. If a quoting intervention claims to reduce malformed shell commands, an error-rate reduction accompanied by unchanged malformed-command incidence suggests workload mix or avoidance rather than the claimed mechanism. This example is a proposed reasoning pattern, not a local diagnosis.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on FM 6-0's explicit planned-versus-actual variance definition and UFMCS's requirement to list observable signposts for competing hypotheses, that predeclared forecasts would expose explanations that are currently unfalsifiable in an observation-only evaluation. **UNVERIFIED: the “Forecast Ledger Backtest” below would settle how often retrospective success claims survive their predeclared disconfirmers.**

### 1.3 AAR: instrument before action, reconstruct facts, then explain

The Army AAR is frequently reduced to four questions. The underlying mechanism is richer. Morrison and Meliza describe it as a professional discussion focused on performance standards in which participants establish what happened, why it happened, and how to sustain strengths or improve weaknesses. A formal AAR uses a facilitator who is normally not part of the trained unit. The facilitator prepares data-collection instruments before the exercise, aggregates multiple data sources afterward, reviews objectives and mission, solicits multiple perspectives for significant events, establishes agreement on facts, and only then guides causal and improvement discussion ([Morrison & Meliza 1999, pp. 1–3](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA368651)).

The same report documents why instrumentation matters. Earlier lecture-style critiques relied heavily on umpire opinion and lacked credibility; AAR development moved toward objective performance indicators and tactical instrumentation. It also notes that opposing-force participants added an enemy viewpoint and casualty-exchange data otherwise hidden by the fog of war ([Morrison & Meliza 1999, pp. 5–9 and 13–14](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA368651)).

Translation to a harness: the AAR is an automatically assembled **evidence packet**, followed by a short review in this fixed order:

1. **Intent:** what was supposed to improve, by how much, without harming what?
2. **Ground truth:** what happened in the assigned current and candidate arms?
3. **Variance:** where did actual observations differ from forecasts?
4. **Mechanism:** did the predicted intermediate signposts move?
5. **Alternatives:** what competing explanation fits the same observations?
6. **Decision:** ship, roll back, revise, or continue sampling?
7. **Learning:** one concrete rule or instrument change, with owner and expiry.

The evidence packet must contain raw denominators, assignment counts, exclusions, treatment version, model/provider version, task strata, cost and latency, errors, retries, owner corrections, completion/adjudication, and links to sampled traces. A summary without denominators is not an AAR-quality record. The “opposing force” translation is a reviewer who sees blinded arm labels and is explicitly tasked to make the strongest case that the apparent winner is an artifact.

This catches:

- an aggregate improvement caused by an easier task mix;
- a change that shifts failures from visible tool errors into silent incompletion;
- a regression isolated to one task class or platform;
- treatment leakage or a mid-test version change;
- a cost or latency regression hidden behind quality gains;
- a result driven by a handful of repeated retry loops;
- a plausible causal story with no movement in its intermediate mechanism; and
- an instrumentation outage that makes “zero failures” indistinguishable from “zero observations.”

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the documented shift from subjective critique to instrumented, multi-perspective AARs, that evidence packets plus blinded challenge would catch at least some false wins missed by top-line metrics. **UNVERIFIED: the “Adversarial AAR Yield” experiment below would settle the incremental defect yield of blinded challenge over the normal review.**

### 1.4 Red teaming: attack assumptions and precommit signposts

UFMCS defines red teaming as a function intended to avoid groupthink, mirror imaging, cultural missteps, and tunnel vision, and to challenge assumptions in plans and operations ([UFMCS handbook, pp. 1–4](https://archive.org/details/ufmcs-red-team-handbook-2012)). Three procedures are especially relevant:

1. **Pre-mortem:** assume the plan became an embarrassing failure; have individuals independently generate causes before group consolidation; revisit the plan; periodically review the list during execution ([UFMCS handbook, pp. 163–164](https://archive.org/details/ufmcs-red-team-handbook-2012)).
2. **Key Assumptions Check:** write the analytic line, articulate stated and unstated premises, challenge why each must be true, reduce the list to premises necessary to sustain the judgment, and specify information that would invalidate each premise ([UFMCS handbook, pp. 176–178](https://archive.org/details/ufmcs-red-team-handbook-2012)).
3. **Indicators/signposts:** for competing hypotheses, list observable events expected under each, review the lists repeatedly, and use both positive and negative indicators. The handbook says this shifts disputes toward agreed observable criteria and makes the reasoning inspectable ([UFMCS handbook, pp. 180–181](https://archive.org/details/ufmcs-red-team-handbook-2012)).

Translation to a harness: a candidate cannot begin a comparison until a ten-minute pre-mortem yields at least one failure mode in each category: measurement, task mix, treatment delivery, behavior adaptation, delayed harm, and owner-value substitution. Each material assumption receives an observable invalidation signpost. At AAR time, the reviewer must test at least two competing hypotheses:

- H1: the harness change caused the observed outcome;
- H2: task mix, time trend, model/provider drift, instrumentation, or chance caused it.

When realistic, add H3: the candidate changed what was attempted rather than improving how attempts performed.

This catches “unknown unknowns” only in the modest sense of forcing more candidate explanations into view. It does not guarantee discovery of genuinely unimagined failure modes. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the UFMCS procedures and their explicit aim of surfacing hidden premises, that assumption checks will uncover consequential evaluation dependencies before deployment. **UNVERIFIED: the “Premortem Recall Trial” below would settle recall against a seeded set of evaluation defects.**

### 1.5 OODA: useful operating rhythm, rejected as the measurement mechanism

Boyd's published discourse presents observation, orientation, decision, and action as interacting feedback processes; the published loop includes feedback and “unfolding interaction with environment,” not merely a four-box linear checklist ([Boyd, *A Discourse on Winning and Losing*, Air University Press, appendix “The OODA Loop,” 2018](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0151_Boyd_Discourse_Winning_Losing.pdf)). It is valuable as a reminder that sensing, interpretation, choice, and action repeat under changing conditions.

It does **not** specify a baseline, counterfactual, outcome metric, sampling scheme, or causal estimator. Faster cycling can make a team react more quickly to noise. Orientation also embeds prior experience and analysis, which can entrench the very success narrative under test. Therefore OODA does not transfer as the answer to this observability problem. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the contents of Boyd's loop and the absence of an evaluation design within it, that “add an OODA loop” would be an under-specified recommendation here. OODA is retained only as the outer cadence—observe evidence, orient with the AAR, decide, then act—not as proof that an action helped.

## 2. The concrete build: Mission Evidence Cycle

The architecture is deliberately small. It uses the existing hook/skill/config bus assumed by the program brief, writes append-only local evidence, and invokes an analysis skill at decision points. It does not introduce a second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. Thus it passes the anti-overfit gate by design.

### Proposal 1 — Intent-to-evidence card — UNVERIFIED (settle with Hook Compatibility Probe E0)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Add a versioned JSON or YAML record for each material harness change. Required fields:

```yaml
change_id: immutable-id
owner_intent:
  purpose: owner-visible problem
  desired_end_state: observable outcome
  constraints: [quality, safety, cost, latency, autonomy]
treatment:
  current_version: exact-id
  candidate_version: exact-id
population:
  eligible: explicit predicate
  excluded: explicit predicate and reason
forecast:
  primary_metric: exact computation
  smallest_meaningful_effect: value
  supporting_signposts: []
  disconfirming_signposts: []
  guardrails: []
  abort_thresholds: []
assumptions:
  - premise: text
    confidence_band: allowed vocabulary
    evidence: citation or measurement
    invalidation_signal: observable event
design:
  assignment: blocked-randomized | deterministic-switchback | replay-pair
  blocks: [task_class, platform, model_version]
  stopping_rule: sample/time plus minimum per block
decision_owner: identity
expiry: timestamp
```

“Exact computation” means numerator, denominator, unit of analysis, aggregation, missing-data treatment, and direction. A schema validator may reject a card that omits these, but it should report all missing fields in one pass and preserve an escape hatch for exploratory changes. This is configuration, not another runtime.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, because the record is simple but integration points were intentionally not inspected in this ideation unit, that this can ride an existing change workflow without material orchestration complexity. **UNVERIFIED: E0 must create a disposable known-present card, pass it through each intended hook/config path, and independently verify both the emitted event and persisted card before adoption.**

### Proposal 2 — Concurrent baseline by blocked switchback — UNVERIFIED (settle with Harness Switchback Trial E1)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

When no historical baseline exists, retain the current harness as the concurrent comparator for a bounded sample. Assign eligible sessions to current or candidate using a deterministic hash of `experiment_id + stable task/session key`, stratified by a few high-value nuisance factors such as task class, operating environment, and model version. Log assignment before treatment. Do not let outcome data determine assignment.

If live randomization is inappropriate, use a hierarchy of weaker designs and label the evidence accordingly:

1. blocked live randomization;
2. deterministic switchback by predeclared time blocks;
3. paired replay of frozen tasks with randomized arm order;
4. interrupted time series with explicit concurrent drift indicators; or
5. forecast-to-actual variance only.

Only levels 1–3 warrant causal language, and even level 3 warrants it only for replayable behavior. Levels 4–5 detect association or surprise, not causation. NIST's blocking guidance supports accounting for nuisance factors within homogeneous blocks and randomizing remaining variation ([NIST randomized block designs](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm)).

The comparison should change one harness factor at a time when feasible. If a full replacement is the treatment, treat it as one bundle and do not attribute the result to any internal component. Report per-block effects before the pooled effect, absolute denominators, missingness, and a guardrail vector rather than a single composite score.

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on randomized-block design principles, that a concurrent blocked comparator will control task-mix and time drift better than comparing post-change sessions with an undefined historical “before.” **UNVERIFIED: E1 must compare its estimated treatment effect with a seeded intervention of known direction under deliberately shifted task mix; success requires correct direction, no false win in the null arm, and balanced assignment within declared blocks.**

### Proposal 3 — Dual-channel evidence packet and AAR skill — UNVERIFIED (settle with Detector Independence Drill E2 and Adversarial AAR Yield E3)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

A post-session hook appends minimal treatment and operational telemetry. A separate evaluation path scores the saved task artifact or obtains blinded owner/reviewer preference. At the stopping point, an existing skill renders an AAR packet from the card plus both channels.

The channels must be mechanically independent enough that a single detector failure cannot create a clean bill of health:

- **Channel A — process telemetry:** hook/tool event stream computes attempts, errors, retries, latency, cost, treatment delivery, and intermediate mechanism signposts.
- **Channel B — outcome evidence:** a separately invoked replay/adjudicator checks completion and artifact quality, or a blinded human preference compares outputs without seeing arm labels.

Each channel gets a known-present sentinel during the trial. A hook sentinel can be a disposable, unmistakable event. The outcome sentinel can be a seeded artifact property detected by the separate scorer. A “zero” is reportable as absence only after its detector finds the corresponding known-present sentinel. A claim that an instrument is broken requires two mechanically independent signals—for example, missing sentinel telemetry **and** an independently observed end-to-end treatment-delivery mismatch. Re-running the same parser does not count.

The AAR skill produces:

- the intent and unchanged predeclared forecasts;
- assignment balance and exclusions;
- channel A and B results side by side;
- per-block and pooled effects;
- guardrail breaches;
- forecast/actual variances;
- assumption/signpost status;
- strongest candidate-favoring and candidate-opposing explanations;
- sampled trace links; and
- a decision with confidence band and evidence.

If channels disagree, the default decision is “inconclusive; investigate,” not an averaged score. This avoids hiding a quality loss behind operational reliability or vice versa.

It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the AAR history's emphasis on objective instrumentation, multiple sources, and opposing-force information, that two channels plus sentinels will catch silent detector failures and process/outcome tradeoffs. **UNVERIFIED: E2 seeds one failure visible only to each channel and one detector outage; the gate passes only if the packet distinguishes all three. E3 compares normal versus blinded-adversarial AARs on the same trials and measures unique consequential defects found.**

### Proposal 4 — Decision and expiry rule — UNVERIFIED (settle with Decision Reproducibility E4)

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Every trial ends in one of four explicit states: ship, roll back, revise and retest, or inconclusive. A ship decision requires:

- the primary metric clearing its predeclared smallest meaningful effect or a predeclared practical decision threshold;
- no abort-threshold breach;
- no unexplained disagreement between process and outcome channels;
- treatment delivery and both sentinels verified;
- no critical block with a material opposite-direction effect hidden by pooling; and
- an expiry/reassessment date.

The rule must not automatically demand conventional statistical significance when sample sizes are tiny; instead it must expose uncertainty, raw counts, practical effect, and the cost of each decision. It is **likely (55-80%)**, based on the need to act under uncertainty reflected in mission command and on the small, heterogeneous samples typical of interactive work as posited by this design, that a four-state rule will be more honest than forcing every trial into win/loss. **UNVERIFIED: the “Decision Reproducibility” experiment E4 will compare independent decision-makers applying the rule to archived blinded packets.**

## 3. What this would catch that ordinary post-change observability misses

The following are capabilities of the proposed design, not claims about defects in the present harness:

| Missed condition | Why a post-change dashboard can call it a win | Mission Evidence Cycle catch |
|---|---|---|
| Easier tasks arrive after deployment | Aggregate error rate falls | Blocked concurrent current arm; per-task-stratum effects |
| Candidate avoids a difficult capability | Tool failures fall because attempts fall | Attempt denominator plus outcome-completion channel |
| Faster but shallower work | Latency improves | Intent guardrail and blinded artifact outcome |
| Error moves downstream | Named error declines, corrections or incomplete artifacts rise | Dual-channel guardrail vector |
| Instrument stops reporting | Metric becomes zero | Known-present sentinel plus independent delivery/outcome signal |
| Candidate is enabled inconsistently | Results dilute or invert | Assignment-before-treatment and observed treatment version |
| One subgroup is harmed | Pooled average remains positive | Minimum samples and effect report per block |
| Model/provider changes simultaneously | Time-series jump is attributed to harness | Concurrent arm on same version/time blocks |
| Explanation is retrospective | Any favorable movement gets a story | Immutable forecast ledger and disconfirming signposts |
| Winner is driven by a few loops | Session mean looks favorable or unfavorable | Raw denominators, clustered unit analysis, trace samples |
| Novelty effect fades | Early window looks strong | Predeclared expiry and repeat switchback |
| Full replacement wins but why is unclear | Bundle has a positive aggregate | Honest bundle-level conclusion; no component attribution |
| Metric definition changes midstream | Revised score preserves apparent success | Versioned computation and immutable original forecast |

It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the mechanics in the table and NIST's account of nuisance-factor control, that the design can detect task-mix confounding and detector silence when the necessary assignment, strata, and sentinel data are present. **UNVERIFIED: E1 and E2 are the settling experiments.** It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, based on uncertainty about the frequency and observability of silent quality loss in this specific harness, that blinded outcome adjudication will often reverse an apparent operational-metric win. **UNVERIFIED: E3 settles that frequency.**

## 4. Adversarial analysis of the analogy

### 4.1 Structural assumptions that do transfer

1. **Unique, changing situations.** Military operations cannot assume a perfectly repeated previous case; interactive agent work also varies by task. The transferable response is prospective criteria and forecast/actual variance, not reliance on a stable historical average ([FM 6-0, paras. 6-6–6-17](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)).
2. **Action before complete certainty.** Mission command explicitly reduces the amount of certainty required to act by anchoring decentralized action in intent ([FM 6-0, para. 1-74](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)). Harness maintainers likewise may need bounded decisions before large samples accrue. The analogy supports reversible trials and explicit uncertainty, not reckless deployment.
3. **Plans alter during execution.** Doctrine allows success criteria and plans to change, while continuous assessment records why ([FM 6-0, paras. 6-77–6-81](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)). Harness experiments also encounter provider changes, task shifts, and discovered instrumentation gaps. Versioning those changes transfers well.
4. **Local perspective is incomplete.** AAR facilitators combine multiple perspectives, and opposing-force participants add information unavailable to the trained unit ([Morrison & Meliza 1999, pp. 13–14](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA368651)). Process telemetry and owner-visible outcome are similarly non-substitutable views.
5. **Success creates complacency and ownership.** UFMCS's pre-mortem and assumption-check procedures explicitly challenge plan ownership and hidden premises ([UFMCS handbook, pp. 163–164, 176–181](https://archive.org/details/ufmcs-red-team-handbook-2012)). That maps directly to authors evaluating their own harness changes.

### 4.2 Structural assumptions that do not transfer

1. **An enemy is not software noise.** Military assessment frequently reasons about an adaptive adversary. Harness failures often arise from stochastic models, platform drift, malformed inputs, or measurement bugs with no strategic intent. An “OPFOR” reviewer can challenge reasoning, but treating every anomaly as adversarial would overfit the analogy.
2. **A battle outcome is not a treatment effect.** Actual-versus-forecast variance says the plan was surprised; it does not say the harness change caused the surprise. Only concurrent assignment, blocking, replication, or another credible counterfactual addresses that question. The Army literature review's demand for causal transfer studies is direct evidence of this limitation ([Keiser & Arthur 2011, pp. 24–25](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA543054)).
3. **Command hierarchy can suppress evidence.** Commander's intent is valuable as a semantic anchor, but military hierarchy and rank have no necessary analogue in a personal harness. A “commander” metaphor could cause reviewers to optimize for declared intent and ignore emergent owner value. The translation therefore gives the owner decision authority but preserves blinded evidence and an “unexpected benefit/harm” field.
4. **Criteria that evolve can become moving goalposts.** Doctrine permits criteria to change during execution ([FM 6-0, para. 6-17 and 6-79](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)). That is operationally sensible but experimentally dangerous. The harness must preserve the original card, version amendments prospectively, and report results under both original and amended criteria.
5. **AAR culture is not causal instrumentation.** Skilled facilitation, candid participation, and objective exercise records were material to the military AAR mechanism ([Morrison & Meliza 1999, pp. 1–9](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA368651)). A generated prose summary without those inputs is cargo cult. It may increase confidence without increasing truth.
6. **Repeated synthetic tasks are not live owner work.** Replays improve comparability but remove interactive adaptation, changing context, and owner intervention. Live blocked trials improve ecological validity but cannot perfectly pair tasks. The design reports replay and live evidence separately rather than pretending either subsumes the other.
7. **Operational success may tolerate costs software owners do not.** Military doctrines balance mission, force, time, and risk in a context unlike maintainability, privacy, monetary cost, and user trust. Those constraints must be supplied by owner intent, not borrowed from military values.

### 4.3 Net judgment

The analogy survives only at the level of **prospective sensemaking and disciplined review**. It fails as a causal inference method. It is **very likely (80-90%)**, based on the close structural correspondence between doctrine's planned criteria/forecast variance and the target's missing retrospective reference, that commander-intent + assessment + AAR + red-team signposts is a useful shell for evaluation. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the Army's own research gap and the lack of a counterfactual in AAR/OODA, that the shell must be paired with a concurrent experimental comparison before it can support “the harness change helped.”

That limited conclusion is stronger than either forced extreme. Rejecting the entire borrow would discard a mature discipline for precommitting purpose, collecting evidence before action, and separating facts from explanation. Importing it wholesale would confuse a learning process with causal proof.

## 5. Settling experiments

These experiments are part of the recommendation because every effectiveness and integration claim above is forward-looking.

### E0 — Hook Compatibility Probe

Goal: settle whether the card, assignment record, telemetry append, and AAR invocation can ride the existing bus without another runtime.

Method:

1. Create a disposable experiment with one known-present card and one sentinel event.
2. Pass it through each proposed existing config/hook/skill point.
3. Verify delivery in channel A.
4. Independently verify the persisted card and rendered AAR packet in channel B.
5. Count implementation lines excluding tests and generated schema.

Pass: all required fields survive, both independent probes find their known-present controls, and each BUILD-SMALL component remains below approximately 200 lines. Failure means downgrade the integration proposal or stop; it does not justify a daemon.

### E1 — Harness Switchback Trial

Goal: settle whether a concurrent blocked comparison recovers a known effect under task-mix drift.

Method:

1. Assemble replayable tasks in at least three declared strata.
2. Seed a safe candidate intervention with a known directional effect on one mechanism metric, plus a null candidate.
3. Shift the task mix over time deliberately.
4. Compare naive before/after, unblocked concurrent, and blocked concurrent estimates.
5. Randomize arm order within blocks and preserve exact treatment versions.

Pass: blocked comparison finds the known direction, does not declare the null a win, reports the deliberately harmed or unchanged stratum, and identifies the task-mix shift. This experiment tests the evaluator, not merely the candidate feature.

### E2 — Detector Independence Drill

Goal: settle whether dual channels prevent false “zero” conclusions.

Method:

1. Seed one process failure visible only in hook telemetry.
2. Seed one artifact defect visible only in the independent outcome scorer.
3. Disable one telemetry detector while preserving actual treatment execution.
4. Include known-present sentinels for both detectors.

Pass: the packet distinguishes all three cases; it never equates missing telemetry with zero failures; and two mechanically independent signals are required before declaring an instrument broken.

### E3 — Adversarial AAR Yield

Goal: settle whether blinded red review adds information rather than ceremony.

Method:

1. Prepare completed evidence packets with arm labels hidden.
2. Have one reviewer apply the normal AAR and another make the strongest artifact/confounding case using the assumption checklist.
3. Adjudicate alleged defects against raw assignments, traces, and outcomes.
4. Measure unique consequential defects, false alarms, review time, and decision reversals.

Pass threshold must be chosen prospectively. A reasonable initial decision rule is ship the adversarial step only if it finds at least one unique consequential defect per ten reviews without more than two false alarms per true defect. It is **roughly even (45-55%)**, because no local frequency data were examined, that this threshold is attainable. E3 settles it.

### E4 — Decision Reproducibility

Goal: settle whether cards and four-state rules improve decision consistency.

Method:

1. Give independent reviewers blinded archived packets, half with the intent/forecast card and half with equivalent metrics but no card.
2. Ask for ship/rollback/revise/inconclusive plus a confidence band.
3. Cross over packet formats after a washout.
4. Score inter-reviewer agreement, evidence citation, post-hoc metric switching, and time.

Pass: preregister the minimum agreement improvement and no material increase in decision time. Do not infer benefit merely because reviewers report liking the card.

### E5 — Forecast Ledger Backtest / Blind Intent Classification

Goal: settle whether predeclared forecasts and intent prevent retrospective rationalization.

Method:

1. For upcoming safe changes, collect developer predictions before results.
2. After results, have blinded reviewers classify whether purpose was met under the original criteria.
3. Separately let reviewers see only post-hoc narratives.
4. Compare mechanism-signpost accuracy, decision agreement, and frequency of selecting undeclared favorable metrics.

Pass: the preregistered ledger must reduce undeclared metric selection and improve out-of-sample prediction or reviewer agreement. A more polished narrative is not a pass criterion.

## 7. Alternatives considered and rejected

### “Just establish a historical baseline first”

Rejected as the sole answer. A calibration period is useful, but task mix, model/provider behavior, and owner work can change between periods. A concurrent comparator better separates treatment from time when safely available; NIST's blocking guidance explains why nuisance factors need explicit handling ([NIST randomized block designs](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm)). Historical data remains descriptive context, not an automatic control.

### “AAR every session”

Rejected. Morrison and Meliza describe formal AARs as preparation- and facilitation-intensive, while FM 6-0 says the best monitoring is the least intrusive ([Morrison & Meliza 1999, pp. 1–3](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA368651); [FM 6-0, para. 6-10](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)). Per-session automatic evidence capture is justified; full review belongs at change decision points or sentinel breaches.

### “A single harness health score”

Rejected. A composite permits quality, cost, latency, reliability, and autonomy to compensate for one another invisibly. Commander's intent instead distinguishes desired end state from constraints, and the proposed decision rule preserves a vector of primary effect plus guardrails ([FM 6-0, paras. 1-68–1-74](https://archive.org/details/FM_6_M_C_C_C_A_F_2003)).

### “AI red-team critic on every trace”

Rejected. That resembles a trained/standing critic and adds cost, correlated model bias, and scaffolding without evidence of incremental yield. It risks a previously rejected component. The proposal uses a bounded existing skill at decision points and requires E3 to earn its continuation.

### “Observability SaaS / durable experiment service”

Rejected by the anti-overfit gate. The required state is a change card, append-only assignments/events, and a rendered packet. None justifies a daemon, durable-execution engine, or external observability platform. If existing hooks cannot support those operations, E0 fails and this unit recommends stopping rather than silently expanding to BUILD-LARGE.

### “OODA is enough”

Rejected for the reasons in §1.5. OODA provides cadence, not a comparator or causal estimator ([Boyd, 2018](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0151_Boyd_Discourse_Winning_Losing.pdf)).

### “Full replacement cannot be tested incrementally”

Rejected. The entire replacement can be one treatment arm for eligible work or frozen replays. The evaluator may conclude that the bundle wins or loses. It must not infer which internal mechanism caused that bundle-level result. This preserves full replacement as an option without pretending a bundle comparison supplies component attribution.

## 8. Failure modes and stop conditions

Stop or downgrade the design if any of the following occurs:

- treatment assignment changes owner-visible risk without explicit owner authorization;
- current and candidate behavior cannot be isolated or treatment version cannot be observed;
- sentinels cannot validate both evidence channels;
- eligible tasks are too few or heterogeneous to support the declared decision;
- replay is the only evidence but the proposed benefit depends on live interaction;
- reviewers repeatedly revise success criteria after seeing outcomes;
- the AAR produces prose without raw denominators, assignment balance, and trace links;
- adversarial review creates more false alarms than its preregistered tolerance;
- the BUILD-SMALL boundary cannot be met on the existing bus; or
- maintaining the system requires any rejected architectural component.

In those cases, retain a descriptive forecast/actual log and use “inconclusive” language. It is **almost certain (90-99%)**, based on the causal disanalogy documented above, that an honest descriptive result is preferable to a causal claim produced without treatment integrity or a valid comparator.

## 10. Final recommendation

Adopt the military borrow only in this constrained form:

> **Intent before metrics; forecast before observation; instrument before action; facts before explanation; disconfirmers before decision; concurrent comparison before causality.**

The first five clauses are the transferable military contribution. The last clause is the correction required by the disanalogy. Start with CONFIG cards, prove value manually, and add only the two BUILD-SMALL pieces that pass their explicit experiments. Do not create a second runtime or an observability platform.
