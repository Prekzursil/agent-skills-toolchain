# X145 — Cross-domain borrow: cartography → finite agent context

## Unit boundary and answer in one paragraph

This unit deliberately does not inspect the local harness, corpus, audit findings, or subject repositories. The assignment identifies this as an ideation lens and says that other units perform local grounding. Accordingly, “currently miss” below means failure modes that a conventional recency-, truncation-, or scalar-relevance context policy cannot represent or detect; it is **not** a claim that a particular local component has been proved to exhibit them.

The cartographic mechanism worth borrowing is **constraint-based, purpose- and scale-dependent generalisation backed by linked multiple representations**. A cartographer does not merely rank features and delete the bottom of the list. At a target scale and for a target purpose, the cartographer selects among qualitatively different transformations—selection, simplification, aggregation, collapse, typification, exaggeration, and displacement—while trying to preserve such properties as connectivity, relative relationships, characteristic patterns, and conceptual identity. Representations at multiple scales can remain linked to the same underlying object. The concrete harness translation is a small “context cartographer”: maintain addressable source events, derive a few linked levels of detail, compose the active window at the scale appropriate to each subproblem, and reject a derived context if explicit semantic-preservation constraints fail. This transfer is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform a recency-only or one-summary compactor on long, stateful tasks because cartographic generalisation explicitly models heterogeneous transformations and preservation constraints, while the baseline classes do not; the evidence for the direction is the mature cartographic literature below, but the magnitude and applicability to agent traces are **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a shadow replay comparing policies on held-out long sessions with invariant probes and task-outcome scoring**.

## 1. What cartography has actually solved

### 1.1 Generalisation is purpose/scale adaptation, not uniform shrinking

The UCGIS Geographic Information Science & Technology Body of Knowledge distinguishes data stored at one scale/resolution from data rendered at another. It explains that cartographic generalisation derives representations suitable for a target context such as a map purpose or zoom level; reducing data volume is common but not the sole motivation. It also warns that generalisation can reduce accuracy and precision and can alter analysis. This matters: omission is a controlled loss operation, not a truth-preserving compression theorem. [UCGIS GIS&T Body of Knowledge, “Scale and Generalization”](https://gistbok-ltb.ucgis.org/current/concept/CV-03-004)

The International Cartographic Association’s examples show that generalisation is plural: simplification, smoothing, exaggeration, displacement, typification, and label selection/scaling are used for different conflicts. Its simultaneous-generalisation example formulates competing cartographic requirements as constraints and solves for a compromise rather than treating all features independently. [ICA Commission on Multi-Scale Cartography, results and examples](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html)

The own-field mechanism can therefore be stated as:

1. Fix the **map purpose**, target scale, medium, and legibility limits.
2. Detect conflicts at that scale: too much detail, overlapping symbols, broken visual hierarchy, or loss of a characteristic network/pattern.
3. Choose operators appropriate to object type and conflict, rather than applying one universal deletion rule.
4. Evaluate the result against both change-seeking constraints (make it legible) and preservation constraints (retain essential structure, relationships, and recognisability).
5. At another scale, use or derive another representation; do not pretend the small-scale result is the detailed reality.

This description is supported independently by (a) the UCGIS account of scale, purpose, fitness for use, and analytical loss and (b) the ICA examples of distinct operators and simultaneous constraint optimisation. These are different publications and different evidence forms (a disciplinary knowledge base versus operational research examples).

### 1.2 The specific operators encode different kinds of deliberate omission

The useful distinction is not the exact cartographic vocabulary but the fact that information loss has **types**:

| Cartographic operator | What it does in its own field | Context analogue |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | Retains some whole features and omits others. | Keep a source event/fact verbatim or leave it out of this view. |
| Simplification | Reduces geometric detail while retaining a feature’s recognisable form. | Remove wording and incidental steps while retaining a claim, actor, object, qualification, and outcome. |
| Aggregation / amalgamation | Combines nearby or similar objects into a larger object. | Turn many homogeneous calls or observations into a group record with count, range, and exceptions. |
| Collapse | Changes representation dimension, such as detailed building footprints to points or groups. | Replace a transcript segment with a state transition: prior state, action, resulting state, and evidence addresses. |
| Typification | Retains representative members/patterns while reducing the number of objects. | Keep representative examples plus population statistics and exception IDs, instead of either every repetition or one unlabelled anecdote. |
| Exaggeration | Enlarges a characteristic but sub-resolution feature so it remains perceptible. | Preserve a rare, high-consequence exception, owner prohibition, or exact literal even when its token footprint exceeds its frequency-based share. |
| Displacement | Moves symbols to resolve conflicts while preserving interpretable relationships. | Move payload out of the active window but leave a stable address, typed synopsis, and dependency edge. This is the weakest operator-level analogy because context storage has no direct geometric collision; it should be treated only as addressable off-window placement, not as permission to distort. |

The ICA examples directly document simplification, smoothing, exaggeration, displacement, typification, and label selection. [ICA Commission on Multi-Scale Cartography](https://generalisation.icaci.org/results.html) A multiple-representation study describes geometric and semantic abstraction changing across resolution and links objects representing the same world entity at scales from 1:25K through 1:500K. [Çobankaya, “Updating in Multiple Representation Databases,” ICC 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/530.html)

### 1.3 Multiple representations remain related to a master

The ICC 2015 multiple-representation database work is especially relevant. It describes the update problem caused by independent datasets at different scales, then creates relationships between representations of the same real-world object so updates can eventually propagate from the detailed master to lower-resolution models. [ICC 2015 paper](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/3/530.html) A separate paper describes a multi-scale tree whose zoom operation replaces a current graphic representation with a more detailed one appropriate to the new scale. [van Oosterom and Schenkelaars, “Multiple representations for cartographic objects in a multi-scale tree,” DOI landing page](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-8493(94)90008-6)

The transferable point is provenance-bearing plurality: “the road” can have several purpose-appropriate renderings while retaining identity across them. That is materially different from recursively summarising prose into prose, where identity and provenance commonly become implicit.

### 1.4 Constraints protect structure that per-item importance cannot

Cartographic quality is relational. Selective road omission is described as difficult specifically because both density differences and network connectivity must be retained; the cited approach calculates local mesh density rather than selecting roads by independent scores alone. [Chen et al., “Selective omission of road features based on mesh density,” Cartography and Geographic Information Science](https://doi.org/10.1559/152304008783475661) Recent USGS work likewise frames evaluation around retention of density and spatial-distribution patterns across scales. [USGS, “Assessment of density pattern retention of generalized data”](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70271253)

Constraint-based generalisation treats legibility, topology/connectivity, relative position, shape, and conceptual consistency as potentially competing requirements. A published quality-control model explicitly evaluates position/orientation, shape, legibility, topological consistency, and conceptual consistency, with violation thresholds. [Touya et al.-related constraint-based quality model discussion, ISPRS International Journal of Geo-Information](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7086/3/2/17)

Thus a tiny feature may be dispensable alone yet indispensable as the only connector between two network components. The corresponding context fact may be low-frequency and old yet be the only causal bridge between a user correction and a later implementation choice. A scalar top-*k* importance score cannot generally express “retain at least one witness for every live causal edge” without adding relational constraints.

### 1.5 What not to borrow: Töpfer’s Radical Law as an eviction formula

Töpfer and Pillewizer’s Radical Law estimates how many objects should remain as map-scale denominators change. The hydrography evaluation describes it as an empirically based root-law for feature counts and also reports limited applicability at large scales. [Wilmer and Brewer, “Application of the Radical Law in Generalization of National Hydrography Data for Multiscale Mapping”](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVIII/part4/files/Wilmer.pdf)

It would be a category error to set an agent token budget with the same square-root formula. Cartographic scale has a physical ratio and symbol-resolution constraints; conversational “distance” and token budgets do not. Task value can jump discontinuously when the goal changes. The Radical Law is useful only as a warning that **density should change deliberately with scale**, not as a transferable equation. Recommending the equation for context eviction is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be sound because the source paper’s independent variable is a map-scale denominator and the target has no validated homologous quantity; **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment would fit candidate count laws on training traces and test calibration and outcomes on held-out task families**, but this unit recommends not spending implementation effort before the stronger constraint-based design is tested.

## 2. Structural transfer test

### 2.1 Where the analogy holds

| Structural property | Map | Long-horizon agent context | Transfer assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finite presentation surface | Paper/screen and perceptual resolution | Token window and attention/compute | Strong: both force selection. |
| A richer source than can be presented | Landscape model / detailed source data | Event transcript, artifacts, tool results, decisions | Strong if source events remain addressable outside the active view. |
| Detail needed varies by purpose | Navigation, thematic map, overview | Current subgoal, diagnosis, planning, execution | Strong: the same history can require different views. |
| Some properties are relational | Connectivity, adjacency, density, pattern | Causality, dependency, contradiction, commitment, provenance | Strong at the abstract graph level, though relation extraction is harder in language. |
| Loss is unavoidable and should be explicit | Smaller-scale map omits/distorts details | Finite prompt omits/paraphrases history | Strong. |
| Multiple representations can share identity | Same geographic object across scales | Same event/decision at verbatim, event, episode, and project levels | Strong if stable IDs/backlinks are enforced. |

The core transfer—typed transformations plus preservation constraints plus cross-scale identity—is **likely (55-80%)** to hold. Evidence: both domains have a finite presentation surface, a richer addressable source, purpose-dependent detail, and relational invariants; uncertainty comes from semantic extraction and model use of the resulting structures. **UNVERIFIED; settle with the shadow replay specified in §5, including ablations for operators, links, and constraints.**

### 2.2 Where it breaks

1. **Map scale is globally ordered; relevance is not.** Zooming out monotonically reduces spatial detail. A conversation can suddenly require an exact literal from 500 turns ago. Context scale is therefore per-object and per-subproblem, not one global zoom level.
2. **Geographic topology is comparatively explicit; semantic topology is inferred.** Roads have encoded junctions. “This correction invalidates that plan” may be implicit, ambiguous, or model-generated. A wrong dependency edge can preserve the wrong story extremely efficiently.
3. **Maps primarily support human visual inference; context steers an acting model.** A misleading map is harmful, but a misleading context can cause an agent to mutate external state. Preservation checks must therefore be stricter for permissions, destructive-action scope, exact identifiers, and completion status.
4. **Spatial locality is not semantic locality.** Adjacent messages may be unrelated; distant messages may form a single dependency chain. Aggregation by transcript proximity is not analogous to spatial aggregation.
5. **Cartographic distortion can be visible; summary distortion often masquerades as fact.** A map reader knows a map is a representation. An agent may not distinguish source text from a lossy synopsis unless the representation declares its level and provenance.
6. **There is no stable universal map purpose.** Agent goals evolve. Derived views must be invalidated or recomposed after goal changes; static summaries will stale.

These disanalogies rule out “one progressively shorter summary” and a universal importance ordering. They do **not** rule out the narrower borrow, because the proposed mechanism uses per-object levels, explicit provenance, and task-specific constraints. Whether semantic relations can be extracted accurately enough is **roughly even (45-55%)** based on the contrast between explicit GIS topology and implicit natural-language dependencies; **UNVERIFIED; settle by double-annotating causal/commitment edges in a stratified trace sample and measuring precision/recall before enabling those edges as hard constraints**.

## 3. Proposed harness translation

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: linked context representations with cartographic operators

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Build a deterministic context-curation hook or skill, not a second orchestrator and not a new memory service. It consumes already-available turn/tool events and emits a small, inspectable “context atlas” alongside the existing task artifacts. The implementation premise—that the existing bus exposes an appropriate compaction or pre-prompt boundary—is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a minimal no-op hook that logs event IDs at the intended boundary in a disposable test session**. This heading inherits UNVERIFIED because that premise controls feasibility.

Use four representation levels, each retaining source IDs:

| Level | Contents | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| L0 source | Immutable/addressable original message, tool result, or artifact reference; large payload can remain outside prompt. | Exact recovery and audit, never wholesale prompt inclusion. |
| L1 event | Typed record: actor, action/claim, object, status, time/order, confidence/qualification, source IDs. | Current-step reasoning and exact state changes. |
| L2 episode | Goal, initial state, actions, resulting state, unresolved items, corrections, errors/retries as grouped distributions, exceptional events, source spans. | Returning to a workstream after many turns. |
| L3 project map | Current objective, accepted constraints, live decisions, dependency/contradiction edges, open commitments, milestone status, pointers to episodes. | Orientation and planning. |

Composition must be **mixed-scale**. For the live subgoal, include L1 records and exact literals where required; for adjacent workstreams, include L2; for dormant workstreams, L3 pointers. Expand an object through its stable ID when it becomes relevant. Never feed “L3 of everything” as though it were equally detailed truth.

Each derived record declares the operator used (`select`, `simplify`, `aggregate`, `collapse`, `typify`, `exaggerate`, `offwindow`) and its source IDs. Operator choice is type-specific:

- Owner instructions, permissions, prohibitions, exact paths/identifiers, and destructive-action scope: **select/exaggerate** exact text; do not paraphrase away negation or quantifiers.
- Repeated homogeneous tool success: **aggregate** with count, time span, representative IDs, and all outlier IDs.
- Retry loops: **typify** the dominant fingerprint but retain counts and every distinct fingerprint; do not keep just the last attempt.
- Completed work episode: **collapse** into before/action/after/evidence, while representing completion separately from partial progress.
- Explanatory prose: **simplify**, retaining qualifiers, provenance, and contradiction edges.
- Large payloads: **offwindow** with typed digest and resolvable address; “displacement” must not alter content.

This design is **likely (55-80%)** to reduce context tokens without the specific information loss of untyped recursive summaries because every transformation is declared and linked, and operators preserve different statistics/semantics. **UNVERIFIED; settle with a token-matched replay against recursive-summary and recency baselines, measuring exact-literal recovery, correction retention, retry-distribution recovery, and end-task success.**

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: semantic preservation constraints as a preflight gate

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Before accepting a derived view, run deterministic checks over typed fields. The feasibility premise—that context records can be produced in a constrained schema reliably enough for deterministic checks—is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is schema-conformance measurement on 200 stratified episodes, including malformed tool outputs and owner corrections**.

Minimum hard constraints:

1. Every derived assertion has at least one resolvable source ID.
2. Negation, modal force (`must`, `may`, `never`), and numeric/path/ref literals marked exact survive byte-for-byte.
3. No status promotion: `planned → started → partial → verified → done` may not advance in a summary without a cited source event of that status.
4. Every unresolved owner request and every explicit correction remains in the active project map until a cited resolution closes it.
5. Each retained decision retains its supersession/contradiction edges; both sides remain reachable.
6. Aggregates carry population count, covered source range, number of distinct outcomes/fingerprints, and exception IDs.
7. Causal/dependency connectivity is preserved for live goals: every live goal has a path to its governing constraints, latest decision, unresolved blocker, and evidence state.
8. A representation records freshness: when a source member changes or the goal changes materially, dependent derived records become stale and must not be presented as current.

Use soft budgets only after hard constraints. If hard constraints exceed the token budget, keep compact typed records and addresses, then evict lower-purpose material; do not silently drop a constraint. A visible overflow state is preferable to a falsely coherent summary.

The gate is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch status promotion, orphaned claims, dropped exact literals, missing aggregate exceptions, and stale summaries *when the relevant facts were typed correctly*, because each is a direct deterministic invariant; evidence is the one-to-one correspondence between those failure classes and checks 1–8. End-to-end prevention is only **roughly even (45-55%)** because the extractor may fail before the gate sees the fact. Both claims are **UNVERIFIED; settle the first with mutation tests that deliberately corrupt each field, and the second with raw-transcript-to-gate recall on human-labelled sessions**.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: task-scale context composition, not global eviction

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Define a small policy table that maps task phase and information type to representation level and exactness. Example: diagnosis expands errors, causal edges, recent changes, and environment facts; implementation expands accepted design, file identifiers, constraints, and verification state; handoff expands outcomes, remaining work, and evidence. The premise that the host supports a declarative inclusion policy is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is inspection of the host’s documented context/compaction configuration schema or a disposable configuration dry run**.

The composer allocates budget in this order:

1. exact owner constraints and safety/authority boundaries;
2. live-goal topology (requirements, dependencies, blockers, contradiction/supersession links);
3. current-subproblem L1 events;
4. adjacent L2 episodes;
5. dormant L3 landmarks and addresses;
6. representative history only with population statistics.

Recency is a tie-breaker inside a class, not the global objective. Frequency is evidence for aggregation, not evidence of importance. Goal change triggers recomposition, not merely appending a new summary.

Task-scale composition is **likely (55-80%)** to recover old but governing facts better than global recency because governing facts occupy an explicit priority class; **UNVERIFIED; settle with adversarial “needle dependency” replays where an old correction, permission boundary, or exact identifier is needed after long unrelated activity**.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: context legend and reversible expansion

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Expose a short legend to the model: representation level, operator, source coverage, freshness, omitted-member count, and how to expand a source ID. The premise that the agent can invoke source expansion cheaply at reasoning time is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a disposable trace in which the model receives ambiguous L2 records and is scored on whether it expands the correct IDs before answering**.

This is cartography’s honesty lesson: a small-scale representation should announce that it is small-scale. A synopsis without a legend invites false precision. The legend is **likely (55-80%)** to improve calibrated source recovery because it makes loss and expansion affordances explicit, but it could also consume attention or cause excessive lookups; **UNVERIFIED; settle with an A/B replay measuring unsupported assertions, lookup count, latency, and task success**.

## 4. What this catches that simpler context policies miss

These are design-class comparisons, not locally measured defect claims:

1. **The bridge fact.** An old, low-frequency correction is the only edge connecting the current choice to the owner’s intent. Recency drops it; per-item frequency underrates it; connectivity preservation retains it.
2. **The misleading representative.** One successful retry is kept and nine failures disappear. Typification with population counts and distinct-exception IDs preserves the distribution rather than presenting the sample as the population.
3. **False completion by collapse.** A long episode is summarised as “implemented” although verification never occurred. A status lattice plus evidence-address constraint rejects the promotion.
4. **The negation casualty.** “Do not delete X” becomes “handle X.” Exact-literal/exaggeration rules for prohibitions prevent lossy simplification.
5. **Superseded decisions.** A newer choice is present, but the reason it superseded the old one is evicted, so the old plan later resurfaces. Cross-scale identity and supersession edges retain reachability.
6. **Rare catastrophic exception.** An aggregate says a tool normally succeeds while omitting the single destructive or permission-related exception. Exaggeration and exception retention preserve it despite low frequency.
7. **Stale overview after a goal pivot.** A project summary remains linguistically plausible after its source assumptions change. Dependency freshness marks the representation stale.
8. **Uniform-detail waste.** A global summary spends tokens evenly across dead and live workstreams. Mixed-scale composition provides L1 detail only where the current purpose requires it.
9. **Semantic islands.** Individually “important” facts survive but their dependency relationships do not, leaving a bag of facts that cannot support a decision. The live-goal topology check rejects disconnected views.
10. **Irrecoverable paraphrase.** A summary contains a dubious claim but no address back to the source. The provenance constraint rejects it or forces it to remain explicitly unsupported.

For cases 1–10, deterministic detection after correct extraction is **very likely (80-90%)** for cases 2–5, 7, and 10 because they map directly to typed counts, states, literals, freshness, and source IDs; it is **likely (55-80%)** for cases 1, 6, 8, and 9 because goal/edge/exception classification requires semantic judgement. Evidence is the proposed invariant mapping; all rates are **UNVERIFIED; settle with one seeded mutation family per case plus blinded human labels of whether the retained view is decision-sufficient**.

## 5. Falsifiable evaluation plan

The analogy earns implementation only if it beats simpler policies at equal budget.

### 5.1 Baselines and ablations

Use held-out long sessions and compare, at the same active-context token count:

- sliding recency window;
- one recursively updated summary plus recent turns;
- scalar relevance/top-*k* retrieval plus recent turns;
- full cartographic policy;
- ablations removing (individually) stable source links, typed operators, hard constraints, mixed-scale composition, and freshness invalidation.

No vector database is required. Candidate source IDs can come from already-created episode records and deterministic lexical/type filters; this keeps the test about representation, not retrieval infrastructure.

### 5.2 Two independent signal families

Do not call the proposal successful from one evaluator score. Require:

1. **Mechanistic signal:** deterministic invariant results and direct source-recovery tests—exact literals, status, unresolved commitments, counts/exceptions, edge reachability, and stale-record rejection.
2. **Outcome signal:** blinded task replays measuring correct next action, owner-correction recurrence, unsupported assertions, completion accuracy, and end-task success. Human adjudication should inspect source evidence without seeing policy labels.

These are mechanically independent: one inspects representation structure against source IDs, while the other observes model behaviour and task outcome.

### 5.3 Adversarial cases

Seed at least:

- an old governing correction after a long distractor span;
- two nearly identical paths differing by one character;
- a prohibition containing negation and a numeric scope;
- 100 repeated successes plus one distinct high-impact failure;
- a plan followed by partial implementation but no verification;
- a decision later superseded for a reason relevant to the final step;
- a goal pivot that invalidates a previously correct summary;
- an implicit dependency whose extraction is genuinely ambiguous.

Measure both false eviction and false retention. Context tokens saved are not a win if task error rises. Also measure lookup amplification: a tiny atlas that forces dozens of expansions may cost more tokens and latency than it saves.

### 5.4 Go/no-go criteria

Pre-register thresholds before looking at results. A reasonable initial criterion is no regression in end-task success, a material reduction in invariant violations, and lower or equal total tokens including expansions. The exact threshold is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a pilot used only to estimate variance, followed by preregistration on a separate evaluation set**. If the full policy does not beat recursive summary and top-*k* baselines, stop; retain only any independently validated deterministic constraint checks.

## 6. Anti-overfit gate and implementation discipline

All four proposals ride the existing hook/skill/MCP/configuration bus. They do not require a second orchestration runtime, vector database or knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework. The “atlas” is a bounded derived artifact with stable source references, not a new authoritative memory system. The source event/artifact remains authoritative.

The complete first experiment should remain small:

- one constrained record schema;
- four representation levels;
- a fixed operator enum;
- deterministic invariant checks;
- a composer policy table;
- a source-expansion command already carried by the host’s normal file/tool bus.

If this cannot fit approximately within the BUILD-SMALL boundary, reduce scope to L0/L2 links plus constraints 1–4 before considering more machinery. A BUILD-LARGE implementation is **very unlikely (5-20%)** to be justified before the small replay establishes benefit, because none of the proposed tests requires a daemon and the anti-overfit constraint explicitly disfavors a second runtime; **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the BUILD-SMALL prototype and token-matched replay itself**.

## 7. Adversarial verdict

The broad analogy “conversation is a map” is too loose to be useful. Spatial distance does not map to conversational distance; map scale does not map to a token ratio; cartographic feature counts do not determine context budgets; and semantic relationships are much harder to observe than GIS topology. Borrowing Töpfer’s formula, spatial clustering, or global zoom would be forced and should be rejected.

The narrower mechanism survives: **purpose-specific, typed generalisation; multiple linked representations; explicit preservation constraints; and a visible route back to the source**. It addresses a real structural match—finite presentation of richer material with relational invariants—while acknowledging that relation extraction is the dominant risk. My final assessment is **likely (55-80%)** that this narrow borrow is worth a BUILD-SMALL shadow experiment, based on the independent cartographic evidence for (1) heterogeneous operators, (2) linked representations across scales, and (3) relational constraint preservation. It is only **roughly even (45-55%)** that a fully automatic semantic-topology layer will be reliable enough for hard gating, because natural-language dependencies are implicit and goal-dependent. Both assessments are **UNVERIFIED; the decisive experiment is the token-matched, held-out shadow replay with the mechanistic and outcome signal families in §5**.

The correct ambition is therefore not a grand “memory architecture.” It is a falsifiable context-generalisation layer on the existing bus. If the relational pieces fail, preserve the source-link, status, exact-literal, aggregate-count, and freshness constraints that independently pass mutation tests, and discard the analogy’s remainder.
