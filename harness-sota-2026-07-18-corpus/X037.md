# X037 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → reliable agent conclusions

## Executive judgment

**Verdict: the analogy transfers, but only in a deliberately narrow form.** The useful nuclear-operations mechanism is a graded combination of (1) STAR self-checking, (2) independent verification with protected “freedom of thought,” (3) conservative disposition under unresolved uncertainty, and (4) defence in depth against common-mode failure. The transferable invariant is not “two actors are better than one.” It is: **a consequential state claim is not released until a self-check has made its expected result explicit and a second, causally independent observation has confirmed it; disagreement produces a safe, visibly unresolved state.**

That invariant is **very likely (80-90%)** to reduce the target failure—an agent confidently declaring absent, broken, or done after one unvalidated probe—because the DOE mechanism explicitly addresses accuracy of documents/calculations, not only physical manipulations; requires the verifier not to rely on the performer’s observations; and warns that multiple people or redundant systems can still share a common-mode error ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 46-48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf); [DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 1, pp. 2-11–2-15](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/doe-hdbk-1028-2009_volume1_0.pdf)). The exact reduction on this owner’s workload is **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is the shadow-mode/replay evaluation specified below**, so no numerical effectiveness claim is made.

The broad analogy does **not** transfer. Nuclear operations concern engineered physical states, bounded procedures, trained and accountable humans, and unusually asymmetric consequences. Agent work often concerns open-world semantics, mutable remote systems, and low-consequence prose. Literal two-person staffing, universal dual checks, or five heavyweight “defence” layers would add cost without preserving the source field’s independence. The proposed borrow is therefore a small release gate on a narrow class of categorical claims, not a nuclear-style operating organization.

## What nuclear operations has actually solved

### 1. STAR: create an expected result before observing the result

DOE’s Human Performance Improvement Handbook describes self-checking as a performer focusing on the correct component or activity, thinking about the intended action and expected outcome, acting, and then verifying the result. Its mnemonic is **Stop, Think, Act, Review**. In the Think step, the performer identifies expected outputs and a contingency for unexpected results; in Review, the performer checks actual against expected and invokes the contingency if they differ ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18-19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The handbook says the method applies not only to equipment but also to data entry, calculations, experiments, computer readouts, and revisions to drawings or procedures.

In its own field, STAR is a point-of-work attention control. It deliberately inserts a cognitive discontinuity immediately before a consequential action. Most importantly for this unit, the expected state is formed *before* the actual result is interpreted. This gives the Review step a comparison target and creates a trigger for stopping when reality does not match the plan. DOE also cautions that self-checking is not a guarantee and identifies “not taking the time to verify that results are correct” as an at-risk practice ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18-19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

This maps well to a probe because a probe also has a target, a detector, an expected observable, and a result. It maps poorly if reduced to an internal prompt such as “double-check your answer”: the same inference can merely be narrated twice. STAR is useful here only when its fields become inspectable evidence: target/scope, expected positive control, probe result, and comparison outcome.

### 2. Independent verification: protect freedom of thought, not merely headcount

DOE distinguishes four practices: peer-checking, concurrent verification, independent verification, and peer review. Peer-checking is explicitly the least rigorous and places two people together before and during the action. Independent verification (IV), by contrast, confirms equipment condition or the accuracy of documents or calculations through a verifier separated in time and distance from the performer. DOE’s stated rationale is that IV has a higher probability of finding error than peer or concurrent checking because the verifier did not produce the state or document, is not cued by the performer, and checks the state directly rather than relying on the performer’s observation or oral confirmation. DOE defines independence as “freedom of thought” and operationalizes it with separation from the performer’s audible and visual cues ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 41, 46-48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The source field’s key insight is that independence is a property of the verification path, not a count of bodies. DOE’s Volume 1 says reliable controls must be independent and warns that a worker can be a common-mode failure across otherwise redundant equipment by applying the same error or assumption to every train. Suggested countermeasures include staggering work, assigning different people, and diversifying equipment so that different practices are required. It also says merely having two or more people does not ensure correctness and recommends maintaining freedom of thought and challenging assumptions ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 1, pp. 2-11–2-15](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/doe-hdbk-1028-2009_volume1_0.pdf)).

This is the closest structural match to the owner’s mandatory “two mechanically-independent signals” rule. For an agent, time/distance are not the literal goal; **causal and informational separation** are. A second shell command that calls the same executable, parser, cache, API, or filesystem enumeration path is dependent. A second model instance that sees the first model’s conclusion is anchored. A different observation mechanism that can fail differently—and whose detector is first demonstrated on a known-present control—is the functional analogue of IV.

### 3. The two-person concept: authorization control, not sufficient epistemic verification

The US Department of Defense Nuclear Matters Handbook calls the two-person rule a central procedural security measure and defines it as requiring at least two cleared, reliability-certified, task-knowledgeable people whenever there is authorized access to a nuclear weapon ([DoD Nuclear Matters Handbook 2020, Chapter 8](https://www.acq.osd.mil/ncbdp/nm/NMHB2020rev/chapters/chapter8.html)). A DoD nuclear accident response definition is even more explicit about purpose: prohibit access by one person while ensuring two authorized persons are capable of detecting incorrect or unauthorized procedures ([DoD Nuclear Accident Response Procedures definitions](https://www.acq.osd.mil/ncbdp/narp/Reference_Docs/Definitions.html)).

This mechanism solves a different problem from single-probe epistemic error: lone malicious or unauthorized action. It contributes mutual observation and intervention, but it does not by itself establish that two observers have independent evidence. DOE’s separate warning about team errors is decisive here. Therefore, “spawn a reviewer agent” is not a faithful translation. A reviewer using the same context, claim, tool wrapper, and underlying data path can reproduce or ratify the same error. The two-person concept transfers only as a **release authorization** pattern for high-impact conclusions, and only when paired with a concrete IV independence test.

### 4. Conservative decision-making: unresolved evidence changes the disposition

NRC safety-culture material describes conservative decision-making as using conservative assumptions and rejecting options unless the action is demonstrated safe, rather than requiring proof that it is unsafe. It also calls for systematic decisions under uncertain or unexpected conditions, interdisciplinary review for risk-significant decisions, and effectiveness reviews of the assumptions behind decisions ([NRC Safety Culture Components, “Decision-Making”](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/enforcement/sc-componets011306)). NRC’s current policy describes a questioning attitude as avoiding complacency and continually challenging conditions and activities to find discrepancies that could produce error or inappropriate action ([NRC Safety Culture Policy Statement](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/safety-culture/sc-policy-statement)).

DOE makes the knowledge-work relevance unusually direct. Its “Validate Assumptions” tool says workers must not inadvertently treat assumptions as facts; assumptions should be documented with applicability, affected attributes, reasoning, worst case, certainty, and conservatism; objective evidence should be sought; and unverified assumptions should be tracked and closed before product delivery. It lists failure to reconcile contradictory or disconfirming information as an at-risk practice ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 22-23](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). Its decision-making tool likewise says to resist discounting contradictory data, minimize uncertainty, challenge assumptions, develop abort criteria, and independently review a proposed decision ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 63-65](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The harness translation is not “always answer pessimistically.” It is **epistemically conservative disposition**: if the evidence gate is incomplete or contradictory, the output must preserve the uncertainty instead of releasing the categorical claim. For example: “I did not establish absence; probe A returned zero, but the detector/control or independent signal is missing.” This is a safe information state, not a claim that the underlying system is safe.

### 5. Defence in depth: independence matters more than the number of layers

IAEA describes defence in depth as consecutive, independent levels of protection such that failure of one leaves a subsequent barrier available; properly implemented, no single technical, human, or organizational failure leads directly to harm. It expressly calls independent effectiveness necessary and assigns levels to prevention, detection/control, accident control, severe-condition control, and consequence mitigation ([IAEA, *Licensing Process for Nuclear Installations*, SSG-12, paras. 3.18-3.20](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1468_web.pdf)).

For the target problem, a useful three-layer reduction is:

1. prevent: STAR-shaped claim planning and a known-positive detector control;
2. detect: independent evidence receipt checked at release time;
3. mitigate/learn: downgrade incomplete claims to `UNVERIFIED` and record mismatch fingerprints for later rule improvement.

This is defence in depth only if the layers fail differently. A prompt rule, a prompt-based self-critique, and a same-model reviewer are three labels on one cognitive dependency. The IAEA/DOE independence condition rules out counting them as three defenses.

## Concrete translation: an Evidence Release Gate

### Proposal A — CONFIG (settings/JSON only): categorical-claim protocol

Add a compact rule to the agent’s operating instructions for categorical state claims. The rule applies to conclusions of **absence**, **broken/nonfunctional**, and **done/completed**, plus any conclusion that authorizes a material destructive or external action. Before releasing one of those conclusions, the agent produces an internal evidence receipt with:

```text
claim: <exact proposition, scope, time>
claim_class: ABSENT | BROKEN | DONE | HIGH_IMPACT_OTHER
STAR:
  stop_target: <what is being judged>
  think_expected: <what a known-present/working/complete case would show>
  act_probe: <probe actually used>
  review_delta: <expected versus observed>
detector_control:
  known_present_item: <specific control>
  result: PASS | FAIL
signal_A: <mechanism, source, result>
signal_B: <mechanism, source, result>
independence_basis: <different failure mechanism/data provenance>
conflicts: <none or exact contradiction>
disposition: VERIFIED | UNVERIFIED | CONTRADICTED
```

Release rule:

- `VERIFIED` requires the detector to find a known-present control and two corroborating signals with a stated mechanical-independence basis.
- `UNVERIFIED` is mandatory when a control is absent, a signal is missing, or the signals are dependent.
- `CONTRADICTED` is mandatory when signals disagree; the agent reports both observations and does not collapse them into a categorical state claim.
- Repeating the same probe, changing only its syntax/shell, asking the same model again, or reading two renderings backed by one data source never satisfies independence.

This rule is **likely (55-80%)** to improve behavior even without enforcement because it makes the expected observation, positive control, and independence rationale explicit; the evidence is DOE’s use of STAR and documented assumption validation for knowledge work ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 18-23](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). It is also **likely (55-80%)** to be inconsistently followed under long or tool-heavy contexts because a text rule remains a human/agent administrative control and the target history already reports repeated single-probe failures. That second forecast is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by scoring rule compliance during the replay experiment below**.

### Proposal B — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED: evidence-receipt release hook

Build a small hook-bus component that recognizes candidate categorical conclusions at completion time and checks for a structured evidence receipt accumulated during the turn. It does not re-run tools, call another model, judge semantic truth, or create a new orchestration runtime. It performs deterministic checks only:

1. Did the turn declare a relevant claim class?
2. Did the declared detector pass on a named known-present item?
3. Are two signal records present?
4. Does each signal name a mechanism/provenance class, and are the classes allowed as independent for this claim type?
5. If signals conflict or evidence is incomplete, does the final wording include the inline `UNVERIFIED` disposition rather than categorical absent/broken/done language?
6. Store only a small failure fingerprint such as `missing_control`, `one_signal`, `dependent_signals`, or `conflict_suppressed` in existing local telemetry.

The recommendation heading inherits **UNVERIFIED** because two premises require experiments: whether the current hook bus exposes final-response text plus per-turn evidence before release, settled by a canary completion that attempts a known block; and whether a useful parser/checker remains below roughly 200 lines, settled by implementing the minimum prototype and counting nonblank, noncomment lines. The design remains valid if the first experiment fails, but enforcement would then stay at CONFIG until an existing native interception point is available; it does not justify a daemon.

If the hook interface exists, a deterministic release check is **very likely (80-90%)** to catch missing receipt fields and explicit one-signal attempts because those are syntactic conditions, with the evidence being the proposed closed schema rather than semantic model judgment. It is only **likely (55-80%)** to catch natural-language attempts to evade or accidentally omit claim classification because deterministic recognition of all paraphrases is incomplete; **UNVERIFIED, settle with a blinded corpus of at least 100 conclusion snippets containing categorical and noncategorical near-neighbors and measure precision/recall**.

Do not make a learned “critic” decide whether evidence is independent. The gate should use a small allow/deny matrix keyed by claim type, for example:

| Claim | Signal A | Qualifying Signal B | Non-qualifying repetition |
|---|---|---|---|
| file/path absent | native directory enumeration that passes a nearby known-file control | direct open/stat of the exact explicit path, or an authoritative manifest/index with distinct generation path | `ls` then `find` when both traverse the same inaccessible or truncated namespace without a control |
| capability absent | enumerator that finds a known installed capability | direct read of a specific authoritative registry/configuration source | rerunning the same capability detector or changing output formatting |
| service broken | functional canary request and protocol result | process/socket state or provider-side status/log record | two retries through the same dead client path |
| change done | state read-back/diff against acceptance criteria | behavior-level test exercising the changed path | trusting a successful write/edit exit status plus rereading the same write response |
| remote fact absent | bounded authoritative search/API with a positive control | separately maintained authoritative source or direct object lookup | two search queries against one index, or two pages backed by the same API |

The table is illustrative, not a universal proof calculus. Whether each pair is genuinely independent depends on the concrete dependency graph. Each deployed pair therefore needs one documented common-cause question: **“What single fault could make both signals agree incorrectly?”** If a plausible single fault exists, the pair is rejected or the residual dependency is disclosed.

### Proposal C — CONFIG (settings/JSON only): conservative conflict disposition and stop authority

Define one machine-readable status vocabulary: `VERIFIED`, `UNVERIFIED`, `CONTRADICTED`. Permit the agent to stop with a useful partial result when it cannot obtain independent evidence. Require `UNVERIFIED` inline in the sentence containing the affected claim, not only in a limitations appendix. Require the exact settling experiment whenever continued work cannot be completed in scope.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to prevent an evidence conflict from being silently rendered as certainty *when the protocol is followed*, because the disposition rule directly forbids that transformation and mirrors DOE’s requirement to track unverified assumptions and not discount disconfirming data ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 22-23, 63-65](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). End-to-end adherence is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by injecting conflicting tool results into replay tasks and measuring whether final answers retain both observations and the `CONTRADICTED` state**.

### Proposal D — BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus) — UNVERIFIED: independence ledger and common-mode lint

Maintain a tiny declarative map of observation mechanisms to dependencies, not a knowledge graph. Example fields are `wrapper`, `executable/API`, `data_source`, `parser`, `cache`, and `auth_context`. At receipt time, a deterministic function rejects a pair if all relevant dependency fields match or if a configured common-cause edge joins them. This makes “different shell” insufficient when both commands delegate to the same failing shim, and makes “two web pages” insufficient when both are views of one backend.

It is **likely (55-80%)** that a small ledger will catch recurring obvious dependencies (same tool wrapper, same endpoint, same detector, same cache) because these are enumerable identities. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to prove independence in the general case because hidden shared infrastructure and correlated upstream data are often unknowable. Both forecasts are **UNVERIFIED; settle them by labeling dependency relationships for a representative sample of historical two-probe sequences, then measuring rejection precision and the fraction left `unknown`**. `unknown` must conservatively fail the release gate for the narrow high-impact claim classes; it must not be guessed independent.

## What the gate would catch that a single-probe workflow misses

The following catch claims are forecasts and are therefore explicitly bounded:

- A capability detector returns zero because its enumerator is broken. The positive control also fails, so “no capabilities exist” cannot be released. This is **almost certain (90-99%)** for a correctly instrumented control because the gate deterministically rejects a failed control; evidence is the gate predicate itself.
- A command reports a file absent because of quoting, path translation, permission, truncation, or namespace mismatch. A direct exact-path open or authoritative manifest disagrees, producing `CONTRADICTED`. This is **very likely (80-90%)** when the fault affects only the first path; DOE’s rationale for IV is precisely that an uncued second path has a higher chance of catching the first path’s error ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, pp. 46-48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
- A tool exits successfully after writing, and the agent equates exit status with task completion. A behavior-level acceptance test is missing, so “done” is withheld. This is **almost certain (90-99%)** for receipts that explicitly record only the write result, because the matrix does not count a write acknowledgement as a behavior signal.
- Two nominal probes share the same dead MCP, API, cache, parser, or underlying index. The independence ledger rejects the pair when that shared dependency is known. This is **likely (55-80%)**, not certain, because hidden common causes will remain; the settling experiment is Proposal D’s labeled dependency evaluation.
- The agent observes conflicting results and resolves them through narrative confidence rather than evidence. The conflict field forces `CONTRADICTED` and preserves both. This is **very likely (80-90%)** with deterministic enforcement and **UNVERIFIED; settle with the conflict-injection test in Proposal C**.
- A second agent sees the first conclusion and agrees without a fresh observation. The receipt lacks an independent mechanism and is rejected. This is **almost certain (90-99%)** if the reviewer record accurately exposes its tool/data provenance, because agreement alone does not satisfy any allowed pair.

It is **almost certain (90-99%)** that the gate will miss at least some common-mode lies in sources that appear independent, stale but internally consistent authoritative records, bad acceptance criteria shared by both checks, and semantic conclusions that do not trigger the categorical-claim classifier. The evidence is structural: the proposed gate checks receipt completeness and declared dependencies, not ground truth, freshness beyond what sources expose, or unrestricted semantic entailment. The miss rate is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the hidden-common-cause, stale-record, and classifier-near-neighbor cases in the fault-injection experiment**. Those are real residuals, not reasons to add an unbounded critic.

## Adversarial transfer analysis

### Structural similarities that support transfer

1. **Both domains infer state through fallible indicators.** Operators see labels, indications, procedures, and calculations; agents see tool output, APIs, files, and rendered text. DOE explicitly includes documents, calculations, computer readouts, and knowledge workers, so the mapping is not limited to valve manipulation ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, foreword and pp. 18, 46](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).
2. **Both suffer common-mode cognition.** A person can apply the same assumption to redundant equipment; an agent can apply one faulty interpretation across repeated probes. DOE directly identifies the former and warns that headcount alone does not ensure correctness ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 1, pp. 2-11–2-15](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/doe-hdbk-1028-2009_volume1_0.pdf)).
3. **Both have a release boundary.** Nuclear work has an action/configuration/document approval point; an agent has a final categorical assertion that downstream users may act on. It is **likely (55-80%)** that a small gate can operate at that boundary without replacing the runtime because the proposed check consumes only a turn-local receipt; this is **UNVERIFIED, settled by the malformed-receipt hook canary specified above**.
4. **Both benefit from graded rigor.** DOE says organizations are not expected to use every tool and should select tools appropriate to need; its verification practices are explicitly differentiated by rigor ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 2, foreword and p. 41](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). That supports limiting dual verification to consequential categorical claims.

### Disanalogies that constrain the borrow

1. **Physical state is more stable and independently observable.** A valve position can often be checked hands-on. A remote service, search result, or semantic property may change between probes or have no independent authority. Therefore disagreement may reflect time variance, not detector error. The receipt must include scope and observation time; no “majority vote” is valid.
2. **Nuclear procedures have bounded vocabularies and qualified roles.** General agent tasks are open-ended, so a universal independence matrix is impossible. Unknown dependency must remain unknown, and the gate must cover only recognizable claim classes.
3. **Two model instances are less independent than two qualified humans with separated cues.** When configured with the same model, instructions, retrieved context, and tool bus, they share those dependencies by construction. It is **likely (55-80%)** that adding calls under those shared dependencies can create apparent assurance while preserving correlated error; the evidence is DOE’s warning that multiple people and nominally redundant trains can share team/common-mode errors ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 1, pp. 2-11–2-15](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/doe-hdbk-1028-2009_volume1_0.pdf)). The agent-specific magnitude is **UNVERIFIED; settle it by comparing same-context review against blind, different-mechanism probing in replay**. Hence no literal two-agent rule.
4. **Cost asymmetry differs radically.** Nuclear false negatives can be catastrophic; agent false positives/negatives range from trivial to severe. Universal dual checking would waste latency and tokens and invite ritual compliance. A graded trigger is essential.
5. **Conservative does not mean refusing all uncertain work.** Nuclear operations can place equipment in a defined safe condition. Many informational tasks have no equivalent safe physical state. The transferable action is accurate labeling and non-release of categorical certainty, not task paralysis.
6. **Instrumentation can become the common-mode fault.** A hook that classifies and validates receipts is itself fallible. It therefore needs a canary showing it catches a deliberately malformed receipt and a fail-visible mode; it must not be represented as proof of truth.

On balance, the transfer is **very likely (80-90%)** to be structurally valid for absence/broken/done release claims because the source mechanism explicitly covers documents/calculations and common-mode human error, while the proposal preserves the crucial independence and conservative-disposition properties. It is **unlikely (20-45%)** to be valid as a universal answer-quality framework because open-world semantic truth lacks bounded controls and independent physical readback. These bands are based on the cited scope of DOE’s tools and the disanalogies above; exact workload results remain **UNVERIFIED and require the replay/shadow experiment**.

## Anti-overfit gate

The proposals pass the owner’s anti-overfit constraint:

- They ride the existing instruction/hook/skill/MCP bus.
- They add no second orchestration runtime.
- They add no vector database or knowledge graph; the independence ledger is a small static dependency map.
- They add no durable-execution engine.
- They add no trained or model-based critic.
- They add no observability SaaS.
- They add no guardrail framework.

The minimal architecture is intentional: one compact protocol, one deterministic receipt check, and optionally one small dependency map. If final-response interception is unavailable, remain at CONFIG and test compliance; do not escalate to BUILD-LARGE. **BUILD-LARGE is not proposed.**

## Settling experiment and acceptance criteria

Run the following before enforcement:

### Phase 1: historical replay

Construct a blinded set containing all available owner-corrected single-probe false conclusions plus matched correct categorical conclusions and noncategorical statements. Do not let the rule author label the evaluation set alone. For each item, replay the task with the CONFIG protocol; when feasible, replay again with the deterministic gate. Record:

- categorical-claim detection precision and recall;
- fraction with a valid known-positive control;
- fraction with two truly independent signals under human dependency review;
- false-release rate for known wrong conclusions;
- false-block rate for correct conclusions;
- added tool calls, tokens, and wall time;
- frequency of `UNVERIFIED` and `CONTRADICTED` dispositions;
- cases where both signals shared an unmodeled common cause.

### Phase 2: synthetic fault injection

Inject at least these faults: empty detector output; detector unable to find its known-present control; misleading zero exit; stale cache; same backend exposed through two wrappers; conflicting direct and indexed state; time-varying remote state; and a completion claim backed only by a successful mutation. Include clean controls so a gate that rejects everything cannot pass.

### Phase 3: shadow mode on live work

For a fixed trial window, log proposed blocks without preventing completion. Review false blocks and missed claims weekly. Promote to enforcement only for claim classes whose measured benefit exceeds their latency and interruption cost.

Recommended initial acceptance thresholds are **UNVERIFIED design targets; the replay experiment should revise them before use**:

- catch at least 90% of the known single-probe wrong categorical conclusions;
- falsely block no more than 5% of correct categorical conclusions;
- classify at least 95% of deliberately malformed receipts as non-verified;
- add no model call and no external service;
- median added latency below 5% on triggered tasks and effectively zero on non-triggered tasks;
- every enforcement event reports the missing field or dependency conflict rather than a generic rejection.

The 90%/5%/95%/5% values are not evidence-backed facts; they are **UNVERIFIED operational starting points**, settled by measuring the trade-off curve during replay and selecting thresholds based on observed owner cost.

## Alternatives rejected

### Literal second-agent approval — rejected

A second agent sharing the first agent’s conclusion, context, model family, and tool bus is not mechanically independent. An additional model invocation is **almost certain (90-99%)** to add some compute latency by construction, and is **likely (55-80%)** to preserve or amplify anchoring when it receives the first conclusion; the latter is **UNVERIFIED and is settled by the blind-versus-anchored replay comparison above**. DOE’s independent-verification logic and team-error warning argue against counting it ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009 Vol. 1, pp. 2-11–2-15](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/doe-hdbk-1028-2009_volume1_0.pdf); [Vol. 2, pp. 46-48](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). A fresh agent can generate an alternate-probe candidate, but only the candidate probe’s dependency structure counts as evidence.

### Majority vote across repeated probes — rejected

Three dependent observations do not beat one independent observation. Majority voting hides common-mode failure and mishandles time-varying state. The correct output for conflicting evidence is `CONTRADICTED`, followed by a discriminating experiment.

### Universal enforcement on every factual sentence — rejected

This would ignore graded rigor, impose disproportionate cost, and encourage checkbox receipts. Limit the initial gate to absent/broken/done and explicitly high-impact release claims, then expand only with measured evidence.

### Model-based semantic critic — rejected

A critic would be another correlated inference layer, not a mechanically independent signal; it also reintroduces a previously rejected trained-critic style component. Deterministic schema/provenance checks are smaller, auditable, and aligned with the existing bus.

### New reliability daemon or durable workflow engine — rejected

The proposed state is turn-local and small. A new service would create availability, deployment, and common-mode dependencies without evidence that CONFIG plus a small hook is insufficient. No BUILD-LARGE justification exists.

## Gaps

- The efficacy of DOE/INPO human-performance tools in software-agent settings is **UNVERIFIED; the replay and shadow experiments above are the required test**. The cited sources establish mechanism and field practice, not transfer-effect size.
- The independence of any concrete tool pair in the owner’s harness is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with per-pair dependency tracing and injected failure tests**. This unit intentionally did not inspect the local repository or subject tree because its assigned lens explicitly forbids duplicating local grounding.
- Availability of a native pre-release/Stop hook that can inspect or block final output is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with a malformed-receipt canary at the actual hook boundary**.
- The proposed under-200-line implementation size is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with the minimum prototype and a mechanical source-line count**.
- No external source found here establishes that a two-person rule alone reduces epistemic false conclusions. The official sources describe access, authorization, mutual detection, and independent verification as distinct controls. Accordingly, this artifact does not make that stronger claim.
- No claim is made that two signals prove truth. They reduce single-detector failure only to the extent that their failure modes and data provenance are genuinely independent.

## Bottom line

Borrow the nuclear discipline of **independent evidence before release**, not the theater of extra people or extra layers. Make the agent STAR its categorical claim, prove its detector on a known-positive control, obtain a second observation with a different failure path, document the common-mode analysis, and conservatively emit `UNVERIFIED` or `CONTRADICTED` when the evidence gate does not close. Enforce the receipt with a small deterministic hook only if the native hook boundary passes a canary. This is an evidence protocol riding the existing bus, not a new orchestration system.
