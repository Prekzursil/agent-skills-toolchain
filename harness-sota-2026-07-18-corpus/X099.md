# X099 — Cross-domain borrow: metrology → finite-context management

## Executive judgment

The borrow transfers, but only at the level of **measurement discipline and decision procedure**. It does not transfer as a claim that semantic context has SI-like units, a stable true value, independent errors, or a justified Gaussian uncertainty distribution.

The strongest transferable mechanism is a combination of:

1. **metrological traceability**: a result is defensible only through a documented, unbroken chain back to a stated reference, with every transformation contributing uncertainty;
2. **an uncertainty budget**: uncertainty is decomposed by source, including covariance rather than assuming independent error;
3. **intermediate checks and adaptive recalibration intervals**: trust is checked between full calibrations, and the interval is shortened or lengthened from observed drift and risk rather than fixed by the calendar; and
4. **guard-banded decision rules**: when a result lies near a consequential threshold, uncertainty changes the decision boundary.

Translated to context management, the harness should treat every summary, extracted fact, plan state, and remembered constraint as a **derived result**, not as free-floating text. Each derived item gets a compact provenance chain to primary evidence, a qualitative uncertainty budget, invalidation conditions, and a next-check trigger. Eviction then becomes a risk decision: retain or rehydrate the evidence whose loss could change a consequential action; evict cheap-to-recover, low-impact detail; and refuse to collapse a near-boundary decision to a single confident sentence.

**Transfer verdict — likely (55-80%)**, based on the shared structure of (a) multiple lossy transformations between a primary reference and a downstream decision, (b) drift over time/use, and (c) asymmetric costs of wrong acceptance and wrong rejection. This is **UNVERIFIED for agent context**; the settling experiment is the traceable-context ablation in §10, comparing ordinary summarization against provenance-carrying summaries on long-horizon tasks with planted contradictions and delayed constraint reuse.

The numeric machinery does **not** transfer directly. **Almost certain (90-99%)**, because the GUM's combination rules presuppose a defined measurand, an explicit measurement model, commensurable standard uncertainties, and modeled covariance, while semantic relevance and downstream decision impact do not presently meet those conditions. The evidence is the GUM's own qualification that uncertainty propagation relies on a mathematical model and that incomplete models must be revised from observed data ([JCGM 100:2008, §§3.3.6 and 3.4.1-3.4.2, pp. 7-8](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf)). Therefore this unit recommends ordinal risk classes and empirical calibration, not invented probabilities.

## 2. Mechanism A in its own field: metrological traceability

### 2.1 What it is

VIM defines metrological traceability as a property of a **measurement result** by which the result can be related to a reference through a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty. VIM separately defines a traceability chain as the sequence of standards and calibrations used to relate the result to the reference ([VIM §2.42](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.42.html)).

Three details are essential:

1. **Traceability belongs to the result, not merely to the instrument.** NIST explicitly warns that having an instrument calibrated—even by NIST—is not enough. The provider must document the measurement process and the chain connecting the particular result to the reference ([NIST TN 2156, terminology and FAQ §5.1](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)).
2. **The chain is transformational, not merely documentary.** Calibration first establishes a relation between a standard's values/uncertainties and an instrument's indications/uncertainties, then uses that relation to obtain a result with uncertainty ([ILAC-G24/OIML D 10:2022, §3.3, pp. 7-8](https://www.oiml.org/en/files/pdf_d/d010-e22.pdf)).
3. **Every link contributes uncertainty.** A copied result does not become reliable because its ultimate ancestor was authoritative. The intermediate transfer, correction, model, environment, and use conditions matter. NIST's practical checklist requires the measured property, system description, value and uncertainty, calibration references, and assurance of both the working and reference standards ([NIST TN 2156, FAQ §5.2 and checklist §6](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)).

This prevents a common category error: “source X is authoritative” is not equivalent to “this downstream number is traceable.” The particular downstream result must preserve the link and account for the transformations.

### 2.2 Concrete translation

For an agent, the closest analogue of a measurement result is a context item that will influence a later decision:

- “the owner requires read-only operation”;
- “test A failed because condition B”;
- “file C contains the current implementation”;
- “the plan is at step 4 and step 2 is complete”;
- “the API accepts field D.”

The closest analogue of the reference is the primary evidence: the owner's exact instruction, a file span at a content hash, a tool result, a test log, an official source, or an external system record with version/time. Summarization, extraction, deduplication, merging, and compaction are the calibration-chain links.

The translation is **not** “keep all raw text.” It is “keep a compact route back to the evidence and describe what was transformed.” A derived item without a recoverable source is an untraceable result. It may still be useful, just as a non-traceable measurement may be useful, but it must not silently masquerade as reference-grade evidence.

**Structural fit — very likely (80-90%)**, because both domains have primary references, intermediate transformations, derived results, and downstream decisions; the evidence is the VIM/NIST chain structure above and the ordinary, inspectable operations of summarization and extraction. The remaining 10-20% uncertainty is the lack of an agent-specific trial; **UNVERIFIED**, settled by the provenance-survival experiment in §10.1.

## 3. Mechanism B in its own field: uncertainty budgets and correlation

### 3.1 What it is

The GUM defines measurement uncertainty as a parameter characterizing the dispersion of values reasonably attributable to a measurand. It recognizes multiple components. Type A evaluation uses statistical analysis of repeated observations; Type B evaluation uses other information, such as prior knowledge or experience ([JCGM 100:2008, §§2.2.3 and 2.3.2-2.3.3, pp. 2-3](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf)).

The combined standard uncertainty is not a naive sum of independent error bars. It is formed from variances **and covariances**, weighted by how the result responds to each input ([JCGM 100:2008, §2.3.4 and §3.3.6, pp. 3 and 7](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf)). This is a vital safeguard: when several inputs share a common standard or method, their errors can move together.

The GUM also makes its assumptions explicit. Practical uncertainty evaluation usually depends on a mathematical model; the model may be incomplete; long-term empirical data, check standards, control charts, and independent determinations should be used; and the model should be revised when observations expose incompleteness ([JCGM 100:2008, §3.4.1-3.4.3, pp. 7-8](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf)).

### 3.2 Concrete translation

A context item needs a small **uncertainty budget**, but initially it should be categorical rather than fake-numeric. Relevant components are:

- **source uncertainty**: primary source, secondary source, model inference, or unsupported recollection;
- **extraction uncertainty**: exact quote/span, deterministic parse, or semantic paraphrase;
- **scope uncertainty**: whether the statement applies to this repo, branch, user, date, platform, or task;
- **freshness uncertainty**: mutable external fact, mutable workspace fact, or immutable/pinned fact;
- **conflict uncertainty**: whether an independent source agrees, is absent, or contradicts it;
- **compression uncertainty**: number and type of lossy transformations since the primary evidence;
- **dependency uncertainty**: how many downstream decisions inherit this item;
- **recovery uncertainty**: whether the evidence can be cheaply re-read or is ephemeral.

The covariance lesson maps especially well. Five summaries produced from the same mistaken extraction are not five independent signals. Two agents repeating the same API response are not independent verification. A context selector should group items by `source_family` and `transform_family`; agreement within one family must not multiply confidence.

**Transfer of decomposition and correlation awareness — likely (55-80%)**, because correlated semantic errors are structurally possible whenever derived items share evidence or a transform, but there is no validated mapping from the GUM's numeric covariance to agent confidence. Evidence for the metrology side is the GUM covariance requirement; the agent-side performance gain is **UNVERIFIED**, settled by the shared-source contradiction experiment in §10.2.

Literal root-sum-of-squares propagation is rejected. **Almost certain (90-99%)**, because there is no defensible common unit for “scope ambiguity,” “staleness,” and “semantic paraphrase loss,” and no justified sensitivity coefficients or distributions. A number such as `uncertainty = 0.37` would add an appearance of metrological rigor without its prerequisites.

## 4. Mechanism C in its own field: intermediate checks and adaptive recalibration

### 4.1 What it is

Calibration is not a permanent badge. ILAC-G24/OIML D 10 describes drift as a change over time in instrument indication caused by changing metrological properties (§3.10). It says maintaining traceability can involve calibration periodicity, process-control measures, and intermediate checks; calibration should help detect whether alterations cast doubt on results produced during the elapsed period (§4.1-4.2). The guide requires balancing the cost of frequent recalibration against increased uncertainty or decreased reliability from longer intervals (§4.5), retaining calibration history, using intermediate checks, and judging results against predetermined limits (§4.8-4.10) ([official PDF, pp. 9, 12-13](https://www.oiml.org/en/files/pdf_d/d010-e22.pdf)).

Crucially, the interval is risk- and evidence-driven. Initial choice considers required uncertainty, risk of exceeding limits, drift, severity of use, environment, comparison frequency, and intermediate-check results (§5.1). With routine data, intervals should be adjusted. The guide says fixed intervals are not recommended absent a normative requirement (§6.1). It describes:

- a **staircase method**, extending the interval when deviation remains within a defined portion of permissible error and reducing it otherwise (§6.2);
- **control charts**, plotting selected calibration points over time to estimate dispersion and drift (§6.3);
- **in-use time**, where recalibration depends on actual use rather than calendar age (§6.4); and
- frequent **in-service “black box” checks** of critical parameters, escalating to full calibration when limits are exceeded (§6.5) ([ILAC-G24/OIML D 10:2022, pp. 14-16](https://www.oiml.org/en/files/pdf_d/d010-e22.pdf)).

NIST gives a narrower example: periodic measurements of check standards at low, midpoint, and high parts of a calibration range can estimate uncertainty, provided the sampling adequately represents the measurement process ([NIST/SEMATECH check-standard method](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc3672.htm)).

### 4.2 Concrete translation

Context has at least three possible drift clocks:

1. **calendar/event drift**: external facts, branch state, files, or owner priorities change;
2. **use drift**: a fact is repeatedly paraphrased, merged, or used to derive further claims;
3. **boundary drift**: the task changes, so a once-relevant summary is now out of scope or dangerously incomplete.

A fixed “keep the last N turns” or “summarize every M tokens” policy is analogous to a fixed calendar interval that ignores usage and drift. The metrology borrow says: set the next check from risk, observed stability, and use. High-impact mutable facts should be re-read before use. Stable pinned constraints can have longer intervals. A repeatedly transformed item reaches its check threshold sooner even if little wall-clock time has passed. A cheap black-box check can validate critical properties without rehydrating the entire history.

Candidate black-box checks for an agent context packet:

- Can it reproduce the owner's non-negotiable constraints verbatim or with lossless structured fields?
- Given a planted contradiction between an old and new instruction, does it select the newer scoped instruction and retain both provenance links?
- Can it name the evidence supporting each “done,” “broken,” or “absent” claim?
- Can it distinguish an observation from an inference and an inference from a proposal?
- Can it identify which context items would be invalidated by a branch, file hash, date, or task change?

**Adaptive checking is likely (55-80%) to outperform token-age-only eviction on delayed-reuse tasks**, because ILAC/OIML shows why drift- and use-sensitive intervals dominate a universal fixed interval for heterogeneous instruments, and context items are similarly heterogeneous in mutability and consequence. This agent-side benefit is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is §10.3, comparing fixed recency, fixed token interval, and risk/use-triggered revalidation under equal token and tool budgets.

## 5. Mechanism D in its own field: guard-banded decisions

### 5.1 What it is

Conformity assessment distinguishes the **tolerance interval** (values truly acceptable under a specification) from the **acceptance interval** used by a decision rule. When uncertainty matters, those boundaries need not coincide.

JCGM 106 shows guarded acceptance: move the acceptance limits inward by a guard band to reduce the probability of accepting a nonconforming item. In its normal-distribution example, a guard band equal to expanded uncertainty `U = 2u` bounds false acceptance at 2.3% at the boundary (§8.3.2). It also shows guarded rejection: place a rejection threshold beyond the tolerance limit when the cost of falsely rejecting a conforming item matters (§8.3.3). The document emphasizes that the decision rule allocates consumer's and producer's risk; it is not a universal one-sided demand for conservatism ([JCGM 106:2012, §8.3, pp. 29-31](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_106_2012_E.pdf)).

### 5.2 Concrete translation

Context selection has a nominal threshold: include an item if its expected relevance exceeds its token cost. That is too crude near consequential decisions. A guard-banded selector introduces an **indeterminate band**:

- If evidence is clearly low impact, cheap to recover, and unrelated to active decisions: evict.
- If evidence is clearly high impact or irreplaceable: retain.
- If an uncertain item sits near a high-consequence decision boundary: retain a compact provenance stub, re-read the source, or defer the decision. Do not force it into “include” or “discard” based on a noisy relevance score.

The band must reflect asymmetric loss. Before a destructive or externally visible action, guard against false acceptance: require stronger evidence that prerequisites are satisfied. Before discarding a possibly important owner constraint, guard against false rejection: keep a compact stub until independent evidence makes irrelevance clear.

**A qualitative guard band is likely (55-80%) to reduce high-severity context omissions**, because it converts uncertainty near a decision boundary into revalidation instead of unjustified binary classification; JCGM 106 supplies the decision-theoretic mechanism. The magnitude and net token cost are **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is §10.4 with asymmetric penalties for omitted constraints versus retained distractors.

The JCGM numeric probabilities must not be copied. **Almost certain (90-99%)**, because those examples depend on a modeled probability distribution and calibrated standard uncertainty, neither of which exists for an arbitrary LLM relevance score.

## 6. Proposed harness design

### Proposal 1 — Traceable Context Envelope

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a compact JSONL sidecar managed by the existing context-compaction hook or skill. Do not add a daemon, database, second orchestration runtime, or external observability service. Each decision-relevant item has:

```json
{
  "id": "ctx-017",
  "claim": "short normalized proposition",
  "kind": "instruction|observation|inference|decision|plan-state",
  "scope": {"task": "...", "repo": "...", "branch": "...", "time": "..."},
  "source": {"type": "user|file|tool|url", "locator": "recoverable pointer", "digest": "optional"},
  "parent_ids": ["ctx-003", "ctx-009"],
  "transform": "quote|parse|paraphrase|merge|inference",
  "uncertainty": ["scope", "freshness", "compression"],
  "source_family": "shared-origin identifier",
  "invalidators": ["newer owner instruction", "file digest change"],
  "next_check": {"trigger": "before destructive action|after 3 derivations|on scope change"},
  "token_cost": 41,
  "recovery_cost": "low|medium|high",
  "impact": "low|medium|high|critical"
}
```

Only the compact envelope occupies routine context. Raw evidence remains where it already lives and is re-read on demand. Ephemeral tool output whose source cannot be recovered gets a stronger retention bias or an intentional snapshot, subject to authorization and privacy rules.

Selection policy:

`retain priority = consequence of loss × uncertainty × irrecoverability × active-dependency count`, with categorical ordering rather than fabricated floating-point precision. Token cost breaks ties; it does not dominate critical constraints.

When compaction merges items, the new item records all parent IDs and the transform. When a later claim depends on it, the dependency is visible. A source-family field prevents repeated derivatives of one source from being mistaken for independent confirmation.

What this could catch:

- **Likely (55-80%)**: a summary sentence that no longer has recoverable support, because a missing/broken locator or parent chain becomes mechanically visible. **UNVERIFIED**; settle by §10.1.
- **Likely (55-80%)**: a “done” or “broken” conclusion inherited from a single stale observation, because its downstream dependencies and invalidators remain attached. **UNVERIFIED**; settle with delayed state-change trials in §10.3.
- **Likely (55-80%)**: false independence among several claims derived from one tool result, because `source_family` exposes common origin. **UNVERIFIED**; settle by §10.2.
- **Roughly even (45-55%)**: meaningful net quality improvement after paying the envelope's token overhead. Evidence is only the structural analogy; **UNVERIFIED** and must be decided by the equal-budget ablation in §10.1.

### Proposal 2 — Task measurand and loss declaration

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

At task start and whenever scope materially changes, require a small declaration:

```yaml
decision_target: the concrete outcome the context must support
reference_authority:
  - newest scoped owner instruction
  - current primary workspace evidence
loss_order:
  critical: violate authority, destructive error, unsupported absence/broken/done claim
  high: wrong implementation decision, omit active constraint
  medium: repeat work, retain distractor
  low: re-read cheap source
scope_invalidators: [task replacement, branch change, source mutation, newer instruction]
```

This is the analogue of defining the measurand and required uncertainty before choosing equipment and intervals. It prevents “relevance” from being a universal property of text: relevance is conditional on a decision target and loss function.

What this could catch:

- **Very likely (80-90%)**: explicit disagreement between the active task and an old plan, because scope invalidators are represented and evaluated at transitions. Evidence is the direct logical effect of the configuration, but actual model compliance is **UNVERIFIED**; settle by a task-switch benchmark with planted obsolete plans.
- **Likely (55-80%)**: eviction of a short but critical constraint in favor of verbose recent detail, because consequence of loss is declared separately from recency and token mass. **UNVERIFIED**; settle by §10.4.

### Proposal 3 — Intermediate context checks with adaptive intervals

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a lightweight validator invoked by existing lifecycle events. It does not judge open-ended answer quality. It checks a few critical, mechanically scorable properties:

1. all `critical` items have a source locator or an explicit `untraceable` marker;
2. locators that can cheaply be checked still resolve and optional digests match;
3. derived items retain parent IDs;
4. no independent-verification count includes two items from one source family;
5. items due for a `next_check` are re-read before the corresponding action;
6. each compaction round runs a tiny “black box” probe: recover one owner constraint, one current plan state, and one evidence chain sampled by impact tier.

Use a staircase interval per item class:

- pass repeatedly with no change → lengthen check interval within a cap;
- fail, conflict, or source mutation → shorten interval and rehydrate parents;
- high transformation count or high usage → check by use count, not calendar age;
- new item class or new model/context policy → check frequently until stability data exists.

Do not use a hidden fixed interval as the final policy. Keep the history of check results so interval changes are evidence-based.

What this could catch:

- **Likely (55-80%)**: progressive paraphrase drift across repeated compactions, because check frequency is tied to transformation count and sampled claims are compared with primary evidence. **UNVERIFIED**; settle by §10.3.
- **Likely (55-80%)**: stale file- or branch-scoped facts surviving a state change, when locators carry digests/scope and lifecycle events trigger checks. **UNVERIFIED**; settle by mutating sources mid-task.
- **Unlikely (20-45%)** to catch subtle semantic omission with purely mechanical probes, because locator validity does not prove the paraphrase preserved meaning. Evidence is the disanalogy in §8; settle by human-scored semantic entailment trials.

### Proposal 4 — Consequence-sensitive context guard bands

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Replace a single inclusion cutoff with three outcomes: `retain`, `evict`, and `rehydrate/defer`. Policy examples:

- Never evict the only recoverable representation of a critical owner constraint.
- For a high-consequence action, rehydrate any prerequisite whose source is indirect, stale, conflicting, or transformed more than the validated limit.
- For low-impact information with a cheap locator, evict the content and retain only the stub.
- Treat “unknown” as an explicit state; do not compress it into false or absent.
- Allocate the guard band by consequence: wider for destructive/external actions and authority constraints; narrower for reversible local exploration.

What this could catch:

- **Likely (55-80%)**: binary relevance decisions made on ambiguous evidence immediately before consequential actions. **UNVERIFIED**; settle by §10.4.
- **Very likely (80-90%)**: cases in the benchmark where the selector itself labels an item uncertain and near-boundary, because the policy deterministically routes that state to rehydration. Whether the upstream labels are calibrated remains **UNVERIFIED**; settle by calibration curves in §10.4.

## 7. Minimal architecture and anti-overfit gate

All four proposals ride existing configuration and hook/skill buses. They introduce no second orchestration runtime, vector database, knowledge graph, durable-execution engine, trained critic, observability SaaS, or guardrail framework.

Minimal data flow:

```text
primary evidence
    │  extract / quote / infer
    ▼
traceable envelope ──compact content──► active finite context
    │                                      │
    │ provenance stub                       │ consequential use / due trigger
    └──────────────── rehydrate ◄───────────┘
                         │
                 intermediate check
```

The sidecar is appendable JSONL or an in-memory structure already supported by the current bus, not durable semantic memory. The proposal is intentionally small: provenance and check triggers, not a general knowledge system. If implementation cannot stay below roughly 200 lines per component, the first response should be to cut fields and checks, not to justify a daemon.

**Minimality is very likely (80-90%) to preserve most of the borrow's value**, because traceability requires identifiers, locators, transforms, and check results—not retrieval infrastructure. This implementation claim is **UNVERIFIED**; the settling experiment is a time-boxed prototype measuring line count, latency, and benchmark effect before any broader build.

## 8. Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### 8.1 No stable, scalar measurand

Physical metrology normally begins with a sufficiently specified quantity. “Context quality” is not one quantity. A packet can be excellent for debugging and poor for contract review; the target changes as the task changes. The VIM itself recognizes definitional uncertainty and that a result depends on how the measurand is defined ([VIM measurement uncertainty §2.26](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.26.html)).

Consequence: never expose a universal “context accuracy” score. Define a task-conditioned decision target and report separate failure dimensions.

### 8.2 No SI-like reference hierarchy

An owner's current instruction can be authoritative for intent, a file can be authoritative for current implementation, and an official specification can be authoritative for an API. These references can conflict because they answer different questions. There is no single apex standard.

Consequence: the envelope needs `reference_authority` and `scope`, not merely `source_rank`. Traceability proves where a claim came from; it does not prove the source governs the present decision.

### 8.3 The “instrument” is not passive

An LLM changes behavior when context wording and ordering change. Selecting context changes the process being evaluated; a summary can prime subsequent inference. Physical calibration often assumes a separable instrument and measurand under specified conditions, while agent context and inference interact.

Consequence: calibration results are conditional on model, prompt, ordering, tool policy, and task distribution. Re-run checks after those change.

### 8.4 Error sources are semantic, correlated, and adversarial

A single bad premise can propagate through many polished summaries. Repeated model calls may share training biases and the same source error. The GUM can handle covariance when it is modeled, but we generally cannot quantify semantic covariance.

Consequence: track source/transform families and avoid numeric propagation. Require mechanically independent evidence for absence, brokenness, or completion; provenance makes independence auditable but does not create it.

### 8.5 Ground truth may be delayed or unknowable

Many agent decisions have no immediate reference answer. Success can depend on future owner judgment or external effects. Check standards cover only selected operating points; NIST explicitly notes adequate sampling is necessary for check-standard uncertainty estimates ([NIST/SEMATECH](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section3/mpc3672.htm)).

Consequence: a context benchmark must span task classes and delayed reuse. Passing a few probes does not certify general context quality.

### 8.6 Traceability can preserve confidently wrong sources

A perfect chain to an obsolete file or mistaken owner assumption remains perfectly traceable and still wrong for the target decision. NIST's warning that traceability is not achieved by a prestigious calibration alone generalizes here: provenance is necessary for auditability, not sufficient for truth ([NIST TN 2156](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability)).

Consequence: source validity, scope, freshness, and independent conflict checks remain separate fields.

### 8.7 Provenance has a token and attention cost

Too much metadata can displace the very evidence it protects or distract the model. Metrology tolerates substantial documentation because measurement consequences can justify it; routine agent turns may not.

Consequence: store full envelopes outside the active window and inject only compact stubs for active/high-risk claims. **Roughly even (45-55%)** that the proposed default envelope is net-positive at equal token budget, because no agent-specific measurement exists; **UNVERIFIED**, settled by §10.1.

## 9. Eviction algorithm, concretely

The algorithm should operate over dependency groups rather than isolated text chunks.

### Step 1: establish the decision target

Load the active task, governing instructions, consequence classes, and scope invalidators. If these cannot be determined, mark the target ambiguous and widen the retain/rehydrate band.

### Step 2: form provenance groups

Group each derived item with its primary locator, parents, dependents, source family, and recovery path. Do not count siblings from the same family as independent corroboration.

### Step 3: assess categorical loss and uncertainty

For each group assign:

- consequence if wrong or absent;
- probability class only if empirically calibrated, otherwise `low/medium/high/unknown` uncertainty;
- recoverability and recovery cost;
- current scope match;
- freshness/use/transform triggers;
- number and consequence of active dependents.

### Step 4: choose among retain, stub, rehydrate, or evict

1. `retain`: critical, irreplaceable, active, or explicitly authoritative content;
2. `stub`: recoverable evidence with a compact proposition and locator;
3. `rehydrate`: due check, conflict, near-boundary high-consequence use, or invalidated scope;
4. `evict`: low-consequence content with no active dependencies and cheap recovery, after retaining any required provenance stub.

### Step 5: compact with a calibration record

Every merge creates a new ID, parent IDs, transform type, and declared information loss. A compactor must not silently upgrade `inference` to `observation`, `unknown` to `absent`, or `planned` to `done`.

### Step 6: run the intermediate check

Sample by consequence, not uniformly. Verify at least one governing constraint, one high-impact current-state claim, and one plan-state claim. On failure, rehydrate the affected family and shorten its interval.

### Step 7: learn intervals from evidence

Record passes, failures, mutation events, transformation counts, and recovery cost. Extend checks only after repeated stable results, cap the extension, and reset after model/prompt/policy changes.

This algorithm intentionally does not prescribe a learned relevance model. **Likely (55-80%)** that a categorical policy is the right first implementation, because it is inspectable and does not pretend to have calibrated semantic probabilities; **UNVERIFIED**, settled by comparing it to a numeric scorer in §10 after both are calibrated on held-out tasks.

## 10. Settling experiments

### 10.1 Provenance-survival and equal-budget ablation

Construct 100+ long-horizon tasks across coding, research, planning, and operational work. Plant primary evidence early, require it after at least two compactions, and include cheap-to-recover distractors. Compare:

- baseline recency/summarization;
- summaries with citations only;
- full Traceable Context Envelope;
- envelope with source-family correlation disabled.

Hold model, total input tokens, tool budget, and task order constant. Score exact constraint survival, correct source recovery, unsupported-claim rate, completion rate, latency, and token overhead. Use held-out task templates. This settles whether provenance beats its context cost and which fields carry value.

Success gate: adopt only if the envelope materially reduces critical omission/unsupported-claim rate without a larger completion or latency regression under a predeclared loss function. Do not choose the threshold after seeing results.

### 10.2 Independence and correlated-error challenge

Create tasks with five agreeing derived statements from one flawed source plus one contradictory independent primary source. Vary surface wording so lexical deduplication fails. Measure whether the harness counts source families rather than statement count and whether it rehydrates the independent source before a consequential conclusion.

This specifically tests the covariance-inspired mechanism. A simple provenance-family implementation must beat both majority vote and embedding similarity without needing a trained critic.

### 10.3 Drift and adaptive-interval trial

During long tasks, mutate authoritative evidence at controlled points: change a file digest, branch, API version, owner constraint, or plan status. Separately, repeatedly paraphrase an immutable fact without changing its source. Compare:

- fixed last-N-turn retention;
- fixed-token revalidation;
- calendar/event checks;
- use/transform-count staircase checks;
- combined risk-adaptive checks.

Measure time-to-detection, stale-action rate, unnecessary rereads, and total tokens/tools. Plot failure rate by interval and item class. Only lengthen intervals on held-out data.

### 10.4 Guard-band loss experiment

Create decisions with calibrated ambiguity and asymmetric costs:

- omit a critical constraint versus retain a distractor;
- falsely declare a destructive action safe versus ask/revalidate;
- retain obsolete guidance versus discard a still-governing instruction.

Compare a binary relevance threshold with a three-way retain/evict/rehydrate rule. Predeclare costs and report both raw confusion matrices and expected loss. Reliability diagrams should compare predicted ordinal uncertainty with empirical error bins; do not report pseudo-precise confidence if bins are poorly populated.

### 10.5 Detector validation and absence claims

Any validator used to claim a broken provenance chain must first find a known-present valid chain and a known-present intentionally broken chain. Use two mechanically independent signals for consequential absence/broken/done claims—for example, locator resolution plus content digest/semantic check, or lifecycle event record plus current primary-state query. Re-running one parser is not independent verification.

## 11. Failure policy and rollout

1. Prototype Proposal 2 (task/loss declaration) and a minimal Proposal 1 envelope first.
2. Run §10.1 before making it default.
3. Add source families and run §10.2.
4. Add only mechanically scorable intermediate checks, then run §10.3.
5. Add qualitative guard bands and run §10.4.
6. Delete fields that show no held-out benefit. Do not preserve them because they sound metrological.

**This staged order is likely (55-80%) to isolate causal value better than shipping all mechanisms together**, because each ablation corresponds to one borrowed mechanism and limits interaction confounds. It is **UNVERIFIED** as an implementation strategy; the settling evidence is whether staged ablations yield stable effect estimates across repeated held-out suites.

Stop conditions:

- If provenance overhead worsens the predeclared expected-loss metric under equal budgets, stop and retain only task/loss declaration.
- If source-family grouping does not improve correlated-error trials, remove it rather than building graph infrastructure.
- If adaptive checks consume more reread cost than the stale errors they prevent, restrict them to critical item classes.
- If uncertainty labels are not empirically calibrated, keep them ordinal and do not use them to publish probabilities.
- If the implementation pressures toward a database, daemon, trained critic, or second runtime, stop: that violates the anti-overfit gate absent new evidence.

## 12. What this lens adds—and what it cannot conclude

The useful metrology insight is not “measure relevance more accurately.” It is:

> A downstream result is trustworthy only for a specified purpose, through a documented chain to stated references, with transformation uncertainty and drift exposed, and with the decision threshold chosen for the consequences of error.

For a finite context window, this changes the unit of selection from **text chunk** to **decision-relevant claim plus provenance and revalidation path**. It changes eviction from “least similar/oldest text” to “lowest expected consequence of loss after accounting for uncertainty and recoverability.” It changes compaction from an opaque rewrite to a traceable transformation. It changes a binary cutoff into retain/evict/rehydrate when uncertainty matters.

**Overall recommendation — likely (55-80%)**: prototype the Traceable Context Envelope, task-conditioned loss declaration, and adaptive intermediate checks as small existing-bus components, then keep only effects demonstrated under equal budgets. Evidence: the mechanisms are established in metrology by VIM, GUM, ILAC/OIML, JCGM 106, and NIST; the structural mapping is explicit above; agent benefit remains **UNVERIFIED**, with §10 providing the settling experiments.

What this unit cannot claim:

- It cannot claim these mechanisms are absent from the current harness; no local inspection was performed by design.
- It cannot quantify current context failure rates; none were supplied for this lens.
- It cannot justify numeric uncertainty propagation for semantic context.
- It cannot establish a universal context “reference standard.” Authority is task- and scope-dependent.
- It cannot establish production benefit without the controlled ablations above.

These are not reasons to force the analogy further. They are the boundary that keeps the borrow evidence-based.
