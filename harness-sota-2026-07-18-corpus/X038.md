# X038 — Cross-domain borrow: nuclear operations → use-versus-mention guards

## Executive judgment

The analogy transfers, but narrowly. Nuclear operations has not solved natural-language use-versus-mention classification. It has solved a structurally adjacent control problem: how to prevent a representation (a procedure step, label, indication, or another person's assertion) from being mistaken for authorization to alter the real plant. Its useful mechanisms are (1) STAR self-checking immediately before actuation, (2) peer or independent verification of the intended action and the actual component, (3) conservative stopping when the facts needed to act are uncertain, and (4) defence in depth whose layers are as independent as practicable. The target harness should borrow those control relationships, not nuclear ceremony or blanket risk aversion.

The concrete translation is an **actuation-centred role gate**. A detector must not infer that an action is occurring merely because its trigger words occur somewhere in a payload. It should first establish, from structural provenance, that the event is a candidate action; bind that action to a typed operation and target; and only then apply the content-specific guard. Quoted text, retrieved documents, code examples, logs, and tool results remain inspectable data. Ambiguity quarantines the candidate action, not the document that describes it.

This is **very likely (80-90%)** to remove the stated class of false fires without weakening guards against genuine tool actions, because the task premise says the common failure is matching payload text rather than semantic role, while DOE practice explicitly places checking at the point where a performer is about to alter component state. This remains **UNVERIFIED; the settling experiment is a replay of all 12+ known false fires plus matched genuine-positive and adversarial near-miss cases through an instrumented shadow implementation**, with decisions compared at the candidate-action boundary.

This is not a proposal for a second orchestration runtime, a vector database/knowledge graph, durable execution, a trained critic, an observability service, or a guardrail framework. It rides the existing hook/tool event bus and therefore passes the anti-overfit gate.

## Scope and method

This is an ideation lens as assigned. I did not inspect or characterize the local harness repository. The only target facts used are those supplied in the unit brief: five detectors and at least twelve false fires caused by matching payload text that merely describes a guarded thing. All claims about nuclear practice below use public primary or regulator sources. All harness effects are hypotheses and are marked with confidence bands and experiments.

The core question is not whether “nuclear is careful” and agents should also be careful. That analogy is too generic to be useful. The structural question is:

> Does nuclear practice distinguish information *about* an operation from the authorized performance of that operation, and does it place controls at the transition from representation to state change?

The answer is yes. The DOE Human Performance Improvement Handbook defines “positive control” as making only what is intended happen, says that a performer must understand the significance and intended result before acting, and locates special attention at critical steps ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 5, 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The same handbook applies its tools not just to workers touching equipment but also to knowledge workers who change the “paper plant,” an important reminder that representations can eventually cause real events (foreword, pp. i–ii, same URL).

## What nuclear operations solved in its own field

### 1. STAR: self-check at the action boundary

DOE's STAR mechanism is specific, not motivational:

1. **Stop** before a critical activity and remove distractions.
2. **Think** about the correct action, correct component, current status, controlling document, expected result, and contingency.
3. **Act** on the correct component under the governing procedure.
4. **Review** whether the observed result equals the expected result and invoke the contingency if it does not.

DOE states that self-checking focuses attention on the component or activity, intended action, and expected outcome immediately before the physical action; it is especially important when component status is about to change. The detailed STAR sequence requires comparison with the controlling document and says not to act while discrepancies or uncertainty remain ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 18–19](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). The NRC independently recognizes inadequate self-checking as a human-performance problem and explicitly gives “Stop, Think, Act, Review” as its example ([NRC Human Factors Definition List, item 197](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)).

The key use-versus-mention insight is that STAR is triggered by impending manipulation, not by encountering the name of a hazardous component. Reading “open valve X” in a procedure is not itself opening valve X. The control becomes decisive when a performer binds the instruction to a real component and is about to change its state. This is a direct structural match to distinguishing text that names `delete`, `send`, `publish`, or `install` from an actual tool invocation that would perform one of those operations.

### 2. Peer checking and the two-person rule: verify authorization and referent, not mere vocabulary

DOE peer checking augments rather than replaces the performer's self-check. Performer and peer separately self-check, agree on the correct action and correct component, observe execution, and confirm the result. DOE reserves or recommends this for critical steps, irreversible/unwanted actions, and state changes; it also lists failure modes such as inattention, deference, overuse, and each person assuming the other has checked ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 42–45](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)).

The IAEA's insider-threat guidance gives the security form of the two-person rule: two experienced people monitor one another in a sensitive area, both verify that actions are authorized, and each must be technically able to detect unauthorized activity. It also warns about complacency and recommends rotating pairs where possible ([IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 8, pp. 16–17 of the printed publication / PDF pp. 23–24](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1359_web.pdf)). That publication is visibly marked superseded by No. 8-G (Rev. 1), so it is evidence for the mechanism's design, not a claim about current binding requirements.

The transferable element is **independent confirmation of an action's authorization, identity, and target**. The non-transferable superficial element is “two entities looked at the same words.” Two models given the same flattened payload and the same prompt can share the same category error; that is analogous to two workers cueing off one another instead of independently checking the component and guiding document.

### 3. Conservative decision-making: stop under uncertainty, but stop the right thing

The NRC defines failure to stop work/non-conservative decision-making as failing to stop or establish controls when work conditions are unfavorable or uncertain ([NRC Human Factors Definition List, items 189–190](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/ops-experience/human-factors/definition-list)). DOE's handbook makes this operational: when uncertainty persists, stop and ask; a graded pause or timeout may be used; workers should obtain clarification or support before resuming ([DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, vol. 2, pp. 16–17](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/1028-2009_Volume%202.pdf)). DOE's safety-culture guidance separately names “risk-informed, conservative decision making” alongside questioning attitude and performance monitoring through multiple means ([DOE G 450.4-1C, attachment 10](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-09/ISM%20Safety%20Culture%20Focus%20Areas%20and%20Associated%20Attributes.pdf)).

“Conservative” is not synonymous with “block whenever scary words occur.” In this target, the hazardous transition is an unauthorized real-world or filesystem action. Blocking the reading of a safety report because it mentions that action does not place the system in a safer state; it can prevent analysis needed to become safe. The correct translation is asymmetric:

- uncertainty about **data being data** permits continued non-actuating inspection;
- uncertainty about whether a **candidate action is authorized** prevents that action from crossing the actuation boundary;
- the detector reports what fact is missing so the ambiguity can be resolved.

This preserves the nuclear relationship between uncertainty and the specific work evolution at risk instead of turning conservative decision-making into indiscriminate lexical censorship.

### 4. Defence in depth: prevention, detection, mitigation—with independence

IAEA training material describes defence in depth as successive prevention, monitoring, and mitigation, explicitly assuming failures will occur. Each level should prevent degradation of the next and mitigate failure of the previous; successive levels should be as independent as possible ([IAEA Basic Principles of Nuclear Safety, Module 3, pp. 23–29](https://gnssn.iaea.org/main/bptc/BPTC%20Module%20Documents/Module03%20Basic%20principles%20of%20nuclear%20safety.pdf)). The UK national report likewise describes five levels and says they should, so far as practicable, be independent, with a later level compensating for failure of an earlier one ([UK Ninth National Report, “Consideration of defence-in-depth”](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/compliance-with-the-convention-on-nuclear-safety-obligations-9th-national-report/compliance-with-the-convention-on-nuclear-safety-obligations-9th-national-report)).

The independence qualifier is essential. IAEA's Fukushima lessons state that flooding simultaneously challenged multiple levels and calls for independence, redundancy, diversity, and protection from common-cause failure ([IAEA Fukushima lessons, Safety Assessment](https://gnssn.iaea.org/FukushimaLessonsLearned/Lists/Fukushima/DispForm.aspx?ContentTypeId=0x01006A6D303BCE89A14FA6083DD8D969FFE0&ID=185)). In the harness, five regex-like detectors over the same flattened payload are five instances, not five independent defences. A shared loss of provenance defeats all of them at once.

## Translation: what to change in the harness

### Proposal A — UNVERIFIED: typed action envelope before content guards

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Add a small normalization hook that emits a typed envelope for every guard-relevant event. It should preserve fields already known at the hook boundary instead of asking a lexical detector to reconstruct them from concatenated text:

```text
event_phase: observe | propose_action | actuate | observe_result
provenance: owner_instruction | harness_policy | assistant_output |
            tool_argument | tool_result | retrieved_content | quoted_example
operation: read | write | delete | execute | send | publish | install | other
target: typed resource identifier, if any
authority: none | requested | policy_granted | approval_required
reversibility: read_only | reversible | difficult_to_reverse
payload_role: control | candidate_action | data
```

The critical invariant is:

```text
content detector may veto action
    only if event_phase in {propose_action, actuate}
    and payload_role == candidate_action
    and a typed operation/target is bound
```

Raw content remains available as evidence, but it cannot alone upgrade `data` into `candidate_action`. A retrieved page containing “delete all records,” a policy document saying “never publish secrets,” or a log containing a serialized tool call remains mention-space. Conversely, a tool call with a bland or aliased payload remains use-space because its operation and target come from the tool event, not trigger words.

This proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to eliminate the stated false-fire mechanism, because it restores the exact distinction the task says is currently flattened and parallels DOE's separation of guiding document, intended action, correct component, and actuation. It is **UNVERIFIED; settle it with experiment E1: shadow-classify the five detectors' historical events, requiring 100% of the 12+ known false fires to remain non-actuating while retaining all confirmed genuine positives**. If genuine positives are unavailable, construct at least one matched use/mention pair for every detector and do not claim recall from synthetic data alone.

What it catches that payload matching can miss:

- an actual destructive API/tool operation whose arguments never contain the detector's favored verb;
- an aliased shell or PowerShell command whose semantic tool operation is execute even if its surface form is indirect;
- a `send` or `publish` action where the dangerous content is in a separate attachment/reference rather than the scanned string;
- a candidate action assembled across fields, where no one field contains the full lexical pattern.

The claim that the existing bus exposes enough structured provenance for this envelope is **UNVERIFIED and roughly even (45-55%)**, because this ideation unit intentionally did not inspect local event schemas. **Experiment E0 settles it: capture one known-present event of each relevant hook/tool type and inventory immutable metadata before any concatenation or templating.** If operation, phase, and provenance are already native, implementation is mostly mapping. If they have already been destroyed upstream, Proposal A must move to the earliest event boundary; it must not guess them from the flattened payload.

### Proposal B — UNVERIFIED: STAR micro-check at actuation, not at ingestion

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Change each detector contract to a four-step decision record, evaluated only for a candidate action:

```text
STOP:   identify the concrete operation, target, event phase, and provenance
THINK:  cite the authority and expected state transition; distinguish content from control
ACT:    allow, deny, or request approval for that one bound action
REVIEW: compare the resulting tool status/diff/receipt with the expected transition
```

The `STOP` and `THINK` fields should be machine-required, enumerated fields rather than free-form chain-of-thought. A detector that cannot name the operation and target returns `NOT_AN_ACTION` or `AMBIGUOUS_ACTION`; it does not fire on the payload. `REVIEW` is a small result check already attached to the original action identifier, not a new workflow engine.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to improve precision beyond Proposal A alone, because it forces referent binding and expected-result declaration, matching STAR's correct-action/correct-component discipline. The lower band reflects the possibility that detector contracts cannot influence early hook execution or that required fields become ceremonial. It is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E2 settles it by ablation: compare typed envelope alone against typed envelope plus STAR fields on the replay suite, and retain STAR only if it catches additional seeded wrong-target or wrong-phase cases without increasing false fires or median latency beyond the owner's chosen budget**.

What it catches that a role label alone can miss:

- a genuine action bound to the wrong target (right verb, wrong component);
- a proposed action that was authorized for preview but reaches execution phase;
- an operation whose expected result does not match its declared purpose;
- a stale action resumed after interruption against a changed target or state.

### Proposal C — UNVERIFIED: two independent signals only for ambiguous, consequential actuation

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: BUILD-SMALL (<~200 lines, rides the existing hook/skill/MCP bus).**

Do **not** send every payload to a second agent. Require concurrence only when all three conditions hold: the envelope says `candidate_action`; reversibility is `difficult_to_reverse` (or existing policy already requires approval); and the first check is ambiguous or policy marks the operation high consequence.

The two signals must be mechanically different:

1. **Structural signal:** tool/event metadata proves phase, operation, target, provenance, and authority.
2. **Semantic/policy signal:** a narrow verifier receives the structured projection plus the applicable policy and decides whether the proposed transition is authorized.

The verifier must not receive a single flattened copy of the same raw payload as its only input. For owner-approval operations, the existing native permission prompt can be the second signal; no synthetic “second person” is needed. Disagreement stops only the action and emits the missing or conflicting field.

This proposal is **likely (55-80%)** to catch high-impact role errors missed by either structure or semantic interpretation alone, because DOE peer checking independently binds action and component and nuclear guidance explicitly warns against dependent layers. It is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E3 settles it using adversarial pairs in which (a) structure is correct but quoted text is alarming, (b) structure is misleading but an actual capability call follows, (c) both checkers receive the same poisoned content, and (d) authorization changes between proposal and actuation. Measure correlated-error rate, not merely individual accuracy.**

Two-model concurrence with identical context is **unlikely (20-45%)** to provide meaningful independence, because DOE warns that peer checking degrades through assumption, cueing, inattention, and overuse, and the IAEA Fukushima lesson demonstrates why nominally multiple layers can fail from one cause. The settling experiment is the correlated-error portion of E3: compare same-prompt model replicas with the structural-plus-semantic pairing.

### Proposal D — UNVERIFIED: asymmetric conservative fallback and graded defence

**NATIVE-vs-BUILD: CONFIG (settings/JSON only).**

Set explicit fallback rules:

| Situation | Conservative outcome |
|---|---|
| Known data/quotation/retrieved content, no candidate action | Continue inspection; no action guard fires |
| Candidate action, authority and target clear | Apply the relevant guard normally |
| Candidate action, role/authority/target ambiguous | Hold action; ask for the missing fact or approval |
| Detector unavailable or malformed at actuation | Hold only guarded high-consequence action; report detector failure distinctly |
| Post-action result differs from expected | Stop subsequent dependent actions and surface the mismatch |

Arrange the defence layers by different evidence and function:

1. **Preserve:** structured provenance and phase at ingestion.
2. **Prevent:** typed actuation gate binds operation, target, and authority.
3. **Detect:** narrow semantic/policy verification for ambiguous high-consequence transitions.
4. **Review/recover:** compare the actual receipt/diff/status with the declared expected result and stop dependent continuation on mismatch.

This proposal is **very likely (80-90%)** to avoid the pathological “document cannot be inspected because it describes danger” outcome, because only a bound actuation can enter the blocking branch. It is **UNVERIFIED; experiment E4 settles it with documents containing every detector trigger in quotations, negations, examples, code blocks, logs, and retrieved untrusted content, followed by genuine actions derived from each document. Reading must always complete; unauthorized actuation must never complete.**

Post-action review is **likely (55-80%)** to catch wrong-target and unexpected-effect failures that pre-action role checks cannot, because DOE STAR explicitly compares observed and anticipated results. It is **UNVERIFIED; E2's seeded wrong-result cases settle it.** It is not a substitute for prevention, consistent with IAEA defence in depth's instruction that mitigation must not justify reducing prevention.

## Adversarial analysis: where the analogy breaks

### Disanalogy 1: physical components have identity; language has nested and contested roles

A valve has a plant identifier and a physical state. Text may contain quotations inside code inside a retrieved document, indirect speech, negation, sarcasm, or an instruction that becomes actionable only after an agent adopts it. There is no universal “touch the component label” equivalent for natural language. Therefore a semantic-role classifier cannot be treated as a perfect engineered interlock.

Consequence: the strongest layer must be event provenance and actual capability invocation, not a claim that an LLM understands mention-space perfectly. The typed envelope proposal remains **very likely (80-90%)** to help when provenance exists, but a general semantic classifier alone is **unlikely (20-45%)** to attain interlock-like reliability; evidence is the categorical difference between physical identity and compositional language, and E1/E4 settle the harness-specific rates.

### Disanalogy 2: nuclear conservatism protects against asymmetric physical harm; false positives also cause harm here

In plant operations, pausing a risky evolution often moves the system toward safety, though even nuclear emergency actions recognize tradeoffs. In an agent harness, blocking benign reading can prevent diagnosis, suppress security analysis, or create retry loops. “When unsure, block everything” does not transfer.

Consequence: only the state-changing action fails closed. Observation fails open with provenance retained. This asymmetric rule is not a relaxation of action safety; it is a definition of the controlled boundary.

### Disanalogy 3: two agents are not two independent people

Two model calls may share training, prompt, context serialization, and the same missing metadata. They can be more correlated than two qualified operators with separate physical observation. Calling both “two-person rule” would overstate assurance.

Consequence: independence must come from different evidence paths—event structure versus semantic/policy interpretation—and human approval where already required. A second LLM is optional, graded, and never sufficient merely because it is second.

### Disanalogy 4: the consequence scale does not justify nuclear ceremony everywhere

Most harness actions are reversible and frequent. Universal peer checks would add latency, cost, and complacency, exactly the overuse failure DOE identifies for peer checking. It would also violate the anti-overfit preference for minimal architecture.

Consequence: grade checks by consequence and ambiguity. Routine reads and reversible operations use the typed boundary once; only ambiguous difficult-to-reverse actions obtain a second signal. This selective approach is **likely (55-80%)** to outperform universal concurrence on cost-adjusted precision because it avoids known overuse failure modes, but is **UNVERIFIED; E3 must compare always-on and graded concurrence using precision, recall, latency, and reviewer-disagreement fatigue/proxy rate.**

### Disanalogy 5: “authorized” is easier to prescribe in a plant than infer in an open-ended conversation

Nuclear work has controlled procedures, trained roles, equipment configuration, and explicit work authorization. Agent authority can be distributed across owner instructions, system policy, tool permissions, and later corrections. A role gate cannot manufacture a single authoritative procedure.

Consequence: the envelope must cite authority provenance, not output a bare authorized/unauthorized guess. Conflicts escalate under existing precedence and approval mechanisms. If the harness cannot preserve authority provenance, the nuclear analogy does not justify inventing it from payload text; that case fails Proposal A's E0 gate.

## Recommended decision and acceptance gates

Adopt the narrow actuation-boundary borrow, contingent on E0. Do not adopt a generic “nuclear-style reviewer” or a second autonomous runtime.

The recommended sequence is:

1. Run E0 to prove structured phase/provenance is present at a known event boundary.
2. Shadow Proposal A on historical and paired synthetic cases; make no blocking change.
3. Add Proposal B fields only if the ablation catches errors Proposal A misses.
4. Add Proposal C only for ambiguous difficult-to-reverse operations and only if its signals show lower correlated error than duplicate model review.
5. Enable Proposal D's asymmetric fallback after the replay demonstrates that documents remain readable and genuine unauthorized actions remain stopped.

The composite design is **likely (55-80%)** to be the best transfer from this field, rather than merely an evocative analogy, because every borrowed mechanism maps to the same invariant: representations inform action, but only a separately identified, authorized, checked act changes state. Confidence is capped because no local schemas or replay traces were inspected in this ideation unit. **UNVERIFIED; the decisive experiment is E0 followed by E1–E4 on the five real detectors.**

Reject the borrow if any of these falsifiers occurs:

- E0 shows the hook bus cannot distinguish tool event phase/provenance before text is flattened, and moving the check earlier would require a new runtime rather than a small bus hook.
- E1 shows known false fires arise primarily from something other than role flattening.
- The envelope loses genuine-positive recall relative to the current detector on confirmed real cases.
- E3 shows the proposed “independent” signals fail together at nearly the same rate as one checker.
- The solution requires a broad semantic classification service or durable workflow to function.

If E0 fails, the honest result is that this analogy does not transfer within the allowed minimal architecture. Do not compensate with more prompt layers over the same flattened payload.

## Experiment suite in executable terms

### E0 — bus feasibility/control validation

Use one known-present event for each of: owner instruction, assistant proposal, tool call, tool result, retrieved document, and quoted/code content. Demonstrate that the probe detects the known-present tool call before making any absence claim. Inspect two mechanically independent signals: native event type/field and observed downstream tool dispatch/receipt. Output a field-preservation matrix. This satisfies the requirement that an absence detector first prove it can see a control.

### E1 — historical replay

Build a frozen labelled set containing all 12+ false fires, all available confirmed true fires, and matched counterfactual pairs differing only in semantic role. Compare current outcome, typed-envelope outcome, and adjudicated label. Primary gates: zero known false-fire repetitions; no confirmed-positive regression; every decision records phase, provenance, operation, and target or explicitly says not an action.

### E2 — STAR ablation

Seed wrong-target, wrong-phase, stale-state, and unexpected-result cases. Compare envelope-only versus envelope-plus-STAR. Retain STAR only for incremental catches. Track latency and malformed-decision rate so mandatory fields cannot silently become prose ceremony.

### E3 — independence/common-cause test

Compare: single verifier; two identical model verifiers; diverse model prompts; and structural signal plus semantic/policy signal. Include poisoned/quoted text shared by both, missing provenance, conflicting authority, and changed state between proposal and actuation. Measure joint error and disagreement, not only per-checker accuracy.

### E4 — nested mention stress test

Generate cases across quotation, negation, policy prohibition, code block, serialized tool call, logs, retrieved malicious instruction, translation, and a document that instructs a later genuine tool call. For each, test both “inspect only” and “inspect then actuate.” The role boundary, not trigger vocabulary, must determine whether the guard evaluates an action.

No experiment may declare a detector absent or fixed from one signal. A “done” claim requires both replay results and observed bus/tool receipts or state diffs.

- The labels and exact failure shapes of the 12+ false fires are **UNVERIFIED in this unit; E1 settles them from the real replay corpus**. The count and cause are accepted only as the supplied task premise.
- The five detectors may protect hazards with different consequence profiles. A shared envelope is **likely (55-80%)** to generalize because role/provenance is cross-cutting, but detector-specific policies are **UNVERIFIED; stratified E1 results settle this**.
- Latency and token cost of semantic verification are **UNVERIFIED; E2/E3 settle them under the owner's deployment model and workload**.
- It is **roughly even (45-55%)** that CONFIG alone can express STAR and asymmetric fallback; this depends on current schema expressiveness, for which this lens has no local evidence. **A schema dry-run during E0 settles it; if CONFIG cannot express the fields, merge them into Proposal A's BUILD-SMALL hook without expanding to BUILD-LARGE.**
